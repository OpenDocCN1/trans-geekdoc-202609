# 4.3.5 Handling large datasets with stochastic gradient descent

When your tabular dataset cannot fit into your system's memory, whether it is a cloud instance or your desktop computer, your options in modeling shrink. Apart from deep learning solutions, which will be discussed in the third part of this book, one other option, using classical machine learning, is resort to out-of-core learning. In out-of-core learning, you keep your data in its storage (for instance, your data warehouse), and you have your model learn from it by bit, using small samples extracted from your data, batches. This is practically feasible because modern data storage allows for picking picking specific data samples at a certain cost in terms of latency: the time interval between the initiation of a data-related operation and its completion or response. In addition, there are also tools for handling and processing data on the fly (for instance, Apache Kafka or Amazon Kinetics) that can redirect the data to out-of-core learning algorithms.

It is also practically feasible of linear/regression models. Both models are made up of coefficients which relate to the features you use for learning. Out-of-core learning involves first estimating these coefficients using small samples from the data and then updating such coefficients using more and more batches extracted from your data. In the end, the process is particularly long, your final estimated coefficient would not be much different the ones you would have obtained if you could fit all the data into memory.

How many batches you have to use for your out-of-core modeling, and if you have to reuse them multiple times, it is a matter of empirical experimentation: it depends on the problem and the data you are using. Though providing new batches of unseen data may prolong your training phase, having the algorithm the same batches again may cause it to overfit.
