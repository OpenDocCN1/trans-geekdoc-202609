# 2.7.6 Errors in data

Spotting incongruencies, misaligned, flipped, or distorted variables is instead a topic of ours because fixing such a problem mostly depends on your knowledge of the application domain and the recording procedures for the data you are handling. Many errors may happen when a phenomenon is observed in the real world and recorded data. Errors range from inescapable measurement errors because of the instruments and sensors we use to an extensive catalog of mismatches, underestimates, and overestimates that will make the recording completely unreliable. More than a single error here in certain situations and you should look for systematic errors. These errors always happen because of biases of the information. Knowing the data schema and the meaning of the data you are dealing with is the only reasonable remedy for such problems. It may sound very general and good advice because it is, in reality, you can only understand your data to the best of your ability and not leave anything for granted. Don't believe that machine learning algorithms are robust and you can fix all the errors in the data: systematic errors can limit the utility of your models to generalize and provide reliable predictions.

# 2.7.7 Leakage features

Finally, the last point of concern you should deal with is the presence of leakage features. You have leakage when predictive information that shouldn't be involved in the model training temporarily inflates results, rendering poor results at subsequent prediction time. Leakage can happen at row and feature levels. We have already discussed leakage when dealing with non-ID rows, which occur when some features are associated with others because of time mediation or the mediation of some other feature. Now it is time to discuss feature-level leakage, which is a more common problem than extracting data from business databases.

The key principle is to ensure temporal consistency in the features used for modeling. Ideally, the features should align with or precede the target variable in time. In the best scenario, no feature should be created or generated after the target variable's own time point.
