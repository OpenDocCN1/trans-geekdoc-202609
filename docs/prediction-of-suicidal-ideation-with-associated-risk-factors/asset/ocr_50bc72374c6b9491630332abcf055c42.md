# ABSTRACT

Background: The prevalence of suicidal ideation has become an urgent issue, particularly adolescents. The primary objective of this research is to determine the prevalence of suicidal ideation among students in the northern region of Bangladesh and to prevent this phenomenon using machine learning (ML) models. Method: The data were collected using a random sampling technique to gather information from university students located in the northern region during the period ranging from April 2022 to June 2022. Upon collection, the sample size was determined to be 584, with 51.5% of identifying as male and 48.5% female. Results: A significant proportion of students experienced suicidal ideation. Participants were free (77%) and ideated (78%). Within the machine learning (ML) framework, the Random Forest (RF) and Gradient Boosting (GB) algorithms exhibited comparable high accuracy of 91.45% and 90.66% respectively. Limitation: None. Conclusion: Mental health practitioners can employ the KNN model alongside patients' histories to identify those who may be at high risk of attempting suicide. This approach enhances healthcare professionals to take appropriate measures, such as counseling, regular sleep patterns, and addressing depression and anxiety, to prevent suicidal behavior.

# 1. Introduction

Suicidal ideation, which involves thoughts of suicide or self-harm, serves as a critical tool for identifying individuals at risk of suicide (Zisstein et al., 2009). The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that suicide is a major public health problem, among teenagers aged 15 to 19 (WHO), often occurring every 40 days and resulting in 700,000 deaths annually, with a significant 77% of these incidents taking place in low- and middle-income countries (WHO, 2020). Furthermore, approximately 17.2% of individuals in developing countries have made suicidal ideation, with adolescents and females being the most affected (Li et al., 2018). Agrawal and Talukipalli (2019) highlight that various mental health conditions, including bipolar disorder, depression, anxiety, and dementia, contribute to the underlying causes of suicidal behavior. Excessive internet use, particularly driven to platforms like Facebook and smartphones, has also been linked to increased risk of suicide attempts (Johan and Hansen, 2021). Specific circumstances, such as HIV status, adverse life experiences, depression frequency, and first episode psychosis, have been associated with high rates of suicide with rates of 16.3%, 88.5%, and 40.8%, respectively. Reported in recent studies (García et al., 2020; Pelliza et al., 2022), suicidal ideation is common among adolescents.

In Southeast Asian, the prevalence of suicidal ideation is concerning, with 17.7% reporting thoughts and planning among 9% of students. Within this region, some countries have the highest numbers of suicide deaths, occurring at a staggering rate of one suicide every 25 days (García et al., 2022).
