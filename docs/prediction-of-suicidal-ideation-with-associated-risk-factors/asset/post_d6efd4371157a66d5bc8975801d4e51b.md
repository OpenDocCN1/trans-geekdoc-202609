# ABSTRACT

Background: The prevalence of suicidal ideation has become an urgent issue, particularly among adolescents. The primary objective of this research is to determine the prevalence of suicidal ideation among students in the northern region of Bangladesh and to prevent this phenomenon using machine learning (ML) models. Method: The data were collected using a random sampling technique to gather information from university students located in the northern region during the period ranging from April 2022 to June 2022. Upon collection, the sample size was determined to be 584, with 51.5% identifying as male and 48.5% female. Results: A significant proportion of students experienced suicidal ideation. Participants were free (77%) and ideated (78%). Within the machine learning (ML) framework, the Random Forest (RF) and Gradient Boosting (GB) algorithms exhibited comparable high accuracy of 91.45% and 90.66% respectively. Limitation: None. Conclusion: Mental health practitioners can employ the KNN model alongside patients' histories to identify those who may be at high risk of attempting suicide. This approach enhances healthcare professionals to take appropriate measures, such as counseling, regular sleep patterns, and addressing depression and anxiety, to prevent suicidal behavior.

# 1. Introduction

Suicidal ideation, which involves thoughts of suicide or self-harm, serves as a critical tool for identifying individuals at risk of suicide (Zisstein et al., 2009). The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that suicide is a major public health problem, among teenagers aged 15 to 19 (WHO), often occurring every 40 days and resulting in 700,000 deaths annually, with a significant 77% of these incidents taking place in low- and middle-income countries (WHO, 2020). Furthermore, approximately 17.2% of individuals in developing countries have made suicidal ideation, with adolescents and females being the most affected (Li et al., 2018). Agrawal and Talukipalli (2019) highlight that various mental health conditions, including bipolar disorder, depression, anxiety, and dementia, contribute to the underlying causes of suicidal behavior. Excessive internet use, particularly driven to platforms like Facebook and smartphones, has also been linked to increased risk of suicide attempts (Johan and Hansen, 2021). Specific circumstances, such as HIV status, adverse life experiences, depression frequency, and first episode psychosis, have been associated with high rates of suicide with rates of 16.3%, 88.5%, and 40.8%, respectively. Reported in recent studies (García et al., 2020; Pelliza et al., 2022), suicidal ideation is common among adolescents.

In Southeast Asian, the prevalence of suicidal ideation is concerning, with 17.7% reporting thoughts and planning among 9% of students. ### 2.2.1. Independent variables

The variables explored in the study included: marital status, family income, residence, physical activity, smoking habits, social quality, relationship with partner, and depression. The assessment of physical activity level was conducted utilizing the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ), which was employed in prior research endeavors (Ranasinghe et al., 2013).

The Suicidal Behaviour Questionnaire (SBQ-R) was used in our study to assess suicidal ideation among the participants. The SBQ-R consists of four items, each having a distinct dimension. The measurement of lifetime suicidal ideation or attempt was conducted using a scale where participants reported the frequency of the suicidal ideation over the past two months. The study evaluated each using a cut-off score (>7) to identify the presence of suicidal ideation among the participants. The Cronbach’s alpha score was 0.78, which could be considered acceptable.

### 2.2.3. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD-7)

A screening tool and scale used for generalized anxiety among the participants. The instrument consists of seven items, each having points ranging from 0 “not all” to 3 “nearly every day”. The GAD-7 score was ranging from 0-21. According to Osman-Samani et al. (2018), the scores were interpreted as: minimal anxiety (0-4), mild anxiety (5-9), moderate anxiety (10-14), and severe anxiety (15-21). The study yielded a satisfactory reliability, with a Cronbach’s alpha value of 0.851.

### 2.2.2. Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9)

The Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9) was used to determine the severity of depression among the participants. The Cronbach’s alpha score was 0.82.
