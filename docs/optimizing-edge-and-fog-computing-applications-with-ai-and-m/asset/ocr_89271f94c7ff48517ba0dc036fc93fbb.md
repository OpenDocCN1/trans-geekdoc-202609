The research paper "Dependency-Aware Assigning and Scheduling in Edge Computing" explores dependency-aware program assignment and scheduling in EC. It identifies issues such as task interdependency, limited varied resources, and unseen spatial-temporal distribution of requests. The study models applications as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) and presents Daar, a unique approach for simultaneously optimizing task assignment and scheduling in an online setting, solving both NP-hard challenges. Daar uses a heuristic technique to assess job priority, allocating and scheduling activities iteratively to optimize resource usage and fulfill application deadlines. Key findings show that Daar allows up to 20% more opportunities to achieve deadlines than baseline approaches, with a maximum improvement of 38% in large-scale cases. Methodological limitations include a failure to account for storage limits, real-time unpredictability in computation times, and simplifications of edge server and base station connections. Drawbacks include the solution's heuristic character, which does not ensure global optimality, as well as computing cost from iterative optimization. Despite its shortcomings, Daar offers a useful and efficient framework for managing dependent applications in dynamic EC settings.

The paper "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Intelligent Building Edge Network" addresses the shortcomings of cloud-based scheduling, which suffers with high latency and inefficient resource use in intelligent edge networks. To overcome these, the authors suggest a DDQN-based task scheduling algorithm with an empirical replay mechanism. This strategy increases scheduling success rates, speeds convergence, and decreases edge server overhead.
