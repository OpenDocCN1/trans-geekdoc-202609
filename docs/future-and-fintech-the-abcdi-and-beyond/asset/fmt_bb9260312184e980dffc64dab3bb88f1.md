
# Chapter 4

## AI Fairness in the Financial Industry: A Machine Learning Pipeline Approach

### Jun Xu

## 4.1 Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies and Machine Learning (ML) algorithms have inspired a lot of excitement in our daily life, from smart television (TV), mobile phones, driver-less cars to the robot landing on Mars. In particular, with the creation, collection, and storage of "Big Data", and dramatically concentrated computing power and storage capacity in large business and financial organizations, ML algorithms have demonstrated the power to identify non-intuitive and implicit correlations in massive datasets that humans may not recognize. Thus, ML models have been widely adopted by financial institutions (FIs) as a means for decision-making processes, from high-frequency trading, cybersecurity, to various financial products and services.

In recent years, how to use AI properly has gained renewed popular interest. In fact, when AI brings tons of benefits, some applications making use of AI may generate harms to human society if AI ethics, particularly AI fairness, is not carefully considered. For example, the Correctional Offender Management Profiling for Alternative Sanctions (COMPAS) software in the US has been proven to be biased against African Americans for the judicial systems. One of the hospitalization score models for healthcare systems favors certain racial groups.

In the financial industry, AI fairness also plays an important role. Decisions made by machines could impact a large group of user population for financial products and services, such as credit score and loan, and some level of biases inherently exist. Some data collected from FI clients may be prone to bias and imbalance, for example, more male holders than female holders own world elite business credit cards. Such biases may also exist in chatbots, online advertisement and marketing champion, facial recognition, etc.

However, due to the black-box nature of most ML and Deep Learning (DL) algorithms, fairness of algorithm output becomes a serious concern, although it is expected to remove human prejudices from decision-making processes with the "fairness" of the machine! Therefore, many studies and experiments have been conducted to embed fairness into AI modeling.

This chapter proposes a practical framework for addressing the problems above. It starts with high-level conceptual prospects on AI ethics and fairness. Then, we develop a guidance on building an ML pipeline approach. Both connections and contradictions between ML and fairness concepts are intensively discussed. Examples are demonstrated on how to consider fairness in terms of ML pipeline. The contribution of this chapter is threefold: (i) it summarizes the key points of AI fairness in FIs; (ii) introduces an ML pipeline approach to explain fairness treatment with structured representations in detail; (iii) elaborates substantive workflow charts and checklists.

## 4.2 AI Ethics and Fairness

AI ethics has been intensively discussed by many government organizations, e.g., the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) proposed FEAT principles (i.e., fairness, ethics, accountability and transparency) and practices, the US Defense Department adopts five principles (i.e., responsible, equitable, traceable, reliable, and governable), the EU released ethics guidelines for trustworthy AI in 2018, and the Australian Government also released a version with eight rules. Other countries such as China, the UK, and Australia also have released AI ethical guidelines. In Figure 4.1, we suggest seven main characteristics.
