We use an overlay network because it can cluster certain nodes and virtualize the network. It also provides an additional security mechanism. It is well known that blockchain is not an absolutely secure solution. Therefore, we must carefully design security protocols at every layer.

Let us consider an example where a smart home sells and publishes its sensed data. [Figure 10.7](#chap10.xhtml#fig10-7) illustrates how sensed data is verified and valued by IoT data brokers and device manufacturers. The links show how data travels among the four parties. First, we must ensure the sensing devices are trustworthy. Therefore, verification between the data broker and the device manufacturer should occur via a public key mechanism. Once the data is verified, the data broker publishes it to the blockchain. Here, we can flexibly choose a consortium or public chain network.

#### 10.4.3.1 Consensus Protocol

Due to the limited computing power of IoT devices, it is usually difficult to apply PoW (Proof of Work) as the underlying consensus strategy. Therefore, we can adopt a so-called PoW + PoS (Proof of Stake) + PoT (Proof of Time) strategy. The PoW here is a pseudo-Proof of Work because it does not perform actual computational work. It works with PoT through an auxiliary reward system consisting of two parts. One is the quality and quantity of sensed data contributed by IoT devices to the data platform. Sensed data can be sold to buyers based on its quality, quantity, and privacy level. The second is the degree of participation in online activities, such as the activeness of IoT devices participating in general platform tasks or missions controlled by smart contracts. For example, completing online tasks such as remote sensing for specific scenarios, service sharing, gaming, etc.

PoS can be implemented via credits directly linked to the exchange platform. This means users can increase credits to gain a higher probability of block generation. Note that a dynamic exchange ratio between real currency and virtual digital currency with relatively stable value should be maintained to a certain extent.

#### 10.4.3.3 Reconfigurable Blockchain

Since certain smart home devices have limited resources, they usually cannot store all blocks of a frequent transaction chain. Therefore, it is necessary to define multiple integration and security levels to allow users to choose their preferences. For example, a user can choose to store only the most recent seven days of data on their IoT device, while querying other data from the smart home hub or its association's cloud server.

### 10.5 Smart Home-based Financial Applications

Once the blockchain and AI-assisted platform is built, we can develop various smart home financial applications with designed business objectives. In this section, we list some potential examples.

#### 10.5.1 Hybrid Currency

Digital assets and virtual/digital currencies are important applications. For example, once a refrigerator is manufactured, a transaction is sent to the blockchain. Thus, it has a unique and immutable record in the network. Any change of ownership can be simply completed online. The following are some general cases:

*   Asset Virtualization/Digitization (Blockchain/DLT)
    *   Physical assets, such as real estate, automobiles, home appliances, etc.
    *   Monetary assets, such as cash, stocks, Provident Fund (CPF), etc.
    *   Data assets, such as IoT data, creative digital works, etc.
*   Virtual Currency with Real Value
    *   Not limited to virtual currency in games
    *   IoTCoin — Securing consumer information, e.g., different tiers and native reward systems for different stores

#### 10.5.2 Smart Payment

Smart payment services are now widely used, such as mobile apps and e-wallets. New use cases and commercial IoT applications capable of initiating remote payments are undoubtedly emerging, including smart assistants (voice devices, such as Amazon Echo, AliGenie, and Google Home), refrigerators, and in-vehicle dashboard systems. Alternative currencies, such as IoTCoin or Smart Home Coin, may be available for your digital items or home facilities in the near future. It can be used for automatic or mobile payments, such as online shopping (possibly via AR/VR/MR, with automatic product comparison and recommendation); licenses for software, games, videos, music, books, etc.; subscriptions for TV, VoD, news, courses, and other digital content; subscriptions for remote monitoring and medical services; utilities (gas, electricity, water, etc.); automatic adjustment of usage and frequency for efficiency and savings; real-time alert systems for abnormal events, etc.

#### 10.5.3 Customized Loans

Loans (housing, personal, auto, etc.) are one of the basic financial services, which still rely on a passive mode. In this mode, customers usually initiate requests. The so-called credit card balance transfer is one of the simplest cases.

For the active mode, banks proactively recommend suitable loans to customers. In this case, the bank needs to understand the customer's actual needs, which can most likely be obtained by analyzing data collected from the smart home hub with user consent. Unfortunately, this may seriously infringe on customer privacy. Therefore, banks need appropriate solutions to maintain a balance between recommendation and privacy, subject to customer consent.

#### 10.5.4 Personalized Insurance

Similarly, personal or home insurance has both passive and active modes. The passive mode is similar to traditional counter services (initiated by the customer), although some online services are also provided (i.e., shopping carts with multiple options, question-based wizards, etc.). The active mode attempts to understand the status of the insured object in real-time, such as a trip. The following are a few examples:

*   Smart home and property insurance with smart home sensing capabilities;
