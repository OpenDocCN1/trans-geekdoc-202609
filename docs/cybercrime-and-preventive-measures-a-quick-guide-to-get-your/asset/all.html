Cybercrime and Preventive Measures

 
Introduction to cybersecurity
 
Digital data is surging day by day or minute by minute. We are dependent on computers and mobile devices even to accomplish the smallest work of our daily regimes.
 
Cybersecurity is a vast subject and includes computer security, network security, application security, website security, software security, mobile security, cloud security, Internet of Things (IoT) devices, smart devices, information security, and so on. To protect hardware devices, software, and information (data) from various kinds of attacks, viruses, or unauthorized access, we need cybersecurity. Cybersecurity is constantly evolving, but we can say that it involves ensuring the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of information.

 
The need for cybersecurity awareness
 
Advanced technologies, especially the internet, have been life-changing for all of us, but they also have many side effects and make us vulnerable. Cyberattacks are increasing minute by minute; almost everyone has been a victim of cybercrime directly or indirectly. Data breaches can be very devastating for business organizations and high-profile individuals. These types of incidences are being reported by the media almost daily. As per leading research organisations, mostly cyber-attacks happen due to a lack of human intelligence. Humans are the weakest link in the chain. Therefore, cybersecurity awareness is necessary for each one of us.

Email-related Cybercrime

 
Introduction
 
Cybercrime is a criminal activity wherein a single computer, multiple computers, or interconnected computers/devices have been used. A cyberattack may involve the intranet and internet. Cybercrime can be committed by an individual, group of people, organizations, and even state-sponsored agencies.

 
Structure
 
In this chapter, we will cover the following email-related popular cybercrimes (along with examples) such as:
  
Spamming
 
Phishing
 
Bombing
 
Spoofing

 
Objective
 
To make you aware of a variety of cybercrimes carried out through email and preventive measures to safeguard.

 
Popular email-related cybercrimes
 
In this diagram, the first line defines cybercrime, and the second line tells the objective.
 
Some of the most popular email related cybercrimes are shown in Figure
 
 
Figure 1.1: Email-related cybercrimes
 
Let us now see them in detail one by one:
  
Unwanted, bulk emails sent to recipients for some motive such as advertising, stealing personal information, installation of malware, or selling illegal/prohibited items. Often it is called the negative side of email
 
 
Figure 1.2: Spam email
 
Cybercriminals hacked Sumit's email ID and took over his laptop.
 
When Sumit opened his email ID to check emails from school, he could not log in. So, he filed a hacking complaint. After a police investigation, it was found that hackers performed an extensive search on Sumit activities, crawled his social media accounts such as Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, and found his date of birth.
 
Almost researching for two months, the cybercriminals sent him an email with some attachments pretending to be his school administration authority. They chose a very catchy subject to grab Sumit's attention, made him download the attachments, and malware was downloaded onto his computer, and attackers got access to his laptop and webcam.
 
Cyber criminals impersonate individuals, organizations, and so on via emails. These fraudulent emails may appear from various government organizations such as the income tax department stating that you have not filed the income tax return on time, so you have to pay the penalty; it may pretend to be from a bank asking to update your KYC details; they send an exciting sale to offer free shopping voucher or cashback offer, or they may send threatening email stating that your email account will be closed if you don't respond to this email. As you click on the mentioned link in the email, it directs you to a fake website which is a look-alike of a legitimate website. An email related to corona update is a new phishing trend.
 
 
Figure 1.3: Phishing
 
Victim receives an email from appearing to be from World Health Organization The email had content related to COVID update and a weblink about coronavirus map update. On clicking, it directs him/her to a web page that asks for his personal information and address. On mouse click, this webpage installs malware to steal sensitive information from the device.
 
Email Cyber criminals send a large number of emails in a very short span of time to flood your inbox using automated programs. It results in bouncing of emails as the email quota exceeds. Due to this, you may miss important communication, which may further result in the hanging of the webserver. Normally, official emails are the target as cyber criminals may have various intentions, such as taking control of your email id and harming the mail server by performing a denial of service attack.
 
 
Figure 1.4: Email bombing
 
A web hosting company was noticing heavy traffic on its web servers for some days, which was unusual. One day, many of its customer's websites go non-functional. After investigation, it was found that one of its competitors hired some hackers to launch an email bombing attack, due to which the company's web server was down and unable to perform.
 
Emails are created with a forged sender address; often, the sender pretends to be someone known to the recipient. In spoofed emails, the sender's real identity is hidden, and he intends to get the recipient's financial details either by luring the recipient or by installing malware.
 
 
Figure 1.5: Spoofing
 
Anjali Gupta, who works as a call centre executive, receives an email that appears to be from her office. As per the email content, the company offered her cashback on the purchase of groceries from an online portal because her performance was outstanding during the lockdown period. She has worked very efficiently. A link was mentioned in the email, and Anjali was asked to provide some banking details. Anjali followed the email instructions, and fraudsters called her pretending to be her office teammate and asked to share bank OTP. Anjali shared the OTP, and Rs 25,000/- were deducted from her account instead of credit. After investigation, it was determined as a case of email Anjali did not crosscheck the email address properly and was trapped by the cyber criminals.

Preventive steps to protect your email ID
 
Some of the preventive steps, which can be taken to protect your email id are as follows:
  
Use a strong password. At least more than eight alphanumerical characters, for example,
 
Always scan the attachments .exe, and so on) before opening them.
 
Never click on links received in emails from strangers.
 
Ignore/delete spam emails.
 
Don't fall prey to gifts offered by the unknown in email.
 
Never share your real date of birth, birthplace, geographical location, and other private information on social media.
 
Implement two-way To activate it, add your phone number by accessing email account settings. Whenever you access your email from a new device, you will receive an SMS code for additional verification.
 
Verify your email ID's login history to make sure that only you had opened your email account.
 
Provide an alternate email address and phone so that the email service provider can contact you in case of suspicious activity.
 
Check your recently used devices to check your email and see if there is any suspicious activity.
 
Manage permissions for apps that are linked to your email account.
 
Never disclose your password to anyone.
 
Never save your password on any device.
 
Crosscheck the website authenticity; look for URLs such as spelling, padlock in the browser.
 
If your email is compromised, contact your email service provider.
 
Avoid similar passwords for multiple email IDs.
 
Avoid free, public, and unsecured Wi-Fi.

QR code/OTP/app-related common tricks used by fraudsters
 
Some of the common tricks used by the fraudsters are as follows:
  
Cybercriminals send QR codes via emails and WhatsApp to hack bank accounts.
 
Fraudulent NPCI/UPI/BHIM handles and portals.
 
Myriad Twitter handles masquerading as @NPCI_BHIM official helpline handle has mushroomed on the micro-blogging site. The fake accounts trick customers looking for help to reveal their account, wallet, or card details.
 
Example 1: Cybercriminals duped a garment store shopkeeper using a QR code and PIN
 
Amit Sharma, a garment store owner in Hyderabad, receives a call from a buyer who wanted to buy garments in bulk. Amit Sharma offered him a good discount on bulk purchases and provided his name, phone number, Google Pay, Paytm and UPI details, and so on for online money transfer. After getting the details, cybercriminals generated the QR code and sent it back to the shop owner via WhatsApp. He was asked to scan it and enter the PIN generated by the fraudster for receiving payment. By doing it, Amit consented to the request of the fraudsters. Amit repeated these steps as the cybercriminal convinced him, and Amit lost Rs. 2 lakh in the hope of getting the money back.
 
Example 2: Fraudsters lists their phone numbers online, pretending as an NGO
 
A Mumbai resident wanted to cremate her pet via NGO. She tried to find one such phone number on the internet. Cybercriminals took her debit card details by asking her to download a remote desktop software tool. Anydesk provides a complete view of the user's screen. They withdrew Rs. 30,000 from her bank account.

 
Preventive steps to protect your bank account/e-wallet
 
Some of the preventive measures which can be taken are as follows:
  
You need to scan the QR code only to make the payment, not to receive money.
 
QR code is like a normal web link; never scan if you don't know its origin.
 
Always use two-factor authentication
 
Avoid making payments using QR codes in unknown people's wallets.
 
Never click on links received via emails from unknown users. Fraudsters add an image containing the malicious QR code to the given link, and this fake URL link redirects the victim to the fraudster's malicious website.
 
Never share the OTP and ATM PIN with anyone.
 
Check your bank account statements regularly.
 
Never save debit and credit details on your devices.
 
Register for SMS and email alerts with your bank.
 
Look for verified-by-twitter blue ticks while interacting with National Payments Corporation of India bank, or payment wallet helplines.
 
While entering a PIN, use your discretion to shield the keypad so that your hand movements are not visible to others.
 
When at an ATM, make sure that no external devices are attached to the ATM, and no wires are hanging around.

 
Debit/credit card cybercrime-related common tricks used by fraudsters
 
Some of the common tricks used by fraudsters for debit/credit card-related cybercrime are as follows:
  
Renewal of debit and credit Fraudsters call innocent people on the pretext of renewing their credit or debit card to avoid getting the cards blocked and ask the victim's credit card number and OTP.
 
Updating KYC Fraudsters send emails or messages containing malicious links to the victim to update their KYC details. As the victim opens the link, the webpage asks for credit or debit card details along with OTP.
 
Reward points or cashback Fraudsters call people saying that they have won the cashback offer, and to claim the offer, they must provide the OTP.
 
SIM swap Cybercriminals manage to get a new SIM card issued against your registered mobile number to receive the OTP.
 
Example 1: A housewife lost Rs. 50,000 to cybercriminals
 
A 35-year-old housewife, Shikha Gupta, received an SMS on her mobile phone of a transaction using her debit card worth Rs. 50,000. Shikha was shocked because she never used her debit card for any shopping or online money transfer. Cybercriminals posed as her bank officials and tricked her into providing One Time Password on the pretext of redeeming recharge points worth Rs. The fraudster said that Rs. 5,000 would be deducted from her bank account if she does not redeem the available points. In the greed of getting Rs. 3,000 in free, Shikha shared the OTP with the fraudsters and lost Rs.
 
Example 2:
 
In Udaipur, an IAS officer lost Rs. 6 lakh when she clicked on a fraudulent link asking her to update her KYC. She was prompted to enter her account details, and the OTP received, following which she received messages from her bank notifying her of debits worth Rs 6

 
Preventive tips for protecting financial frauds
 
The following are some of the preventive tips for financial frauds:
  
Never share your OTP with anyone.
 
OTP is never required to receive the money in your bank account.
 
Pick the bank's customer care numbers directly from the bank's legitimate website. Avoid the numbers being displayed on Google and so on.
 
Never download the apps you don't need.
 
Check the authenticity of the app with the bank before you download it.
 
Check the authenticity of the website before paying any fee.
 
Use separate email ID for matrimonial websites.
 
Never share personal and sensitive information with the people you meet through matrimonial websites.
 
Crosscheck NRI profiles carefully.

Introduction

Structure
 
In this chapter, we will go through the to-do list for working professionals, consultants, and small business houses.

 
Objective
 
Cyber essentials or following cyber hygiene will help working IT, non-IT professionals, NGOs, and small business houses protect their important data by taking proper preventive measures.

 
Regular backup of your data
 
Some data may be very important and crucial such as customer's personal information and their payment details, including their account number, credit/debit card details, and so on. If you have a habit of taking regular data backup, there are almost zero chances of blackmailing by cybercriminals. Most people/organizations do not have a proper backup; therefore, they are forced to pay the ransomware.
 
Identify the important data and take regular backup in a separate external storage media such as external hard disks. You may keep one copy of the backup on a cloud server.

Instruct your office staff to avoid the installation of unnecessary apps from play stores.
