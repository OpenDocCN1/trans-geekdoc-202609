# 78. Hardware protection mechanisms and expected life controls include:

+   - **SLAs:** Ensure that vendors will provide the necessary maintenance and uptime

+   - **MTBF:** Used to calculate the expected lifetime of a device

+   - **MTTR:** Used to estimate the amount of time between repairs

# 79. Fire types and suppression methods include:

+   - **Class A:** Paper or wood; suppressed with water or soda acid

+   - **Class B:** Gasoline or oil fires; suppressed using CO2, soda acid, or halon

+   - **Class C:** Electronic or computer fires; suppressed using CO2 FM200, or halon

+   - **Class D:** Fires caused by combustible metals; suppressed by applying dry powder or using special techniques

80. Halon, an effective fire suppressant, is a banned substance because it depletes ozone.

+   - **Halon 1211:** Found in portable extinguishers and stored as a liquid

+   - **Halon 1301:** Used in fixed flooding systems and stored as a gaseous agent

81. Halon fire-suppression systems can be left in place, but there are strict regulations on reporting discharges. EPA-approved replacements include FM-200, CEA-410, NAF-S-III, and FE-13. FE-13 is one of the newest and safest, at up to 30% saturation tolerable.

82. Water sprinklers are an effective means of extinguishing Class A fires. Four variations are available:

+   - **Dry pipe:** Maintains no standing water. It reduces the risk of accidental flooding and provides some time to cover or turn off electrical equipment.

+   - **Wet pipe:** Widely used and ready for activation. This system is charged and full of water.

+   - **Preaction:** A combination system. Pipes are initially dry and filled
