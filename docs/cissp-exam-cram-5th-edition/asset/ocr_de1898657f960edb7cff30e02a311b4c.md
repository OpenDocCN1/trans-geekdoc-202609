Others require user activation, such as requiring the user to input a key code. One example of a deployed smart card is the Common Access Card (CAC) used by the U.S. Department of Defense. CACs are considered to be dual- factor (or multifactor), and they provide strong authentication; they are used with a public key infrastructure (PKI).

Some organizations provide card users with two key codes. One of the codes is used for normal access, and the second is used as a *silent hostage alarm* or *duress alarm*. The silent hostage alarm code allows an employee to gain access and also silently alerts the authorities of a hostage situation.

**Caution** High-security facilities have a history of mandating that employees make sure their badges are not visible after leaving the workplace to go home or out to lunch. This is an effective control for any organization that uses badges to reduce social engineering or targeting of specific vehicles, briefcases, laptops, tablets, smartphones, and so on.

# # RFID Tags

RFID tags are becoming popular physical access controls. An RFID tag is an extremely small electronic device composed of a microchip and an antenna. These devices transmit small amounts of information. RFID tags can be designed in different ways:
