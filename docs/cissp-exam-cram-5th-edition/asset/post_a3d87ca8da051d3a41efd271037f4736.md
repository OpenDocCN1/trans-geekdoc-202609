58. Access control categories:
- **Technical:** Examples include encryption, authentication, network segmentation, and antivirus.
- **Physical:** Examples include locks, fences, guards, lights, video, and physical IDs.
- **Administrative:** Examples include policies, procedures, training, and pre-employment checks.
- **Controls:** Controls can be preventive, detective, deterrent, corrective, compensating, or recovery.

59. Access control models are established to control how subjects can access data and the user’s level of authorization. There are four primary models:
- **DAC:** The user controls who has access to the system; uses ACLs
- **MAC:** Looks to the system to determine access; typically used by organizations that handle highly sensitive data and is based on labels
- **RBAC:** Considered nondiscretionary; places users into groups and implicitly assigns access; used by companies with high turnover
- **ABAC:** More flexible; can make use of XACML; uses rights to access a resource that is passed on policies that combine attributes

60. Intrusion detection can be signature, statistical, or anomaly based and is host or network based.

61. Access management facility controls include:
- **Lighting:** Used to discourage crime and protect employees. The NIST standard states that an area should be illuminated at 2 feet wide by 8 feet high.
- **Fencing:** A 3- to 4-foot fence is a deterrent; a 6- to 7-foot fence is hard to climb; an 8-foot fence with three strands of barbed wire acts as a serious deterrent.

Perimeter controls include gates, guards, dogs, CCTV, turnstiles, mantraps, and alarms.

62. Lock types:
- Cipher locks: Programmable
- Preset locks: Warded or pin and tumbler
- Device locks: Used to prevent the theft of equipment

## Security Assessment and Testing

63. Assessment and audit perspectives:
- **Internal:** Has the advantage that insiders know the environment; can be conducted before a required third-party audit
- **External:** Has the advantage of no bias; can be used for compliance audits
- **Third-party:** Has the advantage that it helps provide an external perspective; may be required, depending on regulation

64. Penetration testing is the process of evaluating an organization’s security measures. Testing can be performed in a number of ways, including internal, external, whitebox, and blackbox testing. Penetration testing not only tests vulnerabilities but attempts to exploit them.

65. Log review is an important task and typically makes use of a SIEM.

66. Synthetic transactions are actions run against a monitored system to see how it responds.
- **Real-user monitoring:** Monitors actual users
- **Synthetic performance monitoring:** Uses scripts that mimic a typical user; also called proactive monitoring

67. Two types of fuzz testing are generation-based and mutation-based testing.

68. Sensitive information is not included in a SOC 3 report as this type of report is available to the public.

69. Disaster recovery is a subset of business continuity activities.

## Security Operations

70. Operational security can be enhanced by implementing good employee controls, such as new hire orientation, background checks, separation of duties, job rotation, least privilege, and mandatory vacations.

71. Investigation types include administrative, criminal, civil, and regulatory.

72. Clipping levels are the thresholds implemented for certain types of errors or mistakes that are allowed without alarm.

73. A duress code is used when an employee is being threatened. The duress code should be changed regularly and should be easy to remember.

74. The three main types of digital forensics are network forensics, software forensics, and hard drive (or computer) forensics.

75. The chain of custody examines who collected, transported, stored, controlled, and accessed evidence.

76. Common power anomalies:
- **Blackout:** Prolonged loss of power
- **Brownout:** Power degradation that is low and less than normal
- **Sag:** Momentary low voltage
- **Fault:** Momentary loss of power
- **Spike:** Momentary high voltage
- **Surge:** Prolonged high voltage
- **Noise:** Interference superimposed onto the power line
- **Transient:** Noise disturbances of a short duration
- **Inrush:** Initial surge of power at startup

77. Facility management requires review of the facility:
- **Proper construction and design:** Should give attention to walls, doors, ceilings, windows, flooring, HVAC, and fire detection and suppression. Must know where cut-off valves and switches are located.
- **Computing area:** Should be neither on the top floor nor in the basement but should be near the core of the building and offer protection on all six sides.
- **HVAC:** Should be separate for the data center and have positive pressurization to keep contaminants and smoke out of the facility.

78. Hardware protection mechanisms and expected life controls include:
- **SLAs:** Ensure that vendors will provide the necessary maintenance and uptime
- **MTBF:** Used to calculate the expected lifetime of a device
- **MTTR:** Used to estimate the amount of time between repairs

79. Fire types and suppression methods include:
- **Class A:** Paper or wood; suppressed with water or soda acid
- **Class B:** Gasoline or oil fires; suppressed using CO2, soda acid, or halon
- **Class C:** Electronic or computer fires; suppressed using CO2, FM-200, or halon
- **Class D:** Fires caused by combustible metals; suppressed by applying dry powder or using special techniques

80. Halon, an effective fire suppressant, is a banned substance because it depletes ozone.
- **Halon 1211:** Found in portable extinguishers and stored as a liquid
- **Halon 1301:** Used in fixed flooding systems and stored as a gaseous agent

81. Halon fire-suppression systems can be left in place, but there are strict regulations on reporting discharges. EPA-approved replacements include FM-200, CEA-410, NAF-S-III, and FE-13. FE-13 is one of the newest and safest, at up to 30% saturation tolerable.

82. Water sprinklers are an effective means of extinguishing Class A fires. Four variations are available:
- **Dry pipe:** Maintains no standing water. It reduces the risk of accidental flooding and provides some time to cover or turn off electrical equipment.
- **Wet pipe:** Widely used and ready for activation. This system is charged and full of water.
- **Preaction:** A combination system. Pipes are initially dry and filled with compressed air.
- **Deluge:** Not suitable for data centers. Pipes hold a large volume of water and can cover a large area quickly.

83. No demonstrated recovery exists until the BCP has been tested.

84. Backup types:
- Incremental backup: Copies only files that have changed since the last backup
- Differential backup: Copies any files that have changed since the last full backup
- Full backup: Copies everything

85. BCP testing methods:
- Checklist: Copies of the plan are sent to different department managers and business unit managers for review.
- Tabletop: Members of the emergency management team and business unit managers meet in conference to discuss the plan.
- Walkthrough: A simulation of a real event uses only what would be available in a disaster.
- Functional: Operations of the new and old site can be run in parallel.
- Full interruption: A complete test of the BCP plan is performed.

86. Subscription services:
- Cold site: An empty room with only rudimentary electrical, power, and computing capability
- Warm site: Partially configured
- Hot site: Ready to go; an expensive option
