## EXAM CRAM

The CISSP® Cram Sheet

This Cram Sheet contains the distilled, key facts about the exam. Review this information as the last thing you do before you enter the testing center, paying special attention to those areas where you feel that you need the most review.

### Asset Security

- 1. Confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) are the basic building blocks of any good security program and are commonly referred to collectively as the CIA triad.
- 2. Classification of information:
  - **Public:** Built on a four-level model: confidential, private, sensitive, and public
  - **Military:** Built on five-layer model: top secret, secret, confidential, sensitive but unclassified, and unclassified
- 3. Scoping and tailoring:
  - **Scoping:** Used to determine which portions of a standard will be employed by an organization
  - **Tailoring:** Used to customize a standard for an organization
- 4. Protection of data:
  - **Data at rest:** Stored data that resides on a disk or in a file
  - **Data in motion:** Data that is being transferred across a network
- 5. Data classification policy should specify:
  - Who can access the data
  - How the data should be secured
  - The amount of time the data should be stored (retention)
  - How to dispose of the data securely

### Security and Risk Management

6. The (ISC)2 Code of Ethics states that security professionals will:
  - Protect society, the commonwealth, and the infrastructure
  - Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally
  - Provide diligent and competent service to principles
  - Advance and protect the profession

7. Intellectual property types:
  - **Copyright:** Protects the artistic expression of an idea; the length of protection varies but generally extends for at least 50 years after the author’s death
  - **Patent:** Generally protected for about 20 years; should be registered
  - **Trademark:** Registered and can be renewed indefinitely as long as the organization is in business
  - **Trade secret:** Not registered; should be kept secret

8. Three goals of risk management:
  - Identify risks
  - Quantify the impact of potential threats
  - Find an economic balance between the impact of a risk and the cost of a countermeasure

9. Threat versus vulnerability:
  - **Threat:** A natural or human-caused event that could have a negative impact on the organization
  - **Vulnerability:** A flaw, loophole, oversight, or error that makes the organization susceptible to attack or damage

10. Two approaches to dealing with risk:
  - **Quantitative analysis:** Assigns real numbers or dollar amounts to the costs of countermeasures and the amount of damage that can occur. Pure quantitative risk analysis is not possible.
  - **Qualitative analysis:** Looks at different scenarios of risk possibilities and ranks the seriousness of the threats and the sensitivity of the assets.

11. Formulas used for quantitative analysis include:
  - EF (exposure factor) = Percentage of an asset loss caused by an identified threat
  - SLE (single loss expectancy) = Asset value x Exposure factor
  - ARO (annualized rate of occurrence) = Estimated frequency a threat will occur within a year
  - ALE (annualized loss expectancy) = Single loss expectancy x Annualized rate of occurrence

12. Threat modeling techniques:
  - **STRIDE:** Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege
  - **PASTA:** Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis
  - **DREAD:** Disaster, Reproducibility, Exploitability, Affected Users, and Discoverability

13. Risk is dealt with in the following ways (which can be combined):
  - **Risk reduction:** Implementing a countermeasure to alter or reduce the risk
  - **Risk transference:** Purchasing insurance to transfer a portion or the entire potential cost of a loss to a third party
  - **Risk acceptance:** Accepting the potential cost and loss of a risk

14. Security policies can be regulatory, advisory, or informative.
15. Senior management is ultimately responsible for the security of the organization.
16. Types of security documents:
  - **Policies:** General statements produced by senior management
  - **Standards:** Tactical documents that are more specific than policies
  - **Guidelines:** Point to statements in policies or procedures by which to determine a course of action
  - **Procedures:** The lowest level in a policy that provides step-by-step instructions to achieve a certain task

### Security Engineering and Architecture

17. The trusted computing base (TCB) is a combination of protection mechanisms, including hardware, software, and firmware, to maintain security within a computer system.
18. The reference monitor is an access control concept referring to an abstract machine that mediates all accesses to objects by subjects.
19. The security kernel implements the reference monitor concept. The reference monitor concept has the following properties:
  - Provides isolation
  - Is invoked for every access attempt
  - Is impossible to circumvent and is not foolproof
  - Is complete, verified, and tested

20. Resource isolation is a process of segmentation so that memory is separated physically, not just logically.
21. Rings of protection are used to isolate processes:
  - Layer 0: OS
  - Layer 1: Remaining parts of the OS
  - Layer 2: Utilities and I/O
  - Layer 3: Applications and programs
  Lower numbers have higher levels of privileges.

22. A security model defines the structure by which data structures and systems are designed to enforce security policy. Well-known security models include:
  - **Bell-LaPadula:** Enforces confidentiality and uses rules: the simple security rule (no read up) and the * property (no write down).
