# 9.6 ADVERSE IMPACTS OF AI ON SUSTAINABILITY

The preceding sections deal with how AI is making inroads into variety of sectors such as mining; agriculture; construction; manufacturing; retail; media, gaming and entertainment; financial services; and education. AI is able to increase productivity, safety of workers, accuracy and timeliness of process completion; reduce inefficiencies; and also maintain consistency. However, not all of these benefits come without strings attached.

AI as a technology is expensive and not readily available or affordable in all parts of the world. Just as larger as the internet created a digital divide, splitting the world into people who had access to a larger number of resources and those who did not, AI could become another differentiator that gives enormous computing potential and data-driven insights with the means to access versus those who do not (Vinuesa et al., 2020). While the wealthy nations in the world are investing in AI heavily and will reap the benefits, the poorer nations will be struggling, and the gap between the two groups will only widen further.

There will be groups with vested interests who will use AI to further their owns aims. It is possible to study, analyze their weaknesses and influence or exploit them for the benefit of entities like governments and business organizations. People may not be aware that their movements and activities are being monitored, or that they are being influenced to make choices, raising the ethical dilemma of how intrusive AI can be. Further, machine learning algorithms will try to learn from existing data to come up with patterns and trends that it can apply to future data. The presence of biases and prejudices—which will appear in past data—could well be projected on future data, while the opinions of users may have changed in the meantime. For AI algorithms to understand the principles of diversity, social inclusion, affirmative action, gender equity, wage parity and socially important factors will be a challenge.
