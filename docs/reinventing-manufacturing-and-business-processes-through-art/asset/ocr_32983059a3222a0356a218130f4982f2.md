AI-based surveillance systems have been set up to identify animal or human intrusions or breaches. This form of vigilance, which can further be triggered to deliver remedial measures, is necessary to protect crops from predators and threat. Through an elaborate set of sensors placed in the ground and also through the drones that perform aerial surveillance, it is now possible to capture data about factors like soil moisture, mineral content, pH levels, and so on, in understanding growth patterns and forecasting crop output yields. In fact, it has now become possible to determine to a large extent how much yield could be obtained from a piece of land for a particular crop based on current soil conditions and weather conditions (Forbes, 2021). Solar pumps that have a low ecological footprint can be prompted to work based knowledge of humidity and moisture levels (Mungerval & Mehta, 2019) in less irrigation water consumption.

In a general, substantial percentage of crop is destroyed by pests and diseases every year. Having prior knowledge about pest and disease movement can farmers reduce crop losses and get access to expert advice. AI is being used to detect such adverse events through the use of images and to allow farmers of lively attacks. Smart pest monitoring systems have been designed that identify their types and location in the field, and then send out alerts to farmers with this information. Plantix is another app that uses data provided by the International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics (ICRISAT), which has helped around 75,000 farmers in India gain access to precision about pests and diseases (ICRISAT, 2020).

Climate is yet another factor that has a major impact on agriculture. With global warming, many farmers have faced issues with increased unpredictability in weather, unexpected changes in climate patterns, and heavy downflows and other climatic deviations. By analyzing past trends in rainfall, temperature, wind, air pressure and humidity, and by monitoring the current patterns through use of sensors, farmers will have access to more accurate and timely information which can help them determine the appropriate times for planting, harvesting, and so. Farmers can also take advantage of such conditions such as wind direction and intensity for fertilizer application.

By using data generated sensors and global positioning systems, it is possible to determine which chemicals, fertilizers and herbicides, and also how much water, needs to be provided to which parts of the farm. This technology, known as rate application, is an integral part of precision agriculture and provides invaluable information to the farmer, besides helping him save on costs of input (Igor, 2018).

Agrobots use image processing to differentiate between crops and weeds, which will spray weeds selectively. Selective spraying is a good farming practice that will help bring resistance to weeds and herbicides in the future. Harvesting is also becoming increasingly expensive, with many people not preferring to work in this sector. In order to overcome this shortcoming, agrobots have been designed that can pick fruits and vegetables, overcoming the restrictions of fatigue, cost and inexpensive.
