## 1.2.3 DETERMINANTS OF GHRM ADOPTION

With the increasing pressures of customers, stakeholders and orientation towards the environment, organizations have to look forward towards reducing production waste and promoting environmental sustainability by green initiatives through HRM practices (Guerci et al., 2016; Mohd-Yussof, 2016). Employees' intellectual capital also significantly affects HRM practices (Kong & Thompson, 2009). An empirical study was conducted on 112 Malaysian manufacturing firms on the basis of intellectual-based value theory which revealed that green human capital and relational capital significantly influence the organization's incentive to implement green HRM practices (Yong et al., 2019). Green human capital of employees' knowledge, skills, capabilities, experience, attitudes, wisdom, creativity and commitment to green environment or green innovation (Chen, 2008; Li & Chang, 2010; Chahal & Bakshi, 2014). Organizations with such human capital find it easy to implement green HRM practices. Similarly, green relational capital is a combination of an organization's interactive relationship with customers, suppliers, network members and partners about corporate environmental management and green innovation, which enables it to create fortunes and obtain competitive advantage (Chen, 2008). As customers, suppliers, employees and partners are the main stakeholders of the organization, their impact on implementing HRM services is relatively greater.

Moreover, green HRM practices can be adopted and implemented in the organizations. Top management holds the decision to initiate any change in the organization, and they must have orientation toward green environment which helps them to support green HRM practices (Obraid et al., 2020; Sawang & Kivitas, 2014; Teixeira et al., 2012). With effective leadership and employee commitment, top management changes the organizational culture by adopting green HRM practices such as green recruitment and selection, green job descriptions, green training and development, performance appraisal system, green safety and health provision, green employee and labor relations (Ahmad, 2015; Chaudhary, 2019; Gholami et al., 2016; Guerci et al., 2016; Jabbour, 2013; Jabbour et al., 2010; Jackson et al., 2021; O’Donohue & Turga, 2016; Renwick et al., 2018; Rana & Sharma, 2019; Srivastava et al., 2020; Tang et al., 2017; Zibarbar & Coon, 2015). Green HRM practices helps organizations attain attitudes toward adopting green behaviors and developing a green workforce (Aboramadan, 2020; Cabral & Dhar, 2019; Cabral & Dhar, 2020; Chaudhary, 2019; Dumont et al., 2017; Fahehimni et al., 2020; Islam et al., 2020; Jackson & Seo, 2010; Kim et al., 2019; Lu, 2017; Lu, 2018; Misha, 2017; Aboramadan, 2020; Mukherjee & Chandra, 2018; Ojo et al., 2020; Renwick et al., 2013; Rubel, 2021).

Green recruitment and selection organizations attract more talented employees to their policies as most of Generation Y looks for employees with HRM practices (Chaudhary, 2019; Ahmed, 2015). European companies like Siemens, Bayer, Manesmann and Rover Group use environmental activities as part of their job practices (Wehrmeyer, 1996). A systematic review was carried out over twenty-two articles and identified the recommendations of recruitment and selection, mediators and moderators, to establish a relationship between applicant outcomes and corporate sustainability (Paillé, 2019). In order to transform the employees into a green workforce, organizations are integrating environmental performance with management systems to prevent any damage to the environment (Epstein & Roy, 1997). Companies install environmental standards and green information systems to use data on employees’ organizational performance (209). They also initiate green training and educate employees about managing environment, minimizing waste, conserving energy and providing them with opportunities to preserve the environment (Zoogah, 2011). Previous research studies have revealed that is important for implementing HRM policies of green employee behavior as Aboramadan, 2020; Islam et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2020; Ojo et al., 2020; Rubel et al., 2021).

## Reinventing Processes Through AI

Figure 1.2 shows the various factors to which organizations have to adopt HRM practices. In a green HR survey, it revealed that more than 54% of companies have infused green management practices in their organizational activities, 74% used digital innovation to conduct meetings without traveling, 76% of organizations paperless documentation, 60% have adopted employee fitness and wellness programs, and 80% used artificial intelligence to perform their activities (Aggarwal & Sharma, 2015). With the smart and artificially intelligent software, adopt and implementation of HRM services becomes easy for organizations (Garg et al., 2018). For instance, PepsiCo has implemented AI to interview and select candidates for their vacant positions. The software automatically scans CVs (curriculum vitae) from various job portals and selects the candidates having matching profiles, which minimizes the job of interviewers to travel across countries to interview the candidates. Infosys implanted new ERP system and smart meters to reduce their energy consumption by 85%. Companies like Lufthansa Group, Hyatt Group of Hotels, Nokia, Gensol Consultants Private Limited and id8 Media Solutions have also adopted AI technologies to implement GHRM practices for adopting green and healthy environment. ## 1.3 ROLE OF AI IN ADOPTING GHRM PRACTICES

Organizations—whether in developing or developed economies—have already adopted modern technologies to improve their operations and processes. For instance, using Big Data analytics to analyze the trends and patterns of recruitment, sales and production has now become common for organizations, but implementing AI for adopting GHRM practices is still in its nascent stage. With its smart and solution oriented technologies, AI has become the key to attain sustainable competitive advantage; however, it is still limited to few companies. One reason for this reluctance is the inability of talent to manage such applications and another the fear of losing jobs as machines replace human effort. On contrary, AI improves work efficiency and reduces human effort which can be used for attaining green practices. Recently, an application has been developed by an Indian IT company named “AskDester” which manages 22,000 employees by handling HR functions like solving employees' queries about leave and company policies (Garg et al., 2018). This also provides information about upcoming job opportunities and openings. AI is helpful to perform various HR functions. Figure 1.3 explains the role of AI in GHRM practices.

AI applications select the CVs of job seekers and selects the best ones matching the requirement of the organization.
