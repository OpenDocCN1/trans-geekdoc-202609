Signal quality is only shown in the mobile application while the device is placed by a user on their forehead and the reference electrode is placed in its right position. If the user is not wearing the headset, signal quality will not be detected. The headset electrodes have proper contact with skin; if there is no contact or partial contact between the sensor and the brain dry sensor, the application will show poor signal quality, and if the brain dry sensor has proper contact with the forehead, it will show good signal quality. To prevent unwanted action while signal quality is not good, an added safety precaution is to send a stop command. When the signal quality becomes good, the then again starts attending for any incoming data block (Nielsen et al. 2006).

To implement wheelchair operation using the BCI control signal, the control signal is sent using Bluetooth attached to the headset and the signal has to be received in the mobile application; the control signal is passed to another Bluetooth module attached to the wheelchair.

When a strength value received in the wheelchair is above the threshold value, the MindWave application executes in cycling mode through values of forward, reverse, left and right These directions each have a time span of ten seconds known as command mode, and a two-second intermission between each mode for changing the direction value. We have to provide two consecutive blasts, and whatever direction is shown in the cycle at the moment of the double event received will become the chosen direction. To stop force, the cycling time elapsed between two blink events must be less than 400 milliseconds to be recognized by the brain sensor device.

# 3.3.2.2 Hardware Developments

BCI-controlled wheelchair development needs a BCI headset, an Android mobile device and a wheelchair with Arduino microcontroller, Bluetooth and motor driver shown in Figure 3.6. In first module, the BCI headset consists of EEG electrode and a transmitting Bluetooth device; in second module, an Android application and Bluetooth device; and in third module, a wheelchair equipped with a microcontroller, motor driver and Bluetooth. The mobile application connects with headset Bluetooth and wheelchair module simultaneously. First, the headset worn around the user's head will receive the raw brain data and wirelessly transmitting it through its own built-in Bluetooth to the Android application. The Android application acts as a conduit between the MindWaveMobile application and the Arduino microcontroller placed in the wheelchair.

Brain wave data from the MindWaveMobile is not directly transmitted to the wheelchair microcontroller; the Android application filters the desired pattern and ensures that only relevant information is sent to the wheelchair microcontroller. The filtration or feature selection process must be performed in the mobile application, and it reduces overhead from headset and microcontroller.
