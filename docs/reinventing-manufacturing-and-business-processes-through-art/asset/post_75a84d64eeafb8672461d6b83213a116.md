AI-based surveillance systems have been set up to identify animal or human intrusions or breaches. This form of vigilance, which can further be triggered to deliver remedial measures, is necessary to protect crops from predators and threat. Through an elaborate set of sensors placed in the ground and also through the drones that perform aerial surveillance, it is now possible to capture data about factors like soil moisture, mineral content, pH levels, and so on, in understanding growth patterns and forecasting crop output yields. In fact, it has now become possible to determine to a large extent how much yield could be obtained from a piece of land for a particular crop based on current soil conditions and weather conditions (Forbes, 2021). Solar pumps that have a low ecological footprint can be prompted to work based on humidity and moisture levels (Mung & Mehta, 2019) to reduce water consumption.

In general, substantial percentage of crop is destroyed by pests and diseases every year. Having prior knowledge about pest and disease movement can reduce crop losses and get access to expert advice. AI is being used to detect such adverse events through the use of images and allow farmers of lively attacks. Smart monitoring systems have been designed that identify their types and location in the field, and then send out alerts to farmers. Plantix is another app that uses data provided by the International Crops Research Institute for Semi-Arid Tropics (ICRISAT), which has helped around 5,000 farmers in India gain access about pests and diseases (ICRISAT, 2020).

Climate is another factor that has a major impact on agriculture. With global warming, many farmers have faced issues with increased unpredictability in weather, unexpected changes in climate patterns, and heavy deviations. By analyzing past trends in rainfall, temperature, air pressure and humidity, and by monitoring current patterns through use of sensors, farmers will have access to more accurate and timely information which can help them determine the appropriate times for planting. Farmers can also take advantage of such conditions as wind direction and intensity for fertilizer application. Using data generated sensors and global positioning systems, it is possible to determine which chemicals, fertilizers and also how much water needs to be provided to precision agriculture and provides invaluable information to the farmer besides helping him save on costs (Igor, 2018).

This technology, known as rate application, is an integral part of precision agriculture. Agrobots use image processing to differentiate between crops and weeds selectively. Selective spraying is a good farming practice that will bring resistance to weeds in the future. Harvesting is also becoming increasingly expensive with many people not preferring to work. In order to overcome this shortcoming, agrobots have been designed that can pick fruits and vegetables overcoming cost and fatigue. Similarly, it is now possible to run tractors and other heavy machinery on the fields by operating them remotely, ensuring that work goes on without hindrance and reliance on external labor. Finally, during picking and sorting process, imaging can be used to analyze size and shape of crop output to help in categorizing it into different grades which will fetch different rates at the market. AI is known to bring several advantages for farmers owners of setting up and maintaining equipment, making the right inferences from wealth of data that will be generated and scalability.

## 9.5.3 TRANSFORMING CONSTRUCTION

Among the challenges faced by the construction sector today are shortage of skilled labor, increasing cost of materials, safety-related issues and decreasing profits arising from lower productivity (Construction Placement, 2019). Additionally, there are other problems such as delayed shipment, weather deviations wreaking planned schedules and changing awareness about energy consumption or environmental impact that have forced the construction sector to step back and look at what can be done to better serve customers.

Every construction activity is exercise in project management. By assimilating AI in project management function, it is possible to regulate mundane tasks, freeing up the manager’s time for bigger strategic decisions. Given the ability of AI to map competencies and skills, it can be used to identify the most suitable candidate for different jobs on the basis of skill sets and availability. With the use of drones and other surveillance equipment, it is possible for AI to anticipate issues and perform geospatial analyses that can help construction workers stay on schedule (Invonto, 2018).

Generative design is the term given to a repetitive process whereby an AI program produces certain designs that are designed to fit the constraints given by the customer. Each of these designs is a possible solution for the construction company. They can pick any of these designs or further refine them by fine tuning the constraints to produce even more customer-friendly solutions. Eventually, this type of technology might rethink architects and other designs redundant.

Green or sustainable buildings are terms that are increasingly becoming popular in the construction sector. Customers today are more environmentally sensitive than ever, and they often demand that building construction resource be economical and environmentally accountable. The idea is not just to be environmentally responsible during the design and construction phase, but also during the maintenance, renovation and demolition stages. In order to make it easy for customers to know about the buildings, several rating agencies have come up with the Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED), the BREEAM (Building Research Establishment Method), EDGE (Excellence in Design for Greater Efficiency) and Global Sustainability Assessment System (GSAS). The primary aim of all these certifications and rating agencies is to promote environmental stewardship by ensuring economic utilization of resources such as water and energy, to improve the health and safety of occupants and to decrease waste and pollution.

Currently, AI is not extensively used in construction as will be in the future. There is a deficit of “Big Data” and data analysts to train algorithms on different aspects of construction. However, smart construction technologies are slowly but surely making inroads into the sector as the potential for savings, as well as for better designed outputs, increases radically with the use of such technologies.

## 9.5.4 REVOLUTIONIZING MANUFACTURING

The last couple of decades have witnessed a sea change in manufacturing processes that have helped businesses radically change how they operate. With globalization, the focus has moved toward cost-effective production without compromising on quality, which can have in fending off volatility. There is also an increased emphasis on having shorter and shorter production times to enhance competitiveness. With more regulatory restrictions and increased monitoring through inspections, and related litigation costs, businesses are looking to improve quality continuously.

Newer advances in vehicle technologies have helped to semi-automate machines such as bulldozers which are now increasingly used to execute monotonous and banal jobs such as bricklaying and pouring concrete. Autonomous machines and bots are also used to produce partial off-site which will later be incorporated into the main frame, further freeing up workers for other jobs. AI in form of wearable sensors can monitor different conditions and contribute to increasing worker safety at the site.
