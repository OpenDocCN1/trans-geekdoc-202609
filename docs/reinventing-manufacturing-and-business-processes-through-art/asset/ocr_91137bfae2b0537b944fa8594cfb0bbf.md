RockMass Technologies, Ionic Engineering, Thorough Tec, Shift Inc. and Wapware are some of the organizations that have implemented AI technologies in their day-to-day mining operations. Freeport-McRan is a U.S.-based copper mining organization that has always relied on extensive data capturing and analysis. With the use of sensors and other equipment on many types of stationary and machinery, real-time data was recorded over time. Manual analysis of the data would have been time consuming, expensive and quite unlikely to detect trends that may have been entrenched. This is when the organization decided to use the help of McKinsey data analysis to suggest changes that would improve productivity and profitability in the context of falling copper prices in the market. Going by the recommendations suggested by data modeling algorithms, the organization successfully increased mineral production to rates never before achieved with agile operating principles, the organization was able to utilize machine learning outputs to create functional solutions swiftly and efficiently (McKinsey & Company, 2021).

In every stage of mining, be it prospecting, reaching or breaking or smelting the ore, AI is helping organizations to automate processes. By connecting data retrieved from several sources such as satellite images and aerial pictures, machine learning and cognitive computing is used to design optimized solutions and techniques to increase reliability, accuracy and safety of operations.

Even though agriculture is one of the oldest lines of work, changes in the way it is practiced ever gradually for the first few millennia. With a burgeoning population that is expected to touch nine billion by the year 2050, there is high pressure on the sector to improve productivity and satisfy demand for food as well as industrial raw materials, while using fewer inputs and resources.

As a sector, agriculture is plagued by uncertainties on many fronts. Farmers have to tackle a multitude of variables such as sunlight, heat and humidity; soil moisture, nutrient content and pH; rainfall levels; wind speed and direction; bird and insect populations and behavior; market rates and subsidies; increasing consumption patterns of output; cost of inputs like fertilizers, insecticides, weeds; and so on.
