Now the commonly used normalization method is group normalization (GN) algorithm, which can fully pull batch data back to the mean value of 0 and the variance of 1, so as to not only stabilize, but also accelerate the training of the network. Its disadvantage is that it has poor effect for small batch size; performance degradation and increase the rate of error. Group normalization is proposed in [9], which groups the channels and calculates the mean and variance within each group. The calculation of GN is not related to the batch size, and its accuracy is stable under various batch sizes, and the error is reduced compared with BN.

# A. Data Augmentation

In training of neural network, we increase the depth and breadth, so as to enhance the ability of neural network and facilitate the fitting of distribution of data. At the same time, we encounter a problem, that with the increasing of neural network parameters the model will overfit, resulting in overfitting, which is highly fitting the data, but the accuracy is very low for the test, and the generalization is especially when the data set is small!

In the process of experiment, we use the way of data augmentation to prevent the overfitting problem. Data augmentation includes methods like clipping, color jitter, horizontal and vertical flipping of images, which increases the amount of data. Convolution neural network is invariant to the displacement, view angle, size and illumination of objects, that is say, data augmentation does not affect the training of neural network, but will enhance the generalization ability of model and improve the accuracy rate[6]. Figure 2 shows an example of data augmentation.

When we use data augmentation, we usually use formula:

```
$$\hat{x}_i = \frac{x_i - \mu_i}{\sigma_i}$
```

Where x is a point in specific position in the feature map of a layer; i is the coordinates of the four dimensions of N, C, H and W; C is the channel axis, H and W are the height and width axes.

In the formula, $\mu$ is the mean and $\sigma$ is the standard deviation. Their calculation is shown in formula 2, which is a small constant value:

```
$$\mu_i = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j \in G_i} x_j, \\sigma_i = \sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j \in G_i} (x_j - \mu_i)^2 + \epsilon}$
```

And in formula 2, the G is the area of point set to calculate the mean value after fitting the k condition, and m is the size of this set. Therefore we focus on the definition of k condition in group normalization, as as formula 3:

```
$S_i = \{k | k \in L_i, [\frac{c}{G}] = [\frac{c}{G}]$
```

C is the number of channels in each group; $\frac{c}{G}$ means that i and k are in the same group of channels, that is to say, group normalization means grouping in direction, normalizing each group and calculating the mean value.
