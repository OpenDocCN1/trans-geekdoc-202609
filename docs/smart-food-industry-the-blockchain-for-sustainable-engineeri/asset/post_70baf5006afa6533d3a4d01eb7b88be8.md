# 3. Enablers of Food Supply Chain for Carbon Footprint Reduction

From a resource-based perspective, the practices associated with the food chain involve causality ambiguously and socially complex resources—which include every material consumption, solid, liquid, and gaseous wasted to an ecosystem's internal and external processes, interconnected between various members of the supply chain, partners and consumers (Vargas et al. 2018). Therefore, companies need to develop strong coordination and collaboration among different stakeholders to achieve supply chain sustainability (Luthra et al. 2020).

Furthermore, facilitators are proposed in order to facilitate the adoption of sustainable supply chain management practices (Vargas et al. 2018). Similarly, food supply management is also applying these enablers. However, beyond the sustainable bias, this also needs to deals with ensures food quality and safety. Therefore, selection criteria storing facilities, routes, and means of transport, in addition to load and operation must be used efficiently, that they do not affect the fact (Sharma et al. 2020).

To this end, several studies were carried out, and researchers have investigating the main factors to increase sustainability in operations. Among them, criteria such as logistics, traceability, data sharing systems, and cyber networks, inventory management, and process integration are the main key points in improving environmental burdens (Parshad et al. 2020).

So far, the progress of applying these is at a slow pace. However, some promising results can already be seen. Proof of this is the new paths taken by the multinational company manufacturing food packaging, Tetra Pak. Recently, they have started to adapt the Industry 4.0 as an important alternative to reduce productivity and costs in the food and beverage industry, without increasing extraction of natural resources. Through Industry 4.0 criteria such as the optimization of in- and out-of-plant logistics, real-time operation control throughout the plant, in addition to the accessible sharing of key information can be consulted by technical staff anywhere, without displacement charges (whether environmental, social, or financial). Consequently, with the help of the facilitators, Tetra Pak was able to reduce its carbon footprint around 12 million kg CO2 by mid-2019. The future prospects are also promising, and they have committed to reducing it by up to 46% of its emissions by 2030, compared to the base year 2019 (Tetra Pak Reports 2021).

In practice, it is estimated that since 2015, there has already been a reduction of approximately -7.3% of GHG emissions, -16% of the water for land use and transformation, in addition to a 21% increase in recycling and use of plastics materials (Mars 2021).

However, although some have shown prominence, the main bottleneck to be the outcome in the food industry is still associated with waste. It is estimated that 1/2 of all food produced in the world goes to waste. This equates to about 1.3 billion tons of food, vegetables, meat, dairy products, seafood, and grains that never leave the farm, or deteriorate during distribution, or are wasted in the final stage (supermarkets, residences). Food is not just a social or financial concern—it is an environmental concern. When we waste food, we lose all the energy and water needed to grow, transport and pack. Furthermore, in its deterioration and decomposition stage, residues produce methane—a greenhouse gas which is more potent than carbon dioxide. Also, according to FAO data, the total carbon footprint of food waste is 4.4 GCO2 per year (Sialabba 2015, FAO 2021). To circumvent this, reducing post-harvest handling and adopting technologies, planning efficiency as well as conscientious purchasing and consumption, represent important strategies for the carbon footprint.

Given these perspectives, it is important that food and beverage companies start thinking about the opportunities and needs that drive the now. In fact just a few years ago, industry 4.0 deployment strategies, the internet of sustainable developments, and supply chain logistics were not on the table for any food following. However, these enablers have shown prosperous results and are on a growing trend in consolidating the sustainable food industry. Therefore, it is necessary for decision-makers to feel that innovations should be placed on the market in a position to help them, and not just as a value-adding alternative.
