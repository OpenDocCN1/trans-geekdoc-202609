Despite the numerous challenges to be overcome, raceway ponds are undeniably well established in the industrial production of microalgae such as Nannochloropsis sp., Chlorlla sp., Tetraselism sp., Dunaliella linearina, Scenedesmus sp., Haematococcus pluvialis and Phaeodactylum tricornutum (Kumar et al. 2021).

For example, raceway ponds present a promising model for industrial production of astaxanthin, found substantially in species such as Haematococcus pluvialis. Average astaxanthin yields are 2.10 g 10⁻³ dry wet in a 100 m² space (Zhang et al. 2009, Yu et al. 2022).

On the other hand, closed-loop photobioreactors provide a system with controlled conditions (Fu et al. 2021). In particular, tubular closed-loop photobioreactors have been highlighted for their industrial relevance in microalgae production systems (Lam et al. 2018, Yu et al. 2022).

As a result, tubular photobioreactors have been designed, including horizontal, vertical, conical, horizontal inclined types made of plastic or glass tube in which microalgae cultures were recirculated with either a pump or air technique (Kumar et al. 2021).

The improved design allows a controlled environment, high biomass production (up to 10 times more than the open pond), low evaporation rate, reduced risks of contaminants, and efficient transfer of nutrients (Kumar et al. 2021).

By comparison, studies Benidas et al. (2013) evaluated the biomass production by Phaeodactylum tricornutum in consolidated systems. The average closed-loop photobioreactors (1.0 g L⁻¹) values outperform raceway ponds (0.6 g L⁻¹). However, the critical point is the high capital and operating costs that limit the scaling of this system. Also, closed photobioreactors affect light quality and, consequently, trigger effects on microgal productivity (Yu et al. 2022).

Despite the photosynthetic nature of microalgae, some species have the potential to grow in the dark. Heterotrophic systems allow the use of conventional fermenters (i.e., stirred tank reactors), which operation and maintenance are considered more straightforward, reducing the production costs (Perez-Garcia et al. 2011, Hu et al. 2018). When using this cultivation method, the productive capacities are superior to the photautotrophic systems in biomass production (Lopes et al. 2020). The main interests with this condition are related to the substantial content of lipids. Especially the species Cryptochenium boomii has been grown in a heterotrophic environment for commercial production of long-chain unsaturated fatty acids (Chew et al. 2018, Lam et al. 2018). However, although it is an attractive advantage, only a few species can grow heterotrophically.

Given these aspects, it is important to mention that microalgae cultivation still faces barriers attributed to technological limitations. Finally, the scale of production needs to be expanded at reasonable costs (Torres-Tiji et al. 2018, Jacob-Lopes et al. 2020, Fu et al. 2021).
