## 4.2 Microalgae

Microalgae represent extensive biodiversity with about 40,000 identified species. Studies evaluating the importance of microalgae on health and human nutrition and their use in the food and nutraceutical industries have gained momentum after 2018 (Sathasivam et al. 2019, 2020, Abu-Goshi et al. 2021, Kusmaydi et al. 2021). Microalgae species such as Chlorlla vulgaris, Arthospira platensis, Euglena gracilis, Auxenlorella proteotheoides, Chlamydomonas reinhardt, Dunaliella barren were granted ‘Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS)’ by Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and EFSA (Torres-Tji et al. 2020). The confirmed species of Chlorlla are C. vulgaris (Canada, EU, and Japan), C. pyrenooides (EU and Canada), C. proteotheoides (US, Japan), C. luteovioides (EU, and Japan), C. sorokinina and C. regularis (Canada). Dunaliella salina (China and Canada), Euglena gracilis (Canada, Japan, and Japan). Recently, dried Euglena gracilis biomass was also reported to be safe by EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Foods, and Food Allergens at the recommended level as a new food (Turk et al. 2020).

Novel micro-based protein and peptides can be used to support animal and plant-based protein sources because algae can produce all EAS. The profiles of microalgae are similar to high-quality protein sources, including casein and EAA composition in microalgal proteins from various sources are comparable to AA in traditional protein sources such as red meat but higher than plant-based protein (Singh et al. 2021). The total amount of protein in the cell is species-dependent and can range from 30 to 55% of the dry weight (which could be as high as 70% in species such as Arthospira platensis) (Barke and Blekler 2016).

Microalgae-derived hydrolysed protein is not only a protein for the diet but also a source of peptides that exhibit immuno-functional properties. It has good AA and digestibility and affects health positively (Eteman et al. 2021, Wang). Over the last decade, in silico, in vitro or in vivo, various biological activities performed by the microalgal-derived bioactive peptides sequence: antioxidant (Azmán et al. 2019, Tejano et al. 2019), antiinflammatory, and anti-amyloidogenic (Olashinde et al. 2019), antioxidant (Li et al. 2019), antihypertensive, antihypertensive (Lin et al. 2018), hypolesteresterolemic, and anticoagulant (Petruk et al. 2018), and antithrombotic activity all others (Tejano et al. 2019).

The first step in processing of microalgae is the upstream process (cultivation), which directly impacts the quality, nutrient composition, content and yield throughout the process (Daneshvar et al. 2021). All sequential processes (harvesting, drying, lysis, extraction, and fractionation) from biomass harvesting to the final product are downstream processes (Nitos et al. 2020). Enzymatic hydrolysis (utilising glycosidases, glucanas, cellulose, pectinases and lipases) is an environmentally friendly and low-energy alternative degradation commonly performed under mild conditions to promote the recovery of intracellular components (Al-Zuhair et al. 2017). Cell lysis disrupts the structure of the cell wall by increasing its porosity and thus enabling the extraction of intracellular components. It is an essential step for extracting proteins and obtaining bioactive peptides from algae. Steam explosion, bead milling, high-pressure homogenisation and physical methods (drying, free-drying, ultrasonication, microwave radiation, pulsed electric field) are some of the latest methods (Alavij et al. 2020).

Powders from different microalgae species Spirulina, Chlorella vulgaris, Tetra and Phaoactoderma sp and/or Dunella sp are used in many produced foods as as pasta, cookies and crackers to enrich their protein and AA content (Singh et al. 2021, Terrien-Palacios and Castellari (2022). The AA profiles of commercial pasta and crackers enriched with algae protein from different sources. In to the high protein and EAA content, the products (particularly those prepared with algae species) contain sulfonic acid taurine, the precursor hypotaurine and homologous homotaurine. Taurine is a semi-amino acid and has become increasingly important in treating the nervous system and cardiovascular disorders, cancer and other metabolic diseases such as type 2 diabetes. Because taurine is derived from cysteine and found naturally in fish and meat, microgae can be considered a good sustainable protein source for vegetarian and vegan diets (Terrien-Palacios and Castellari (2022)).

The use of microalgal protein in foods has been limited for many reasons. Using without extracting the intracellular protein results in low digestibility in humans. Mover, there is insufficient data on the molecular properties and bioactivity of microalgal protein hydrolysates obtained through a combination of Lysis (Nitsos et al. 2020). Recently, Singh et al. (2021) reviewed the future insights, obstacles, and suggestions for selecting the appropriate extractions of protein and bioactive compounds from microalgae. More studies are to determine the best strain, processing techniques, cell lysis and the appropriate food mrix to increase the digestibility and AA bioavailability of microalgal proteins (Lafarga 2019).

## 5. Sugar Reduction

The worldwide prevalence of obesity nearly tripled between 1975 and 2016 (WHO 2020). Dental caries, type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases have become common, especially among children and adolescents, due to excessive sugar intake. Excessive consumption causes health problems associated with an increased risk of chronic diseases such as non-alcoholic fatty liver, obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, some types of cancer and cognitive decline (Rippe and angelopoulos 2016). The WHO recommends limiting free sugar consumption to 10% of energy intake or decreasing it to 8%. Many countries develop and implement initiatives to the consumption of sugar-rich foods and beverages (WHO 2018, Caporizzi et al. 2021).

In September 2021, EFSA’s Report on Nutrition authored a draft opinion for the safeake dietary sugars from all sources, providing a comprehensive review of 30,000 publications (EFSA 2021). The report recommended that the intake of free sugars be as low as possible and be limited in Europe, corroborated by scientific evidence. From public health perspective, the FSa must adopt a more cautious approach regarding the production sugar-rich products (Deliza et al. 2021).

Sugar has many techno-functional properties apart from sweetness to foods, such as ad adding volume, water retention, reducing water and providing crispness. The incorporation of sugar prevents the growth of microorganisms and extends the shelf life of products (McCain et al. 2018, Caporizzi et al 2021). Reducing, replacing or eliminating sugar in fractured foods is challenging for the food industry. Because such processes are likely to affect foods’ overall quality and sensory properties. Consumers generally describe sugar-reduced products as “unpalatable” (WHO 2017, Ilyasoglu et Elki 2021). Multiple approaches as partial/complete sugar substitution, direct/gradual reduction of the amount of sugar in the product, and hancing sweetness via multisensory interactions exist (Dötsch et al. 2009, Di Monaco et al. 2018).
