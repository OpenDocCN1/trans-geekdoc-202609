Another problem with the production and consumption of veggie meat alternatives is that it can alienate humans from nature and from our own. Such ultra-processed substances that we use for food require an increased dependence on technology, leading humans closer to an estrangement from nature. I think a virtuous individual must detect such an estrangement. The eagerness of certain individuals exhibit[ing] embracing and consuming veggie meat is fundamentally due to self-indulgence and short-sightedness. For a temperate person, the taste of food are not so important to relinquish or deviate from our moral integrity and nature by producing, selling, and consuming ultra-processed substances when it is possible and desirable to adjust taste to whole plant food.

Humans, as Aristotle called them, are rational animals endowed with reason. As animals, humans experience care. But morality is not our unique aspect or function. Our specific function is natural nature, and thus our specific function is to reason well. As such physical pleasures should not be repressed, but should be disciplined and regulated by reason in a way that is conducive to our flourishing. Temperate individuals, through practice and reasoning, control their pleasures. They do not merely exercise discipline to curb their appetites and abstain from certain pleasures. Rather, they are individuals who choose only what is conducive to flourishing. For example of a person who refrains from smoking cigarettes because she understands that smoking is a deleterious habit, but enjoys smoking and would do it if it were not harmful. This would be an example of a continent person. But a virtuous person is not merely continent.

In this view, consuming veggie meat is an example of what a virtuous ethical outlook might regard as self-indulgence if consumed infrequently. Very extreme. I have two points in response to such action. First, the idea is that the temperate individual consumes only substances that are beneficial and conducive to flourishing. In the first place there would be no reason for an individual to ever consume sub-optimal or unhealthy substances. In the second place, the temperate individual does not abstain or force himself to avoid certain substances. The temperate individual will adjust his accordingly and will not desire undesirable substances or consume certain substances or engage in sexual intercourse only for taste's or pleasure's sake. In other words for the temperate individual not consuming certain substances will not be restriction or sacrifice. Rather, temperate individuals will not be psychologically dependent on any food in particular.

Second, since certain substances are unhealthy and not optimal for humans, it follows that the temperate individual will not merely avoiding consuming substances that are potentially unhealthy for humans, but also shun the production and sale of such substances. Again, the expression goes, “We are supposed to eat live, not to eat”. Going to modern technology, in affluent societies are constantly encouraged to consume new substances. This is a form of alienation from their natures and misplaces their desire for the wrong food. Note that I do not mean to understand the role of food in a flourishing human life. I think that enjoying the taste and olfaction of the foods has its importance, but it should be food that at no cost to our health—and, ultimately, is flourishing—or to the environment.

It has been reemphasized that the distinguishing factor between a temperate individual and an intperate or a continent is that the temperate individual will never be the case that he will desire unhealthy and unfit substances just because they might look, smell, or taste. Moral virtues are not habits; they are solid and reliable dispositions of one's character to act in ways and consistent with reason that are necessary for human flourishing. Moreover, to possess a virtue implies a complex psychological framework concerning right feelings, understanding, insight, and experience. Thus, in acting, the virtuous person will act with a certain attitude and feelings and insight in such a way she will express her virtue in the right amount, the right way, to the right person and circumstances, and (though not for every action) enjoying acting in such a way.

For example, the virtue of truthfulness. A truly virtuous person does not just tell the truth most of the times and does it in order to score points or reluctantly fulfill his duty. Rather, he tells the truth, not indestly. He brings his children according to such values and encourages others to do the same. Also, he would not even enjoy jokes about dishonesty and would be saddened by honesty in friends. Moreover, he cares about truth for his own sake and feels good about the truth, having determined that consumption of veggie meat alternatives is a form of self-deception, that are hyperpalatable and tasteless, that they are not the most environmentally friendly food, and are not substances to consume as principle. Therefore, the idea of consuming them in moderation and even the ideas of producing and seeing them are inconsistent with a virtuous character. This much, in view, is implied by a thorough understanding of the virtue of temperance and a virtuous approach to food in general.

As a concluding remark, I would like to point out that my discussion about the morality of producing, selling, and consuming veggie meat alternatives serves to illustrate what a virtuous-oriented approach can enable us to show that other ethical theories fail. Our moral understanding has been shaped by certain theories that favor duty, utility, or rights. A peculiar aspect of such theories is that they advise we look at aspects of the world outside ourselves or specific characteristics of animals or people that have should motivate us or show us what we ought to do. However, I believe that their approach often fail to track our moral intuition and in some cases we fail to see important aspects of a certain issue, as I hope to have shown that it is in the case of veggie meat alternatives.

The contribution of a virtue-oriented approach, in the analysis of what it is to be temperate, the nature of human function, excellence of character, and of flourishing, can show us an unexamined side of the issue consuming food ethics. I have attempted to show that most people take it and other forms of psychological and physical pleasure are a moral gain. As I have tried, we must question these assumptions through an analysis of the role of food and pleasure in flourishing. When we do so, as I have argued, we realized from a point of view, the point of view of virtue, should not promote, rather award, the production, sale, and consumption of veggie meat alternatives.
