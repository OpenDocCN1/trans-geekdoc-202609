Sunflower is one of the most consumed oils in the world (Şahin and Elhussain 2018). During the production of refined oil from sunflower seeds, some by-products such as shell and meal are formed (Fig. 12). It has been reported that an average of 1 million tons of waste is generated annually during the processing of sunflower seeds (Mustafayev and Shmychagin 2018), and approximately 50% of the sunflower seed is disposed of as a solid lignocellulosic waste (Casoni et al. 2019). Sunflower hull is a low-cost and abundant residue constituting 18–20% of the sunflower seed (Figlas et al. 2016). Sunflower seed is also rich in protein, carbohydrate, lipid, phenolic acids and anthocyanins (Hassanejad and Nouri 2018). Sunflower seed meal is also released as a by-product during industry. In the 2020/2021 season, 19 million tons of sunflower seed oil was produced while 20 million tons of sunflower seed meal was released (USDA 2021).
