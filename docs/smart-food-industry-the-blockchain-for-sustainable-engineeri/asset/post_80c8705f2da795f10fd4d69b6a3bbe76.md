Wang et al. (2018) investigated the influence of sodium chloride on the rheological properties, water holding capacity, gel strength, and other parameters on the printability of surimi mixtures. The improved rheological properties of the mixtures due to the addition of sodium chloride resulted in better flow of ink through the mouthpiece and maintenance of the surface of the printed material. This was related to the salt-in effect that led to solubilization of myofibrillar proteins and protein structural changes. Finally, it was suggested that surimi gels are promising inks for the production of 3D printed food with complex shapes.

Powdered milk has also been studied as a potential source of protein for the production of 3D printed food (Lille et al. 2018). A highly viscous ink was produced using 50 wt.% of skimmed milk powder, which hindered printing due to stickiness. On the other hand, 60 wt.% of semi-skim milk performed better as ink which might have contributed to its fat and carbohydrate content.

## 2.2.3 Lipids

The structure and composition of triglycerides can modify the properties of inks and the functional properties of the 3D printed products, crystalline structure, and fat index (Godai et al. 2016).

Chocolate is the ingredient with the highest lipid composition used in 3D food printing (Lanaro et al. 2019, Manithal et al. 2020). The reasons for choosing chocolate as an ingredient are its ability to be a viscous fluid that can easily solidifying controlling the demolding and packaging of the product after a cooling process (Hao et al. 2010). However, the use of high-quality chocolate for 3D printing is a challenge due to its softness and complex structure, which includes crystalline phases that must be controlled throughout the printing process to guarantee the quality of the final product.

Le Topic et al. (2018) sought to understand the influence of the 3D printing process on the properties of processed cheeses. The results indicated that the melting and extrusion processing promoted by the 3D printing affected the properties of the cheese-melted and printed cheeses had hardness and higher melting compared to the untreated. It was assigned to control the microstructure lost due to food phase separation and the presence of fats.

Lipton et al. (2010) used bacon fat as a flavor enhancer to print turkey puree using tenderness as an advantage. The authors also used butterfat in a traditional pasta recipe to prevent the melting of the printed structures when tasted. Another interesting aspect related to the use of lipids for 3D printing is that they can act as a plastic or lubricant, which can help the extrusion, facilitating the flow of the ink, as observed by Lille et al. (2018).

## 2.3.1 Rheology

Food inks used for 3D printing must present some characteristics to ensure the stability of the material (Godai et al. 2016). Therefore, for a more efficient 3D printing job, all these factors must be considered and adapted to the specific conditions of each study. The rheological behavior of the food ink depends on viscosity, yield stress, and shear rate. The inks can behave as both Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids (Lille et al. 2018).
