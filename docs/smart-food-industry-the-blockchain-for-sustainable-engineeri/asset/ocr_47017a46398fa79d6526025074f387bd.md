Let's consider the current state of intensive agriculture for, a moment, which is known to be a leading cause of environmental degradation due to the excessive production of greenhouse gases, farm waste that contaminates waters and soil, and other environmental issues (Cameron et al. 2017, Koneswaran and Nierenberg 2008). Vegan diets are said to be environmentally friendly—less friendly than animal-based diets (Tuismio 2019, Premack 2016). Even according to the United Nations, it is imperative that humans consume less meat and plant-based food animal-based diets are no longer sustainable. As us, getting up meat-based and adopting plant-based diet is necessary to “save the world from the worst impacts of climate change” (Casorius 2010). For one thing, animal-based diets require considerably more natural resources than vegetarian and vegan diets.

Also, farms are known for carrying diseases. Vector-borne diseases are caused by infections by insects. These diseases are caused, and become more severe, because of the environmental changes that result from the practices of animal agriculture, as deforestation and reduction of biodiversity. For example, forest clearance in the early 1960s in Bolivia for the purpose of creating arable land caused the migration of cattle that concentrated and overpopulated other areas; the mice carried along a viral fever called Machupo that killed one seventh of the population. A similar epidemic also happened in Argentina. Most diseases, like HIV, influenza, the Nipah virus, are vector-borne diseases (LeDuc 1989, Carroll and Labellerie 2003). While the COVID-19, COVID-19 is a zoonotic virus, like other zoonotics, come from wet markets, commercial factories, dairy, and meat farms. SARS, the bird flu, cholera and Ebola are all zoonologic that start with animals in these types of situations and spread to humans. Who consume animal products, contribute more to the emergence of zoonotic and vector-borne diseases that cause illnesses and kill people more than those who consume vegan diets.

Another problem is that intensive agriculture uses too much agricultural land. To be precise, too much means that “livestock” use nearly 80% of global agricultural land, yet less than 20% of the world’s supply of calories. Seventy percent of arable land is used to grow feed animals, while many people in the world are malnourished or starving) (Riche and Rock 2017). These facts show that animal-based diets are very inefficient. Vegetarian diets, generally require less land and contribute less land degradation than plant-based diets (Pimentel and Pimentel 2021, Pitchichi). Animal agriculture’s use of land is not efficient because feeding animals requires considerably large areas of arable land, protein, and water. Furthermore, plant-based diets make it possible to feed more people than animal-based diets and more adequately. If animal farming were deconstructed, it could be possible to use less land (as well as other resources) to grow human food.

Another issue is the depletion of minerals from the soil, which results in a chain of negative effects on the environment. The soil is depleted of minerals due to intensive agriculture; as a result, farmers use phosphorus fertilizers to supplement the quantities available in the soil. A process has led to the buildup of phosphorus in the soil, and in turn the potential for phosphorus to become soluble. Dissolved phosphorus is transported from farms to lakes, rivers, and streams causing excessive aquatic pollution, such as eutrophication (rapid growth of algae). Decomposition of algae also leads to hypoxia (suffocation of aquatic ecosystems) in rivers and seas. Eutrophication can also cause Pfizer Piscicidia, a lethal group of fish-killer eukaryotes (Webster 2013).

Regarding water usage, the livestock sector uses more water than any other sector. First, as previously mentioned, pollutes more water than any other sectors. Furthermore, farmers use fertilizers and pesticides that can cause the formation of nitrates that leak into the groundwater resulting in negative health effects for animals and humans. Farmers in the USA use recombinant bovine somatotropin (rBst), hormones that pollute waters.
