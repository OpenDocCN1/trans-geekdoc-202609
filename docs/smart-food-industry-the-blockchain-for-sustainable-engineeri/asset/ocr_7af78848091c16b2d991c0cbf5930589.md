Water soluble minerals, peptides, collagen, gelatin, enzymes, oligosaccharides, and fatty acids are all present in large quantities in the aquatic products (Shen et al. 2019). Therefore, all such resources can be utilized to produce a variety of high-valued goods such as bioplastics, lactic acid, or preservative compounds, which are very useful in food, pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics industry (Davy and Routledge 2018, Marti-Quijal et al. 2020). For instance, in a study, antioxidant compounds were produced through the fermentation of fish-products by bacteria isolated from sea bass (Marti-Quijal et al. 2020). As a result, authors concluded that the lactic acid bacteria from sea bass can be promoted and synthesize peptides with strong antioxidant capacity.

There is an increasing worldwide awareness and interest in the valuing of available resources, particularly the nutrients and by-products, to produce a circular economy in aquaculture. Therefore, The European Commission promoted “Blue Growth” in 2012, a green economy focused on marine and coastal sectors (EC (2012)) (Campanari et al. 2021). To recover the bioactive components from aquatic waste, three major techniques, namely chemical/physical, enzymatic and microbial methods, are used. The enzymatic method is regarded as one of the best techniques for all types of fish among the three (Ashraf et al. 2022). The crude protein content of fish products varies from 8% to 35%. Several methods for recovering protein and peptides from fish by have been developed, including acid and alkaline hydrolysis, and enzymatic hydrolysis (Zamora-Siller et al. 2018). As the valuable components, fish collagen and fish gelatin (partially hydrolyzed collagen) can be extracted from heads (92.3–100), fish (0.8–8.0), skin (2.0–12.6), scales (0.8–6.0), bones (9.0–19.0), and fish bodies (Zarubin et al. 2021). In a study, collagen was extracted from the skin of two species of chondrocytes using peppsin, with yields ranging from 14.2% to 61.2% (Bianco et al. 2017). The main components of fish scales are hydroxyapatite (30%), and protein (70%) consisting primarily of collagen and keratin (Shen et al. 2019).

Fish proteins are highly valued for their properties, such as the network formation, plasticity, elasticity, and gas barrier properties. Araijol et al. (2018a) aimed to produce and create bioplastics from myofibrillar proteins found in gilded catfish (Brachyplatystoma rousseauxei). Consequently, the authors produced a transparent and flexible bioplastic with low solubility and water permeability.

Fish viscera is a potential source of bioactive materials from fish processing, and as a rich source of digestive enzymes. Pepsin, trypsin, chymotrypsin, and elastase are the primary enzymes found in fish viscera, the proteins are major groups of naturally available in fish and aquatic invertebrates (Ate and Ojah 2017). Fish oil is rich in proteins and also contains oil, eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). Depending on the season and type, fish oil contains 15–30% of these unsaturated fatty acids (Shen et al. 2019). EPA and DHA have biological effects on lipoproteins, blood pressure, cardiac function, colon cancer, metal health disorders, endothelial function, and cardiac electrophysiology, in addition to potent antiplatelet and anti-inflammatory effects. Fish oil is a good source of vitamin D K which is known to have a wide range of health effects (Ate and Ojah 2017).
