3DD printing has sparked interest in several applications, ranging from food for people with specific needs to it in the military and space field (Le-Bail et al. 2020). NASA (USA) has also demonstrated interest in this technology, mainly due to the producing of foods with adequate nutritional requirements, acceptability, and food safety (Lipton 2017).

Another interest is the application of this technology in the development of foods for specific groups of individuals, which this technology can facilitate and motivate people to have control over food intake through personalized diets (Le-Bail et al. 2020), for example, as already mentioned, people with specific needs related to food composition (such as nutritional intolerances and allergies) or its physical properties (such as texture and chewing and swallowing problems, as dysphagia).

Many others studied the development of 3D printed foods with different textures, nutritious and suitable for people with dysphagia. Dick et al. (2019) introduced hydrocolloids to prepare a 3D printed pork with modified textures for chewing and swallowing difficulties. Xing et al. (2022) developed 3D printed foods based on black fungi added as a diet for dysphagia, gaining insights into the development of visually appealing foods suitable for dysphagia using 3D printing.

Moreover, the potential uses of 3D printing encompass home or small-scale 3D printing, resulting in easier food acquisition and better food experience. In fact, in the case of developing 3D printing, one of the first goals was the idea of 3D printing. In addition, small-scale 3D food production allows for the customization of food products, for example, the confectionery and bakery industry (Lipton et al. 2020).

The printing of food changes our view about the manufacture and preparation of food, since in the future, an individual could get food ready in moments. All of this results in less work involved and, consequently, lower cost, and food becomes more versatile as an individual can make their food at home (Tran 2019). Course, 3D printing will still face several obstacles to be completely suitable for all the purposes. As discussed this chapter, many factors directly influence 3D printing, making it necessary to optimize processes for each specific demand. Despite this, this technology has great advantages in the food sector, as promised the use of this technology will revolutionize the industrial scenario as it can provide customized solutions, with nutritional appeal and built to measure for people who even now have been excluded from certain markets due to diverse conditions, such as health. There is still a long path to further progress, development, and application on a large scale, although these are difficult challenges, they are not impossible to overcome.

# 5. Perspectives and Conclusion

This chapter highlighted 3D printing as a revolutionary and promising technology to transform conventional food manufacturing, but this futuristic technology presents many challenges, from the materials to be processed, the processing, and post-processing methods. Among the different techniques, extrusion-3D printing has been the explored in the food sector. In addition, different ingredients, based on carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids, have been used to create foods based on the layer-by-layer method. The 3D printing technology has made significant contributions to the food sector, offering new possibilities for the application of alternative ingredients such as insect meals, algae, microorganisms, and agro-food resides, efficient use of food ingredients with minimal or no waste. This technology opens avenues for the increased acceptance of foods that can contribute to better use of underprivileged food. On-demand functional ingredients, resulting in a better food experience, automation, labor savings, lower energy, and transportation costs, and easy supply chain are other examples of the advantages of 3D printed foods. Therefore, it is undeniable how 3D printing can contribute to a sustainable future.
