Oliveira et al. (2017) found that the combination of palm kernel oil cake and sesame oil cake led to higher lipase production rate compared to their single use as substrate. The lipase enzyme has been successfully produced from canola oil cake (Amein and Bhatti 2014), coconut oil cake (Venkatesagowda et al. 2015) and peanut oil cake (Ammaraj et al. 2011) used SSF by Penicillium fellulatum, Lactobacillus theobiae, and Bacillus licheniformis, respectively. Proteases play an important role in the hydrolysis of protein groups, and have application areas in many industries such as food, leather and detergent. Although proteases are derived from plant, animal and microbial sources, obtaining from by-products has been promising with the increasing interest in natural factors (Thakur et al. 2015). Rajmalwar produced protease from soybean, sesame, linseed, mustard and mustard oil cake by Aspergillus flavus using SSF, and reported that soybean oil cake resulted in maximum activity, followed by sesame oil cake after 72 hours of incubation.

Waste and by-products can be used as an energy source. For example, biodiesel is produced from rapeseed meal and rapeseed waste, while the rapeseed meal produced 35% efficiency in coal-fired power plant, 30% efficiency in combined heat and power, and 60% thermal efficiency. Rapeseed meal reduced the total energy requirement of a small-scale biodiesel production by 180% and the total requirement of heat and electricity by 216% (Stephen et al. 2008). The energy value of straw obtained from 1 hectare of rapeseed has an energy value equivalent to 65.7-8.12 gigajles (Jankowski and Dużyński 2003).

Different literature evaluated oil seed products in the formulation and development of bread, biscuits, snacks, desserts and dairy products (Acuta and Sona 2020). For example, bread enriched with wheat oil showed higher yield, phenolic acid content and antioxidant capacity (Pycia et al 2020). Koneva et al. (2018) analyzed the rheological properties of the dough using different proportions of flour obtained from flaxseed cake (7.5%, 10 and 12.5%), and found that as the level of flax flour increased, water absorption increased and the viscosity, amylolytic activity and retrogration properties decreased. Authors stated that it is possible to enrich the bread and preserve the flavor parameters under industrial production conditions. Li et al. (2021) replaced wheat flour with different levels (10%, 15%, and 20%) of okara (a by-product of soy milk) for noodles, sweet and bread making. They stated that the glycemic index of the obtained products was lower than the control group, and observed glycemic effect was due to the dietary fibers of okara. Cookies are one of the most popular bakery products with their long shelf life, ready-to-eat properties and affordable costs. Cookies enriched with oil seeds replacing wheat peanut and soybean oil cakes offered better nutritional content in terms of protein, dietary fiber and polyphenols (Behera et al. 2013). Similar results were obtained when defattered, soybean and flaxed flour were used in cookie formulation (Bhise et al. 2014). In addition, the authors reported that cookies prepared with combinations of soybean-hog flour and wheat-soy flour were acceptable by school children. Consuming 28 grams of these combination biscuits provides the protein needed for children aged 3 to 10 years. For this, functional products such as the cookies produced with defattered soy can be used as a protein source to eliminate the protein deficiency of children (Serrem et al. 2011).

In addition to bakery products, Kumar et al. (2011) added 4% soybean hull flour in chicken nuggets formation, and reported enriched chicken nuggets showed higher protein, and crude fiber contents. In another study, different concentrations of flaxseed oil cakes were used to make a new kefir-like beverage, without any addition of substantial. With the results obtained, it is seen that kefir-like beverages enriched withed oil cakes are a good source of antioxidants, and ideal for the consumption by vegans and lactose intolerant consumers (Lopusiewicz et al. 2019). In addition, okara drink co-cultured with Lactobacillus reasei and Lindera saturnes carries high amounts of free amino acids and probiotics, indicating that co-cultured okara can be used as a new functional probiotic beverage (Vong and Liu 2019).
