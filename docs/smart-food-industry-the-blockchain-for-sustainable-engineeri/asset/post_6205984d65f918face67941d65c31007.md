
However, the concentration of these same ingredients exceeded a certain value, the extrusion capacity of the material compromised. The formulation containing water (29.0 g), butter (6.0 g), flour (48.0 g), sucrose (6.6 g), and egg (10.4 g) showed the best printing capacity, elasticity, and lower ductility.

Dick et al. (2019) studied the fat content (0, 1, 2, 3 fat layers within a structure) and fat density (50, 75, and 100%) on product and print properties. For the 3D printing of the rectangular prism, a food ink was prepared using base of meat (85%) and water (15%); NaCl (1.5%), and guar gum (0.5%). The chewability, hardness, and moisture parameters were proportionally influenced by the fat density; this factor inversely affected the cohesion and shrinkage of the printed material. Fat content inversely affected chewiness, fat retention, hardness, and moisture retention, proportionally affecting shrinkage, cooking loss, and cohesiveness.

## 2.3 External Factors that Influence 3D Printing

3D printing is also affected by external aspects, such as the type of equipment, processing parameters, techniques, print design, among others. External influences can affect print accuracy and final product quality. In this section, we will discuss how external factors affect 3D printing.

### 2.3.2.1 The 3D printers

The use of 3D printing technology for food applications is one more recent development when compared to the 3D printing technology involving the mechanism for plastic filaments. In this way, previous works, many of the 3D printers used have been modified or customized to print food effectively, including the addition of a motorized system for food extrusion and a syringe for food intake. For example, Reprap Prover 1, and Delta 2040 printers were originally designed for plastic materials but were modified and reconfigured with a custom-designed syringe that was appropriate for food extrusion.

In this scenario, we observe that 3D printers have varying capabilities, specifications, and design differences that make standardization and reproducibility difficult. Thus, there is a need for further clarification of the configuration, modifications, and customizations of 3D printers for developing universal printers for food 3D printing.

### 2.3.2.2 Printing Parameters

The quality of 3D printed is directly related to the optimization of the printing parameters, namely nozzle height, diameter and movement speed, extrusion rate, and filling (Fig. 6).

The distance between the nozzle tip of the extruder and the deposited layer is called the nozzle height. Under ideal conditions, without shrinkage or expansion of the lines, ideally, smaller nozzles provide better print quality, as they ensure that the material can be fixed to the previous layer inaccuracies caused by delay (Yang et al. 2018) in deposition. However, under non-ideal conditions, a critical nozzle height can be calculated (Eq. 1).

```
hc = \frac{V_d}{v_n D_n}
```

- $hc$: critical nozzle height (mm),
- $V_d$: volume of the extrusion rate (mm³/s),
- $v_n$: nozzle movement speed (mm/s),
- $d_n$: nozzle diameter (mm).

![](img/456d62956459ae12abbe8bcaa76dab9d_205_0.png)

**Figure 6**: Schematic diagram of the 3D printing processes: nozzle height, nozzle diameter, extrusion rate, movement speed, and filling.

The literature describes that the nozzle height greatly influences the geometric shape of the printed samples and when using the critical value, it provides the desired shapes with greater fidelity and a good connection between the layers (Wing et al. 2018).

The diameter ($D_n$) of the ink outlet nozzle is largely responsible for the thickness of the line to be deposited. Depending on properties of the inks, the effect of high pressure in the flow channel in a nozzle with reduced diameter causes the material to store more elastic energy, and therefore, a suitable diameter is required to provide smooth extrusion without a considerable increase in the system pressure. Yang (2018) produced corn starch gels added with lemon juice and evaluated the influence of nozzle diameter (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 mm) on the quality of the printed samples. The authors noted that the larger nozzle diameter resulted in relatively delicate and fine lines, while the smaller nozzle diameter resulted in relatively delicate and fine lines, a better quality of the printed food. Huang et al. (2019) evaluated the effect of nozzle diameter (0.84 mm, 1.20 mm, 1.56 mm) on the dimensional properties of 3D printed samples based on brown rice. The authors stated that the smaller diameter (0.84 mm) of the nozzle resulted in printed samples with deviations closer to the design project. In addition, the nozzle diameter was able to change the void rate and the number of layers, directly affecting the texture characteristics (hardness and guminess). The authors also noted that larger nozzle (1.56 mm) significantly reduced print time but resulted in dimensional deviations. Nijdam et al. (2022) evaluated the validity of different extrusion models directly using open-source software and commercial slicers like Slicer. The authors evaluated different inks made on peanut butter, cheese, fractionated animal fat or 20% rice-starch gel, different nozzle diameters (0.41, 0.83, and 1.5 mm), and extrusion speeds (10 and 30 mm/s). The most that corresponded to the behavior observed by the lines under the nozzle diameter and print speed conditions evaluated in this work. Fractionated animal fat and de-fretted rice-starch gel presented several ideal conditions for 3D printing, forming smooth, continuous, and stable filaments, considering the wide range of operating conditions evaluated in this work.

The nozzle movement speed and extrusion rate parameters simultaneously affect 3D printing. In this sense, according to Yang et al. (2019), the nozzle movement speed and extrusion rate can be determined considering the nozzle height and diameter (Eq. 2).

```
$V_d = \frac{\pi}{4} v_n D_n^2 = \frac{\pi}{4} v_n h_c^2$
```

where:
- $V_{di}$: volume of the extruded rate (mm³/s),
- $v_{ni}$: nozzle movement speed (mm/s),
- $D_{ni}$: nozzle diameter (mm),
- $h_i$: nozzle height.

Although there is a positive relationship between nozzle movement speed and extrusion rate, there is a limit to printing efficiency when changing these parameters. High speeds can cause a drastic decrease in the quality of the product. On the other hand, low extrusion rates can lead to collapse and a considerable difference between the objective and the actual product. Therefore, it is necessary to determine a suitable extrusion rate to print a smooth line (Yang et al. 2018).

Heagarajan et al. (2020) evaluated the parameters nozzle speed (800 to 2200 mm/min) and extrusion rate (120 to 240 rpm) for 3D printing of rice gel. The authors observed that for high speed (2400 mm/min), the samples showed structural defects; for intermediate speed (1500 to 800 mm/min), the samples showed better appearance, and for low speed (below 800 mm/min), the samples showed zones with excessive material. Furthermore, these authors observed that the best printability was obtained for values from 180 to 220.

Another crucial parameter in the final characteristics of the printed, namely in its dimensional stability, is the infill which can be regulated by the fill level, pattern and perimeter. The fill level is the percentage of the inside of the printed object that is filled. The fill pattern is the pattern that the nozzle draws to fill the object (Liu et al. 2018). The perimeter consists of the amount of layer on the outside (Huang et al. 2019). Liu et al. investigated the effect of different filling levels (10%, 40%, and 70%) and filling patterns (honeycomb, rectilinear, honeycomb, and Hilbert curve) on the texture of printed foods based on mashed potatoes. It was noted that the filling percentage had a positive effect on the hardness, firmness, and rigidity of the printed products, while the infill pattern had no significant effect.

### 2.3.2.2 Print Design

The choice of the design designed for the final product is essential to ensure the originality and essence of the 3D object. The complexity of the design will influence the quality and printing time. In this sense, a compromise between the complexity and printing time must be reached.

3D printing is a valuable technology in prototyping it allows the development of personalized foods based on the needs of individuals. In this sense, choosing the right and personalized print design is essential to attract the individual's attention, presenting food with a pleasing appearance. Zhang et al. (2018) performed 3D printing of cereal-based foods containing probiotics and applied the post-processing technique consisting of baking the structures in an oven at different temperatures, followed by a cooling step. The authors reported that a print design with a higher volume could ensure the survival of probiotics, improving the final quality of the developed product.

In general, even simple structures, like stars, can be challenging due to the fine adjustment of angles and shapes required for the process.

### 2.3.2.4 Post-processing

Raw materials and ingredients used as food inks for 3D printing may require post-processing techniques, which can cause significant changes in the physicochemical properties of the product, also altering the nutritional value and sensory acceptance (Keerth et al. 2020).
