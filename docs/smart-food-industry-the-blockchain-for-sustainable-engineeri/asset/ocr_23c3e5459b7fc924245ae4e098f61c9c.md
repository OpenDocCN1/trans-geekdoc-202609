To deal with the issue of increased water demand, a balanced green-blue water usage strategy would be required. More research is needed to identify the spatiotemporal variability of water availability, as well as the amount of blue water that may be utilized sustainably in a specific watershed without causing environmental damage.

# 3.4 Cases Associated with WF of Rice and Animal Products

Rice is a major crop for feeding the world's population, especially in Southern Asia and Africa. The construction of large irrigation systems is common to fulfill the water required in farming. Hence, rice production is divided into two categories: wetland and upland. Wetlands supply around 85% of the world's area where rice is harvested with irrigated wetland accounting for roughly 75% of total rice production (Chapagain and Hoekstra 2010).

Among the rice-producing countries, India consumes the largest amount of water produced by China, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Thailand, Myanmar, Vietnam, the Philippines, and Brazil as mentioned in Fig. 4. In comparison to India (239 m/cap/yr), Indonesia (299 m/cap/yr), China (134 m/cap/yr), and the United States (29 m/cap/yr), Thailand's per-capita WF for rice consumption is quite high (547 m/cap/yr). This difference is because some countries' diets contain more rice than others.

South Asian have high WF for rice production and consumption. In these countries, however, the majority of the WF is grounded in the monsoon, therefore the water scarcity is rather minor, contrary to popular opinion. The overall WF uses similar amounts of green and blue water. When comparing to the blue WF, the green WF (rain) has a lower production cost (evaporation of irrigation water). The environment of rice production's blue WF is influenced by location and when the water is used. It would take a detailed analysis to determine the location and timing of WF in rice production that cause significant problems in the environment, but rice from the United States and Pakistan, which is majority dependent on blue water, is likely to have greater impacts per unit of product than rice from the Vietnam. Comparing the local implications of different rice sources may be useful from a perspective of sustainable perspectives for nations or areas where the importance of rice import is maximum for their consumption (Chapagain and Hoekstra 2010).
