# Environmental Sustainability Assessment of Food Waste Valorization Options 365

Preventing the disposal of oil will will reduce the environmental damage, and allow to use a cheap and protein-rich raw material for new studies (Moure et al. 2006). Among oils, the use of rapeseed has been limited due to its anti-nutritional compounds such as glucosinates and phytates. Glucosinates become harmful when rapeseed seeds are crushed and the enzymes are released. For this reason studies have focused on reducing the effect of rapeseed glucosination. Many have subjected rapeseed proteins to such hot-pressing, cold-pressing and solvent-extraction for human consumption. In fact, the protein recovery of cold-pressed cake (45%) was higher than that of hot-pressed rapeseed meal (26%) and solvent-extracted rapeseed meal (5%). In addition, cold-pressed rapeseed cake exhibited better emulsification properties (Ostring et al. 2020). Rapeseed meal has higher protein than soybean meal, and has a potential to be used as a nutritional protein source due to its well-balanced amino acid composition. Tan et used 5% NaCl, 0.1 M NaOH and 70% ethanol treatments, and standard alkaline extraction method. The authors showed that the cake proteins obtained by the solvent method provides higher protein yield and recovery rate of water-soluble proteins by the alkali method, and suggested that the cake meal should be included in the human diet (Tan et al. 2011). Sirilatha and Krishnakumar (2003) showed that defeated sunflower meal offers higher protein and fiber content compared to soybean meal and partially dehulled seed meal. Ermis and Karaso (2020) showed that it is possible to use sunflower seed cake protein powder in the formulation of vegan, confectionery and meat products.

In recent years, with the emergence of a healthy diet trend, consumers prefer natural instead of synthetic. In this case, researchers are directed to find a new source of antioxidants such as some oil wastes rich in antioxidant compounds (Lula and Özemir 2017). Antioxidant compounds in oil seeds and cakes are mostly in the form of lignans, phenolic acids and flavonoids (Terpine et al. 2012). Based on high pressure, microwave and supercritical fluid methods have been used to extract እንደሚ from oil wastes. For instance, canola seed cake extracted with methanol/aqueous solution showed the higher phenolic (210.4 mg GAE/100) and flavonoid compounds (37.9 mg LUE/100), followed by her and las seed cakes (Teh et al. 2014). Antioxidant compounds also observed from the by-products of oils can be evaluated in the formulations of beverages, energy bars and bakery products (Ancuta and Sonia 2020; Terpine in 2020). Many studies report the presence of different antioxidant compounds in oil-byproducts: chlorogenic acid in sunflower seed cake (Zardo et al. 2019); ferulic acid, p-coumaric acid in sesame bran and flavonoid glycosides derivatives in canola seed cake (Zardo et al. 2020); isoflavone and gallic acid in soybean cake (Kao and Chen 2006). The oil meals rich in phenolic compounds also show higher antioxidant activity than synthetic antioxidant hydroxy toluene, which provides many benefits in terms of human health and economic recovery (Terpine et al. 2012).

Various enzymes can be produced for industrial applications using solid state fermentation (SSF) from oil cakes and slags. These oil by-products are suitable raw materials that can be used in enzyme applications because they are rich in carbon and nitrogen, and inexpensive components (Ancuta and Sonia 2020). Many authors have worked on obtaining enzymes such as lipase (Parihar 2012, Thakur et al. 2015; Gupta et al. 2018; Singh and Sanyatarayana 2006) and L-asparaginase (Satya et al. 2014) from oil cake substrates. Lipases are hydrolytic enzymes that catalyze esterification, transesterification in various environments. Lipases are widely used in food applications as dairy and bakery products, as well as in the detergent, pharmaceutical and leather industries. Obtaining enzymes from agricultural waste by applying SSF in a solvent-free system provides higher end product production and stability, avoiding solvent costs and providing economic improvement (Parihar 2012, Oliveira et al. 2017). Parihar (2012) obtained lipase enzyme from flaxseed and mustard oil by SSF by bacterial strain of Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
