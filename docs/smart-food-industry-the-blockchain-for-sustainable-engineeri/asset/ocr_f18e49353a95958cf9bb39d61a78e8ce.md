Recycling of waste provides a significant amount of economic gain as well as environmental. The economic gain from the recycling of waste products may increase even more with the factors such as recycling and reuse of waste products, minimizing losses, and increasing the utilizing efficiency of resources (Bec et al. 2019). It has been reported that the cereals generate $101,751 worth of waste products during agricultural production, $159,766 in post-harvest processing and $96,608 in processing and packaging, and $52,146 in consumption only in South Africa (Nahman and de Lange 2013). Bread is the most important as a grain product that could damage in natural resources such as water, soil, and energy used during production and transportation; in addition to the loss of raw materials, whereas bread caused an economic loss of 400 million euros in the Netherlands, the situation is even worse in developed countries. For example, in the UK, 20 million slices of bread are wasted every day, resulting in 84,000 tons of CO2 equivalent emissions (Natiraj et al. 2021). In India, farmers lose 16% of their crop to waste, burning 60% of rice is straw. The burning of rice straw causes 7,300 kg of CO2-equivalent greenhouse emissions per hectare, soil pollution and a decrease in biodiversity. The economic expenditure hectare for bread disposal was reported as $28, and the environmental loss as $46. Moreover, the product value of bioethanol from straw is $926/ha, the fossil fuel saving would $65/ha (Bhattacharjee et al. 2022).

The cereals are incredibly useful in food ingredients, but the use of cereals in feed formulations provides an alternative both in terms of environmental sustainability and in terms of producing low-cost alternatives. The inclusion of different levels of rice waste in the feed of sheep significantly reduced the cost per kg body weight gain. The cost of cheap feed without adding any rice waste was $5.1 per kg weight gain, consuming feeds with 30% rice added reduced feed costs to $1.5 per kg weight gain (Muhammad et al. 2008). In wheat processing, consumer interest in functional products high dietary fiber and phytochemicals has started to attract attention especially in recent years due to their beneficial effects such as the prevention of chronic diseases. The World Health Organization (WHO) and FAO recommend a daily intake of 25-30 grams of dietary fiber for humans. Therefore, researchers and developers in the bakery industry have begun to create new types of food products, new formulations, and processes. All considering this, studies on the development of feed-enriched grain-based products are increasing day by day. For these reasons, as a result of the knowledge of the nutritional content of grain products and their wastes, cereal waste has been used as new and promoting raw materials in the production of functional foods.

Microfluidized corn bread was used to develop high-fiber wheat bread with a new strategy. Production of high fiber bread was achieved by replacing 20% of flour with microfluidized corn. In addition, bread developed with containing corn bread had a smaller specific loaf volume and improved textural properties such as firmness, springiness and cohesiveness, compared to the white bread (de Erive et al. 2020).
