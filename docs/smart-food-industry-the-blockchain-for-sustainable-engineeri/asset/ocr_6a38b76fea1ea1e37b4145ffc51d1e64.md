They reside under the basement membrane of muscle fibres and are capable of differentiating into myocytes which form the multinucleated tubes that then transform into fibers (Reiss et al. 2021). MyoSCs are the most-abundant tissue-resident adult source of cells (Benzinger et al. 2012, Reis et al. 2021) provide an easily accessible source of cells which can be isolated from animal muscle skeletal tissues through biopsies, using straight forward protocols. They are lineage committed cells, requiring the induction of the differentiation process simply a number of doublings before losing their differentiation potential, rendering them unsuitable for meat production at scale, unless they are functionally immortalized, similarly to myoblast cell lines used for the research of skeletal muscle cells (e.g., L6 and C2C12 lines) (Post 2014, Ding et al. 2018, Post et al. 2020).

Another possible cell source with an attractive potential for cultivated meat production is pluripotent cells due to their ability to theoretically proliferate indefinitely, as to differentiate to any cell type present in the three germ layers. ESCs are derived from the inner mass of the blastocyst during the early stages of embryonic development, while iPSCs are derived from somatic cells through reprogramming using specific transcription factors to induce pluripotency (Takahashi and Yamanaka 2006). Isolation of ESCs (Thomson et al. 1998, Reinnoff et al. 2000, Gour, 2006, Chen et al. 2009) and cell for obtaining iPSCs (Takahashi and Yamanaka 2006, 2016) have been developed for human cells, these methods are less explored or established for livestock species. ESCs are often difficult to isolate due to their short lifespan of the blastocyst and require highly skilled cells which have been published to date attempting to reprogram somatic cells of livestock species to iPSCs, including bovine (Pillar et al. 2019, Brussel et al. 2020, Su et al. 2021), Cheng (2012, Xu et al. 2019) and avian (Lu et al. 2012, 2015). However, contradictory results have been reported the translatability of protocols for human and mouse pluripotent cells to livestock species. ESCs or iPSCs have an increased proliferation capacity compared to MyoSCs as they can propagate indefinitely.
