Neither our ancestors, nor contemporary humans, process plants and animal flesh in various ways in order to improve their health or to improve the quality of a substance, but rather in order to increase calorie density and thereby survive through scarce conditions. Obviously processing food enables consumption of substances that we cannot consume in their raw state. But it certainly doesn't follow from these facts that processing food is beneficial. In fact, processing can be detrimental to human health. Consider the decline of human health (Wang et al. 2021). In the US, alone National Health and Nutrition Survey (NHANES) reports that the obesity rate among adults in 2015-16 was 39.8. Since 1999, obesity rates have increased. It is estimated 65 million more obese adults in the USA and 11 million more obese adults in the UK by 2030 (Wang et al. 2021). According to the American Heart and Stroke Association's Statistics, almost half of U.S. adults suffer from cardiovascular disease (AHA 2019). Taking the United States as example, Americans are eating more calories than they require (Gould 2017). This is not because increase in calories is due to an increased availability of varieties. The point is that cause of such a declined status of human health is due to processing food, which enables high calorie concentration. Eating fruit and greens practically could never cause obesity, heart disease, and other chronic conditions.

No one would argue, that if we started processing substances to giraffes, on the contrary, every expert would agree that while giraffes might be able to survive by eating processed substances, what substances are optimal for the health of giraffes. By the same sake, no reason exists to believe that processed substances, whether plant or animal-based, could be optimal for humans if it is not optimal for other animals. The thousands of years during which our species foraged had no significant effects on our digestive anatomy (Milton 2000). Nowadays we eat cooked substances, but their digestive systems are still the same that nature designed for eating fruit and leafy greens millions of years ago. As primatologist Katherine Milton, "the widespread prevalence of related health problems, particularly in highly industrialized nations, suggests that many humans are not eating in a manner compatible with their biology" (Milton 1999). It is not surprising, then, that the health of modern humans is declining because they are not consuming diets—on which the primate line has for millions of years and produced—" (Dehnault 2005). As Donald S. Coffey offers,

[Humans] were not biologically selected by the evolution process to eat what we do today, and the damage is manifested in prostate and breast cancer. As all of the present suggestions of the National Cancer Institute and the American Cancer Society as to how humans might reduce their chances of getting prostate and breast cancer by adopting dietary changes in our lifestyle back toward the early human diet of more fruits; a variety of fresh vegetables and fiber; less burning, cooking, and processing; (Coffey 2001).

Thus, the first step is to show that humans were not biologically selected to eat ultra-processed substances, not all living organisms can be flexible and eat substances that are not optimal for their health. It is also true that all living organisms have a specific diet according to their nature. Note also that all wild living organisms consume as much food as they can without damaging their functions and health. However, research shows that companion animals and other similar animals suffer from obesity (German et al. 2018). The interesting question is why this is the case. The most congruent answer is when animals consume a diet that is not proper and specific to their nature, they experience a decline in their health. Granted, humans can cook grain or animal flesh and somehow manage to survive. Clearly this does not mean that cooked substances or ultra-processed foods such as veggie are healthy for us. Rather, we must eat food that agrees with our biological nature—food that is conducive to the highest level of health.

The second step is to bring attention to the fact that human diets have been shaped by different cultural factors and moreover, by consumer capitalism. Free market capitalism is an economic system whose principle is to sell as much as possible, irrespective of whether the goods sold are detrimental. Consider the tobacco industry as an example, which produces and sells carcinogens to people. Under capitalism, consumer demand is deliberately manipulated through mass-marketing techniques for the advantages of sellers. In order to sell, people are disciplined into believing that they need things when in time we remind ourselves of the waste of food that those of us in affluent countries waste.
