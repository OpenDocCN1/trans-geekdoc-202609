Most countries, have a potential of self-sufficiency on green water-based and can produce their food requirement locally. There is a substantial possibility to increase water productivity in rain-fed agriculture by increasing production levels by up to fourfold within the existing water balance (Rockström 2003). This is a potential to boost water production in rain-fed agriculture by increasing the water productivity omitting the need to introduce new blue water (Critley et al. 1991). However, available precipitation in semi-arid dry regions is extremely limited, making agricultural productivity without the use of blue water impossible. Thus, global cereal crop output could be drastically reduced (Siebert and Doell 2010, Chaplain and Hoek 2013). As a result, in a world with finite freshwater supplies, to deal with the issue of increased water demand, a balanced green-blue water usage strategy would be required. Additional research is essential to identify the blue water availability by analysing the spatiotemporal variability, as the amount of blue water that may be utilised in a sustainable catchment without creating environmental damage.
