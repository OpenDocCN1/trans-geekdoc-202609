Between the pathways for action, developed by the Food 2030 Research and Innovation Policy for sustainable and water food systems, of them—Alternative proteins and diet shift; Food waste and resource efficiency; Healthy, personalized nutrition and Food safety systems of the future are very related with food safety and circularity (European Commission 2020a).

The Standing Committee on Agricultural Research (SCAR) Foresight Expert Group led the 5th SCAR Foresight Exercise which was focused on the question: "Whether to reach a safe and just operating space" for society, by better management of agricultural food systems and resources? The question comes from policy research among the SDGs, the 21st session of the Conference of Parties (COP21) to the United Nations Framework on Climate Change (UNFCCC), the Green Deal initiative and the European Commission (EC) "Farm to Fork" strategy for healthy, environmentally friendly food systems. Three main goals or three transition-related priorities were identified: sustainable and healthy diets for all a society, diversifying resilient bio-economy as a road to sustainability (European Commission 2020b).

In order to feed a growing number of people and to increase food production, ensuring a healthy and regenerative environment, food systems should take into account CO2 emissions, energy and water consumption, food and waste.

In terms of food safety, new challenges arise among food systems due to the increase of microbial resistance, changes in raw materials (waste become new materials in some cases), and producing new products. A better resource efficiency of food systems can impact food safety, for increased sustainability through changes in food systems by more energy efficiency and sustainable freezing and cooling systems and/or reducing utilization of water in cleaning procedures, also re-circulate waste as new materials may compromise food safety and subsequently another decade.

It was estimated that almost 80% of the economic costs of a product are defined during the product design phase. There is potential to reduce environmental and economic costs, and accordingly improve sustainability, by assessing products proactively before their manufacture. Preventive approach is desired instead of using a reactive approach to minimize impacts of a product already designed (Cooper and Chew 1996, DG Enterprise & Industry and DG Energy European Commission 2014, McAloney and Bee 2009, Garcia-Garcia et al. 2020).

At the global level of food system, general measures should be taken such as: searching the nutritional profile of material resources and water for the industry; re-engineering the technological processes in the sense of efficiency and sustainability; recovery waste and by-products with high added value; optimizing consumption in terms of quantity and quality, ensuring a sustainable eating behavior.

Food safety and security are two complementary elements of our resilience and for better and sustainable future. In the near future, it will be necessary to approach the situation by taking into consideration the One Health concept (Boqvist et al. 2018).

Several foodborne diseases are zoonotic, which means that animals and people diseases can be transmitted. Some pathogens can change their host, or their geographical location and even their impact, and therefore we have to deal with new and emerging diseases (for example, bovine spongiform encephal or new strains of highly pathogenic avian influenza). A population may be affected by the emerging diseases that have the ability to trigger pandemics. Problems about production and consumption of food, related and linked with food safety include food allergies, non-communicable diseases like obesity, food bioterrorism, antimicrobial drug resistance and food waste.

Emerging and re-emerging foodborne pathogens are continuously changing the epidemiology of foodborne diseases.
