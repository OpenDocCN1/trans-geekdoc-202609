# 2. Technological Enablers for Smart Food Logistics

# 2.1 Robots

Robots and automation represent major elements of the Industry 4.0 vision and are widely used across several industrial sectors. Robotics and automation are utilized to create devices and equipment that operate with little or no human intervention (Vallandingham et al. 2018). Robotic technology aspires to alleviate and eventually eliminate strenuous or unsafe human labor while contributing to productivity (Duck et al. 2018). While robotics is the process of developing and using robots to perform certain jobs, automation is the use of different types of mechanized technology. Agri-food systems have considerably considered benefits from incorporating robotics in activities such as monitoring (e.g., monitoring of crops by drones), weeding, precision agriculture, disaster management. In food logistics, robotics encompasses several application areas. Operationally, robots were primarily employed for packing and palletizing (Khan et al. 2019). Incorporating robotics and automation in food makes it possible to monitor, track and trace products through the supply chain. This is especially critical for products that need careful monitoring and handling conditions (Pal and Kant 2019). The significant advantage of robotics in logistics is the optimization of products’ handling and transportation. Robotics applications provide more efficient material movement and tracking throughout the logistics processes.

From a sustainability perspective, robotics could be utilized to increase productivity, reduce costs, and boost production volume to complete some tasks. The high accuracy with automation minimizes defects and waste in food logistics (Duong et al. 2020). Robotics and automation can also reduce human error and help with consistent product quality and aid in adherence to production standards and regulatory compliance, leading to consumer satisfaction and more demands for the products. Similarly, automation could improve operational flexibility and help to adapt and meet changing consumer needs (Echelmeyer et al. 2008). Additionally, robots enable businesses to protect workers from dangerous working conditions. Typically, these environments require workers to load and unload heavy products, standing in production and assembly lines for long periods to feel physical strain, or utilizing equipment in unpleasant conditions. Robots could be used to retrieve, move, sort, package and distribute items between warehouses and production lines (Karabović et al. 2015).
