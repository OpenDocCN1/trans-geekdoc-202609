# 4. Mealworm and Food Safety

Mealworm is commonly reported to infest grain products and lower quality products (Vignerer et al. 2019). Many studies were documented methods to get rid of this pest from grain products as essential oil. The oil was reported to possess insecticidal activity against mealworm larvae. For example, Szoglya et al (2014) claimed that Thuja occidentalis and Tanacetum vulgare essential oil could work against lesser mealworm, Albophilus diopter. Other essential oils such as Origanum vulgare and Artemiaiculata also had inhibitory activity against mealworm (Szczepanek et al. 2018). In addition, entomopathogenic fungi such as Metarhizium anisopliae be used as a biocontrol agent against the lesser mealworm (Gindin et al. 2009). Other safety issues in using mealworms as an alternative source for animals and humans are antibiotic residues (Osimani et al. 2018), heavy metals and pesticide residues (Poma et al. 2017), allergens (Nebbia et al. 2019) and toxin compounds (Poma et al. 2017). Antibiotic-resistant microorganisms are a primary issue in food production for human uses. The incidence of antibiotic resistance among pathogenic microorganisms posed a threat to public health and the environment. The study of Osimani et al (2018) revealed that high antibiotic resistance was reported among laboratory reared mealworms. However, no antibiotic resistance were detected in the feed given to the mealworm. The study concluded that antibiotic resistance could happen naturally in the mealworms. # 5. Mealworm in Animal Nutrition

Many small-scale mealworm farming has been carried out to demand from the market to feed pet companions such as birds, mice, reptiles, and amphibians. Mealworm is available in live, frozen and dried mealworms can be consumed directly, whereas powdered mealworms is used as feed additives and protein replacement in animal feed formulation. Hence, mealworm farming becomes an industry where many people earn a living. Therefore, the impact of mealworms on animal nutrition, economic and social, is huge. This section will discuss and summarize the roles and impacts of mealworms for animal production, namely livestock, poultry, and aquaculture.

## 5.1 Livestock

Based on the literature review survey, some of the studies only focused on the viability of mealworms in monogastric livestock, i.e., pigs and rabbits (Table 4). A recent study by Vole et al. (2021) showed that mealworm could be used as a feed additive in rabbit feed. Although containing mealworm did not improve growth performance significantly compared to the control group, the inclusion also had no adverse effects on the rabbits. Besides, it was found that mealworm can be used as a feed additive and protein replacement of fishmeal in pig feed. Instance, mealworm was utilized as a feed additive to promote growth and enhance the health of pigs, as claimed in the study of Jin et al. (2016). Moreover, studies by Meyer et al. (2020) demonstrated that the application of mealworm as a feed additive in pig feed has no effect on pigs' growth performance and health. Mealworm performed promising as replacement of fishmeal in pig feed. Yoo et al. (2019) revealed that mealworms could replace 10% of fishmeal in pig growing without any adverse effect on the pig's growth and health. Mealworm can also replace fishmeal in pig feed as high as 50% in the study of Ko et al. (2020). Another study by Ao et al. (2020) claimed that mealworms could only reduce fishmeal proportion in pig feed but cannot replace. Overall, replacing mealworms in pig feed is promising and can reduce the reliance on conventional protein sources such as fishmeal.

### Poultry

Mealworm can only be used as feed additive in poultry farming, as described in many studies in the literature. Mealworm was reported to be viable as a feed additive without any adverse effect on growth performance and health of the broiler (Biasato et al. 2019, Bovera et al. 2016, Ramos-Elorduy et al. 2002) and layer (Stnik et al. 2021). In the meantime, many studies revealed the potential of mealworms as a feed additive in poultry formulation to promote growth, increase flesh quality, enhance the immune system and stimulate disease resistance of poultry. Islam and Yang (2017) claimed that mealworm with probiotics, namely lactic acid bacteria, Lactobacillus plantarum, Saccharomyces cerevisiae can stimulate disease-resistant Ross broiler chick against Salmonella enteritidis and Escherichia coli. Furthermore, Hussain et al. (2017) revealed that mealworms could help broiler chick stimulate disease resistance to Newcastle disease. Sedgh-Gooya et al. ## 5.2 Aquaculture

The application of fishmeal in aquaculture species feed has been debated for a long time. As aquaculture is a fast-growing industry, more fishmeal is needed in the area for future (Olsen and Hasan 2012). The aquaculture industry faces its sustainability issue as the demand for alternative protein sources such as mealworms would be introduced into aquaculture feed formulation. A recent study by Tran et al. (2022) suggested that insect meat, especially mealworm, can be used as a feed additive.
