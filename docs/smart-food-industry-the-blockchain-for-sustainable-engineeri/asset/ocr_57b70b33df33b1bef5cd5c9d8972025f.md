Many factors can influence the nutritional values of mealworms, especially in dried mealworm preparation, such as temperature, storage, and many more (Zhao et al. 2016). Therefore, it is important to establish a mealworm processing method in order to maintain the quality of consistency.

Mealworm is rich in glucosamine compounds and chitosan are found in mealworms and are very useful for maintaining human health (Son et al. 2020). Both chitin and glucosamine compounds found in mealworms can be further processed through deacetylation to produce chitosan (Shin et al. 2019). At present, chitin and chitosan are widely used in the healthcare industry, as antimicrobial agents, cosmetics, wound healing, and many more (Eleih-Al-Komi and Hamblin 2016). The commercial chitin and chitosan derived from mealworms are equally similar to that of shrimp (Son et al. 2021). Thus, industry have more options in selecting the source of ingredients for chitin and chitosan production.

Many studies have reported the medical properties of mealworms. For example, Ding et al. (2021) reported that mealworm larvae extract can up-regulated expression of caspases, i.e., caspase-8 and-9, followed by activation of caspase-3 which induces apoptosis in cancer cells. Dávalos Terán et al. (2020) discovered that strategic processing of yellow mealworm proteins are able to produce dipeidyl peptidase (DPP) inhibiting peptides. This DPP can be used in diabetes treatment. Other medical properties of mealworms were been reported in the study of Song et al. (2018). The study claimed that mealworm whole body, blood and excretion are good for chitin and chitosan sources. The extension study of Shin et al. (2019) showed that chitosan extracted from mealworm possesses antimicrobial activities against Bacillus cereus, Lister monocytogenes, Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus.
