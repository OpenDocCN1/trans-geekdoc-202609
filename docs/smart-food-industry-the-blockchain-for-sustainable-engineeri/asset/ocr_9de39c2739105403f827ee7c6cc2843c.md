As research on grains gains pace, several information gaps will need to be filled. With regards to energy and carbohydrate supply, systematic characterization is urgently required from natural populations and those incorporated in the mainstream food systems. Such will be largely enhanced by the food processing methods and post harvesting handling procedures applied on the superficies. More research is needed in this area to include how indigenous communities have been utilizing processing methods to obtain energy from crops, seed storage and production systems. There is scope for innovations in the development of products from pseudocereals and their promotion into the food value chains.

Under the agriculture for health research area, questions such as the bioavailability of nutrient elements in pseudocereals superfoods and their use across age groups and gender will be. This information could be important in determining the products and dishes which adults and children can consume. Minimum daily consumption rates will also need to be determined for healthy diets.

# 3.1.2 Supercereals for Readily Available

Much of the world's population relies on plant-based protein for their nutrition and wellbeing (Nadatur et al. 2016). A sharp increase in animal-protein rich diets was closely related to the growth in the global economy, arguments supporting plant-based diets seem to be winning respect over debates on systems that are sensitive and environmentally protected (Pardey et al. 2014, Santé and Soret 2014). In developing countries, the current increase in animal meat products is being driven by fast-paced urbanization trends and related improvement in per capita income of educated young population involved in economic activities (Sharma et al. 2019). However, over-night, the number of undernourished people is on the increase (> 800 million) and malnutrition counts for over 30% of children under the age of 5 years. There is evidence of large-scale shortages in protein-rich food sources in several communities leading to stunted growth or poor child-development.
