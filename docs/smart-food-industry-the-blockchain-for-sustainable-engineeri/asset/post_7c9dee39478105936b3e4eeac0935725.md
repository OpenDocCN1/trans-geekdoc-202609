## Mitigating and adapting to climate change

There are several key components that are indicators of the importance of mitigating and adapting to climate change in all value chains and in all sectors (Giuntoli et al. 2020).

- 1. Indicators related to climate change mitigation efforts, for example: carbon balancing in bioeconomy sectors.
- 2. Indicators related to adaptation to climate change, in natural ecosystems through the specific management actions (for example: species/plant selection as the function of future environmental constraints).
- 3. Referring to resilience and adaptation in the built environment if it involves a biomass component (for example: urban trees, green roofs, etc.).

## 2.2 Sectors in Bioeconomy

The European Commission lists the sectors that make up the entire bioeconomy. The sectors are considered directly related to the Nomenclature Statistique des activités économiques dans la Communauté européenne (NACE system).

Bioeconomy industry can be broadly divided into three distinct types of economic activities (Kardung 2019):

- 1. Natural resource-based activities that directly utilize the biological resources (agriculture, forestry, fisheries) and provide biomass as the input to other industries.
- 2. Conventional activities to further process biomass from food, feed, tobacco, beverages, and wood products, textiles, apparel, leather, paper and pulp,
- 3. New activities to further process biomass and/or residues from food, feed, tobacco, beverages, and wood products, textiles, apparel, leather, paper and pulp, furniture or to use processing residues from biorefineries, biofuels, chemical base, bio-plastics, and biogases.

## 3. Food and Agriculture

Food is a basic need, therefore its history is as long as the human life. At first, the human need for food was met only by taking the food around him. The increase in human population resulted in insufficient food supply in nature, and is agricultural activities began. Agriculture is defined as the activity of managing natural resources with the help of technology, capital, labor, and management to produce agricultural commodities that include plants and animals that are useful for meeting human needs. Furthermore, agriculture only discussed how to produce plants and animals. The definition of agriculture is developing, as stated by Zocca et al. (2018) that agriculture includes a very broad sense, and refers to almost all the instincts of human life as the ability to satisfy hunger and the sustainability of species.

To fulfill hunger and species, agriculture focuses on food commodities, although in the end other commodities are needed by humans. In other words, can be said that human life can continue if agricultural activities exist. The success of agriculture is indicated by the world freedom from hunger as stated as one of the SDGs, namely zero hunger.

At the beginning, hunger was simply stated as insufficient to meet the needs for human life, but the concept of hunger later developed into the concept of food security. The concept of food security continues to develop. Starting from only focusing on the side of food production and consumer purchasing for available food, it then developed the consumption side and quality of life. The more comprehensive concept of food security is in line with the agricultural objective by FAO in 2015 that agriculture does not only grow crops but also grows healthy and nutritious food to create a well-nourished population. The definition of good resilience is not only providing sufficient food for the community but also how humans can obtain food for a healthy life. Globally, FAO in 2003 stated that food security consisted of four pillars, namely supply, accessibility, utilization, and stability.
