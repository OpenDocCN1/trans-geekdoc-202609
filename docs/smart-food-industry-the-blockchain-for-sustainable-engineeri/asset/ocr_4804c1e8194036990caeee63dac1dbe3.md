Process simulation tools have been used for decades in the chemical and petrochemical industry, where small operational improvements translate to significant operating cost savings. However, low profit margins, short lead times, the ever-increasing palate of products, the demand for timely production and delivery due to the perishable nature of most foods are some of the factors that force the food industry into adopting more modern digital technologies in the spirit of Industry 4.0 initiative (Kouroulis et al. 2021). In order to increase product quality, minimize costs and ensure delivery of orders, it is necessary to optimize the food process both at the design and at the operational level; process simulation can play a major role towards achieving these objectives.

The implementation of process simulation in the food industry depends on the type of processing and product characteristics. According to Maroudis and Saravanan (2000), food processing plants can be divided into three types: (1) Food Processing Plants that utilize raw materials and ingredient preservation, (2) Food Ingredient Manufacturing Plants that utilize commodity products or by-products to produce food ingredients, and (3) Food Product Manufacturing Plants that produce a multitude of packaged food products ready for consumer use. By their, food ingredient manufacturing plants are much closer to traditional chemical processing utilizing many unit operations and producing large quantities of bulk products; therefore, these types of industries are more apt to adopting process simulation tools. An ever-growing subset of ingredient manufacturing plants category is composed of the food waste valorization industries that utilize waste as inputs in order to produce a multitude of useful end products.

Food waste valorization is one of the most important areas in the food processing and production research.
