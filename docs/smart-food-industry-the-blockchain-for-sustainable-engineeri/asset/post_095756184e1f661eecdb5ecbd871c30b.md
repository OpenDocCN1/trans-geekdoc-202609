## 1. Introduction

The bioeconomy is the sustainable use of biological resources to produce food, energy, industrial products, and services. It is an approach that seeks to balance economic growth with social equity and environmental sustainability. According to the FAO (2018), estimates that food demand will increase by 60% by 2050. This trend is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which aim to address global challenges such as poverty, inequality, and environmental degradation. The bioeconomy is a key component of achieving these goals, as it focuses on the sustainable use of natural resources.

This new approach in the field seeks to examine the application of innovation and its combination with. Activities such as research and innovation, coherent policy and setting bioeconomy strategies at the country and regional level as well as international and cross-sector cooperation are indispensable for bioeconomic development. Bioeconomic priorities seek to achieve economic growth on traditional and emerging industries. The improvement will be embodied through the creation of new value chains based on the biological resources.

### Ensuring food and nutrition security

The four pillars of food security from FAO are availability, access, utilization, and stability.

- Availability: including the indicators regarding food resource supply, food production and storage.
- Access: including the concept of physical and economic access to food.
- Utilization: indicators for the sufficient food quality and quantity
- Stability: indicators of the stability of the above components

To ensure the food and nutrition security, the four components above must be available in such a way that none of the factors affect the others. For example, an individual may have adequate economic status but may be at risk of becoming food insecure if an event reduces the availability of food resources or limits physical access.
