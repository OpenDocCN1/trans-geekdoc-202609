### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1 Why is "Plant-based" Important?

A plant-based diet is defined as an eating pattern dominated by fresh or minimally processed plant foods, vegetables, whole grain, legumes, nuts, seeds, herbs and excludes animal products including red meat, poultry, fish, eggs, and dairy. According to a definition by Ostfeld (2017), it consists of... Over the few years, there has been a tremendous consumer interest in reducing meat consumption and choosing plant-based. According to a report by the Good Food Institute, the retail market for plant-based foods was $7 Billion in 2020, from $5.5 billion in 2019 (Good Food Institute 2021). Furthermore, plant-based diet (i.e., plant meat, cheese, milk, yogurt, etc.) grew 2 times faster than overall sales and 3 times the rate of animal-based. The number and the biggest reason motivating the consumers to move toward plant-based is an increased awareness about the benefits of these foods on health (Aschemann-Witzel and Peschel 2019). There are numerous studies showing the positive correlation between high consumption of plant based foods and decreased risk of cardiovascular diseases and certain cancers (Massera et al. 2016). The second driver is the moral and ethical concerns related to the climate and resource impact of animal-based products. Plant-based diets are known to be more sustainable they use fewer natural resources and, therefore, are less taxing on the environment (Aschemann-Witzer 2020). According to some studies, the land required to produce soy proteins is 6-17 times less than land required to produce animal protein. Similarly, the ratio of water used in production of soy protein with the same quantity of animal protein is 4 to 26 (Sabate and Sorte 2014). There are other factors driving the shift points toward plant-based diets which include taste and sensory variety.
