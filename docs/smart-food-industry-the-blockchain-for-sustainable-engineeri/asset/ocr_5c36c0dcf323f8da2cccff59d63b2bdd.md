# 1.2 Sources of Plant-based Food Ingredients

Fruits, vegetables, grains, and plant-based protein sources are the building blocks of any plant-based diet. Plant-based proteins are mainly derived from sources such as legumes which include beans, peas, and lentils. Among all plant-based proteins, soybean is the largest source of plant-protein and is considered to a complete protein with all the essential amino acids with a nutritional and biological roughly equivalent to that of animal protein (Watanabe et al. 2018). Additionally, pea protein is a relatively new type of plant protein obtained from leguminous crop of field pea. Compared to soybean or other proteins, pea protein is characterized by its high digestibility and relatively less allergic response (Zhao et al. 2020). Moreover, it has a well-balanced amino acid profile, with high level of lysine and is widely available at a low cost. Recently, proteins derived from hemp (Cannabis sativa.) are gaining popularity in the food industry. Hemp protein is difficult to grow and due to various governmental regulations on it, it can be costly for suppliers and manufacturers.

Some other sources of plant-based proteins include grains such as rice and wheat. Even though rice is not a rich source of protein, some of the hydrolyates of rice processing such as rice bran, endosperm and broken rice kernels can serve as a cheap source of plant-protein. # 1.3 Plant-based Products and Brands in the Market

As a growing food category, plant-based foods and ingredients have penetrated many food sectors, including dairy, meat, seafood, and egg alternatives. According to the report from Markets and Markets, the dairy market was valued at 22.6 billion in 2020 and is projected to reach 40.6 billion by 2026. Plant-based dairy categories in aggregate grew by 24% in the U.S.; plant protein-based milk accounts for 15% of all dollar sales of U.S. retail milk (Spins and GFI). Plant-based meat grew 45% of all dollar sales in 2019. Over 290 million units of plant-based meat were sold for retail packaged market and approximately 1.4% of all dollar sales for total retail meat. In 2020, plant-based seafood sales accounts for less than 1% of dollar sales, compared to the conventional U.S. seafood market worth tens of dollars. The sales ($27 million) of plant-based egg grew by 168% in the past year, which experienced the greatest dollar sales growth.
