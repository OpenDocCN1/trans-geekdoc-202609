# 2.4 Plant-based Ice Cream

Recently, there has been a significant shift in consumers’ interest towards “better-for-you”, functional, and non-dairy ice creams. A negative perception of dairy-based desserts like ice cream has motivated manufacturers to launch plant-based versions of their dairy ice creams to get consumers’ attention. The brands of ice creams with lower fat and sugar contents and labeled as a good source of protein were observed to have strong growth in the market (Sipple et al. 2022).

The first step in making plant-based ice cream is to prepare an ice cream by blending solids and liquid ingredients to form a hydro own dispersion. This ice cream is further whipped to incorporate it into an aqueous matrix. Some of the ingredients used in the ice cream formulation are fats, solids-nonfat, sweeteners, stabilizers, emulsifiers, water, and flavors. The ingredients used, such as the solids-nonfat, are the main source of protein. The continuous phase is comprised of concentrated and unfocused sugar solution, whereas the dispersed phase contains tiny, flocculated fats and globules with some air surrounding them. These bubbles are surrounded by proteins and emulsifiers forming a complex multiphase system (Kot et al. 2021; Abraham et al. 2020; Romulo et al. 2021).

Macolecules such as fats, proteins, and carbohydrates play an important role in the texture and flavor of plant-based ice cream. Fats improve the melting resistance and recrystallization by stabilizing the ice base and increasing the level of fat aggregation. They also a key contributor to improving mouthfeel which is a desirable attribute in ice cream (Huyönen et al. 2003). Dairy plant-based products that exhibit good stability and gelation properties are desirable for this application. Plant-based proteins can form a thicker interfacial layer at oil/water interfaces compared to dairy products because of their larger molecular size and structure through which disulfide crosslinks (Le Roux et al. 2020). Sources such as fruits, tubers, legumes, and cereals are widely used in plant-based ice cream production, of pea, soy, and coconut are very familiar. The addition of sweeteners not gives desirable taste but also affects the solids’ content to contribute to improving the viscosity and texture of the ice cream. The addition of sweeteners also reduces the freezing point and allows large crystal formation during storage. Stabilizers aid in improving the viscosity and delay the rate of meltdown thus increasing the stability of ice cream during temperature fluctuations, whereas emulsifiers help water and fat to hold together during recrystallization of ice cream storage (Kot et al. 2021; Romulo et al. 2021).

# 2.5 Plant-based Butter

Consumers’ interest in plant-based butter and spreads has increased considerably in the past few years. Some of the common plant-based butter and spreads that are widely available in the market are made from nuts or seeds such as peanuts, almonds, pumpkin seeds, pistachios, cashews, soy, sunflower and sesame seeds. In the mentioned sources, manufacturers’ addition was drawn to chickpeas as they have their rich protein content. These plant-based products are referred to as butter if it contains at least 90% of nut/legume as ingredients, whereas they are referred to as spreads if the product has at least 40% of nut/legume as ingredients (Gerpai et al. 2015).

The industrial production of plant-based butter involves basic steps such as roasting the nuts with blending ingredients. Various ingredients such as the sugars, salts, stabilizers, and emulsifiers are added to improve the flavor profile and stability of the product during storage. The addition of stabilizers prevents the phase separation in plant-based butter during storage, while the addition of anti-oxidation agents in the product formulation is recommended when higher amounts of unsaturated oil are present in the plant-based source.
