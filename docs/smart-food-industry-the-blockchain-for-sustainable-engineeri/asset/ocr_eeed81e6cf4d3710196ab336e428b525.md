# 2.7 Cherry Waste and By-products

The rapid increase in the production rate of cherry products has caused the formation of significant amounts of cherry waste, from industrial processes (Bagheri et al. 2021). The most common and important species of cherry is Prunus avium L. known as sweet cherry and Prunus cerasus L. known as sour cherry (Yilmaz et al. 2019). Most of sweet cherries are consumed fresh, while sour cherries (approximately 85%) are generally consumed after processing into food such as fruit juice, jam, puree, concentrate, frozen, dried or canned fruit, marmalade, and jelly (u). Recently, many studies revealed that cherry by-products are plentiful sources of bioactive compounds such as phenolic acids and flavonoids. These compounds have a high antioxidant and other health promoting properties (anti-inflammatory, anticancer, anti-diabetic activity). Additionally, these compounds are mainly taking in cherry skin, contributing sensory and organoleptic properties of cherry pomace from liquor industry. Mauricio et al. (2018) investigated the recovery potential of cherry pomace and revealed that cherry pomace can be converted into extracts having healthy-and promoting properties that can be used as functional ingredients in food, nutraceutical and cosmetic industries.

# 2.8 Grape Waste and By-products

Three-quarter of the grape pomace is utilized into wine-making which is globally around 27 billion liters on annual basis. Grape pomace is mostly discarded from wine industry (nearly 1200 tons per year), consists of skin, stem, pulp, and seeds which are equal to 25% of total weight (Beres et al. 2017). Phenolic compounds such as anthocyanins, hydroxybenzoic and hydroxycinnamic acids, flavonoids, and flavon-3-ols are abundant present in grape pomace. Additionally, malvidin, petunidine, cyanidin, peonidin and delphinidin are located in grape skin which are responsible for characteristic red color. Currently, grape seed oil is a commercialized product used in food and cosmetics formulations. Studies indicate that potential for further valorization attempts thanks to their unique bioactive components (Bordiga et al. 2019).

# 2.9 Mango Waste and By-products

Mango is the most-produced tropical fruit in the world, exceeding 55 million tons production rate in last two years (Manhobo et al. 2021b). A considerable quantity of processing wastes (350-600 kg/ton) of mango mostly consists of peel and seed (Banieer et al. 2018). Especially, mango peels can be used in the formulation of biodegradable packaging materials thanks to high polysaccharide content. Accordingly, Torres-Leon et al. (2018b) observed that the biofilms prepared with mango peel showed good barrier properties and high water permeability.

# 2.10 Avocado Waste and By-products

The avocado processing industries such as guacamole, avocado pulp and oil produce large amounts of waste and by-products, mostly the pulp, seeds and skin (Castillo-Llamas et al. 2021). Nevertheless, these by-products are a significant source of extractable bioactive molecules, which could be used in the formulation of functional foods or cosmetic products. In particular, pulp of avocado (nearly 15% of the fruit) is mainly discarded from the extraction process of avocado oil.
