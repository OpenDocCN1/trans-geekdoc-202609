Celiac disease: Celiac disease is also known as gluten-dependent enteropathy. It is an autoimmune condition resulting in intestinal injury caused by the consumption of dietary gluten present in the endosperm of some cereals, namely barley, rice, and wheat (Webster-Garty et al. 2020). The only confirmed treatment for celiac disease is consumption of gluten-free diets; however, gluten-free diets are deficient in other nutrients. Thus, pseudo cereals such as amaranth, buckwheat, millets and quinoa which have high nutritional profile are considered candidate crops for a stop dietary option for celiac patients (Caeiro et al. 2022).

Diabetes: This is a chronic metabolic disorder responsive for elevated levels of blood glucose. This condition could occur when there is deficiency of insulin, a regulatory hormone for blood sugar level in the body (Type 1 diabetes). There could also be a situation in which the body is unable to effectively utilize the insulin due to obesity and is referred to as type 2 diabetes. While type 1 requires intake of insulin, type 2 requires a special diet for the patients to help in weight loss. Amaranth flours have better flavor than of wheat, as they are not only nutritious, but very useful in regulation of the blood sugar levels such that sugar is released into the blood stream in a slower manner, thus preventing weight gain. Quinoa, another pseudoreal with a glycemic index of 53 (lower than the benchmark of 55), is a choice crop for diabetic patients because the protein and fiber helps slow down the rate of digestion. Thus, Amaranth and quinoa are anti-oxidation and anti-diabetic help (Tang and Tsao 2017).
