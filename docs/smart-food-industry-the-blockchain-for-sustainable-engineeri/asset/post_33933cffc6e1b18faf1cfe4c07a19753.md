Let me say what is positive about veggie meat. In the first place, regarding a concern with protein consumption, most veggie meat alternatives on the market today provide adequate amounts of proteins. Bohrer (2019: passim) explained the nutritional contents in several meat products, such as burger, beef meatballs, pork ham, chicken nuggets, veggie meat products. The study found that the protein content in animal-based products and veggie meat products are approximately the same. However, the advantage of veggie meat products is that they have less cholesterol and more dietary fiber than traditional meat products. Furthermore, veggie meat products are suitable to consumers who cannot eat meat products, who, due to religious beliefs cannot eat certain meats (e.g., halal and kosher), and, obviously, veggie meats are convenient to vegetarians and others who refuse to eat animals for ethical reasons—although it is quite peculiar that vegetarians refuse to eat animal flesh but these substances that taste like it.

Regarding the environment, once again, whether veggie meat alternatives have a lower environmental impact is not entirely clear—although virtually researchers are of, even veggie meat products have some negative environmental impacts, meat is much, much worse for the environment. For example, a study conducted by the University of Michigan detected that veggie burgers have a significantly lower environmental impact than traditional burgers. Specifically, Beyond Burger uses 99 percent less water, 93 percent less energy. Also, veggie Burger uses 90 percent fewer greenhouse gases than animal flesh burgers (Heller et al. 2018). However, it is important to mention that this study is just not completely unbiased as it was sponsored by the company Beyond Meat. Moreover, consider that the production of veggie meats, unlike traditional meats, does not involve manure pollution (Santo et al. 2020, Ernst et al. 2019).

A significant aspect of the environmental implications of veggie meats is that, in the end, our food production has negative environmental impacts, and this includes veggie meat alternatives. Farming involves tilling, which destroys soil carbon. The preparing for soy foods destroys the soil. A process releases carbon in the atmosphere. Thus, the growing of industrial crops contributes too much carbon in the atmosphere (Haddaway et al. 2017). Now, of course I am not suggesting that farming food should be stopped. But the question is whether if the world substituted all its meat with veggie meat, global land use will be reduced. It would be interesting to know whether producing veggie meats would require more land use than if the world adopted whole food, unprocessed, veggie diets (devoids of veggie meats).

- We analyse options for reducing the environmental effects of the food system, including dietary changes, more plant-based diets, improvements in technologies and management, and reductions in food loss and waste. Surprisingly, the researchers do not conclude that the most environmentally diet is veganism.
