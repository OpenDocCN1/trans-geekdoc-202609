The majority of studies, have been carried out in stirred tank bioreactors for a variety of adherent cell types (Hewitt et al. 2011, Chen et al. 2011, Badenes et al. 2015, Gupta et al. 2016, Rafiq et al. 2017, 2018, Hanga et al. 2020, 2021). It is also worth mentioning that the microcarriers cultures of mesenchymal/stem cells and satellites in stirred tanks have been highly successful (Molnar et al. 1997, Torgan et al. 2000, Hewitt et al. 2011, Rafiq et al. 2013, Hervy et al. 2014, Heatman et al. 2015, Nienow et al. 2016, Rafiq et al. 2016, Lawson et al. 2017, Rafiq et al. 2017, Verbruggen et al. 2018, Rafiq et al. 2018, Hanga et al. 2020, 2021).

Advantages of microcarrier based systems include the ability to hold adherent cells in suspension, promoting even distribution of media and gases through the mixing action of the impeller and a very straightforward scale up process. However, there is a difficult culture strategy for the carrier surface with many uncontrolled variables. Common challenges include uneven cell distribution on the carrier surface due to preferential adhesion during cell seeding (bead to bead phenomenon), limited capacity of some types of cells to populate newly adhered and hydrodynamic forces acting on the carrier/cell surface during aggregation. All the above can cause cell proliferation and negatively impact (Wu 1999) if minim minim through controlled approaches. For example, uneven cell distribution can be minimized through the control of aggregation in the bioreactor vessel. Hanga et al. (2020) used an aggregation strategy showing improvement in the bead-to-bead transfer to date successfully demonstrated with some cell types including human MSCs (Desoure et al. 2016, Takahashi et al. 2017, Rafiq et al. 2018), Verou and Yang (1999, Yang et al. 2019), HEK293 (Yang et al. 2019) and more recently bovine-derived stem cells (Hanga et al. 2021).

Bead-to-bead is a manufacturing approach that facilitates process intensification, resulting in increased cell yields and reduced process time. However, the choice of time point for addition of microcarriers is critical to initiate successful bead-to-bead transfer at certain levels of aggregation could enable the cells capacity to transfer to microcarriers (Hanga et al. 2021). The other limitation of hydrodynamic forces could be addressed by porous microcarriers as they can absorb the hydrodynamic forces on the cells during aggregation, since the cells grow mostly within the carrier rather than on the surface; however, harvesting of the cells from porous carriers is more challenging (Yang and Yang 2011).

For the production of cultivated meat products, any of the commercially available microcarriers could be used. However, there are several concerns, particularly regarding downstream polystyrene-microcarriers, as validation of their removal during downstream processing steps is important to ensure the safety of these products. Ideally, the microcarriers to be used should be edible which can be easily broken down to release the cells, eliminating all aspects of dissociation and separation de-facto. Ideally, the building blocks of the degraded microcarrier itself should also be food-grade substances with no need to eliminate them from the product. Tuneable degradation rates have been seen to enhance MSC spread and differentiation capacity due to the more dynamic microenvironment (Toh et al. 2012). It may be possible to use tunable degradation as a micropatterning technique, selecting and tuning degradation rates of certain regions on the microcarrier to form a topography that can guide cell adhesion, fusion and eventually muscle fibre formation (Zhao et al. 2009).

Another option could potentially enhance the nutritional and textural profile of the cultivated meat product. A similar type of microcarrier made of pectin (a compound found in fruits) has been previously commercialised, but now discontinued. The Corning dissolvable microcarrier has been used successfully to culture and harvest iPSCs (Rodrues et al. 2018) and MSCs (Kalra et al. 2019) which are both possible starting sources for cultivated meat production.
