I am particularly interested in the virtue of temperance, as Aristotle argues, it encompasses the relevant virtue in the context of sensory and physical appetites. Temperance encompasses a rational and harmonious approach to pleasure, which of course includes food pleasure. A temperate individual is not indiscriminately attracted by food just because it may emit a pleasant aroma or may have a certain texture or flavor. But because such characteristics are not conducive to good health or potentially deleterious. As the saying goes, “I do not live to eat but I eat to live”. In other words, the primary purpose of eating for a heterotrophic organism is to acquire for various activities and growth from food. To be sure, this does not mean that a virtuous person should derive no pleasure at all from eating. The pleasure of eating has physiological and psychological importance, which is evident as early as infants’ feeding at their mother’s breast.

The problem in affluent societies is the excess amount and the variety of food available, hyperpalatable food (that can create psychological addiction), all of which are reasons for the decline of human health. Cooked and ultra-processed substances cause the body to release chemicals that trigger a sense of reward, which often leads to over. This is one of the reasons why it is virtually impossible to resist fruit and salad. The temperate individual will consume food for the purpose of nourishing without adding. Moreover, she will choose food that is psychologically and physically rewarding. Veggie meat alternatives are hyperpalatable substances. There is an obvious danger regarding hyperpalatable substances because they are specifically designed to override one’s ability to control the amount consumed. Some of the most egregious characteristics of such substances are very high count of calories in rather small portions, high count of fats, sodium, and refined sugar. Gearhardt et al. suggest, “Foods, particularly palatable demonstrate similarities with addictive drugs”. These researchers show that consuming hyperpalatable foods are capable of “triggering an addictive process” (Gearhardt et al., 2019).

The obvious danger of ultra-processed, hyperpalatable substances is food addiction, which leads to a number of mental problems (Blumenthal and Gold 2010, Melroy et al. 2009). It is an important point to distinguish habits from addictions. Addictions not merely to indicate a habit, but a much stronger psychophysical condition suggesting a disease for which one is not responsible.

A temperate individual consumes only food that is vitally essential and is conducive to good health and ultimately to her flourishing. This analysis seems to fit Aristotle’s definition of the temperate individual who eats substances in moderation, as Aristotle argues, “as long as they are not incompatible with health or vigor, contrary to what is, or beyond his means” (Aristotle 2002). It is clear from the foregoing analysis that veggie meat alternatives are not the type of food substances that are compatible with temperance. The temperate individual is emotionally and physically trained to so the right toward food. This enables the virtuous temperate to make the right choices and enjoying eating foods that are healthy and good food that is potentially unhealthy and addictive.
