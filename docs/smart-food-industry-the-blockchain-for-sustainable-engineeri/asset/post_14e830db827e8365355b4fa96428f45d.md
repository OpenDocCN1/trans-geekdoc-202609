## 3.2 Sustainable Valorization Options

Recycling of waste provides a significant amount of economic gain as well as environmental. The economic gain from recycling of waste products may increase more with factors such as recycling and reuse of waste, minimizing losses, and increasing the efficiency of resources (Bec et al., 2019). Bread is the most important grain product that could damage natural resources such as water, soil, and energy used during production and transportation; in addition to the loss of raw materials, it caused an economic loss of 400 million euros in the Netherlands, a situation even worse in developed countries. For example, in the UK, 20 million slices of bread are wasted every day, resulting in 84 tons of CO2 equivalent (Natiraj et al., 2021). In India, farmers lose 16% of their crop to waste, burning 60% of rice straw. The burning of rice straw causes 3300 kg CO2-equivalent emissions per hectare, soil pollution and decrease in biodiversity. The economic expenditure for bread disposal was reported as $46/ha, and environmental loss as $92/ha. Moreover, the value of bioethanol from straw is $65/ha, saving fossil fuel (Bhattacharjee et al., 2022).

The cereals are incredibly useful in food ingredients, but the use of cereals in formulations provides an alternative both in terms of environmental sustainability and in terms of producing low-cost alternatives. The inclusion of different levels of rice waste in feed significantly reduced the cost per kg. The cost of feed without adding any rice waste was $5.1/kg, while consuming 30% rice added reduced costs to $1.5/kg (Muhammad et al., 2008). In wheat processing, consumer interest in functional products with high dietary fiber has started to attract attention in recent years due to their beneficial effects such as the prevention of chronic diseases. The World Health Organization (WHO) and FAO recommend a daily intake of 25-30 grams of dietary fiber for humans. Therefore, researchers and developers in the bakery industry have begun to create new types of food formulations. Considering this, studies on the development of fiber-enriched-based products are increasing day by day. For these reasons, knowledge of the nutritional content of grain products and their wastes has been used as new raw materials in the production.

Microfluidized corn was used to develop high-fiber wheat bread with a new strategy. Production of high fiber bread was achieved by replacing 20% of flour with microfluidized corn. In addition, bread developed with corn had a smaller loaf volume and improved textural properties such as firmness, springiness and cohesiveness, compared to white bread (Erive et al., 2020). Concentrated dephytinized wheat and rice exhibited higher fiber content in the product, and concentrated grains decreased the softness, cohesiveness and resilience, while significantly increasing its hardness (Ökaya et al., 2018). In addition to the textural properties, the nutritional content of value-added bread is also very important in the search for new cereal-based sources in bread production. showed showed replacing 20% of wheat with rice provides maximum protein, ash, fiber, K, and Mg, but the addition of 50% defatted rice has the highest quality for valorization on all chemical and sensory properties (Aimal et al., 2006). It has been reported that sourdough fermentation improves some sensory properties of wheat grain. Wheat breads developed using fermented milling-byproducts (wheat grain and bran) showed better protein density, nutritional indices and starch hydrolysis, as well as improved chemical, functional and sensory properties of the bread compared to the use of wheat. Obtained bread with higher fiber content and a lower glycemic index value (Pontonio et al., 2017). Mikusová et al. (2013) enriched wheat and rice breads with wheat bran (10%), cereal β-glucan (12.5%) and a lactobacilli starter culture consumption of functional bread resulted in lower glucose levels in healthy men compared to control-group bread characteristics. Some processes such as extrusion, fermentation and enzymatic hydrolysis can be applied to improve the rheological properties of bread and dough enriched with wheat corn. The addition of 15 mg/kg glucose oxidase and 30 mg/kg hexose oxidase was the most effective doses in improving the bread and dough properties (Güil et al., 2009).

Pasta is one of the fastest foods which is very popular in the current lifestyle of all the world, with low glycemic index. Pasta, which is a universal food, can be used to provide adequate fiber support as a result of the increase in health awareness and consumer demands. For this reason, the production of functional pasta with food wastes has gained momentum. Since cereals such as wheat, rice, bran and legumes also have high dietary fiber and protein content, they are used in pasta production to increase the functional properties of durum wheat semolina. Depending on the texture and concentration of grain bran, adding fiber-based sources to pasta has been reported to improve the color, brightness, water absorption, and volume expansion values (Kaur et al., 2012). In a study, enriched pasta was produced by replacing 40% and 50% of semolina with treated wheat-heated bread offered approximately 5.2 times more dietary fiber, while the samples processed bread had lower cooking quality (Sudha et al., 2011).

Turkish noodles are one of the traditional cereal products in Turkey that are obtained by mixing the ingredients, the shearing, the dough and drying the noodles (Tukuk, 2017). The addition of infused stabilized rice bran increased the nutritional content of Turkish noodles such as crude fat, protein, dietary fiber, vitamins and minerals, while decreasing the textural properties such as adhesiveness, cohesiveness, springiness, and gumminess (Tukuk et al.). In other study, noodle and pasta formulations were enriched with depleted cereal bran (20%) of rice, wheat, and oat to enhance the nutritional quality. Demonstrated that the bread improved the calcium, phosphorus, magnesium and mineral levels, while rice increased potassium, and wheat improved maize, magnesium and iron contents in bread and noodle properties (Levent et al., 2020).

High fat intake has adverse effects on health such as cardiovascular diseases, diabetes and obesity. Therefore, the production of functional pasta with food wastes has gained momentum. Since cereals such as wheat, rice, bran and legumes also have high dietary fiber and protein content, they are used in pasta production to increase the functional properties of durum wheat semolina. Depending on the texture and concentration of grain bran, adding fiber-based sources to pasta has been reported to improve the color, brightness, water absorption, and volume expansion values (Kaur et al., 2012).
