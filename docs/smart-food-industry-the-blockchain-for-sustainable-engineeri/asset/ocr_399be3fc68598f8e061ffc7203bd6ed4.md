According Van Hu (2016), food safety is of utmost importance when dealing with food sources. In the context of insects, there are four ways through which food safety risks can arise: (1) the insect itself could be toxic; (2) the insect could have acquired toxins or human pathogens from its environment during its life cycle; (3) the insect could become spoiled after harvest; (4) consumers could experience an allergic reaction to the insect.

# 5. Production and Processing

Insect production can play a significant role in the future, particularly when the circular economy concept is applied, insects can be raised in sustainable organic systems by recycling and cost-effectively transforming agricultural and industrial by-products and/ into proteins, promoting food safety and minimizing climate change and biodiversity loss, contributing to the SDGs (Orsi et al. 2019, Morozzo et al. 2021).

Insects can be bred to provide an indirect source of food for humans; and fish, to support the growth of other foods for animals such as pork, poultry, and fishensis (mushroom also known as the ‘fungus caterpillar’ that parasitizes moth larvae through the larva stage) (Nawowski et al. 2021).

Various researchers have noted for the creation of insects as ‘mini livestock’ because insects have a more sustainable creation to the animal protein production and are suitable for different locations, both in natural artificial environments; reached in days; high density growth (animal weight); greater efficiency in meat production (12 to 15 times more than conventional animals); greater use of meat, less food and feed water; use of agricultural waste as slaughter blood and waste; less land; lower GHG emissions (out); reduced water pollution (mainly in use for slaughter and processing); lower methane production (only cockroaches, termites, and beetles produce CH4—a result of bacterial fermentation by Methanobacteriaceae in the intestine); cheaper and simpler processors (Abbasi et al. 2016, Sun-Waterhouse et al. 2016, House 2018, Orsi et al. 2019, Patel et al. 2019, Raheem et al. 2019, FAO 2021b, Lner 2021, Patel et al. 2021). Sustainable insect breeding can also contribute to using marine resources to reduce overfishing by replacing oil and fish meat in animal food (Morozzo et al. 2021).
