From an economic point of view, it is reported that the avocado oil market size reached 484.6 million USD in 2020 (Solarte-Toro et al. 2021). Permal et al. (2020) investigated the utilization potential of waste (peel, stone, pomace, and wastewater) discarded during commercial pressed avocado oil process. They found that wastewater had significantly higher lipid content compared to seed, starch. Furthermore, wastewater powder in pork sausage formulation, additionally showed the inhibition of lipid oxidation. Avocado seeds contain 30% starch, making the seeds an alternative natural starch source. Additionally, extracted avocado seed can be considered as a new source of raw material for the production of bioethanol. In a recent study, the production of industrial-grade starch from avocado seeds have been investigated (Tesfaye et al. 2021). Authors found that avocado are technically and economically feasible for industrial-grade starch with 56% yield. Also, the selling potential of industrial-grade avocado seed starch of $0.8375/kg indicated the feasibility. Sánchez et al. (2021) produced biodegradable films from avocado seeds. They easily extracted starch and cellulose having high solubility, low water absorption and low swelling power, which affects the formation of biodegradable films. Similarly, Jimenez et al. (2021) stated production from avocado wastes was feasible thanks to high polymer such as starch and pectin.
