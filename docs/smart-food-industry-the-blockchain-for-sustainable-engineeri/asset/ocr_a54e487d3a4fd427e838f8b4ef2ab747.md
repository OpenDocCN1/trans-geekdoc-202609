#  4. Mealworm and Food Safety

Mealworm is commonly reported to infest grain products and lower quality products (Vignerer et al. 2019). Many studies were documented methods to get rid of this pest from grain products as essential oil. The oil was reported to possess insecticidal activity against mealworm larvae. For example, Szoglya et al (2014) claimed that Thuja occidentalis and Tanacetum vulgare essential oil could work against lesser mealworm, Albophilus diopter. Other essential oils such as Origanum vulgare and Artemiaiculata also had inhibitory activity against mealworm (Szczepanek et al. 2018). In addition, entomopathogenic fungi such as Metarhizium anisopliae be used as a biocontrol agent against the lesser mealworm (Gindin et al. 2009). Other safety issues in using mealworms as an alternative source for animals and humans are antibiotic residues (Osimani et al. 2018), heavy metals and pesticide residues (Poma et al. 2017), allergens (Nebbia et al. 2019) and toxin compounds (Poma et al. 2017). Antibiotic-resistant microorganisms are a primary issue in food production for human uses. The incidence of antibiotic resistance among pathogenic microorganisms posed a threat to public health and the environment. The study of Osimani et al (2018) revealed that high antibiotic resistance was reported among laboratory reared mealworms. However, no antibiotic resistance were detected in the feed given to the mealworm. The study concluded that antibiotic resistance could happen naturally in the mealworms.
