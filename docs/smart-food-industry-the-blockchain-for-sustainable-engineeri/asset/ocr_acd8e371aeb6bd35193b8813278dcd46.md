Food safety should also be addressed from the perspective of food consumption, e.g., changes generated by the choice of diet. High protein consumption, such as meat-diet, leads to the use of land areas for livestock compared to the areas used for agricultural crops. Human diets undiverse and environmental sustainability and have the potential to feed. Traditional diets (usually rich in herbal foods) have passed a "Western food model", characterized by high calorie consumption, very processed foods (refined carbohydrates, added sugars, sodium and unhealthy fats) along with negative effects on human health associated with this nutritional transition, this dietary model is also unsustainable. Current food production already threatens climate change, biodiversity loss, drastic pollution and changes in water and use (Williet et al. 2019, Harvard School of Public Health).

More than 4 billion people depend on three types of crops—rice, corn and wheat. These three cultures provide us with two-thirds of our energy consumption. Although there are over 50,000 edible plants, only a few hundred species contribute to intake.

In 2050, we will need to feed three more people than a century ago. The choices we make in terms of production and consumption of food already have direct or indirect consequences, result we use of water and soil, and also the ability to feed and have a decent living. Climate change also puts pressure on food safety, and some countries feel the pressure more than others. Drought, fires or floods directly make production capacity. Unfortunately, climate change often affects factors that are more vulnerable and may not have the methods of adaptation.

According to Our Data publication, the food industry is responsible for 26% of total carbon emissions in a year. It is hard to believe that this industry can cause a quarter of greenhouse gases. If we analyze the whole process at the microscopic level, starting with production and ending with product distribution to the customer, we will notice that there are many parts involved in this process. From this point of view, each entity is responsible for a certain percentage of the total 26% (Riche and Rerer 2020). The food industry contributes to:

+   - global warming more than all machines, planes and trains take together;
- eutrophication of aquatic media due to pesticides and fertilizers;
- accelerated loss of biodiversity;
- deforestation and desertification.

Achieving a level that responds to our needs, from a natural resource base already exhausted, will be impossible without profound changes in our food and agricultural systems. As food production systems turn to adapt to current conditions, it is necessary to consider the impact on food safety to assess the optimal ways of approaching potential risks. As a consequence, agriculture is confronted with unprecedented pressure that cause changes in the food industry (cultures, animals, fisheries, and aquaculture). The industrial agri-food systems involve the extension of arable land and increased yields per hectare by intensive use of agricultural fertilizers, the development and widespread use of select plant hybrids, breeds and genetically modified organisms and production concentration (agricultural sectors and far farmers). The impact—on the environment, society and human health—of this system proves to be more and more devastated. The consequences are measured in terms of air pollution and groundwater, soil degradation, ocean acidification, reduction of energy resources, loss of biological and cultural biodiversity to ecosystems (EEA 2020).
