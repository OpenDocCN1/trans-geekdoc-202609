*Arthroplaster plantensis, Chlorlla vulgaris, Dunaliella salina, Haematococcus pluvialis, Cryptococdenii and Nitzschia lavia* are the main strains commercially used for production of microal-based ingredients (Souza et al. 2019).

In this context, microal have great potential to meet the needs of the population, generating interest in these microorganisms due to their nutritional composition as well as the presence of bioactive compounds, physiologically active substances with functional properties at the metabolic level. These biocounds, such as polysaccharides, sterols, pigments (carotenoids, chlorophylls), volatile organic compounds, polyphenols, vitamins, minerals, fatty acids, and amino acids (proteins/enzymes) are changing their status in field of microal research, and gaining space in relevant research for the application of pharmacological, nutraceutical and functional products, holding countless opportunities for the development of innovative and sustainable products in the food industries. These microal biomolecules have shown essential activities at the level of health regulation and disease prevention, as antioxidant, antitumor, anti-inflammatory, antiviral and antimicrobial activity, among other important potentialities (Jacob-Lopez et al. 2019, Wu et al. 2021, Nürnberg et al. 2022).

#  3.1 Proteins

The great advantage of microal proteins lies in the constitution of amino acids in their structures, as they have a complete profile of amino acids that cannot be synthesized by the human body, requiring consumption through food. For Koyande et al. 2019), these essential compounds are histidine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, and valine. Other amino acids such as arginine, glycine, cysteine, proline, tyrosine, and aspartic acid also appear in the protein fraction of some species (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020).

Some microal have a high percentage of protein in their dry biomass (40–70%). Some *Spirulina* are reported to have up to 70% in their biomass, one of the richest sources of protein in nature. Examples of different microal with the potential to obtain proteins include Aphanomonas flaq-aquae (60%), Chlorella pyrenoidosa (55%), Dunaliella salina (55%), and Scenedesmus oblique (55%), which is also superior to other plant sources like soy (38%) or animal sources like milk (4%), and eggs (13%) (Souza et al. 2019, He et al. 2020, Torres-Tiji et al. 2020, Boukid et al. 2021).

#  3.2 Lipids

Lipids are indispensable components of cells and precursors of many essential elements. Adequate intake is crucial for human nutrition. Some species can accumulate up to 70% of lipids in dry biomass (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020). However, in average terms most species accumulate levels of 5 to 15% (Barkia et al. 2019).

The lipid profile of microal consists mainly of triacylglycerol (Tang et al. 2020). Microalacylglycerols are produced by a mixture of unsaturated fatty acids, such as palmitoleic (16:1) and oleic (18:1), polyunsaturated, such as linoleic (18:2) and linolenic (18:3), and saturated fatty acids, such as palmitic (16:0) and stearic (18:0) (Souza et al. 2019).
