## 6 Dairy and Egg Waste and By-products

Egg and dairy products have an important role in daily diet by providing the energy and nutrients needed by the human body. Especially, milk and dairy products are valuable nutritional sources of calcium and protein (Kart and Demirian 2014). The types of dairy products vary from country to country depending on cultural and social factors, the amount of production and consumption varies according to consumer demand, population and nutritional habits. Dairy products can be categorized as cheese, butter, cream, ice-cream and yogurt. According to 2019, 883 million tons of milk was produced worldwide, 715 million tons of which was consumed by approximately 232 million people (FAOSTAT). According to 2018, approximately 35 million tons of dairy products were produced in the world, where cheese is the most produced dairy product with an annual rate of 11.7 million tons (FAOSTAT 2018). Egg is the most consumed protein source after meat. Apart from direct consumption, products from eggs include egg white, egg powder, egg white, liquid white and liquid yolk. Egg is also widely used in the production of various ready-to-eat foods and pastries in powder or dehydrated form (Gosh et al. 2016). Additionally, egg white has become popular among youngsters due to its high protein content. In order to meet the increasing demands, egg production has also increased worldwide since 1990 (55 million tons), and reached 88 million tons in 2019. China is the leading country in egg-production with an annual rate of 28.8 million tons (FAOSTAT 2019).

The- and waste streams generated in the production of milk and dairy products vary depending on the product being used: Buttermilk is a by-product of butter production while whey is discarded in cheese production (Yırım ve Güzeler 2013). These by-products having valuable nutrients in the cheese industry can be utilized in value-added products or as raw material in the animal feed industries. In addition, more than 6 liters of water is generated for 1 liter of milk processed (Ahad et al. 2019). Although the eggshell is edible, it is considered as waste since it is not generally consumed (Rehaud-Godbert et al. 2019). Considering that a large egg weighs 60 g, 5.7 g is the eggshell, 37.8 grams is egg white and 16.5 grams is egg yolk, eggshells make up 9–12% of the egg weight, indicating that out of the 83 million tons of eggs produced in 2019, approximately 8.5 million tons of eggshell was produced (Waheed et al. 2020b). The solid wastes, which are directly released to the environment, damage natural resources by causing environmental pollution. The wastewater generated in dairy production can pollute drinking waters due to improper waste management. Studies and applications have revealed that dairy and egg-products can be utilized in different applications instead of disposal practices (Abdulrahman et al. 2014). In order to protect the environment and ensure sustainability, wastes should be properly disposed of or recycled. In particular, converting egg and dairy wastes, which constitute more than 26% of food waste in the world, should become a priority for the food industry (Ganesh et al. 2021, Abdulrahman et al. 2014).
