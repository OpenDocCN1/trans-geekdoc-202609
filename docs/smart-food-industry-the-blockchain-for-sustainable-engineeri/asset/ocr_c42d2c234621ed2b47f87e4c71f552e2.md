Generally, in low moisture extrusion process, the feed moisture content is between 10–30% and it usually occurs with a short retention time (5–15 seconds) and high temperature (100–200°C). Depending on extrusion process parameters, feed moisture content, physical characteristics of the viscoelastic mass, and the properties of extruded material can vary. TVP products have been made using many different plant protein sources such as soybeans, rapeseed, and peas (Riaz 2011). It has been shown that process parameters have a significant impact on extruder responses and final product characteristics, including but not limited to specific mechanical energy, textural characteristics, sensory attributes, solubility, quality, and structure in low-moisture extrusion cooking (Riaz 2004, Yu et al. 2013).

In a recent study, fortification of rice starch with pea protein and pea fiber was achieved by low moisture extrusion process and effects of blending behaviors, different speed levels, temperatures and feed moisture contents on expansion behaviors, and microstructures of the extrates were investigated (Beck et al. 2018). According to results, processing parameters have a significant impact on the physical characteristics of the final extrates. It was shown, that up to 25% pea protein isolate and 16% pea fiber can enhance the expansion relative to the control group, the expansion is significantly lowered above these inclusion levels of pea protein and fiber.

In another study, the use of rice protein isolate producing low moisture meat analogue was investigated (Lee et al. 2022). Rice protein isolate and soy protein isolate were blended at different levels (25:75, 50:50, 75:25, and 100:0) and extruded at low moisture level using a screw extruder for manufacturing a meat analogue. The physicochemical analysis results showed that replacing soy protein isolate with rice protein isolate resulted in a decrease in physical parameters as specific mechanical energy, porosity and water absorption capacity, the nutritional quality of meat increased significantly with the addition of rice protein isolate. It was also shown that the total soluble matter of meat analogues from the combination of soy protein and rice protein isolates were lower than that of the control group made from dehydrated soy flour. This was explained by the hydrophobicity, formation of aggregated bodies and disulfide cross-linking of glutenin. Because low moisture meat analogues often require the addition of a liquid before consumption, it was suggested that TVPs with lower content could be better in maintaining the nutritional quality after the rehydration.
