Rice (*Oryza sativa L.*) is one of the most important grain products, which is a significant source in the diet of nearly a quarter of the world population (Alexandri et al. 2020). The process steps of milled rice, known as white rice, includes harvesting, cleaning, husking, polishing and grading (Fig. 7). During these steps, some waste products such as bran, germ and straw may be formed (Alexandri et al. 2020). The husk is the outer layer of the grain and is not not, and is removed during the de-husking process. Rice bran is a wasted product discarded in milling process, and is obtained from the outer layer of rice. On average, by-product fraction of rice is 20% rice bran, 2% germ and 8% bran. In addition, 0.4–1.4 kg of straw is released for every kilogram of milled rice (Alexandri et al. 2020, Papageorgiou and Skendi 2018).

Cere such as wheat, corn and rice are rich sources of bioactive compounds (especially ferulic and coumar acid). The amount of phenolic acids in bran is reported to be 10–20 times higher than the endosperm. Ferulic acid constitutes 60–70% of the phenolic acids found in grains, and is mostly in the cell walls of the aleuron and pericarp layers (Hromádová et al. 2008, Zhao et al. 2009). Zhao et al.
