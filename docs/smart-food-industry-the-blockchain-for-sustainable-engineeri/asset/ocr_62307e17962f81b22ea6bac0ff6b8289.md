The formulation of the equations is based on the mathematical models of all processes in the software library. The solution of the entire flows is done using a sequential-modular, it procedures are solved sequentially using the material flow. If recycle loops exist, the tear-stream is implemented; using an iterative solver, the calculations are required until the user-defined criteria are satisfied.

In this study, an annual production of approximately 5111 metric tons (MT) of pectin, the calculated amounts of raw materials are showed in Table 2.

With respect to utility requirements as calculated by the energy balances, the plant operations are expected to recover about 450,000 MT/yr of steam, 45,100 MT/yr of cooling water and 16,900,000 kWh/yr of electricity. It should be noted that the recovery and recycling of water from many stages in the process (e.g., condensate streams, from the reverse osmosis), the water requirement is large (114.7 kg per kg of pectin produced). However, it would be much larger (~30 kg per kg of pectin) if water recycling was not implemented. Similarly, the isopropanol is kept because of its recovery and reuse (0.02 kg of IPA per kg of pectin produced instead of 22 kg in the absence of recycling). This recycling, comes at a price: because of the extra equipment needed (e.g., distillation column), the cost of utilities and requirements are increased.

In this model, utility consumption is reduced somewhat by implementing heat integration with the use of several heat exchangers in the process. Despite the use of these units, the process still requires 88 kg of steam and 9 MT of water for every kg of pectin produced. It would be possible to reduce the consumption (and their associated costs) further through more extensive energy integration. In general, optimizing a process with respect to energy and water integration is an important activity in process design with a potentially great impact on the economics and the environmental footprint of the process.
