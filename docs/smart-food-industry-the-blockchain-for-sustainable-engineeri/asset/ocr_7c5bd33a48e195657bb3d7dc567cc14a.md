# 6.3 Improve the Production and usage of Plant Protein Ingredients

Meeting the food demands of global population within limited planetary resource is a challenge. Henchon et al. (2021) have informed the development of pathways to the sustainable production by analyzing the trends in animal-sourced foods, including the significance of “animal-sourced protein to plant-protein sources”. The ratio of animal-sourced to plant protein varies across regions, ranging from 0.29 in Africa to 1.08 in Europe in 2017. Therefore, to deal with the challenge of sustainable livestock production and consumption, the importance of planning regional specific strategies should be highlighted. The challenge is not merely for science and technology but one much based on wider factors of the food system and its diverse stakeholders.

Plant proteins from different crops show quality difference, such as soybean, pea, wheat, rice, lentils,, peanuts, sunflower, pumpkin, and seeds. Volatile properties and quality of raw materials cause trouble. That is why breeding and crop development are challenging for plant protein supplier. Figure 10 shows some suppliers worldwide.

A single type of plant protein is difficult to provide satisfactory functional performance. It is crucial to seek low-cost plant protein sources and create novel combinations with other ingredients for the production of plant-based meat, egg, milk, and seafood analogues. Researchers are mapping the rheological properties (strain, stress, and elasticity) of dense plant protein blends. An interesting phenomenon on the market is that meat—plant protein blends meets the flexitarian’s need. The traditional meat brands have begun to blend meat with plant-based analogues while promoting these “hybrid meat” products as more sustainable and healthier.
