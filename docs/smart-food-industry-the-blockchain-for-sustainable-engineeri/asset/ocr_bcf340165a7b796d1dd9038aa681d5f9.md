Portable devices opened the door to a more democratic and sustainable Analytical Chemistry. The miniaturization combined with increased sensitivity of detector makes portable detectors more reliable. They can be more reachable and widespread (including less developed countries), thus contributing to SDGs in a more responsive way (Chem et al. 2019). The smartphone that nowadays carry in their pocket can be used as a detector, as Böck et al. in depicted (Böck et al. 2020). For example, Gu et al. (2013) developed a highly sustainable method using a very low-cost detection using a smartphone camera with open image processing software and novel green solvents like Natural Deep Eutectic Solvents (NADES). This method allowed antioxidant capacity in NADES media, which are naturally formed within the cells by metabolism, and they play a crucial role in the production, transport, storage and change of compounds phenols.

“Green Chemistry” and “Green and Sustainable Analytical Chemistry”, it is evident from the discussion above how they can contribute to the improvement of the Development Goals (SDGs). In the field of Sustainable Food Science, the SDG (zero hunger) is particularly important. It requires food production systems that are efficient, sustainable and able to meet the different dimensions of food security: availability, global food access, stability and stability over time. Biorefinery processes increase the potential of food security, as powerful food production approach found in the circular biobased economy, for large-scale production of high-quality foods, since this improved utilization of biological resources by particular value. Green Analytical Chemistry principles and Green Chemistry principles are closely correlated as well. Their implementation of these principles in Food Science not only promotes food security and zero-hunger (SDG 2) accomplishment but also helps to strengthen other SDGs, which can improve clean water (SD6), health and welfare (SD3), clean-energy production and consumption (SD7), industrial innovation and infrastructure (SD9), promoting the sustainable cities and communities’ development (SDG11), as well as responsible consumption and production (SDG12) (Ballesteros-Vivas et al. 2021).
