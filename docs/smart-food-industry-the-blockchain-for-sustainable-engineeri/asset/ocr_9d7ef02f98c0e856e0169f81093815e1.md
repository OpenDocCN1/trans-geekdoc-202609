et al. 2021. In addition, it has been reported that the rice bran oil is used in cosmetics to improve skin defects caused by melanin, and as a sunscreen in cosmetic formulations due to its high antioxidant activity (Lerma-García et al. 2009).

Studies on the antiviral, antibacterial, antifungal and antioxidant effects of cereal-based wastes and by-products have gained momentum in recent years. It has been reported that wheatgrass has antidepressant, diuretic and anti-inflammatory effects while the maize grain is used as a bioactive substance carrier in the human intestine (Sielcka-Różyska and Giazdowska 2020, Skendi et al. 2020). Cereal wastes and by-products are also used in the treatment of acne and chronic diseases, thanks to the bioactive compounds. Corn is known for its high antioxidant properties and highly phenolic, glycoside and anthocyanin contents (Doan et al. 2020). In addition, the parts of cereals in the left after harvest can be used as animal feed due to their low-cost applicability and rapidly changing nutritional habit of animals. Quality residue of cereals are used in the feed of animals such as pigs, ducks and cattle, as they provide high energy and protein (Ajila et al. 2012). Specifically wheat, barley and oat cereal wastes are frequently used in animal feed compositions.

# 4. Oil Waste and By-products

More than 200 oil crops are grown worldwide, which can be divided into two main groups: edible oil sources used for direct consumption, and inedible oil sources used in the production of oleochemicals. The most important oils in the food processing industry are soybean (Glycine max), rapeseed (Brassica napus L.), sesame (Sesamum indicum L.), sunflower (Helianthus annual L.), coconut (Cocos nucifera L.) and olive (Olea europaea L.) (El-Hamidi and Zaher 2018, Kotecka-Majchrzak et al. 2020). According to USDA (2021), major oil production in worldwide increased from 580 million in 2019/2020 to 603 million tons in 2020/2021 season with a growing rate of 3.9%, soybeans being the primary type of oil with 366 million tons of production. Rapeseed is the second produced oil with a production of 73 million tons, while peanut rises with 50 million tons (Fig. 8). Refined oil from oilseeds are widely used in the manufacture of margarine, confectionery, baked goods and products. On the other hand, unfilled cold pressed oils are considered a pro-health ingredient to enhance the flavor of salad, sauce and pastes (Kotecka-Majchrzak et al. 2020). It has been stated that the oilseed wastes produced during processing constitute 20% of total oil wastes generated worldwide (Otles et al. 2015).
