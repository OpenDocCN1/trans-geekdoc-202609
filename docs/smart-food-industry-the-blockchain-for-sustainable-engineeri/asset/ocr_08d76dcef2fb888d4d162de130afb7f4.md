Studies have reported success in printing using inks with G' values in the range of 100 Pa to 10 Pa, for mouthfeel properties (Wang et al. 2019, Yang et al. 2018, Lib et al. 2018).

The pseudoplastic materials usually behave in a non-Newtonian way. Pseudoplastic foods present a reduction of shear viscosity (Fe et al. 2019). Pseudoplastic foods are ideal for 3D printing as the decrease in viscosity allows easier extrusion into the mouthpiece. Upon completion of printing, the printed food can recover to a state that maintains structural integrity (Fe et al. 2019, Jiang et al. 2019). Pseudoplastic foods that have been successfully printed include baking dough, mashed potatoes, avocado, and yam (Wang et al. 2018a, Liu et al. 2018, Fe et al. 2019, Hamilton et al. 2018). Researchers have reported successful 3D printing with viscosities ranging from 1000 to 10 Pa, corresponding shear rates of 0.1 in 500 s⁻¹ (Hamilton et al. 2018, Lib et al. 2018, Yang et al. 2018). The vast range of values demonstrate that shear printing applied directly to 3D food result in a variety of successfully printed materials.

Chen et al. (2019) evaluated the effect of the rheological properties of different inks on 3D printing by hot extrusion. The authors observed that the storage modulus of inks is essential to the various layers used during 3D printing and to maintain their shape. The results indicated that 15-25% wt at 80°C, cassava starch of 20-25 wt% at 75°C, and potato starch of 15-20 wt% at 70°C bears G' (150-6909 Pa) values, which were preferable for 3D printing with great quality, resolutions, and shape quality. In addition, the flow behavior influenced the smoothness of the food extrusion, ensuring uniformity and resolution of the printed objects.

Liu et al. (2018) investigated the rheological properties of 3D printing at different concentrations (i.e., 1, 2, 3, and 4). The authors concluded that highly desirable materials for 3D food printing not only have an adequate modulus of elasticity (G') to hold the printed shape but also proper properties to be easily extruded from the nozzle in an extrusion-based type.
