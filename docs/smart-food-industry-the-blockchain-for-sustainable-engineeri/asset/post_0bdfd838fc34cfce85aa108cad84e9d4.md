# Food Safety in the Sustainable Food Industry

Ioana Mihela Balan, Tiberiu Iancu, Nastasia Belc, Isidora Radulov, Camelia Tulcan and Teodor Ioan Trasca

## 1. Food safety in circular economy

Scientific knowledge and technology development—both related to food safety—have grown considerably in the last centuries with direct consequences in the significant decrease of foodborne disease (FBD) in developed countries. Nowadays, people are expecting that all the food they buy is safe. Unfortunately, there are still several cases of large FBD outbreaks caused by vegetable or animal origin. Food safety affects worldwide and will remain a global challenge for human health in the future. Due to this large food safety issue, a huge quantity of food is being taken from the market and destroyed. In the future, with the rapid growth of the world’s population, the food systems will face a very high demand for food and our duty is to minimize the waste which occurs along the food chain, during processing when it is about food safety issues.

For a sustainable development and a healthy population, food safety is one of the most relevant factors, being an integral part of food and nutrition security-deification.

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) set up in 2015 by the United Nations Assembly call for numerous actions. Ending famine, improving nutrition and encouraging sustainable food systems act to attain security. Food industry plays an important role in achieving SDGs such as: SDG2–Zero Hunger, SDG3–Good Health and Well-Being, SDG12–Responsible Production and Consumption and SDG13–Climate Action.

The food industry affects the quality of the environment. Water depletion and use directly influence climate change. The European Union’s Research and Innovation Policy Food 2030, launched in 2016, aims to change the food systems and to ensure that everyone has nutritious food for a healthy life.

# Food Safety in the Sustainable Food Industry 219

Between the pathways for action, developed by the Food 2030 Research and Innovation Policy for sustainable and water food systems, of them—Alternative proteins and diet shift; Food waste and resource efficiency; Healthy, personalized nutrition and Food safety systems of the future are very related with safety and circularity (European Commission 2020a).

The Standing Committee on Agricultural Research (SCAR) Foresight Expert Group led the 5th SCAR Foresight Exercise which was focused on the question: "Whether to reach a safe and just operating space" for society, by better management of agricultural food systems and resources? The question comes from policy research among the SDGs, the 21st session of the Conference of Parties (COP21) to the United Nations Framework on Climate Change (UNFCCC), the Green Deal initiative and the European Commission (EC) "Farm to Fork" strategy for healthy, environmentally friendly food systems. Three main goals or three transition-related priorities were identified: sustainable and healthy diets for all a society, diversifying resilient bio-economy as a road to sustainability (European Commission 2020b).

In order to feed a growing number of people and to increase food production, ensuring a healthy and regenerative environment, food systems should take into account CO2 emissions, energy and water consumption, food and waste.

In terms of food safety, new challenges arise among food systems due to the increase of microbial resistance, changes in raw materials (waste become new materials), and producing new products. A better resource efficiency of food systems can impact food safety and subsequently another decade.

It was estimated that almost 80% of the economic costs of a product are defined during the product design phase. There is potential to reduce environmental and economic costs, and accordingly improve sustainability, by assessing products proactively before their manufacture. Preventive approach is desired instead of using a reactive approach to minimize impacts of a product already designed (Cooper and Chew 1996, DG Enterprise & Industry and DG Energy 2014, McAloney and Bee 2009, Garcia-Garcia et al. 2020).

At the global level of food system, general measures should be taken such as: searching the nutritional profile of material resources and water for the industry; re-engineering the technological processes in the sense of efficiency and sustainability; recovery waste and by-products with high added value; optimizing consumption in terms of quantity and quality, ensuring a sustainable eating behavior.

Emerging and re-emerging foodborne pathogens are continuously changing the epidemiology of foodborne diseases.
