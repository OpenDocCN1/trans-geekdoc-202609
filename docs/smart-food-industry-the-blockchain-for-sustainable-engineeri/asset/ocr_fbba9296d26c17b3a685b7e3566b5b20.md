While the plant-based alternative products are designed to provide the sensory, flavor, and nutritional attributes of the original product, egg alternatives are developed for dual purposes. Egg alternatives are developed to meet consumers' expectations to be used as an ingredient in the food applications such as bakery, creams, mayonnaise, and pasta in addition to being able to make omelets, scrambled and folded eggs. Identifying plant proteins that have similar functional properties as egg protein is a challenge.

Plant proteins with emulsifying and foaming properties to stabilize oil-in-water emulsions are desirable for applications such as salad dressings, mayonnaise, sauces, etc. Soy protein concentrate and milk are some of the suitable sources that can be used for these applications. In addition to soy, a few other plant-based protein sources such as sunflower, tomato seed, wheat, lupin, and bean were also successful in these product applications. Stabilizers such as xthan gum and guar gum are commonly used in these product applications to stabilize the emulsions (Nkcade et al. 2012).

Similarly, plant proteins with gelling properties similar to egg are desirable for lipid-based applications. However, denaturation temperatures of plant proteins are often higher when compared with egg proteins (63-93°C). Therefore, to obtain the desirable final texture, plant proteins often have to be heated at higher temperatures or for a longer period (Grossmann and McClements 2021). Soy, however, the literature suggests that off-flavors like beans or green are observed when soy or pea are used as plant protein sources for the presence of sapalone, aldehyde, and ketone compounds (Söderberg 2013). Other ingredients that are used to formulate the liquid plant-based eggs are stabilizers, emulsified oils, pH modifiers, preservatives, and colorant. The addition of carotenoids to emulsified oil enhances functional attributes of lipoproteins in eggs giving plant-based egg products their desirable mouthfeel and texture. Stabilizers in the formulation increase the viscosity of the plant-based egg gel delaying separation during storage. Curcumins and carotenoids are preferred as natural coloring pigments to give plant-based egg its characteristic yellowish color (McClements and Grossmann 2021, Alcorta et al. 2021).

Although with the nutritional profile of egg can be challenging when formulating plant-based egg alternatives, choosing plant protein or plant protein blends with desirable functionality and amino acid profile will result in a successful product.
