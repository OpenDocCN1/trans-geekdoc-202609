Rapeseed meal is a valuable source with high bioactive compounds such as tocopherols, B vitamins, calcium, zinc, magnesium and copper (Sydzkowska-Cerniak et al. 2010). The most abundant phenolic compounds in rapeseed meal are tannins and tannic acids, accounting for 80% of the total phenolic content. Depending on the variety and processing method of the oil, the phenolic content of rapeseed meal may vary between 6.40 and 18.4 mg/g dry matter (Zago et al.2015). The high levels of anti-nutrient compounds have limited the use of rapeseed meal, but as an animal feed, and a functional additive with balanced amino acid compound has been extensively explored (Sydzkowska-Cerniak et al. 2010).

Sunflower seed cake is a by-product of the sunflower oil industry. It is used in the cattle feed due to its lower nutrient compared to rapeseed and soybean cake. Sunflower seed cake contains approximately 1.5–2.5% oil, and this oil can be used as a lubricant to speed up processes in the extruder (Ayrrık et al. 2013). Sunflower seed cake proteins have been reported to exhibit antioxidant antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-hypertensive properties. Considering their nutritional potential, researchers have started to work on the use of sunflower cake in promoting functional foods (Chauhan 2021). With cottonseed and canola meal, sunflower seed meal has also been used as an alternative to soybean meal (Liu et al. 2015).

Palm kernel cake (PKC) is a by-product of oil extraction from palm fruit. Palm kernel is rich in saturated fatty acids, especially lauric acid, followed by myristic and palmitic acid (Oliveiro et al. 2018). In addition, PKC has a higher content (16–18%) and a high calcium/phosphorus ratio.
