# 7.3 Complementarity without Competition

Pseudocereals play an important role in the performance of cropping systems, for instance, when they are grown together in intercrops or in rotation with cereal crops. It is evident that current cropping systems need to evolve in one few crop species that are beneficial to human health and are of ecosystem services that ensure sustainable production. If cropping systems could be designed to increase diversification of food products and hence nutrient and nutritional sources, healthier diets will be easier to develop and at international level. Such cropping systems could contribute to the challenges faced in communities where sharp test of food products are driving markets. Developing value additions which includes cereals, legumes and pseudocereals is an area with great potential for scientific discovery.

Unlike previous production models, where a few crops were promoted at the expense of the rest, there is now an urgent need for consensus on a global food mix constituted by major and minor crop to promote healthy diets. The examples of dietary diseases and solutions emanating from superfoods, the food quality improvements demonstrated by mixtures of minor and major crops, e.g., use of chia seeds in wheat bread, all to the point of a shift in mindset and promotion of a range of crop types. Complementarity between pseudocereals and other crop types leads to practical solutions to dietary human health issues and hence the need to reduce negative competition in production of nutritious crops.

# 8. Conclusions

The majority of prominent pseudocereals qualify as superfoods as they provide high nutrient content and healthy foods for human body. Pseudocereals are good sources of energy and starch, proteins, vitamins and minerals and anti-inflammatory compounds. Though underutilized, pseudocereals can complement mainstream crops such as rice, wheat in reducing hunger burden in many communities. Superior quality gluten free foods can be provided to persons with healthy conditions if barriers to production of pseudocereals are removed. Similarly, food-based supplements and minerals can be delivered to the needing blindness will be lower with increased availability of healthy crop based foods.
