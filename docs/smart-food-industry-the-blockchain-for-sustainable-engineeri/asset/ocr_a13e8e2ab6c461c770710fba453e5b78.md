# 4. Discussion and Conclusion

We have shown that smart policy can be well understood as a governance heuristic for dealing with complex issues across a range of topics, such as climate change, health digitalisation and SDGs. We have also shown that smart policy claims a multi-actor approach to deal with these challenges, and there is a need to develop skills and access to advanced instrumental tools and innovations for designing, implementing and evaluating policy.

First, we argue that capacity-building at the socio-cyber-physical system level and at the policymaking level is crucial for the overall performance of the food system to become more efficient, effective and sustainable. In particular, we need policy capacity to the specific resources, instruments and abilities that governments have for implementing smart food policy. We put forward the need (i) to increase resources for digitalisation and future-oriented tools, e.g., for foresight strategies; (ii) that governmental actors acquire appropriate skills for translating complex system modelling and; (iii) of deliberative processes and anticipatory knowledge in the food system towards sustainability. We recognize that abilities and resources linked to engaging social actors in continuous evaluations are crucial for understanding and tackling grand challenges. Moreover, this type of knowledge needs deliberation in wide stakeholders platforms for social learning. This type of reflexive knowledge needs to be coupled with the policy capacity to increase the overall capacity for smart food policy-making (Kugelberg et al. 2021).

We concluded that a sustainable food system is related to its overall capacity at the socio-cyber-technical system and policy level. We argue that capacity-building efforts at these two levels help increase performance of the food system for dealing with complex issues and grand challenges. In particular, we put forward a set of resources and tools that enhance the diffusion of innovations and readiness for change, feasibility of collective envisions of long-term food system goals and desirability of courses of actions and disruptive change.
