In some studies, rice bran in hamburgers (Marvadeh and Akbari 2019), and bran in cookies (Milićević et al. 2020), rice bran in meatballs (Yilmaz 2004) and oat hull in burger (Summo et al. 2020) are among the substances with approved fat replacement ability. According to Marvadeh and Akbari (2019), adding 4% rice bran to burger caused a 25% decrease in the fat content. Researchers have proven that rice bran can be used as a fat replacer in hamburgers without loss of quality in the final product. In another study, total fatty acid content of meatballs treated with rye bran was found to be lower than the control group (Yilmaz 2004). With low fat and high beta-glucan content was obtained using a fat replacer from an oat hull-based ingredient. Beef with100% oat hull replacer presented very low fat content after cooking (3.48%) and met the daily intake of gluten (2.96 g) with one serving size (Summo et al. 2020).

Tarhana is a cereal-based fermented product. Although the recipe for tarhana for various region, the main ingredients are yogurt and flour. After adding salt, yeast and various spices and vegetables, tarhana is fermented for 1-7 days before formation. Lactic acid, acetic acid, alcohol, carbon dioxide and oxidation processes produce the specific taste, smell and aroma of tarhana We are formed during formation (Aktaz and Akin 2020, Çelik et al. 2020). The use of cereal waxes may be beneficial to produce high value-added and functional tarhana. Corn and rice grains, which are dietary fiber sources, were used in tarhana formulation in order to increase the nutritional content. The 15% rice bran showed the highest ash, protein, phenolic content, and antioxidant capacity, while the 15% corn bran showed the highest cellulose content (Aktaz and Akin 2020). Wheat bran and grain were successfully added to the tarhana formulation, and an increase was observed in crude protein, mineral and total protein content in final product Bilgici et al. 2006). Çelik et al. (2010) also reported that the fiber content of tarhana increased almost 7 times by substituting 40% wheat flour in tarhana formulation. Considering all the studies mentioned it has been confirmed that food products enriched with grain and by-products superior nutritional, functional and rheological properties. However, more studies are required to eliminate some restrictions on the production of food from cereal waxes and by-products.

In addition to the use of cereal waxes and by-products in the food industry, they can also be evaluated in different industries including packaging, fertilizer, cosmetics, chemicals, biofuel and packaging (Skendi et al. 2020). Considering the fuel demands, it will be necessary to obtain ethanol from starch-rich grains, and agricultural and food residues. However, biofuels have many advantages such as low unit cost, being environmentally friendly and with high hybrid efficiency. Cereal bran are in both starch and cellulose/ hemicellulosic contents, which significantly increase the ethanol production yield (Kumar et al. 2020, Palmarola-Adrados et al. 2005). Straw is considered as a safe raw material in the production of biofuels such as ethanol, biodiesel and biogas (Ghos et al. 2017). Packaging materials are problematic due to the depletion of non-renewable resources as well as not being destroyed in nature. Therefore, it is promising to use biopolymers from renewable resources such as agricultural waxes and by-products in packaging production. For instance, polylactic acid (PLA) packaging films can be produced by lactic acid obtained from cereal-by-products. PLA creates lower CO2 emissions and environmental footprints compared to non-renewable polymers. Since CO2 will be absorbed during the cultivation of cereal the use of these films creates less greenhouse gas emissions from (Skendi et al. 2020).
