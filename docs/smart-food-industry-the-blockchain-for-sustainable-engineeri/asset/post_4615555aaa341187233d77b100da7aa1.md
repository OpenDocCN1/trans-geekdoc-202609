The statistics on deaths from non-communicable diseases (NCDs) reported by the World Health Organization (WHO) have shown a clear relationship to nutrition, with countries accounting for a large share of the malnutrition burden. For example, about 85% of the NCD deaths occur in higher- and mid-income countries in 2016, and in these countries these have a high risk compared to counterparts in developed countries. Globally, NCDs account for 71% of the 57 million total deaths (WHO 2018b). Four leading NCDs are responsible for most deaths include cardiovascular (44%), cancers (22%), respiratory diseases (9%) and diabetes (4%) (WHO 2018). In rewards balanced diets made to focus-on risk factors and prevention of several NCDs. The latest strategies by the WHO focus on the risk factors driving mobility and mortality by NCDs, i.e., tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, and harmful use of alcohol (2013). Healthy diets and increased consumption of nutrient-rich superfoods can significantly contribute to the reduction in the mortalities in NCDs in developing countries.

Several superfoods have been recommended for the reduction of incidences, severity and in some case correct disorders or health conditions in humans. Positive reviews on the conditions and some pseudocereals seem to contribute to the reduction of NCDs. Cardiovascular, celiac, diabetes and eyesight are used as examples of the effectiveness of pseudocereals and other superfoods in dealing with prevalent cdss and health conditions.

Cardiovascular diseases: Several risk factors associated with heart diseases have been studied intensively and these include high blood pressure, and high blood cholesterol. High blood pressure and cholesterol have been controlled using plant-based products as substitutes of animal meat products. Major benefits in this substitution provide more energy and other nutrients. Pseudocereals such as (1) amaranth, (2) quinoa and (3) chia seed that contain high levels of protein have been used to supply the nutrient in non-meat diets.

- (1) Amaranth leaves an important source of protein in many communities of Africa and Asia. The amaranth leaves steamed, fried, or cooked in peanut butter consist of some dishes that are consumed together with cereals as main meals. Amaranth seed is rich source of protein and other nutrients. Amaranth is widely available in many communities its use can be improved through awareness and demonstration of the benefits in humans.

- (2) Quinoa is a rich source of quality protein and other benefits suitable for vegetable-based diets. Being rich in protein, quinoa has widely been characterized for its nine amino acids important for human health.

- (3) Chia seed is well known for its contribution to edible oils. With a protein content of 15–29%, dietary fiber 18–35% and fat content of 1–35%, chia stands out as a nutritious crop. The bioactive compounds and the nutraceutical capabilities makes its products important in the human health conditions (Katunzi-Kilewe et al. 2021).

Celiac disease: Celiac disease is also known as gluten-dependent enteropathy. It is an autoimmune condition resulting in intestinal injury caused by the consumption of dietary gluten present in the endosperm of some cereals, namely barley, rice, and wheat (Webster-Garty et al. 2020). The only confirmed treatment for celiac disease is consumption of gluten-free diets; however, gluten-free diets are deficient in other nutrients. Thus, pseudo cereals such as amaranth, buckwheat, millets and quinoa which have high nutritional profile are considered candidate crops for a stop dietary option for celiac patients (Caeiro et al. 2022).

Diabetes: This is a chronic metabolic disorder responsive for elevated levels of blood glucose. This condition could occur when there is deficiency of insulin, a regulatory hormone for blood sugar level in the body (Type 1 diabetes). There could also be a situation in which the body is unable to effectively utilize the insulin due to obesity and is referred to as type 2 diabetes. While type 1 requires intake of insulin, type 2 requires a special diet for the patients to help in weight loss. Amaranth flours have better flavor than of wheat, as they are not only nutritious, but very useful in regulation of the blood sugar levels such that sugar is released into the blood stream in a slower manner, thus preventing weight gain. Quinoa, another pseudoreal with a glycemic index of 53 (lower than the benchmark of 55), is a choice crop for diabetic patients because the protein and fiber helps slow down the rate of digestion. Thus, Amaranth and quinoa are anti-oxidation and anti-diabetic help (Tang and Tsao 2017).

Eyesight: Consumption of pseudocereals has also been found in the prevention and treatment of eye defects. For example, quinoa seeds and leaves have been used to cure a range of diseases including eye defects in several regions of the world (Lale 2017). According to Eshete et al. (2016), leaves of amaranth are used for treatment of eye defects in Ethiopia.

## 3.2.2 Vitamin and Antioxidant in Pseudocereals

Healthy diets are important for human wellbeing and child development. Unhealthy eating habits have been the major cause of wasting, increased hospitalization, and poor cognitive development in children. In adults, higher hunger during early year of development cause health conditions and exacerbate the impact of curable diseases. Micronutrients, vitamins, antioxidant, and other plant-based compounds are an essential component of healthy eating habits. However, in developing countries where cereals and proteins are in short supply, there was little emphasis on the vitamins and antioxidant for a long time. It is in recent times that community-wide programs on healthy foods for the first 1000 days have been launched with water and sanitation interventions. More is required for adults' diets in order to reduce frequencies and severity of illness. Several pseudocereals crops have been analyzed and can potentially support the daily capita requirements of vitamins, micronutrients, and antioxidants.

Vitamins, defined as chemical compounds found in food that are essential for human body building, are required in smaller quantities than calories and proteins. Similarly, micronutrients and antioxidants are essential for balanced diet absorbed in small quantities. Table 3 shows a range of vitamins that can be supplied by pseudocereals.

Both plant and animal products are reliable sources of vitamins and micronutrients for daily capita requirements. However, in many developing countries, diets do not naturally contain enough nutrients and hence fortification with vitamins and micronutrients is a common practice. Food fortification, defined as the deliberate inclusion of requisite amount of nutrient in affordable foods, has assisted in averting community wide deficiencies in vitamins and micronutrients. Common examples of fortified foods include meal from maize, salt, and wheat flour. Several beverages are also fortified with nutrients.
