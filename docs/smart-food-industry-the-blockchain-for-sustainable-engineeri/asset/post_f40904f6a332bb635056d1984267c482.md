## 2.1.1.6 IPA Recovery

The vapors from the P-70 dryer (Fig. 6) are condensed in a condenser (P-72), combined with the filtrate of the pectin centrifuge, and sent to the distillation tank (P-73) in the IPA recovery system (Figure 6). The IPA solution is then preheated in a heat exchanger (P-74) and a heater (P-75) feeds into a distillation column (P-76). The purified IPA solution, exits the top of the column with an IPA concentration of about 83% w/w, is used to treat the distillation feed in P-74. Then it is recycled back to the process via a flow adjuster (P-78), which adds fresh "makeup" IPA, and a flow distributor (P-79) shown in Fig. 5. The stillage from the bottom of the distillation column, which contains all the washed-out impurities from the pectin separation centrifuge, is cooled down to 35°C using a cooler (P-77) and removed as waste.

## 2.1.2 Simulation Results

Once the process and configuration of all input streams and the operating conditions of all processes are specified, SuperPro Designer solves the material and energy balance equations to calculate the state (chemical composition, flowrate, and condition) of all intermediate and output streams and all internal processes, the required amounts of utilities and the required size of all equipment. The formulation of the equations is based on the mathematical models of all processes in the software library. The solution of the entire flows is done using a sequential-modular, it procedures are solved sequentially using the material flow. If recycle loops exist, the tear-stream is implemented; using an iterative solver, the calculations are required until the user-defined criteria are satisfied.

In this study, an annual production of approximately 5111 metric tons (MT) of pectin, the calculated amounts of raw materials are showed in Table 2.

With respect to utility requirements as calculated by the energy balances, the plant operations are expected to recover about 450,000 MT/yr of steam, 45,100 MT/yr of cooling water and 16,900,000 kWh/yr of electricity. It should be noted that the recovery and recycling of water from many stages in the process (e.g., condensate streams, from the reverse osmosis), the water requirement is large (114.7 kg per kg of pectin produced). However, it would be much larger (~30 kg per kg of pectin) if water recycling was not implemented. Similarly, the isopropanol is kept because of its recovery and reuse (0.02 kg of IPA per kg of pectin produced instead of 22 kg in the absence of recycling). This recycling, comes at a price: because of the extra equipment needed (e.g., distillation column), the cost of utilities and requirements are increased.

In this model, utility consumption is reduced somewhat by implementing heat integration with the use of several heat exchangers in the process. Despite the use of these units, the process still requires 88 kg of steam and 9 MT of water for every kg of pectin produced. It would be possible to reduce the consumption (and their associated costs) further through more extensive energy integration. In general, optimizing a process with respect to energy and water integration is an important activity in process design with a potentially great impact on the economics and the environmental footprint of the process.

Using all above inputs, it is estimated that the plant requires a CAPEX of $116 million while the annual OPEX is estimated at $52.9 million. Figure 7 shows the breakdown of the operating cost of its various components. It can be seen that (facility-dependent) costs (e.g., depreciation and maintenance) are the major contributors to OPEX followed by the raw materials and utilities' cost.

Table 5 summarizes the main economic results and the values of the economic indices (ROI, PBT, IRR, NPV) regularly used to assess the economic feasibility of a process. Based on these indices, it is concluded that the process is economically viable and stable. However, it is worth noting that the operating cost per kg of pectin (10.35/kg) exceeds its selling price (10/kg); therefore, the economic viability should be attributed to the other assumed by-products of the (vinasse and animal feed). This is a point of concern since fluctuations in the selling price of the by-products (as as pectin itself) are expected to have a major impact on the economic line. In these cases, a sensitivity or variability analysis should be performed to estimate the probability of profitable operation in the face of uncertainty in the product selling prices and other design parameters that have significant effect on the economic results.

Process design modifications and additions should also be considered in the context of optimal process design. For example, the extraction and selection as by-products of flavonoids and citrus oils (such as d-limonene) contained in the peels may be investigated using process simulation as a way to improve and robustify the process economics.
