Conjugation can offer possibilities to introduce new food structures that are more functional, but this can be negated by the undesirable changes in the nutritional value of proteins (Mshaya et al., 2021).

Traditional methods (e.g., alkaline extraction, isoelectric precipitation) are simple and easy to use. Still, they can have a low extraction rate and negatively affect the extracted protein's properties. Therefore, obtaining efficient results with green technologies can be advantageous. For example, the High Hydrostatic Pressure (HHP) treatment of mealworm and cricket powders significantly affected protein function and antioxidant activity (Bolat et al. 2021). To use edible insects as a sustainable protein source, more studies are needed on insects’ (1) extraction using green technologies, (2) techno-functional properties and (3) bioactivity and use as agents in nutritious food formulations.

# 4.2 Microal

Microal represent extensive biodiversity with about 40,000 identified species. Studies evaluating the importance of microalgae on health and human nutrition and their use in the food and nutraceutical industries have gained momentum after 2018 (Sathasivam et al. 2019, 2020, Abu-Goshi et al. 2021, Kusmaydi et al. 2021). Microalgae species such as Chlorlla vulgaris, Arthospira platensis, Euglena gracilis, Auxenlorella proteotheoides, Chlamydomonas reinhardt, Dunaliella barren were granted ‘Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS)’ by Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and EFSA (Torres-Tji et al. 2020). The confirmed species of Chlorlla are C. vulgaris (Canada, EU, and Japan), C. pyrenooides (EU and Canada), C. proteotheoides (US, Japan), C. luteovioides (EU, and Japan), C. sorokinina and C. regularis (Canada). Dunaliella salina (China and Canada), Euglena gracilis (Canada, Japan, and Japan). Recently, dried Euglena gracilis biomass was also reported to be safe by EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Foods, and Food Allergens at the recommended level as a new food (Turk et al. 2020).

Novel micro-based protein and peptides can be used to support animal and plant-based protein sources because algae can produce all EAS. The profiles of microal are similar to high-quality protein sources, including casein and EAA composition in microalgal proteins from various sources are comparable to AA in traditional protein sources such as red meat but higher than plant-based protein (Singh et al. 2021). The total amount of protein in the cell is species-dependent and can range from 30 to 55% of the dry weight (which could be as high as 70% in species such as Arthospira platensis) (Barke and Blekler 2016).

Microal-derived hydrolysed protein is not only a protein for the diet but also a source of peptides that exhibit immuno-functional properties. It has good AA and digestibility and affects health positively (Eteman et al. 2021, Wang). Over the last decade, in silico, in vitro or in vivo, various biological activities performed by the microalgal-derived bioactive peptides sequence: antioxidant (Azmán et al. 2019, Tejano et al. 2019), antiinflammatory, and anti-amyloidogenic (Olashinde et al. 2019), antioxidant (Li et al. 2019), antihypertensive, antihypertensive (Lin et al. 2018), hypolesteresterolemic, and anticoagulant (Petruk et al. 2018), and antithrombotic activity all others (Tejano et al. 2019).

The first step in processing of microal is the upstream process (cultivation), which directly impacts the quality, nutrient composition, content and yield throughout the process (Daneshvar et al. 2021). All sequential processes (harvesting, drying, lysis, extraction, and fractionation) from biomass harvesting to the final product are downstream processes (Nitos et al. 2020). Enzymatic hydrolysis (utilising glycosidases, glucanas, cellulose, pectinases and lipases) is an environmentally friendly and low-energy alternative degradation commonly performed under mild conditions to promote the recovery of intracellular components (Al-Zuhair et al. 2017).
