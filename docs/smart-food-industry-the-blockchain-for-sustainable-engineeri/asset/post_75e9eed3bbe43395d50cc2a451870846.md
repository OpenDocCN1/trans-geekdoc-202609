## 2.3.1.2 Texture

3D printing enables the production of foods with adjustable textures, which is essential for people with chewing disorders. For instance, texture is one of the main parameters to evaluate the quality of 3D printed food (Yang et al. 2018). Textural analysis (TPA) includes hardness, chewiness, cohesiveness, resilience, and toughness, which are used to evaluate the structural properties of food (Zhang et al. 2018).

Reported texture profile includes hardness, chewiness, cohesion, and resilience (Nishinari et al. 2019). Toughness and resilience are good indicators of the stability of the food (Zhang et al. 2021). Chewability and stickiness can provide information about the mouthfeel of food.

Keerlana et al. (2020) used 3D printing technology to produce fiber-enriched snacks. Zhang et al. (2020) presented FoodFab that allows users to modify the texture and internal structure via two 3D printing methods: infill pattern and infill density. The authors evaluated how to modify the chewing time by changing the infill pattern (honeycomb, rectangle, and Hilbert) and density (25%). In addition, they observed that the higher bite force was necessary for the Hilbert pattern, and the longer chewing time affected people' sense of satiety (Zhang et al. 2020).

In general, it has been reported in the literature that 3D printed food has values from 4.5 to 60.0 N for hardness and springiness ratio below 1 (Zhang et al. 2021). Foods with springiness ratio below 1 are much fragile and present poor structural stability (Kim et al. 2017).

## 2.3.1.3 Composition and Ingredient Concentration

The composition and concentration of ink ingredients influence the printability, composition of nutrients, and the additives, which can directly affect the viscosity and texture of the final product (Zhang et al. 2021).

Food inks can be prepared from a single to a multicomponent composition, achieving different levels of effectiveness when printed. Chen et al. (2019b) prepared a mixture of protein isolate (SPI) with poor printability and low structural integrity, leading to inadequate behavior (1.18 N). The authors evaluated the addition of alginate (0.5% wt.) and gelatin (0, 2, 6, and 10 wt%) to the SPI-based inks. The most synergistic effect of materials prepared when SPI was mixed with alginate and gelatin (2–10%). The hardness increased up to 15.6 N in their studies.

Yang et al. (2018a) highlighted in their study the quality of 3D printing of dough for baking. They observed that the rheology of the ink and the physical properties of the product were affected by the concentration of water, sucrose, flour, butter, and egg. The gel strength, elasticity, and viscosity increased while the ductility was reduced with an increased concentration of sucrose, butter, and flour.
