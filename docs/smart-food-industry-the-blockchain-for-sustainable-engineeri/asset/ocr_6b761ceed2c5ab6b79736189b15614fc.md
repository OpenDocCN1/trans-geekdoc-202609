In Thailand, live mealworm is served in various cuisines or snacks (Tan et al. 2015). Consumption of insects is ingrained in the diets and cultures of insecteating communities such as Thailand and the African continent. However, insect consumption has become a global trend and is expected to grow in popularity in the coming years due to concerns about sustainability.

The scientific report about mealworms was as early as 1939 by Evans and Goodlittle (1930), who reported the nutritional value of mealworm larvae. Martin et al. (1976) claimed that mealworms could be used to feed aquatic animals such as Periductus trout. Recent studies show that mealworm could be sustainable food for the future because it is rich in nutrients. However, the nutrient profile of mealworms is influenced by the preparation methods (Ramos-Elorduy 2009). There is no doubt that the nutrient profile of mealworms can compete with other human food as a protein source. For example, protein in fresh mealworm is around 18% (Ooninck et al. 2015), whereas the protein is around 23% (Kenya 2018), 22% in beef and 23% in pork (Smil 2002). Hence, mealworms can become an alternative protein source in the future. Mealworm is starting to gain attention all around. This insect was easy to raise and the most reared in Europe (Bordaien et al. 2020). The advantages of rearing mealworms are cost-effective and can sustain the agricultural industry, growing agricultural wastes such as wheat for grain (Ooninck et al. 2015). Furthermore, the European Commission has approved the mealworm production for food and feed in the European Union (European Commission 2015, 2017). It is noted that mealworm can be used as a biodegradable agent in degrading plastic-based products in a recent study (Brandon et al.2018). The biodegradable activity of mealworms can be enhanced with the presence of probiotics such as Lactobacillus and Musicillarum in the gut of mealworms (Lou et al. 2021).

# 2. Source, forms, and Derivatives of Mealworm

Mealworms consist of three species: yellow mealworm, Tenebrio molitor L., giant mealworm, Zobobas astophilus panzer. These mealworms can be found in many countries as they are nasty pests to grains and floors (Ramos-Elorduy et al. 2002). Furthermore, these species can survive in very tough environments and are challenging to eliminate, as reported by Renault et al. (2003). However, nutrient, mineral and trace-elements mealworms (Grau et al. 2017) have been commercially used to get pet food for birds (Báñura et al. 2013), amphibians (Pams et al. 2012), reptiles (Gorovičová and Cernovská 2016), ornamental fish, cats (Bosch et al. 2014) and dogs (Bosch et al. 2016).
