Some key characteristics define a circular economy such as: efficiency of using natural resources and inputs, including energy and water which should be minimized and optimized; efficient exploitation of raw materials and ingredients; reducing emissions and loss/waste; keep values of waste in the food system by re-using and recycling; increasing share of recyclable resources and renewable energy (EC 2016).

CE has its main purpose to preserve resources such as raw materials, water and energy in a regenerative way. These resources should be used as long as possible by food systems, also taking into consideration sustainable production and consumption through resources, environmental protection and consumer awareness.

A new strategy that supports the transition to an EU CE was launched in December 2015 by EC with an Action Plan, which consists of a CE Package that adopted several legislative proposals including, others, directives on, packaging waste and landfill. The restraint has started initiating a call for a major shift towards sustainable food production, and consumption system based on CE approach (Fei et al. 2020, Borsellino et al. 2020, Ibn-Mohamed et al. 2021).

The breakdown of agri-food chains as a result of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic has generated the need to have a durable monitoring assessment system that will make for possible scenarios in the near (Rower and Galanakis 2020).

A CE report in agroecosystems focuses on a new and reliable prevalent linear economy approach of “take-make-waste” by reducing the amount of external agricultural inputs, and closing nutrient cycles. This system approach also has the potential to mitigate environmental impacts through the elimination of pollution from fertilizers, runoff contamination, excess nutrient load, eutrophication, and food waste. Indeed, the CE offers optimal and reuse of agricultural raw materials with a great emphasis on assessing and mitigating environmental impacts that may result in unfavorable climate change (Tseng et al. 2019, Barros et al. 2020).

CE can be defined in general terms as a model of production and consumption that focuses on the sharing, leasing, repairing, refurbishing, reusing, and recycling of available products and reduction of generated wastes (Baha-Walkowiak et al. 2019).

CE is defined by Ghellini as a way to focus on achieving sustainability via restorative objects and design (Ghellellini et al. 2014).

As earlier stated, global food availability is threatened by demographic, economic, and climate change factors and CE has been reported to provide an effective framework for achieving a closed-loop system by attending the issues mentioned before (Kirherr et al. 2017a, Tseng et al. 2019).

CE offers potential applications in improving food security and sustainability in the food systems. CE concept has found applications in nutrient cycling and inputs in the agri-food industry (Verger et al. 2018, van der Wiel et al. 2020, Billion et al. 2019).
