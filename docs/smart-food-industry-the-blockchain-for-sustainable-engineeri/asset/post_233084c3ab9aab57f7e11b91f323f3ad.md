
## 220 Smart Food Industry: The Blockchain for Sustainable Engineering

More than 200 diseases are also caused by contaminated food with human pathogens or chemical substances, for as heavy metals. This increasing public health problem causes significant socioeconomic impact on health-care systems and also harms tourism and trade (Fig. 1).

These diseases contribute significantly to the global burden of disease and mortality.
Contaminants can occur at any stage of the food production, delivery and consumption chain because of environmental contamination including water, soil or air pollution, as well as unsafe methods for food processing and storage.

In order to achieve SDG2 – Hunger in eradication of famine—to feed 10 billion people by 2050, it is necessary to get the trade-offs right between food security, food safety, sustainability, and better use of food already produced.

Sustainable food requires (a): sufficient food production and availability in the market, (b) ability to buy food, (c) nutritionally correct recipes with valuable food products including energy, proteins and micronutrients as well as safety, and (d) the steadiness and unforeseen ability of these conditions (Helland and Sörbö 2014).

There, feeding 10 billion people in a sustainable way will probably bring disruptive changes of the food supply chain by increase 60%, over the next 20 years. According to some stories, the total food production should increase SDG2, while others have shown that the solution is not to increase production but food distribution, change diet pattern and reduce waste. Moreover, minimizing the food loss and waste are part of the solution. Another challenge is the double of edible crops (e.g., corn, sugar cane) to biofuel production.
