From a sustainability perspective, IoT devices and IoT-enabled technologies could transform logistics massively. By making real-time data available, IoT improves process traceability and decision-making, allowing for more collaboration, and communication across food chain participants (Almeida and Villabos 2009). IoT sensors and other information and communication technologies (ICT) could be used to ensure the quality, security, and safety of the food (Zhong et al. 2017). Also, with IoT the agility of food chain could be improved. It is done by providing a flood of infant that can be examined by powerful predictive analytics, enabling firms to discover supply chain gaps and implement preventive remedies (Devroud et al. 2016). Additionally, businesses can use IoT in supply chain to maintain the freshness of products, ensuring compliance with standards related to food safety, reduce of waste, and its security (Ray et al. 2019).

IoT and IoT-enabled technologies such as Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), and sensors, could control transport, warehouse management, and delivery process more efficient, convenient, and traceable. For transportation, RFID is frequently utilized in transportation to determine and track products and vehicles. RFID can be used to obtain and track data regarding logistics (Liu et al. 2019), freight data and containers' location (Zhuang et al. 2014), and order information such as identity and quality (Cheung et al. 2008). These data serve to optimize path planning, vehicle configuration (Liu et al. 2019), as well as routing strategies (Cheung et al. 2008). Other supplementary technologies, such as a geographic information system (GIS) and a global positioning system (GPS), are frequently used in RFID to support route planning, navigation, and monitoring of transportions (Liu et al. 2019). IoT-enabled technology, particularly WSN, allows a higher control and security level during transit. WSN also allows remote monitoring of product cores, thereby reducing losses, ensuring food safety (Jedderman et al. 2014).

In a warehouse, IoT can connect objects and operators. Connected goods such as products, shelves, trolley, and logistics operators can be tracked and controlled through WSN embedded with environmental actors, decentralized decision support. RFID is frequently employed to locate items for inventory management (Garrito-Hidalgo 2019) and interim placement (Wu et al. 2019). Additionally, it is capable of collecting real-time warehouse data (Gouardi et al. 2016). The combination of sensing technologies and WSN improve the security and transparency of the warehouse. Ambient sensors incorporated in WSN nodes control variations in environmental indicators, in a warehouse, such as temperature and humidity (De Venuto and Mezzina 2018).

IoT technology enables monitoring and tracking the delivery process of food products, as well as making decision data intuitive and changeable. A variety of IoT technologies include sensor and RFID technology, ad networking and wireless technology, and embedded systems which allow delivery service providers to share information virtually and more efficiently (Ji et al. 2015). Increased delivery efficiency and resource optimization may be achieved by integrating physical logistics with the IoT (He et al. 2020). Different sensors, GPS tools, and RFID tags are implemented and linked to users' smart devices and on delivery vehicles, ensuring that data is tracked and showed (Samani et al. 2014). In addition to delivery trucks, a person using a mobile terminal with a device and activated RFID tag may be monitored both indoors and outside (Lin et al. 2011). IoT adds to delivery dependability and failure reduction. RFID has traditionally been used as a means to track and control the movement of food products and vehicles in order to improve delivery reliability, such as on-time delivery and accurate accounts. With the implementation of RFID, losses of goods and incorrect destinations can be significantly reduced (Fu et al. 2015). By gathering, monitoring, and recording ambient environmental conditions throughout, IoT-enabled technologies can ensure the quality and safety of commodities and perishable products. WSNs are capable of automatically capturing ambient temperature and humidity data, which may be used to monitor food as it moves through the supply chain (Tsang et al. 2018).
