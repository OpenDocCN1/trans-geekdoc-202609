Dairy effluents contain dissolved lactose and fats, and food additives. Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) and chemical oxygen demand (COD) are significant parameters that determine the chemical properties of effluents, and COD value is approximately 1.5 times the BOD value ranges from 0.8 to 2.5/kg (Ahmad et al. 2019). Lactose, which forms 72% of the total solids, is the main component that causes an increase in BOD and COD values (Papadamas and Kotsaki 2019). Especially, when a large amount of lactose and 1 kg lactose is equal to 1.13 kg of COD (Ahmad et al. 2019). During cheese production, about 115 million tons of whey are produced each year, and 54 million tons of it is discharged into the drains (Zandona et al. 2021). There are two types of whey obtained by separating the coagulated casein using enzymes: sweet whey (pH 6.3) and acid whey (pH 4.6) which contains 46.0–52.0 and 44.0–47.0g/L, respectively (Yildirim and Guzeler 2013, Papadamas and Kotsaki 2019).

Buttermilk is the liquid phase formed after separation from the butter during churning stage. 166 kg of buttermilk is formed for 100 kg of butter. Buttermilk, which contains most of the water-soluble components and is similar in terms of skim milk composition, contains 5.1% lactose, 3.5% protein, 0.8% ash (Yildirim and Guzeler 2013). Protein, lactose, minerals and fat is transferred into buttermilk as a result of the breakage of fat globe membrane by churning process.
