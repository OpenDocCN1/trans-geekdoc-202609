# 7. General Discussion

# 7.1 Germplasm Diversity and Breeding Effort

Wild population and their distribution are important sources of variability in breeding for improved cultivators of pseudocereals and other superfoods. Successful breeding programs rely on wide genetic variability; there is a need for more effort in characterizing the gene pools associated with pseudocereals and superfoods, especially those found in marginal area and those that are used as the principal food sources by traditional communities.

The overarching goals of food security for all and deployment of carbon neutral technologies in agriculture will be attained when the food and material needs of population are hot spots where natural resources are usable in marginal areas. The world must grapple with the needs of a growing affluent society with fast cooking foods and, at the same time, address the development gaps that exist in marginal communities. In all high potential areas, the food chain systems are well developed and functional, while in low marginal areas more innovative natural resource management practices will need to be developed and applied without delays. Improved agronomic practices on pseudocereals and other minor crops are required in both high and low potential areas to keep pace with the population increase.
