## 1.2 Sources of Plant-based Food Ingredients

Fruits, vegetables, grains, and plant-based protein sources are the building blocks of any plant-based diet. Plant-based proteins are mainly derived from sources such as legumes which include beans, peas, and lentils. Among all plant-based proteins, soybean is the largest source of plant-protein and is considered a complete protein with all the essential amino acids with a nutritional and biological roughly equivalent to that of animal protein (Watanabe et al. 2018). Additionally, pea protein is a relatively new type of plant protein obtained from leguminous crop of field pea. Compared to soybean or other proteins, pea protein is characterized by its high digestibility and relatively less allergic response (Zhao et al. 2020). Moreover, it has a well-balanced amino acid profile with high level of lysine and is widely available at a low cost. Recently, proteins derived from hemp (Cannabis sativa) are gaining popularity in the food industry. Hemp protein is difficult to grow due to various governmental regulations on suppliers and manufacturers. Other sources of plant-based proteins include grains such as rice and wheat. Even though rice is not a rich source of protein, hydrolyates of rice such as rice bran, endosperm and broken rice kernels can serve as cheap source. ## 1.3 Plant-based Products and Brands in the Market

As a growing food category, plant-based foods and ingredients have penetrated many food sectors, including dairy, meat, seafood, and egg alternatives. According to the report from Markets and Markets, the dairy market was valued at $22.6 billion in 2020 and is projected to reach $40.6 billion by 2026. Plant-based dairy categories in aggregate grew by 24% in the U.S., accounting for 15% of all dollar sales in 2019. Over 290 million units of plant-based meat were sold for retail packaged market and approximately 1.4% of all dollar sales. In 2020, plant-based seafood accounts for less than $27 million, and plant-based egg grew by 16% in the past year, which experienced the greatest dollar growth. ## 2.1 Plant-based Milk

Non-dairy or plant-based milk have seen a tremendous growth due to consumer interest in such products. This increased interest is not only because of absence of allergens (with few exceptions) and lactose but also consumers are seeking those products which have health-promoting effects and are sustainable as well. However, to compete with bovine milk in terms of flavor, functionality, and nutritional properties, plant-based milk manufacturers are at a continuous expansion to evaluate new plant-based sources. The protein digestibility corrected amino score (PDCAAS) and functional properties of soy-based milk are at par with bovine milk; its allergenicity and genetically modified (GM) food status initiated the search for “label-friendly” plant-based sources.
