Here, they are all derived from healthy animals slaughtered in slaughterhouses and fitted for human consumption (Beauchaux et al. 2019). The yield of by-products from animals varies greatly depending on species, live weight, fat, and collection methods (Castro-Muhoz and Ruby-Figueroa 2019). Processes in the meat industry, such as slaughtering, cutting, and processing different by-products (fat, red and white offal, blood, bone, meat trimmings, hide, tissues, horns, fur, skin, etc.) can be valorized (Halloway and Wu 2019). Biologically, the majority of non-carcass material is edible if it is properly cleaned, handled, and processed. Red viscera, also known as “variety meat”, include the liver, heart, kidney, tongue, and other products that are frequently used as co-products. Blood and trimmings would also be included in white offal (intestines and stomach).

Hides, skins, pelt, hair, feathers, horns, fur, heads, bones, toenails, blood, organs, intestines, fatty tissues, and on the other, are inedible by-products. It has been noted that other meat and connective tissues account for approximately 50% of the live weight, 42% of pigs, 28% of cattle and 24% of turkey (Castro-Muhoz and Ruby-Figueroa 2019). Meat and processing products generally contain high biological value protein and essential amino acids. Meat-products, in addition to proteins, may be a good source of minerals (Ca, Fe, Mg, Cu, or Se) or vitamins (niacin, vitamin B2, folate or vitamin C). Also, iron, which is found primarily in meat and their by-products, is more abundant than non-human iron (Mullen et al. 2017). It is possible to summarize the value-added and bioactive compounds obtained from the meat industry as fertilizer, animal food, blood meal, and meat meal, lactic acid, and probiotics (S et al. 2022). Nevertheless, protein induced waste has gained popularity in the food processing industry for the production of bioactive by-products. Bioactive peptides are made of amino acids that range in size from 2 to 30 units, and have specific sequences that show potential functional components (Saed et al. 2020). Hemoglobin is the most abundant compound in animal blood, which is a valuable by-product. It can be hydrolyzed to produce bioactive peptides, and its hydrolyates possess antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitory (antihypertensive), and opioid activities, with peptides receiving the biscuit interest (Shen et al. 2019). Shen et al. (2020) investigated the anti-hypertension and antioxidant activity of meat-product hydrolys by enzymatic hydrolysis. According to the authors, liver hydrolys had higher activity. Bioactive peptide production is critical in the development of functional foods and a potential solution to address economic and environmental issues (Saed et al. 2020). In the animal body generally accounts for 20-30% of total body weight, and bones are rich in a variety of minerals, such as zinc, phosphorus, calcium and iron (Shen et al. 2019). Animal wastes from the meat industry also contain significant amounts of insoluble structural proteins as anti-aging collagen, elastin, and keratin, which are major constituents of bones, organs, and tissues. These by-products are highly protein in content, and can be utilized for the delivery of novel functional ingredients (Brandelli et al. 2015). Furthermore, they contain chondroitin, phospholipids and phospholipids several times than fresh meat, which is essential to the human brain (Shen et al. 2019). A specific example of the utilization of meat-products, Long and Mohan (2021) produced spray dried beef powder as a value-added and multipurpose food ingredient. Protein hydrolyates are also used in aquaculture as nutrients. The palatability of meat-product hydrolyates in feed can be increased by cleaving hydrophobic acids from peptides which bitterness. Also, keratin-containing by-products, such as hair, feather, and the outer layer of skin, can be used in food formulations after enzyme hydrolysis with keratinase (Halloway and Wu 2019).
