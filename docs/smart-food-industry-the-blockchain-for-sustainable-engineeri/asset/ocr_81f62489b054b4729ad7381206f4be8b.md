The literature describes that the nozzle height greatly influences the geometric shape of the printed samples and when using the critical value, it provides the desired shapes with greater fidelity and a good connection between the layers (Wing et al. 2018).

The diameter ($D_n$) of the ink outlet nozzle is largely responsible for the thickness of the line to be deposited. Depending on properties of the inks, the effect of high pressure in the flow channel in a nozzle with reduced diameter causes the material to store more elastic energy, and therefore, a suitable diameter is required to provide smooth extrusion without a considerable increase in the system pressure. Yang (2018) produced corn starch gels added with lemon juice and evaluated the influence of nozzle diameter (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 mm) on the quality of the printed samples. The authors noted that the larger nozzle diameter resulted in relatively delicate and fine lines, while the smaller nozzle diameter resulted in relatively delicate and fine lines, a better quality of the printed food. Huang et al. (2019) evaluated the effect of nozzle diameter (0.84 mm, 1.20 mm, 1.56 mm) on the dimensional properties of 3D printed samples based on brown rice. The authors stated that the smaller diameter (0.84 mm) of the nozzle resulted in printed samples with deviations closer to the design project. In addition, the nozzle diameter was able to change the void rate and the number of layers, directly affecting the texture characteristics (hardness and guminess). The authors also noted that larger nozzle (1.56 mm) significantly reduced print time but resulted in dimensional deviations. Nijdam et al. (2022) evaluated the validity of different extrusion models directly using open-source software and commercial slicers like Slicer. The authors evaluated different inks made on peanut butter, cheese, fractionated animal fat or 20% rice-starch gel, different nozzle diameters (0.41, 0.83, and 1.5 mm), and extrusion speeds (10 and 30 mm/s). The most that corresponded to the behavior observed by the lines under the nozzle diameter and print speed conditions evaluated in this work. Fractionated animal fat and de-fretted rice-starch gel presented several ideal conditions for 3D printing, forming smooth, continuous, and stable filaments, considering the wide range of operating conditions evaluated in this work.

The nozzle movement speed and extrusion rate parameters simultaneously affect 3D printing. In this sense, according to Yang et al. (2019), the nozzle movement speed and extrusion rate can be determined considering the nozzle height and diameter (Eq. 2).
