# 2.2 Plant-Based Yogurt

Plant-based yogurt is another trendy dairy alternative which has increased sales in the past years. In the U.S., plant-based yogurt alternatives are expected to be valued at 1.3 billion dollars by 2027. Plant-based yogurt has proven to popular, especially among millennials, due to its benefits from bioactive compounds produced during the fermentation process (Craig and Brothers 2021). In addition to that, they satisfy the consumers' organptic needs with an appealing taste and other attributes such as sweet, moist, soft, and smooth have contributed to their increased market value.

Plant-based yogurts are generally prepared by fermenting hydrated, homogenized, and heat-treated plant-based yogurts derived from different sources such as cereals, legumes, nuts, seeds, and pseudo-cereals (Brückner-Gümann 2019, Mäkinen et al. 2016, Pandey et al. 2021). Some of the challenges faced by the plant-based yogurt manufacturers are often associated with stability, appearance, texture, flavor, and nutritional properties of the product. While fermentation of plant-based sources implies desirable flavor and health benefits from having probiotics and probiotics, acidification during the fermentation often leads to poor gel formation and synthesis. To avoid phase separation during storage, hydrocolloids are used in the product formulation. The addition of oil and gels such as gum, starch, and proteins improves the mouthfeel of the yogurt and stabilizes the particles in suspension to avoid syneresis (Greis et al. 2020, Craig and Brothers 2021). Recent literature also suggests the use of methods like high pressure processing (HPP), heat treatment, and pH adjustment during the process to improve the texture and stability of plant-based yogurt (Kloost et al. 2020, Kyriakoulou et al. 2021, Sim et al. 2021).

The functional properties of plant-based sources must be evaluated and considered when selecting them to be in the formulation as they significantly contribute to the stability, texture, and nutritional properties of plant-based yogurts. Plant-based yogurts prepared using soy, coconut, cashew, and almond sources recommend consumers' interest because of their similarity to dairy yogurts. Plant-based source blends are also proven to enhance the nutritious and organoleptic properties of plant-based yogurts.
