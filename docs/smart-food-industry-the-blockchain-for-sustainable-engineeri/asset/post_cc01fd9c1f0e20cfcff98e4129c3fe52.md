*Arthroplaster plantensis, Chlorlla vulgaris, Dunaliella salina, Haematococcus pluvialis, Cryptococdenii and Nitzschia lavia* are the main strains commercially used for production of microal-based ingredients (Souza et al. 2019).

In this context, microalgae have great potential to meet the needs of the population, generating interest in these microorganisms due to their nutritional composition as well as the presence of bioactive compounds, physiologically active substances with functional properties at the metabolic level. These compounds, such as polysaccharides, sterols, pigments (carotenoids, chlorophylls), volatile organic compounds, polyphenols, vitamins, minerals, fatty acids, and amino acids (proteins/enzymes) are changing their status in field of microal research, and gaining space in relevant research for the application of pharmacological, nutraceutical and functional products, holding countless opportunities for the development of innovative and sustainable products in the food industries. These microal biomolecules have shown essential activities at the level of health regulation and disease prevention, as antioxidant, antitumor, anti-inflammatory, antiviral and antimicrobial activity, among other important potentialities (Jacob-Lopez et al. 2019, Wu et al. 2021, Nürnberg et al. 2022).

### 3.1 Proteins

The great advantage of microal proteins lies in the constitution of amino acids in their structures, as they have a complete profile of amino acids that cannot be synthesized by the human body, requiring consumption through food. For Koyande et al. (2019), these essential compounds are histidine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, and valine. Other amino acids such as arginine, glycine, cysteine, proline, tyrosine, and aspartic acid also appear in the protein fraction of some species (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020).

Some microalgae have a high percentage of protein in their dry biomass (40–70%). Some *Spirulina* are reported to have up to 70% in their dry biomass. Examples of microalgae with high protein content include *Aphanomonas* (60%), *Chlorella* (55%), *Dunaliella salina* (55%), *Scenedesmus* (55%), and others (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020).

### 3.2 Lipids

Lipids are indispensable components of cells. Some microalgae can accumulate up to 70% lipids (Tiji et al. 2020). However, most species accumulate 15% (2020).

The lipid profile of microalgae consists mainly of unsaturated fatty acids, such as palmitoleic (16:1), oleic (18:1), polyunsaturated, such as linoleic (18:2) and linolenic (18:3), and saturated fatty acids such as palmitic (16:0) and stearic (18:0).

PUFA include omega-6 like linoleic (18:2), γ-linolenic (18:3), and arachidonic (20:4), well as omega-3 like α-linolenic (18:3), docosentaenoic (22:5), and dochexaenoic (22:6) (Souza et al. 2019).

### 3.3 Carbohydrates

Microalgae can contain approximately 25% of their weight as carbohydrates, depending on the species. The polysaccharides can modulate the immune system and inflammatory reactions (Souza et al. 2019).

One example is the β-glucans, a group of polysaccharides composed of D-glucose joined by β bonds in the cell walls of plants, fungi, and bacteria. They act physiologically as soluble fiber, reducing LDL cholesterol and the risk of cardiovascular diseases. They have recognized anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, immunomodulatory effects, and implications for blood glucose reduction, as well as prebiotic properties. This type of molecule is produced by many green microalgae (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020, Norberg et al. 2022), with *Chlorella* recognized as the microalgae that have the highest content of β-glucans.

### 3.4 Pigments

The main pigments (1–9%) present in microalgae are carotenoids, chlorophylls, and in some, phycobiliproteins. Micro pigments have found a market in the food industry due to the current search for natural ingredients that stand out in synthetic ones for being non-toxic, having been used as natural dyes, and food supplements. Chlorophyll, carotenoids, and phycobiliproteins have colors ranging from green, yellow, and brown to red. These colors vary with microalgae. For example, the pigment of *Spirulina* (provided by phycocyanins), yellow pigment of *Dunaliella* (due to the presence of β-carotene), and yellow to red pigment of *Haematococcus* (due to astaxanthin), which, in addition to pigmentation, have anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antioxidant, antidepressant, and antiaging properties (Souza et al. 2019, Vendruscolo et al. 2021).
