Soybean cake has a protein content of approximately 50%, enough to make up for the protein deficiency of developing countries with high production quality (Odebele et al. 2018). Soybean cake also contains polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA)/saturated fatty acid (SFA) ratio higher than 0.4 which is recommended by WHO/FAO for a balanced nutrition (Ivanov et. 2010). Soybean meal is a by-product obtained from dehulled soybeans which frequently used in diets due to its high protein and balanced amino acid composition. The growing area, soil type, climatic and processing methods affect the nutrient content of soy. For instance, the protein content of soybean meal grown in Brazil (49.3%) and Argentina (49.9%) have been reported to be higher than those of China (45.1%), Argentina (46.7) or the US (47.3%) (Lagos and Seitin 2021). Soybean meal is the most widely used protein source in the animal feed processing industry, but despite its high production, studies have to find new alternatives to gain demand and market share. The realization of the high protein content of rapeseed meal, also has become a competitive material in animal feed production (Hao et al. 2020). In addition, the phenolic content of raeseed meal is 5 times higher than that of soybean meal (Sydzowska-Czerniak et al. 2010).

The most abundant phenolic compounds in rapeseed meal are tannins and tannic acids, accounting for 80% of the total phenolic. Depending on the variety and processing method of the oil, the phenolic content of rapeseed meal may vary between 6.40 and 18.4 mg/g dry matter (Zago et al. 2015). The high levels of antinutrient compounds have limited the use of rapeseed meal, but as an animal feed and functional additive with balanced amino acid compound has been extensively explored (Sydzowska-Cerniak et al. 2010). Sunflower seed cake is a by-product of sunflower oil industry. Sunflower seed cake contains approximately 1.5–2.5% oil, and this can be used as a lubricant to speed up processes in the extruder (Ayrrılı et al. 2013). Sunflower seed proteins have been reported to exhibit antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-hypertensive properties. Considering their nutritional potential, researchers have started to work on the use of sunflower cake in promoting functional foods (Chauhan 2021). With cottonseed and canola meal, sunflower seed meal has also been used as an alternative to soybean meal (Liu et al. 2015). Palm kernel cake (PKC) is a by-product of oil extraction from palm fruit. Palm kernel is rich in saturated fatty acids, especially lauric acid, followed by myristic and palmitic acid (Oliveiro et al. 2018). In addition, PKC has a higher content (16–18%) and a high calcium/phosphorus ratio. In terms of amino acid content, glutamic acid (3.15%) is the most dominant one, followed by arginine (2.18%) and leucine (1.11%) (Sue 2004). PKC is a very suitable raw material for bioethanol production with its high polysaccharide and fermentable hexose sugar contents (Cerver et al. 2010). The high fiber content of palm kernel cake also makes it an attractive ingredient for the food industry. Palm kernel fiber (PKF) is an agricultural waste produced during the pressing of palm oil. The main components of PKF are carbohydrates like lignin and cellulose, and residual fats. There are also lipids and carboxylic acids on the PKF surface after extraction. Due to the functional groups, sorption may occur on the fiber surface; therefore PKF can be used to remove lead ions from aqueous solutions (Ofomaja et al. 2005; and Ofomaja 2006).

In addition to cake, agricultural wastes such as hull, straw and seed are formed during oil extraction. Seeds are mostly coated with shell. These parts are separated from seeds before processing, which restrict the extraction of oil. Generally, shells and hulls are widely used in the food and fuel industry, but some oilseed have nutritional contents, so there are many areas of use in different industries. It has been reported that oilseed hulls and skins possess important antioxidant activities with the effect of tannin and proinflammatoryin compounds (Schmidt and Pokorný 2005). It was also reported that sunflower seed has high antioxidant capacity due to caffeic and chlorogenic acids (De Leonardis et al. 2005). For a comparison of antioxidant potential of different oilseed by-products, Amarowicz et al. (2000) reported that crude tannins extracted from canola showed significantly higher antioxidant activity than rapeseed hulls.

## 4.2 Sustainable Valorization Options

In the food industry, highly biodegradable wastes with high oxygen demand and chemical oxygen demand are formed. Some legal obligations have been created by governments for the evaluation and treatment of this waste. Applications such as incineration, fermentation, and landfill are the main methods used in the evaluation of agricultural wastes (Otles et al. 2015). Incineration takes place by oxidizing the combustible materials, and is more suitable for food wastes with a relatively low water content such as grains and seeds. Although the incineration process can be an easy method to dispose of waste, it raises concerns about the negative effects of its emissions on the environment and yielding high costs (Despoudi et al. 2021). For example, burning sunflower hulls without the use of appropriate filtration system releases a large amount of dust into the atmosphere and increases the amount of CO emissions (Demir et al. 2005). The burning of agricultural waste creates air pollution and creates a worrying situation on human health, while leaving the fields vulnerable to wind and water erosion (Burton 2007). Besides incineration, agricultural wastes are also produced by anaerobic degradation leads to the formation of methane gas as more than 45% of the total landfill gases. In terms of causing global warming, in 2005 greenhouse gas emissions from the waste sector reached a million tons of CO2. In addition, the concentration of methane has doubled in the past 15 years (Omar and Rohani 2015).
