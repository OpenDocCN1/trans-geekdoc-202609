# 2.3 Food Ink

# 2.3.1 Rheology

Food inks used for 3D printing must present some characteristics to ensure sufficient stability of the printed object, such as ease of extrusion and printing and the ability to maintain structure. All these characteristics are related to the rheology of the material (Godoi et al. 2016). Therefore, 3D printing quality and demand depend on the rheological behavior of the food ink, on the properties of viscosity, yield stress, shear stress, and shear rate (Zhang et al. 2021, Sweeney et al. 2017). For example, hydrogels used as food ink must be viscous enough to flow through the nozzle, maintaining shape and after printing (Kyle et al. 2017).

The inks can be classified as viscoelastic and pseudoplastic and can behave as both Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids.
