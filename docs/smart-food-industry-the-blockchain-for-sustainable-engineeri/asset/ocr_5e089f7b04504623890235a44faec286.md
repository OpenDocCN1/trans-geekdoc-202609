It is estimated that 1/2 of all food produced in the world goes to waste. This equates to about 1.3 billion tons of food, vegetables, meat, dairy products, seafood, and grains that never leave the farm, or deteriorate during distribution, or are wasted in the final stage of consumption (supermarkets, restaurants, residences). Food is not just a social or financial concern—it is an environmental concern. When we waste food, we lose all the energy and water needed to grow, transport and pack. Furthermore, in its deterioration and decomposition stage, residues produce methane—a greenhouse gas which is more potent than carbon dioxide attributed to food waste. Also, according to FAO data, the total carbon footprint of food waste is about 4.4 GCO2 per year (Sialabba 2015, FAO 2021). To circumvent this, reducing post-harvest handling and adopting technologies, planning efficiency as well as conscientious purchasing and consumption, represent important strategies for the carbon footprint.
