A major positive aspect of veggie meat alternatives is that they do not involve (at least not directly) the exploitation and suffering of animals. Foods that involve no direct animal exploitation and suffering are *prima facie* morally superior to foods that do involve direct animal exploitation. Consequently, the production, sale, and consumption of veggie meats would seem to be morally superior to the production of animal-based meat. Therefore, veggie meat alternatives would seem to be morally recommended approach to global food and nutrition. This argument sits well with ethical approaches to animal ethics because most of such arguments rely on the notion of suffering. Vegan ethicists argue, one way or the other, that eating animals is immoral or virtuous upon deontological, teleological, or alternative principles, such as veganism and care ethics (Ryder 1974, 1989, 1999, 2003, 2010, Singer 1975, 1989, Regan 1983, Rachels 1990, Francione 1996, Nobis 2002, Deckers 2016, Alvaro 2017, 2019, 2020, Horsource 2006, Adams 1990, Donovan 2006, Luke 1992). Such arguments are well known to scholars and people, and thus I do not explain them.

Although each of the theories just mentioned offers one unique approach to ethical veganism, a supporter of veganism may suggest veggie meats are environmentally friendlier than traditional meat, and their production is free from animal exploitation and suffering. ## 7. Veganism Against Veganism

An important question that we should consider is the goal of veggie meat alternatives—not the immediate, but the ultimate goal—is to replace animal consumption, and, to completely eliminate it. Another way to put it is that the ultimate goal of veggie meat alternatives is to achieve the vegan world, which is a vegan world. Consuming veggie meats can ease the transition to a vegan world. First, people would get to use the food that can taste good and be nutritious although it doesn't involve animal flesh. Meat substitutes are meant to overcome that sense of lack and dissatisfaction, that nothing is missing from a meal when they take meat, look away. A potential problem to veganism may be surprising: veganism itself. The abundance of veggie meat alternatives that have recently become available on the market has not significantly helped people closer to the vegan world. Rather, such food items have contributed to becoming a commercial food trend among many others. Thus, it seems that the goal of veggie meat alternatives has set out to accomplish has backed, for veganism has shifted its focus from saving the world to promoting consumerism and people's self-indulgence.

In fact, more and more people are consuming vegan products—but this does not necessarily import that more and people are becoming vegans. In fact, it looks like meat consumption is increasing, which indicates that meat eaters don't have any intention to become vegans. Rather, they are happy to incorporate veggie meat products. Should vegans be happy about it? Perhaps not so.
