Therefore, protein concentrate, or isolates are critical in creating meat-like structure in HMMA protein (Dekkers et al. 2018). Liu and Siech (2008) suggested the protein-protein interactions happened when two commercial protein isolates were turned into fibrous analogues by high moisture extrusion or gelation process. Their findings showed that protein gels and extrates contain covalent disulfide bonds and non-covalent interactions in different properties leading to differences in their textural properties. Non-covalent bonds play a fundamental role in protein gels, but non-covalent covalent disulfide bonds are equally important in a fibrous structure of protein extrates.

The raw material for a HME process can be either a single component as one of protein isolate or concentrate where two or more materials are blended. It has been suggested that although both types of raw materials are capable of creating an anisotropic meat-like structure, multicomponent systems are more successful in forming a meat-like texture than single component systems due to reasons such as the formation of a dispersed phase from the added component, the influence of the concentrate on aggregation/linking reactions, and changes of the rheological properties (Wittek et al. 2021). In a recent study, the inclusion of protein concentrate in protein isolate-based material at varying on the HME process conditions, anisotropic structure and morphology development of extrates, and phase behavior of whey protein in the matrix were evaluated. It was observed that with the increase in whey concentrate in the blending formula, the anisotropic structure became more distinct and the pressure in the process decreased significantly because of the decrease in viscosity. Multase morphology of extrates was affected by the addition of whey protein depending on the inclusion ratio. As addition of 15% whey protein concentrate did significantly alter the morphology, at the 30% inclusion rate, it was detected that they became more dense, shorter, and thinner compared to extrates made with only soy protein isolate and with 15% whey protein concentrate. Their findings suggested that blending of protein-rich sources carries a great potential to improve the structural characteristics of meat analogues produced by HME process.
