# 3.2 Policy Capacity

In the global demands for food system, policy-makers are increasingly confronted with demands to become more responsive and efficient, given budgetary constraints, and the growing pressure for keeping low food prices and the push for taking action on climate change and public health. In particular, policy-makers are supposed to learn new capabilities and practices in order to adapt to system innovation, digitalisation and changing policy goals often labeled as “smart” (Lovell 2007). Therefore, the success of governments to implement smart food policies depend on their existing policy capacity–appro as a specific type of capacity for smart making. Policy capacity can be largely understood as the resources, skills and abilities that these actors bring to the table (Bali and Ramesh 2018).

Combining management ideas to organisations (Christensen 2009), has identified two main criteria for smart policies relating to policy capacity: feasibility and desirability. Feasibility broadly concerns the “quality of organisational thinking and the potential for controlling the process and its implementation.” feasibility is however about the extent to which the choice of policy instruments is strategically aligned with long-term policy goals and the efficiency of these instruments in term of implementation (Howlet et al. 2017). Bettian et al. 2015) point to the need to develop policy actors’ capacity in terms of detecting problematic features of the current system and to strategically align the system for targeting average performance (Bettin et al. 2015). Hence, it is also linked to governance the system for collective efficiency, engagement and cycles of adaptation (Kemp et al. 2007, VoB et al. 2009). In particular, this translates to governments’ ability to execute, generate and interpret results from foresights and scenario planning systems (Howlett 2015, Allain et al. 2020).

The second criteria of desirability is related to the wider society and political-administrative systems and points to the importance of policies with the wider context (Howlett 2009, Chhindarkar 2017). As such, desirability is closely related to the overall fit between smart food policy and capacity of socio-cyber system. Here, policy-makers are confronted with the task to assess stakeholders’ barriers and opportunities. In particular, this demands attentive formative evaluation and continuous engagement processes (Allain et al. 2020).
