Palm is an important oil for the palm processing industry. During palm oil production, many by-products such as kernel, nut, fiber, shell, empty fruit bunches and pressed cake are formed cake (Fig. 13). Palm oil and palm kernel oil are obtained from mesocarp and kernel parts, respectively. Approximately 20–22% crude palm oil and 5.5–6% crude palm oil is produced from each fresh fruit bunch. In addition, 23–25% empty fruit bunches, 13–15% fiber and 6–6.5% palm shells are disposed of in the palm oil industry. It has been reported that 10 million tons of palm meal is released during oil production (USDA 2021). Other by-products such as fiber and husk can also be recycled for different purposes such as the energy source (Yan et al. 2019).

Although the nutrient content of oils varies depending on oiled genotype, soil type, climate, cultivation area, agricultural practices and processing conditions, they are quite rich in carbohydrate, protein, oil, fiber, vitamin, and phytochemical contents (Table 6). The nutritional contents can be increased by using oiled by-products as a raw material in design of new-value products with appropriate evaluation techniques. Furthermore, the disposal of oil cakes to the environment has started to decrease with the realization of their high phytochemical content since after extraction, such as antioxidants such as phenolic acids, lipids or flavonoids remain in the cake. Although cakes from these crops are traditionally used in the animal feed and fertilizer industry, the technology utilizing these as source of processed ingredients, and antioxidants has gained momentum in recent years (Acosta and San Jose 2020).

Soybean cake is a product of soybean oil production and is rich in isoflavones and other functional components such as saponins and phenolic compounds. Soybean cake contains four different isoflavone groups, i.e. aglycones, malylglucosides, malonylglucosides and acetylglucosides, and these isoflavones have been found to have different antioxidant activities. Kao and Chinese (2008) reported that the malonylglucoside, glucoside, acetylglucoside and acylcone contents of soybean after solvent extraction were 2.411, 2.18, 0.26 and 0.16 mg/g, respectively.
