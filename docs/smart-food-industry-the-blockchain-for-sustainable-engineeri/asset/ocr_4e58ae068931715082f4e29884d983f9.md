Thermal disposal can be used for a variety of waste types, including municipal waste, industrial waste, hazardous waste, and sludge disposal. For this reason, several countries restrict or prohibit strategies like incineration and landfilling. The ability to convert waste to a safe material, a significant reduction in the weight and volume of waste and the ability to recover amounts of heat are many advantages of this approach. However, the incineration of food waste with a high moisture content results in the release of dioxins (Bulk 2019), which can cause a number of environmental issues. Furthermore, incineration reduces the value of the substrate by destroyed substrate (Paritosh et al. 2017). Composting is a valuable way of waste treatment that contributes to reducing organic waste for landfill or incineration, which can reduce costs and environmental impact due to collection, transportation, and treatment. However, the main problems with composting are the development of potential odors and the presence of insects and rodents (Storino et al. 2016). One of the issues for food and environmental scientists is to provide practical solutions for the disposal of food-by-products and wastes, which actively contribute to pollution. Decantation, dissolved air filtration, de-emulsification, flocculation, adsorption, centrifugation, coagulation, oxidation technologies, bioreactors, oxygenation, enzymatic treatments, and membrane technology are all used for the treatment of by-products and wastes (Castro-Múñoz and Ruby-Figueroa 2019). As a result, proper methods for managing waste are required. Food waste has gained prominence in the European political debate as a result of the Circular Economy (CE) package (EC 2015a). Indeed, CE Action Plan (EC 2015b) is defined food waste as one of the "priority areas," or areas that should be carefully considered in order to strengthen the circularity of the European economy (Cristóbal et al. 2018). In line with this, current chapter aims to provide a detailed overview of food waste valorization options, with particular emphasis on each major waste stream discarded during processing.

# 2. Fruit and Vegetable Waste and By-products

Fruit and vegetables are the most utilized and preferred commodities among consumers, and have a crucial role in human diet and lifestyle (Ganesh et al. 2022). On the other hand, considering the rate of food waste and by-products, fruit and vegetable wastes constitute the largest share by the ratio of 44% (Vilarño et al. 2017, Ganesh et al. 2022). Especially, juice processing industry has led to a huge amount of fruit and vegetable waste and by-products. The juice processing steps for various fruits involve occured waste and amounts for each process.

Most of the fruits and vegetables include a considerable amount of peel, skin, and seed, which are disposed of as or by-products (Cheok et al. 2019). Recently, researchers have focused on the recovery of these wastes to overcome the adverse environmental, and to convert them into biofuels including health beneficial compounds (Ng et al. 2020). The applications of fruit and vegetable wastes include edible films and coatings (Torres-León et al. 2018a), enzyme processing technologies (Srivastava et al. 2021), microbiological media (Vieira et al. 2021), food additives (Hussain et al. 2020), green nanoparticles (Mahmoudi et al. 2020, Bambu et al. 2021a) and the remedy for healthier human life (Chauch and Benvenuti 2020). Besides, non-use of these wastes causes the production of biochar, adsorbent, biosorbent, carbon quantum dots, biogas, etc. (Ng et al. 2020).
