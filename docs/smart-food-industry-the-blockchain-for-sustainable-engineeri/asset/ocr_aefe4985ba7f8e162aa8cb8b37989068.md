(Goers et al. 2014). Examples of co-culture could prove beneficial in the production of cultivated meat include the addition of macrophages and fibroblasts, both used in muscle repair, where macrophages contribute an inflammatory response and fibroblasts a pro-migratory effect on the myoblasts (Venter and Nieler 2018). Increasing the myoblast potential presents an opportunity to enhance self-organisation and differentiation through mechanical stimuli via higher connectivity. Dendritic cells have also been shown to enhance the proliferative capacity of myocytes, but not to increase the myoblast number (Ladiskan et al. 2018). This may induce a delay in the induction or differentiation of further proliferative cycles, which could be useful in muscle cell banking if a two-stage 'proliferation-differentiation' process was to be developed. The further addition of hemoglobin, a protein naturally occurring in skeletal muscle and cardiac cells, has been shown to increase proliferation and metabolic activity of bovine satellite cells, with the addition of hemoglobin can improve colour of cultivated meat products (Simsa et al. 2019).

To obtain a tissue that resembles the structure and surface of livestock meat, co-culture of different cell types (fat, muscle and connective cells) will be necessary particularly for structured meat products. The co-culture of adipocytes by myotubes has been previously reported (Kovalik et al. 2011). Another co-culture model of muscle with cardiomyocytes has been developed by An et al. adipocytes were first mixed with a gel solution in an inner dish which was then solidified at 37°C. The cardiomyocytes were then seeded on the surface of the inner dish and the inner dish was placed in an outer dish containing complete growth medium (An et al. 2011). However, both of these examples did not facilitate scale up of the two cell types nor were performed with humans. Moreover, these models did not support the survival of the two cells for more than 48 h which is not sufficient for achieving tissue maturation. The co-culture needs to be three-dimensional, scalable and to sustain the growth of all cell types involved. Critical co-culture of cells in food-grade hydrogel microcapsules could enable a stable method for production of meat bioreactors. The incorporation of hydrogels would streamline the manufacturing process by eliminating the need for proteolytic enzymes to detach cells from culture which are polymeric besides and could raise safety concerns regarding the regulatory approval. After et al. have shown that co-immobilisation of human umbilical vein endothelial cells and human osteo-progenitors in alginate and their culture under dynamic conditions significantly promoted mineralization over a 3-week period (Grellier et al. 2009).

# 5. Conclusions

Cultivated meat products are highly complex and thus production is challenging. Several critical challenges need to be overcome to make these products affordable and available to the population. Bioprocess development for their production requires a very good understanding of cell requirements including media formula and environmental conditions. The development of a bioprocess for the scalable production of cultivated meat is even more complex as it requires knowledge and understanding of multiple cell types—a source for muscle and a source for cells as a minimum. In addition, their production has multiple stages—expansion, differentiation, and co-culture of multiple cell types targeting a structured product. It needs to be noted that at each of these stages, the culture requirements are different. Because of that, typical strategies involve separate processes being carried out in parallel and in multiple bioreactor vessels. Ideally, a simplified bioprocess where subsequent stages as expansion and differentiation would take place in the same bioreactor would be desirable. However, this one-step bioprocess is highly challenging as it requires flexibility and specific tools to address the requirements of differentiation and maturation. A particular challenge is formation of mature muscle fibres to the mechanical stimulus that needs to be incorporated in the bioprocess.
