# 6.2 Sustainable Valorization Options

The dairy industry releases greenhouse gases as carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide into the atmosphere. Greenhouse gases cause ocean acidification, oxygen depletion and pollution as well as global warming. In addition, these toxic gases affect the plant flora (Sinha et al. 2019). The greenhouse gas emission of cheese is 5.9 kg CO2 eq./kg, and it is said the dairy sector contributes 2.7% of total greenhouse gas emissions (Milani et al. 2021).
Wastewater discharge, which has significant effects on the ecosystem, is the main source of pollution from industries. The wastes generated in dairy industry vary in concentration and composition depending on the type of product produced, operating methods and the type of water treatment applied affect the quality of air, water and soil (Adesra et al. 2021). Dietary wastewater may affect the physical and chemical properties of the soil, thereby reducing the crop yield (Ahmad et al. 2019). The organic constituents, high content of nitrogen and phosphorus in the dairy wastewater create intense foul odor or by reducing the level of dissolved oxygen in the water (Sinha et al. 2019, Ahmad et al. 2019). The dissolution of oxygen is one of the serious problems caused by wastewater discharge into the environment. Also, the oil and grease in wastewater form a film on the water surface that prevents the transfer of oxygen. High concentrations of total soluble solids found in dairy wastes lead the fish to become clogged, and foster growth of some algae and bacteria that deplete oxygen in the water. Wastewater may also provide a suitable environment for flies that carry several diseases such as malaria (Ahmad et al. 2019). In addition, when wastewater plays a significant role in the growth of microbes and the development of eutrophication is discharged into sewers, various fungi can develop due to the slime-producing bacterial colonies (Ahmad et al. 2019, Adsera et al. 2021). Milk-based wastewater is much higher than that of municipal wastewater, biological degradation occurs faster and creates serious problems in the system (Ahmad et al. 2019). Milk containing organic matter concentration is clear and white in color contributes to the load of effluents from dairy processing. In addition to fats, 97.7% of the total COD in cheese wastewater consists of components like lactose and protein (Goli et al. 2019). Lactose is the main carbohydrate component due to its high levels of BOD and COD (Ahmad et.). Nitrogen is also found in dairy-products in the form of other organic compounds or ions (Goli et al. 2019). It has been stated that the discharge of wastewater contains phosphorus and nitrogen causes eutrophication of the water (Aritoyannis and Giakoudis 2006). High phosphorus level is stated as one of the most important quality indicators in fresh water (Osei et al. 2003).
