Research on superfoods that are rich in protein and other minerals was driven by the quest to fill in the gap in the access and availability of protein-rich foods that exists globally. To date, protein undernutrition is in many children in the developing world and is exhibited through marasmus or kwashiorkor. Acute deficiency of protein in children is a major cause of death and wasting in adults the compound lead to low productivity and secondary infections. Consumption of protein-rich food serves more than 60% of the world (De Boer and Aiking 2011). Pseudocereals have gained popularity in recent years and the consumption rates are increasing fast.

Amaranth and quinoa are leading candidates for gaining popularity in recent years. Amaranth is grown for its leaf and seed. Amaranth leaves contain 17–37% protein (Oliveira and De Carvalho 1975) while the grain averages 14% in protein (Pedersen et al. 1987). Similarly, wheat, buckwheat, canary seed and chia are among pseudocereals with high protein contents that support protein-rich diets in humans.

Besides pseudocereals, research is on producing sources that can be scaled and produced at low cost and low carbon footprints. The potential of microalgae, water lentils and a range of indigenous mushrooms has been discussed. Microalgae, for example Arthospira sp., has shown potential for production and use as protein supplements both and in the form of dry processed products (Barka and Blecker 2016).

# 3.2 Healthy Diets

Human health is central to development and the attainment of aspiration of humanity. Food is a major source of energy and nourishment. Food diets are a function of income as it determines ability and willingness to pay, access to and availability of quality food. However, in marginalized locations where production is dependent on the abiotic and biotic factors of production, the diminishing production potential can jeopardize opportunities for healthy diets. For the urban population and population hotspots where land limitation prohibits production and individual purchasing capacity is high, food chain performance becomes an important determinant of access to food. Both adults and children require a balanced diet for proper health and wellbeing. Unbalanced diets led to many health conditions and if unchecked, can cost huge health bills.

# 3.2.1 Human Nutrition

Malnutrition has been defined as the severe lack of adequate nutrition consumption of insufficient food, inappropriate foods, or the inability of food to utilize available food. More health aligned definitions of malnutrition as a disordered nutritional state resulting from a combination of inflammation and a negative nutrient balance, leading to changes in body composition, function and outcome have been put forward by several workers (Soeters et al. 2017). There is an overlap between malnutrition and the availability and quality of food. A growing number of cases of malnutrition reported in developing countries has been explained by heavily swept eating habit relying on one source and accumulating nutrient sources. In parts of sub-Saharan Africa where maize or cassava dominate daily intake, protein energy malnutrition, vitamin deficiencies and other mineral limitation in the food-basquets are apparent and cut across all age groups. Fundamental elements of micronutrients and vitamins are difficult to diagnose during the early stages of this and has led to occurrence of “silent hunger” in many communities. Solutions of averting silent hunger are found in the basic designs of the diets that are used by the majority.
