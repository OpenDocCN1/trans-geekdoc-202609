In order to reduce food waste in general, and during food processing in particular, broadly accepted management hierarchies such as the Lans can be added to determine the order of the most favorable actions. This is frequently referred to as Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (3R). After recycling, the preferred action is to produce energy/or to burn (incinerate) and finally to dispose. This scheme, captures the efforts of research and food processing industries. For food processing industries, the focus is primarily on reducing the processing steps, the amount of solvents and other raw materials used as well as the energy requirements. In parallel, the yields and efficiencies of the main processes are maximized and the by-products are reduced. This is achieved by extracting valuable components from the by-products. Following this, many options are established for by-products, such as animal feed. If there are still considerable amounts of by-products and wastes, then the focus is on how to produce energy with these. There are many available options depending on the food product, the type of by-products and the chemical composition and content of the relevant streams

Particular processing food industries have different challenges and waste minimization approaches within the border of foodization. Clever wet milling processes cereal grains into gluten, starch and low value fiber by-products used for animal feed. These processes primarily aim to operate at maximum yields, while maintaining quality of the products. In the corn milling, the process separates the germ, the bran, the starch of the grains in this particular sequence. In order to minimize water usage, fresh water is only added at the final step of the fractionation that washes the natural starch. Then, water is distributed back to the process to support the fractionation and wash the other products of the plant with a counter-current flow. Ultimately, the water is used in the initial processing step, which is the steeping of the grains. The water solution at this stage has been effectively picked up all the solubles from the process and is sent for evaporation, producing the corn steep liquor (CSL). The CSL is then mixed with the solid products to form the corn gluten, a by-product used as animal feed. Many fermentation industries use CSL to enhance their fermentation performance, since it contains a number of soluble proteins and nutrients that facilitate microbial growth. This counter-current flow of water effectively minimizes the water usage in the plant (Reduce) and recycles the same solution in many steps, specific aspects about the low value by-product that is produced (Reuse and Recycle).

In some cases, dilute by-product streams of food processes may be considered and treated as wastewater. Their potential value, considered dried and so as low value product (e.g., for animal feed), might not cover the associated energy cost. On other hand, if these streams are not valorised they are used for the production of energy, anaerobic digestion to produce biogas, and then upgrade and burn the biogas to energy (electricity and steam). More concentrated low value streams with suitable chemical composition might be used for biofuel production. So, there exist many options that have been adopted by different plants depending on the chemical composition and water content of these streams, the cost of water and wastewater treatment, the location of the plant and, ultimately, the availability of capital or subsidies for supporting-waste-to-energy investments.
