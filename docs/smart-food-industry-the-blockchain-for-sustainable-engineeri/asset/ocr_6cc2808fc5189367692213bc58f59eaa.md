# Pseudocereals as Superfoods: Complementarity with No Competition for Food and Nutrition Security

There is evidence that pseudocereals are resilient to both land degradation and climate change; however, more work is required in this area so that the relative superiority of these crops compared to mainstream crops can be empirically determined. The potential of utilizing pseudocereals to solve the increasing challenges on affected soil, such as salinity and acidity, needs further research. Similarly, the food and drought tolerance across the whole range of crops needs to be developed.

The value chains of the majority of pseudocereals are underdeveloped because of the history of relying on a handful of cereals and legumes for food and nutrition over centuries. Developing value chains, in the seeds and input, distribution network and the output markets, is of paramount importance in the 21st century of hunger and malnutrition to be addressed. Investing in big data equipment on small and marginalized farms is an important step in the use of multiple sensing devices and machine computing and learning capabilities. Digital connection between producers and consumers of pseudo cereals is highly feasible using modern-day internet of technologies. The interest of policy makers in securing and use of small farms requires serious lobbying.

Complementarity between mainstream crops and pseudocereals superfood crop is evident and strategies used in reducing the population threatened by food needs need to take this into account. Promotion of healthy diets, diverse foods and improved distribution networks locally and globally is critical in avoiding widespread hunger and malnutrition.
