# 186 Smart Food Industry: The Blockchain for Sustainable Engineering

The implementation of 3D printing technology in the food industry depends on the following aspects:

+   - Ingredients: Choice of ingredients by in-depth understanding of their physicochemical and rheological properties. In the food field, these materials have been named food ink. Different food ingredients such as sugar, chocolate, gelatin, and flours have been used to create a design shape on the layer-by-layer method. In addition, 3D printing has made possible the use of alternative ingredients such as insect flour, algae, microorganisms, and agro-residue foods.
- 3D printing technology: The choice of technology is based on the material's physicochemical and rheological properties, applicability, and post-processing requirements. Diversified techniques can be used to produce food, such as extrusion, selective laser sintering, and inkjet.
- 3D model and formats: A diversity of software for designing the printed construction is available, from beginners to advanced (e.g., SketchUp, 3D Builder, OnShape, and Tinkercad). The design is then converted to a .stl file and "sliced" by the slicing software (e.g., PrusaSlicer, Repeter, Simplify3D and Cura). A G code that contains of the commands necessary to guide the 3D printer under predetermined conditions of flow (i.e., temperature and speed) is generated. This step is crucial as the slicing of the printing process is closely tied to selecting the optimal plan for guiding the object's design.
- Evaluation of the printing parameters and quality of the printed object: The printing parameters and the quality of the printed material can be evaluated with regards to its high fidelity (compared to the original design) and mechanical properties.

Figure 1 shows a schematic representation of the steps involved in 3D printing, from CAD design to the final 3D printed construction (Mantial et al. 2020).

In this context, this chapter brings the background knowledge on (1) 3D techniques for food printing, (2) major food ingredients for 3D printing, (3) crucial factors that influence the 3D food printing, and a discussion about (4) acceptance of 3D printed foods, (5) sustainable contributions, and (6) perspectives on 3D technology for food production.

# 2. 3D Technology for Food Printing

# 2.1 Techniques and the Machines

Several 3D printing techniques can be used for the production of food. They are classified according to either the materials used (paste, powder, solutions, etc.) or based on the mechanisms used to confer structure and stability to the final products (Godoi et al. 2016). This part of the chapter will address three different 3D printing techniques for food production and their peculiarities.
