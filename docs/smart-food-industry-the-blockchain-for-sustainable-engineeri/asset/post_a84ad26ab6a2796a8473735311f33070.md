The mixture is then fed to the saccharification reactor where enzymatic hydrolysis takes place. The amount of enzymes fed is 20 mg per g of cellulose. The lignin-rich filter cake is directed to the Utilities section for the generation of steam and electricity.

## 3.1.2 Fermentation

The fermentation media are first prepared in a blending tank operating in continuous mode with a residence time of 8 h, equal to the process cycle time (as explained below). Primary nutrients present in the fermentation media include hexoses and pentoses obtained from the hydrolysis of biomass (carbon sources) all as diammonium phosphate (NH₄)HPO₄ which is the phosphorus and nitrogen source. The media solution is distributed to a three-stage seed fermentation and one production stage. The fermentation time in each seed fermentation is 48 h, and 72 h for the production fermentation. Ambient air is continuously filtered, compressed, cooled and distributed to the fermenters via a flow distribution procedure. Fermentation is modeled as a two-step process with the biomass formation first, followed by the isobutanol production stage. The mass stoichiometry of the biomass reactions in the fermentation steps is:

1. (NH₄HPO₄ + 2 Ash + 100 Hexoses/Pentoses + 90 Oxygen + 7 Solubles → 50 Biomass + 100 Carbon Dioxide + 50 Water

The exothermic reaction is assumed to be 3700 kcal/kg of oxygen consumed. In the production fermentation procedure, the pentoses and hexoses are also converted to isobutanol, according to the following molar stoichiometry:

- Pentoses → 1 Isobutanol + 1 Carbon Dioxide + 1.78 Water (95% conversion)
- Hexoses → 1 Isobutanol + 2 Carbon Dioxide + 1 Water (99% conversion)

The conversion refers to the amount of monosaccharides (pentoses or hexoses) left from the biomass formation step. The exothermic reaction enthalpy is assumed to be 250 kcal/kg of consumedaccharides.

The sequence of operations executed as part of each fermentation step is: SIP (Seed-In-Place) of the fermenter, Pull-In Substrate, Pull-In Water, Pull-In Inoculum, Fermentation (biomass), Fermentation (isobutanol), Transfer Out and CIP (Clean-In-Place). During fermentation, the temperature is maintained at 37°C with the use of cooling water. The aeration rate is 1 VVM (volume of air per volume of liquid per minute) in the seed fermenters and 0.5 VVM during the biomass production stage. No air is supplied for the isobutanol production stage.

Because of the large batch size, a single batch requires 2 identical fermentors of 416 m³ that run in parallel. In order to achieve the desired annual throughput (as explained below), 10 sets of 2 fermenters operate in Stagger Mode; in other words, a total of 20 production fermenters are employed in this process. Each of fermentation pair is transferred to a blending tank which serves as a buffer storage between fermentation and purification.

The production fermenters are equipped with a vacuum stripping system that processes 78 MT/day of fermentation broth to maintain the isobutanol concentration in the fermentor below the toxicity limit of 22 g/L. The fermentation broth is circulated through a vacuum flash tank operating at very low pressure (–0.04 bar) where isobutanol-rich vapor and isobutanol-depleted liquid phase are generated. The liquid phase is recycled to the batch. After the end of the fermentation process, the isobutanol titer in the tank that combines the final fermentation broth and the condensed vapor from the flash tank is around 65 g/L, and the overall fermentation yield is 0.39 g of isobutanol/g of reducing sugars.

## 3.1.3 Purification

Isobutanol and water azeotropic can be purified to a very high degree using a system of two distillation columns in combination with a decanter tank that exploits the low solubility of isobutanol in water to break the azerope (Vane 2008). In the first distillation column, the product is concentrated by removing the azeotrope, approximately 95% of the water content. Then the concentrated top stream (with sub-azeotropic composition) is allowed to settle in the decanter, where it forms two liquid phases: an aqueous phase with 8.6% wt of isobutanol and an organic phase with 80.1% wt of isobutanol (i., the organic phase is above the azeotropic composition). Next, the organic phase goes to the second distillation column where isobutanol is recovered in the bottom stream with a very high purity (over 99.9 wt%). The top stream of the second column is recycled back to the decanter, and the aqueous phase of the decanter is recycled back to the first column to minimize product losses. In addition, the stream that feeds the first column is the wastewater that comes from the bottom of that column, in order to save energy.

## 3.1.4 Utilities

In the Utilities section, lignin-rich residue from pretreatment and hydrolysis (19.7 MT/h) and untreated corn (1.0 MT/h) are used to produce high-pressure and low-pressure steam as well as a modest amount of electricity. The amount of untreated corn was adjusted so that the output of high-pressure steam matches the requirements of the entire process and reduces the annual operating cost.

## 3.1.5 Process Scheduling and Deblenecking

Because of the sequence of long fermentations, the batch time is around 9 days. However, the availability of multiple fermenters that can operate in staggered mode (i.e., alternate between batches) allows the implementation of a new batch every 8 hours. Figure 9 shows the process Gantt chart where the utilization of seed and production fermenters determines the annual throughput of about 426 batches per year. Increase in the annual throughput is possible if the cycle time is further decreased by introducing additional fermenters as absorbed by continuous equipment with large throughput.

## 3.2 Simulation Results

The calculated amounts of raw materials needed for the production are shown in Table 7. With respect to utility requirements calculated by the energy balances, the plant is expected to require 204,500 MT/yr of low-pressure steam, 12,700 MT/yr of high-pressure steam, 40,250 MT/yr of cooling water, 363,500 MT/yr of chilled water and 125,700 MWh/yr of electricity. The entire amount of steam (both low and high-pressure) and 14,500 MWh of electricity (−12% of total needed) are produced in the co-generation plant by burning biomass (lignocellulosic residue from the process and untreated corn). If the plant performance is to be optimized, the percentage of available corn that is used as fuel to the co-generation plant should be one of the optimization variables.

Based on the relative consumption of each section to the material and energy, the fermentation section is the hot spot with utilities' consumption, the pretreatment/hydrolysis for materials and the purification for waste generation (Fig. 10). Therefore, all efforts in reducing any of the above operating costs should focus on the uses in the relevant sections.

Table 8 presents the economic assumptions on the cost of the main materials and utilities as well as the selling price of isobutanol.
