## 3. Meat Alternatives

Meat alternatives, sometimes also called meat substitutes, mock meat, or faux meat, are food products that contain similar physical and chemical properties of meat. Plant-based meat alternatives are made from plants with an intention to imitate the appearance, flavor, and the fibrous texture of animal meat (Boukid 2021). In the past decade, there has been an increase in the consumer demands towards plant-based meat products and a growing interest in their development and production. The increased interest in healthy diets and concerns about ethical issues, safety, processing, and high prices of meat are the main drivers of this shift in consumer preferences towards the consumption of plant-based meat alternatives (Kumar et al. 2017, Xazela et al. 2017). The factors affecting the consumers' acceptance towards of plant-based meat alternatives were summarized in the previous study (Boukid 2021).

According to the Food Institute 2020 State of the Industry Report, the number of plant-based meat manufacturing companies has increased, and many large-animal meat companies entered the plant-market, including plant-based meat alternatives into their portfolios. These not only helped plant-based meat products to become more available and accessible, but also enabled them to reach a price parity with animal-meat. Therefore, with their high nutritional value, cost-effectiveness and improved physical attributes, plant-based alternatives carry a great potential to meet the meat demand of growing world population by reducing the environmental impact and sustainability problems associated with animal protein.

In this section, textured vegetable proteins (TVP) and high moisture meat analogues (HMMA) will be introduced as "food products made from edible protein sources and characterized by having a structural integrity and identifiable structure such that each unit will withstand handling, and other procedures used in cooking for food consumption" by the USDA (USDA). Although there are various texturizing techniques to obtain textured protein such as high-temperature induced shearing, wet-spinning, freezing structuring and 3D printing, the majority of the textured protein products are produced using extrusion technology which can resemble the texture and the appearance of meat while maintaining or improving the nutritional quality of products (Boukid 2021, Raiz 2021). Singh et al. (2021) have brought a comprehensive list of the major companies/brands involved in this business with their product range.

Extrusion cooking has been defined as a process in which a material is forced to flow under a variety of conditions through a screw system and pushed through a mold at a variety of forms (Alam et al. 2016). Proteins in raw food materials lose their native structure during an extrusion process due to the exposure of thermal and mechanical energies which result in the formation of a continuous and viscoelastic mass. As illustrated in Fig. 1., while the product moves through the extrusion system in the presence of heat, moisture and shear, its molecules are denatured, crosslinked and reformed causing it to transform into an expandable structure in the final product.

Through the adjustments of extrusion process parameters, various TVP products can be obtained and depending on their moisture contents they can be categorized into two groups, namely, low moisture TVP (LM-TVP) and high moisture TVP (HM-TVP), which contain about 10–40% and 40–70% moisture, respectively (Zhang et al. 2019). The structural changes happening during the production of these two types of meat analogues are illustrated in Fig. 2.

### 3.1 Texturized Vegetable Protein (TVP)

Low moisture texturized vegetable products are mostly produced by a dry extrusion process carried out to intermediate moisture level, generally below 40%. As illustrated in Fig. 3, during this process, the viscoelastic mass passes through the barrel inside contains with a sudden pressure drop resulting in expanding the protein matrix. The expanded material is then cut into pieces, further dried until its moisture content drops below 2% and finely cooled and packed for storage. In low moisture extrusion, the final product resembles a sponge, and it needs to be rehyd prior to consumption. Because of its spongy structure, it absorbs the water quickly dipped in a cream, sauces, or other liquids (Singh et al. 2021). Therefore, the most common applications of low moisture texturized vegetable products are meat extenders, such as nuggets, burgers, sausages and hands, and ground meat substitutes (Ryu 2020).

Generally, in low moisture extrusion process, the feed moisture content is between 10–30% and it usually occurs with a short retention time (5–15 seconds) and high temperature (100–200°C). Depending on extrusion process parameters, feed moisture content, physical characteristics of the viscoelastic mass, and the properties of extruded material can vary. TVP products have been made using many different protein sources such as soybeans, rapeseed, and peas (Riaz 2011). It has been shown that process parameters have a significant impact on extruder responses and final product characteristics, including not limited to specific mechanical energy, textural characteristics, sensory attributes, quality, and structure in low-moisture extrusion (Riaz 2004, Yu et al. 2013).

In a recent study, fortification of rice starch with pea protein and pea fiber was achieved by low moisture extrusion process and effects of blending behaviors, different speed levels, temperatures and feed moisture contents on expansion behaviors, and microstructures of the extrates were investigated (Beck et al. 2018). According to results, up to 25% pea isolate and 16% pea fiber can enhance the expansion relative to the control group, the expansion is significantly lowered above these inclusion levels.

In another study, the use of rice protein isolate producing low moisture meat analogue was investigated (Lee et al. 2022).25:75, 50:50, 75:25, and 100:0 levels were extruded at low moisture level using a screw extruder for manufacturing a meat analogue. The physicochemical analysis results showed that replacing soy protein isolate with rice protein isolate resulted in a decrease in physical parameters as specific mechanical energy, porosity and water absorption capacity, the nutritional quality of meat increased significantly with the addition of rice protein isolate. It was also shown that the total soluble matter of meat analogues from the combination of soy protein and rice protein isolates were lower than that of the control group made from dehydrated soy flour. This was explained by the hydrophobicity, formation of aggregated bodies and disulfide cross-linking of glutenin. Because low moisture meat analogues often require the addition of a liquid before consumption, it was suggested that TVPs with lower content could be better in maintaining the nutritional quality after the rehydration.
