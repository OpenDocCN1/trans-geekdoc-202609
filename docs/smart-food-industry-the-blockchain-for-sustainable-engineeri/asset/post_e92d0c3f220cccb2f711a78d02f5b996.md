
# Environmental Sustainability Assessment of Food Waste Valorization Options

$\beta$-glucans are dietary fibers found in high amounts in grains, particularly oat and barley. $\beta$-glucans are found 3–11% in oat and barley, respectively, but in lower amounts in rye (up to 2% in the flour) and wheat (2.5% in barley). $\beta$-glucans are linear polysaccharides formed by the linking of D-glucopyranose units with $\beta$(1–4) and $\beta$(1–3) bonds. Their ladder-shaped structures consist mainly of two parts: cellulose (70–72%) and hemicellulose (20–34%). The molecular weights of cereal $\beta$-glucans vary depending on their botanical origin, genotype and agronomic factors. High concentration and molecular weight $\beta$-glucans exhibit better solubility properties. It has been noted that $\beta$-glucans lower the glycemic index, blood sugar and LDL cholesterol levels (Skendi et al. 2020, Henrion et al. 2019). Gal et al. (2014) investigated viscosity and solubility of $\beta$-glucan extracted from oat by enzymatic extraction with alkali, protease and lipase. The authors concluded that the final solution viscosity is due to $\beta$-glucan; higher molecular weight $\beta$-glucan increased the final viscosity before the separation of $\beta$-glucan from food was facilitated by the addition of enzymes. $\beta$-glucan has an important role in the food industry with its emulsifier, as well as having desired health effects such as the prevention of diabetes, relieving constitution and reducing the risk of cancer (Leal et al. 2021). Grain $\beta$-glucans caused a reduction in weight and an improvement in blood lipid levels in high-fat diet fed mice. In addition, Chen et al. (2017) investigated the effects of $\beta$-glucan extracted from oat homeostasis in mice fed on high-fat diet, and found improved insulin sensitivity, weight and glucose homeostasis. Karp et al. (2019) showed that cereal $\beta$-glucans exhibited a high degree of emulsion stability (80%) at the end of 14 days of storage and caused visible viscosity. Yu et al. (2021) reported that water-insoluble $\beta$-glucans extracted from oat exhibit a more fibrous layer and greater viscosity than water-soluble $\beta$-glucans. As a result of their animal experiments, authors also observed that water-insoluble $\beta$-glucans caused a reduction in weight and an improvement in blood lipid levels in high-fat diet. Protein is an important part of the daily diet, as well as improving the sensory and functional properties of food. Many cereals, especially wheat and rice, contain significant amounts of proteins. These macromolecules found in grains are mostly bound to lignocellulosic components. This creates a barrier to protein extraction so for this reason the protein is unable to extract with enzymes or solvents (Leal et al. 2021). Many researches have been carried out to improve the efficacy of protein recovery by different methods such as ultrasound (Phong et al. 2017), microwave (Bedin et al. 2020), extrusion (Leal et al. 2021), and high pressure (Zhu et al. 2017), to obtain protein fractions. Initial studies on plant-based proteins have mainly focused on rice as the raw material. Rice bran is a by-product that is released during milling and very rich in nutrients, especially protein (11–13%). Therefore, non-allergenic rice protein can be used as a potential source. In addition, rice oil is a vegetable oil obtained by processing rice and is frequently consumed in countries such as Japan, India, China and Indonesia. Rice oil contains high amounts of unsaturated fatty acids with 45% oleic and 33% linoleic acid. Rice oil contains significant amounts of antioxidant compounds such as tocopherols and $\gamma$-oryzanol. The oil can be used as an anti-inflammatory and antioxidant agent to reduce the low-density lipoprotein and cholesterol concentrations in cardiovascular disease (Lui et al. 2019). Specifically, fatty esters in $\gamma$-oryzanol have been found to possess strong radical scavenging properties (Fraterigor al 2021). Considering all these features, it can be interpreted that many cereal wastes, especially bran, contain significant amounts of protein and bioactive compounds with numerous evaluation areas.
