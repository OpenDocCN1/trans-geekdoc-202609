Different methods have been applied to the post-processing of 3D printed food, such as steaming (Thangalshmi et al. 2021), baking (Severini et al. 2018a, Palutsu et al. 2020), hot air drying, refractive wind drying, and microwave drying (Yoha et al. 2021). The post-processing method depends on the specific needs of each food. For example, Jo et al. (2021) applied freeze-drying technology after 3D printing of snacks in the post-processing of 3D printed foods. The authors reported that post-processing methods based on freeze-drying can result in 3D printed snacks with a texture similar to commercial products. Furthermore, Yoha et al. (2021) evaluated the effect of different post-processing methods on the stability of encapsulated and 3D printed probiotics. It was observed that 3D printing had no negative impact on the viability of the encapsulated probiotics and that the freeze-drying method was the most efficient method for the survival of the probiotics.

In this context, Cailier et al. (2020) evaluated the acceptance of snack bars by 3D printing with Dutch soldiers, considering the possibility of personalization of a product they already consume after training. By focusing on a specific public, the authors gave an important step toward understanding the potential of 3D printing technology for personalization. The panelists were 12 men of 32 years old, who consumed and evaluated a variety of sweet and savory for snacks fillings and dough. Starting with no choice (the benchmark bar) and finalizing with increased customization over four weeks, 4 types of dough and 13 types of filling, considering textures, tastes, and ingredients. It is interesting to notice even considering so many personalization options and information, the final result shows the panelists scored the printed snacks or slightly lower than the benchmark product (control treatments) with scores from 5.3-6.8 on a scale up to 9. In addition, they pointed to the "interest of health" as the most important interest for the potential of 3D printing technology for personalization in this context (scoring this importance as 5/8).
