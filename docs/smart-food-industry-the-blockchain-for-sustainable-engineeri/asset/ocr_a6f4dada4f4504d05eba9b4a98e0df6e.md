### 1. Introduction

Since the beginning of mankind, in the search for food for survival, insects have been part of animal (entomophagy) and human (anthropoentophagy) food due to their abundance, availability, and ease of collection. Insects have been used as food throughout human history and are one of the food sources that humans relied upon. Despite the absence of insects in the conventional western diet, they remain popular. Every primate is, to some degree, insectivorous (MacGrew 2014, Soagri et al. 2019, Hunter 2021).

Human evolution has caused mankind to go through adversity in the search for survival; so, as hunters, they began to collect edible larvae, available or cultivated, and realized that insects on the same plants, they could then be a great source of food (Minas et al. 2016). Eating insects can be considered taboo, an ancient and exclusive tradition of indigenous peoples, and unnatural to western countries' social precepts and beliefs (Cho and Hong 2018). Eggs, larvae, pupae, and adult insects have been used by humans as food ingredients, and this trend has continued into modern times. Man was omnivorous in early development and ate insects extensively, and before humans had tools for hunting or agriculture, the insects constituted a critical component of the human diet. What is more, people mostly lived in warm regions where different types of insects were available throughout. Insects used to be a great source of protein in hominins, especially the absence of vertebrate meat (i.e., prey meat). Arthropods were an important food source for early hominids (Kourská and Adamková 2016, Meyer-Roch and Jung 2020).

The appetite for insects seems to have the main driver of our ancestors' discovery of tool use. Our closest primate relatives, chimpanzees, are famous for their ability to use tools, and many of them have been used to efficiently collect edible insects (Koops et al. 2015). There is evidence from early human sites that bone tools were demerite for termite extraction. In caves in the USA and Mexico, evidence of insect consumption in human history has been found by analyzing fossils. For example, coprolites found in the caves of Mexico include ants, beetle larvae, lice, ticks.
