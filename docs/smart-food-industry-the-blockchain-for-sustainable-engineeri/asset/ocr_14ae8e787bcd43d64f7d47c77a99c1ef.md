The soy straw remaining after the soybean harvest is released as a by-product, approximately as much as the equivalent produced each year. Although soy straw is a cheap, abundant and renewable resource, its applications outside the animal feed industry are still limited (Reddy and Yang 2009). Soybean milk or cake obtained after oil extraction is used in feed production for livestock and fish (Dalgaar et al. 2008). Soy protein concentrate, extracted soy meal, fat soybean meal or low-oligaccharide meal are some products produced from soybean processing-products (Chou et al. 2004). While protein and oil components are valuable during soybean processing, other waste materials such as soy husks and straws are of less interest as low-cost raw materials (Cassales et al. 2011). The by-product of soybean milk and tofu is produced according to the soy milk industry, 1.1 kg of okara is released during the processing of each kilogram of soybean milk. High moisture content of okara makes it susceptible to decay, which poses a potential problem to be dumped into the environment. Therefore, finding alternative techniques for the use of okara in fuels will not only eliminate the environmental pollution problem, but also transform this into new value-added products (Redondo-Cuenca et al. 2008, O’Toole 1999).
