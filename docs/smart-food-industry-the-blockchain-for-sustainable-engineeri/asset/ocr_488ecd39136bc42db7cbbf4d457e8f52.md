PUFA profile in microalgae includes omega-6 fatty acids as linoleic acid (LNA, 18:2n-6), γ-linolenic acid (GLA, 18:3n-6), and arachidonic acid (ARA, 20:4n-6), as well as omega-3 fatty acids that α-linolenic acid (ALA, 18:3n-3), docosentaenoic acid (D, 22:5n-3), dochexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6n-3), and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5n-3 (Morais et. 2015, Katsiar and Arosa 2020).

Apart from amino acids, there are fatty acids that are also essential, including α-linolenic acid and linoleic acid. In addition, there are lipids that have been proven to have a positive effect on human health. The most prominent examples are omega-3 fatty acids, DHA and EPA, which are generally introduced into the human diet through the consumption of fish or through supplements. The traditional source of such nutrients in the human diet is cold-water fish and seafood in general. However, fish are enriched with these omega-3 fatty acids because they consume plankton and algae as part of their diet, and it is algae that actually produce polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA). In this context, microalgae like Phaeodactylum tricorn accumulate up to 30% of the total fatty acids produced as EPA, and other species, such as Schizochytrium sp. can accumulate about 50% of the cell's total lipids as DHA. Therefore, microalgae can be an effective source for fish supplements, providing health-beneficial fatty acids (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020, Moradi and Saidi 2022).

# 3.3 Carbohydrates

Microalgae can contain approximately 25% of their dry weight as carbohydrates, depending on the species. The polysaccharides can modulate the immune system, and inflammatory reactions, making them favorable to act as active molecules in food products and natural therapeutic agents (Souza et al. 2019).

One example is the β-glucans, a group of polysaccharides composed of D-glucose joined by β bonds in the cell walls of plants, fungi, and bacteria. β-glucans act physiologically as soluble fiber, with effects reducing LDL cholesterol and the risk of cardiovascular diseases. They have also recognized anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, immunomodulatory effects, implications for the reduction of blood glucose, as well as prebiotic properties. This type of molecule is produced by many green microalgae, increasing the nutritional value of products containing them (Torres-Tiji et al. 2020, Norberg et al. 2022a), with Chlla recognized as the microalgae that have the highest content of β-glucans in its composition (Souza et al. 2019).

# 3.4 Pigments

The main pigments (~1–9%) present in microalgae are carotenoids, chlorophylls, and in some, phycobiliproteins. Micro pigments have found a market in the food industry due to the current search for natural ingredients that stand out in synthetic ones for being non-toxic, having been used as natural dyes, and food supplements, in addition to being added as ingredients in foods. Chlorophyll, carotenoids, and phycobiliproteins have colors ranging from green, yellow, and brown to red. These colors vary with microalgae. For example, the pigment of Spirulina (provided by phycocyanins), yellow pigment of Dianella (due to the presence of β-carotene), and yellow to red pigment of Haematococcus (due to astaxanthin), which, in addition to pigmentation, have anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antioxidant, antidepressant, and antiaging properties (Souza et al. 2019, Vendruscolo et al. 2021).
