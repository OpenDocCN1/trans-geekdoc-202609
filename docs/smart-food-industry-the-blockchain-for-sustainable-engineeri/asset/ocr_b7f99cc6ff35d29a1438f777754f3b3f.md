This black carbon emission is a short-lived climate pollutant that contributes to climate change by absorbing solar radiation. It has also been reported that black carbon creates a global warming problem 5000 times more than CO, and has negative effects on human health. Reina-Bensusan et al. (2019) showed that black carbon emissions from burning wastes have more adverse effects on climate change compared to CH4 (Reina-Bensusan, 2019); for instance, burning a turn of straw causes a loss of 5.5 kg of nitrogen, 2.3 kg of phosphorus, 25 kg of potassium and 1.2 kg of sugar. In addition, the burning of wastes also causes greenhouse gases that cause global warming, loss of biodiversity and reduced productivity of agricultural land (Bhuvaneshwari et al., 2019). In this context, the evaluation of food waste also to improve air quality and respiratory health by preventing open burning. For example, the EU Waste Framework Directive (2008) of the European Parliament and the Council has introduced the 3 R (reduce, reuse, recycle) policy for the aim of reducing waste source, ensuring its reuse and recycling. The studies carried out and the guidelines highlighted the critical importance of transforming food wastes into valued added products, and contributed to a clean, reliable and sustainable environment (Fritsch et al. 2017, Skendi et al. 2020).
