The high fiber content of palm kernel cake also makes it an attractive ingredient for the food industry. Palm kernel fiber (PKF) is an agricultural waste produced during the pressing of palm oil production. The main components of PKF are carbohydrates like lignin and cellulose, and residual fats. There are also lipids and carboxylic acids on the PKF surface after extraction. Due to the functional groups, the sorption may occur on the fiber surface; therefore PKF can be used to remove lead ions from aqueous solutions (Ofomoja et al. 2005; and Ofomoja 2006).

In addition to meat and cake, agricultural wastes such as hull, straw and seed are formed during oil extraction from oilsseeds. Seeds are mostly coated with shell and seeds. These parts are separated from seeds before processing, which restrict the extraction of oil. Generally, shells and hulls are widely used in the food and fuel industry, but some oilseed have nutritional contents, so there are many areas of use in different industries. It has reported that oilseed hulls and skins possess important antioxidant activities with the effect of tannin and proinflammatoryocyanin compounds (Schmidt and Pokorný 2005). It was also reported that sunflower seed has high antioxidant capacity due to caffeic and chlorogenic acids (De Leonardis et al. 2005). For a comparison of antioxidant potential of different oilseed by-products, Amarowicz et al. (2000) reported that crude tannins extracted from canola showed significantly higher antioxidant activity than repeseed hulls.

# 4.2 Sustainable Valorization Options

In the food industry, highly biodegradable wastes with high oxygen demand and chemical oxygen demand are formed. Some legal obligations have been created by governments for the evaluation and treatment of this high waste. Applications such as incineration, fermentation, and landfill are the main methods used in the evaluation of agricultural wastes (Otles et al. 2015). Incineration takes place by oxidizing the combustible materials, and is more suitable for food wastes with a relatively low water content such as grains and oilsseeds. Although the incineration process can be an easy method to dispose of waste, it raises concerns about the negative effects of its emissions on the environment and yielding high costs (Despoudi et al. 2021). For example, burning sunflower hulls without the use of appropriate filtration system releases a large amount of dust into the atmosphere and increases the amount of CO emissions (Demir al. 2005). The burning of agricultural waste creates air pollution and creates a worrying situation on human health, while being leaves the fields vulnerable to wind and water erosion (Burton 2007). Besides incineration, agricultural wastes are also produced by anaerobic degradation leads to the formation of methane gas as more than 45% of the total landfill gases. In terms of causing global warming, In 2005 greenhouse gas emissions from the waste sector reached a million tons of CO2. In addition, the concentration of methane has doubled in the past 15 years (Omar and Rohani 2015). Consequently and the disposal of agricultural wastes in inappropriate methods creates many environmental and economic disadvantages. For this reason, studies on the evaluation, processing, and use of wastes in the development of new functional products have given momentum.

The use of plant proteins as a substitute for animal proteins becomes more important considering the increasing world population, ecological and economic factors. The incorporation of plant proteins to foods improves functional properties such as whipping capacity, viscosity, emulsification, water/oil holding capacities, and organoleptic factors such as color, odor and taste. Meals resulting from the oil extraction of soybean, rapeseed, sunflower seed and palm kernel have high protein content. Many studies have evaluated the use of protein obtained from oilseed by-products as a substitute for human nutrition and animal feed as promoting protein sources.
