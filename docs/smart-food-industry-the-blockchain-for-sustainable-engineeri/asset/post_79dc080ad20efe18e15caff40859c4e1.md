However, the use of rice bran is limited due to the rapidity caused by the high amounts of unsaturated fatty acids, and strong hydrolytic and oxidative enzymes (such as lipase and lipoxygenase). For this reason, researchers suggested that fresh rice bran should be stabilized immediately after the milling step, and studies on the stabilization methods of rice bran have increased in recent years (Wu et al. 2020). In addition, phenolic compounds in rice bran are mostly present in bound. Therefore, there has been a great interest to find an effective method for the release of bound phenolic compounds (Căliniu and Vnodar 2020, Liu et al. 2017, Wanyo et al. 2014). Enzyme application is one of the promising techniques to increase the bioavailability of phenolic compounds. Liu et al. (2017) showed that large amounts of phenolic compounds are released as free and soluble conjugate forms after enzyme hydrolysis. As well, cereal bran can be utilized in beverage and other food industry branches to produce phenolic-, antioxidant- and flavonoid-rich products.

The losses of grain throughout the entire food chain arise from all processes, from harvest, postharvest and industrial processing, to the consumption steps. Globally, more than two billion consumers do not have access to municipal waste collection; therefore, they dispose their wastes by burying, burning or discharging into waterways (Bansen et al. 2019). Unconsciously applied methods cause negative effects on many environmental factors such as energy and water resources (Skendi et al. 2020). The burning of solid waste in open areas causes a significant amount of emission. This black carbon is a short-lived pollutant that contributes to climate change by absorbing solar radiation. It has also been reported that black carbon creates a global warming problem 5000 times more than CO2. Reina-Bensusan et al. (2019) showed that black carbon emissions from wastes have adverse effects on climate change compared to CH4; for instance, burning a turn of straw causes a loss of 5.5 kg of nitrogen, 2.3 kg of phosphorus, 25 kg of potassium and 1.2 kg of sugar. In addition, the burning of wastes also causes greenhouse gases that cause global warming, loss of biodiversity and reduced productivity of land. In this context, the evaluation of food waste to improve air quality and respiratory health by preventing burning. For example, the EU Framework Directive (2008) of the European Parliament and the Council introduced the 3 R (reduce, reuse, recycle) policy for the aim of reducing waste, ensuring its reuse and recycling. The studies carried out highlighted the critical importance of transforming food wastes into valued products, and contributed to a clean and sustainable environment. The main component is a renewable biopolymer in the plant cell wall. Lignocellulosic materials are one of the most frequently used natural resources due to their biodegradability, removability, low density, resistance to strength. Cellulose is usually produced from wood pulp, but this production requires high costs, so the focus of researchers on cellulose production from agricultural residues has increased (Fritsch et al. 2017).
