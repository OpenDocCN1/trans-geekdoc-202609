# 2.4 Availability, Accessibility and Affordability of HSDs

Every human has the right to acquire adequate, nutritious and safe food. As discussed previously, the FS is failing to supply optimal nutrition to everyone when the following points are considered:
(1) Inability of the system to deliver a healthy diet (2) Inability of the system to produce equitable and social and economic benefits (3) Unsustainability of the system and its implications on the environment (Ben et al. 2019).

The food environment provides the freedom to the physical, economic, and socio-cultural context in which consumers engage with the FS to acquire, prepare and consume food (HLPE 2017). Downs et al. (2020) providing an expansion definition of the food environment, encompassing availability, affordability, convenience, promotion and quality sustainability. The food environment is critical in the FS since it is complex information-processing procedures for purchasing a food item (Blake et al. 2021, Downs et al. 2020). At the same time, the food environment significantly influences what we buy and, in turn, what we eat (WHO/EURO 2015). Healthy and sustainable foods provided by the food environment need to be widely available and affordable for all, but this does not yet seem to be the case.

Many people cannot afford healthy diets due to high cost of food and income inequalities. Healthy diets cost approximately five times more than energy-sufficient diets (Herford et al. 2020), and it is noted they spend the most significant proportion of income on food (UN World Food Programme 2020). Healthy diets were unaffordable for three billion people in every region of the world in 2019 (FAO UNICEF WFP and WHO 2021), with sub-Saharan Africa and Southern Asia not being able to afford a nutrient-adequate diet and therefore most affected by the problem (Herford et al. 2020, Miller et al. 2021).

The food industry can enhance the availability, accessibility and affordability of healthy food by:

+   - Expanding food production to offer more nutrient-rich foods with greater diversity
- Making healthy foods more widely available at a lower cost
- Reducing production costs through technology and innovation
- Implementing methods to ensure the preservation of nutrients along the food chain
- Reducing food loss and waste

+   - Collaborating with the public sector, non-governmental organisations and academia to develop a partnership strategy to deliver healthy and sustainably obtained foods (IFST 2017, Global Panel 2020, Miller et al. 2021, Reyes et al. 2021).
