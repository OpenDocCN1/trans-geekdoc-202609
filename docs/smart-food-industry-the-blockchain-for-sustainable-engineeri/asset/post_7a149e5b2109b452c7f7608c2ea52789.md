## 2.6 Cybersecurity

There is an abundance of data pertaining to logistics strategy and execution. The use of technology simplifies the process of storing and navigating this data, paving the way for more straightforward and quicker planning. As a result, the days of paper-based monitoring are past, and supply chains are more dependent on computer-based intelligent management systems, making them susceptible to cyberattacks (Cooper 2015). Globally, targeted attacks have increased significantly, affecting many industries and sectors, including corporations, hospitals, and government websites. When an industry adopts a new technology, cybersecurity becomes a concern. Businesses must safeguard the security of their systems to prevent the negative repercussions of technology. Cybersecurity is critical for businesses that rely on computer-based systems (tablets, laptops, smartphones, GPS), RFID, internet sensors of any sort, automated vehicles (AGVs), or Artificial Intelligence (AI) (Cooper 2015). Cybersecurity issues in the food business are few; nevertheless, with the growth of Industry 4.0 and the rising use of technology, they become a regular concern (Du 2019). Due to the food industry's particular infrastructure, IoT is a significant risk, endangering welfare, resources, and the economy (2015). Due to the huge number of parties engaged in the food industry, such as suppliers, manufacturers, and retailers, the food chain is regarded as even more vulnerable and prone to cyberattacks. Cyber-Physical Systems are the consequence of connecting physical and virtual systems over the Internet. IoT in context poses a number of issues since there are no security unknowns. Because these new technologies gather data and manage the systems of the sector, they are vulnerable to intrusion and hacking. Guidance related to this field of Industry 4.0 is still scarce.

From a sustainability perspective, these benefits are for the firms that addressed cybersecurity beforehand as well as those who postpone such a concern. Cybersecurity acts as an enabler and incentive for implementing the cutting-edge technologies. Hence, firms should secure their cyber and physical systems to flourish and take advantage of the opportunities that other technologies and their complementarities provide.

## 2.7 Cloud Computing

Cloud computing is an enabling technology that allows for faster and more efficient, storage, processing, and sharing of supply chain information (Gnimpiba et al. 2015). The cloud is a data storage technology that utilizes numerous servers to store data. These are located remotely from the data owner and are maintained by a hosting business ensuring the data is available and secure. Cloud logistics is a term that refers to the process of developing a logistics information sharing platform based on cloud computing's powerful communication, operation, and matching capabilities (Gong and Yang 2012, Holtkamp et al. 2010). Cloud manufacturing refers to the practice of utilizing IoT in cloud manufacturing (Li et al. 2020). Cloud manufacturing enables users to access virtualized manufacturing resources, competencies, and capabilities via internet-based services (Siderska and Jadaan 2018).

From a sustainability perspective, cloud computing has the potential to transform the manufacturing industry into one that is more service-oriented, collaborative, and innovative (Ren et al. 2017) by enabling the sharing of manufacturing services, including software, computational, and physical manufacturing resources that are available on-demand (Simeone et al. 2019). Hence, firms could cut costs based on the economics of the cloud provider and integrate their systems with their supply chain members more effectively and efficiently, which may aid in achieving competitive advantage. Cloud computing provides various benefits to food chain trading 298 Smart Food Industry: The Blockchain for Sustainable Engineering partners, including parallel processing, data security, resource virtualization, and increased data storage capacity (Subuddhi et al. 2019).

Cloud manufacturing is critical in developing sustainable industries thanks to the high automation, collaborative designing, strong process resilience, and improved waste minimization, reuse recovery (Fisher et al. 2018). For instance, cloud technology could be utilized to access real-time data about traffic, resulting in distribution optimization and pollution mitigation (Chen 2020). Also, using cloud manufacturing and IoT, Qu et al. (2016) developed a control method for coordinating logistics industries. This technology enables dynamic resource management and collection of real-time data. Furthermore, logistics scheduling, including cloud manufacturing, have been modeled by Zhou et al. (2020). In order to speed up the delivery of items to customers, they proposed a scheduling method. Employees from different departments, as well as companies, may easily access and examine data in real time. This improves interaction between the food chain players and speeds operations, particularly requiring logistics based on customer demand. Zhou et al. Data may be readily exchanged and viewed in real-time by employees from other functional departments and even firms. This enhances communication between the many agents in the food chain and accelerates processes, especially logistics dependent on client demand. Additionally, the food firm could reduce infrastructure and maintenance costs (Keogh et al. 2020). Although cloud computing is not free, users only pay for what they use (Benarnath et al. 2018). Alternatively, if the organization keeps its own data, it will likely have unused space on its servers and backup devices. ## 2.8 3D Printing

3D printing or additive manufacturing has been around for some years, the technology is now gaining momentum. It is a new technology that has the potential to alter and revolutionize a variety of industries, including manufacturing, construction, aviation, and healthcare, as well as the food industry (Wieczek 2017). 3D printing refers to methods that include joining or solidification of material(s) under control in order to construct a three-dimensional item. Through the successful accumulation of layers of materials, computer-aided software makes this approach, which creates three-dimensional slices of intended and desired objects (Jiang et al. 2019). Additionally, 3D printing enables the layer-by-layer printing of intended and desired objects (Portaugeau et al.). For example, binder jetting, material jetting, directed energy deposition, powder fusion/binding, vat photopolymerization, material lamination and extrusion are all techniques associated with 3D printing.

From a sustainability perspective, 3D printing establishes itself as a viable technique of preparing and presenting food in the modern day (Raj 2017). In turn, could increase consumer engagement and satisfaction and contribute to developing competitive advantage. 3D printing technology has shown to be effective in reducing the complexity of the food supply chain by displacing old production techniques (Chen 2016), and fabricating customized food (Sun et al. 2015). Implementing this technology will enhance the closeness of production operations to customers/users, resulting in a reduction in transit volume and associated packaging, distribution, and shipping costs. As a result, inventory management for food producers would become more cost-effective and simple, as they can make products on demand. Additionally, less transportation within the food chain may result in less damage to the environment due to reduced traffic from shorter vehicle journey.
