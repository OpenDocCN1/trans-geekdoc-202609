# 3.2 Calculation of WF Related to Crops

The blue and green water consumption in terms of depth (mm) can be computed using CROPWAT.0's stated as crop water requirements CWR (Chapagain and Hoekstra 2013). The green water (UG,m/ha) can be determined using the minimum CWR and effective rainfall (the amount of rainfall that is available in the soil for crop growth). The computation of blue water (UBWater, measured in cubic meters per hectare) involves multiplying the minimum irrigation water requirement and effective supply by the proportion of the total area of irrigated crops. Effective supply represents the stored soil moisture that is available for crop evaporation. The volume of fresh water needed to dilute pollutants to an acceptable level is referred to as grey (UWgrey, m/ha). It is derived using Eq. (1) and is based on the rate of pollution of various chemicals like insecticides, pesticides, and fertilizers.

```
WFgrey=(a * AB)/(Cmax- Cmin)γ
```

AB, (kg/ha) represents the pollutant application rate per unit area; a is the rate of leaching; Cmax(kg/m³) represents the maximum allowable concentration; Cmat and Yon(tonne/ha) represents the yield of crop. Due to a lack of local data, this analysis used nitrate-N as the pollutant, with a concentration of 100% (at a flat rate) as per the previous study (Chapagain et al., 2006), Cmat and Cmax as 11.5 mg/l under Nepal water guidelines. However, soil conditions (irrigation frequency, percolation rate, rainfall pattern, soil texture, and so on) and fertilizer application methods (rate, timing, agronomic practices, so so directly impact the amount of nitrogen leaching (Chapagain and Hoekstra 2010).

# 3.3 WF status Associated with Food Production

The overall volume of water used in agriculture is 7,980 Gm³/yr, with blue water accounting for one-third and green water accounting for the two-thirds (soil water) (Mekonnen and Hoekstra 2011). Wheat consumes consumes the most of this total volume, accounting for 1,087 Gm each year (70 %, 19 %, 11%). The lowest proportion of green water to the total WF among the principal crops is date palm (43%) and cotton (64%). Large volumes of water are required and polluted in the production of meat, especially for the production of animal feed (Chapagain and Hoekstra 2013). The amount of water utilized in the production of a crop is calculated using the total volume of the crop and the crop's water content (WF). In the case of animals, the model from Eq. (2) takes into account the water content of feed as well as the amount of water consumed over the lifetime of an individual. The internal (water for products generated in that) and external (water for products imported into that) water consumption of a nation can be calculated using these calculations.
