### 1. Introduction
Three-dimensional (3D) printing is a widespread use of additive manufacturing technology with potential application in several fields as civil engineering, textile, packaging, medical, veterinary, and dentistry. In the food industry, this technique enables the creation of food by adding the ingredients layer-by-layer to build edible 3D structures from digital designs (Zhao et al. 2021).
The primary advantage of this technique compared to the traditional manufacturing process is the possibility of creating products with complex geometries and controlled internal structures. Furthermore, 3D printing can be successfully applied in the personalized-food industry to manufacture foods with bespoke texture, taste, and nutrition, to customize diets with individualized nutrition for health benefits. This technique can also visual appeal increasing food acceptability and reducing food waste through the efficient use of the ingredients. Other positive aspects of this technology are the possibility of using functional ingredients on demand, improved food experience, automation, labor savings, lower energy and transport costs, easier supply chain, and food manufacturing closer to consumers (Jiang et al. 2019, Le et al. 2020, Nachal et al. 2019).
