# 3.4.1 Carotenoids

Microgae are considered the major sources of obtaining natural carotenoids. Carotenoids stand out as diverse group of compounds with potential health benefits. These microorganisms can synthesize complex mixtures of carotenoids, ranging from structures found in conventional plants, such lutein, β-carotene and zeaxanthin, to specific carotenoids with potentiated bioactive abilities, such as echinenone, astaxanthin, and canthaxanthin. The growing interest in this class of compounds results from its effectiveness in promoting human health, which is why it has potentially been directed to biomedical applications. Carotenoids play a remarkable role as antioxidants, as precursors to vitamin A, and in eye health. In addition, specific evidence suggests its role in reducing cardiovascular disease, obesity, diabetes, cancer, and protecting neurons. The biomedical contribution of these isomers is made possible part by their structural properties that orchestrate essential biological abilities, mainly antioxidants and provitamin A, related to production, chemical structure, relationships between structural and biological activity, and challenges for use (Nörnberg et al. 2021, Nascimento et al. 2022).

# 3.4.2 Chlorophylls

Chlorophylls are the major pigments involved in the metabolism of photosynthetic organisms. Original types of chlorophyll were known, a (from higher plants), b (from higher plants and green algae), c (from diatoms), and d (only from red algae). Recently, a new type of chlorophyll has been discovered, the type f, in cyanobacteria (Chen et al. 2012).

Chlorophylls as we naturally sourced food colorants can have health benefits, acting as an antitumor (Vesenick et al. 2012), and presenting anti-inflammatory effects (Subramonium et al. 2012).

# 4. Foods Enriched with Microalgae Biomass

The interest in the nutritional quality and bioactivity of high-quality compounds of microal increases the common sense that health and the environment are essential factors for maintaining a good quality of life. In this sense, the use of microal as ingredients/functional food products and/or nutraceuticals has been a booming market in recent decades (Fernandes et al. 2021a, Dias et al. 2022). This incorporation is feasible to increase the nutritional function and value of products that are consumed regularly (Tang et al. 2020). One of the advantages of using microal in nutrition is the possibility of offering several compounds of interest simultaneously. Furthermore, microal can be used to increase the shelf life of the product and as a source of natural dyes (Souza et al. 2019).

Spirulina and Chlorella are the first microal to be marketed as food recognized as safe (GRAS); therefore, they are among the most commercially exploited species in terms of profile and characterization of bioactive compounds (Fernandes et al. 2020, Markou et al. 2021).

Spirulina is one of the main trends in the food industry (Lafarga 2019), among other microal such as Chlorella. Most of the Spirulina that is produced today is consumed as a nutritional supplement as- 'superfood' and marketed as a powder, flake, or capsules. Aware, of the richness of its bioactive compounds, Spirulina supplementation (1 g daily) was highlighted for its high lipidemic effect, especially triglyceride content, in dyslipemic individuals (Mazakopakis et al. 2014).

Numerous other microal combinations can be found on the global market as nutritional supplements, such as in the form of pills, lozenges, and liquids. They can also be incorporated into food products (e.g., bakery items, cheeses, yogurts, vegetables ice creams, meat analogs, beverages, sweets, etc.), providing health benefits (Benava-Valdes 2021).
