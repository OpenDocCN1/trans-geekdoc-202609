Studies on the antiviral, antibacterial, antifungal and antioxidant effects of cereal-based wastes and by-products have gained momentum in recent years. It has been reported that wheatgrass has antidepressant, diuretic and anti-inflammatory effects while the maize grain is used as a bioactive substance carrier in the human intestine (Sielcka-Różyska and Giazdowska 2020, Skendi et al. 2020). Cereal wastes and by-products are also used in the treatment of acne and chronic diseases, thanks to the bioactive compounds. Corn is known for its high antioxidant properties and highly phenolic, glycoside and anthocyanin contents (Doan et al. 2020). In addition, the parts of cereals in the left after harvest can be used as animal feed due to their low-cost applicability and rapidly changing nutritional habit of animals. Quality residue of cereals are used in the feed of animals such as pigs, ducks and cattle, as they provide high energy and protein (Ajila et al. 2012). Specifically wheat, barley and oat cereal wastes are frequently used in animal feed compositions.

## 4. Oil Waste and By-products

More than 200 oil crops are grown worldwide, which can be divided into two main groups: edible oil sources used for direct consumption, and inedible oil sources used in the production of oleochemicals. The most important oils in the food processing industry are soybean (Glycine max), rapeseed (Brassica napus L.), sesame (Sesamum indicum L.), sunflower (Helianthus annual L.), coconut (Cocos nucifera L.) and olive (Olea europaea L.) (El-Hamidi and Zaher 2018, Kotecka-Majchrzak et al. 2020). According to USDA (2021), major oil production in worldwide increased from 580 million in 2019/2020 to 603 million tons in 2020/2021 season with a growing rate of 3.9%, soybeans being the primary type of oil with 366 million tons of production. Rapeseed is the second produced oil with a production of 73 million tons, while peanut rises with 50 million tons (Fig. 8). Refined oil from oilseeds are widely used in the manufacture of margarine, confectionery, baked goods and products. On the other hand, unfilled cold pressed oils are considered a pro-health ingredient to enhance the flavor of salad, sauce and pastes (Kotecka-Majchrzak et al. 2020). It has been stated that the oilseed wastes produced during processing constitute 20% of total oil wastes generated worldwide (Otles et al. 2015). Oilseeds pups, shells, leaves and seeds discarded in the food industry are of great interest worldwide since these streams contain high levels of phytochemicals. For this reason, considering the principle of zero waste, recovery studies have been carried out with the aim of increasing the energy savings and finding potential usage areas. In addition, it has become a trend to improve the quality of seed oils' waste and by-products, which are rich in phenolic compounds, carotenoids, tocophoreols, pigments and vitamins, and to use them as a raw material source in the production of new functional products (Şahin and Elhussein 2018).

## 4.1 Waste Fractions Form during Processing

Soybean is the most produced oilseed in the world. Waste and by-product fractions formed during soybean processing are illustrated in Fig. 10. Soybean meal, hull and straw are the main by-products of the soybean processing industry.

![](img/456d62956459ae12abbe8bcaa76dab9d_366_0.png)

**Figure 10.** Flow chart of soybean products (Dourado et al. 2011, Vishkarma et al. 2015).

Approximately 20 million tons of hull waste is generated annually which represents 8–10% of the whole grain (Cassales et al. 2011). The soy straw remaining after the soybean harvest is released as a by-product, approximately as much as the equivalent produced each year. Although soy straw is a cheap, abundant and renewable resource, its applications outside the animal feed industry are still limited (Reddy and Yang 2009). Soybean milk or cake obtained after oil extraction is used in feed production for livestock and fish (Dalgaar et al. 2008). Soy protein concentrate, extracted soy meal, fat soybean meal or low-oligaccharide meal are some products produced from soybean processing-products (Chou et al. 2004). While protein and oil components are valuable during soybean processing, other waste materials such as soy husks and straws are of less interest as low-cost raw materials (Cassales et al. 2011). The by-product of soybean milk and tofu is produced according to the soy milk industry, 1.1 kg of okara is released during the processing of each kilogram of soybean milk. High moisture content of okara makes it susceptible to decay, which poses a potential problem to be dumped into the environment. Therefore, finding alternative techniques for the use of okara in fuels will not only eliminate the environmental pollution problem, but also transform this into new value-added products (Redondo-Cuenca et al. 2008, O’Toole 1999).

Sunflower is one of the most consumed oils in the world (Şahin and Elhussein 2018). During sunflower production, some by-products such as shell and meal are formed (Fig. 12). It has been reported that an average of 1 million tons of waste is generated annually during processing (Mustafayev and Shmychagin 2019). Sunflower hull is a low-cost and abundant residue constituting 20% of sunflower seed (Hassane and Nouri 2018). Sunflower seed is also rich in protein, carbohydrate, phenolic acids. In the 2020/2021 season, 19 million tons of sunflower oil was produced while 20 million tons of seed meal was released (USDA 2021).

Palm is an important oil for the palm processing industry. During palm oil production, many by-products such as kernel, fiber, shell, empty fruit bunches and cake are formed (Fig. 13). Palm oil and palm kernel oil are obtained from mesocarp and kernel parts, respectively. Approximately 20–22% crude palm oil and 5.5–6% crude palm oil is produced from each fruit bunch. In addition, 23–25% empty fruit bunches, 13–15% fiber and 6–6.5% palm shells are disposed of in palm oil industry. It has been reported that 10 million tons of palm meal is released (USDA 2021). Other by-products such as fiber and husk can also be recycled for different purposes (Yan et al. 2019).
