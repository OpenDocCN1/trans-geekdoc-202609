# Table 1. Major findings of selects works using extrusion-based 3D printing.

| In composition | Printing parameters | Major findings | References |
|---|---|---|--|
| Mashed potatoes added to 0%, 1%, 2%, 4% (w/w) potato starch | Nozzle height: 3.0 mm | • The inks showed yield stress ($\sigma_y$: 195.90 Pa) and the printed objects were easily deformed.\ • The addition of 4% potato starch resulted in better shape retention, as the inks had higher $\sigma_y$ (370.33 Pa) and adequate G'. | Liu et al. (2018) |
| Lemon juice added to different starch content (10, 12.5, 15, 17, 20, 1/100 g) | Nozzle diameter: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 mm Nozzle speed: 15, 25, 30, and 35 mm/s Extrusion rate: 20, 24, and 28 mm/s Printing temperature: 35°C | • 15 g/g of starch was the optimal concentration (apparent viscosity: 807.9 Pa, G': 492.2 Pa, G'': 760.8 Pa and tan: 0.155).\ • The parameters that presented the best-printed-lines were nozzle diameter (1 mm), the extruded rate (24 mm/s), and the nozzle speed (30 mm/s) | Yang et al. (2018b) |
| Wheat flour and the insect's mealworm powder (100:0, 90:10, and 80:20 d.b.) | Nozzle diameter: 0.84 mm Nozzle speed: 30 mm/s Travel speed: 50 mm/s Nozzle height: 0.5 mm Infill density: 15% | • The softer dough obtained with ground inserts to 20 g/100 g (d.b).\ A significant increase in total amino amino acids was observed in snacks printed with 20% ground inserts (from 32.5 to 41.3 g/100 g of protein). | Severini et al. (2018a) |
| Soy protein (20%); Soy protein (20%) + Alginate (22%); Soy protein (20%) + Alginate (26) + Gelatin (2, 6, 10) | Nozzle diameter: 1.5 mm Nozzle speed: 20 mm/s Flow rate: 80% Fill density: 100% Printing temperature: 35°C | • The addition of alginate increased G and G'' values of the inks and the hardness and chewiness of the 3D printed geometry.\ Good quality with lower viscosity and higher G and G' was observed for soy protein mixes with 6% and 10% alginate. | Chen et al. (2019b) |
| Rice, cassava, potato starch suspensions at 5, 10, 15, 25, and 30% (w/w, dry basis) | Nozzle diameter: 0.8 mm Nozzle height: 2.0 mm Nozzle speed: 20 mm/s Extrusion rate: 30 mm/s Printing temperature: 70, 75, 80°C | • Rice starch: 15-25% (w/w) at 80°C, cassava starch: 25-20% (w/w) at 70°C possessed appropriate, $f$ (140-722 Pa, 32-45 Pa), and G' (1150-6909 Pa) values, were preferable, superior printable, shape retention, and resolutions. | Chen et al. (2019a) |
| Cassava starch native and modified by ozone processing (15 and 30 min) Inks: 10 g starch/ 100 g (d.b.) | Nozzle diameter: 0.8 mm Nozzle speed: 20 mm/s Nozzle height: 18 mm Extrusion rate: 30 mm/s Printing temperature: 20°C | • At the gelatinization temperature of 65°C, ink gels were based on native starch and modified starch (30 min) showed good printable, but up to this temperature, only modified starch (30 min) produced gels with good printable. | Maniglia et al. (2019) |
