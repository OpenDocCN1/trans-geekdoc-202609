There is no doubt that the nutrient profile of mealworms can compete with other human food as a protein source. The advantages of rearing mealworms are cost-effective and can sustain the agricultural industry, growing agricultural wastes such as wheat for grain (Ooninck et al. 2015). It is noted that mealworm can be used as a biodegradable agent in degrading plastic-based products in a recent study (Brandon et al. 2018). ## 2. Source, forms, and Derivatives of Mealworm

Mealworms consist of three species: yellow mealworm, Tenebrio molitor L., giant mealworm, Zobobas astophilus panzer. These mealworms can be found in many countries as they are nasty pests to grains and floors (Ramos-Elorduy et al. 2002). Furthermore, these species can survive in very tough environments and are challenging to eliminate, as reported by Renault et al. (2003). However, nutrient, mineral and trace-elements mealworms (Grau et al. 2017) have been commercially used to get pet food for birds (Báñura et al. 2013), amphibians (Pams et al. 2012), reptiles (Gorovičová and Cernovská 2016), ornamental fish, cats (Bosch et al. 2014) and dogs (Bosch et al. 2016).

A commonly farmed mealworm is T. molitor, which has not during during life stage (adult): egg, larva, and pupa (Lu et al. 2013). Mating will occur during the life stage, and female beetle will produce eggs. Mealworms require food during their life stages as larva and beetle transform into larva and pupa during its egg and pupa life stages (Fig. 1). The diet of mealworms comprises vegetables, wheat, fruit, rice bran, and many (Van Broek et al. 2016). Mealworms prefer warm temperatures ranging between 25 and 28°C in their life stage (Kim et al. 2015). It also favors high humidity, especially at the beetle stage (Hardoux and Mahaux 2003) and dry environments (Ooninck et al. 2015). In commercial farms, mealworms are fed with wheat bran, maize, rapeseed, sunflower, soybean, and bean pulp (Sauvant et al. 2004). Thévenot et al. (2018) reported that mealworms would be harvested and processed into meal and oil at the larval stage. These mealworm products would be used in animal food production and human use (Son et al. 2020). Few studies suggest that mealworm farming has lower impact on the environment than other ingredients for feed production. For instance, Ooninck and De Boer (2012) reported that dairy products and meat production have a higher environmental impact than mealworms. Another study by Thévenot et al. (2018) revealed that mealworm production has a lesser environmental impact than soybean meat and fishmeal production.

## 3. Nutritional Composition of Mealworm

Mealworm nutritional content has been extensively researched and recorded in the literature. The nutritional content of mealworms can undoubtedly compete with that of conventional protein sources on the market. Mealworms have a comparatively high crude protein composition and are on par with conventional animal feed ingredients such as soybean meal and fishmeal. Soybean meal has about 40% crude protein (Bovera et. 2015) whereas it has about 60% in fishmeal (Wang et al. 2005). Live mealworm is also rich in other nutrients to cater the nutrition needed for many species, including humans (Table 1). However, the nutrient profile of mealworm reported is inconsistent and varies depending on the studies and may be due to the larvae (Table 2). For example, the highest protein value of mealworm (53%) was recorded in the study of Khan et al. (2018) and Mari (2020), whereas the lowest protein value of mealworm recorded in the study of Hou et al. (2017) was 45.8%. Another example of the variation of mealworm protein content is highest was 34.2%. A similar pattern also exists in amino acid profile of lysine (3). For instance, Finke (2002) claimed that methionine in mealworms is about 0.6% while Bovera et al. (2015) showed methionine in plants is 1.6%. At present, essential amino acids for animal uses are derived from the plant through the fermentation process (Karau et al. 2022, 2021, Zhou et al. 2020). The amino acid profile of consumer is not reflective of input. DL-Methionine, L-lysine, L-tryptophan are essential amino acids required for commercially fed animal and are present in reasonable amounts in mealworms. Thus, the inclusion of mealworms in feed can serve as organic source of essential amino acids. Another important nutrient in mealworms is fatty acids such as palmitic acid, oleic acid and linoleic acid (Ravzani et al. 2012). These fatty acids are crucial in animal food production. Furthermore, Jeon et al. (2016) claimed that mealworm oil has fatty acids that stand at high temperatures. Kim et al. (2013) claimed that mealworm has vast potential to be used as feed additive. In addition, entomopathogenic fungus, Beauveria bassiana, was introduced to Tenebrio molitor to enhance green fluorescent protein in the nutrient de antioxidant properties in the mealworm. Many factors can influence the nutritional values of mealworms, especially in dried mealworm preparation, such as temperature, storage, and many more (Zhao et al. 2016). Therefore, it is important to establish a mealworm processing method in order to maintain the quality of consistency.

Mealworm is rich in glucosamine compounds and chitosan found in mealworms and are very useful for maintaining human health (Son et al. 2020). Both chitin and glucosamine compounds found in mealworms can be further processed through deacetylation to produce chitosan (Shin et al. 2019). At present, chitin and chitosan are widely used in the healthcare industry, as antimicrobial agents, cosmetics, wound healing, and many more (Elie-Al-Komi and Hamblin 2016). The commercial chitin and chitosan derived from mealworms are equally similar to that of shrimp (Son et al. 2021). Thus, industry have more options in selecting the source of ingredients for chitin and chitosan production.

Many studies have reported the medical properties of mealworms. For example, Ding et al. (2021) reported that mealworm larvae extract can up-regulated expression of caspases, i.e., caspase-8 and-9, followed by activation of caspase-3 which induces apoptosis in cancer cells. Dávalos Terán et al. (2020) discovered that strategic processing of yellow mealworm proteins are able to produce dipeidyl peptidase (DPP) inhibiting peptides. This DPP can be used in diabetes treatment. Other medical properties of mealworms were been reported in the study of Song et al. (2018). The study claimed that mealworm whole body, blood and excretion are good for chitin and chitosan sources. The extension study of Shin et al. (2019) showed that chitosan extracted from mealworm possesses antimicrobial activities against Bacillus cereus, Lister monocytogenes, Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus.
