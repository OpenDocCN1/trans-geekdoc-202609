# 4.1 Dietary Factors and Dietary Pattern Analysis

Increased global demand for agriculture has from an increasing human population and rapidly shifting diets, including greater reliance on animal-sourced foods (ASFs). It has become increasingly vital to determine sustainable diets that promote health while decreasing environmental consequences, and understanding the influence of food production and population-level dietary behaviors over water usage is crucial for long-term water management. An increasing number of studies indicate that lowering ASFs in the diet, particularly beef, poultry, and meat, is linked with fewer environmental effects and resource demands. Furthermore, reducing ASF content in diets does not guarantee reduced usage of water, specifically if ASF are substituted with foods that are reliant on irrigation, such as fruits and lentils.
