## 2.1.1 Process Description

The process is divided into six sections, i.e., 'Peel Preparation and Sugar Wash', 'Sugars Processing', 'Pectin Extraction', 'Pectin Purification', 'Pectin Precipitation' and 'IPA Recovery'. Sections in SuperPro Designer represent groups of processing tasks (unit procedures) that serve a common processing objective. Because of its large size, the flowchart is presented in different flowcharts representing different sections. In the description follows, the procedures are referred by their IDs (names) as shown in the relevant charts. Note that all procedures in SuperPro Designer are tagged by their ID, the hosting equipment ID and a textual description of the procedure. All these tags are shown before every procedure icon in the flowcharts.

### 2.1.1.1 Peel Preparation and Sugar Wash

In this section (Fig. 1), the peels are ground and washed to remove all the soluble solids. The peels are fed to the plant through silo P-1, mixed with a water recycle stream in P-2 and wet ground in P-3. The slurry is then sent to a tank (P-4) where it is mixed with another aqueous stream to allow solid components to dissolve in the liquid phase. The slurry is then fed to the screw

### 2.1.1.2 Sugars Processing

The aqueous solution from the first press (P-5) containing most of the solutes is stored, then heated to 50°C and fed into an enzymatic treatment tank (P-12) as shown in Fig. 2. It then pasteurized in P-13 (heated at 75°C and cooled down to 50°C) and centrifuged (P-14). The centrifuge separates any remains (sent back to pectin extraction) and sends the liquor to a tank (P-15) which acts as a balance tank for the evaporator (P-18). Before being fed to the evaporator, the liquor is preheated (in P-16) with the evaporator condensate and heated with steam (in P-17). In a 4-effect evaporator (P-18), water is evaporated to reach a dry solid (DS) concentration of 75%. This sugar solution is a by-product called Vinainose, and it is sold to the fermentation industries. The vapor from the final stage of the evaporator is condensed (in P-20), mixed (in P-22) with the condensates from the first 3 evaporators and used to reheat the feed and sent to the water distribution system of plant.

### 2.1.1.3 Pectin Extraction

The solids from the final press (P-8) that contain all the pectin are heated (in P-28) to 60°C and loaded into reactor P-29 (see Fig. 3). The reactor operates in batch and includes the following operations that are executed sequentially: charge of solids, charge of hot water, react of sodium carbonate and transfer-out. Hot water is used to achieve a final water concentration of about 95%. Under the acidic conditions and high temperature in the reactor, pectin becomes soluble and extracted from the solid to the liquid phase. However, a considerable quantity of pectin is decomposed into various impurities. Similarly, other polymers such as cellulose hemicellulose also decompose. These decompositions were modeled as reactions with the following

- Pectin $\rightarrow$ 1 PEC_W (40% conversion)
- Pectin $\rightarrow$ 1 IMP (60% conversion)

### 2.1.1.4 Pectin Purification

In this section (Fig. 4), the filtrate from the press (P-36) containing the solubilized pectins is sent to a buffer tank (P-37) then loaded onto granulated carbon (GAC) column (P-47). The GAC binds the furfurals formed during extraction.

The following operations are executed sequentially in the column: load, wash with water, regenerate with HCl, regenerate with NaOH. The ions are removed during regeneration with sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid which are prepared in custom mixers P-49 and P-50. The water needed for the columns is supplied by distributor-57.

The last precipitation step is diafiltration (P-53). The membrane in this unit concentrates the pectin solution and then filters out small molecule impurities. The filtrate is then concentrated by a reverse osmosis membrane (P-54)

### 2.1.1.5 Pectin Precipitation

The purified pectin solution is sent to an evaporator feed (P-58) and through a preheater (P-59) to a four-stage evaporator (P-60) as shown in Fig. 5. The vapor from the last stage is condensed in a condenser (P-61) and pushed to a mixer (P-63). It is combined with the liquid condensed from P-64 and recycled back to the extraction section for use in the reactors.

The purified and concentrated pectin solution is mixed with a recycled aqueous solution of isopropanol (IPA) in P-64. The desired IPA concentration is adjusted using a custom mixer (P-65) and sent to the pectin precipitation reactor (P-66). In this flow reactor, the precipitation of the solubilized proteins was modeled as a reaction with the following stoichiometry:

+ → 1 Pectin solids conversion

The pectin precipitate is separated in a decade centrifuge (P-67). More IPA solution is added to the pectin solids using a custom mixer (P-68). The solution is then pressed (in P-69) in order to concentrate and recover the pectin solids. The press produces the cake. The filtrate of the press, composed of aqueous IPA solution and trace amounts of washed-out impurities, is recycled back for use in pectin precipitation. Finally, the pectin solids are dried in a dryer (P-70) and cooled (P-71). This is the main product of the process.
