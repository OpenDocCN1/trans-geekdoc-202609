# 2. The Origin and Purpose of Veggie Meats

Let me begin by saying something about veggie meat alternatives and their evolution. Believe it or not, they have a long history. A popular example is a gluten product that nowadays goes by the name of "seitan”. Seitan dates back to century China. Buddhist monks accidentally discovered seitan by letting dough soak in water, which caused the starch to dissolve obtaining pure wheat gluten (Shortruff al. 2014). Most of gluten obtained is a chewy and edible substance that is sustained and cooked just as it were a piece of traditional meat. Seitan is possibly the simplest of all veggie meats. Today, most grocery stores sell even wheat flour to make home-made seitan.

Also, veggie meat can be made from soy, legumes, seeds, and fungi (Bonny et al. 2015, Asgar et al. 2010). Another popular product is tofu, which was consumed as early as 965 CE. Other products include yuba and tempeh, also used for decades by some people (Bohr 2019, Shurtle and Ayoyagi 2014). These products are still used in this many day when veggie dishes that emulate meat-based dishes. In the West, the Seventh-day Adventist company Loma Foods started in the late 1800s making the earliest examples of soy- and wheat-based meats (Land 2005). In the sixties, British scientists discovered the microorganism *Fusarium venenatum*, a high-protein substance that was used (Wiebe 2002).

Thus, veggie meat have a long history. Since their inception, the market has been growing exponentially as a result of increasing social demands. The growth is due to many people, the most important of which is that veggie meat products have become more and more appetizing. In fact, veggie meat are not only consumed by vegans and vegetarians. Even, traditional meat eaters enjoy veggie meat. Not only do people enjoy veggie meats, but also all sorts of plant-based analogues, such as plant-based eggs, milk, cheese, and veggie. Veggie meat has gained popularity that the market will be increasing over $21.2 billion US per year (Bohr 2019, Shurtle and Ayoyagi 2014).
