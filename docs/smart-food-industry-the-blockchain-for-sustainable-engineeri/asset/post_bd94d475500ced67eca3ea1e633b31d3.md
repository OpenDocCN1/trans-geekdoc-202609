Whey is defined as a by-product of cheese production that can be produced as sweet or acidic, as a result of the coagulation of casein obtained by separating from the curd after cutting process (Božić et al. 2014, Yıldırım ve Guzeler 2013). Whey basically contains half of the dairy ingredients, such as lactose, proteins, and a low amount of fat. Calcium, phosphate, lactic acid and lactose content of whey is generally lower than that of acidic whey (Božić et al. 2014). The nutritional composition of sweet and acidic whey is presented in Table 9. Whey proteins are stated to have a higher degree of bioavailability than animal-derived proteins, including eggs. In addition, whey contains bioactive peptides that have positive effects on health such as antimicrobial, antioxidant, antithrombotic and antihypertensive effects. In cheese production, most of the carbohydrates within the milk pass into the whey, 90% being lactose as the major component. Lactate facilitates the absorption of calcium and phosphorus in the body, and improves the digestion of fat and other nutrients. Also, some water-soluble vitamins are transferred into whey. Among them, riboflavin gives whey its characteristic color (Božić et al. 2014).

Another byproduct of the dairy industry is buttermilk (Skryplonek et al. 2019). Buttermilk contains water-soluble components (lactose, casein, whey) found in milk (Skryplonek et al. 2019, Ali 2019). In addition, milk contains about 0.12 mg/g of phospholipids, while buttermilk contains about seven times more (0.89 mg/g), as the milk fat globule membrane is rich in phospholipids (Barukçi et al. 2019). The cholesterol-reducing and anti-inflammatory properties of the milk fat globule membrane have shown to have a positive effect on human health. Vitamin A, carotenoids, and fatty acid-binding proteins in buttermilk also have a beneficial effect on health such as the anticancer properties (Skryplonek et al. 2019). Good dairy-products, whey, buttermilk and eggs have been used in the food industry to produce value-added functional foods, to the developing technology and elevated amounts of scientific studies.

Emulsification, gelation, foaming stability and viscosity are important functional properties of whey proteins. Accordingly, whey-based beverages or whey-added foods have been developed using powdered, denaturized or diluted whey (Barukçi et al. 2019). Some potential uses of whey include the enrichment of taste, the production of salami and sausages with fresh yeast-fermented whey proteins, and hardening the yogurt stretch with whey protein concentrates or whey power (Dinçoğlu and Ardıç 2012). In a study conducted by Pareek et al. (2014) onated orange juice drink containing 70% orange juice and 30% whey, it was observed that there was an increase in vitamin C, sodium and potassium levels compared to orange juice. Yetim et al. (2001) stated that adding whey to the frisbutter sugar formation increased the emulsion stability, chemical and sensory properties. Bilgin et al. (2006) conducted a comparative study by adding different rates of pasteurized whey or buttermilk to the bread formulation. Authors reported that the buttermilk added bread had a higher water absorption capacity than whey containing bread. This may be associated with the increased hydration capacity of whey and buttermilk due to water-soluble components such as lactose, albumin and globulins. Breads containing buttermilk showed more browning in crust color, as more intense Maillard and caramelization reactions occur during baking due to higher lactose and protein content. Skrypronek et al. (2019) carried out a study using buttermilk as a raw material in the production of quark cheese. Although the hardness of the produced cheese was improved, it showed similar physicochemical properties with classical quark. This study has shown that buttermilk is a successful alternative. Sheth and Hirdyani (2016) developed a buttermilk-based beverage containing barley and fructosaccharides which was highly appreciated by the sensory analysis panel. Also, Mudgil et al. (2016) increased the viscosity of the buttermilk by adding aloe vera juice and a new cultured beverage was formulated using dietary.

It is known that dairy-products have a wide range of uses in different industries, apart from the food industry. Biogas, which is formed as a result of fermentation of organic wastes, is an alternative energy source for heating or electricity generation (Sozer and Yaldz 2006). In whey, biogas can be produced by anaerobic digestion of buttermilk and used as fertilizer. In addition, the biomass from biological digestion can be converted into renewable energy (TÜ 2016). Andrade et al. (2019) produced edible film from whey proteins and used in packaging, and proved that the whey film inhibited oxygen permeability and lipid oxidation. Whey protein film was also applied to various foods such as chicken breast (Fernández-Pe et al. 2014), salmon (Boyaci et al. 2016) and Ricotta cheese (Di Pierro et al. 2011). Results showed an inhibitory effect obtained by crystallization of the remaining liquid after removing proteins, fats and minerals in the whey can be used in the production of special diet. The utilization of eggs in various industries depends on its main component calcium carbonate, which contains about 40% (Wei et al. 2019, 2020b). Since extraction of calcium from the eggshell is not difficult due to high solubility, eggshells can be used as calcium supplement. Daengrok et al. (2002) has developed a new functional food by converting calcium carbonate into eggshell powder, used in fermented pork sausage. A study conducted by De Paula et al. (2014), calcium citrate produced from eggs was added to roasted and ground coffee. Authors observed no significant difference in the sensory properties of coffee, even an increase in calcium bioavailability was achieved. The studies regarding the calcium fortification in bakery products by adding eggshell powder directly to formulations include chocolate cake (Ray et al. 2017), muffins (Afzal et al. 2020), and white bread (Platon et al. 2020). Eggshells can also be used as calcium supplement for animal feeds to meet the daily calcium requirements of animals. It is reported that eggshell powder used in poultry feeds plays an important role in providing calcium source to hens for laying eggs. Furthermore, calcium obtained from eggshell can also be used in the pharmaceutical industry for patients having calcium deficiency, since it is a cheap material unlike other calcium sources (Aditya et al. 2021).
