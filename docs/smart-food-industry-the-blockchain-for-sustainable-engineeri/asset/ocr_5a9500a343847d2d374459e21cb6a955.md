The solution is then transferred to the buffer tank (P-30), which ensures constant flow to feed the downstream process. The slurry is sent to the decanter (P-31), where most of the solids are removed. The aqueous stream from the decanter is sent to another tank (P-32) which feeds a starch centrifuge (P-33) where most of the remaining solids are removed. The light stream from the centrifuge is combined with filter water (via P-34) to remove almost all remaining solids using a screw press (P-36). The filtered solution is sent to another tank (P-37). Meanwhile, the solids collected from the decanter (P-31) are mixed with water to recover any solubilized pectin from the solids. This is achieved by mixing water with the solids in a mixer (P-38) and then pressed in a screw press (P-39). The filtrate stream is then fed to the same tank (P-32) as the decanter's aqueous stream, which the cake is combined with the suspended solids' streams and sent to a rotary dryer (P-43). The dryer is fired and uses natural gas to heat the dryer inlet air (via the mixer P-41) and the burner P-42. The burner was modeled as a plug flow reactor (PFR), with two reactions to represent the combustion of natural gas's methane and ethane. The reactions have the following molar stoichiometry:

1 Methane + 2 Oxygen $ightarrow$ 1 Carbon Dioxide + 2 Water (100% conversion)

The dried solids which exit P-43 are sold as animal feed with a moisture content of about 10%.

# 2.1.1.4 Pectin Purification

In this section (Fig. 4), the filtrate from the screw press (P-36) containing the solubilized pectins is sent to a buffer tank (P-37), and then loaded onto a granulated activated carbon (GAC) column (P-47). The column procedure has the following operations: loading, washing with NaOH and final washing with water.

During operation, the activated carbon binds the impurities and the furfurals formed during extraction, and the flavonoids originating from the peels. The peel is then washed with a sodium hydroxide-solution (from mixer P-46) and with water (from the water distribution system, P-57). The output from P-47 is then loaded onto a mixed ion exchange column (P-51).
