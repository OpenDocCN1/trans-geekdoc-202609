# A brief history of money

4000 BCE
Correspondence counters are used in Mesopotamia to tally equivalent quantities and values of particular commodities.

6th century BCE
Awards in Ancient Greek athletic competitions were measured in units of currency.

16th century CE
Private debt and credit markets become standard throughout European marketplaces.

14th century CE
Cheques are commonly used in southern Europe.

1694
The Bank of England is chartered, to manage the debt of the government.

1717
Isaac Newton creates UK's de facto gold standard by forcing all silver currency out of existence.

1921
Fox, Fowler, and Co., the last private bank in England still permitted to issue its own banknotes, ceases to mint currency.

1914
The outbreak of the First World War causes UK and many other European countries to vacate the gold standard.

1925–31
UK briefly adopts a gold exchange standard, only to cease as massive amounts of gold depart the country.

1933
President Franklin Roosevelt nationalizes gold within the US, and ceases making payments in gold, effectively departing the gold standard.
