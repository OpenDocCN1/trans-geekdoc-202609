XRP can even be used to facilitate the transaction, without the nodes at either end even knowing that a cryptocurrency is involved. These transactions are settled in about one minute, no matter where they are taking place.

By relying on a system of consensus among trusted nodes, the Ripple network allows worldwide transactions between participating banks with almost no overhead costs other than the price of currency exchange. The company hopes that it will cut down on the fees associated with international money exchange. However, only banks and companies can be nodes in the Ripple network. Ordinary customers must open up an account with one of these banks, and that company uses Ripple to send the money to another bank for them. This allows the bank to continue to set the price for the transaction. In this way, Ripple hopes to put blockchain ledger technology in the mainstream by marketing to those who already control the system, rather than disrupting the money transfer industry. As of February 2017, Ripple's XRP cryptocurrency is the third-largest cryptocurrency in total value, after bitcoin and Ethereum.
