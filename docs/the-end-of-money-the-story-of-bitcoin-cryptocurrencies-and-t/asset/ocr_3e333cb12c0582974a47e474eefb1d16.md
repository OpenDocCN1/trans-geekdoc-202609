Meanwhile, although the criminal complaint against Ulbricht listed the information gleaned from the CAPTCHA leak and the 'altiod' online postings as the evidence that led the FBI to identify DPR, it has since become apparent that the Tor network was compromised at least once. In 2014, researchers from Carnegie Mellon University, along with the Department of Defense, were able to compromise enough Tor nodes to expose the IP address of a Brian Farrell. This information was then used by the FBI to prosecute Farrell for his involvement in running one of the Silk Road's successor websites.Although the Tor Project has now claimed to have fixed this particular vulnerability, there have been speculations that perhaps this exploit was also used in the investigation of the original Silk Road.
