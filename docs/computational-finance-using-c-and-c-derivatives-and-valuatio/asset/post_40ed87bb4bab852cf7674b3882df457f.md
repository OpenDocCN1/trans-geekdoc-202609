### FX Call

The time $t$ value (in domestic currency) of an FX call to buy one unit of foreign currency can be found from equation (4.4.55) and substitutions in (7.3.8). We have

$C_d(t) = X_d^f \exp(-r^f(T-t))N(d_1) - K \exp(-r^d(T-t))N(d_2),$

$d_1 = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}} \left(\log\frac{X_d^f(t)}{K} + (r^d - r^f)(T-t) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2(T-t)\right),$

$d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T-t}.$

where $K$ is the strike rate that has been agreed for pay one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency, and $\sigma$ is the implied foreign/domestic currency exchange rate volatility which may depend on effects such as time to maturity, volatility smile, etc. In practice, the following modified version of equation (7.3.9) is usually used

$C_d(t) = X_d^f \exp(-r^f(T-t))N(d_1) - K \exp(-r^d(T-t))N(d_2),$

where $P$ is the number of units of foreign currency and all other symbols have their previous meanings. The value $C_b(t)$ of the call in base currency can be found by using $C_b(t) = X_b^f(t)$. From equation (7.3.12),

$C_b(t) = X_b^f(t) \exp(-r^f(T-t))N(d_1) - K \exp(-r^d(T-t))N(d_2),$

where we have used fact that $X_d^f(t) = X_b^f(t)$, $DF(t,T) = \exp(-r^f(T-t))$, and $DF(t,T) = \exp(-r^d(T-t))$. We can also re-express the values for $d_1$ in equation (7.3.10)

$d_1 = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}} \left(\log\frac{X_d^f(t)}{X_b^f(t)} + \log\frac{DF(t,T)}{FD(t,T)} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2(T-t)\right),$

where we have used that $\log DF(t,T) = -r^f(T-t)$ and $\log FD(t,T) = -r^d(T-t),$

and

$\log\left(\frac{X_d^f(t)}{K}\right) = \log\left(\frac{X_b^f(t)}{K X_b^d(t)}\right) = \log\left(\frac{X_b^f(t)}{K_d^f(t)}\right)$

We note that the term $K_d$ is the strike in units of base currency, that is the amount that has been agreed to pay for one unit of foreign currency in units of base currency. In the case $N(d_1) = N(d_2) = 1$ (i.e. there is no uncertainty), equation (7.3.13) becomes

$C_b(t) = X_b^f(t) DF^f(t,T) - K X_b^d(t) DF^d(T),$

which is the same as already given in equation (7.3.4) for FX Forward.

### FX Put

The time $t$ value of the corresponding put in units of base currency is

$P_b(t) = -X_b^f(t) DF^f(t,T) N(-d_1) + K X_b^f(t) DF^d(t,T) N(-d_2). (7.3.15)$

where the symbols have the same meanings as the FX call.

## 7.4 CREDIT DERIVATIVES

Credit derivatives take into account the fact that a counterparty may not honour (by bankruptcy, etc.) the obligations set out in a given financial contract. In order to obtain the time $t$ value of the probability that the counterparty (and thus the contract) will survive until some time $T > t$, we denote the survival probability between times $t$ and $T$ by $S(t,T)$, and we compute value from the hazard rate.

### The Hazard Rate

As previously mentioned, the survival probability between times $t$ and $T$, where $T > t$, is denoted by $S(t,T)$. This means that the probability of default between times $t$ and $T$ is

$P_{def}(t,T) = 1 - S(t,T)$

and the probability of default, as seen from time $t$, between times $T_1$ and $T_2$ is

$P_{def}(T_1,T_2) = S(t,T_1) - S(t,T_2), \quad T_2 > T_1. (7.4.1)$

The time $t$ discrete hazard rate between times $T$ and $T + \Delta T$, denoted by $H(t,T,T+\Delta T)$, is defined by

$H(t,T,T+\Delta T) = \frac{1}{\Delta T} \frac{P_{def}(t,T,T+\Delta T)}{S(t,T+\Delta T)} = \frac{1}{\Delta T} \frac{S(t,T) - S(t,T+\Delta T)}{S(t,T+\Delta T)}.$

which means that

$H(t,T+\Delta T) = - \frac{S(t,T+\Delta T) - S(t,T)}{\Delta T} = - \left\{ \frac{1}{S(t,T+\Delta T)} \right\} (7.4.2)$

As $\Delta T \to 0, H(t,T+\Delta T) \to h(t,T)$, where $h(t,T)$ is termed the continuous hazard rate between times $t$ and $T$. We observe that as $\Delta T = 0$,

$h(t,T) = - \frac{\partial S(t,T)}{\partial T} \frac{1}{S(t,T)} (7.4.3)$

Now equation (7.4.3) can be re-expressed

$h(t,s) = - \frac{\partial \ln(S(t,s))}{\partial s} = -\frac{\partial \ln(S(t,s))}{\partial s} (7.4.4)$

where $s > t$, and we have

$\frac{1}{S(t,T)} = - \frac{\partial \ln(S(t,T))}{\partial T}$

Integrating equation (7.4.4) yields

$\int_{s=t}^T h(t,s) ds = -\int_{s=t}^T d \ln(S(t,s)) = -\ln(S(t,T)) - \ln(S(t,t)). (7.4.5)$

where we have used $S(t,t) = 1$ and $\ln(S(t,t)) = 0$. So using equation (7.4.5), the survival probability can be expressed as

$S(t,T) = \exp \left( -\int_{s=t}^T h(t,s) ds \right) (7.4.6)$

or

$S(t,T) = \exp \{I(t,T)\} (7.4.7)$

where $I(t,T)$ is the cumulative hazard rate from time $t$ to $T$. It is useful to approximate $I(t,T)$ as follows:

$I(t,T) \approx \sum_{i=1}^k h(t_{i-1},t_i) (t_i - t_{i-1}). (7.4.8)$

where $t_0 = 0, t_k = T$, and the following section details how to estimate $h(t_{i-1},t_i)$ from market observables. One way representing the hazard rates is to use a hazard curve which is defined as

$\{t_i, I(0,t_i)\}, i = 0, \dots, n. (7.4.9)$

If we define $t_0 = 0$ and $t_n = T$, then equation (7.4.9) becomes

$\{t_1, I(0,t_1)\}, \{t_2, I(0,t_2)\}, \dots, \{t_n, I(0,t_n)\}, (7.4.10)$

where we have used the fact that $I(0,t_0) = 0$.

### Estimating the Hazard Rate from Market Observables

From equation (7.4.10), we know that the $t$ forward rate between times $T_1$ and $T_2$ is given by

$F(t, T_1, T_2) = \frac{1}{T_2 - T_1} \left\{ \frac{DF(t, T_2)}{DF(t, T_1)} \right\}. (7.4.11)$

where $DF(t, T_1)$ and $DF(t, T_2)$ are the prices of non-defaultable coupon bonds with maturities $T_1$ and $T_2$ respectively. Letting $T_1 = T$ and $T_2 = T + \Delta T$, we obtain

$F(t, T, \Delta T) = \frac{1}{\Delta T} \left\{ \frac{DF(t, T+\Delta T)}{DF(t, T)} \right\}. (7.4.12)$

which can be re-expressed

$F(t, T, \Delta T) = -\left\{ \frac{DF(t, T+\Delta T) - DF(t, T)}{\Delta T} \right\} \frac{1}{DF(t, T+\Delta T)}, (7.4.13)$

If $\Delta T \to 0$, then $F(t, T, \Delta T) \to f(t, T)$, and from equation (7.4.13), we obtain

$f(t, T) = -\frac{\partial DF(t, T)}{\partial T} \frac{1}{DF(t, T)} = -\frac{\partial \ln(DF(t, T))}{\partial T}, (7.4.14)$

Using equation (7.4.14), the instantaneous forward rate computed using non-defaultable coupon bond bond and the corresponding instantaneous forward rate computed from default coupon bond prices is

$f(t, T) = -\frac{\ln(DF(t, T))}{\partial T}, (7.4.15)$

$\bar{f}(t, T) = -\frac{\ln(\overline{DF}(t, T))}{\partial T}, (7.4.16)$

Taking the survival probability $S(t,T)$ to be the ratio of the prices of defaultable and non-defaultable zero coupon bonds, and now from equations (7.4.15) and (7.4.16), we have

$S(t, T) = \frac{\overline{DF}(t, T)}{DF(t, T)}, (7.4.17)$

$\bar{f}(t, T) - f(t, T) = -\frac{\ln(\overline{DF}(t, T) - DF(t, T))}{\partial T} = -\frac{1}{\partial T} \ln \left\{ \frac{\overline{DF}(t, T)}{DF(t, T)} \right\}. (7.4.18)$

### 7.4.1 Defaultable Bond

For a defaultable bond, we need to take into account the fact that the issuer may default, that is, cease to make the bond payments. The time $t$ value of a defaultable bond is

$\bar{B}(t) = P DF(t,t_m) + \sum_{j=1}^m C_j DF(t,t_j) + PR \sum_{j=1}^m DF(t,t_j)\{S(t,t_{j-1}) - S(t,t_j)\},$

where $t_m$ is the maturity of the bond, $P$ is the principal, $C_j$ is the value of $jth$ coupon, $R$ is the recovery rate, $S(t,t_j)$ probability that the bond will survive until time $t_j$, and the zero coupon defaultable bond prices are defined by $\bar{DF}(t,t_m) = S(t,t_m)DF(t,t_m)$ and $\bar{DF}(t,t_j) = DF(t,t_j)S(t,t_j)$.

### 7.4.2 Credit Default Swap

A Credit Default Swap (CDS) is a contract between two counterparties in which one (A) pays periodic payments to the other (B) in order to obtain protection against the default of a reference credit. In the event of default B pays A, the default payment of $1 - R$, where $R$ is the recovery rate, and contract ceases. The time $t$ value of the CDS to A, the purchaser of the, is

$CDS(t) = - \sum_{j=1}^m C_j DF(t,t_j) + P (1 - R) \sum_{j=1}^m DF(t,t_j)\{S(t,t_{j-1}) - S(t,t_j)\}.$
