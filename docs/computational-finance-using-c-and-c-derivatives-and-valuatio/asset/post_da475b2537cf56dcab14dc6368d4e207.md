
## 7.4.3 Total Swap

A total return swap (TRS) is a synthetic replication of the return of a reference asset (bond) $B$. The receiver of the TRS receives the coupon payments of the reference asset during the life of the swap for making periodic coupon payments at the risk-free rate plus an agreed margin. In the event of default, the receiver makes a default payment to the payer equal to the agreed price of the reference asset less the price at default, and the transaction terminates. If there is no default, the difference between the initial asset (bond) price $B_0$ and the price at maturity $B(t_m)$ is settled by the payer and the receiver, with the receiver paying (receiving) if the (bond) is worth less (more) at maturity. The maturity of the reference asset (bond) may be longer than the maturity $t_m$.

To the receiver of the reference asset coupons, a TRS has value $TRS$, which is given by

$TRS = $ total bond return – total floating coupon payments of the swap
total bond return = total bond fixed coupons over the duration of the TRS + increase in the bond value at maturity of the TRS – default payment, if the bond defaults over the duration of the TRS.
The value of $TRS_t$ at time $t$

$TRS_{(t)} =$

$= \hat{C} \sum_{t_j=t_1}^{t_m} DF(t,t_j) \quad \text{fixed reference bond payments}$
$+ P \left\{ \frac{B(t_m) - B_0}{B_0} \right\} DF(t,t_m) \quad \text{increase in value of reference bond}$
$- \sum_{t_j=t_1}^{t_m} C_j DF(t,t_j) \quad \text{floating payments of swap at LIBOR + margin}$
$- P (1 - R) \sum_{t_j=t_1}^{t_m} DF(t,t_j) \{S(t,t_{j-1}) - S(t,t_j)\} \quad \text{bond default payments,}$

and the reference bond satisfies

$B(t_r) = 1$
$B(t_m) = \overline{DF(t_m,R)}t + \hat{C} \sum_{t_j=t_m}^{t_r} DF(t_m,t_j)|t$
$+ R \sum_{t_j=t_m}^{t_r} DF(t_m,t_j)|t S(m,t_{j-1}) - S(m,t_j)\}$.

All symbols already defined in this chapter have their previous meanings. For addition,

$P$ – the swap principal.
$\tilde{C}$ – the fixed coupon of the reference bond.
$t_m$ – the swap maturity.
$t_R$ – the maturity of the reference bond.
$B_0$ – the initial price of the reference bond.
$B(t_m)$ – the final price of the reference bond (at swap maturity).
$C_j$ – the floating coupon payment at time $t_j$. It is computed as
$C_j = P\{F(t_{j-1}, t_j) + margin^{TRS}\{t_j - t_{j-1}\}$
$DF(t_1, t_2)$ – the discount factor between times $t_1$ and $t_2$ (as seen from time $t_i$) is

$DF(t_1, t_2) = \frac{D(t_2)}{D(t_1)}$

$\overline{DF(t_1, t_2)}|t$ – the default discount factor between times $t_1$ and $t_2$ (as seen from time $t_i$)

$\overline{DF(t_1, t_2)}|t = \frac{\overline{DF(t_1, t_2)}}{\overline{DF(t_1, t_1)}} = \frac{DF(t_1, t_2) S(t_2)}{DF(t_1, t_1) S(t_1)}$

$\overline{DF(t_1, t_1)}$ – the default discount factor between times $t$ and $t_1$ is

$DF(t_1, t_1) = DF(t, t_1)S(t, t_1)$

## 7.5 EQUITY DERIVATIVES

### 7.5.1 TRS

An equity TRS consists of an equity leg (whose coupons are determined by the change in value of the equity) and a floating leg which pays according to the forwards of the interest rate.
Here, we ignore the effect of equity dividends and also assume that the currencies for both the floating and equity legs of the swap are.

#### Equity Leg

Let the equity leg be specified by coupon payments at times $t_k, k=1, \dots, N_e$, where $\tau_k = t_k - t_{k-1}$. If time $t$ the next coupon payment occurs at $t_i$, then the value of the equity leg is

$V_e(t) = \frac{S(t)}{DF(t, t_i)} - L(t_{i-1}) DF(t, t_i) + \sum_{k=i+1}^{N_e} \frac{S(t)}{DF(t, t_k)} \frac{S(t)}{DF(t_{k-1})} DF(t, t_k), (7.5.1)$

Where $S(t)$ is the equity at current $t$, $L(t_{i-1})$ is the reset value of the equity at time $t_{i-1}$, and $DF(t, t_k)$ is the discount factor between times $t$ and $t_k, t_k > t$. It can be seen that the value of the equity $S(t_i)$ at time $t_i$ is obtained by inflating the current value, $S(t)$ by the reciprocal of the discount factor, $DF(t, t_i)$, that is $S(t_i) = S(t)/DF(t, t_i)$.

#### Floating Leg

Let the floating leg have coupon payments at times $t_m, m = 1, \dots, N_f$, where $\tau_f = t_m - t_{m-1}$. If the next coupon is at time $t_j$, then the value of floating leg is

$V_f(t) = L(t_{j-1})(R(t_{j-1}) + \Phi) \tau_f DF(t,t_j) + \sum_{m=j+1}^{N_f} \frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_{m-1})} \{F(t,t_{m-1}, t_m) + \tau_f DF(t,t_m)\}, (7.5.2)$

where $\Phi$ is the margin added to the forward rate used to compute coupons, $F(t,t_{m-1}, t_m)$ is the time $t$ forward rate between $t_{m-1}$ and $t_m$, and $R(t_{j-1})$ is the reset rate that is used between $t_{j-1}$ and $t_j$ to compute the payment at time $t_j$.

#### Payer Equity TRS

The owner of a payer TRS swaps the equity leg, and thus at time $t$ the swap has value

$ERS_{Sp}(t) = V_f(t) - V_e(t). (7.5.3)$

The receiver of a TRS receives the equity leg coupons, and the value of the swap is

$ERS_{Tc}(t) = -V_f(t) + V_e(t). (7.5.4)$

We will now compute an expression for the value of a payer equity swap. Since

$F(t,t_{m-1}, t_m) = \frac{1}{(t_m - t_{m-1})} \left( \frac{DF(t,t_{m-1})}{DF(t_m)} - 1 \right),$

we have

$\tau_f F(t,t_{m-1}, t_m) = \left( \frac{DF(t,t_{m-1})}{DF(t_m)} - 1 \right), (7.5.5)$

Substituting equation (7.5.5) into equation (7.5.2) and using equation (7.5.3), we obtain

$ERS_{Sp}(t) = \sum_{m=j+1}^{N_f} \frac{S(t)DF(t,t_m)}{DF(t,t_{m-1})} - 1\} + \sum_{m=j+1}^{N_f} \frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_{m-1})} \Phi \tau_f DF(t,t_m) + L(t_{j-1})(R(t_{j-1}) + \Phi) \tau_f DF(t,t_j) - \sum_{k=i+1}^{N_k} \left( 1 - \frac{DF(t,t_k)}{DF(t,t_{k-1})} \right) - \left( \frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_i)} \right\} DF(t,t_i). (7.5.6)$

If $N_e = N_f = N$, and $\tau_e = \tau_f = \tau$, then all the equity and float leg coincide and equation (7.5.6) simplifies to

$ETRS_p(t) = S(t) \sum_{k=j+1}^{N} \frac{DF(t,t_k)}{DF(t,t_j)} \Phi \tau DF(t,t_k) + L(R + \Phi) \tau DF(t,t_j) - \left\{\frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_j)} - L\right\}DF(t,t_j). (7.5.7)$

Thus if the spread $\Phi$ is zero, the value of the equity TRS is

$ETRS_p(t) = L(t_{j-1}) R(t_{j-1}) \tau DF(t,t_j) - \left\{\frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_j)} - L(t_{j-1})\right\}DF(t,t_j). (7.5.8)$

In these circumstances, the value of the equity TRS at time $t$ only depends on the current, which is extended from $t_{j-1}$ to $t_j$, where $t_{j-1} < t_j$.

#### Equity Swap

A special case of equity TRS is an equity swap. Here, one party (say A) pays the total returns on one equity and receives (from party B) the returns on another, with interest on the net difference of the two assets. An equity swap, ESWP, can be constructed from a long position in one equity and a short position in another, with the same payment dates and currency. If the individual equity TRS are denoted by $ERS^p_1$ and $ERS^p_2$, then the value of the equity swap at time $t$ is

$ESWP(t) = ERS^p_1(t) - ERS^p_2(t). (7.5.9)$

Substituting equation (7.5.8) into (7.5.9), we have

$ESWP(t) = L(t_{j-1}) R(t_{j-1}) \tau DF(t,t_j) - \left\{\frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_j)} - L(t_{j-1})\right\}DF(t,t_j) - \left\{\frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_j)} - L(t_{j-1})\right\}DF(t,t_j)$ 

so the value of the equity swap for party A is

$ESWP(t) = - \left\{\frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_j)} - L(t_{j-1})\right\}DF(t,t_j) + \left\{L(t_{j-1}) - L(t_{j-1})\right\}R(t_{j-1}) \tau DF(t,t_j) + \left\{\frac{S(t)}{DF(t,t_j)} - L(t_{j-1})\right\}DF(t,t_j)$

### 7.5.2 Equity Quantos

The Black-Scholes equation can also be used to price Equity quanto options (Reiner, 1992). We have the following processes:

$dS^f = \mu_{S^f} dt + \sigma_{S^f} dW_{S^f},$
$dX^f = \mu_{X^f} dt + \sigma_{X^f} dW_{X^f},$
$dB^f = r^f dt,$
$dB^d = r^d dt.$

Here, $S^f$ is the price (in foreign currency units) of the foreign stock, $B^d$ the domestic money market account where money grows at the (constant) risk-free interest rate $r^d$, $B^f$ the foreign money market account where money grows at the (constant) risk-free rate $r^f$, and $X^f$ the foreign exchange rate, which is the value of one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency. The tradables for the domestic money market account priced in currency units are $X^f B^d$ and the foreign stock priced in domestic currency $X^f S^f$.

We know from (4.4.30) that there is a probability (the neutral measure) $Q$ under which the relative price of tradables such as equities are martingales. We also established that under $Q$ the process followed by these tradables is GBM with constant drift $r^d$. For the process of the equity $S^d$

$dS^d = r^d S^d dt + \sigma_{S^d} dW_{S^d}.$

Similarly, the process followed by the price of a foreign equity $S^f$ under the foreign risk neutral measure $F$ is

$dS^f = r^f S^f dt + \sigma_{S^f} dW_{S^f}.$

However, the process followed by the price of a foreign equity $S^f$ under the domestic risk neutral measure $Q$ is

$dS^f = S^f (r^f + \alpha) dt + \sigma_{S^f} dW_{S^f},$

where $\alpha$ (the quanto adjustment) is to be determined. We will derive the value for $\alpha$ and then use this to price both forwards and quanto options.

#### Determining the Quanto Adjustment, $\alpha$

Since $X^f_d B^f$ and $X^f_d S^f$ are domestic tradables, it means that the relative prices $(X^f_d B^f/B^d)$ and $(X^f_d S^f/B^d)$ are also martingales under the probability measure $Q$. Since $(X^f_d B^f/B^d)$ is a martingale,

$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{X^f_d B^f}{B^d}\right) = \sigma_X dW_{Q^X}.$
