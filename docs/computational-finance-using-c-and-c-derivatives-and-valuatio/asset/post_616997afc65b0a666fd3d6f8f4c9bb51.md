## Appendix C | Standard Statistical Results

### C.1 THE LAW OF LARGE NUMBERS

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots$ be a sequence of identically distributed random variables (IID), with expected value $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. Define the sequence of averages

$$
y_n = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n}, \quad n=1, 2, \dots
$$

Then the law of large numbers states that $y_n$ converges to $\mu$ as $n \to \infty$, that is $Var[y_n] \to 0$.

The mean of $y_n$ is

$$
E[y_n] = \frac{1}{n}(E[x_1] + E[x_2] + \dots + E[x_n]) = \frac{1}{n}n\mu = \mu.
$$

For the variance of $y_n$, we have

$$
Var[y_n] = Var\left[\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n}\right] = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n Var[x_i] = \frac{1}{n^2}n\sigma^2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{n},
$$

where we have used the fact that the variance of the sum of independent variables is the sum of their variances, see C.2. We have therefore as $n \to \infty, Var[y_n] \to 0$.

### C.2 THE CENTRAL LIMITOREM

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots$ be a sequence of IID random variables, with expected value $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. If we define $u_i = x_i - \mu$, then

$$
E[u_i] = E[u] = 0, \quad Var[u_i] = E[u_i^2] = \sigma^2.
$$

Let

$$
s_n = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n u_i}{n} + n\mu.
$$

So

$$
s_n = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n}.
$$

We introduce the normalized value $z_n$, as follows:

$$
z_n = \frac{s_n - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} u_i.
$$

The central limit theorem states that as $n$ tends to infinity the probability distribution of $z_n$ tends to a normal distribution with zero mean and unit variance, mathematically $z_n \to N(0,1)$ as $n \to \infty$.

Proof. From Appendix C.5 equation (C.5.3),

$$
M_{z_n} = E[\exp(tz_n)] = E \left[ \exp \left\{ \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} u_i\right\}\right].
$$

and using (C.5.5)

$$
M_{z_n} = M_u \left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^n.
$$

Equation (C.5.1) then yields

$$
M_u \left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \approx 1 + \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} E[u] + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^2 E[u^2] + \dots..
$$

As $n \to \infty, t/\sqrt{n} \to 0$,

$$
M_u \left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \to 1 + \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} E[u] + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^2 E[u^2] = 1 + \frac{t^2}{2n}.
$$

Thus,

$$
M_u \left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^n = \left( 1 + \frac{t^2}{2n} \right)^n \to 1 + \frac{t^2}{2n} \text{ as } n \to \infty,
$$

where we have used the fact that $t \ll 1$ Grimmett and Welsh (1986).
We have therefore shown that as $n \to \infty$

$$
M_{z_n}(t) \to e^{\frac{t^2}{2}}.
$$

Thus from Appendix D.1, the moment generating function $M_z(t)$ for a standard normal distribution ($\mu = 0, \sigma^2 = 1$) is

$$
M_z(t) = e^{\frac{t^2}{2}}, \text{ where } z \sim N(0,1).
$$

Thus, we have proved that $z_n \to N(0,1)$ as $n \to \infty$.
