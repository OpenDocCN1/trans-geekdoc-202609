# Basis Swap

This is very similar to an IRS, but now there are two floating legs each with their associated principal amount.

For example, leg 1 could be associated with the 1-month LIBOR (London Inter-Bank Offer Rate) and have a schedule of monthly payments, while leg 2 could use the 3-month LIBORs and have quarterly payments. In this case, the forward rates and discount factors for leg 1 would be computed using the 1-month LIBOR yield curve, and the forward rates and discount factors for leg 2 will be computed using the 3-month LIBOR yield curve.

We will use the subscripts 1 and 2 to denote quantities associated with legs 1 and 2 respectively. The payment times associated with leg 1 are $t'_{1,i}=i=1\dots,n_1$. While those for leg 2 are $t'_{2,i}=i=1\dots,n_2$, (and for this example) $n_1=3n_2$.

If counterparty A makes the quarterly payments (that receives the payments made on leg 1), the time $t$ value of the basis swap is

```
$BS(t) = C_1DF(t,t_k) + FLT_1(t) - C_2DF(t,t_k) - FLT_2(t), \quad (7.2.21)
```

where we have similar notation to that used in equation (7.2.12),

```
$FLT_1(t) = P_1DF(t,t_k) - DF(t,t_n)) \quad (7.2.22)
```

and

```
$FLT_2(t) = P_2DF(t,t_k) - DF_2(t,t_n)) \quad (7.2.23)
```

In equations (7.2.22) and (7.2.23), $P_1$ is the principal for leg 1 and $P_2$ is the principal for leg 2. The time of the next coupon payment for leg 1 is $t_{k_1}$, while for leg 2 is $t_{k_2}$; in addition, we have used the fact that $t_{n_1}=t_{n_2}=t_n$
