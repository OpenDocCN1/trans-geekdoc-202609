Suppose that the price of shares is at 580, and I think that they will continue to rise considerably. . . . I ask people who want write options how much they are asking for the obligation to sell me shares at 600 per share on a particular date. When we have agreed the premium, I immediately have transferred it to them at the bank. Now I know that I lose no more than the amount that I have deposited at the bank. If the shares rise above 600, the amount above 600 is my profit. And if they are worth less than that, my peace of mind, sanity and honour is not in danger.

# 9.3.3 Other Early Financial Centres

# London

In 1665, Gresham made a proposal to build a Royal Exchange, modelled on the Antwerp Exchange – the building was opened in 1570 by Queen Elizabeth I, see 9.9.

The overthrow of King James II by William in 1688 (known as the Glorious Revolution) meant that many of the speculative practices carried out in Amsterdam were adopted in England. Dutch investors and spectators also conducted a considerable amount of their British securities trading outside the Amsterdam Bourse at various locations in London, such as on the Royal Exchange and in Exchange Alley where coffee-house trading was conducted.

# New York

The name Wall Street comes from the seventeenth century when Wall Street formed the northern boundary of the New Amsterdam settlement, see Fig. 9.10. It was constructed as a protection from neighbouring colonists. In the 1640s, only picket and plank fences denoted plots and residences in the colony.
