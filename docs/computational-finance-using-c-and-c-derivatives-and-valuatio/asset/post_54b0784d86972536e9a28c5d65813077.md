In the binomial model, over the interval $\Delta t$, the variate $\log(S_{1,t})$ is only permitted to jump up or down by an variate $v_1 = \sigma_1\sqrt{\Delta t}$, and similarly the variate $\log(S_{2,t})$ is only permitted to jump up and down by the $v_2 = \sigma_2\sqrt{\Delta t}$. We will denote the probability of both $\log(S_{1,t})$ and $\log(S_{2,t})$ having an up-jump over $\Delta t$ by $P_{uu}$, and the probability of $\log(S_{1,t})$ having an up-jump and $\log(S_{2,t})$ having a down-jump by $P_{ud}$, etc. The mean values in equation (6.5.10) and equation (6.5.11) then yield

$E[\epsilon_{1,t}] = v_1(P_{uu} + P_{ud} - P_{du}) = \mu_1\Delta t$,
$E[\epsilon_{2,t}] = v_2(P_{uu} + P_{ud} - P_{du}) = \mu_2\Delta t$,

and the variance/covariance terms yield

$Var[\epsilon_{1,t}] = v_1^2(P_{uu} + P_{ud} + P_{du}) = \sigma_1^2\Delta t$,
$Var[\epsilon_{2,t}] = v_2^2(P_{uu} + P_{ud} + P_{du}) = \sigma_2^2\Delta t$,
$E[\epsilon_{1,t}\epsilon_{2,t}] = v_1v_2(P_{uu} + P_{ud} - P_{du}) = \rho\sigma_1\sigma_2\Delta t$,

where $\rho$ is the correlation coefficient between $\epsilon_{1,t}$ and $\epsilon_{2,t}$. We obtain

$P_{uu} + P_{ud} - P_{du} = \frac{\mu_1\Delta t}{\sigma_1}$
