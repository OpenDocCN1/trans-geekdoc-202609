### Quanto Swap

A quanto (also known as diff or differential swap) is an agreement in which one party makes floating rate payments based on the foreign forward rate while the other makes fixed or floating payments based on the domestic interest rates.

Here, we consider quanto swaps in which the receiving floating coupons (in domestic currency) are computed using foreign forward rates.
The value of a standard swap (which all the currencies are domestic) can be found by using (7.2.90) and (7.2.74) to write

$Caplet(t_0) - Floorlet(t_0) = P\tau D^f(t_0, t_k)E^{Q^k} [max(F^f_k - K, 0) - max(K - F^f_k, 0)] = P\tau D^f(t_0, t_k)E^{Q^k} [F^f_k - K] = P\tau D^f(t_0, t_k)E^{Q^k} \{F^f(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) - K\}.$ (7.24)

From equation (7.2.94), we can see that Caplet(t_0) - Floorlet(t_0) is the value of a swap in which the owner pays the fixed rate $K$ and receives the floating rate $E^d(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)$.

## 7.3 FOREIGN EXCHANGE DERIVATIVES

Here, we consider derivatives based on the exchange rate between a domestic currency and a foreign currency. We will use the convention that quantities relating to the domestic currency will have the superscript $d$, while those for the foreign currency will have the superscript $f$. The notation for the various exchange rates is as follows:

- $X_t^f(t)$ is the spot value of one unit of foreign currency in domestic currency at time $t$,
- $X_t^b(t)$ is the spot value of one unit of foreign currency in base currency at time $t$,
- $X_t^d(t)$ is the spot value of one unit of domestic currency in base currency at time $t$,
- $X_t^f(t,T)$ is the (time $t$) forward value of one unit of foreign currency in domestic currency at time $T$.

### Covered Interest Arbitrage
When the current spot rate, $X_t^f(t)$, is known as Covered Interest Arbitrage, it is possible to obtain a value for future spot exchange rate – we denote this foreign exchange rate by $X_t^f(t,T)$, where $T > t$.

Let us consider the following scenarios:

Scenario A
At time $t$ an investor deposits one unit of foreign currency which grows at the (constant) foreign risk-free interest rate $r^f$. By time $T$ the initial amount will have increased to $1/DF^f(t,T)$ units of foreign currency, where $DF^f(t,T) = \exp(-r^f(T-t))$. The foreign currency is converted into domestic currency at the time $T$ forward rate $X_t^f(t)$ and yields $X_t^f(t)/DF^f(t,T)$ units of domestic currency.

Scenario B
At time $t$ an investor deposits $X_t^f(t)$ units of domestic currency (the scheme is equivalent to one unit of foreign currency), and this grows at the (constant) domestic risk-free interest rate $r^d$. At time $T$ the initial sum will have increased to $X_t^f(t)/DF^d(t,T)$ units of domestic currency, where $DF^d(t,T) = \exp(-r^d(T-t))$.

For arbitrage to occur the final amount of domestic currency in both scenarios must be the same - we assumed that there is no charge in converting one currency into another. We thus have

$\frac{X_d^f(t)}{DF^f(t,T)} = \frac{X_d^f(t)}{DF^d(t,T)}$

which means that the forward exchange rate, at time $T$, is

$X_d^f(t,T) = \frac{DF^f(t,T)}{DF^d(t,T)}, \quad (7.3.1)$

### 7.3.1 FX Forward
A foreign exchange (FX) forward is a contract to exchange an agreed amount of foreign currency at a future time $T$. If $P^f$ is the amount (number of units) of foreign currency, and $P^d$ is the amount (number of units) of domestic currency, then the value (in currency) of FX contract at time $T$ is

$FX_d(T) = P^f X_d^f(T) - P^d$.

The value of the contract at time $t$ is

$FX_d(t) = P^f X_d^f(t,T) - P^d DF^d(t),$

where $t < T$. Substituting for $X_d^f(T)$ from equation (7.3.1) then gives

$FX_d(t) = P^f X_d^f(t) \frac{DF^f(t,T)}{DF^d(t,T)} - P^d DF^d(t),$

which can be re-expressed as

$FX_d(t) = P^f X_d^f(t,T) DF^f(t) - P^d DF^d(t). \quad (7.3.2)$

The value of this FX contract in base currency is

$FX_b(t) = P^f X_b^f(t,T) DF^f(t) - P^d DF^d(t) X_b^d,$

that is,

$FX_b(t) = P^f X_b^f(t,T) DF^f(t) - P^d DF^d(t) X_b^d(t), \quad (7.3.3)$

where we have used $X_d^f(t) = X_d^d(t)$.

An alternative way of expressing (7.3.3) is as

$FX_b(t) = P^f X_b^f(t,T) DF^f(t) - K DF^d(t) X_b^d(t), \quad (7.3.4)$

where $K = P^d/P^f$. In the next section, we will see that $K$, the agreed rate to be paid for one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency, corresponds to the strike of an FX option.

### 7.3.2 European FX Option
Foreign exchange option can be priced using the Black Scholes formula, (Garman and Kohlhagen, 1983). There are three processes involved in foreign exchange options and, under the real world probability measure P, they

$dB^F = r^F dB^d,$
$dX_d^F = X_d^F \mu dt + X_d^F \sigma dW^P, \quad dW^P \sim N(0,dt),$
$dB^d = r^d B^d.$ (7.3.5)

where $X_d^F$ is the value of one unit of foreign currency in units of domestic currency, $B^d$ is the domestic money account where money grows at the (constant) risk-free rate $r^d$, $B^F$ is the foreign money account where money grows at the (constant) risk-free rate $r^f$ and $\sigma$ is the volatility of $X_d^F$. From a domestic point of view, there are only assets - the money market account $B^d$ and the value of the foreign money market account in domestic currency, $X_d^F$.

From Ito's product rule in Chapter 2 and using equation(2.6.3) with $X_1 = X_d^F$ and $X_2 = B^d$, we have

$d(X_d^F B^d) = (X_d^F B^d)\r^f + \mu\} dt + (X_d^F B^d)\sigma dW^P$.

We will now consider $B^d$ as the numeraire and the process for $B_d^F/B^d$ using the Ito rule given in Chapter 2. Substituting $X_1 = B_d^F$ and $X_2 = B^d$ in equation (2.6.8), we obtain

$d(\frac{B_d^F}{B^d}) = \frac{B_d^F}{B^d} d(\frac{B_d^F}{B^d})\{r^f - r^d + \mu\} dt + \frac{B_d^F}{B^d}\sigma dW^P$.

If we choose the probability measure Q such that

$dW^P = dQ - \frac{r^f - r^d}{\sigma} dt,\quad(7.3.6)$

then $(B_d^F/B^d)$ is a martingale since

$d(\frac{B_d^F}{B^d}) = (\frac{B_d^F}{B^d})\sigma dW^Q$.

Substituting for $dW^P$ in equation(7.3.5) yields

$dX_d^F = X_d^F \mu dt + X_d^F \sigma{\frac{r^f - r^d + \mu}{\sigma}} dt,\ndX_d^F = X_d^F \mu dt - X_d^F (r^f - r^d + \mu) dt + X_d^F \sigma dW^Q$.
