We will now consider the case in which the basis swap has been traded at time $t$ and shall assume that $C_1 = C_2 = 0$ and $t = t_{k_1} = t_{k_2}$. In addition, we will specify that principal exchange occurs at the start (time $t$) and end (time $t_n$) of the swap.

The cash flows associated with principal exchange at the start of the swap are in the opposite direction for the remainder of the swap, Hull (2003).

$$FLT_1(t) = P_1 - P_1 DF(t,t_n) + \{-P_1 + P_1 DF(t,t_n)\}$$

and

$$FLT_2(t) = P_2 - P_2 DF(t,t_n) + \{-P_2 + P_2 DF(t,t_n)\}$$

where the principal exchange terms are in the curly brackets and discount factors $DF(t,T)$ derived from the main currency curve rather than $DF_1(t,T)$ or $DF_2(t,T)$.

It can be seen from (7.2.21) that principal exchange at the start of the swap causes leg 2 to contribute the positive amount $P_2$ to the value of the swap, while leg 1 contributes the negative amount $P_2$ to the value of the swap. In contrast, principal exchange at the end of the swap results in leg 2 contributing the negative $-P_2 DF(t,t)$ to the swap value while leg 1 contributes the positive amount $P_1 DF(t,t)$. If $P_1 = P_2 = P$, principal exchange does not affect the value of the swap. It can also be seen from (7.2.24) if leg 1 used the main GBP currency curve of the 1-month LIBOR curve $DF_1(t,t_n) = DF(t,t)$, which would result in $FLT_1(t) = 0$. $DF_2(t,t_n) = DF(t,t)$ would mean $FLT_2(t) = 0$.

If the validation time $t$ is after the swap has started, then equation (7.2.21) and (7.2.25) need to be used to price the value of the swap, but equations be modified as follows:

$$FLT_1(t) = P_1 DF(t,t_{k_1}) + \{-P_1 DF(t,t_{k_n})\}$$

and

$$FLT_2(t) = P_2 DF(t,t_{k_2}) - P_2 DF(t,t_n) + \{P_2 DF(t,t_n)\}$$

We will now consider the timing of the coupon payment in relation to its associated forward rate affects the present value, $V_0$, of the coupon.

## Coupon Payment on Time

In this section, we will justify the approach we have adopted in obtaining the present value of future cash flows generated from vanilla swaps.

From equation (4.2.1), we know that the value at time $t_k$ of a coupon payment at time $t_k$ is

$$V_0 = DF(t_0, t_k) E^{Q^k} \frac{F(t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}, t_k)}{DF(t_k, t_k)} rP$$

where the symbols have their usual meaning, and we have chosen the numeraire to be the zero coupon bond which matures at time $t_k$. Since $DF(t_k,t_k) = 1$, we can write

$$V_0 = DF(t_0, t_k) E^{Q^k} [F(t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}, t_k)] rP$$

In Section 7.2.3, we show that $F(t,t_{k-1},t_k)$ follows the process

$$d(F(t,t_{k-1},t_k)) = \sigma_k F(t,t_{k-1},t_k) dW^k. \quad (7.2.29)$$

If we assume $\sigma_k$ is constant, then equation (7.2.29) is GBM and (see Chapter 2) has the solution

$$F(t,t_{k-1},t_k) = F(t_0,t_{k-1},t_k) \exp\left(\frac{(t-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k W_t^k\right) \quad (7.2.30)$$

where we have taken $W_0^k = 0$.
Substituting $t=t_{k-1}$ into equation (7.2.30) gives

$$F(t_{k-1},t_{k-1},t_k) = F(t_0,t_{k-1},t_k) \exp\left(\frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k W_{t_{k-1}}^k\right) \quad (7.2.31)$$

which means

- $E^{Q^k} [F(t_{k-1},t_{k-1},t_k)] = E^{Q^k} [F(t_0,t_{k-1},t_k) \exp\left(\frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k W_{t_{k-1}}^k\right)]$
- $= F(t_0,t_{k-1},t_k) E^{Q^k} \exp\left(\frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k W_{t_{k-1}}^k\right)$
- $= F(t_0,t_{k-1},t_k) E^{Q^k} \exp\left(\frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k \sqrt{(t_{k-1}-t_0)}N(0,1)\right)$
- $= F(t_0,t_{k-1},t_k),$

where we used the fact (Appendix D.2) that

$$E^{Q^k} \left\left(\frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k \sqrt{(t_{k-1}-t_0)}N(0,1)\right\right] = \exp\left(\frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2}\right) = 1.$$

Substituting for $E^{Q^k} [F(t_{k-1},t_{k-1},t_k)]$ in equation (7.2.28)

$$V_{t_0} = P\tau F(t_0,t_{k-1}) = DF(t_0,t_k) \frac{1}{\tau} \left(\frac{DF(t_0,t_{k-1})}{DF(t_0,t_k)} - 1\right) P\tau$$

This yields

$$V_{t_0} = P DF(t_0,t_{k-1}) - DF(t_0,t_k), \quad (7.2.32)$$

which is our method of valuing the future coupons generated by forward rates.

## General Payment Timing

For the general case, when the coupon payment date does not correspond to the end of its associated forward rate, we use the result from equation (7.2.3)

$$dW^k = \left(\frac{\mu_k}{\sigma_k}\right)dt + dW^i, i \neq k. \quad (7.2.33)$$

(7.2.33) states that Brownian motion $W^i$ under measure $DF(t, t_i)$ can be transformed into Brownian motion $W^k$ under measure $DF(t, t_k)$ by the addition of a drift term, which can be found in Section 7.2.3. If we assume constant drift $\mu_k, W^k_0 = 0$, we obtain

$$W^k_{t_k} = (t_k - t_0)\frac{\mu_k}{\sigma_k} + W^i \quad (7.2.34)$$

and in equation (7.2.30)

$$\sigma_k W^k_t = (t - t_0)\mu_k + \sigma_k W^i.$$

A $\mu_k$ can be achieved by freezing the forward rates that make up $\mu_k$, for example $F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k)$, see Section 7.2.3 for more details concerning $\mu_k$. This means that $F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k)$ follows the process

$$d(t, t_{k-1}, t_k) = F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k)\mu_k dt + F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k)\sigma_k dW^i.$$

The above equation is GBM with drift and can be modified by solving equation (7.2.30) to

$$F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k) = F(t_0, t_k)\exp\left((t-t_0)\mu_k - \frac{(t-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k W^i_t\right). \quad (7.2.35)$$

As before, the $t_0$ value of the coupon payment at time $t_i$

$$V_0 = DF(t_0, t_i)E^{Q^t}\left[\frac{F(t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}, t_k)}{DF(t_i, t_k)}\right]\tau P,$$

which since $DF(t_i, t_i) = 1$

$$V_0 = DF(t_0, t_i)E^{Q^t}\left[F(t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}, t_k)\right]\tau P.$$

Now from equation (7.2.35), we have

$$E^{Q^t}[F(t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}, t_k)] = E^{Q^t}F(t_0, t_k)\exp\left((t-t_0)\mu_k - \frac{(t_{k-1}-t_0)\sigma_k^2}{2} + \sigma_k W^i_{t_{k-1}}\right)$$
$$= F(t_0, t_k)\exp\left((t-t_0)\mu_k\right), \quad (7.2.37)$$

where, as before, we have used the expectation given in Appendix D.2.

By expanding equation (7.2.37) to first order, we obtain

$$E^{Q}[F(t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}, t_k)] = F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) + F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)(t_{k-1} - t_0)\mu_k. \quad (7.2.38)$$

Substituting equation (7.2.38) into equation (7.2.36), we obtain a general expression for the value of the coupon payment,

$$V_{t_0} = DF(t_0, t_k) \tau \{ F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k) + F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)(t_{k-1} - t_0)\mu_k \}. \quad (7.2.39)$$

We will now consider the cases of early and late coupon payments.

## Early Coupon Payment

Let consider the case when $i = k - 1$. From equation (7.2.50),

$$\mu_k = \frac{\sigma_k^2 F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k)}{(1 + \tau F(t, t_{k-1}, t_k))}$$

First, we freeze the rates in $\mu_k$, we use the following:

$$\mu_k = \frac{\sigma_k^2 F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)}{(1 + \tau F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k))}$$

Substituting for $\mu_k$ in equation (7.2.39), we obtain

$$V_{t_0} = DF(t_0, t_k) t_R P \frac{\sigma_k^2 \tau F^2(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k)}{(1 + \tau F(t_0, t_{k-1}, t_k))}$$

see Brigo and Mercurio (2001), p 387, Hull (2003).

## Late Coupon Payment

We consider the case when $i = k + 1$. From equation (7.2.66),

$$\mu_k = \frac{\tau P_{k,k+1} \sigma_k \sigma_{k+1} F(t, t_k, t_{k+1})}{(1 + \tau F(t, t_k, t_{k+1}))}$$

Freezing the rates, we obtain

$$\mu_k = \frac{\tau P_{k,k+1} \sigma_k \sigma_{k+1} F(t_0, t_k, t_{k+1})}{(1 + \tau F(t_0, t_k, t_{k+1}))}$$
