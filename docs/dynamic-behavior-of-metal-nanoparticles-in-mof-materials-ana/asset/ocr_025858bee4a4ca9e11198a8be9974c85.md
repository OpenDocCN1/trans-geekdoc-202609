# Analysis of Co NPs dynamic behavior with deep learning

To study the behavior of the resulting Co nanoparticles in the BiF-66 sample, we analyzed videos obtained by TEM using deep learning. To analyze the topological characteristics of the observed particles, we considered the metric Solidity (completeness), which characterizes the ratio of contour area to its convex hull area, the perimeter, and the area of the particle. The given graphs and histograms (Fig. 6) illustrate the dynamics of change and the law of distribution of the topology of nanoparticles.

According to the data of the particle solidity analysis, the average and maximum particle solidity remain constant during the experiment, i.e. close to a convex shape (Fig. 6A). The most expressed solidity dynamics turned out to be in the case of the change value. This indicates the instability and tendency to change in the shape of particles with a solidity in the range of 0.6–0.7. The solidity distribution of the particles showed during the analysis of the video that the solidity of most particles is in the range of 0.920–0.930 (Fig. 6B).

An analysis of the dynamics of the particle perimeter showed that the minimum and average values remain relatively stable and are approximately equal to 10 and 65 nm, respectively (Fig. 6C). This indicates that particles 2 and 10 nm in size (in the case of their rounded shape) retain the stability of their geometric parameters. At the same time, the maximum perimeter tends to change during the experiment. This indicates that particles with a perimeter in the range of 150–200 nm (diameter 24–32 nm) are the most unstable and tend to divide and merge with other particles. The distribution of particles along the perimeter showed that the perimeter of the largest number of particles was in the range of 60–70 nm (10–11 nm in

the case of their rounded shape) during the experiment (Fig. 6D).

By analogy here. The “perimeter” metric, a trend in area is also observed here. The minimum and average values remain relatively stable at approximately 15 and 320 nm2 (2 and 10 nm for round particles) (Fig. 6E). The maximum area tends to change, which indicates the instability of particles with an area in the range of 1250–2250 nm2. The area distribution of particles showed that the area of most particles was in the range of 310–370 nm2 (10–11 nm) during the experiment (Fig. 6F).

The average velocity of moving particles is in the range [0, 1.6] nm s−1 (Fig. 6G). The moving distance of the particles of the analyzed sample during the observation time is in the range of [0, 295.22] nm with an average value of 27.75 nm (Fig. 6H).

It should be noted that the observed nanoparticles are individual and do not represent an aggregate of subnanometer particles. Under the studied conditions, the Co NPs moved as a whole and did not disintegrate. In the case of aggregates of subnanometer particles, this would not be the case (uncorrected movement or disintegration should be the case). The integrity of the NPs was also evidenced by HR-TEM (Fig. S12 and S13, ESI†) and SAED (Fig. S14, ESI†) analysis.
