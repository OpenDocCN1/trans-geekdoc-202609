## 14.2.4 MISINFORMATION, DISINFORMATION AND FAKE NEWS

After the 2016 US elections, investigations in U.S. found presence of co-ordination media campaigns by a Russian "troll farm", the Internet Research Agency [7] (IRA). The focus of these campaigns was to create an atmosphere of anger and division in the country. IRA had hired round the clock who stole identities of real Americans to create fake profiles, with thousands of Americans to understand their behaviour, created fake information and posts and rumours, posted content that appropriately blends in American population without raising a doubt and were said to have such impact on the harmony and stability of atmosphere in the US around the elections [9].

For achieving these outcomes, the troll farm had invested millions of dollars and hours of human resource. Today, to achieve similar or more potent results the amount of human involvement and money troll farm would have to invest by lesser, thanks to advancements in AI technology. High powered language generators can create thousands of authentic posts by humans in no time. AI application can now allow you to create realistic pictures and videos of people who never existed. Deepfake technology allows anyone to create highly believable videos of people. These incidents have raised serious issues of misinformation, disinformation and fake issues on social media. Misinformation occurs when the inaccuracy is unintentional, while disinformation refers to intentionally misleading information. These can be in form of text (posts resembling those real people), photographs, video and audio data. The trending topic and recommendation algorithms of social media platforms often feel the spread of such false information.

Social media platforms are trying to come with solutions to filter these posts, but the sophistication of their rumour campaigns is continuously on rise to dodge these policing attempts. Agencies like fact-checkers and scientists who to debunk these rumours are often under added pressure to provide precise evidence for debunking false posts. On the contrary, rumour spreading companies are rarely under pressure to provide evidence. Not just in political domains, other domains have also started using fake posts on their platform that were created by South Asian telecom companies to discredit their rivals. Facebook removed several pages that created false 'news' and 'memes' against rival companies their services in front of consumers. Many of the groups and communities where this fake information is shared are closed groups. This makes access of this data to filtering & fact-checking tools even more difficult. Hence, it isn't currently possible to curate all the content in different media platforms.

The effort to generate a viral disinformation campaign has gone down while the sophistication of these efforts have increased. Most of these applications are based on open-source AI technology like Deep Neural networks (DNN), generative adversarial networks (GANs – artificial image generators) and Generativeative Transformer (GPT language generators). Access to this technology is freely available to anyone and it can be used to build any kind of application (good or bad). This is raising concerns publishing of such potent AI technologies. Indeed these technologies will lead to creation of many socially productive applications, the fact they can very easily be used for many malicious purposes is a growing concern. The latest version of GPT-3 is in fact not made completely public due to such concerns. This is again an ethical dilemma in front of AI community that needs to be addressed.

If the attackers have auxiliary information from other sources, they may still be able to retrieve user level data. To prevent such attacks AI experts have proposed various algorithmic techniques to protect the privacy. Some of them are:

- **Differential Privacy**: Differential privacy refers to algorithmic techniques used to split/modify part of sensitive data in order to make it unusable for adversaries. Instead of providing the actual value the system introduce noise. While the system internally would know how to filter out noise, attackers may not. However, if the attackers can query the dataset multiple times, they may be able to find patterns in the noise.
- **Location obfuscation**: To protect location and tracking data of users, algorithms may introduce a distance error in order the data. This would provide an inaccurate location to the adversary and prevent them from identifying an individual.
- **K-anonymity**: This technique hides the actual identity of a user by mixing it with a set of other users' data.
- **Transformation based approaches**: Makes of cryptography techniques to hide sensitive data.

### 14.3.2 NON-ALGORITHMIC APPROACHES TO SAFEGUARD USER PRIVACY

Apart from interventions, non-algorithmic interventions are also being made at policy levels to improve privacy of user data. One line of work in these domain focuses on addressing the complexity of 'Terms and conditions' that users often agree without understanding. A major deterrent to users is the heavily complex and technical language used in these agreements. One solution to this problem is use of 'Privacy Nutrition Labels'. The CyLab & Security Laboratory (CUPS) at Carnegie Mellon University developed the concept of nutrition label to make privacy policies easy to understand and comparable by the end users [10]. Like nutrition label on a food packet, privacy label is meant to clearly list out keys of any application in a clear and easy language. Apple has recently adopted this feature for its iOS14 platform and has developers to provide these privacy nutrition labels for all the applications on its app store [11]. Such interventions are needed to give the users more control over their data and privacy.
