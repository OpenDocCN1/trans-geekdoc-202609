## 14.2.1.2 Biased AI Solutions Amplifying Existing Power Imbalance in Society

Poorly designed AI systems can further shift the power balance of society by unintentionally or intentionally discriminating against certain members of the society. The way an AI system has been conceptualized, trained and defines its performance and impact on end users. If these systems are not adequately trained on data representative of all sections of society and if these systems are not appropriately tested with all categories of users, the system may solidify biases in their performances. For example, a prediction algorithm for selecting candidates in a healthcare program in US was found to be biased against the African American members of the community. In another example, Goldman Sachs and Apple faced backlash when their payment card solution assigned higher credit limits in comparison to women with even better credit ratings [2].

Even if these biases are identified once the application is rolled out, it is difficult to hold someone accountable, the company employing the system may not necessarily understand the code behind the application. In Apple's case, the company, developers and Goldman Sachs denied the algorithm was biased, but could not provide explanation of why the algorithm was making those decisions. Most of the deep learning AI applications are 'Black Box' models, where even the developers do not understand the rules used by algorithms to make decisions. developers often measure success on parameters like accuracy which the code does deliver the biases present. Clustering algorithms, for example, are often used to make large changes of data by dividing it into homogenous chunks of data points. However, unless specifically taken care of, the algorithm may not follow 'individual or group fairness ' principles, and may result in highly skewed clusters that may amplify existing biases in the society.

Speech recognition systems are widely used across geographies today. But these systems are generally trained on data from native English speakers mostly from western countries. This lack of diversity in training is felt by non-native English speakers struggling with these systems. In fact, instead of Machine Learning systems adapting to users' needs, these users are nudged to change their speech to accommodate these devices. This issue can be even more serious for users with serious speech impairments. Such users may be completely unable to use the systems. The problem is not with the algorithm itself, but due to a lack of diversity in training data, and lack of representation of the wide variety of end users

## 14.2.2 LABOUR ISSUES

One of the major feared threats of AI/ML applications is that these will completely replace humans and lead to mass unemployment in society. There are indeed many examples of AI systems which claim to automate labour-intensive operation and remove the need of humans as intermediaries. Call centres, customer service kiosks, which out personnel at retail shops, and many such rules are under the threat of becoming redundant due to AI interventions. There are multiple aspects of this threat that need attention. Two key aspects are:

### 14.2.2.1 What Kind of Jobs Are Most Likely to Be Impacted by the Threat of AI?

The key capability of AI/ML as a technology is to be able to predict events efficiently. AI applications for job application filtering to predict which candidate is mostly likely to perform well on the job, AI applications for medical diagnostic may try to predict if a tumour is likely to be cancerous or benign, autonomous driving vehicle to predict the next move of vehicles/pedestrian on the road and accordingly making decisions. These and many other examples of AI systems basically rely on the algorithm's efficiency to predict an outcome. The effect these systems will on the workforce depend on the importance of this prediction capability in the jobs. For jobs that are completely dependent on the prediction ability before forecasting, legal summary, email response by executive assistants, the threat of replacement would be strong. However, for jobs that are a mix of prediction and some other kind of skills like medicine, diagnostics or education where the decision-making skill, human touch of the practitioner is also important AI application may complement the existing jobs but not completely replace it [4].

### 14.2.2.2 Can AI Actually Remove All Need for Human Intervention?

For AI applications that can be efficient at replacing needs of humans completely, the actual truth may not be the. AI applications still rely on human workers for smoothing out their edges. Many new kinds of jobs are created to support the. Human workers are often required for training the applications. Labour’, refers to this workforce hired on contract by the companies, to help in accommodating related tasks [5].

These people, from developing countries, spend hours collecting, categorizing and cleaning data for AI applications. In some cases, the results of the applications needs to be smoothed out, which requires human support. For example, in content moderation on platforms like Facebook, the use of sophisticated AI algorithms, human intervention is still required in many cases. Thus, AI application being positioned to reduce manual labour are also creating for new forms of jobs. The problem is that these jobs are often invisible without ends, leading to mistreatment of workers, lack of fair wages and limited guidelines. The case of workers of content moderation wing of Indian firms suffering severe mental stress is an extreme example of such scenarios. Other forms of jobs that may arise of AI implementation are the ones that compliment AI usage at. Catering to the prevalent stereotype of women being more suitable for care and support jobs, the jobs emotional labour often prefer female workers.
