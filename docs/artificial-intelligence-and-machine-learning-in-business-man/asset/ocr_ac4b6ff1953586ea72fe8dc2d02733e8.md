# 14.2.1.2 Biased AI Solutions Amplifying Existing Power Imbalance in Society

Poorly designed AI systems can further shift the power balance of society by unintentionally or intentionally discriminating against certain members of the society. The way an AI system has been conceptualized, trained and defines its performance and impact on end users. If these systems are not adequately trained on data representative of all sections of society and if these systems are not appropriately tested with all categories of users, the system may solidify biases in their performances. For example, a prediction algorithm for selecting candidates in a healthcare program in US was found to be biased against the African American members of the community. In another example, Goldman Sachs and Apple faced backlash when their payment card solution assigned higher credit limits in comparison to women with even better credit ratings [2].

Even if these biases are identified once the application is rolled out, it is difficult to hold someone accountable, the company employing the system may not necessarily understand the code behind the application. In Apple's case, the company, developers and Goldman Sachs denied the algorithm was biased, but could not provide explanation of why the algorithm was making those decisions. Most of the deep learning AI applications are 'Black Box' models, where even the developers do not understand the rules used by algorithms to make decisions. developers often measure success on parameters like accuracy which the code does deliver the biases present. Clustering algorithms, for example, are often used to make large changes of data by dividing it into homogenous chunks of data points. However, unless specifically taken care of, the algorithm may not follow 'individual or group fairness ' principles, and may result in highly skewed clusters that may amplify existing biases in the society.

Speech recognition systems are widely used across geographies today. But these systems are generally trained on data from native English speakers mostly from western countries. This lack of diversity in training is felt by non-native English speakers struggling with these systems. In fact, instead of Machine Learning systems adapting to users' needs, these users are nudged to change their speech to accommodate these devices. This issue can be even more serious for users with serious speech impairments. Such users may be completely unable to use the systems. The problem is not with the algorithm itself, but due to a lack of diversity in training data, and lack of representation of the wide variety of end users
