There are three forms of consumer orientation:

- 1. Profit-centred
- 2. Knowing customers
- 3. Partnerships with customers

There are some basic (private) and some general (public) criteria for each orientation that are general to other orientations. The public and private criteria in the three orientations follow below:

## Profit-centric orientation

The aim in the orientation is on gathering and evaluating the customers' prior purchases and preference details. The consumer who adds more to the benefit and the consumer who has strong pricing expectations are maintained and dealt for the future. It then allows for the parameters: history records, requirements and capacity.

## Customer understanding

The requirements and expectations of existing and prospective consumers contribute to the perception of the need of the consumer. Greater understanding of consumer’s conditions and behaviour in latent levels amounts to sales leveraging. Demands, match and priority are the three criteria of this orientation.

### 3.1.3 Broker AND BROKERING

The middle agent (broker agent) scants its marketing database for a request for products by buyer agent that can satisfy such request when a buyer agent enters requests for products. Requests are satisfied by the middle agent when the advertising of the seller agent is sufficient close to the definition of the buyer agent’s requirement. The relationship between the vendor and the client is based on restrictions imposed on agents [39]. The broker’s efficacy is fully dependent on the market. Agents involved in the same thing have to match up. When a selling agent is found, the marketplace must supply it with a list of possible buyer agents and notify the broker agent of the presence of this new selling agent to all potential buyers [47]. Therefore, the broker is the agent who serves as a mediator between buyers and sellers. By assessing the profile of the different sellers, the broker agent determines the requirement of the buyer and then chooses the best seller and ultimately negotiates between the buyer and seller and this method is known as brokering. There are primarily two forms of the brokering process [70]:

#### 1. Merchant brokering

The agent seeking a good merchant (seller) to buy the commodity is engaged in merchant brokering. Delivery time, warranty, pricing and services are involved in merchant brokering process problems for choosing the right seller representative. Also, many merchants choose not to judge their offers on price alone. Therefore there is a stage to expand these agents to take attributes into account. The agent seeking a good merchant (seller) to buy the commodity is engaged in merchant brokering. The significance of the various characteristics can vary between consumer agents [36].

#### 2. Product brokering

Having ascertained a need, the product brokering stage involves an agent determining what to satisfy this need. The main techniques used by the brokers in this stage are feature-based filtering, collaborative filtering and constraint-based filtering. Feature-based filtering selecting products based on feature keywords. Collaborative filtering involves giving an agent personalized recommendation based on the similarities between different users’ preference agents. Constraint-based filtering requires an agent defining restrictions (e.g., price range and date limit) [36].

### 3.1.4 BUSINESS MODEL

The business model consists predominantly of two components: Financial model and Ease model. The different definitions and terms in these two models are discussed below:

## Financial Model

Price-based problems are known as a financial model. Thus, all qualities integrated with price reconsidered by agents. Of course, the meaning of the various characteristics will differ between buyer and seller agent according to the price. The assessment is based on a cumulative score based on weighted score of these individual qualities, measured for each seller and buyer. The buyer and seller acquire these weights, defining them with a specific profile under which the weights are given [1].

## Ease Model

In the ease model, problems other than expense, as shipping time, warrants and services, are considered. Further, many merchants choose not to judge offers on price alone. Consequently, agents consider several ease-based utility attributes. Of course, between buyer and seller agent, the meaning of the various characters can differ. The analysis is based on a combined score on the weighted score of these individual characteristics, measured for each seller and buyer. The purchaser and seller acquire these weights, defining them with a clear profile in which the weights are provided [51].
