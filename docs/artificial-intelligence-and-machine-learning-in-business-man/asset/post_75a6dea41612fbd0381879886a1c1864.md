## 8.3.8 NEUROMORPHIC COMPUTING

Neuromorphic computing attempts to replicate the computational properties of logical elements; their ability to store information and modify, update and adapt to environmental triggers. The current trend utilizes the mixed-signal techniques to imitate the organic carbon-based material using the alternative silicon-based material. The European Brain Project is a collaborative effort to emulate biological functions and accelerate learning and over and above the speed possible in biological systems.

The much-tout Moore's law in computing is no longer relevant as the focus of the latest research has shifted to large scale computing modelled on the way nature and biological systems work. The main advantage of this approach is significantly lower consumption of power. Artificial Intelligence, in general, requires huge power resources but a neuromorphic chip consumes 70 watts power which is one-tenth of the power consumed by an equally powerful Intel chip.

## 8.3.9 BIOCHIPS

Biologic computing uses nanomorphic cells that combine the biology and nature to use DNA as the medium of storage. The juxtaposition of nanoengineering and biotechnology is a great example of the 4th Industrial Revolution. The fusion of biological and technological systems will have a significant bearing for energy systems and computing speed. The devices such as biological and magneto-resistive sensors, hybrid car batteries and onboard networked devices represent the unfolding of the 4th Industrial Revolution [28].

## 8.4 AI APPLICATIONS IN THE 4TH INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION

Artificial Intelligence has already reached the threshold where it touches human lives in many ways, analyzing to predict data in various industrial applications. The inclusion of AI has not only increased the operational efficiency of infrastructure, but also brought costs down. Some industrial applications of AI, characteristic of the 4th Industrial Revolution are described in this section.

### 8.4.1 GAMING INDUSTRY

The global gaming market was $152 billion in 2015 and is likely to reach $257 by 2025, registering a CAGR of 17%. From the board games to interactive strategy games, AI is in everywhere. Carnegie Mellon University developed a neural net called Libratus. This deep learning makes statistical predictions, and also has a behavioural element bluff as a strategy in poker. The AI is not designed to learn strategies like chess, but to play by the rules and the information provided to it by the other player. Libratus has been effective where responses are based on imperfect information. The deep learning schedule for Libratus included the evaluation of 15 million hours of training to polish its strategies. Libratus won on a 20-day poker tournament [29].

### 8.4.2 SURVEILLANCE AND HUMAN BEHAVIOURAL MARKETING

Surveillance and human behaviour tracking are areas where Artificial Intelligence has created a useful niche. Machine Learning can use the surveillance to flag security risks arising out of employees breaking their normal behavioural patterns.

Here the Machine Learning captures the data such as the domain of the files that are routinely picked and whether any file picked up is outside the domain of that particular employee. This way, snooping employees or employee espionage in various industries or even governments can be detected early. This results in a reduction of security threats and increases communication.

For example, if an employee starts making unauthorized copies of files that they chance upon a sudden. Or employees stealing information which might be of interest to competitors. The surveillance also checks employees responding to a phishing attack or opening any malware in emails [30].

### 8.4.3 IDENTITY MANAGEMENT

With the increased access of banks and other security information and security of digital wallets using voice-activated authentication AI to match the vibrations of the owner’s voice with the known patterns. The digital assistant responds based on whether the audio match is established else the digital assistant stops.

### 8.4.4 CHATBOTS

Websites with high customer traffic and queries are employing AI-assisted chatbots which handle customer queries. Amazon uses chatbots to handle customers’ queries regarding sales or delivery issues. University websites can handle 24x7 queries related to their courses and DOTH operator TaskSky uses chatbots to handle and resolve customer problems. Deep neural training involves up to 200,000 questions and their 800,000 answers and the result is a chatbot detects the question type and its context and offers answers in a sympathetic tone to the user resulting in significant cost reductions and increased efficiency [32].

### 8.4.5 HEALTHCARE

AI was used in the UK to combat the problem of detection of skin cancer. Early detection raises the five-year survival rate to 97% but can only be detected early by a trained dermatologist. Stanford University researchers applied Machine Learning using over 100,000 images for recognition of skin cancer patterns. After machine training, the software was tested vis-a-vis the expert dermatologists in three groups of carcinoma. Stanford University engineers and computer scientists trained the software to isolate the deadly and so deadly skin cancers from the collection of images. The AI algorithm was able to match the pattern recognition ability when compared to 21 experienced clinicians [33].

### 8.4.6 WEARABLE BIOMETRIC MONITORS

Wearable technology is a recent phenomenon and new wearable gadgets are coming onto the market. Smartwatches and smart bands are now affordable and available in the consumer electronics market. These wearable gadgets collect data from the user to determine the baseline which is then used to issue alerts when the readings hit the alarming levels. These wearable technologies can detect when a person is going ill by monitoring the body temperature, pulse rates, blood oxygen levels etc. Wearable gadgets are, therefore having an important role in managing the health of users [34].
