# 8.3.4 INTERNET OF THINGS, MICROELECTROSENSORS AND BIENSENSOR TECH

IoT started around the RFID technology but has come far in the intervening years. Today, IoT employs various services and telemetry. In its physical fusion, IoT is present in Jet propulsion engines turbines to operate condition monitors and nuclear power plants, supply chain automation and robotics to meteorological measurement and optimization. Unm drones work through sensors to read accurate information and ground swath and then using the onboard computer device on their planned locations. Microscopic IoT sensors can be inserted in the skin, ingested or injected to monitor the biological health indicators of the host. These microscopic sensors are useful in monitoring the health using platforms such as mobile and internet-based platforms as mHealth and eHealth, also in remote healthcare and diagnostics. Wearable technologies are now available which can measure psychological and physiological stress levels that be used with analytics for better monitoring and integration.

The size of the IoT market has been expanding exponentially. Gartner estimates 8.5 billion connected objects in 2017, while Cisco estimated to 50 billion objects in 2020 [20]. IDC estimated at $8.6 trillion and 212 billion connected things by 2021 [21]. BCG forecasted market size of $276 billion. Gartner forecasted that by 2020 more than 50% of all businesses would incorporate some element of IoT and that IoT security would be the next big thing.
