
# Forensic Imaging

Forensic imaging is the process of creating a bit-by-bit copy of digital storage media, ensuring that the original evidence remains untouched. Code-based procedures illustrate how to create forensic images of hard drives, solid-state drives, and other storage devices.

## Example: PowerShell script for creating a forensic image of a drive

```
Copy-Item -Path \\.\PhysicalDrive1 -Destination C:\ForensicImages\Drive1.img -Recurse
```

# Data Preservation: Code-Based Best Practices

Preserving the integrity of digital evidence is paramount. Code-based best practices demonstrate how to securely store digital evidence, ensuring that it remains unaltered until it is needed for analysis or legal proceedings.

## Example: Bash command to create a read-only forensic image mount

```
mkdir /mnt/forensic_mount
mount -o ro,loop,offset=512 forensic_image.dd /mnt/forensic_mount
```

By integrating code-based examples and practical guidance, this section empowers students with the knowledge and skills needed to effectively collect and preserve digital evidence. Digital evidence is fragile and can be easily compromised if not handled correctly. Therefore, mastering the techniques of evidence identification, forensic imaging, chain of custody documentation, and data preservation is fundamental for cybersecurity professionals and digital forensic experts alike.
