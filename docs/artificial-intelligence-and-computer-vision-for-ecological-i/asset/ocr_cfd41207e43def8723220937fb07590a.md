# Overview of RS Platforms

RS platforms are the carriers or vehicles that house the sensors used for collecting data. These platforms can be classified based on altitude, coverage area, and operational capabilities.

# Satellite Platforms (Space-based)

Satellite platforms orbit the Earth and provide extensive coverage, making them ideal for large-scale agricultural monitoring. Different types of orbits, such as geostationary and polar orbits, offer unique advantages for RS (Khanal et al., 2020). Geostationary satellites remain fixed over a single point on the Earth’s surface, providing continuous monitoring of the same area, which is helpful for weather monitoring and tracking large-scale agricultural trends (Oymatov et al., 2023). On the other hand, Polar-orbiting satellites pass over the Earth’s poles and cover the entire globe as the Earth rotates. They provide high-resolution data and revisit the same area regularly, which is crucial for monitoring changes in agricultural fields over time. Examples of agrarian satellite platforms include the Landsat series, Sentinel-2, and MODIS, which avail crucial data for crop monitoring, soil assessment, and land use planning (Jiang et al., 2022).

# Aerial Platforms

Aerial platforms, including manned aircraft, offer high-resolution data and can be deployed flexibly (Tang and Shao, 2015). Manned aircraft are suitable for covering large areas quickly. They can be equipped with various sensors to capture detailed images and data, making them ideal for regional agricultural surveys and mapping projects.

# Ground-Based Platforms

Ground-based platforms include fixed installations and mobile systems that provide highly detailed and accurate data at specific locations. Fixed installations, such as weather stations and ground-based spectroradiometers, continuously monitor specific parameters like soil moisture, temperature, and crop growth (Bougettaya et al., 2022). These provide valuable ground truth data that can be used to validate and calibrate RS data from other platforms. Mobile systems, mounted on vehicles or handheld devices, can be moved to different locations to collect data on-demand. They are helpful for detailed field surveys and validate RS data with high precision. Ground-based platforms offer the highest level of detail and accuracy, making them indispensable for specific agricultural applications, such as soil analysis, crop scouting, and localized environmental monitoring (Li et al., 2023). Other ground-based platforms include UAVs or drones which are increasingly becoming popular for precision agriculture (Kattenborn et al., 2021). They can fly at low altitudes, capturing high-resolution images and data and providing spatial information about crop health and field conditions.
