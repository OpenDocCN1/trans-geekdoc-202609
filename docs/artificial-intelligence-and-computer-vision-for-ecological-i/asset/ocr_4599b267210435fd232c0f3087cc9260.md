Agriculture, as the art and science of soil cultivation, stands as a cornerstone of national development. Despite the modernization of plant breeding techniques and the advent of agrochemicals such as pesticides and fertilizers, farmers often lag in leveraging the latest technological advancements. Therefore, there’s a critical need to equip them with up-to-date information and techniques to ensure optimal agricultural productivity.

The proliferation of Information Technology (IT) in recent years, particularly in rural areas, presents an opportunity to address these challenges. With the exponential growth of mobile connectivity, especially in rural regions, mobile devices have become powerful tools for disseminating agricultural information. However, challenges such as language diversity and rural populations’ hesitancy towards modern technologies persist, complicating the dissemination of accurate information (Jain, 2020). Chat-bot systems, serving as question-answering systems, bridge the gap between humans and machines, enabling users to query and receive accurate responses. In domains such as medicine and travel, significant research has been conducted on question-answering systems. However, in agriculture, retrieving precise answers from web data poses unique challenges, as websites often present irrelevant information alongside relevant responses. To address this, deep learning techniques, particularly Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), are increasingly employed (Singh et al., 2019). In this context, the utilization of chatbot systems holds promise as a solution to disseminate accurate agricultural information efficiently. By leveraging the ubiquity of mobile devices and advancements in natural language processing, chatbots can empower farmers with timely and relevant agricultural knowledge, thereby enhancing productivity and supporting rural livelihoods.

# Natural Language Processing (NLP)

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a branch of computer science that falls under the broader field of Artificial Intelligence (AI), with a primary focus on enabling computers to comprehend and interpret human language, both spoken and written, in a manner akin to humans (Jurafsky and Martin, 2019). This interdisciplinary field combines computational linguistics, which involves rule-based modeling of human language, with statistical, machine learning, and deep learning models. NLP technologies encompass a range of tasks aimed at providing computers with the ability to understand human language in various forms, such as text or speech data, and to grasp its full meaning, including the speaker’s or writer’s intention and sentiment. These tasks include speech recognition, where software deciphers spoken commands or translates text between languages, and part-of-speech (POS) tagging, which identifies the grammatical role of each word in a sentence.

However, creating software that accurately discerns the intended meaning of text or voice data is highly challenging due to the inherent ambiguity of human language. From homonyms and homophones to idioms and metaphors, human language is replete with complexities that take years for humans to master. Programmers must meticulously teach NLP-driven applications to recognize and understand these nuances from the outset to ensure their usefulness (Jurafsky and Martin, 2019).
