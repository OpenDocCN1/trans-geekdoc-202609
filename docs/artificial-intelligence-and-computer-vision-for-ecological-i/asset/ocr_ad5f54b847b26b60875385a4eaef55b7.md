Let us introduce computer vision and artificial intelligence. When paired with Computer Vision’s ability to interpret visual input, Artificial Intelligence’s ability to process massive volumes of data and spot patterns presents a potent solution to these problems. Large datasets can be analyzed by Artificial Intelligence algorithms, especially machine learning and deep learning models, to find nuances in patterns and relationships that human observers might miss. These technologies can be used to track several growth metrics in plants, identify early symptoms of illness or stress, and offer useful information to improve crop management techniques.

Phenotyping, or the measuring of an organism’s physical and biochemical characteristics, is one of the main uses of Artificial Intelligence and Computer Vision in plant growth mapping. High-resolution photos of plants can be taken by automated phenotyping systems using Computer Vision technology. These images are subsequently subjected to Artificial Intelligence algorithms to extract specific information about plant morphology, including leaf size, shape, color, and texture. These systems can run constantly, giving researchers and farmers access to real-time data that supports their decision-making regarding plant health and growth circumstances. For example, autonomous vehicles such as drones that are fitted with cameras and sensors can drive across agricultural fields, take pictures and gather information about the health of the plants, the state of the soil, and the surrounding environment (Zhang et al., 2017). After that, this data is processed by Artificial Intelligence systems to provide detailed maps of plant growth trends. With the help of these maps, which may identify stress points for plants as well as regions where they are doing well, specific interventions like modifying irrigation schedules, adding fertilizer, or putting insect control measures in place can be made.

Artificial Intelligence and Computer Vision technology can also improve plant disease identification and diagnosis. To minimize productivity losses and prevent extensive crop damage, early disease diagnosis is essential. Visual inspection is a common component of traditional illness diagnosis techniques, however it can be arbitrary and unreliable.

# History of Plant Growth Mapping

Earlier plant growth mapping was done by observing changes in plant sizes, shape and color to assess growth stages and health and the tools used during these processes included rulers and measuring sticks but these provided rudimentary data and lacked accuracy and scope. Moving on new approaches were adopted which included documenting growth stages, environmental conditions and plant resources. Scientists such as Carl Linnaeus and Charles Darwin proposed models and taxonomies providing a diverse understanding of plant development. Furthermore, more accurate plant growth quantification was made possible by the use of more exact measurement tools, such as micrometers, planimeters, and calipers (Islam et al., 2024). The systematic analysis of growth patterns and environmental impacts in agricultural research was made possible by the introduction of statistical tools. Massive amounts of surveillance of crop health and growth patterns were made possible by the development of remote sensing technologies including satellite images and aerial photography, which completely changed the field of plant growth mapping.
