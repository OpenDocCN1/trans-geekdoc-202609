# **Types of Sensors Used in Agricultural Mapping**

Different types of sensors are utilized in RS to capture a wide range of data relevant to agricultural mapping. These sensors can be broadly categorized based on the data they collect from optical, thermal, radar, and LiDAR sensors.

# *Optical Sensors*

Optical sensors detect light reflected from the Earth’s surface across numerous wavelengths, such as visible, near-infrared (NIR), and shortwave infrared (SWIR) (Nurdin et al., 2022). These sensors frequently gauge plant vitality, soil characteristics, and crop status. Instruments that record data across several distinct spectral ranges are often employed to compute plant health indicators, like the NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) (Nurdin et al., 2022). Hyperspectral sensors, which capture information across numerous fine spectral divisions offer intricate spectral data, enabling crop identification, nutrient shortage detection, and stress monitoring in plants. These instruments find use in scientific studies and precision farming, providing sophisticated tools for in-depth examination and informed choices (Rahimi et al., 2021).

# *Thermal Sensors*

Thermal sensors calculate the thermal infrared radiation emitted by the Earth’s surface, providing information about surface temperature. In agriculture, thermal sensors monitor soil moisture levels, detect plant water stress, and manage irrigation systems efficiently (Kluger et al., 2021). For example, sensors on the Landsat 8 satellite provide thermal data applicable in assessing crop water use and manage water resources. Thermal imagery helps farmers optimize irrigation schedules, minimize water consumption, and enhance crop yields by guaranteeing that plants get a sufficient amount of water in a timely manner (Kumалawati et al., 2021).

# *Radar Sensors*

Radar sensors, or Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), emit microwave signals and measure the reflected signals to capture Earth’s surface information (Javaid et al., 2023). Radar sensors can see through cloud cover and function around the clock, making them crucial for uninterrupted surveillance (Vyas et al., 2021). In agriculture, SAR sensors measure soil moisture, monitor crop growth, and detect changes in field conditions. Examples include the Sentinel-1 satellites, which provide high-resolution radar imagery. The ability to obtain data under all weather conditions and during both day and night makes radar sensors a reliable source of information for agricultural monitoring, particularly in regions with frequent cloud cover (Anuradha, 2024).
