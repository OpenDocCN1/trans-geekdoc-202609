# Neutrons Probing Sensors

Measuring hydrogen atom density in the soil using a neutron source at a point is how neutron probe sensors work. The slowdown of neutrons by hydrogen atoms and this change are measured to determine the moisture content of the soil. Neutron probe sensors require special handling because they have radioactive sources that need safety precautions but they give accurate readings as well as can measure various depth levels of soil moisture. Neutron probes find applications in research activities and critical monitoring of soil moisture. They are highly accurate and hence suitable for detailed hydrological investigations and large-scale agricultural schemes. Evett et al. (2009) determined spatiotemporal variability of electromagnetic (EM) and Neutron Probe Sensors to determine the soil profile water content. They stated that the EM sensor had low accuracy and high variability than the Neutrons Probing Sensors with a standard comparison with gravimetric samples. Similarly, a study conducted by Kodikara et al. (2014) reported the low variation in the lower subsurface measurements than the upper subsurface using a Neutron Probe sensor and gave a prediction equation to determine the subsurface soil moisture (Fig. 7).
