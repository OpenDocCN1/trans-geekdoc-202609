# Challenges of RS in Agriculture

RS has emerged as a powerful tool for monitoring and managing agricultural practices, providing valuable insights into crop health, soil conditions, and environmental factors that impact crop yield. However, the effective utilization of RS in agriculture faces several challenges that must be addressed. This section highlights the critical challenges in RS for agriculture, including data availability and accessibility, spatial and temporal resolution limitations, validation and accuracy assessment, cost and resource constraints, and technical and operational challenges.

# Data Availability and Accessibility

Various factors can hinder the acquisition of high-quality RS data for agricultural applications. Cloud cover and atmospheric conditions can obscure the Earth’s surface, leading to data gaps or low-quality imagery, which is particularly problematic for time-sensitive agrarian monitoring (Martos et al., 2021). Additionally, sensor limitations in spectral and radiometric resolution may affect the ability to accurately detect and quantify specific crop characteristics or environmental factors (Jafarbiglu and Pourreza, 2022). Data sharing policies and licensing restrictions imposed by data providers can limit access to particular data sources, creating barriers for small-scale farmers or research institutions with limited budgets (Wood et al., 2024). Furthermore, the complexity of data discovery and retrieval mechanisms may require specialized skills and knowledge to navigate various data repositories and platforms effectively (Li et al., 2021).

# Spatial and Temporal Resolution Limitations

Agricultural applications often require high spatial resolution for detailed crop monitoring and frequent temporal resolution for tracking crop growth and conditions throughout the growing season. However, balancing these two aspects can be challenging. High spatial resolution imagery, essential for identifying crop patterns, monitoring plant health, and detecting small-scale features, often comes at the cost of reduced temporal resolution or a limited coverage area (Wu et al., 2023). Conversely, broad-scale imagery with frequent revisit times, such as Sentinel-2 satellites, lack the necessary detail for precise analysis of individual fields or crops (Li et al., 2022). Reconciling this trade-off between spatial and temporal resolutions is a significant challenge in RS for agriculture.

# Validation and Accuracy Assessment

Validating RS data and assessing its accuracy is crucial for reliable decision-making in agricultural applications. Ground-truthing or collecting in-situ data for comparison and calibration can be labor-intensive, time-consuming, and logistically challenging, especially for large-scale agrarian operations or remote areas (Bennion et al., 2019).
