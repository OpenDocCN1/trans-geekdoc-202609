# Machine Learning Across Different Water Scenarios

A potent technique for tackling a variety of problems in water management systems and treatment is machine learning. Artificial intelligence approaches are transforming the way we manage water resources, from hydrologic modeling to real-time monitoring and predictive analysis. Recently, there has been a lot of interest in the use of machine learning in water resource management. The dire need for viable options is highlighted by the deterioration of water quality in metropolitan areas, which is mostly attributable to industrial and municipal effluent from human activities. The ML utilization in water quality research has gained prominence due to significant advancements in analysis and prediction techniques. With integrated and periodic water quality monitoring data serving as standards for water system management, data collecting is the key first step in the development of models for machine learning. Despite being widely used, standard environmental monitoring techniques have difficulties when it comes to in situ monitoring because of practical constraints (Li et al., 2020). Poor model performance can result from missing, inaccurate, or damaged data brought on by machinery malfunctions or human mistakes, underscoring the need for data cleaning in machine learning applications. There are several approaches to data cleaning, such as removing problematic data, imputation based on average or median, or using approaches for matrix completion and machine learning to improve raw data (Donders et al., 2006). Prediction accuracy in machine learning applications is influenced by selecting the kind of model and the caliber of the training set (Zhu et al., 2022).
