However, the financial exigencies associated with data procurement serve as a deterrent to the widespread adoption of ML algorithms within the domain of water resources. This challenge is further exacerbated by technological constraints. Moreover, even in instances where data is accessible, it may not invariably conform to the requisite resolution (Zhu et al., 2022).

Machine learning-derived models are predominantly empirical and are tailored to the specific conditions for which they were formulated. The intrinsic complexity and heterogeneity of real-world aquatic systems present significant challenges to the generalizability of water quality predictions. Moreover, the environmental dynamics are distinct across different water bodies, thereby limiting the applicability of a model to its system of origin. This limitation poses a substantial impediment to the broad-scale deployment of ML methodologies. To mitigate this issue, models necessitate recalibration when applied to disparate systems from those they were originally designed for, incurring additional resource expenditures. Consequently, reliance on ‘black-box’ solutions should be eschewed in favor of approaches that yield process-based scientific insights (Schafer et al., 2022). The initial stage in the development of any machine learning (ML) model necessitates the judicious selection of pertinent input parameters. It is crucial to select a subset of parameters affecting the water quality that encapsulate sufficient predictive information regarding water quality. This selection is paramount, as an excess of input variables may result in redundancy, whereas an insufficient number could lead to inaccuracies in the model. Optimal variable selection can enhance model accuracy, as inappropriate choices may adversely affect model performance. Furthermore, each ML model exhibits unique responses to different sets of input variables and data patterns. Therefore, it is imperative to identify variables that augment the efficacy of a specific ML model. The methodologies such as the partial autocorrelation function (PACF), autocorrelation function (ACF), correlation coefficient, and cross-correlation function, are instrumental in discerning the salient predictors (Asadollah et al., 2021). Better data acquisition through sophisticated sensors, enhancing the applicability of the model to a universal scale and an interdisciplinary approach are to be considered to address the above-mentioned challenges.

# Conclusion

The evaluation of water quality, which has historically relied on labor-intensive approaches, gains a great deal from machine learning (ML) techniques since they improve accuracy, efficiency, and affordability. Applications of machine learning include controlling shortages and allocating water resources optimally. Notwithstanding, several obstacles persist, such as the requirement for extensive and superior quality data sets, limitations in terms of finances and technology, and the intricacy of actual aquatic systems. Resource requirements rise when machine learning models require to be recalibrated for diverse contexts.
