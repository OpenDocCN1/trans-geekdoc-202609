Additionally, deep learning models are computationally intensive, requiring substantial resources for training and inference. Deep learning shows promise in integrating with remote sensing data. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been applied to satellite imagery to extract spatial patterns relevant to soil moisture, enhancing the spatial resolution and coverage of predictions and enabling comprehensive monitoring across large areas.

Researchers are developing new architectures and hybrid models that combine deep learning with physical process-based models to leverage their strengths. Efforts to collect and annotate large-scale soil-moisture datasets are underway, supporting the training of robust models to capture soil-vegetation-atmosphere dynamics. Overall, deep learning holds great potential for soil moisture prediction due to its ability to find hidden relationships in large datasets. Neural networks and advanced architectures enable accurate, high-resolution estimations of soil water dynamics, providing valuable input for environmental management effective agricultural practices, and further research and development.

Hachani et al. (2019) used Sentinel-1 SAR data and artificial neural networks (ANNs), incorporating auxiliary information like digital elevation models, local incidence angles, and NDVI. They combined satellite measurements with electromagnetic model simulation data (based on the IEM model) for robust, location-independent training, achieving a high correlation (R = 0.77, RMSE = 0.0184). Li et al. (2020) found that a basic feedforward three-layer ANN could simulate and predict soil moisture with lower computational costs and less reliance on prior conditions than traditional physical models. ANNs excel in predicting soil moisture due to their autonomous learning capabilities (Zhang, 2023). Hou et al. (2016) showed strong concordance between ANN predictions and real measurements using meteorological data. Li et al. (2018) used an adaptive genetic ANN model with atmospheric forcing data, significantly improving soil moisture predictions.
