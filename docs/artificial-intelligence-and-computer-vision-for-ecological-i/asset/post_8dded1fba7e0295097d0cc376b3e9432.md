
## Techniques for Feature Extraction

As presented below, there are four broad techniques for feature extraction in terms of its application to agriculture.

### Spectral Indices

These are essential tools for feature extraction in RS in the quest for sustainable agricultural practices. These indices take advantage of the reflectance properties of various wavelengths of light to evaluate the soil qualities and the plants. Soil Adjusted Vegetation Index (SAVI) and Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) help with climatic and soil background effects, respectively, giving more exact assessments in challenging settings (hard-to-reach areas) (Shanmugapriya et al., 2019). The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) compares the reflectance of near-infrared and red light to analyze plants’ health and thickness. Therefore, by using spectral indices, agricultural specialists can attain beneficial insights concerning plant health, biomass, and stress levels, making it possible for informed decision-making concerning watering, fertilization, and insect monitoring (Chuphal et al., 2024).

### Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

A highly successful technique for extracting features in RS, particularly in hyperspectral data. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) converts the original spectral bands into orthogonal components known as principal components. These principal components effectively capture the highest amount of variation in the data (Ladi et al., 2022). PCA reduces the dimensionality of data by retaining the most significant components and discarding the less important ones while preserving crucial information. The dimensionality reduction simplifies future analysis tasks, such as categorization and clustering, and improves the comprehensibility of the data (Peters et al., 2002). PCA is useful in agricultural applications for identifying patterns and trends in hyperspectral images. It helps recognize different types of plants (Chouhan et al., 2024), disease outbreaks, and soil qualities.

### Texture Analysis

Since data appears in different formats, texture analysis further helps obtain spatial information from RS images using approaches that extract spatial-based attributes. Texture evaluation considers the spatial arrangement of pixels inside an image rather than focusing solely on spectral properties (Grossiord, 2020). The Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) quantifies textural properties such as contrast, entropy, and homogeneity. These attributes provide valuable information on surface roughness, soil structure, and crop patterns. Texture evaluation is advantageous in differentiating between different land cover types, detecting small variations in crop conditions, and identifying anomalies such as pests or diseases (Chouhan et al., 2021; Qiao et al., 2024; Khajuria et al., 2018). Thus, by integrating texture analysis into feature extraction procedures, agricultural specialists foster a significantly more comprehensive comprehension of agricultural landscapes and make even more educated decisions regarding management.

### Object-Based Image Analysis (OBIA)

This approach moves away from traditional pixel-based techniques and focuses on extracting features from RS data. OBIA categorizes images into meaningful objects or areas using spectral, spatial, and contextual information. By extracting features such as shape, dimension, and spectral reflectance qualities from these objects, OBIA allows for a more context-specific and semantically relevant evaluation than pixel-based approaches (van Klompenburg et al., 2020). OBIA is a technique used in farming to accurately identify boundaries between different areas, types of crops, and zones for effective management. This technology plays a crucial role in implementing precision agriculture methods. Moreover, OBIA enhances the precision of categorization and mapping tasks by incorporating spatial relationships between objects, resulting in more robust and interpretable results (Kumar et al., 2024a). ## Feature Selection Methods

These features are selected using the most practical approach presented in this section.

### Filter Methods

They are frequently employed for feature selection in RS applications because of their straightforwardness and effectiveness. These methods assess the significance of variables regardless of the selected predictive model. The correlation coefficient is a commonly employed filtering approach that quantifies the linear association between each characteristic and the target variable (Chuphal et al., 2024). Features that have high correlation coefficients are deemed to be more predictive and are kept for subsequent studies. Another illustration is the Chi-Square Test, which evaluates the lack of dependence between category characteristics and the target variable in classification tasks. Features with substantial Chi-Square values are chosen, demonstrating their importance in distinguishing across different classes. Filter methods are beneficial due to their high computational efficiency and capacity to handle big datasets (Ullo and Sinha, 2021). However, they may fail to consider the relationships between features, which could result in less-than-ideal selections of feature subsets.

### Wrapper Methods

These are used in assessing feature subsets by measuring their influence on model performance, resulting in higher computational complexity compared to filter methods. These methods evaluate the quality of feature subsets by directly training and testing a predictive model using various combinations of features (Singh et al., 2022). Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) is a widely used technique that systematically eliminates the least significant features in a model based on performance indicators like accuracy or error rate. Forward and backward selection are alternative wrapper approaches that begin with either an empty or complete set of features. They progressively include or exclude features based on their impact on the model's performance (Nadeem et al., 2022). Wrapper methods have the advantage of considering feature interactions, but they can be computationally expensive, particularly for datasets with high dimensions.

### Embedded Methods

Embedded methods incorporate feature selection directly into training the model, providing a smooth and integrated approach to feature selection. These strategies utilize regularization techniques or inherent feature importance measures in the predictive model to choose relevant features (Tey et al., 2024). Lasso, also known as the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator, is a widely used embedded method that uses L1 regularization to penalize the absolute magnitude of feature coefficients. This regularization technique effectively forces some coefficients to become zero, resulting in selecting a subset of features. Tree-based approaches, such as decision trees and Random Forests, naturally conduct feature selection by dividing nodes according to feature relevance metrics such as Gini impurity or information gain (Kumar et al., 2024b).
