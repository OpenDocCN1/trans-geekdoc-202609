The process of field sampling involves gathering water samples by hand from different depths and places. These samples are then subjected to laboratory analysis utilizing methods like spectrophotometry, chromatography, and titration. These techniques offer accurate measurements of water quality indicators such as microbiological content and chemical concentrations, but they are generally restricted in terms of their temporal and spatial coverage, necessitating a large investment of both financial and human resources.

On the other hand, contemporary methods make use of cutting-edge technologies to improve the effectiveness, scope, and real-time capabilities of water quality evaluation. For example, automated *in situ* sensors are placed in water bodies to continuously check for elements including temperature, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, and pH. Because these sensors give real-time data, fewer human samplings are required, and problems with water quality can be identified right away. Observing long-term, large-scale changes in water quality through satellite imaging and aerial photography is another way that remote sensing and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) broaden the scope of monitoring. The integration of spatial and temporal data made possible by GIS technology enables thorough study and visualization of pollution sources and trends over large geographic areas.

Another advancement is the incorporation of machine learning (ML) and AI into the evaluation of water quality. Large datasets can be analyzed by AI algorithms, which can then be used more effectively and accurately than traditional statistical approaches to find patterns, forecast trends, and spot abnormalities. Trained on previous data, machine learning models are able to predict future water quality conditions, offering useful insights for proactive management.

Water quality testing is made more thorough and efficient by combining old techniques with new technology. For specific pollutants and biological assessments in particular, traditional approaches continue to be crucial for getting accurate and comprehensive results. Modern methods, on the other hand, provide larger temporal and spatial coverage, allowing for large-scale, ongoing monitoring. Together, these techniques provide a more comprehensive understanding of water quality, facilitating improved decision-making and water resource management. In addition to improving assessment efficiency and accuracy, this integrated approach aids in the creation of more effective policies and interventions that preserve and protect aquatic habitats.

# *Basics of AI and Machine Learning*

AI is the term used to describe how technology, especially computer systems, can simulate human intelligence processes. Learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and language comprehension are some of these processes. AI can be divided into two categories: broad AI, which can carry out any intellectual work that a human can, and narrow AI, which is made to accomplish a specific task (like facial recognition or internet searches).
