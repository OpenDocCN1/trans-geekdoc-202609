# Monitoring Urban Water Bodies in Real-Time with CV

A case study was carried out in an urban environment to use Computer Vision (CV) technology to monitor the water quality of lakes and reservoirs. Pollution from stormwater runoff, industrial discharges, and recreational activities is a common problem for urban aquatic bodies. Throughout many urban lakes, a network of smart cameras and drones with high-resolution imaging capabilities was set up for this study. To process the visual data that these devices collected, CV algorithms were created with an emphasis on surface litter, turbidity, and the presence of algae. Specific contaminants, such as plastic garbage and oil spills, were recognized by object identification models, while areas of interest were isolated using image segmentation techniques. Due to the real-time analysis that CV systems provide, local officials can now continuously monitor water quality and react quickly to pollution situations. Furthermore, the information gathered helped create long-term strategies for improving the quality of the water, proving the usefulness of CV in managing the water quality of cities.

# Improving Agricultural Areas’ Water Quality

A significant contributor to water pollution is agricultural runoff, which enters neighboring water bodies and introduces sediments, fertilizers, and pesticides. A case study examined the use of AI and CV technologies to monitor and manage water quality in streams and rivers affected by farming activities in a Midwest agricultural region of the United States. A comprehensive monitoring system was created by combining data from ground-based sensors with remote sensing data from drones and satellites. To forecast nutrient levels (such as nitrogen and phosphorus) in water bodies based on meteorological conditions, soil types, and agricultural activities, artificial intelligence models—such as neural networks and regression algorithms—were employed. Drone imagery was analyzed using CV techniques to determine the amount of stream silt deposition and erosion. By using an integrated approach, farmers and water managers were able to reduce nitrogen runoff and enhance water quality by using targeted best management practices (BMPs) including buffer strips and cover crops. This case study demonstrated how AI and CV may support environmentally friendly farming methods and safeguard rural communities’ water supplies.
