# Techniques for Feature Extraction

As presented below, there are four broad techniques for feature extraction in terms of its application to agriculture.

# Spectral Indices

These are essential tools for feature extraction in RS in the quest for sustainable agricultural practices. These indices take advantage of the reflectance homes of various wavelengths of light to evaluate the soil qualities and the plants. Soil Adjusted Vegetation Index (SAVI) and Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) help with climatic and soil background effects, respectively, giving more exact assessments in challenging settings (hard-to-reach areas) (Shanmugapriya et al., 2019). The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) compares the reflectance of near-infrared and red light to analyze plants’ health and thickness. Therefore, by using spectral indices, agricultural specialists can attain beneficial insights concerning plant health, biomass, and anxiety degrees, making it possible for notified decision-making concerning watering, fertilization, and insect monitoring (Chuphal et al., 2024).

# Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

A highly successful technique for removing attributes in RS, particularly in hyperspectral data. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) converts the original spectral bands into orthogonal components known as main elements. These primary elements effectively capture the highest amount of variation in the information (Ladi et al., 2022). PCA reduces the dimensionality of information by retaining the most significant components and discarding the less interesting ones while preserving crucial information. The dimensionality reduction simplifies future analysis tasks, such as categorization and clustering, and improves the comprehensibility of the material (Peters et al., 2002). PCA is useful in agricultural applications for identifying patterns and trends in hyperspectral pictures. It helps recognize different types of plants (Chouhan et al., 2024), disease outbreaks, and soil qualities.

# Texture Analysis

Since data appears in different formats, text analysis further helps obtain spatial information from RS images using approaches that remove spectral-based attributes. Texture evaluation considers the spatial arrangement of pixels inside an image rather than focusing solely on spectral properties (Grossiord, 2020). The Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) quantifies textural properties such as contrast, entropy, and homogeneity. These attributes provide valuable information on surface roughness, soil structure, and crop patterns. Structure evaluation is advantageous in differentiating between different land cover types, detecting small variations in crop conditions, and identifying anomalies such as pests or diseases (Chouhan et al., 2021; Qiao et al., 2024; Khajuria et al., 2018). Thus, by integrating texture analysis into feature elimination procedures, agricultural specialists foster a significantly more comprehensive comprehension of agricultural landscapes and make even more educated decisions regarding management.
