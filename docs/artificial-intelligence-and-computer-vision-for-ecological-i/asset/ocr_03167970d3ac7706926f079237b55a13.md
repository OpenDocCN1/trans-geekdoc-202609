RS offers accurate, up-to-date, and detailed information for tracking plant development, detecting signs of crop distress, and forecasting harvest quantities (Anuradha, 2024; Zhang et al., 2023a). This information is vital for making informed resource allocation, pest control, and irrigation management decisions. Furthermore, RS enhances the efficiency of precision agriculture by enabling site-specific management practices, which results in enhanced crop yields and diminished environmental impact (Hunt et al., 2020). The ability to monitor large and inaccessible areas also supports better planning and disaster management, guaranteeing food security and sustainable agrarian practices (Verma et al., 2023).

Fusing RS techniques with other advanced tools, including Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and ML algorithms, has further amplified its capabilities in agricultural mapping (Karnawat et al., 2020). GIS enables the spatial analysis and visualization of RS data, making it cheaper to detect patterns and trends in crop health and soil conditions (Verma et al., 2023). ML algorithms can swiftly analyze enormous datasets with high precision, generating forecasts and spotting unusual patterns that might elude human observation. Together, these technologies enable a more holistic approach to farm management, where data-driven decisions can be made to optimize productivity and sustainability. For example, ML models can anticipate insect infestations or pathogen proliferation by analyzing records and the present environmental factors, enabling growers to implement preventive strategies (Sachithra and Subhashini, 2023). Additionally, integrating RS data with GIS enables targeted agriculture by generating high-resolution maps. These guide the precise application of nutrients, pest control substances, and irrigation, optimizing resource use and effectiveness.

Moreover, the evolution of RS technology is paving the way for more innovative applications in agriculture. Advances in sensor technology, including the development of hyperspectral and thermal sensors, provide more detailed and specific information about crop conditions and soil health (Shanmugapriya et al., 2019). Hyperspectral sensors can detect subtle differences in plant physiology that indicate stress or nutrient deficiencies, while thermal sensors can identify areas of water stress by measuring surface temperatures. These advancements allow for even more precise monitoring and management of agricultural activities (Chuphal et al., 2024). Furthermore, drones equipped with advanced sensors are becoming increasingly popular, offering high-resolution data and real-time monitoring capabilities (Khaki et al., 2020). Integrating RS data with other agricultural techniques, such as automated machinery and Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, is also on the horizon, promising a future where farming is even more sustainable, efficient, and responsive to environmental changes. This continual innovation in RS technology underscores its critical role in the future of agriculture, driving progress toward more resilient and productive farming systems (Zakari et al., 2024). This chapter aims to provide an exhaustive review of the role of RS in agriculture. We explore the fundamental principles of RS and the different platforms and types of sensors used for agricultural mapping. The chapter will also cover data acquisition techniques, preprocessing steps, and the methodologies employed for image classification. Feature extraction and selection, crucial for accurate analysis, will be discussed, along with the various applications of RS in agriculture, including crop health monitoring, yield prediction, and precision agriculture.
