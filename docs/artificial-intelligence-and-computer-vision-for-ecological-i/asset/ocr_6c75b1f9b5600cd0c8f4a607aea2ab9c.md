# Machine Learning

# Supervised Learning Techniques for Soil Moisture Prediction

Supervised learning is a central element in the methodology of soil moisture prediction that involves training predictive models on datasets labeled with noise-free data (Karthikeyan et al., 2020). As in the aforementioned supervised learning techniques, training is performed using input features—temperature, humidity, soil type, and remote sensing—separating the indices and furthering the test on the target variable, in other terms, the soil moisture for every input data point. The last step in this process is the training of the dataset on which they want the model to learn; that is, the soil moisture results. There are various key steps in the process of implementing supervised learning for soil moisture prediction (Fig. 1). Supervised learning techniques provide potent tools for soil moisture prediction based on labeled datasets, enabling the training of models to effectively predict soil moisture levels associated with a vast array of input features. Some commonly used algorithms (Fig. 2), such as decision trees, random forests, and SVMs, generate robust and highly interpretable predictions, which are very useful for proper management in terms of agriculture practices, water resources, and environmental conservation methods.

# Classification

Classification models play a vital role in segregating soil moisture levels into different classes such as dry, moist, or wet. The models proved to be effective in classifying soil conditions for practical decision-making and were generally not suitable for predicting the exact numerical values of soil moisture. By categorizing continuous soil moisture data into meaningful classes, classification models provide more actionable information, especially in agriculture, where certain moisture thresholds must be closely monitored for irrigation and crop management.
