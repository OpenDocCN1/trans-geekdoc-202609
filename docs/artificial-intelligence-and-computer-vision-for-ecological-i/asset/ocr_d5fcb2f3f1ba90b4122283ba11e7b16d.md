# Topographical Features

Topographical features such as slope, aspect, elevation, and natural barriers significantly impact wildfire behavior. Fires tend to move faster uphill due to the preheating of fuels above the fire, making steeper slopes conducive to more rapid fire spread (Piralilou et al., 2022). The direction a slope faces, known as its aspect, affects the amount of sunlight and wind exposure, influencing fuel drying and fire behavior. Elevation impacts weather patterns, temperature, and fuel types, all critical to fire dynamics. Natural barriers like rivers, cliffs, and previously burned areas can act as obstacles to fire spread, providing potential control points for firefighting efforts (Kadir et al., 2023; Tonini et al., 2020).

# Weather Conditions

Weather conditions are among the most critical factors influencing wildfire ignition and spread (Bhowmik et al., 2023). Higher temperatures preheat fuels and reduce their moisture content, making them more susceptible to ignition. Conversely, lower humidity levels dry out fuels, increasing fire risk, while higher humidity can suppress fire activity (Wang et al., 2023c). Wind plays a crucial role in fire spread by directing the flames, carrying embers, and providing a continuous oxygen supply, with strong winds leading to rapid and often unpredictable fire behavior (Bouguettaya et al., 2022a). Precipitation can dampen fuels and reduce fire risk; however, prolonged dry periods can create conditions conducive to wildfires. Lastly, lightning strikes are a natural ignition source for wildfires, particularly in dry conditions, adding another layer of complexity to wildfire prediction and management (Bouguettaya et al., 2022b).

# Overview of Datasets

The foundation of any ML or DL model is the dataset used for training and evaluation. In wildfire detection and prediction, datasets typically comprise various data types, including satellite imagery, weather records, topographical information, and historical fire occurrences (Rezaie et al., 2023). These datasets are sourced from numerous agencies and platforms, such as NASA’s Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) and Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS), the European Space Agency’s Sentinel satellites, and local meteorological stations. Comprehensive and high-quality datasets are crucial for building robust models that accurately detect and predict wildfires (Rodrigues and De la Riva, 2014).

Data Preprocessing: Refining raw data for analysis is a crucial preliminary stage known as data preprocessing. This multi-step process enhances data quality and readies the information for effective use in ML algorithms.
