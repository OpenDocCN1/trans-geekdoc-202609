The creation of automated monitoring systems that continuously evaluate water quality in real-time is also made easier by the combination of AI and CV technologies. These systems can be installed in a variety of settings, including distant natural lakes and rivers and metropolitan water delivery networks. These devices, which are outfitted with cameras and sensors, gather continuous streams of data that are instantly analyzed by AI algorithms. Through real-time analysis, water quality problems, such as abrupt declines in dissolved oxygen levels or spikes in pollutant concentrations, can be identified and addressed quickly (Khan et al., 2021). This results in alarms being sent out. These automated solutions drastically lower operating costs and resource needs while simultaneously improving the efficacy and accuracy of water quality monitoring.

Furthermore, the use of AI and CV technologies in evaluating water quality encourages the development of extensive and cohesive data platforms. These platforms provide a comprehensive picture of water quality over time and place by aggregating data from several sources, such as historical records, satellite imaging, and field sensors (Khang et al., 2024) (Chai et al., 2021). AI-powered advanced data analytics and visualisation tools can promote evidence-based decision-making by assisting stakeholders in understanding intricate relationships and trends. Predictive models, for example, can mimic the results of different pollution control strategies, assisting legislators in creating environmental legislation that are more successful. In conclusion, by improving data collection, analysis, and administration, AI and CV technologies are revolutionizing the assessment of water quality and eventually paving the way for more proactive and knowledgeable management of water resources.

# Parameters and Indicators of Water Quality

To determine the health and fitness of water bodies for various uses, a range of physical, chemical, and biological factors are measured and analyzed. These processes provide the basis of the water quality assessment process. Physical characteristics of water, including temperature, turbidity, color, taste, and odor, offer fundamental insights into its quality. Temperature impacts the solubility of gases such as oxygen and the metabolic rates of aquatic organisms, whereas turbidity—a result of suspended particles—affects light penetration and, in turn, aquatic plant photosynthesis. Variations in color, flavor, and odor can be indicators of possible pollution and ecological disruptions caused by organic waste, algae, and industrial pollutants (Kothari et al., 2021) (Aydin et al., 2021).
