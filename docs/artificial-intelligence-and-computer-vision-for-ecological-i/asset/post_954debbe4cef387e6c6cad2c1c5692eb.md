## Water Quality Parameters

Water quality is determined by measuring various physical, chemical, and biological parameters. Each parameter provides insight into the condition and usability of the water.

**Physical Parameters:**
- **Temperature**: Affects the biological and chemical processes in water.
- **Turbidity**: Indicates the clarity of water; high turbidity can be harmful to aquatic life.
- **Color**: Can indicate the presence of organic matter or pollutants.

**Chemical Parameters:**
- **pH**: Measures the acidity or alkalinity of water; important for aquatic life.
- **Dissolved Oxygen (DO)**: Essential for the survival of fish and other aquatic organisms.
- **Nutrients (Nitrate, Phosphate)**: High levels can lead to eutrophication, causing algal blooms.

**Biological Parameters:**
- **Coliform Bacteria**: Indicators of microbial contamination and potential presence of pathogens.
- **Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)**: Indicates the amount of organic matter in water; high BOD can deplete oxygen levels.

The summary of common water quality parameters and their significance are given in Table 2.

Table 2. Common Water Quality Parameters and their significance (EPA, 2021).

| Parameter | Type | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Physical | Affects biological and chemical processes |
| Turbidity | Physical | Indicates water clarity; impacts aquatic life |
| pH | Chemical | Indicates acidity or alkalinity; important for aquatic organisms |
| Dissolved Oxygen (DO) | Chemical | Essential for aquatic life |
| Nitrate | Chemical | High levels can cause eutrophication |
| Coliform Bacteria | Biological | Indicates microbial contamination |

## Traditional Water Quality Monitoring Methods

Traditional water quality monitoring involves periodic sampling and laboratory analysis (Table 3). These methods include:
- **Grab Sampling:** Collecting water samples at specific locations and times for laboratory analysis.
- **Composite Sampling:** Combining multiple samples taken at different times or locations to get an average measurement.
- **On-site Testing:** Using portable kits and meters to measure parameters like pH, temperature, and turbidity directly at the sampling site.

While these approaches provide accurate and complete information, they have limitations such as time and labor requirements, as well as the inability to deliver real-time data.

Table 3. Comparison of Traditional Water Quality Monitoring methods (AWWA, 2020).

| Method | Description | Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grab Sampling | Collecting samples at specific points | Accurate and detailed analysis | Time consuming, not real-time |
| Composite Sampling | Combining samples from different times/locations | Average measurement | Labor intensive, may miss temporal variations |
| On-site Testing | Using portable kits for immediate measurements | Quick results, portable | Limited to basic parameters, less accurate than lab analysis |

### Limitations of Traditional Methods

Despite their widespread use, traditional water quality monitoring methods have several limitations:
- **Temporal Limitations:** Periodic sampling may miss short-term pollution events or fluctuations in water quality.
- **Spatial Limitations:** Sampling at fixed locations may not capture the full variability of water quality in a water body.
- **Resource Intensive:** Requires significant human and financial resources for sampling, transportation, and laboratory analysis.
- **Delay in Results:** Laboratory analysis can take days to weeks, delaying the response to pollution events.

These limitations (Table 4) highlight the need for more efficient and real-time monitoring approaches, which is where AI-based systems can offer considerable benefits.

## Overview of India’s Water Resources

India finds itself in an enviable position of having such gigantic water belts like rivers, lakes and groundwater aquifers. Urbanization, industrialization and agricultural expansion are putting unsustainable pressure on the world’s scarce water resources resulting in pollution and depletion. As per a report published by CPCB, 70% of India’s water sources are threatened including the major rivers like Ganges and Yamuna (CPCB, 2020).

## Current Challenges in Maintaining Water Quality

Despite efforts to improve water quality, India continues to grapple with various challenges, including industrial pollution, agricultural runoff, untreated sewage, and inadequate wastewater treatment infrastructure. These challenges are further compounded by ineffective regulatory enforcement, lack of public awareness, and limited resources for monitoring and management.

## Impact of Poor Water Quality on Public Health and Environment

Poor water quality has far-reaching effects on human health, ecology, and socioeconomic development. Waterborne diseases including cholera, typhoid fever, and diarrhea are common in many parts of India, especially among underprivileged people with limited access to clean water and sanitation facilities. Furthermore, water pollution harms aquatic biodiversity, disrupts ecosystems, and jeopardizes livelihoods based on fishing and agriculture.
