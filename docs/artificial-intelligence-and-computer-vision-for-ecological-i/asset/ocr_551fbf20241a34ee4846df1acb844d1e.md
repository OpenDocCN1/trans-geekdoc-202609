The ability of models to get better at a task via practice, or by processing more data, is what defines machine learning. Three primary categories of machine learning algorithms exist: reinforcement learning, unsupervised learning, and supervised learning. A labeled dataset is used to train the algorithm in supervised learning, where each training example has an output label associated with it. The programme is supposed to be able to anticipate new, unobserved data with accuracy (Singh et al., 2023). The objective of unsupervised learning is to find underlying structures or hidden patterns in the input data by training on data without labeled replies. By rewarding good behaviors and penalizing undesired ones, reinforcement learning allows an algorithm to learn the best course of action through trial and error.

A kind of machine learning known as “deep learning” has become a potent tool in the field of artificial intelligence. It uses multi-layered neural networks, hence the name “deep” learning. Because these neural networks are made to resemble the structure and operation of the human brain, they can analyze and learn in complicated ways from vast amounts of data. Deep learning is especially helpful for applications like picture recognition, natural language processing, and speech recognition because it performs well on tasks involving unstructured data, such as text, audio, and photos (Singh et al., 2024). While recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are better suited for sequential data analysis, such time series or language modeling, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), a sort of deep learning model, are especially good at image-related tasks. Deep learning’s progress has greatly improved AI systems’ capabilities, enabling them to achieve previously unheard-of levels of accuracy and efficiency across a wide range of applications. Deep learning and AI have the potential to revolutionize many industries, including environmental sciences, as computing power and algorithmic complexity increase.

# Applications of AI in Environmental Sciences

There are now more options for tracking, controlling, and reducing environmental problems thanks to the use of AI in environmental sciences. AI finds widespread use in environmental monitoring, where algorithms analyze massive amounts of data from several sources to spot patterns and abnormalities. AI is capable of analyzing remote sensing data and satellite pictures, for instance, to track the health of coral reefs, monitor deforestation, and identify illicit fishing activities, some of the applications are depicted in Fig. 3. By using past pollution data and climatic variables, machine learning algorithms can forecast air quality levels and issue early warnings for days with dangerously low air quality (Zhong et al., 2021).

Artificial Intelligence is revolutionary in the field of water quality evaluation. AI systems can continuously monitor water quality indicators including pH, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, and the presence of pollutants by analyzing data from in situ sensors, satellite photos, and historical records (Gupta et al., 2021). Proprietary analysis techniques may overlook some patterns and correlations in the data, but machine learning algorithms can, making it possible to anticipate changes in water quality more precisely.
