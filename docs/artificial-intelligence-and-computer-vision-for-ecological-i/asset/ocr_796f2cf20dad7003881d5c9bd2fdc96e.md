# Traditional Soil Moisture Measurement Methods
Gravimetric or Oven Drying Technique

Currently, the most common practical approach for determining soil moisture content (θm) is the gravimetric method (Kashyap et al., 2021). All indirect methods are calibrated using this most natural and traditional approach. Using an auger, gather a sample of soil from the field and weigh it to determine its wet and dry contents. Weighing the soil sample (M_s) at the moment of sampling yields the moist weight, and drying the soil sample in an oven at 105°C for 24 hours yields the dry weight. The soil water (M_w) is represented by the weight loss.

θm = Mw / Ms

This technique guarantees an accurate assessment of the moisture content regardless of the type and salinity of the soil. Nevertheless, a repeated measurement is not possible due to the method’s destructive character (Zazueta et al., 1994). However, because of the labour and time-intensive techniques, it is not appropriate for large-scale or real-time field studies. Stated differently, the gravimetric approach-based measurements of soil moisture are not very practical for agricultural or hydrological applications where a large number of in situ observations are required on a regular basis, nor for validating remote sensing-based products that necessitate sampling within a limited time window during satellite passes (Fig. 2).

# Volumetric Method

The volume of water in a particular amount of dry soil, typically in 1 m³ is known as the volumetric water content. VWC can be computed using volume (cm³/cm³) or mass (g/g). This moisture content percentage is displayed for output. With this method, a soil sample is taken from the field using a core sampler of known volume at appropriate root zone depth. Its wet and dry weights are then determined using a procedure similarly to the gravimetric water content method. The amount of water in the sample is shown by the difference between the wet and dry masses of soil.
