+   - Net Present Value Calculation: The NPV is computed by discounting the net cash flows (benefits minus costs) from each period to their present value using the chosen discount rate. The summation of discounted cash flows yields the NPV, indicating the net economic value generated by the remote sensing applications (Wongchai et al., 2022; Yousefzadeh-Najafabadi et al., 2021).

Example: Suppose, for this analysis, that a farmer spends $50,000 on remote sensing equipment for crop management and monitoring. The benefits of higher crop output and lower input costs are projected to be worth $20,000 yearly, while the operational costs come in at $10,000. With a five-year timeframe and a 5% discount rate, we can compute the net present value (NPV) as follows:

+   - Year 1: Benefit = $20,000 – $10,000 (Cost) = $10,000
+   - Year 2: Benefit = $20,000 – $10,000 (Cost) = $10,000
+   - Year 3: Benefit = $20,000 – $10,000 (Cost) = $10,000
+   - Year 4: Benefit = $20,000 – $10,000 (Cost) = $10,000
+   - Year 5: Benefit = $20,000 – $10,000 (Cost) = $10,000

Calculating the present value of cash flows for each year and summing them up, we obtain:

NPV = 10,000/(1+0.05)^1 + 10,000/(1+0.05)^2 + 10,000/(1+0.05)^3 + 10,000/(1+0.05)^4 + 10,000/(1+0.05)^5 - 50,000 (3)

NPV = 10,000/1.05 + 10,000/1.1025 + 10,000/1.1576 + 10,000/1.2155 + 10,000/1.2763 - 50,000 (4)

NPV = 9523.81 + 9072.64 + 8638.55 + 8222.41 + 7823.48 - 50,000 (5)

NPV = $12,280.89

The calculated NPV of $12,280.89 suggests that the investment in remote sensing technology generates a positive net economic value over the specified time horizon. This indicates that the benefits accrued from improved crop yield and reduced input costs outweigh the initial investment and operational expenses, thereby justifying the adoption of remote sensing applications in agriculture.
