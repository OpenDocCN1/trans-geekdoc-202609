# Technologies and Tools for Plant Growth Mapping

Monitoring, measuring, and analyzing plant growth and health is done through the use of several technologies in plant growth mapping. While multispectral, hyperspectral, and thermal imaging technologies are used to analyze plant health and stress levels, remote sensing—which makes use of drones and satellite imagery—provides large-scale data. Soil moisture, nutritional levels, and climatic conditions are tracked by sensors positioned on the ground. 3D modeling examines structural alterations, while computer vision scans photos to identify growth patterns and diseases. Anomaly detection, automated categorization, and predictive analytics are provided by Artificial Intelligence and ML. Web of Things (IoT) devices allow for continuous data integration and monitoring, while Geographic Information Systems (GIS) incorporate spatial data. Robotics enables precision agriculture and automated monitoring, while cloud computing manages big databases and real-time analytics. Improved plant growth control is made possible by these coupled technologies, which improve farming methods.

Moreover, measurements of gene expression, cell division, cell expansion, and their rates and geographic distributions are crucial for providing a thorough characterization of the dynamic behaviors of the cells responsible for organ growth and development. Cell division and lineage patterns in shoot and root meristems were first identified by image-based research. The creation of imaging technologies to see cellular dynamics in living things resulted from this. Researchers can examine data and generate testable predictions by combining computer modeling and experimental methodologies. Predictive models that take system behavior into account may be developed using biological studies, such as z-stacks of confocal microscope pictures. After that, these models can produce counterintuitive predictions for more experimental investigation (Yang et al., 2020).
