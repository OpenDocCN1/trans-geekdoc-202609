# Role of AI and Computer Vision (CV) Technologies

Water quality evaluation is being revolutionized by Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Computer Vision (CV) technologies, which offer novel approaches for accurate analysis and real-time monitoring. With unmatched accuracy and speed, AI can handle and analyze enormous volumes of data about water quality through its many branches, including machine learning and deep learning (Khang et al., 2024). Traditional monitoring methods may miss minor patterns and anomalies that point to pollution or changes in water quality, but these technologies can identify them. Predictive analytics is made possible by the ability of machine learning algorithms, for instance, to identify trends in data sets about chemical concentrations, temperature variations, or biological markers (Ward et al., 2021). This enables the prediction of possible environmental problems before they become serious, an approach towards the application of AI is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Environmental monitoring greatly benefits from the application of computer vision, a branch of AI that focuses on giving computers the ability to read and comprehend visual data. With the use of CV technology, water quality factors including turbidity, color changes, and the presence of floating trash or algae blooms can be evaluated by analyzing photos and videos taken by satellites, drones, and underwater cameras (Ma et al., 2023) (O’Mahony et al., 2020). For large-scale and remote water bodies where traditional sampling is logistically difficult, this capability is vital. For example, CV-analyzed satellite images can track the evolution of algal blooms over time, offering vital information for water resource management and eutrophication
