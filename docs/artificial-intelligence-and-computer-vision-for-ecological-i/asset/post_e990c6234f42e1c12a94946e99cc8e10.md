
AI’s application in processing and analyzing soil moisture data represents a significant advancement in agricultural and environmental sciences. By leveraging AI techniques, stakeholders can make informed decisions, enhance resource efficiency, and mitigate risks associated with soil moisture variability, thereby contributing to sustainable land management practices and food security.

## Benefits and Challenges of Integrating AI into Soil Moisture Evaluation Systems

Integrating AI into soil moisture evaluation systems brings significant advancements but also introduces a set of challenges that need to be carefully managed.

### Benefits

- **Improved Accuracy and Precision:** AI algorithms, such as machine learning and deep learning, can analyze complex and large datasets to accurately predict soil moisture levels, considering various environmental factors. AI provides high-resolution insights into soil moisture variability across different parts of a field, enabling precise irrigation management.
- **Increased Efficiency:** AI-driven systems adjust irrigation schedules based on real-time soil moisture data, reducing water waste and ensuring crops receive the right amount of water. Continuous, automated monitoring reduces the need for manual soil moisture checks, saving time and labor.
- **Cost Savings:** By optimizing the use of water and nutrients, AI systems help reduce operational costs. Automation of data collection and analysis minimizes labour costs associated with manual monitoring.
- **Environmental Sustainability:** Efficient water use reduces runoff and leaching, decreasing the environmental impact and promoting sustainable farming. AI systems help farmers adapt to climate variability by providing accurate soil moisture forecasts and irrigation recommendations.
- **Risk Management:** AI can detect abnormal soil moisture levels that may indicate drought or flood risks, allowing for timely preventive measures. AI-driven insights support informed decision-making, enhancing resilience against environmental and climate-related risks.
- **Comprehensive Data Integration:** AI integrates data from various sources like satellite imagery, IoT sensors, and weather stations to provide a holistic view of soil moisture conditions. AI systems can handle extensive datasets and large geographic areas, making them suitable for both small-scale and large-scale agricultural operations.

### Challenges

- **Data Quality and Availability:** Inconsistent or incomplete data can affect the accuracy of AI models. Reliable and continuous data collection is crucial. Soil moisture sensors may have limitations in accuracy and durability, which can affect data quality.
- **Technical Complexity:** Building and maintaining AI models requires expertise in AI and soil science, which may not be readily available. Incorporating AI systems into existing agricultural infrastructure can be complex and may require significant changes.
- **Cost of Implementation:** The cost of deploying AI systems, including sensors, software, and training, can be high, which may be a barrier for small farmers. Regular maintenance, sensor calibration, and model updates incur additional costs.
- **Scalability Issues:** AI models trained on data from one region may not perform well in other regions due to differences in soil types, climate, and farming practices. Ensuring AI models are adaptable to various conditions and scales is a significant challenge.
- **User Adoption:** Farmers and land managers need adequate training to effectively use AI systems and interpret results. There may be reluctance to adopt new technologies among those accustomed to traditional practices.

The integration of AI into soil moisture evaluation systems offers substantial benefits, including improved accuracy, efficiency, cost savings, sustainability, and enhanced risk management. However, addressing challenges related to data quality, technical complexity, costs, scalability, user adoption, and ethical concerns is crucial. Collaborative efforts among researchers, technologists, policymakers, and the agricultural community are essential to ensure AI technologies are accessible, reliable, and beneficial for all stakeholders involved.

## Case Studies Highlighting the Application of AI in Soil Moisture Evaluation

Jung et al., 2017 studied the spatial soil moisture estimation using a multiple linear regression model in South Korea. The study used Terra MODIS land surface temperature (LST) and normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) data from January 2013 to December 2015. For three chosen LST stations, the original LST and observed LST had a coefficient of determination (R²) of 0.71, whereas the corrected LST and observed LST had an R² of 0.95. The MLR model can be an effective tool for estimating soil moisture, especially in regions where *in situ* data is scarce. Anusha et al., 2020 emphasized soil moisture as a key variable in environmental processes, including evaporation and plant transpiration. The researchers used a sensor connected to a microcontroller to measure soil moisture. This sensor was inserted into the soil to retrieve values that are stored in a database. The KNN algorithm is applied to analyze the collected soil moisture data, helping to make informed decisions regarding soil management and agricultural practices. Hegazi et al., 2023 develop a CNN model that can accurately predict soil moisture using Sentinel-2 satellite imagery. The model consists of six convolutional layers, one pooling layer, and two fully connected layers. The study found that using combinations of Sentinel-2 bands as input data for the CNN architecture is more effective than using single bands or all bands together. The best results were achieved using a combination of Red, Red Edge 1, Red Edge 2, Red Edge 3, NIR, and Red Edge 4 bands (R² = 0.7094, MAE = 0.0277, RMSE = 0.0418).

Imanpour et al., 2023 discussed the downscaling of Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) soil moisture data from 3 km to 1 km resolution using remote sensing. The study evaluates two methods, regression and artificial neural network (ANN), across different climates and land covers. Land Surface Temperature (LST), Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), and brightness temperatures were used as the input features. DEM and SWIR data do not significantly improve downscaling. The performance was best in homogeneous climatic conditions, with high accuracy in bare soil and flat regions. Liu et al., 2023 employed random forest (RF), Extra Tree (ET), and linear regression (LR) algorithms to estimate soil moisture at different depths using optical satellite data sources in Farming Land of Northern China. The random forest model was particularly noted for its ability to handle large datasets and complex relationships between variables, making it well-suited for predicting soil moisture levels from remotely sensed data.

## Challenges, Future Directions, and Conclusion

Challenges in integrating remote sensing and AI techniques for soil moisture evaluation include addressing data quality and availability issues, ensuring consistent and reliable data for accurate modeling. Technical complexities arise in developing and maintaining AI models that effectively capture the variability of soil moisture across different environmental conditions, requiring specialized expertise in both AI and soil science. The high initial implementation costs and ongoing maintenance expenses pose financial barriers, particularly for small-scale farmers. Scalability remains a challenge, as AI models may not generalize well across diverse geographic regions without robust calibration and adaptation. Moreover, achieving widespread user adoption necessitates comprehensive training and support to overcome reluctance toward adopting new technologies in agriculture. Future directions should focus on enhancing data standardization, advancing AI algorithms for real-time predictive modeling, and reducing costs to make these technologies more accessible. Collaborative efforts among researchers, policymakers, and practitioners are essential to overcome these challenges and ensure the sustainable integration of AI and remote sensing in soil moisture management, promoting efficient water use and environmental stewardship in agriculture.
