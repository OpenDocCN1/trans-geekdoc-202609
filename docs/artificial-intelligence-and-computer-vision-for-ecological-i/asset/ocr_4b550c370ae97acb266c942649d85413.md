Rising global temperatures have increased the likelihood of severe wildfires, particularly in areas experiencing prolonged dry spells (Pérez-Sánchez et al., 2019). With ongoing global warming, we anticipate more frequent severe weather occurrences, including intense heat and prolonged hot spells. These conditions will likely create environments increasingly prone to widespread and recurrent wildfires. Intense heat episodes further dry out the landscape, creating an ideal environment for these destructive blazes (Gaudreau et al., 2016). This, in turn, leads to amplified carbon dioxide emissions from wildfires, further triggering climate change and perpetuating a cyclical pattern of severe wildfires (McColl-Gausden et al., 2022). This feedback loop, illustrated in Fig. 1, highlights the interconnectedness of wildfires and climate change.

Cutting-edge technologies, sophisticated modeling, enhanced sensing systems, and improved observation methods have significantly enhanced our ability to detect and forecast wildfire progression (Ladi et al., 2022). The concurrent evolution of computational theory, mathematical innovations, and increased processing capabilities have armed us with powerful tools for data collection and analysis (Ding et al., 2022). Implementing these innovative technologies has markedly improved our capacity to tackle the complex issues inherent in wildfire control and prevention (Coen et al., 2020). Applications of AI-driven approaches, such as ML, have gained popularity in recent years and have become versatile tools for technological advancement (Rolnick et al., 2019). ML, a subfield of AI, employs algorithms to use big datasets to identify patterns (Jafar Ali Ibrahim et al., 2022; Kalinaki et al., 2024a; Kalinaki et al., 2023b).
