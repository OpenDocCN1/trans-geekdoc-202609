# Introduction

“Remote sensing is a method of obtaining information about the properties of an object without coming into physical contact with it”. It has become an indispensable tool in modern agriculture, offering insights into crucial aspects such as crop health, soil conditions, water availability, and environmental factors. This technology, utilizing sensors deployed on satellites, drones, and other platforms, has evolved significantly over time due to advancements in sensor technology, data processing algorithms, and satellite capabilities (Coppin and Bauer, 1996). Early satellite missions such as Landsat laid the groundwork by providing initial imagery for land cover and crop monitoring, marking the genesis of remote sensing’s integration into agricultural practices. Subsequent developments in high-resolution optical sensors, thermal sensors, and radar sensors have enabled the collection of detailed data on various agricultural parameters, including crop health metrics, soil moisture levels, temperature fluctuations, and pest infestations (Wu et al., 2015).

This chapter aims to explore the diverse applications of remote sensing in agriculture mapping, with a particular emphasis on key areas such as crop health monitoring and soil analysis (Wu et al., 2015).
