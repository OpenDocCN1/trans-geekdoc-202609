## Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence and Computer Vision in Agriculture

Artificial Intelligence (AI) in agriculture incorporates leveraging algorithms and machine learning models to overhaul farming practices, boost crop yields, and improve resource management. Precision farming may be an essential application where Artificial Intelligence analyzes data from sensors, weather stations, and satellite imagery to optimize field-level management. Crop monitoring through Artificial Intelligence enables farmers to assess crop health, recognize diseases early, and take preventive measures, thus to protect crops. Besides, Artificial Intelligence models predict soil quality by analyzing data from soil sensors, helping farmers select and apply fertilizers and treatments. Predictive analytics fueled by Artificial Intelligence can forecast crop yields, pest infestations, and climate patterns, allowing farmers to make informed choices about planting, water management, and harvesting. Other than that, mechanized devices such as autonomous tractors and harvesters utilize Artificial Intelligence to perform farming tasks with high precision, diminishing the requirement for manual labor and extending efficiency. Computer Vision (CV), a subset of AI, enables computers to interpret and analyze visual data from the environment. In agriculture, Computer Vision is critical for various applications, such as crop and weed recognition (Hesami and Jones, 2020). Computer Vision systems can distinguish between crops and weeds, allowing for more effective herbicide application, reduced chemical use, and the development of sustainable farming strategies. Plant disease detection is another pivotal application where Computer Vision systems analyze pictures of plants to recognize illnesses at an early stage, allowing for timely interventions and treatment. Besides, Computer Vision advancement helps in fruit counting and yield estimation by precisely evaluating the number of fruit items on trees, helping farmers predict and manage their harvests. Furthermore, Computer Vision-powered drones and robots can inspect wide areas, providing real-time data on crop conditions, growth patterns, and prospective difficulties, allowing farmers to make data-driven decisions and improve overall efficiency.

## Technologies and Tools for Plant Growth Mapping

Monitoring, measuring, and analyzing plant growth and health is done through the use of several technologies in plant growth mapping. While multispectral, hyperspectral, and thermal imaging technologies are used to analyze plant health and stress levels, remote sensing—which makes use of drones and satellite imagery—provides large-scale data. Soil moisture, nutritional levels, and climatic conditions are tracked by sensors positioned on the ground. 3D modeling examines structural alterations, while computer vision scans photos to identify growth patterns and diseases. Anomaly detection, automated categorization, and predictive analytics are provided by Artificial Intelligence and ML. Internet of Things (IoT) devices allow for continuous data integration and monitoring, while Geographic Information Systems (GIS) incorporate spatial data. Robotics enables precision agriculture and automated monitoring, while cloud computing manages big databases and real-time analytics. Improved plant growth control is made possible by these coupled technologies, which improve farming methods.

Moreover, measurements of gene expression, cell division, cell expansion, and their rates and geographic distributions are crucial for providing a thorough characterization of the dynamic behaviors of the cells responsible for organ growth and development. Cell division and lineage patterns in shoot and root meristems were first identified by image-based research. The creation of imaging technologies to see cellular dynamics in living things resulted from this. Researchers can examine data and generate testable predictions by combining computer modeling and experimental methodologies. Predictive models that take system behavior into account may be developed using biological studies, such as z-stacks of confocal microscope pictures. After that, these models can produce counterintuitive predictions for more experimental investigation (Yang et al., 2020).

## Deep Learning Applications in Plant Growth Monitoring

Multi-layer artificial neural networks (ANNs) are used in deep learning (DL), a subset of machine learning (ML), to model data. Since these models are data-driven, the parameters and outcomes they produce are predicated on the real-world instances they were trained on. DL may acquire data representations in several ways, including:

- **Supervised Learning:** Refers to predetermined results.
- **Unsupervised Learning:** This method concentrates on finding patterns in the data.
- **Semi-Supervised Learning:** Blends supervised and unsupervised techniques, weighing the advantages and disadvantages of each.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Based on incentives and punishments, this approach employs trial-and-error techniques.

Plant development is difficult to generalize for traditional machine learning (ML) models like decision trees, k-nearest neighbors (k-NNs), and support vector machines (SVMs) because biological factors like physiology and genetics are so complicated (Sharma et al., 2023).

## Dataset Acquisition and Processing

In plant growth mapping, there are several procedures and difficulties involved in the dataset gathering and processing. Field research, greenhouse trials, open databases, and remote sensing are some of the sources from which data is gathered. This data consists of genotypic information, phenotypic measures, environmental data, and high-resolution photographs. To maintain dataset integrity throughout processing, data cleaning, normalization, and annotation are required. The diversity and resilience of data are increased by data augmentation techniques including flipping, rotating, and creating synthetic data. While segmentation approaches separate individual plant sections and analyzes growth trends over time, feature extraction, both automatic and human, find important features. Multimodal data fusion improves analysis, whereas data integration merges sources to provide an extensive dataset. After that, the dataset is divided into training, validation, and testing sets, and its model dependability is checked using cross-validation before being ready for analysis.

## IoT in Plant Growth Mapping

Plant growth mapping is greatly improved by the Internet of Things (IoT), which allows for continuous monitoring and data integration of several environmental and plant-specific characteristics. Field-based Internet of Things (IoT) sensors monitor temperature, humidity, nutrient levels, and soil moisture in real-time, sending the collected data to a central system for analysis. This consolidated data gathering offers a thorough understanding of the dynamics of plant development.

IoT systems that use real-time monitoring can quickly identify problems such as nutrient deficits or drought stress and may send out automatic notifications that allow for early response. To forecast future plant development, yield, and possible problems, machine learning evaluates the gathered data. This helps with anomaly identification and informed decision-making (Dawn et al., 2023).

By providing targeted fertilization, irrigation, and pest management that are suited to individual plant needs, optimizing resource consumption, and boosting sustainability, IoT makes precision agriculture possible.

Using computers or cellphones, farmers may remotely oversee their crops, and the Internet of Things (IoT) can automate equipment like greenhouse climate management and irrigation pumps.
