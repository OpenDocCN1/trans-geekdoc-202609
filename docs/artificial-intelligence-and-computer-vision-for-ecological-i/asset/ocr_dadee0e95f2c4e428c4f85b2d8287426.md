# Benefits and Challenges of Traditional Soil Moisture Evaluation Techniques

Traditional soil moisture evaluation approaches have been around for an extended period and provide a few unique advantages while also posing certain challenges.

# Benefits

+   - **Accuracy**: Many tried and tested approaches, like the gravimetric method, yield very accurate findings when applied appropriately. They offer an accurate standard for calibrating more recent sensor technologies.
+   - **Simplicity**: Certain methods, like the feel and appearance of the soil or using a tensiometer, are quite simple to perform and do not require a lot of specialist tools.
+   - **Standardization**: Since these techniques frequently follow well defined steps, the data gathered can be compared between other studies and fields.

# Challenges

+   - **Labor intensive**: It can take a lot of time and labour to gather and analyse soil samples for gravimetric procedures.
+   - **Destructive**: Certain techniques, such as the gravimetric procedure, obliterate the soil sample under study. This can be problematic if you need to monitor the same spot over time.
+   - **Limited scope**: Most traditional methods provide data from a single point. This can be misleading if soil moisture varies significantly across a field.
+   - **Time-consuming**: Techniques like the gravimetric method can take a while to get results, which is not ideal for real-time irrigation management.

Conventional techniques yield useful information, but they might not always be the best or most sensible course of action. Newer sensor-based systems that provide continuous monitoring and data collection across bigger areas are emerging as technology progresses.

This prediction model used an analytical ring resonant model with polynomial interpolation approximation via lumped element model. The sensing area and the measurement accuracy were also improved. This prediction model was found to agree well with the commercial dielectric probe in the dielectric prediction of peat (P 28% m.c.) and sandy (P 10% m.c.) soils.
