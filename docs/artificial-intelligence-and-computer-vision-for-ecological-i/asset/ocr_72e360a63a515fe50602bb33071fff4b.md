# Water Salinity Prediction

Predicting climatic fluctuations and comprehending ocean dynamics depend on precise estimation of the ocean subsurface salinity (Qi et al., 2024). Salinity has a major effect on the physical, chemical, and biological activities that take place in the ocean. Estuaries are critical for material exchange between land and sea, with salinity indicating freshwater mixing. Influenced by river inputs, tides, and weather, salinity helps assess water quality and ecosystem health in estuaries (Guillou et al., 2023). The distribution of salinity is essential for the development and reproduction of marine life Seawater’s density is strongly impacted by its salt concentration, which in turn affects circulation and stratification (Schmidt et al., 2018). An efficient machine learning and regression model was developed to estimate future salt levels based on past data and predict the salinity of water quality (Chawla et al., 2023). Support Vector Regression delivered the most accurate surface salinity estimates, particularly enhancing projections derived from complex numerical models in low-salinity periods. This demonstrates the potential benefits of applying algorithms for machine learning alongside traditional physical models (Guillou et al., 2023).

# Water Turbidity Prediction

The quantity of light scattered by suspended particles in water, often referred to as suspensions, is measured as turbidity, which may be operationally defined as the percentage that can be eliminated by a 0.22 μm (or 0.45 μm) pore size filter (Kirk, 1985). Turbidity in water affects aquatic life, and measuring pollution levels requires a precise understanding of it. An algorithm based on neural networks is being used in the marine environment to track turbidity (Kumar et al., 2022). Wang et al., 2021 showed that using an artificial neural network with tide levels as inputs and NTU readings as outputs accurately predicts turbidity. Key data includes tide levels from the two cycles before the forecast period (Wang et al., 2021). High-turbidity zones were successfully predicted by a machine learning technique utilizing meteorological and satellite TSS data, with random forest regression producing the best results. The region of elevated turbidity from the day before, the wind, and the tide level were important predictions; rainfall had little bearing (Nagayama and Tonooka, 2023). However, forecasting turbidity is still a topic for future research since the causes of turbidity occurrence and dispersion might differ.
