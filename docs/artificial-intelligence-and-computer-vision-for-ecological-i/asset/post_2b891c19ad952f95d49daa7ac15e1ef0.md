Freshwater algal blooms are regarded as an undesirable ecological outcome of eutrophication. The Han River’s eutrophication and algal blooms have been successfully predicted using machine learning (ML) techniques, which have also shed light on the fundamental principles governing these processes (Ly et al., 2021). Three models of deep learning—multilayer perceptrons (MLP), RNN, and LSTM—were employed to forecast chlorophyll-a (Chl-a), an indicator of algal activity, and their performance was evaluated using RMSE and ordinary least squares (OLS) regression analysis. LSTM regression proved to be the most accurate for predicting harmful algal blooms compared to OLS regression (Lee and Lee, 2018). Similarly, (Kim et al., 2022) demonstrated four models of ML—k-Nearest Neighbour, Decision Tree, LR, and SVM—to forecast cyanobacterial bloom occurrences. By using logCyano as a feature, which is the logarithm of cyanobacterial cell density, these models achieved in general fine precision. Growing machine learning models have shown important connections among microbiological populations, nutrients, and algal bloom dynamics; moreover, they have tremendous promise in fields where chemical, physical, and biological data are easily accessible. These models might help determine the significance of groundwater contributions by identifying signals from groundwater in algal blooms. All things considered, machine learning techniques can improve our capacity to quickly and precisely determine the part that groundwater plays in algal blooms (Sheik et al., 2024).

## Machine Learning Across Different Water Scenarios

A potent technique for tackling a variety of problems in water management systems and treatment is machine learning. Artificial intelligence approaches are transforming the way we manage water resources, from hydrologic modeling to real-time monitoring and predictive analysis. Recently, there has been a lot of interest in the use of machine learning in water resource management. The dire need for viable options is highlighted by the deterioration of water quality in metropolitan areas, which is mostly attributable to industrial and municipal effluent from human activities. The ML utilization in water quality research has gained prominence due to significant advancements in analysis and prediction techniques. With integrated and periodic water quality monitoring data serving as standards for water system management, data collecting is the key first step in the development of models for machine learning. Despite being widely used, standard environmental monitoring techniques have difficulties when it comes to in situ monitoring because of practical constraints (Li et al., 2020). Poor model performance can result from missing, inaccurate, or damaged data brought on by machinery malfunctions or human mistakes, underscoring the need for data cleaning in machine learning applications. There are several approaches to data cleaning, such as removing problematic data, imputation based on average or median, or using approaches for matrix completion and machine learning to improve raw data (Donders et al., 2006). Prediction accuracy in machine learning applications is influenced by selecting the kind of model and the caliber of the training set (Zhu et al., 2022). Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and Support Vector Machines (SVM) excel in predicting water quality components, with SVM often demonstrating superior accuracy and generalization compared to ANN (Wang et al., 2013). SVM’s effectiveness is attributed to its stability in optimizing model parameters and setting an upper bound on generalization error, minimizing errors more effectively than ANN (Kim, 2003). Real-time monitoring or predictive modeling is crucial for managing river systems effectively, with Bootstrapped Wavelet Neural Networks (BWNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks demonstrating proficiency in handling variable and non-seasonal time-series data on water quality (Zhi et al., 2021). Machine learning techniques including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Decision Trees (DT), Random Forests (RF), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) have been widely used in groundwater quality assessments in recent years. A comparative study of five algorithms for data mining—the typical DT, RF, chi-square automatic interaction detector, and iterative dichotomizer 3—was carried out to determine the critical factors affecting the quality of groundwater in semiarid regions and how they affect high-quality groundwater areas in Tabriz City, Iran (Jeihouni et al., 2020). Moreover, the groundwater quality maps that are produced via the application of geographic information system techniques are a typical way to identify groundwater contamination with greater accuracy (Lee et al., 2019). Machine learning is essential to wastewater treatment because it helps with the prediction, operation, and administration of wastewater treatment facilities, technological optimization, and monitoring of water quality. Before treatment, a water quality evaluation is required for both industrial and household wastewater due to the variety of contaminants present (Nishat et al., 2023). Soft sensors built on black box models provide real-time online monitoring, which improves the monitoring of indicators such as E. coli (Foschi et al., 2021). Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and soft sensors work together to overcome maintenance and operating issues in wastewater treatment facilities, allowing for real-time ammonia and chlorine monitoring. Numerous factors affect the effluent quality of wastewater treatment plants, and machine learning helps with maintenance and operation management. While deep learning improves measurement accuracy in flow-measurement sensors, XGBoost and RF detect contaminants and their source spots in wastewater networks (Buras and Solano Donado, 2021). Machine learning applications in wastewater treatment facilities have chances to save expenses and enhance overall performance (Wang et al., 2021).

## Challenges in Predicting Water Quality Using ML

Machine learning (ML) represents a formidable computational paradigm for addressing multifaceted issues within the domain of water resources, offering applications ranging from the optimization of water resource allocation to the prediction of water quality and the management of recurrent water scarcity. Notwithstanding its potential, the deployment of ML algorithms for water quality assessment in this sector is impeded by a plethora of challenges. Those challenges are described below.

The utilization of machine learning (ML) algorithms for any designated application necessitates the availability of a substantial corpus of high-fidelity data. ML models are calibrated based on the acquired datasets, enabling them to discern patterns or trends within the data. Such identified patterns are instrumental in the prediction of variables. After model calibration, validation processes are undertaken, and only upon achieving a predefined threshold of accuracy are these models deemed suitable for deployment in real-world field applications. However, the financial exigencies associated with data procurement serve as a deterrent to the widespread adoption of ML algorithms within the domain of water resources. This challenge is further exacerbated by technological constraints. Moreover, even in instances where data is accessible, it may not invariably conform to the requisite resolution (Zhu et al., 2022).

Machine learning-derived models are predominantly empirical and are tailored to the specific conditions for which they were formulated. The intrinsic complexity and heterogeneity of real-world aquatic systems present significant challenges to the generalizability of water quality predictions. Moreover, the environmental dynamics are distinct across different water bodies, thereby limiting the applicability of a model to its system of origin. This limitation poses a substantial impediment to the broad-scale deployment of ML methodologies. To mitigate this issue, models necessitate recalibration when applied to disparate systems from those they were originally designed for, incurring additional resource expenditures. Consequently, reliance on ‘black-box’ solutions should be eschewed in favor of approaches that yield process-based scientific insights (Schafer et al., 2022). The initial stage in the development of any machine learning (ML) model necessitates the judicious selection of pertinent input parameters. It is crucial to select a subset of parameters affecting the water quality that encapsulate sufficient predictive information regarding water quality. This selection is paramount, as an excess of input variables may result in redundancy, whereas an insufficient number could lead to inaccuracies in the model. Optimal variable selection can enhance model accuracy, as inappropriate choices may adversely affect model performance. Furthermore, each ML model exhibits unique responses to different sets of input variables and data patterns. Therefore, it is imperative to identify variables that augment the efficacy of a specific ML model. The methodologies such as the partial autocorrelation function (PACF), autocorrelation function (ACF), correlation coefficient, and cross-correlation function, are instrumental in discerning the salient predictors (Asadollah et al., 2021). Better data acquisition through sophisticated sensors, enhancing the applicability of the model to a universal scale and an interdisciplinary approach are to be considered to address the above-mentioned challenges.

## Conclusion

The evaluation of water quality, which has historically relied on labor-intensive approaches, gains a great deal from machine learning (ML) techniques since they improve accuracy, efficiency, and affordability. Applications of machine learning include controlling shortages and allocating water resources optimally. Notwithstanding, several obstacles persist, such as the requirement for extensive and superior quality data sets, limitations in terms of finances and technology, and the intricacy of actual aquatic systems. Resource requirements rise when machine learning models require to be recalibrated for diverse contexts.
