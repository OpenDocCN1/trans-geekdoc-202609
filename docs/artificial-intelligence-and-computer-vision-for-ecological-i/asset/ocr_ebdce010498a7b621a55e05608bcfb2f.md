Traditional soil moisture evaluation methods like gravimetric methods, neutron probes, and tensiometers are accurate but have notable limitations. Gravimetric methods, though precise, are labor-intensive and time-consuming, providing only point-based measurements. Neutron probes are costly, involve radioactive sources, and need careful handling, while tensiometers require regular maintenance and are effective only at specific moisture levels. These limitations—high cost, laboriousness, and limited spatial coverage—hinder their use for large-scale or real-time assessments (Ochsner et al., 2013). Consequently, remote sensing systems have been developed, significantly improving soil moisture predictions (Wang and Qu, 2009). Advances in remote sensing technology now enable the monitoring of spatial time-series soil moisture using optical, thermal infrared (TIR), and microwave observations. New methods and algorithms combining satellite-based optical/TIR or optical/microwave data have enhanced soil moisture retrieval. However, TIR and passive microwave observations, while useful for regional and global scales, have limitations in spatial and temporal resolution for detailed agricultural applications.

The integration of deep learning and advanced machine learning techniques has revolutionized the retrieval of soil parameters, surpassing traditional models. These modern methods are adept at modeling the complex, nonlinear relationships inherent in soil moisture (SM) estimation. For example, deep learning approaches, like the Unsupervised Self-Training Method (USTM), has demonstrated higher precision and accuracy in estimating SM using various indices and ground truth data compared to traditional techniques (Sedighi et al., 2024). Machine learning algorithms are now pivotal in SM estimation studies, effectively simulating patterns and deriving insights from scientific data. They handle the nonlinear and complex nature of SM relationships adeptly and are used in scale transition processes such as gap filling, downscaling, and upscaling, enhancing the spatial completeness and resolution of SM products.

AI and computer vision techniques further advance SM assessment by analyzing visual data from various sensors, including RGB, multispectral, and thermal cameras, with high accuracy and non-invasively (Zhang et al., 2024). Integrating AI and computer vision into SM evaluation supports precision agriculture and daily management practices by enabling site-specific management and optimizing resource use for higher agricultural productivity. These techniques offer deeper insights into water movement within the soil, informing water resource management and ecosystem conservation strategies. AI and computer vision bridge the gap between point measurements and remote sensing, providing essential information on the spatial and temporal variability of SM, crucial for informed decision-making to sustain water resources, ensure food security, and implement ecosystem conservation strategies.

The benefits of integrating AI and computer vision are numerous. AI algorithms can process vast amounts of data from various sensors, improving the accuracy of SM measurements and enabling real-time monitoring. These techniques assess SM without disturbing the soil, preserving ecosystem integrity, and can be applied across large agricultural fields, offering comprehensive SM data that informs irrigation practices and crop management. By automating data collection and analysis, AI and computer vision reduce the need for expensive and labor-intensive traditional methods.
