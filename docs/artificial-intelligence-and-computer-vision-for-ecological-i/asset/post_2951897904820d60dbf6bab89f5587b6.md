## Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming numerous industries, including water quality monitoring. With an emphasis on its potential in environmental monitoring and management of water quality, this section presents the fundamental concepts of artificial intelligence (AI), along with its types, applications, and evolution. Gaining a grasp of these foundational concepts is essential to understanding how artificial intelligence (AI) may solve problems with conventional water quality monitoring techniques.

## What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial intelligence is the simulation of human intelligence in machines with decision-making, learning, and to detected issues, reducing the need for human intervention.

## AI in Water Quality Monitoring: A Paradigm Shift

Artificial Intelligence (AI) provides a variety of methods that can be used in water quality monitoring to improve the capacity to identify, anticipate, and address problems with water quality. This portion discusses particular AI methods and their applications in water quality monitoring systems, such as data analytics, machine learning, and deep learning.

## Machine Learning Techniques

Machine Learning (ML) is a subset of AI that focuses on building systems that can learn from and make decisions based on data. In water quality monitoring, ML techniques analyze large datasets, identify patterns, and predict future water quality trends (Bishop, 2016; Murphy, 2012).

- **Supervised Learning:** This technique involves training an algorithm on a labeled dataset, where the input-output pairs are known. It is useful for predicting specific outcomes, such as contaminant levels in water.
  - **Example:** Predicting the concentration of nitrates in a river based on historical data of agricultural runoff and weather patterns.
- **Unsupervised Learning**: This technique deals with datasets where the output is not labelled. It is used for discovering hidden patterns or grouping data into clusters.
  - **Example**: Clustering different water samples based on their chemical properties to identify sources of pollution.
- **Reinforcement Learning**: This technique involves training algorithms to make a sequence of decisions by rewarding desired behaviors and penalizing undesired ones.
  - **Example**: Optimizing the operation of water treatment plants by continuously adjusting parameters to achieve the best water quality.

## Deep Learning Techniques

Deep Learning (DL) is an advanced form of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers to process complex data (Goodfellow et al., 2016; LeCun et al., 2015). DL is particularly effective in analyzing large volumes of water quality data and identifying intricate patterns that simpler algorithms might miss.

## Data Analytics in Water Quality Monitoring

Data analytics involves processing and analyzing data to extract meaningful insights. In the context of water quality monitoring, data analytics helps in understanding trends, detecting anomalies, and making data-driven decisions (Davenport and Harris, 2007; Provost and Fawcett, 2013).

- **Descriptive Analytics**: Summarizes historical data to understand what has happened over a given period. This is often the first step in data analysis.
  - **Example**: Summarizing annual water quality data to identify periods of high pollution.
- **Predictive Analytics**: Uses historical data to predict future outcomes. It is critical for proactive water quality management.
  - **Example**: Predicting future contamination events based on past pollution patterns and environmental factors.
- **Prescriptive Analytics**: Suggests actions based on predictive analytics to achieve desired outcomes. It helps in decision-making processes.
  - **Example**: Recommending specific interventions to prevent predicted contamination events.
