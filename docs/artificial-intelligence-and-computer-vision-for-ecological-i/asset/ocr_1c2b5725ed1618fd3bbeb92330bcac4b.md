Artificial intelligence in the form of Deep Learning/Machine Learning Technique utilizes algorithms to address problems without explicit programming. This procedure is frequently rapid and more efficient than conventional ones. New algorithms that can precisely forecast the moisture content of soil have been created using machine learning. These algorithms can subsequently be utilized for irrigation or other applications (Ahmad et al. 2010). Using remotely sensed data, machine learning and deep learning algorithms have been successfully deployed to predict soil moisture (Prasad et al., 2018). Using deep learning and machine learning techniques, several vegetation indices and spectral bands along with meteorological, soil, and crop factors could be used as input variables for determining soil moisture (Li et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2021). Artificial neural networks and convolutional neural networks are two examples of machine learning techniques that have been effectively applied for this purpose in recent years (Yinglan et al., 2022). It has been shown that these algorithms outperform conventional rule-based methods (Song et al., 2016). High-performance soil moisture sensors that are resistant to weather fluctuations can be created using this method. Farmers will be able to save money on irrigation water, which is a valuable resource in dry areas (Cai et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2019; Sarwar et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2022).

These days, technology is a huge part in so many facets of our life. Agriculture might seem to be exempt from that, as all it involves is planting seeds and watching them grow, but that is definitely not the case. Farming is not an easy task. The population’s exponential increase presents extremely dynamic challenges to maintaining food supplies. To feed more people, food must be produced in greater quantities. However, an increase in population also means that more buildings and water use will occur, depleting the precious land and water resources required for food production. With fewer resources, the agriculture sector must rely on technical advancements to feed this expanding population. Technological advancements like Genetic engineering, GPS, aerial imaging, herbicide tolerance, robotics, soil sensors, and precision agriculture are just a few examples that have transformed farming to a high-level industry. Both irrigation fields and gardens for plants depend heavily on the soil’s moisture content. Over the past ten years, many farmers have found that implementing remote sensors to monitor a field’s soil moisture content is an essential management tool. By using precision approaches, it helps to minimize fertilizer runoff from over-saturation, irrigate crops precisely when they need it, and lower input costs.
