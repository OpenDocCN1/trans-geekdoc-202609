# Identification of Plant Species

Different plant species can also be identified by integrating remote sensing technology with machine learning and deep learning techniques. A team of researchers employed a convolutional neural network (CNN), a deep learning algorithm, to identify six different plants based on their morphological characteristics (Chouhan et al., 2024).

# Challenges and Future Direction

Remote sensing technologies and data-driven approaches have emerged as powerful tools in modern agriculture, offering unprecedented insights into crop health, soil conditions, and environmental factors. However, their implementation faces several challenges and technological constraints that need to be addressed for their widespread adoption and effective utilization.

Based on research conducted by The World Bank, it has been identified that around 65% of individuals working in agriculture are situated below the poverty threshold, equating to less than US $3.10/day (Castaneda et al., 2016). This creates a significant barrier for many smallholder farmers to embrace advanced technologies like satellite technologies, UAVs for precision agriculture on their farms, as the considerable cost disparity between one agricultural UAV and its financial benefits was unfeasible for them to afford such technology (Fernández et al., 2019). For small farm owners in places like India and Africa, even if they could buy such a device, they would still encounter problems with costs because the services and working costs for these instruments are already quite costly. Additionally, research indicates that previously utilized PA techniques like mapping of yield; GPS; and variable rate mechanisms had minimal influence on total returns. Yield mapping led to approximately a 2.5% increase, GPS guidance systems to a 2% increase, and variable-rate systems to a 1.1% increase in yield (Schimmelpfennig, 2016). Therefore, for many farmers, particularly those who own smaller-scale farms, it may be challenging to justify investing a substantial amount of time in setting up the initial capital required for these technologies. Bridging this technological gap requires innovative pricing models, capacity-building initiatives, and government support to make remote sensing technologies more affordable and user-friendly for farmers worldwide.

The next strong struggle is the complexity and variability of agricultural landscapes. Different regions exhibit diverse topographies, soil types, and crop varieties, making it challenging to develop universal remote sensing models that accurately capture agricultural dynamics across various environments (Hosseini et al., 2020).
