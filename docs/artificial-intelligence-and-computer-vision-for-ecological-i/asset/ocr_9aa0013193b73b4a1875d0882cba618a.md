# Case Studies and Applications

# Bearing an Eye on Coastal Water Algal Blooms

The monitoring of harmful algal blooms (HABs) in coastal waters is one of the most important uses of AI and computer vision (CV) technology in the assessment of water quality. Because HABs can create poisons that contaminate seafood and drinking water, they pose serious dangers to human health, the local economy, and marine ecosystems. Using AI models, in situ sensor data and satellite pictures were combined in a case study throughout the Gulf of Mexico to identify and forecast HAB occurrences. High-resolution satellite photos were analyzed using deep learning methods, namely convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to look for indicators of algal blooms, such as changes in water color and chlorophyll concentrations. The AI models were able to recognize the temporal and spatial patterns connected to HAB occurrences since they had been trained on historical bloom data. Early warning capabilities offered by this strategy made it possible to promptly issue public health alerts and take mitigation measures. The accomplishment of this case study demonstrates how AI and CV technologies can improve coastal water quality monitoring and management, protecting both human communities and marine life.

# Machine Learning for River Pollution Prediction

Machine learning models were created to forecast pollution levels based on different environmental and anthropogenic parameters in a case study that focused on the Ganges River in India. One of the world's most polluted rivers, the Ganges, is hampered by home sewage, agricultural runoff, and industrial discharge. Machine learning models were trained using data from many sources, such as weather stations, remote sensing, and manual water sampling. The data was analyzed using supervised learning techniques, such as support vector machines (SVM) and random forests, to forecast the quantities of important contaminants, such as heavy metals, nitrates, and biological oxygen demand (BOD). Significant indicators of pollution levels, including population density, industrial activity, and rainfall patterns, were identified by the models. The identification of important pollution hotspots and the streamlining of remediation activities were made possible by these predictive capabilities, which also made it possible to implement more effective pollution management techniques. This case study's incorporation of machine learning showed how it may improve the efficiency and predicted accuracy of water quality tests in vast, intricate river systems.
