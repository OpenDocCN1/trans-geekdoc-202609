## Chapter 20. Sustainable Agriculture: Economic Perspectives on AI and ML in Crop Yield Estimation

*Harshit Mishra* and Aditya Bhooshan Srivastava

### Introduction

Crop yield estimation has always been a critical aspect of agricultural economics, serving as a fundamental predictor of food supply, market trends, and resource allocation. Traditional approaches, reliant on historical data and empirical observations, have provided valuable insights but are increasingly inadequate in the face of modern challenges. The need for more accurate, efficient, and timely estimations has driven the incorporation of innovative technologies such as AI and ML into agricultural practices (Vuppalapati, 2021). These technologies offer transformative potential in improving the economic sustainability of agriculture by enhancing crop yield predictions, thereby contributing to global food security and economic stability. The economic framework of sustainable agriculture is grounded in principles that balance profitability with environmental stewardship and social equity. Sustainable agriculture economics emphasizes the long-term viability of farming practices that protect natural resources and support rural communities (Thamilselvan et al., 2021; Chouhan et al., 2021). The introduction of AI and ML into this framework marks a significant shift towards precision agriculture, where data-driven decisions optimize resource use, reduce waste, and improve crop management. By leveraging vast quantities of data, AI and ML can identify patterns and predict outcomes with unprecedented accuracy, thus enhancing economic sustainability (Araújo et al., 2023).

There are inherent limits to traditional agricultural yield estimation techniques like visual inspections, farmer reports, and historical yield data. These techniques are frequently labour-intensive, time-consuming, and prone to prejudice and human error. Moreover, they have difficulty taking into consideration dynamic aspects that might change quickly and affect crop results, such as pest infestations, weather, and soil health. Traditional methods are static, which emphasizes the necessity for technical developments that can produce accurate, adaptive, and real-time estimations. Thanks to their capacity to analyze massive information from a variety of sources, such as satellite imaging, weather forecasts, and soil sensors, AI and ML bring about a paradigm shift in crop yield estimation. These tools use complex algorithms to find patterns and small correlations that might go unnoticed by the human eye. As an example, a study showing that ML algorithms could forecast wheat yields with up to 90% accuracy showed how well machine learning models handle past and real-time data to predict crop productivity. This level of accuracy facilitates more effective planning and resource allocation, which eventually improves economic results (Singh et al., 2022).

The economic implications of adopting AI and ML in agriculture are substantial. A cost-benefit analysis reveals that the initial investment in these technologies can be offset by significant returns, primarily through increased crop productivity, reduced input costs, and optimized resource use. For example, growers using AI-driven precision agriculture techniques have reported up to 30% savings on fertilizer and water usage. Additionally, the ROI for AI and ML technologies in agriculture can be substantial, with studies indicating potential ROI ranges from 2:1 to 5:1, depending on the scale and type of agricultural operation (Sachithra and Subhashini, 2023). Further supporting economic viability are the advantages of increased productivity and resource management. Data-driven perceptions enable targeted water and fertilizer applications, saving waste and having a positive environmental impact. AI-based early stress factors and disease outbreak identification can result in pre-emptive treatments that lower crop losses and improve yield quality. These developments help create a more sustainable agricultural system in addition to saving farmers money (Mishra, 2022).

Another significant benefit of combining AI and ML is increased crop yields and market prospects. Farmers may make well-informed decisions that are in line with market demands and meteorological conditions when they have improved predictability and risk management. Increased predictability facilitates market positioning and strategic planning, which may boost agricultural income and resistance to shocks. To avoid potential production losses and ensure market supply, farmers can benefit from the timely implementation of irrigation solutions by using AI-driven models that anticipate crop stress caused by drought. Crop management techniques, soil properties, pest and disease control, and climate variables are only a few of the many variables that affect crop yield estimation. AI and ML models excel at analyzing these complex and interrelated elements to create precise yield forecasts. High-quality data collection and preprocessing are crucial for the effectiveness of these models, ensuring that the inputs are reliable and comprehensive. Despite the promising advantages, challenges and limitations persist in the implementation of AI and ML in agriculture. Concerns such as data quality, model complexity, and the interpretability of AI decisions pose significant hurdles. Additionally, adoption barriers in agricultural communities, including lack of access to technology and digital literacy, must be addressed (AlZubi and Galyana, 2023).

Ethical and societal considerations, such as equity in access to AI and ML technologies and environmental impact assessment, also perform a critical role in shaping the future of AI in agriculture. Policy implications are vital to support the widespread adoption of AI and ML in sustainable agriculture. Government support through subsidies, incentives, and public-private partnerships can facilitate infrastructure development and research. Regulatory guidelines for data privacy and security are essential to protect farmers’ interests and promote trust in these technologies (Chouhan et al., 2024). The future of AI and ML in sustainable agriculture is promising, with emergent tools poised to further revolutionize the sector. Integration with robotics and the IoT, advanced AI algorithms for real-time decision-making, and AI-powered tools for personalized farm management are on the horizon. Addressing sustainability challenges through AI-driven optimization can help combat climate change and resource scarcity, promoting a circular economy in agriculture (Zhang et al., 2021).

The economic perspectives on AI and ML in crop yield estimation highlight a transformative potential that, if harnessed efficiently, can lead to a more sustainable and prosperous agricultural future. Considering the aforementioned factors, this chapter focuses on addressing the following objectives: to examine the economic principles and sustainability frameworks underpinning modern agricultural practices, and providing a foundational understanding of how these principles guide sustainable agriculture (Singh and Mishra, 2023). Additionally, the chapter aims to analyze the transformative influence of AI and ML on the economic sustainability of crop yield estimation, highlighting how these technologies can revolutionize traditional methods. Furthermore, it seeks to evaluate the cost-benefit and ROI of adopting AI and ML technologies in agriculture, offering insights into the financial viability and long-term benefits of these innovations. Lastly, the chapter will identify the economic benefits and potential challenges of implementing AI and ML in improving crop yields and farm incomes, ensuring a comprehensive understanding of both the opportunities and obstacles faced by farmers in the agricultural sector (Saiz-Rubio and Rovira-Mas, 2020).
