NPV = ∑_{t=1}^{n} CF_t / (1 + r)^t − Initial Investment (1)

where:

+   - NPV = Net Present Value
+   - CF_t = Cash flow at time t
+   - r = Discount rate
+   - n = Number of periods
+   - “Initial Investment” refers to the initial outlay or investment required for the project.

Each cash flow (both costs and benefits) is discounted to its present value using the formula:

PV = CF_t / (1 + r)^t (2)

where:

+   - PV = Present value
+   - CF = Cash flow at time t
+   - r = Discount rate
+   - t = Time period

Then, the present values of all cash flows are summed up, and the initial investment is subtracted from the total to obtain the NPV.

# Methodology:

+   - Identification of Costs and Benefits: The first stage is to list and classify all pertinent expenses and advantages related to the use of remote sensing applications in agriculture. Costs might include opportunity costs, operational costs (like maintenance and training), and initial investment costs (like software development and equipment acquisition). Conversely, advantages include increased crop output, more effective use of resources, lower input costs (fertilizers, water, etc.), and possible income increases.
+   - Time Frame Selection: The time horizon for the cost-benefit analysis should be carefully chosen to capture both the short-term and long-term effects of remote sensing technologies. Typically, a multi-year time frame is preferred to encompass the lifecycle of the technology and its impact on agricultural productivity and profitability.
+   - Discount Rate Determination: Because it takes the time worth of money into account and represents the opportunity cost of capital, choosing the right discount rate is essential. Several variables, including the rate of inflation, the risk profile, and the current interest rates in the market, might affect the discount rate.
