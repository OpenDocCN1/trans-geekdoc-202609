# Types of Agro-based Waste

Agro-based wastes may be divided into three broad categories, namely crop residues, animal manures and agrochemical residues.

+   1) Crop residues: Crop residues include the stalks, leaves, husks, and roots left in the field after the harvest of crops like wheat, rice, maize, and sugarcane. According to Sharma, 2020a, farmers use 20% of rice straw and burn the other 50% for crop preparation. The availability of these residues can be substantial, for instance, rice straw constitutes about 50% of the total biomass of the rice plant (VanHung, 2020). Improper management of crop residues, such as open burning, leads to severe air pollution, contributing to greenhouse gas emissions and particulate matter. Conversely, leaving residues on fields can improve soil organic matter and moisture retention, reducing soil erosion and improving fertility. However, through the use of AI algorithms and real-time data, innovative approaches can be developed to effectively utilize agro-based waste and crop residues, contributing to sustainable agricultural practices and establishing food security in the face of evolving environmental conditions and growing food demand (Ziesche, 2023).

+   2) Animal/livestock excreta: Animal excreta includes the faeces and urine or other bodily waste products from livestock, such as cows, pigs, and chickens. It is rich in nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, making it a valuable fertilizer. Bashir and Mohan (2023) demonstrated that sound manure management as a fertiliser can boost environmentally friendly farming and agricultural productivity. Moreover, it can be processed into biochar, a recognized method for sustainable land reclamation and enhancing plant growth on degraded soils.
