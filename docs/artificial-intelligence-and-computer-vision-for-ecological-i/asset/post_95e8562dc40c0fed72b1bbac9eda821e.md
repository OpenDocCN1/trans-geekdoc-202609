### Introduction
The circular economy strives to reduce waste and resource use efficiently, transforming from the traditional linear model of production that follows a ‘take-make-dispose’ approach (Edirisinghe, 2024). The circular economy represents a shift towards a more sustainable approach. In contrast, the circular economy is characterized by its restorative and regenerative design, aiming to close the loops of product lifecycles through increased resource efficiency and sustainability (Kirchherr, 2017).

The existing linear economic model is unsustainable, promoting the unjust use of finite natural resources and generating excessive waste, which severely harms the environment. In contrast, a circular economy (CE) offers a restorative, sustainable, and regenerative alternative and is gaining global traction. Among all the existing digital technologies, artificial intelligence (AI) is a key promoter for CE, significantly facilitating its adoption and implementation in practical applications. This chapter explores the intersection of AI and CE and discusses policies for implementing CE principles using AI. ### Key Principles and Strategies: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (3R)
The principles of reduce, reuse, and recycle (3R) are foundational to the circular economy, aiming to produce a sustainable system where resources are employed efficiently and waste is reduced significantly.

The principle of reducing involves minimizing the number of resources used and waste generated in the first place. For example; Companies reduce packaging materials to decrease waste; and use energy-efficient appliances and machinery. The principle of reusing involves using items more than once, either for the same purpose or for a different function, to extend their lifecycle. For example, reusable shopping bags, water bottles, and containers; converting and refurbishing and reselling used electronics and the principle of recycling involves the processing of used materials into new products to prevent the waste of potentially usable materials. For example, recycling paper products into new paper items; and converting used plastics into new plastic products or alternative materials.
