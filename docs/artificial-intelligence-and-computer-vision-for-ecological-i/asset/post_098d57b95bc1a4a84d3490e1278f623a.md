## Decision Tree (DT)

The Decision Tree (DT) is an uncomplicated, fundamental method that bases judgments on pertinent input variable values. After utilizing entropy to determine the root parameter, the weights of the other factors are assessed. DT bases its judgments on a range of values derived from certain qualities, arranging all variable selections into an orderly framework. Research has demonstrated that decision tree models function effectively with imbalanced data. Nonetheless, group techniques for instance, Gradient Boosting (GB) and Random Forest (RF) consistently beat individual decision trees (Malek et al., 2022). Decision-tree-based models have the advantages of being very efficient, robust in lieu of missing values, and competent to handle both standard attributes along with data. When it comes to short-term forecasting, decision-tree-based techniques are more appropriate than other machine learning algorithms and frequently provide faster computation speed (Lu and Ma, 2020).

## Biochemical Oxygen Demand Prediction

Aquatic organic contamination can be detected using the commonly used biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) test. It calculates how much oxygen aerobic bacteria need in the process of breaking down organic matter in a water sample over a given length of time (33). The United Kingdom’s Royal Commission on Sewage Disposal first suggested the current technique for calculating biological oxygen demand, or BOD5, in 1912. (United States Geological Survey (USGS)) BOD5 calculates the amount of oxygen used per litre of water during five days of incubation at 20°C (Zaitsev et al., 2018). The most important factors influencing the prediction accuracy among the 17 water quality measures were determined to be temperature, pH, BOD, and phosphorus content (Siljic Tomic et al., 2018). By using information from past BOD5 readings, machine learning models can predict the BOD5 levels with greater accuracy (Ooi et al., 2022). The amount of biological oxygen demand (B.O.D.) in the Johor River in Malaysia was predicted using a support vector Regression and an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) to anticipate BOD values for traditional water reclamation plants (WRPs) (Zhu et al., 2018). Ma et al. (2020) utilized a combination of Deep Matrix Factorization and Deep Neural Networks to effectively predict BOD values. The Indian River Narmada’s water quality was estimated using a decision tree model that predicted six water quality parameters: pH, temperature, ammonia-nitrogen (NH₃-N), nitrate-nitrogen (NO₃-N), BOD (biochemical oxygen demand), and COD (chemical oxygen demand) (Jaloree et al., 2014). The study by (Cipullo et al., 2019) used the random forest (RF) approach to estimate the bioavailable chemical concentration in soil sample tests with superior prediction accuracy. Numerous research has also presented other machine learning techniques, such as random forest regression (RFR), M5 trees, and boosted regression trees (BRT) (Yuchi et al., 2019). More sophisticated activation functions, which enable greater model convergence than the sigmoid function usually employed in conventional ANNs, are credited with this improvement, as well as the numerous layers amid the input and output in DNNs, which simplify the training process (Schmidhuber, 2015).

## Dissolved Oxygen Prediction

An essential quality of surface water indicator is dissolved oxygen (DO), it symbolizes the overall condition of the aquatic environment and its capacity to support aquatic life. Heat, salinity, and air pressure are some of the variables that affect dissolved oxygen, which is a representation of oxygen molecules in the aqueous environment (Zaitsev et al., 2018). Fish and other aquatic life in water bodies depend on D.O. The wind and aerating action reflect the water’s dissolved oxygen content. The stability of aquatic creatures and fish life depends on the D.O. level, which needs to be maintained within a certain range. The higher the D.O. level, the better the conditions for aquatic life and fish survival. D.O. is utilized as an indicator to show the condition of any aquatic system, and while it is in the water column, it is vital to microorganisms (Chen and Liu, 2014). A linear polynomial neural network (PNN) model was utilized to predict the concentrations of DO in the Danube River (Siljic Tomic et al., 2018). A different group of researchers (Solanki et al., 2015) presented a deep learning network model that outperformed supervised learning-based techniques in terms of accuracy and performance when it came to predicting the pH and dissolved oxygen contents of water. The viability of employing machine learning to forecast dissolved oxygen levels has been shown by the successful implementation of earlier scenarios. ANNs (such as BPNN, MLP, and FENN) have been successfully used among other computational approaches for DO prediction, forecasting, modeling, and estimating (Wen et al., 2013). While it is possible to estimate dissolved oxygen using machine learning, gathering data on water quality in difficult-to-reach locations is still a challenge.

## Water Salinity Prediction

Predicting climatic fluctuations and comprehending ocean dynamics depend on precise estimation of the ocean subsurface salinity (Qi et al., 2024). Salinity has a major effect on the physical, chemical, and biological activities that take place in the ocean. Estuaries are critical for material exchange between land and sea, with salinity indicating freshwater mixing. Influenced by river inputs, tides, and weather, salinity helps assess water quality and ecosystem health in estuaries (Guillou et al., 2023). The distribution of salinity is essential for the development and reproduction of marine life. Seawater’s density is strongly impacted by its salt concentration, which in turn affects circulation and stratification (Schmidt et al., 2018). An efficient machine learning and regression model was developed to estimate future salt levels based on past data and predict the salinity of water quality (Chawla et al., 2023). Support Vector Regression delivered the most accurate surface salinity estimates, particularly enhancing projections derived from complex numerical models in low-salinity periods. This demonstrates the potential benefits of applying algorithms for machine learning alongside traditional physical models (Guillou et al., 2023).

## Water Turbidity Prediction

The quantity of light scattered by suspended particles in water, often referred to as suspensions, is measured as turbidity, which may be operationally defined as the percentage that can be eliminated by a 0.22 μm (or 0.45 μm) pore size filter (Kirk, 1985). Turbidity in water affects aquatic life, and measuring pollution levels requires a precise understanding of it. An algorithm based on neural networks is being used in the marine environment to track turbidity (Kumar et al., 2022). Wang et al., 2021 showed that using an artificial neural network with tide levels as inputs and NTU readings as outputs accurately predicts turbidity. Key data includes tide levels from the two cycles before the forecast period (Wang et al., 2021). High-turbidity zones were successfully predicted by a machine learning technique utilizing meteorological and satellite TSS data, with random forest regression producing the best results. The region of elevated turbidity from the day before, the wind, and the tide level were important predictions; rainfall had little bearing (Nagayama and Tonooka, 2023). However, forecasting turbidity is still a topic for future research since the causes of turbidity occurrence and dispersion might differ.
