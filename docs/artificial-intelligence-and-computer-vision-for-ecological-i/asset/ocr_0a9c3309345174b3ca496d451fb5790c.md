# Computer Vision (CV) Technologies in Environmental Monitoring

# Understanding Computer Vision and Image Processing

The artificial intelligence discipline of CV makes it possible for computers to analyze and comprehend visual data from their environment. Fundamentally, CV entails the retrieval of significant data from pictures and videos to carry out operations that would typically need human vision. A key element of CV is image processing, which includes a range of methods for improving, interpreting, and modifying images (Liu et al., 2024). Filtering, segmentation, feature extraction, and pattern recognition are some of these approaches that together enable the conversion of unprocessed visual data into meaningful insights.

Image processing begins with the acquisition of images through cameras, satellites, drones, or other sensors. These images often undergo preprocessing steps, such as noise reduction, contrast enhancement, and normalization, to improve their quality and suitability for further analysis. Segmentation divides an image into meaningful regions or objects, making it easier to analyze specific features (Valipoor and de Antonio, 2023). Feature extraction identifies relevant attributes, such as edges, textures, or shapes, that can be used for classification or recognition tasks. Pattern recognition, often powered by machine learning algorithms, enables the identification and categorization of objects or phenomena within the images.

Deep learning has transformed CV by dramatically increasing the precision and effectiveness of image analysis, especially when convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are employed. CNNs are very good at tasks like object identification, picture classification, and semantic segmentation because of their ability to automatically and adaptively learn spatial hierarchies of characteristics from images. These abilities have wide-ranging applications in many fields, including as autonomous driving, environmental monitoring, and medical imaging (Liu et al., 2024) (Liu et al., 2023).

# Role of CV in Water Quality Assessment

The use of deep learning has transformed CV by dramatically increasing the precision and effectiveness of image analysis, especially when convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are employed. CNNs are very good at tasks like object identification, picture classification, and semantic segmentation because of their ability to automatically and adaptively learn spatial hierarchies of characteristics from images. These abilities have wide-ranging applications in several fields, including as autonomous driving, environmental monitoring, and medical imaging (Rawat et al., 2023).

The identification and tracking of turbidity is one of CV’s main functions in the evaluation of water quality. Elevated levels of turbidity, frequently resulting from suspended particulates including silt, algae, and microbes, can negatively impact aquatic ecosystems by diminishing light penetration and interfering with photosynthesis. To precisely measure turbidity levels, CV algorithms examine how light is scattered and absorbed in photographs of water (Adelani et al., 2024).
