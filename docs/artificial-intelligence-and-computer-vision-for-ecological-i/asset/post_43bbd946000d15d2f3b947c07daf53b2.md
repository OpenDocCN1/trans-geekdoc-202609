
## Preface

The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Computer Vision has revolutionized the way we perceive and analyze the natural world. In the field of Ecological Informatics, these technologies offer groundbreaking opportunities to monitor, model, and understand ecosystems with unprecedented accuracy and efficiency. This book, Artificial Intelligence and Computer Vision for Ecological Informatics, explores the synergy between AI-driven computational techniques and ecological data analysis, bridging the gap between technology and environmental science.

In recent years, the increasing availability of remote sensing data, camera trap images, and automated monitoring systems has created vast amounts of ecological information. Traditional methods of analysis, while effective, often struggle to keep pace with the scale and complexity of modern ecological datasets. AI and Computer Vision provide powerful solutions, enabling automated species identification, habitat classification, environmental monitoring, and predictive modeling. These techniques not only enhance research capabilities but also support conservation efforts by providing real-time insights into ecosystem dynamics.

This book is structured to provide both theoretical foundations and practical applications of AI and Computer Vision in ecological research. It is intended for researchers, students, and professionals in fields such as ecology, environmental science, computer science, and data analytics who seek to harness the power of AI in ecological studies. Readers will find discussions on key AI methodologies, case studies demonstrating real-world applications, and insights into the future of ecological informatics in an increasingly data-driven world.

By integrating AI and Computer Vision into ecological research, we take a crucial step toward a deeper understanding of our environment, empowering scientists and conservationists to make informed decisions for sustainable management and biodiversity conservation. We hope this book serves as a valuable resource and inspires further innovation in this exciting interdisciplinary domain.

### Introduction

Various factors are responsible for distorting agricultural production. Factors such as climatic shifts, limited or nonexistent arable land, water scarcity, plant diseases, population growth, industrialization, and many more have occurred in the last few years. These biotic or abiotic components not only disrupt the quality and quantity of an agricultural product but also deteriorate the economic growth of farmers as well as the nation (Radoglou-Grammatikis et al., 2020; Tahir et al., 2016; Li et al., 2021).

### Need for Drones

Drones have been widely espoused in Precision Agriculture (PA). They are capable of offering solutions to many problems that a farmer experiences on and off the field. These applications and their valuable impact have been extensively observed in the past few years. Because of this, productivity, efficiency, and profitability in the agriculture sector have been boosted. Drones in agribusiness save time, help troubleshoot potential problems and offer better data and cost-saving solutions. Farmers, ranchers, and other agriculture business owners have already started to adopt technological aspects for their assistance (van Wynsberghe and Comes, 2020; Boukoberine et al., 2019; Hassanalian and Abdelkefi, 2017; Jerhamre et al., 2022). The information collected using drones is analyzed with the help of experts, and effective solutions are provided to the farmers. These solutions are provided in less time and at a lower cost, therefore enhancing the quality and quantity of yields. The data collected using drones is in the form of high-resolution images. Depending on the necessity of the information needed, the images collected can be RGB, multispectral, and hyperspectral.

From the perspective of ecological conditions, drones are very important. They can fly and gather data even from terrestrial regions that are far beyond the reach of human beings. Some of these applications include the identification of historical wetland regions (Stahl and Weimann, 2022), mapping of dryland savannahs (Amputu et al., 2023), detection of amphibian species (Senturk, 2022), classification of desert grassland species using hyperspectral images captured with the help of drones (Zhang, 2022), identification of maize leaf diseases with the help of aerial images (Ishengoma et al., 2022), extraction of soybean planting area using drone images (Yang et al., 2022), plant species monitoring, mapping, and quantifying with the help of drone images (Baena, 2018), columnar cactus recognition (López-Jiménez et al., 2019), among others.

### Applications of Drones

#### Crop Mapping and Surveying

An effective place to start is with drones; they are excellent for monitoring and sensing, and they can swiftly cover a region to examine crop development and soil health. Drones are typically utilized for this task because their sensors can identify the absorbance of a particular wavelength, producing a color contrast image that highlights potentially problematic areas (Prado Osco et al., 2021; Khan et al., 2020; Corcoran et al., 2021).

#### Seeding and Planting

Due to the high level of skill required to carry out its operations, agriculture is by definition a labor-intensive and time-consuming sector. Particularly seeding, which is a time-consuming operation, requires human labor. To make this strenuous task easier, seeds of several crop varieties are sown using drone technology. Drones can plant seeds quickly and efficiently thanks to their built-in lasers, sensors, tanks, and other characteristics. Seed planting is one of the more recent and less popular uses of drones in agriculture. Currently, automated drone seeders are largely utilized in the forestry sector, but a broader application may be applied in the future. Drone planting makes it possible to replant inaccessible locations without putting workers in danger. With a crew of two operators and 10 drones that can plant 400,000 trees every day, they are also able to plant considerably more effectively (Yang et al., 2022).
