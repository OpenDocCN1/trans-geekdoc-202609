# Introduction

India, a country with an extensive record of agriculture and a wide variety of farming landscapes, is always faced with the challenge of providing food security for its ever-expanding population. By 2030, the World Bank estimates that there will be 1.5 billion people living in India. In addition to boosting overall food production, feeding this expanding population will need effective management of the agricultural resources that are in existence. Navigating this difficulty requires accurate crop yield estimation (Yu, 2021). The amount of harvested produce, such as grains, per unit of land area is referred to as crop yield. Historically, India has assessed crop yields using self-reported data from farmers or manually conducted field surveys, as indicated in Fig. 1. Government organizations (Zhang, 2022). Although these approaches are useful, they frequently fall short of the timeliness and accuracy required to make good decisions.
