# Energy Interactions

As EM waves interact with Earth’s terrain, they may be absorbed, pass through, or bounce back. The degree of energy retention or reflection varies based on the characteristics of the surface material encountered (Victor et al., 2024).

# Spatial Resolution

This refers to the level of detail in an image. Higher spatial resolution means more detail can be seen in the image. Spatial resolution is determined by the sensor’s capabilities and the altitude of the platform (Karnawat et al., 2020).

# Temporal Resolution

Temporal resolution refers to how often a sensor can revisit the same area to collect data. Higher temporal resolution allows for more frequent monitoring of changes over time (Okengwu et al., 2023).

# Spectral Resolution

Spectral precision denotes a detector’s capacity to differentiate various EM wave frequencies (Ullo and Sinha, 2021). More spectrally sensitive instruments can extract finer details about the makeup and state of observed features or regions (Shanmugapriya et al., 2019).

# Radiometric Resolution

Radiometric resolution refers to the sensitivity of a sensor to detect variations in radiation intensity. Higher radiometric resolution allows for more subtle differences in the reflectance or emission of energy to be detected (Jiang et al., 2022).
