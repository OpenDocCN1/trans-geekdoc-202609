# Challenges and Limitations

The incorporation of Artificial Intelligence in plant growth analysis poses substantial challenges and limitations that must be overcome to ensure successful implementation. Data quality and quantity problems occur because there aren’t enough big and good-quality datasets needed to train strong models, and the process of labeling data is both time-consuming and expensive. The diversity of agricultural data, encompassing different types and formats, poses challenges when attempting to integrate them into comprehensive models. Advanced Artificial Intelligence models, especially deep learning networks, are often intricate and demand significant computational power, which can result in overfitting and limited applicability. Furthermore, these models serve as “black boxes,” making it challenging to understand their decision-making processes, which is essential for building trust among farmers and stakeholders (Kumar et al., 2024). The challenges of scalability and deployment arise from the need for substantial infrastructure, including high-performance computing and dependable internet connectivity, which are often scarce in rural regions (Pérez-López et al., 2007). The expense of implementation can be prohibitive, restricting accessibility for small-scale farmers. Broader systemic issues encompass ethical and privacy concerns related to data ownership, the ever-changing regulatory and policy landscape, and the environmental consequences of deploying Artificial Intelligence infrastructure. Overcoming these challenges necessitates a collaborative effort among Artificial Intelligence researchers, agricultural scientists, policymakers, and farmers to create sustainable, efficient, and trustworthy Artificial Intelligence solutions in the field of agriculture.
