The shortcomings of individual models can be mitigated by ensemble approaches by using the benefits of several models, producing yield projections that are more accurate and dependable (Chouhan et al., 2024).

Ensemble approaches are typically classified into two types: bagging and boosting. Bagging, also known as Bootstrap Aggregation, is the process of training many models independently on distinct subsets of data and then averaging their predictions. This strategy decreases variance and helps to avoid overfitting. Boosting develops models consecutively, with each new model attempting to remedy the flaws of its predecessor. This approach focuses on improving model accuracy by reducing bias.

In crop yield estimation, ensemble methods can incorporate various data inputs such as weather conditions, soil properties, satellite imagery, and historical yield data. By combining these diverse data sources, ensemble models can capture the complex, non-linear relationships that influence crop yields. For instance, a Random Forest, which is a bagging method, can be used to average predictions from multiple decision trees, each trained on a different subset of data, thereby enhancing the model’s generalizability and robustness (Mishra et al., 2024).

**Example:** The application of ensemble methods in crop yield estimation, consider a case study where we aim to estimate the yield of a specific crop using three different models: a Decision Tree, a Linear Regression, and a Support Vector Machine (SVM). We will use bagging to create an ensemble model.

Steps:

+   1. *Data Preparation:*
+      - Collect data on crop yields along with relevant features such as temperature, rainfall, soil pH, and historical yield data.
+   2. *Model Training:*
+      - Train a Decision Tree, a Linear Regression, and a Support Vector Machine on the dataset.
+   3. *Ensemble Creation:*
+      - Create multiple subsets of the training data using bootstrapping (random sampling with replacement).
+      - Train each of the three models on these subsets to create an ensemble of models.
+   4. *Prediction and Aggregation:*
+      - Each model in the ensemble predicts the test data.
+      - Aggregate the predictions by averaging them to obtain the final yield estimate.
