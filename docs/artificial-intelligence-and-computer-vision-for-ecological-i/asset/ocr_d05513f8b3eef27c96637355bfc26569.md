For applications like crop classification, disease detection, and yield prediction, researchers can create reliable models by utilizing AI techniques like deep learning, CNNs, and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). These models provide comprehensive evaluation of crop health and climatic conditions by learning intricate patterns and connections from a variety of data sources, including as satellite photography, UAV footage, and ground-based sensors (Singh and Kaur, 2022).

Moreover, the integration of computer vision algorithms enables automated feature extraction and object detection from remote sensing imagery. This capability facilitates the identification of crop types, pest infestations, and other critical factors influencing agricultural productivity. Furthermore, advancements in edge computing and onboard processing enable real-time analysis of remote sensing data, empowering farmers with timely insights for proactive decision-making in the field (Xu et al., 2020). Nevertheless, the widespread adoption of AI and computer vision technologies in agriculture faces challenges related to data quality, model interpretability, and algorithm scalability. Addressing these challenges will require ongoing research efforts to improve algorithm robustness, enhance data integration pipelines, and ensure the ethical and responsible use of AI in agricultural decision support systems (Veerachamy and Ramar, 2022; Dhanaraju et al., 2022).

# Addressing Scalability and Data Accessibility Issues

Scalability of data collection and analysis workflows is one of the main issues with remote sensing for sustainable agriculture. Scalable solutions for data collection, processing, and distribution are required as agricultural landscapes continue to grow and diversity and as there is a growing need for thorough and timely information. Researchers need to look for new ways to optimize resource allocation and streamline data collection workflows to overcome scaling issues. This could entail creating automated data gathering technologies that can effectively cover vast agricultural areas with little assistance from humans, like satellite constellations or UAV swarms. Furthermore, advancements in cloud computing and distributed processing architectures can facilitate parallelized analysis of large-scale remote sensing datasets, enabling rapid generation of actionable insights at scale (Chatterjee et al., 2020).

In addition to scalability concerns, ensuring data accessibility and interoperability is crucial for maximizing the impact of remote sensing technologies in agriculture. Open data initiatives, data sharing agreements, and standardized data formats play a pivotal role in promoting collaboration and knowledge exchange among stakeholders (Mishra, 2024). However, achieving widespread data accessibility and interoperability requires concerted efforts to overcome technical, institutional, and cultural barriers. This necessitates the development of data governance frameworks, data management protocols, and capacity-building initiatives to promote data literacy and empower stakeholders with the necessary skills and resources to harness the full potential of remote sensing technologies for sustainable agriculture (McLennon et al., 2021).
