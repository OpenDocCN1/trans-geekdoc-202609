
Procedures and open data platforms make it easier for information to be shared, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of environmental challenges. When dealing with intricate, multifaceted issues like water contamination, which frequently have multiple sources and channels, a coordinated approach is crucial (L. G. Jr et al., 2021). Ecological informatics makes it possible for us to work together across disciplines and break down silos to better monitor, manage, and safeguard our water resources. To summarize, the significance of ecological informatics in environmental monitoring is immense; it offers the technological framework and analytical instruments required to progress our comprehension and management of aquatic environments.

## Role of AI and Computer Vision (CV) Technologies

Water quality evaluation is being revolutionized by Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Computer Vision (CV) technologies, which offer novel approaches for accurate analysis and real-time monitoring. With unmatched accuracy and speed, AI can handle and analyze enormous volumes of data about water quality through its many branches, including machine learning and deep learning (Khang et al., 2024). Traditional monitoring methods may miss minor patterns and anomalies that point to pollution or changes in water quality, but these technologies can identify them. Predictive analytics is made possible by the ability of machine learning algorithms, for instance, to identify trends in data sets about chemical concentrations, temperature variations, or biological markers (Ward et al., 2021). This enables the prediction of possible environmental problems before they become serious, an approach towards the application of AI is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Environmental monitoring greatly benefits from the application of computer vision, a branch of AI that focuses on giving computers the ability to read and comprehend visual data. With the use of CV technology, water quality factors including turbidity, color changes, and the presence of floating trash or algae blooms can be evaluated by analyzing photos and videos taken by satellites, drones, and underwater cameras (Ma et al., 2023) (O’Mahony et al., 2020). For large-scale and remote water bodies where traditional sampling is logistically difficult, this capability is vital. For example, CV-analyzed satellite images can track the evolution of algal blooms over time, offering vital information for water resource management and eutrophication mitigation.

CV decreases the need for manual inspection and expedites response times by automating the detection and measurement of these visual cues.

The creation of automated monitoring systems that continuously evaluate water quality in real-time is also made easier by the combination of AI and CV technologies. These systems can be installed in a variety of settings, including distant natural lakes and rivers and metropolitan water delivery networks. These devices, which are outfitted with cameras and sensors, gather continuous streams of data that are instantly analyzed by AI algorithms. Through real-time analysis, water quality problems, such as abrupt declines in dissolved oxygen levels or spikes in pollutant concentrations, can be identified and addressed quickly (Khan et al., 2021). Furthermore, the use of AI and CV technologies in evaluating water quality encourages the development of extensive and cohesive data platforms. Predictive models, for example, can mimic the results of different pollution control strategies, assisting legislators in creating environmental legislation that are more successful. In conclusion, by improving data collection, analysis, and administration, AI and CV technologies are revolutionizing the assessment of water quality and eventually paving the way for more proactive and knowledgeable management of water resources.

## Fundamentals of Water Quality Assessment

### Parameters and Indicators of Water Quality

To determine the health and fitness of water bodies for various uses, a range of physical, chemical, and biological factors are measured and analyzed. These processes provide the basis of the water quality assessment process. Physical characteristics of water, including temperature, turbidity, color, taste, and odor, offer fundamental insights into its quality. Temperature impacts the solubility of gases such as oxygen and the metabolic rates of aquatic organisms, whereas turbidity—a result of suspended particles—affects light penetration and, in turn, aquatic plant photosynthesis. Variations in color, flavor, and odor can be indicators of possible pollution and ecological disruptions caused by organic waste, algae, and industrial pollutants (Kothari et al., 2021) (Aydin et al., 2021). Chemical parameters have a substantial impact on aquatic ecosystems and human health by providing information about the concentration of different compounds in water. pH, dissolved oxygen (DO), nutrients, heavy metals, and organic contaminants are important chemical indicators. Extreme pH levels can be hazardous to aquatic life and have an impact on the toxicity of chemicals. pH levels also alter the solubility and availability of nutrients and metals. Fish and other aquatic life depend on DO for respiration, and low DO levels are frequently indicative of organic contamination. While excess of certain nutrients, such as phosphates and nitrates, can cause eutrophication, which can result in toxic algal blooms and oxygen depletion, is necessary for plant growth. Heavy metals that come from air deposition and industrial discharges, such as lead, mercury, and cadmium, are hazardous even in small amounts. Because of their toxicity and endurance, organic pollutants, such as pesticides and herbicides, pose long-term dangers to human health as well as ecosystems (M. V.-E., 2020).

By evaluating aquatic species’ existence and condition, biological factors serve as clear markers of the quality of the water. High BOD levels indicate considerable organic pollution and possible oxygen depletion (Wen et al., 2020). BOD measures the quantity of oxygen required by microorganisms to breakdown organic materials. Microbial markers, such as coliform bacteria, indicate pathogen contamination, frequently from sewage and other sources of faecal pollution. Fish population diversity and abundance, as well as variations in species composition, are important markers of the general health of aquatic ecosystems. These indicators can also reveal ecological disruptions and the effects of pollution. To sum up, a thorough evaluation of the physical, chemical, and biological aspects of water bodies is necessary to give a thorough grasp of their appropriateness and overall health. These indicators aid in locating pollution sources, evaluating the effects on the environment, and directing the creation of management and conservation plans for water resources. Water quality assessments can guarantee the sustainability and safety of aquatic habitats for a range of uses by integrating these characteristics.
