### Introduction

#### Overview of Water Quality Assessment

A complex process known as “water quality assessment” is used to determine if a body of water is safe to use for drinking, recreational activities, farming, or industrial processes. The measurement of physical, chemical, and biological parameters is included in this assessment. Physical characteristics that include temperature, turbidity, color, taste, and odour serve as fundamental water quality indicators (Neama et al., 2022). The solubility of gases and the metabolic rates of aquatic organisms are influenced by temperature, whereas light penetration and photosynthetic aquatic plants and creatures are affected by turbidity, which is the result of suspended particles (Petropoulou et al., 2023) (de Mello et al., 2020). Chemical parameters are used to quantify the concentrations of various substances, such as dissolved oxygen (DO), pH, nutrients, heavy metals, and organic pollutants. For example, phosphates and nitrates are important for the respiration of aquatic organisms; heavy metals, like mercury and lead, can be toxic even at low concentrations; and pesticides and herbicides are examples of organic pollutants that can be harmful to ecosystems and human health. Indicators of water quality include Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD), which reveals levels of organic pollution, and microbial indicators, which show pathogen contamination. Biological parameters evaluate the existence and health of aquatic species. The diversity and quantity of fish populations indicate environmental health. *In situ* sensors for ongoing monitoring, remote sensing for extensive observation, and field sampling for laboratory analysis are some of the techniques used in the assessment of water quality (Uddin and Nashi, 2021) (Krishnan et al., 2021). To model water quality and forecast future conditions, the gathered data is analyzed to find trends, pollution sources, and possible hazards. This is done using statistical and computational tools. For the sake of maintaining ecosystems, safeguarding public health, and assuring sustainable water management, this thorough examination is crucial. It offers the scientific foundation for creating laws and rules that manage resources, reduce pollution, and lessen the negative effects of climate change and human activity on aquatic habitats. This helps stakeholders make decisions that will preserve and enhance the quality of the water they work with (S. A., 2021).

#### Importance of Ecological Informatics in Environmental Monitoring

By combining data management, analysis, and computational modeling, ecological informatics contributes significantly to environmental monitoring and advances our knowledge of ecological systems. Ecological informatics provide the methods and tools required to effectively handle massive, complicated datasets in the context of water quality monitoring (Gupta, 2021). Conventional monitoring methods are frequently labor- and time-intensive since they mainly rely on manual sampling and laboratory analysis. To overcome these constraints, ecological informatics uses cutting-edge data gathering methods including automated sensors, remote sensing, and geographic information systems (GIS) to quickly and thoroughly collect data. This makes it possible to continuously monitor water bodies, which makes it easier to identify pollution incidents early on and put mitigation plans in place promptly.

Furthermore, by using advanced statistical and computational methods, ecological informatics improves data analysis. For example, machine learning algorithms can spot patterns and trends in water quality data that traditional approaches would miss. Based on past data, these algorithms can forecast future conditions, offering important insights into possible hazards and assisting in the direction of resource management choices.

Furthermore, ecological informatics facilitates the creation of ecological models that replicate the interplay among diverse environmental elements (Yuh et al., 2023) (Yang, 2023). These models can forecast how various management approaches will affect the quality of the water, which can help with the creation of sensible laws and rules.

Ecological informatics enables scientists, decision-makers, and stakeholders to make well-informed decisions about maintaining and enhancing water quality by converting unprocessed data into useful knowledge.
