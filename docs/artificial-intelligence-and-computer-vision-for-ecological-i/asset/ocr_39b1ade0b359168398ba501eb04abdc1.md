**Data Normalization and Standardization:** Normalization adjusts values to fall within a 0 to 1 range, whereas standardization alters the data distribution to achieve a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one (Bouguettya et al., 2022b). These scaling approaches are crucial for algorithms affected by input feature magnitudes, as they ensure each feature has a comparable influence on the model’s training process (Vyas et al., 2021).

**Temporal and Spatial Alignment:** Wildfire data often includes time-series and spatial information that must be aligned accurately (Zhang et al., 2023). Temporal alignment ensures that data points from different sources are synchronized in time, while spatial alignment ensures that data from various geographic locations are correctly mapped. For example, aligning weather data with corresponding satellite images based on time and location is crucial for accurate model training (Rjoub et al., 2023).

**Data Augmentation:** Data Augmentation expands the training dataset by applying diverse modifications to existing samples, and generating new, synthetic examples. This can include rotating, flipping, or scaling images and adding noise to simulate different conditions. Data augmentation helps improve model robustness by increasing the diversity of the training dataset, thereby reducing overfitting (Zakari et al., 2024).

**Feature Extraction:** Feature extraction converts raw data into refined characteristics that machine learning models can efficiently utilize. This step is crucial for highlighting the relevant information and reducing the complexity of the data.

**Remote Sensing Features:** Remote sensing provides a wealth of data for wildfire analysis, mainly through satellite imagery and UAV data. Various features can be extracted through advanced image processing techniques to aid in wildfire detection and prediction (Zhong et al., 2023). Thermal anomalies, indicating hotspots that could signify potential fire locations, are identified from infrared imagery. Vegetation indices such as the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) are computed to assess vegetation health and fuel availability. Additionally, multispectral and hyperspectral data allow for the analysis of spectral bands, enabling differentiation between burned and unburned areas based on their reflectance properties (Bhowmik et al., 2023).

**Meteorological Features:** Meteorological data is crucial in understanding and predicting wildfire behavior, as weather conditions significantly influence fire spread (Masoudian et al., 2023). Key meteorological features include temperature, humidity, wind speed, and precipitation. Elevated temperatures raise the probability of fire initiation and propagation, and reduced atmospheric moisture can desiccate plant life, enhancing its flammability. Wind speed and direction are critical factors, as wind can rapidly spread fires and change their direction. Conversely, precipitation, such as rainfall, can dampen fires and reduce their intensity (Pereira et al., 2022).
