### Introduction

Soil fertility evaluation is the assessment of the soil’s capacity to provide essential nutrients to plants. Assessing the fertility of the soil is a crucial component of an efficient agricultural system. Through comprehending the soil’s nutrient condition, farmers and agronomists may make well-informed choices to boost soil health, optimize crop yields, and assure the implementation of sustainable farming techniques (Salomon and Cavagnaro, 2022). Conducting regular soil testing and implementing suitable management actions are crucial for preserving productive and healthy soils. The basis for assessing soil fertility is on a thorough comprehension of soil characteristics and their impact on plant development (Guo et al., 2021). Soil fertility evaluation involves five primary procedures. Firstly, it examines the indications of nutrient deficiency in plants. Additionally, the second way entails conducting plant and tissue tests, while the third method focuses on cultivating higher plants and microbes. The fourth approach involves soil chemical analysis, while the last technique utilizes isotopic dilution techniques. Through a methodical examination of various soil characteristics, we can ascertain its ability to provide necessary nutrients, preserve ideal circumstances for growth, and facilitate environmentally-friendly farming techniques (Cakmakcı et al., 2023). This comprehensive approach guarantees that soil management practices are both effective and efficient, while also being ecologically sustainable.

Artificial intelligence is a discipline within the field of computer science which utilizes various methods, including machine learning and deep learning, to analyze and interpret data to imitate human intellect (Chouhan et al., 2024b). These kinds of networks make recommendations by continuously linking input and output elements, allowing for the development of several solutions to both simple and intricate scenarios (Tyagi and Chahal, 2022). AI's interdisciplinary nature, incorporating computer science, mathematics, statistics, and cognitive science, empowers it to address intricate difficulties in agriculture. AI technology is advancing the productivity of agriculture, maintaining food security, and promoting sustainable farming practices by imitating human intellect in robots and other automated systems (Shaikh et al., 2022). Machine learning allows computers to acquire knowledge and make decisions autonomously, without the need for explicit programming, by recognizing and analyzing patterns in data (Javaid et al., 2023). This requires training algorithms on large datasets, allowing them to steadily improve their performance as they acquire information.

### Scenario of Soil Testing

Assessing soil fertility may be a tedious and time-consuming procedure, but its comprehensiveness is crucial for obtaining precise and significant outcomes. The complexity occurs due to the necessity of comprehending and incorporating diverse soil variables and their interactions with plant development (Alaoui et al., 2022). Conventional methods for measuring soil nutrients involve using the random grid sample approach to collect soil cores. Subsequently, the cores are carefully enclosed and sent to a laboratory (Lawrence et al., 2020). Furthermore, this procedure will require costly laboratory equipment and supplementary solvents for further chemical examination. While the laboratory approaches exhibit great accuracy, they do not possess the capability to provide continuous real-time examination of agricultural soil. Large scale farmers have two significant problems when trying to map their terrain: duration per sample and expense per sample. Farm terrain exhibits variability, indicating that the characteristics represented on one crop land may differ from those found on another over the same terrain.

### Rise of AI in Soil Fertility Management

Machine learning models in soil nutrient analysis can accurately forecast nutrient levels, optimize fertilizer utilization, and improve crop yields by assessing various data sources such as soil samples, remote sensing satellite images, and meteorological events (Folorunso et al., 2023). Regression algorithms and neural networks are widely used for soil nutrient monitoring due to their ability to accurately predict continuous values using input data. Both machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) methods are used to predict soil nutrients. ML applications utilize both supervised and unsupervised learning methods to enhance data analysis and produce ample data for statistical solutions (Trontel and Chambers, 2021). Deep learning techniques are utilized to efficiently analyze a vast quantity of data for the purpose of soil nutrient testing. These approaches provide precise forecasts of soil nutrient levels by using extensive datasets that encompass diverse soil features and environmental circumstances. This predictive capability aids in optimizing the use of fertilizer and enhancing strategies for managing crops.

### AI Techniques for Soil Fertility Management

**Decision Tree**

Decision tree is another popular machine learning method used for classifying and predicting data in problems involving regression as well as classification. It represents decisions and their potential outcomes using a hierarchical structure like a tree. The decision tree operates by constantly dividing the dataset into smaller groups, or subsets, according to the value of a certain characteristic (Priyanka and Kumar, 2020). The objective is to enhance the uniformity of the desired outcome within each subset. This technique generates a hierarchical arrangement of choices that culminates in a definitive forecast.

Decision trees are useful in categorizing soil samples according to their nutrient content and other characteristics during the assessment of soil fertility. The nodes of the tree symbolize determination of nutrient thresholds, while the leaves symbolize the anticipated fertility status. This assists farmers and agronomists in making informed decisions based on information.
