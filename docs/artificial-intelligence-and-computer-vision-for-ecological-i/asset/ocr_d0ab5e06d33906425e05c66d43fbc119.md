Patil et al. (2017) found that ANN was the most accurate, achieving an astounding 92% accuracy rate in the identification of sugarcane illnesses from leaf photos (Singh et al., 2024). With the help of textural features and the maximum-minimum color difference technique, Suttapakti and Bunpeng (2019) developed a novel method that produced an impressive 91.67% accuracy in disease classification. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) were first used by Arshaghi et al. (2023) to categorize potato diseases. They achieved previously unheard-of accuracy rates of 100% and 99% across a range of disease groups, signifying a substantial advancement in automated disease identification. Arya and Rajeev (2019) also investigated the efficacy of CNN and AlexNet designs; AlexNet performed exceptionally well, achieving an astounding 98.33% accuracy rate in disease identification. Further highlighting the promise of DL in disease diagnosis, Mahum et al. (2023) developed an advanced DL technique including Efficient DenseNet and reweighted cross-entropy loss, attaining a testing set accuracy of 97.2%. Al-Amin et al. (2019) achieved accuracies of up to 98.33% in their development of a specialized deep learning architecture for detecting late and early blight in potato leaves, proving the effectiveness of DL models in disease detection. In addition, Sharma et al. (2021) achieved remarkable accuracy rates of 97.66% by using DL CNNs to predict potato illnesses, outperforming traditional ML classifiers. The conclusion of these investigations points to a positive direction for the incorporation of ML and DL methods into farming operations, providing long-term remedies for crop protection and disease control in potato production (Sinshaw et al., 2022).
