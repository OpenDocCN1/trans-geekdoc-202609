# Introduction

In the twenty-first century, agricultural production is confronted with numerous difficulties. An expanding world population needs more food to be produced with as little negative influence on the environment as possible. To do this, sustainable agricultural methods are essential. When combined with machine learning approaches, remote sensing provides a potent means of maximizing agricultural yield and financial gains (Lin et al., 2021). Farmers have historically made decisions regarding crop health, irrigation, and resource allocation based on field observations and experience. These techniques, however, are frequently laborious, subjective, and have a constrained scope in terms of both time and space. For getting beyond these restrictions, remote sensing offers a useful tool. It serves as a way of learning about an object or part of the Earth’s surface without embarking there in person (Bharadiya et al., 2023; Sharma et al., 2020).
