## K-Nearest Neighbor

KNN is a non-parametric and robust technique that is utilized to address classification and regression difficulties. It does not rely on hypotheses regarding the fundamental data distribution. The K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) approach operates on the principle of similarity. Through the examination of the numerical values of its K nearest neighbors in the training dataset, it predicts the value for a new data point (Uddin et al., 2023). There is no need to make any conjectures about the distribution of data. The K value denotes the total quantity of nearest neighbors. KNN aims to ascertain the precise category for the test data by computing the proximity that exists between the test data and each training point (Zhang et al., 2021). There are several techniques available for computing this distance, with the most widely recognized ways being Euclidean distance (continuous data), Manhattan distance (continuous data), and Hamming distance (categorical data). The computational cost of updating distance measures with every loop is high, which is why KNN is considered a lazy learning algorithm.

The KNN algorithm is both time-consuming and resource-intensive. The algorithm encounters difficulty in accurately categorizing the data points when the number of dimensions is very large. It is also susceptible to the issue of overfitting.

## Support Vector Machine

The Support Vector Machine (SVM) represents a resilient and adaptable trained machine learning method frequently employed for classification problems, such as assessing soil fertility. The core principle underlying Support Vector Machines (SVM) is to ascertain an ideal hyperplane that efficiently distinguishes among data points belonging to different groups (Cervantes et al., 2020).

SVM can effectively forecast the fertility status of soil samples by identifying the best hyperplane that splits various classes, using information about their nutrient content and other features (Sujatha and Jaidhar, 2023). This facilitates the process of making well-informed judgments on soil management and agricultural practices.

## Computer Vision Technologies in Soil Fertility Evaluation

### Drones in Soil Fertility Evaluation

The adoption of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for assessing the quality and productivity of the soil is a cutting-edge method that exploits sophisticated technology to improve agricultural techniques. Drones are employed to monitor soil fertility by gathering precise and up-to-date information from the atmosphere (Chouhan et al., 2024a). Drones utilize modern imaging technology and spatial analysis to offer comprehensive information on soil health, facilitating accurate and effective soil management techniques (Raza et al., 2023). The introduction of drone technology, coupled with sophisticated sensors, has completely transformed the process of soil sampling and analysis in the field of agriculture. Their objective is to provide farmers with precise and timely information that may be used to improve soil management techniques, increase crop yield, and promote sustainable agriculture practices worldwide (Kumar et al., 2024). Drones outfitted with multispectral cameras are transforming soil analysis by delivering prompt, thorough, and practical data on a previously unachievable scale using conventional techniques (Mushtaq et al., 2024). This method signifies a notable advancement in sustainable agriculture since it maximizes resource use and enhances crop productivity while reducing negative effects on the environment.

Drones offer accurate and up-to-date information that aids the agricultural community in efficiently managing soil health. Drones may combine their obtained data with other sources, such as satellite imaging, meteorological data, and historical crop production data, to create thorough models for assessing soil fertility (Weiss et al., 2020). The utilization of sophisticated data processing and machine learning approaches significantly enhances understanding of this data (Chouhan et al., 2021).

## Practical Implications and Challenges

### Variations in Soil Composition across Different Regions

Developing accurate and reliable AI models for identifying soil nutrient levels is inherently difficult due to the substantial variability in soil characteristics across diverse geographical regions.

### Effect of Data on Performance

The technological expense of deep learning algorithms has considerable effects on the speed and effectiveness of the training process. Analyzing and processing high-dimensional data, such as hyperspectral photographs with a large number of spectral bands, necessitates significant computer resources (Ang and Seng, 2021). The complexity of the task is determined by various aspects, including the size of the dataset, the complexity and structure of neural networks, and the need for meticulous data preparation and feature extraction. To overcome these obstacles and enhance the effectiveness of deep learning in soil fertility evaluation, it is crucial to optimize algorithms and utilize efficient computer resources.

### Ultra-High-Resolution Hyperspectral Images

The substantial dimension of hyperspectral data is a result of its extensive range of spectral bands or channels. Hyperspectral imaging is a technique that collects data over several closely spaced spectral bands, allowing for precise spectral information to be obtained for every pixel in an image (Lu et al., 2020). The abundance of data enables precise detection and characterization of materials, but it also presents obstacles in terms of data processing, storage, and analysis due to its high complexity.

### Interference from Sunlight

Obtaining soil samples using a smartphone camera to forecast soil pH might be challenging because of the inconsistent natural lighting conditions. Variations in lighting conditions can impact the fidelity of colors in photographs, resulting in inconsistent and untrustworthy results (Alhakamy and Tuceryan, 2020). To address this issue, it is essential to either maintain regulated lighting settings or utilize AI algorithms that can compensate for differences in illumination. This would enhance the accuracy and dependability of pH predictions made from smartphone photos.
