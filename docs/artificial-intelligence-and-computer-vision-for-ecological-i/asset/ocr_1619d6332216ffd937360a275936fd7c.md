The interaction of soil texture, topography, crop cover, and irrigation practices results in significant spatial and temporal variations in soil moisture (Hendrickx et al., 2024). The soil textural triangle provides a standardized method for classifying soils based on the heterogeneous sizes of soil particles.

Vegetation cover intercepts precipitation, reduces surface evaporation, and enhances soil structure through root systems. Meanwhile, irrigation practices, in terms of frequency and application method, directly regulate soil moisture levels by adding water to the soil profile. Collectively, these factors dictate the soil’s capacity for water retention and transmission, thereby critically influencing both agricultural productivity and ecosystem functioning (Farouki et al., 1981).
