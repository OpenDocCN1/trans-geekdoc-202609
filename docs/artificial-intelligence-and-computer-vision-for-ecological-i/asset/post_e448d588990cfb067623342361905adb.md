
First, we calculate the mean of the crop yield and temperature:

$$
\bar{y}=\frac{2.5+3.0+3.5+4.0+4.5+5.0}{6}=3.5
$$

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{15+16+17+18+19+20}{6}=17.5
$$

Next, we calculate the deviations from the mean:

$$
y_i - \bar{y}= y_i - 3.5
$$

$$
x_i - \bar{x}= x_i - 17.5
$$

Then, we calculate the covariance between the deviations:

$$
Cov(y,x)=\frac{\sum_{i}(y_i-\bar{y})(x_i-\bar{x})}{n-1}
$$

$$
Cov(y,x)=\frac{(-0.5\times-2.5)+(0.0\times-0.5)+\cdots+(1.5\times2.5)}{6-1}
$$

$$
Cov(y,x)=\frac{1.25+0.75+\cdots+1.25}{5}
$$

$$
Cov(y,x)=\frac{5.0}{5}=1.0
$$

Finally, we calculate the slope and intercept using the following formulas:

$$
\beta_1=\frac{Cov(y,x)}{Var(x)}
$$

$$
\beta_0=\bar{y}-\beta_1\bar{x}
$$

$$
\beta_1=\frac{1.0}{Var(x)}
$$

$$
\beta_0=3.5-\beta_1\times17.5
$$

$$
\beta_1=\frac{1.0}{(20-15)^2\times6}=0.05
$$

$$
\beta_0=3.5-0.05\times17.5=2.25
$$

The simple linear regression model for crop yield estimation based on temperature is:

$$
\hat{y}=2.25+0.05x
$$

This model can be used to predict crop yields based on temperature. For example, if the temperature is 18°C, the predicted crop yield would be:

$$
\hat{y}=2.25+0.05\times18=4.0
$$

This example shows the application of simple linear regression for crop yield estimation. The model can be extended to include multiple predictor variables and more complex relationships using multiple linear regression and other machine learning algorithms.

## Multiple Linear Regression

Multiple linear regression is the extension of simple linear regression that contains multiple predictor variables. This model is useful when there are multiple factors that influence crop yields, such as temperature, precipitation, and soil type. The equation for multiple linear regression is:

$$
\hat{y} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \beta_2 x_2 + \dots + \beta_n x_n \quad (7)
$$

where \(\hat{y}\) is the predicted crop yield, \(\beta_0\) is the intercept, \(\beta_1, \beta_2, \dots, \beta_n\) are the slopes for each predictor variable, and \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\) are the predictor variables.
