Rising global temperatures have increased the likelihood of severe wildfires, particularly in areas experiencing prolonged dry spells (Pérez-Sánchez et al., 2019). With ongoing global warming, we anticipate more frequent severe weather occurrences, including intense heat and prolonged hot spells. These conditions will likely create environments increasingly prone to widespread and recurrent wildfires. Intense heat episodes further dry out the landscape, creating an ideal environment for these destructive blazes (Gaudreau et al., 2016). This, in turn, leads to amplified carbon dioxide emissions from wildfires, further triggering climate change and perpetuating a cyclical pattern of severe wildfires (McColl-Gausden et al., 2022). This feedback loop, illustrated in Fig. 1, highlights the interconnectedness of wildfires and climate change.

Cutting-edge technologies, sophisticated modeling, enhanced sensing systems, and improved observation methods have significantly enhanced our ability to detect and forecast wildfire progression (Ladi et al., 2022). The concurrent evolution of computational theory, mathematical innovations, and increased processing capabilities have armed us with powerful tools for data collection and analysis (Ding et al., 2022). Implementing these innovative technologies has markedly improved our capacity to tackle the complex issues inherent in wildfire control and prevention (Coen et al., 2020). Applications of AI-driven approaches, such as ML, have gained popularity in recent years and have become versatile tools for technological advancement (Rolnick et al., 2019). ML, a subfield of AI, employs algorithms to use big datasets to identify patterns (Jafar Ali Ibrahim et al., 2022; Kalinaki et al., 2024a; Kalinaki et al., 2023b). It is rapidly evolving and valuable for understanding complex networks, discovering knowledge patterns in big data, and future predictions (Ding et al., 2022). ML techniques are indispensable in various application fields, including wildfire spread detection, urban planning, resource distribution, energy requirement forecasting, food security forecasting, and air quality monitoring and forecasting (Sreenath et al., 2022). Given that wildfire spread detection and prediction are highly complex and require research from multiple disciplines (Yandouzi et al., 2022), the use of ML techniques has proven to be a valuable tool for understanding and forecasting issues related to wildfires (Massetti et al., 2019). Generally, ML approaches typically fall into two main categories: supervised and unsupervised. Supervised methods map input data to known outcomes, using labeled datasets to discern patterns that can predict results for new inputs. Unsupervised techniques, on the other hand, explore data without predetermined outcomes, potentially uncovering novel relationships or distant correlations in wildfire propagation models (Kalinaki et al., 2023c; Umunakwe et al., 2022). ML methods can capture strong nonlinearities, influencing many aspects of wildfire behavior (Ghali and Akhloufi, 2023), challenging to elaborate using conventional regression approaches (Ghali et al., 2022).

Wildfires represent a significant environmental and socio-economic challenge worldwide. Their occurrence has increased due to climate change, anthropogenic activities, and natural weather patterns (Küçükarslan, 2024). The ramifications of wildfires are multifaceted and severe, affecting ecosystems, human health, infrastructure, and economies. Ecologically, wildfires can result in loss of biodiversity, degradation of habitats, and alteration of natural processes. From a human perspective, wildfires pose direct threats to life and property, cause displacement of communities, and result in significant economic losses.

Furthermore, the release of smoke and particulate matter during wildfires has substantial health implications, contributing to respiratory and cardiovascular diseases (Dillis et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023a). Early detection and accurate prediction of wildfires are crucial for effective mitigation and management. Prompt detection allows for rapid response, reducing the potential for large-scale spread and minimizing damage. Accurate prediction models enable resource allocation, strategic planning, and proactive measures, such as controlled burns and evacuation plans. The ability to anticipate wildfire behavior and spread can significantly enhance emergency response efforts, safeguarding human lives and minimizing ecological and economic impacts (Wang et al., 2023b).

RS plays a vital role in identifying and monitoring wildfires. It employs various data collection methods, including satellite imagery, aerial photography, and diverse sensor systems, to obtain information about active fires, smoke plumes, and areas affected by burns (Charizanos and Demirhan, 2023). This technology enables broad-scale wildfire detection and tracking, offering critical insights for strategic planning and resource deployment. Analyzing data from satellites, meteorological stations, and ground-based sensors is fundamental to effective wildfire surveillance, requiring sophisticated processing and interpretation techniques (Kalinaki et al., 2024b). Advanced analytical methods, including image analysis, statistical modeling, and machine learning algorithms, are employed to recognize fire indicators, spot unusual patterns, and deliver prompt, actionable intelligence for wildfire response teams (Casas et al., 2023; Ding et al., 2023).
