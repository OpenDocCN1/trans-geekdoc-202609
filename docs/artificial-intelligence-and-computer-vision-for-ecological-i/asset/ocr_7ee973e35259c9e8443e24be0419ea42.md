# Data Volume

RS generates large volumes of data, especially when using high-resolution images and frequent temporal observations (Bei et al., 2022). Managing and processing this data requires substantial storage capacity and computational power. Efficient data handling techniques and robust infrastructure are necessary to cope with the sheer volume of information, ensuring timely and accurate analysis for agricultural applications (Khanal et al., 2020).

# Data Integration

Combining data from multiple sources, such as satellite images, aerial photographs, and ground-based sensors, can enhance the overall accuracy and detail of agricultural mapping (Kashyap and Kumar, 2021a). However, integrating these diverse datasets poses significant challenges due to differences in spatial resolution, temporal frequency, and data formats. Harmonizing these datasets requires sophisticated algorithms and careful preprocessing to ensure compatibility and reliability of the integrated information (Yan et al., 2023).

# Environmental Factors

Environmental conditions such as weather, seasonal variations, and atmospheric disturbances can significantly impact the quality and availability of RS data (Kashyap and Kumar, 2021b). Cloud cover can obstruct satellite and aerial imagery, while atmospheric conditions can introduce distortions. Seasonal changes in vegetation and soil moisture also affect data consistency. Addressing these challenges requires advanced correction techniques and adaptive data acquisition strategies (Steven and Clark, 1990).

# Data Preprocessing Steps

# Data Cleaning

Data cleaning is the initial step in preprocessing, aimed at removing noise, correcting errors, and handling missing values (Liu, 2020). Noise can be reduced using filtering techniques, while errors are corrected by comparing data against known standards or reference datasets. Missing values can be addressed through interpolation or imputation methods. Effective data cleaning ensures the reliability and accuracy of subsequent analyses (Zakari et al., 2024).

# Geometric Correction

Geometric correction adjusts for distortions in RS data caused by sensor movement, Earth’s rotation, and terrain variations. Ground Control Points (GCPs) and orthorectification techniques are commonly used to align images accurately with geographic coordinates. This step is crucial for ensuring that spatial relationships in the data are accurate, enabling precise mapping and analysis.
