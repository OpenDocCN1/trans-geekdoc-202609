# Computer Visions

Computer vision technology plays a crucial role in soil moisture analysis by extracting meaningful information from remote sensing imagery. This involves a range of techniques for image data acquisition, preprocessing, and detailed analysis. Utilizing data from satellites and UAVs, computer vision leverages multispectral, hyperspectral, and thermal imaging to assess soil moisture accurately (Tang et al., 2022). Key computer vision techniques for soil moisture estimation include image preprocessing (radiometric and geometric corrections), feature extraction, and machine-learning-based classification. These methods improve image quality and identify relevant spatial and spectral soil patterns (Khan et al., 2022). Machine learning algorithms like support vector machines, random forests, and convolutional neural networks analyze complex relationships between features and soil moisture content, using extensive spectral, spatial, and temporal data from remote sensing imagery. By training these algorithms with ground-truth soil moisture measurements, researchers can develop predictive models for various landscapes and temporal scales. This capability enables accurate soil moisture estimation for agricultural management, hydrologic studies, and environmental monitoring (Brocca et al., 2017). Ultimately, computer vision technologies provide a comprehensive toolbox for extracting valuable information on soil moisture dynamics, aiding in informed decision-making for water resources, land-use planning, and ecosystem conservation.

# Future Perspective

Soil moisture retrieval models have advanced significantly in recent decades, yet there is still potential for further enhancement. Future improvements should focus on achieving greater spatial and temporal resolution, extending time series monitoring, reducing transmission delays, enhancing accuracy, and improving model performance and interpretability.

# Future Directions

Future advancements in soil moisture retrieval models should focus on multi-source data matching by integrating optical and SAR remote sensing to improve accuracy while addressing data format and resolution differences and exploring short-wave infrared data. Enhancing spatial and temporal resolutions is crucial for applications like irrigation and drought prediction, necessitating methods for root zone penetration and providing daily or hourly data. Establishing ground networks and launching more satellites will enable long-term data series with low latency, supported by improved satellite materials for extended operational life. Improving model accuracy involves refining empirical, semi-empirical, and physical models, enhancing machine learning interpretability, and integrating physical and AI methods to develop models requiring minimal auxiliary data. Technological advancements are needed to achieve better spatial and temporal resolution, longer monitoring, shorter delays, and higher accuracy through innovations in satellite materials, observation networks, and models.
