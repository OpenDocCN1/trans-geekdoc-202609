## Introduction

Agriculture, a pivotal driver of economic development in nations such as India, is presently experiencing its fourth revolution, commonly referred to as Agriculture 4.0, due to swift technological advancements. Remote sensing (RS) technologies have revolutionized various fields, including agriculture, by offering crucial information about crop conditions, soil health, and environmental parameters influencing agricultural productivity. Additionally, RS techniques can be combined with advanced technologies like global positioning systems (GPS), the Internet of Things (IoT), and artificial intelligence (AI) to create powerful tools for the precise management of agricultural inputs. Given the growing global population and the resulting food demand, there is an urgent need for innovative solutions to enhance agricultural efficiency, sustainability, and resilience. These technologies together offer a powerful toolset to address these challenges by facilitating thorough monitoring and management of agricultural systems and by decreasing the expenses of cultivation and yield loss while concurrently enhancing production and economic gains (Elijah et al., 2018; Jha et al., 2019). Projections suggest that adopting precision agriculture practices could potentially boost global yield by as much as 67% by the year 2050 (Rosegrant, 2014). As per the 2018 report from the California Department of Food and Agriculture, a pivotal aspect of PA involves the extensive gathering of data on a large scale, primarily achieved through RS techniques, serving as the fundamental basis of the entire PA procedure.

The past decade has witnessed significant advancements in remote sensing technologies, ranging from traditional satellite imagery to emerging platforms such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and hyperspectral sensors. Remote sensing involves collecting or measuring data about specific characteristics of a body or event using a recording device that does not have direct bodily contact or proximity to the subject being studied (ASPRS) (Estes et al., 2001). This process involves employing instrumentation from a distance. This RS technique throws electromagnetic radiation to capture diverse features of soil, plants, or other objects or phenomena (Jones and Vaughan, 2010). Various platforms such as ground-based, airborne, and spaceborne platforms carry a range of sensors of RS systems to collect data about remote objects or phenomena (Sishodia et al., 2020). These technological innovations have opened new avenues for agricultural research and management, facilitating the gathering of high-resolution spatial and temporal information at different scales. For instance, satellites equipped with multispectral sensors, such as Landsat and Sentinel, provide consistent and wide-area coverage, allowing for the monitoring of large-scale agricultural landscapes (Foody, 2019). On the other hand, UAVs offer flexibility and higher spatial resolution, enabling detailed assessments of crop health and variability within smaller agricultural fields (Colomina and Molina, 2014).

While numerous studies have discovered the usage of remote sensing in the domain of farming, there is a growing demand for comprehensive reviews that synthesize recent advances, identify current challenges, and outline future research directions. Such reviews play a crucial role in guiding researchers, policymakers, and practitioners in leveraging remote sensing technologies to address key agricultural issues, including yield optimization, resource management, and climate change adaptation. By critically evaluating the strengths and limitations of existing methodologies, these reviews pave the way for the progress of more efficient and robust remote sensing-based agricultural monitoring systems.

Furthermore, the complexity of agricultural systems necessitates interdisciplinary approaches that integrate remote sensing with other scientific disciplines, such as agronomy, ecology, and computer science. Researchers can obtain important insights into variables such as soil, plants, climate, and other environmental factors by integrating RS data with laboratory-based measurements and modeling techniques (Tucker et al., 2018). This interdisciplinary approach is essential for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of remote sensing-based agricultural assessments, ultimately supporting informed decision-making and sustainable agricultural practices.

Moreover, the need for sustainable agriculture has prompted increased interest in utilizing remote sensing technologies to monitor and mitigate environmental impacts associated with agricultural activities. From assessing soil erosion and nutrient runoff to monitoring deforestation and habitat loss, remote sensing offers valuable insights into the ecological footprint of agriculture (Turner et al., 2015). By combining environmental monitoring with agricultural management strategies, stakeholders can work towards enhancing productivity through improved land management practices while safeguarding natural environmental resources.

In this context, this chapter seeks to offer a complete summary of the most recent advances in RS technologies for farming, highlighting their applications, limitations, and prospects. By synthesizing findings from diverse research domains, this review will contribute to the collective understanding of how RS can be efficiently exploited to address the evolving challenges facing modern agriculture. Additionally, by identifying knowledge gaps and emerging trends, this review will guide future research efforts aimed at harnessing the full potential of remote sensing technologies to support sustainable and resilient agricultural systems.

The key research questions addressed in this review are:

- What are the latest advancements in remote sensing technologies for agriculture?
- How can RS technologies be effectively integrated with other advanced technologies for precision agriculture?
- What are the primary challenges and limitations of using RS in agricultural monitoring and management?
- How can RS contribute to sustainable agriculture practices and mitigate environmental impacts?
- What future research directions should be pursued to enhance the efficacy and adoption of RS technologies in agriculture?

## Remote Sensing Data Acquisition—Platforms and Sensors

### RS Platforms

The platforms employed in remote sensing, such as aircraft or satellites, are referred to as vehicles capable of transporting sensing devices, known as sensors, to conduct measurements from distant locations (Jafarbiglu and Pourreza, 2022). These platforms are constantly getting better in terms of operational speed, consistency, ease of use, and temporal resolution, which ultimately determines spatial resolution. As per Huang et al. (2018), RS systems possess the capability to produce extensive amounts of spectral data characterized by high resolutions, ranging from spatial to temporal resolutions which are particularly crucial for implementation in PA.
