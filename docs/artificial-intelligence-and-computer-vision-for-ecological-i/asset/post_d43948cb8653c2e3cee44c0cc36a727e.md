### Introduction

In today’s rapidly evolving technological landscape, chatbots have emerged as versatile tools capable of understanding human language and executing predefined tasks. Stemming from Joseph Weizenbaum’s pioneering creation of Eliza in 1966, these digital entities have undergone significant advancements since Alan Turing posed the fundamental question of machine intelligence. Today, they permeate various industries, serving as invaluable customer interaction and support resources. However, despite their widespread adoption, the effectiveness of chatbots varies considerably. The challenge lies not merely in their creation but in crafting chatbots that excel in functionality and user experience.

India’s agricultural sector contributes significantly to the country’s economic growth, accounting for approximately 16% of the GDP and employing around 52% of the population. Despite its importance, many farmers lack access to authentic and up-to-date farming information, hindering their decision-making processes. Traditional methods of disseminating information, such as field visits, are often hindered by accessibility issues, resulting in inefficiencies and decreased crop yields (Singh et al., 2019). Agriculture, as the art and science of soil cultivation, stands as a cornerstone of national development. Despite the modernization of plant breeding techniques and the advent of agrochemicals such as pesticides and fertilizers, farmers often lag in leveraging the latest technological advancements. Therefore, there’s a critical need to equip them with up-to-date information and techniques to ensure optimal agricultural productivity.

The proliferation of Information Technology (IT) in recent years, particularly in rural areas, presents an opportunity to address these challenges. With the exponential growth of mobile connectivity, especially in rural regions, mobile devices have become powerful tools for disseminating agricultural information. In domains such as medicine and travel, significant research has been conducted on question-answering systems. However, in agriculture, retrieving precise answers from web data poses unique challenges, as websites often present irrelevant information alongside relevant responses. To address this, deep learning techniques, particularly Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), are increasingly employed (Singh et al., 2019). In this context, the utilization of chatbot systems holds promise as a solution to disseminate accurate agricultural information efficiently. By leveraging the ubiquity of mobile devices and advancements in natural language processing, chatbots can empower farmers with timely and relevant agricultural knowledge, thereby enhancing productivity and supporting rural livelihoods.

### Natural Language Processing (NLP)

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a branch of computer science that falls under the broader field of Artificial Intelligence (AI), with a primary focus on enabling computers to comprehend and interpret human language, both spoken and written, in a manner akin to humans (Jurafsky and Martin, 2019). This interdisciplinary field combines computational linguistics, which involves rule-based modeling of human language, with statistical, machine learning, and deep learning models. NLP technologies encompass a range of tasks aimed at providing computers with the ability to understand human language in various forms, such as text or speech data, and to grasp its full meaning, including the speaker’s or writer’s intention and sentiment. These tasks include speech recognition, where software deciphers spoken commands or translates text between languages, and part-of-speech (POS) tagging, which identifies the grammatical role of each word in a sentence.

However, creating software that accurately discerns the intended meaning of text or voice data is highly challenging due to the inherent ambiguity of human language. From homonyms and homophones to idioms and metaphors, human language is replete with complexities that take years for humans to master. Programmers must meticulously teach NLP-driven applications to recognize and understand these nuances from the outset to ensure their usefulness (Jurafsky and Martin, 2019).

Word sense disambiguation is another critical NLP task, involving the selection of a word’s meaning from its potential meanings based on semantic analysis and context. Named entity recognition (NER) identifies words or phrases as specific entities, such as locations or individuals, while sentiment analysis delves into intangible elements within the text, including attitudes and emotions.

These NLP tasks underpin various applications that range from voice-activated GPS devices and digital assistants to customer service chatbots and speech-to-text dictation programs (Jurafsky and Martin, 2019). Furthermore, NLP’s utility extends beyond consumer conveniences to corporate solutions, where it enhances business operations, boosts worker productivity, and streamlines critical procedures. Thus, NLP continues to play a pivotal role in advancing human-computer interaction and driving technological innovation across diverse domains. Chatbots, powered by AI, NLP, and ML, come in two main types: task-oriented and data-driven/predictive. Task-oriented chatbots focus on specific tasks, offering structured, conversational responses using rules and minimal ML. On the other hand, data-driven/predictive chatbots, like virtual assistants, use NLP, NLU, and ML to provide personalized, interactive experiences, learning from user interactions to anticipate needs and make suggestions (Jurafsky and Martin, 2019). Examples include Apple’s Siri and Amazon’s Alexa.

NLTK, the Natural Language Toolkit, is a powerful platform for Python programming with human language data. It offers a wide range of text processing tools and access to various language resources like WordNet. NLTK is suitable for linguists, engineers, students, and industry users alike, providing comprehensive guides and documentation. It’s free, open-source, and compatible with Windows, Mac OS X, and Linux (Bird et al., 2009).
