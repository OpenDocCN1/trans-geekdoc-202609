This process utilizes diverse data inputs, including ground sensors, satellite imagery, climatic data, and geographical inputs. AI encompasses two primary methodologies, viz., machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL). ML is defined as the development of algorithms that enable programs to autonomously enhance their performance with experience (Mitchell, 1997), while DL applies intricate algorithms and neural networks with multiple layers to train models (LeCun et al., 2015). ML algorithms are categorized into supervised and unsupervised learning:

Supervised learning involves training models with datasets that have known outcomes. The goal is for the model to learn to predict outputs based on input data. It is primarily employed for classification and regression tasks. Soil moisture assessment frequently uses supervised ML algorithms like Support Vector Machines (SVMs), linear regression, logistic regression, neural networks, and Random Forest (RF).

Unsupervised learning deals with unlabeled data, allowing algorithms to identify patterns and infer insights autonomously. It is used for pattern discovery, data grouping, exploratory analysis, and reducing data dimensions. Clustering and association are key techniques in unsupervised learning. Algorithms such as k-means, hierarchical clustering, Apriori algorithm, expectation maximization algorithm, and principal component analysis are examples of unsupervised ML methods.

Recent studies have demonstrated the efficacy of standard cameras paired with AI algorithms for monitoring soil moisture, presenting a cost-effective and straightforward method for smart farming practices. The integration of machine learning algorithms has revolutionized soil testing, providing more precise and efficient analyzes. Deep learning, in particular, has shown promise in simulating soil moisture dynamics, with research exploring various network structures to enhance prediction capabilities (Wang et al., 2024). These advancements in AI-driven soil analysis contribute to sustainable water conservation and soil texture analysis (Awais et al., 2023), while addressing the limitations of deep learning methods by incorporating physical mechanisms into data-driven models (Geng et al., 2024).
