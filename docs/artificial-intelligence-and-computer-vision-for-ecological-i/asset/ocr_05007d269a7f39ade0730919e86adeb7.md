Object detection, in particular, enables the automated identification and localization of specific objects within digital images or video frames. YOLO (You Only Look Once) is a pioneering object detection architecture known for its real-time performance and accuracy (Sukhadeo Bajait et al., 2023). It is well-suited for agricultural applications where timely decision-making is critical. YOLOv8 represents an evolution of the YOLO architecture, incorporating advancements in model design, training techniques, and computational efficiency.

By leveraging a combination of deep convolutional neural networks and efficient inference strategies, YOLOv8 promises improved detection performance and reduced computational overhead compared to earlier iterations (Yang et al., 2023; Kunduracioglu and Pacal, 2024). Basil crops are susceptible to various environmental factors, pests, and diseases, making timely and accurate detection critical for maintaining crop health and maximizing yield. This study aims to develop a practical and efficient solution using YOLOv8 to detect basil crops in real-time, enabling farmers and agronomists to promptly make informed decisions and interventions (Fuentes-Pacheco et al., 2024; Mao et al., 2023). # Literature Review

Precision agriculture has witnessed substantial advancements driven by integrating deep learning and computer vision techniques. Object detection has been pivotal in automating tasks crucial for efficient crop management (Bougettaya et al., 2022). In this context, YOLO (You Only Look Once) models have emerged as a prominent choice, given their real-time capabilities and accuracy (Badgujar et al., 2024). YOLOv8, a recent iteration in the YOLO series, stands out for its robustness and versatility. Numerous studies have showcased the potential of YOLOv8 in diverse applications, from pedestrian detection to vehicle counting. However, the deployment of YOLOv8 in the domain of precision agriculture, specifically for basil crop detection, remains a relatively unexplored avenue. The proposed research contributes to this burgeoning field by presenting a practical study that leverages YOLOv8 for basil crop detection, demonstrating its adaptability and efficacy in a high-value herb context.
