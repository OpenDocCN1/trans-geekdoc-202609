**Resolution of Data:** High-resolution data is essential for accurate fire detection and prediction. However, such data can be expensive and logistically challenging, especially for large areas. Satellite imagery may have limitations in temporal resolution, missing rapid changes in fire conditions or small-scale fires (He et al., 2023).

**Real-Time Data Integration:** Integrating real-time data from diverse sources such as ground sensors, weather stations, and satellites is complex and requires robust data management systems. Delays in data acquisition and processing can hinder timely detection and response efforts (Wang et al., 2023b).

# *Computational Resources and Efficiency*

**High Computational Demands:** Advanced predictive models, especially those based on ML and DL, require significant computational power for training and real-time operation (Rim et al., 2018). High-performance computing resources are needed to handle large datasets and complex simulations, which may not be readily available in all regions (Phan et al., 2022).

**Scalability:** Scaling models to cover extensive areas with diverse environmental conditions can be challenging. Models must be adapted and optimized for different regions, requiring substantial computational resources and expertise (Kale et al., 2022). Ensuring that predictive models can run efficiently on available hardware while maintaining accuracy is a constant challenge (Kumar et al., 2024).

**Energy Consumption:** The computational power required for running advanced models can lead to high energy consumption, which is both costly and environmentally taxing (Kadir et al., 2023).

# Operational Constraints and Real-Time Application

**Timeliness and Speed:** For wildfire detection and prediction to be compelling, they must operate in real-time or near real-time (Umunkakwe et al., 2022). Any delays in data processing, analysis, or dissemination can reduce the effectiveness of early warning systems. Rapidly changing fire conditions require models to update predictions frequently, which challenges maintaining computational efficiency (Elia et al., 2020).

**Integration with Existing Systems:** Integrating new technologies and predictive models with existing fire management and emergency response systems can be challenging (Dillis et al., 2022). Compatibility issues and the need for personnel training can hinder smooth implementation (Khajuria et al., 2018b). Operationalizing predictive models within the workflow of fire management agencies requires robust systems for data communication, decision support, and actionable intelligence (Wang et al., 2023a).
