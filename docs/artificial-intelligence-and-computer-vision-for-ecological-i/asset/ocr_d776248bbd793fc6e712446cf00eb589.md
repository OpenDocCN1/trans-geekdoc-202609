It can also be necessary to perform primary chemical treatment, such as pH correction, pre-reduction of a large organic load, oxidation for cyanide destruction and Cr(VI) reduction. For example, a lot of SS, such as fibres, fillers, and other solids, are found in paper mill effluent (Pokhrel and Viraraghavan, 2004; Anjaneyulu et al., 2005; Sharma, 2015).

Despite frequently being alkaline and having a high organic content, textile mill wastewater has a very changeable pH. Pre-treating these effluents is therefore essential before thinking about further treatment. But frequently, these therapies by themselves are unable to satisfy the legal criteria. The pre-treated effluent must undergo secondary purification treatment using the most suitable biological, physical, or chemical technology available to eliminate the chemical pollutants before it is released into the environment or used again. To get rid of any leftover contaminants or molecules created during the secondary purification, a final or tertiary treatment may occasionally be necessary (e.g., the removal of salts formed by the mineralization of organic waste). Although tertiary therapy is not often used in Europe, it may be in the future if new regulations are put in place. Adsorption employing activated carbons (AC), ion-exchange, membrane filtration (ultrafiltration, reverse osmosis), advanced oxidation, and built wetlands (CW) are the primary tertiary treatments now used at a few industrial sites. The majority of CWs are used in Europe to treat municipal and household wastewater. On the other hand, the variety of CW designs makes them adaptable to the treatment of industrial effluents (such as wastewater from tanneries and post-treated pulp and paper).

Thus, the wastewater characteristics will determine which approach is chosen (Anjaneyulu et al., 2005; Crini, 2005; Crini and Badot, 2007; Cox et al., 2007). Every treatment has unique limitations concerning cost as well as practicality, efficiency, dependability, environmental effect, sludge generation, operation complexity, pre-treatment needs, and the creation of potentially hazardous byproducts. However, for technological and financial reasons, the industrial sector only often uses a small number of the several treatment procedures now recommended for wastewater treatment. Pollutants are typically removed from effluents using physicochemical and/or biological methods; research is focused on developing novel, more affordable alternatives or more efficient system combinations.
