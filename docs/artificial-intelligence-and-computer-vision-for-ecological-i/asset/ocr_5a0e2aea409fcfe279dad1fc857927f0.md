An ANN and fuzzy logic are combined in the FNN, a hybrid neural network model, to handle unclear or uncertain scenarios. After processing input data using fuzzy logic reasoning, an ANN is used by the FNN to train and generate the output. Though fuzzy logic is used to establish the fuzzy relationship between inputs and outputs and the weights of connections among neurons (normalisation, fuzzy inference, and membership function), the topology of an FNN is similar to that of a traditional neural network. An input, membership function, fuzzy inference, normalisation, and output layers make up the FNN architecture. When handling difficult situations, FNN provides a great lot of advantages over ordinary NNs and is widely used in pattern recognition and control (Mahesh et al., 2020).

A DNN is an ANN type that has several hidden layers positioned in between the input and output layers. With the help of successive layers that combine lower-level characteristics to learn higher-level features, the deep architecture of DNN enables it to learn hierarchical data representations. Just like other neural networks, DNNs can include more neurons and hidden layers. They are frequently used to learn highly nonlinear mappings from inputs to outputs or to capture complicated patterns in data. However, because of their complex network design and the need for a substantial quantity of data for training, DNNs may be difficult and computationally costly to train (Yadav et al., 2020).

Convolutional computations and a deep structure feedforward neural network are two components of CNNs, a sort of deep learning technology. Natural language processing, computer vision, and other domains all make extensive use of CNNs. CNNs use fully connected layers for classification or regression tasks, pooling layers to lower the feature of dimensionality, and convolutional layers to extract complex features from input pictures (Alzubaidi et al., 2021). Input, output, convolutional, pooling, and fully connected layers are common components of a CNN design.

The treatment of wastewater is important to know and understand for further enhancement of the process. Before wastewater from commercial, industrial, and municipal sources is released into natural water bodies, it must first be treated (WWT) to remove carbonaceous contaminants (such as organic carbon compounds) and nutrients (such as phosphorus and nitrogen species) (Russell, 2019). By lowering the dissolved oxygen content in surface water courses such as rivers and lakes, carbonaceous pollutants can harm the ecosystem (Jin et al., 2014). The aquatic life is at risk due to nutrient-induced eutrophication and algal bloom production in surface water (Elsayed et al., 2023a; Yaqub et al., 2020). Before being released into a river or another body of water for recycling (such as recycled water or consumption in agriculture), wastewater treatment facilities (WWTP) filter wastewater collected from cities, homes, companies, and other sources (Corominas et al., 2018).
