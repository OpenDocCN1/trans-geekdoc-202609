Sensor-based methods such as time domain reflectometry (TDR), neutron probes, and capacitance probes offer non-invasive alternatives, each operating on distinct principles to measure VSWC efficiently. TDR estimates water content by measuring soil dielectric characteristics; neutron probes use neutron scattering to assess moisture; and capacitance probes use soil capacitance changes to calculate VSWC. Using the TDR approach, the research investigated the effect of various vegetation coverings on volumetric water content in higher soil layers. The bare ground had the greatest VWC values, whereas plots with vegetation covers had lower values. The presence of vegetation cover on fallow land decreased VWC values relative to the bare ground but stabilized daily fluctuations (Brant et al., 2006). These techniques enhance spatial and temporal resolution in soil moisture studies through point measurements like gravimetry (Cao et al., 2020). An effective approach to enhancing irrigation efficiency involves integrating soil moisture sensor technology into irrigation scheduling.
