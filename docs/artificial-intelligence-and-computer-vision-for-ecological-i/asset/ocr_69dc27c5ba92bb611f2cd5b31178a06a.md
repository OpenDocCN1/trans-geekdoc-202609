Using sequence data as input, an RNN, a kind of NN, iterates in the direction of sequence evolution. Learning nonlinear features in time-series issues is facilitated by RNN's memory, parameter sharing, and Turing completeness. According to Sherstinsky et al. (2020), the LSTM is the RNN that is most frequently utilized to solve the gradient disappearance issue. Numerous applications, including water, WWT, water quality management, and water-based agriculture, have seen noteworthy success using RNN.

To process data, artificial neural networks (ANNs) mimic the behaviour of biological neural networks (BNNs). With the use of unit nodes, artificial neural networks (ANNs) imitate neurons and process information by varying the connectivity weights between different nodes within the network. An ANN typically consists of an input layer, an output layer, and several hidden layers positioned in between. ANNs use numerous variable weights across neurons in addition to a variety of activation functions, including sigmoid, tanh, and ReLU functions, to execute complicated nonlinear calculations. An ANN's expressive skills may be improved and more complex nonlinear models can be created by increasing the number of hidden layers in the ANN (Basheer et al., 2000).
