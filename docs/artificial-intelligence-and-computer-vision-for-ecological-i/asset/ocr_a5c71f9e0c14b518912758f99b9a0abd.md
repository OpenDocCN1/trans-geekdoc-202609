# Object-Based Image Analysis (OBIA)

This approach moves away from traditional pixel-based techniques and focuses on extracting features from RS data. OBIA categorizes pictures into meaningful objects or areas using spectral, spatial, and contextual information. By extracting features such as shape, dimension, and spectral residential qualities from these objects, OBIA allows for a more context-specific and semantically relevant evaluation than pixel-based approaches (van Klompenburg et al., 2020). OBIA is a technique used in farming to accurately identify boundaries between different areas, types of crops, and zones for effective management. This technology plays a crucial role in implementing precision agriculture methods. Moreover, OBIA enhances the precision of categorization and mapping tasks by incorporating spatial relationships between objects, resulting in more robust and interpretable results (Kumar et al., 2024a). Thus, OBIA is widely utilized in monitoring agriculture, planning land utilization, and monitoring natural resources, where the quality of imagery can be determined as per Fig. 4.

# Filter Methods

They are frequently employed for feature selection in RS applications because of their straightforwardness and effectiveness. These methods assess the significance of variables regardless of the selected predictive model. The correlation coefficient is a commonly employed filtering approach that quantifies the linear association between each characteristic and the target variable (Chuphal et al., 2024). Features that have high correlation coefficients are deemed to be more predictive and are kept for subsequent studies. Another illustration is the Chi-Square Test, which evaluates the lack of dependence between category characteristics and the target variable in classification tasks.
