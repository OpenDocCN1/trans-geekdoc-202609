## Types of Agro-based Waste

Agro-based wastes may be divided into three broad categories, namely crop residues, animal manures and agrochemical residues.

- 1) Crop residues: Crop residues include the stalks, leaves, husks, and roots left in the field after the harvest of crops like wheat, rice, maize, and sugarcane. According to Sharma, 2020a, farmers use 20% of rice straw and burn the other 50% for crop preparation. The availability of these residues can be substantial, for instance, rice straw constitutes about 50% of the total biomass of the rice plant (Van Hung, 2020). Improper management of crop residues, such as open burning, leads to severe air pollution, contributing to greenhouse gas emissions and particulate matter. Conversely, leaving residues on fields can improve soil organic matter and moisture retention, reducing soil erosion and improving fertility. However, through the use of AI algorithms and real-time data, innovative approaches can be developed to effectively utilize agro-based waste and crop residues, contributing to sustainable agricultural practices and establishing food security in the face of evolving environmental conditions and growing food demand (Ziesche, 2023).
- 2) Animal/livestock excreta: Animal excreta includes the faeces and urine or other bodily waste products from livestock, such as cows, pigs, and chickens. It is rich in nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, making it a valuable fertilizer. Bashir and Mohan (2023) demonstrated that sound manure management as a fertiliser can boost environmentally friendly farming and agricultural productivity. Moreover, it can be processed into biochar, a recognized method for sustainable land reclamation and enhancing plant growth on degraded soils. While manure is a potent fertilizer, improper management can lead to nutrient runoff, water pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions. ## Biogas Production

It involves the anaerobic digestion of organic waste to produce methane-rich biogas. Similar to anaerobic digestion, organic waste is placed in a digester where it undergoes microbial decomposition to release biogas, which provides a renewable energy source, decreases greenhouse gas emissions, and reduces reliance on fossil fuels.

## Incineration and Pyrolysis

Incineration is the combustion of organic waste to produce energy, while pyrolysis involves thermal decomposition in the absence of oxygen. Agro-waste is burned at high temperatures in incineration or decomposed thermally in pyrolysis units. Reduces waste volume significantly, generates energy, and can produce by-products like ash or bio-oil.

In general, the benefits and advantages of Agro-waste processing and refining methods are believed to be cost-effective and eco-friendly, as well as it could contribute positively toward more sustainable waste management and a less-carbon-intensive energy utilization path (Hoang, 2023).

## Biomass Valorization

Nzihou (2010) defined valorization as the conversion of waste and biomass to energy, fuels and other useful materials, with a particular focus on environmental indicators and sustainability goals, i.e., the process of converting Agro-waste into valuable products like biofuels, chemicals, and materials. Organic materials such as food waste, grass, and manure are being converted into VFAs, carboxylic acids, bioenergy, and bioplastics to help minimize waste and foster a circular economy (Onwucha, 2023).

## AI-based Technologies for Agro-waste Conversion

Current developments in AI-based technologies for Agro-waste conversion have shown significant potential for sustainable agriculture by improving waste management and resource efficiency (Taneja, 2023). Machine Learning Algorithms are utilized in optimizing anaerobic digestion processes, predicting compost quality, and enhancing biochar production (Cruz, 2022). It improves efficiency, reduces operational costs, and increases yield and quality of end-products. Image Processing facilitates monitoring crop residue levels, and detecting contamination in waste streams thus, enabling precise and timely interventions, reducing waste and enhancing resource recovery. Predictive Analytics are applicable in forecasting waste generation patterns, and optimizing waste collection and processing schedules and therefore, enhance planning and resource allocation, and minimize waste management costs. AI plays an important role in addressing food waste issues by monitoring and optimizing food production and supply chains, redistributing excess food, and supporting circular economy initiatives. By leveraging AI in Agro-waste conversion, the agricultural sector can reduce waste, enhance sustainability, and contribute to a more efficient and equitable food system.

## Utilization of IoT in Improving Waste Management

The utilization of IoT technology significantly enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of waste management practices in urban areas by providing real-time monitoring and data-driven insights. The Internet of Things (IoT) is being used to improve Agro-waste management (Koshyaria, 2023). Sensors facilitate monitoring temperature, moisture, and pH levels in compost piles, detecting gas emissions from manure. Provides real-time data, enhances process control, and improves safety and compliance. Smart waste bins may be used to improve waste management by providing early automatic identification of waste at the bin level (Ali, 2020). Smart bins are furnished with sensors to detect fill levels, compact waste, and signal collection needs. Reduces collection frequency and costs, and improves efficiency in waste management. Moreover, remote monitoring through tracking the performance of waste treatment facilities, and monitoring environmental impact facilitates timely maintenance, ensures regulatory compliance, and enhances decision-making.

## Comparative Economic Analysis Between Traditional and AI-based Tools

AI-based tools in Agro-waste management offer significant economic and operational advantages over traditional methods. While the initial investment is higher, the extended benefits for the long term such as reduced operational costs, increased efficiency, and lower environmental impact make AI-based tools a more sustainable and profitable choice. The ability to scale operations, adapt to market changes, and achieve higher quality outputs further strengthens the case for AI integration in Agro-waste management.
