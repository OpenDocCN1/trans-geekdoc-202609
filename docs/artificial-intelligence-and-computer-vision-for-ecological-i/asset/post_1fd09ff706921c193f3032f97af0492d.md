## The NDVI’s Power in Crop Yield Estimation

For farmers as well as other agricultural stakeholders, accurate crop yield estimation is essential. Finally, it fosters market stability, enhances the ability to make well-informed decisions, and improves global food security (Chouhan, 2021). Conventional techniques frequently fall short because they are not precise, timely, or scalable. This is where the usefulness of the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) becomes apparent.

One significant benefit of NDVI is that it’s an objective, straightforward metric. The NDVI offers a consistent method for evaluating crop growth and health since it is based on the reflectance characteristics of vegetation at particular wavelengths. Rich in chlorophyll, healthy plants reflect more near-infrared (NIR) light and absorbs more visible light. As a result, the NDVI value rises, signifying a robust association with multiple metrics gauging crop health and productivity, such as biomass, leaf area index (LAI), and ultimately, the farmer’s harvest.

NDVI has a significant advantage over other metrics, namely, remote sensing capability. Data from a variety of platforms, including handheld sensors, multispectral satellites, and unmanned aerial vehicles, can be used to compute the NDVI. Large agricultural areas can now be effectively and non-intrusively monitored thanks to this technology. Throughout the growing season, farmers can use NDVI to collect data at regular intervals (Wang, 2021). This temporal monitoring enables the tracking of crop growth patterns and the identification of places with potential changes in production. Equipped with this data, agriculturalists can adjust management strategies, such as watering or applying fertilizer, to maximize crop productivity throughout the whole area.

Moreover, NDVI works flawlessly with contemporary farming technologies. It provides useful input for AI models that estimate agricultural yield. In addition to other variables like weather patterns, soil conditions, and past yield data, AI systems are capable of analyzing (Khajuria, 2018) NDVI data. Compared to conventional techniques, our multi-modal approach greatly increases accuracy and refines yield estimates. NDVI also promotes a standardized approach. NDVI makes it easier to compare crops across various fields, farms, and even geographical areas by offering a standardized indicator of crop health. In addition to empowering individual farmers, this encourages cooperation and knowledge exchange among agricultural researchers, which eventually advances the field.

## NDVI’s Limitations on Crop Yield Estimation

Even though NDVI has many advantages, it’s critical to recognize its drawbacks. A significant obstacle is its susceptibility to outside influences as depicted in Fig. 5. NDVI values can be influenced by soil moisture, background soil colour, and even cloud cover, which could result in yield projections that are off. For example, an area with dry soil may have a lower NDVI value, which could be an indication of poor crop health when there may be a water shortage.

## Calculation of Vegetation Index

Initially, a combination of visible and near-infrared wavelengths was used to calculate various ratios and normalized indices as depicted in Equation 1.1. NDVI values span from +1 (strongest vegetative growth) to −1 (typically water). The NR (λ = 700 – 1300 nm) and VS (λ = 550 – 700 nm). The optical properties of the cellular structure and air-cell wall of leaf tissues determine their reflectance levels. Air cell wall contacts between the chloroplasts and the protoplasm.

NDVI = (NR − VS) / (NR + VS) (1.1)

whereas,

- NDVI stands for Normalized Difference Vegetation Index
- NR - Near Infrared Radiation
- VS - Visible Red Spectrum

This formula states that the difference in the red and infrared range’s reflected light intensities divided by the sum of these intensities equals the density of vegetation (NDVI) at a specific place in the image.
