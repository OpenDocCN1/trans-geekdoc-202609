## Introduction

In the twenty-first century, agricultural production is confronted with numerous difficulties. An expanding world population needs more food to be produced with as little negative influence on the environment as possible. To do this, sustainable agricultural methods are essential. When combined with machine learning approaches, remote sensing provides a potent means of maximizing agricultural yield and financial gains (Lin et al., 2021). Farmers have historically made decisions regarding crop health, irrigation, and resource allocation based on field observations and experience. These techniques, however, are frequently laborious, subjective, and have a constrained scope in terms of both time and space. For getting beyond these restrictions, remote sensing offers a useful tool. It serves as a way of learning about an object or part of the Earth’s surface without embarking there in person (Bharadiya et al., 2023; Sharma et al., 2020). This information is collected by sensors mounted on satellites, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV), or ground-based platforms. Sensors detect and record electromagnetic radiation emitted or reflected from the Earth’s surface, providing valuable insights into various agricultural parameters (Martos et al., 2021).

Different types of remote sensing data are available, each with its advantages and limitations. Satellite imagery offers wide-area coverage at regular intervals, making it suitable for regional and national-level monitoring. UAVs, also known as drones, provide high-resolution images and can be deployed on-demand for targeted monitoring of specific fields. Ground-based sensors provide continuous data collection for highly localized areas (Diaz-Gonzalez et al., 2022). Hyperspectral imaging captures data across a large number of spectral bands, allowing for detailed analysis of crop health and nutrient status. To utilize remote sensing data effectively, data acquisition and preprocessing are crucial steps. Radiometric calibration compensates for differences in sensor response, whereas geometric correction provides precise spatial representation. Data fusion combines information from multiple sensors or sources to generate a more comprehensive view of the agricultural landscape (Weiss et al., 2020; Salaria and Suresh, 1990).

Once the data is prepared, image classification techniques are employed to extract meaningful information. Supervised classification uses labelled training data to categorize pixels into specific classes, such as different crop types, soil types, or areas of water stress. Unsupervised classification identifies natural groupings within the data without predefined labels. Object-based classification analyzes groups of pixels representing specific things, such as trees or crop rows, for a more comprehensive description (Benami et al., 2021; Saxena et al., 2024). By leveraging machine learning algorithms, remote sensing data can be used for a variety of applications in crop monitoring and management. Crop type classification enables farmers to map their fields and track changes in planting patterns over time. Crop health monitoring allows for early detection of stress caused by nutrient deficiencies, pests, or diseases, facilitating timely interventions. Remote sensing data also plays a vital role in detecting and mapping the spread of pests and diseases, enabling targeted application of pesticides and minimizing environmental impact (Tiwari et al., 2024; Li et al., 2022).

Yield prediction models are another key application of remote sensing in agriculture. Machine learning algorithms can analyze historical yield data along with remote sensing data on crop health, weather conditions, and soil moisture to predict future crop yields. This information empowers farmers to make informed decisions about resource allocation, fertilizer application, and harvest planning, potentially increasing overall yield and economic returns. Water management and irrigation optimization are critical aspects of sustainable agriculture (Benos et al., 2021; Solanki et al., 2024). Remote sensing data helps estimate soil moisture content, enabling farmers to identify areas requiring irrigation and optimize water use efficiency. By monitoring water stress levels in crops, farmers can schedule irrigation more effectively, reducing water waste and conserving precious resources (Sishodia et al., 2020).

Soil mapping and fertility assessment are crucial for targeted nutrient management. Remote sensing data enables the identification and mapping of different soil types across a field. By analyzing spectral reflectance patterns, it is possible to assess soil fertility and identify areas deficient in specific nutrients.
