# Image Classification Techniques

Using remote sensing data to optimize agricultural practices requires an understanding of the nuances of picture categorization algorithms, which we explore in this section. Image classification serves as a cornerstone in the analysis of remotely sensed data, facilitating the identification and categorization of various features within the imagery. This section delineates three primary methodologies employed in image classification: Supervised classification, Unsupervised classification, and Object-based classification. Each technique offers distinct advantages and is tailored to specific contexts, contributing significantly to the enhancement of agricultural practices through the utilization of remote sensing data.

# *Supervised Classification*

Supervised classification stands as a fundamental approach in image analysis, relying on a priori knowledge of spectral signatures to categorize pixels within an image. The process entails the utilization of training data, wherein samples representing different land cover classes are identified and associated with their corresponding spectral characteristics. These spectral signatures serve as reference points for the classification algorithm, enabling it to assign pixels in the image to the predefined classes based on spectral similarity (Delgado et al., 2019). The workflow of supervised classification encompasses several key stages, commencing with the selection and acquisition of training data representative of the various land cover classes under consideration. This step necessitates meticulous attention to ensure the adequacy and representativeness of the training samples, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of the classification outcomes. Subsequently, feature extraction techniques may be employed to extract pertinent information from the spectral bands, facilitating the discrimination of distinct land cover classes (Nay et al., 2018).

After obtaining training data, supervised learning approaches are employed to train a classification algorithm, which teaches it to distinguish between various land cover classes according to their spectral signatures.
