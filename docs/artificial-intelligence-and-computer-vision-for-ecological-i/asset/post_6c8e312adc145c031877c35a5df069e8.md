India, a country with an extensive record of agriculture and a wide variety of farming landscapes, is always faced with the challenge of providing food security for its ever-expanding population. In addition to boosting overall food production, feeding this expanding population will need effective management of the agricultural resources that are in existence. Navigating this difficulty requires accurate crop yield estimation (Yu, 2021). The amount of harvested produce, such as grains, per unit of land area is referred to as crop yield. Historically, India has assessed crop yields using self-reported data from farmers or manually conducted field surveys, as indicated in Fig. 1. Government organizations (Zhang, 2022). To establish marketing tactics. Furthermore, timely shortages or surpluses might avoid market disruptions through effective estimations and predictions (Javed, 2022). ## Estimating Crop Yield: A Fundamental View

Precise assessment of crop production is essential for many farming techniques. It determines the optimal amount of seeds to plant, how much fertilizer to apply, and how much watering is required. Yield projections have an impact on market pricing tactics as well, which helps to minimize shortage risks. It also makes proactive risk management strategies against illnesses, pest infestations, and unfavourable weather possible (Zheng, 2018). Traditional ground-based surveys require a lot of time and effort, and can involve human error especially when dealing with big fields but satellite imagery has drawbacks such as spectral resolution limitations, reliance on weather, and is expensive for frequent monitoring.
