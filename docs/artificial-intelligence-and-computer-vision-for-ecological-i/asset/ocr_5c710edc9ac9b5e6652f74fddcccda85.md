# Soil Moisture Sensors and their Application in Agriculture

Soil water sensors (SWS) are among the most effective tools for implementing scientific irrigation scheduling (Brant et al., 2004). Accurate monitoring of soil water content is critical for precision agriculture, enabling optimized productivity, resource conservation, and environmental protection (Saleem et al., 2024). SWS estimates water availability through various methods, providing essential data for irrigation management, drought monitoring, and soil health assessment. In recent decades, a plethora of techniques have emerged to estimate volumetric soil water content (θv). These include direct methods like gravimetry and volumetry, while in indirect methods such as neutron scattering, tensiometers, frequency domain reflectometry (FDR) sensors, resistive sensors, time-domain reflectometry (TDR), and capacitance-type devices (Umarfarooque et al., 2011). These devices offer precise measurements of soil moisture levels, thereby supporting effective water management strategies. Soil moisture sensor placement and the optimum number of sensors are also important actors that significantly affect sensor-based surface irrigation automation, performance and cost-effectiveness. This section focuses on different types of soil moisture sensors, their principles of working and their respective strengths and limitations.

Gaikwad (2015) describes a variety of soil moisture sensors, each with its own set of characteristics and applications. The study introduced a potentiometric sensor that is affordable, lightweight, and probe size independent, making it ideal for on-site investigation. Kojima et al. (2016), developed a low-cost soil moisture profile probe utilizing thin-film capacitors and a capacitive touch sensor. Soil moisture content was measured through the capacitance changes in the capacitors, constructed from electrode probes serving as electrode plates. Yamamoto et al. (2002), presented sensors that utilize the thermal characteristics of soil and water, offering high sensitivity in low moisture concentrations and minimal ion effects. You et al. (2013) developed a handheld microwave reflectometer-sensor system for precise moisture monitoring in various soil types. Placidi (2020) focused on the experimental characterization of a low-cost capacitive soil moisture sensor, demonstrating its reliability in determining soil moisture for IoT applications. These sensors collectively provide a range of options for soil moisture analysis, each with its own strengths and potential applications. Different types of soil moisture sensors are discussed below.
