+   5. *Real-Time Prediction*: Once validated, the model is deployed for real-time yield prediction. New remote sensing data collected during the season is fed into the model to generate updated yield forecasts (Ruan et al., 2022).

# ## Challenges and Limitations

Despite their promising capabilities, yield prediction models encounter many challenges and limitations that merit careful consideration. Below, we explore these factors in detail, shedding light on the complexities inherent in implementing such models.

# ### ***Data Quality and Availability***

+   - One of the foremost challenges in developing accurate yield prediction models is the quality and availability of data. Remote sensing data, while abundant, may vary in resolution, accuracy, and consistency, posing significant challenges for model training and validation.
+   - Furthermore, accessing ground truth data for model calibration and validation purposes can be arduous, especially in remote or resource-constrained regions. Discrepancies between ground truth data and remote sensing observations can undermine the reliability of yield predictions.

# ### ***Spatial and Temporal Variability***

+   - Agricultural landscapes exhibit considerable spatial and temporal variability, influenced by factors such as soil composition, weather patterns, and agronomic practices. Capturing this variability accurately is essential for robust yield predictions.
+   - However, modelling such variability poses significant challenges, as it requires sophisticated algorithms capable of integrating heterogeneous data sources and accounting for spatial and temporal dynamics effectively (Muniasamy, 2020).

# ### ***Model Complexity and Interpretability***

+   - As yield prediction models become increasingly sophisticated, they also tend to grow in complexity, incorporating numerous variables and intricate algorithms. While such complexity enhances predictive accuracy, it may hinder model interpretability and transparency.
+   - Farmers and stakeholders often require interpretable models that elucidate the underlying factors driving yield predictions. Balancing model complexity with interpretability is thus a critical consideration in model development.

# ### ***Transferability and Generalization***

+   - Deploying yield prediction models across diverse geographical regions and cropping systems necessitates robust transferability and generalization
