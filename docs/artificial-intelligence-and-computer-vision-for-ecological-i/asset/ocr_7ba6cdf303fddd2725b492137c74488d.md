Over-application of fertilizers and pesticides is a common issue in traditional farming practices, leading to runoff that contaminates water bodies and harms ecosystems. AI and ML models can analyze past data and real-time conditions to forecast the exact needs of crops, thereby preventing the unnecessary application of inputs. This targeted approach reduces the volume of agricultural chemicals released into the environment, leading to a significant decrease in pollution and soil degradation. Moreover, the enhanced efficiency in water usage contributes to the conservation of water resources, a critical consideration in regions facing water scarcity (Renda, 2024). The reduction in waste not only has environmental benefits but also translates into economic gains for farmers. By decreasing the amount of wasted inputs, farmers can lower their operational costs. Studies have shown that implementing AI-driven precision agriculture can reduce input costs by up to 20%, providing significant savings over time (Mittal et al., 2020).

# Cost Savings for Farmers

The financial implications of AI and ML in crop yield estimation extends beyond resource management to encompass substantial cost savings for farmers. The predictive capabilities of AI and ML enable growers to make informed decisions that improve yield and profitability. For example, by accurately forecasting crop yields, farmers can better plan their embedding and harvesting schedules, align their supply chain logistics, and reduce the risk of crop failure. This predictive insight helps in mitigating financial losses caused by unforeseen weather events or pest infestations. Additionally, the optimization of input usage—fertilizers, pesticides, and water—directly correlates with reduced expenditure on these resources. The integration of AI and ML also facilitates precision farming techniques that lower labour costs. Automation of routine tasks, such as monitoring crop health and applying treatments, reduces the need for manual labour. This not only cuts down labour costs but also allows farmers to allocate human resources to more strategic activities, further enhancing farm productivity (Piekarskim and de Francisco, 2020).

# Increased Crop Yields and Market Opportunities

Early Detection of Stress Factors and Disease Outbreaks The amalgamation of AI and ML technologies in crop yield estimation offers substantial economic benefits by enabling the early recognition of stress factors and disease outbreaks. Through sophisticated algorithms and data analysis, AI and ML systems can monitor vast agricultural datasets, including soil conditions, weather patterns, and crop health indicators, in real-time. These technologies facilitate the identification of anomalies or patterns that signal the onset of stress factors such as nutrient deficiencies, water scarcity, or pest infestations. Early detection allows farmers to implement timely interventions, thereby mitigating potential yield losses. For instance, AI-powered imaging systems can detect minute changes in leaf coloration or texture that may indicate disease presence long before it becomes visible to the naked eye (Coble et al., 2018). By addressing these issues promptly, farmers can avoid significant crop damage, ensuring a higher yield and reducing the economic impact of potential crop failures.
