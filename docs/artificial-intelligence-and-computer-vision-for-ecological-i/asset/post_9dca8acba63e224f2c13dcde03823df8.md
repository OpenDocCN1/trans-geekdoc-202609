## Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI is a crucial financial indicator that's used to assess how profitable an investment is. From the perspective of AI and ML in agriculture, ROI measures the financial gains obtained from these technologies relative to their costs. The calculation of ROI helps farmers and agricultural stakeholders to determine whether the implementation of AI and ML technologies in crop yield estimation is financially viable.

ROI is defined as the ratio of net profit to the entire cost of the investment. The formula for calculating ROI is:

$$ROI = \left(\frac{Net\ profit}{Cost\ of\ investment}\right) \times 100 \tag{1}$$

where:

- *Net Profit* is the difference between the gross revenue generated from the investment and the total cost of the investment.
- *Cost of Investment* includes all expenses incurred in the adoption and implementation of AI and ML technologies.

## Factors Influencing ROI in Agriculture

Many factors influence the ROI of AI and ML technologies in agriculture:

- *Initial Investment Costs*: These include the cost of technology acquisition, installation, and training.
- *Operational Costs*: These encompass maintenance, data management, and system upgrades.
- *Increased Yield*: AI and ML can optimize planting schedules, irrigation, and pest control, leading to higher crop productivity.
- *Resource Efficiency*: Reduction in the use of water, fertilizers, and pesticides due to precise application methods.
- *Market Prices*: Fluctuations in the prices of crops can affect the gross revenue, thus impacting the ROI.
- *Scalability*: The extent to which these technologies can be scaled to cover larger areas or different types of crops (Attri et al., 2023).

## Potential Economic Benefits

The adoption of AI and ML in agriculture can lead to several economic benefits:

- *Enhanced Productivity*: AI-driven insights can optimize various farming practices, leading to increased productivity and higher yields.
- *Cost Savings*: Enhanced resource management can result in significant cost savings in inputs such as water, fertilizers, and pesticides.
- *Risk Mitigation*: Predictive analytics can help in early detection of crop diseases and pest infestations, reducing potential losses.
- *Market Competitiveness*: Farmers leveraging advanced technologies can be more competitive in the market due to higher quality and more consistent yields.

## Economic Viability of AI and ML Technologies

The economic viability of AI and ML technologies in agriculture depends on their ability to generate sustainable economic benefits while being affordable and accessible to farmers. Several factors contribute to this viability:

- 1. *Affordability:*
  - *Cost of Technology:* The upfront and recurring costs of AI and ML technologies must be within the financial reach of farmers. This includes costs associated with hardware, software, data acquisition, and maintenance.
  - *Financing Options:* Availability of favourable financing options, such as loans, grants, and subsidies, can make these technologies more affordable for small and medium-sized farmers.
- 2. *Scalability and Adaptability:*
  - *Customizability:* AI and ML solutions should be adaptable to different types of crops, farming practices, and climatic conditions. Customizable solutions ensure broader applicability and effectiveness.
  - *Scalability:* Technologies must be scalable to accommodate varying sizes of farming operations. Solutions that work for both small-scale and large-scale farms enhance economic viability.
