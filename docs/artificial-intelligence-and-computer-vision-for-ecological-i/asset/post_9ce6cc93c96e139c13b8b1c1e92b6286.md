## CV Techniques for Image-Based Analysis

For the purpose to analyze image-based data for the purpose to assess water quality, CV techniques are essential. With the aim to extract valuable information from visual data, such as satellite imagery, drone footage, and underwater camera feeds, CV uses cutting-edge image processing and deep learning techniques. These methods provide very accurate and efficient methods for the detection, categorization, and monitoring of several indicators of water quality.

The analysis of image-based data in the assessment of water quality depends heavily on CV algorithms. Advanced image processing and deep learning techniques are utilized by CV to derive significant information from visual data, including underwater video feeds, drone footage, and satellite imagery. These methods allow for the very accurate and efficient detection, categorization, and monitoring of a variety of water quality indicators.

To detect certain pollutants and occurrences in water bodies, object detection and classification are essential. Using labelled datasets, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and other deep learning models are trained to identify patterns and categorise things, such as invasive aquatic species, plastic garbage, and oil spills. With their ability to handle massive amounts of image data quickly, these models can offer real-time insights on the distribution and presence of pollutants (Chhipi-Shrestha et al., 2023; Jadhav et al., 2023; Taşan, 2023; Chouhan et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2024).

Additionally, CV approaches make it possible to track how factors related to water quality vary over time. Through the examination of image sequences, CV systems can identify changes in features such as color, turbidity, and the size of algal blooms over time. Understanding the evolution of water quality problems and assessing the success of management actions require this temporal study. Thus, the use of AI and CV for water quality evaluation improves monitoring efforts' precision, scalability, and promptness, leading to better-informed and successful environmental management plans.

## Case Studies and Applications

### Bearing an Eye on Coastal Water Algal Blooms

The monitoring of harmful algal blooms (HABs) in coastal waters is one of the most important uses of AI and computer vision (CV) technology in the assessment of water quality. Because HABs can create poisons that contaminate seafood and drinking water, they pose serious dangers to human health, the local economy, and marine ecosystems. Using AI models, in situ sensor data and satellite pictures were combined in a case study throughout the Gulf of Mexico to identify and forecast HAB occurrences. High-resolution satellite photos were analyzed using deep learning methods, namely convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to look for indicators of algal blooms, such as changes in water color and chlorophyll concentrations. The AI models were able to recognize the temporal and spatial patterns connected to HAB occurrences since they had been trained on historical bloom data. Early warning capabilities offered by this strategy made it possible to promptly issue public health alerts and take mitigation measures. The accomplishment of this case study demonstrates how AI and CV technologies can improve coastal water quality monitoring and management, protecting both human communities and marine life.

### Machine Learning for River Pollution Prediction

Machine learning models were created to forecast pollution levels based on different environmental and anthropogenic parameters in a case study that focused on the Ganges River in India. One of the world's most polluted rivers, the Ganges, is hampered by home sewage, agricultural runoff, and industrial discharge. Machine learning models were trained using data from many sources, such as weather stations, remote sensing, and manual water sampling. The data was analyzed using supervised learning techniques, such as support vector machines (SVM) and random forests, to forecast the quantities of important contaminants, such as heavy metals, nitrates, and biological oxygen demand (BOD). Significant indicators of pollution levels, including population density, industrial activity, and rainfall patterns, were identified by the models. The identification of important pollution hotspots and the streamlining of remediation activities were made possible by these predictive capabilities, which also made it possible to implement more effective pollution management techniques. This case study's incorporation of machine learning showed how it may improve the efficiency and predicted accuracy of water quality tests in vast, intricate river systems.

### Monitoring Urban Water Bodies in Real-Time with CV

A case study was carried out in an urban environment to use Computer Vision (CV) technology to monitor the water quality of lakes and reservoirs. Pollution from stormwater runoff, industrial discharges, and recreational activities is a common problem for urban aquatic bodies. Throughout many urban lakes, a network of smart cameras and drones with high-resolution imaging capabilities was set up for this study. To process the visual data that these devices collected, CV algorithms were created with an emphasis on surface litter, turbidity, and the presence of algae. Specific contaminants, such as plastic garbage and oil spills, were recognized by object identification models, while areas of interest were isolated using image segmentation techniques. Due to the real-time analysis that CV systems provide, local officials can now continuously monitor water quality and react quickly to pollution situations. Furthermore, the information gathered helped create long-term strategies for improving the quality of the water, proving the usefulness of CV in managing the water quality of cities.

### Improving Agricultural Areas’ Water Quality

A significant contributor to water pollution is agricultural runoff, which enters neighboring water bodies and introduces sediments, fertilizers, and pesticides. A case study examined the use of AI and CV technologies to monitor and manage water quality in streams and rivers affected by farming activities in a Midwest agricultural region of the United States. A comprehensive monitoring system was created by combining data from ground-based sensors with remote sensing data from drones and satellites. To forecast nutrient levels (such as nitrogen and phosphorus) in water bodies based on meteorological conditions, soil types, and agricultural activities, artificial intelligence models—such as neural networks and regression algorithms—were employed. Drone imagery was analyzed using CV techniques to determine the amount of stream silt deposition and erosion. By using an integrated approach, farmers and water managers were able to reduce nitrogen runoff and enhance water quality by using targeted best management practices (BMPs) including buffer strips and cover crops. This case study demonstrated how AI and CV may support environmentally friendly farming methods and safeguard rural communities’ water supplies.

### Automated Ocean Microplastic Detection

Microplastics represent a growing threat to human health and aquatic life as they contribute to marine contamination. A case study that used CV technology to automatically detect and quantify microplastics in ocean waters was presented. The manual collection and microscopic inspection of samples are involved in the labor-intensive and time-consuming traditional methods of microplastic analysis. Underwater robots fitted with cutting-edge imaging equipment were used in this investigation to gather visual data at different depths.
