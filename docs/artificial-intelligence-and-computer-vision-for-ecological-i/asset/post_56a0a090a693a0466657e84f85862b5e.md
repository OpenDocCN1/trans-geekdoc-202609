## Conclusion

Sustainable agriculture necessitates the integration of economic sustainability with technological advancements, particularly through AI and ML in crop yield estimation. Traditional approaches have often fallen short, constrained by limited accuracy and the inability to swiftly adapt to environmental changes. Data from the Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO), a United Nations agency, indicates that global agricultural productivity needs to grow to 70% by 2050 to meet the food demands of the growing population. AI and ML offer a transformative solution, providing precise yield predictions and facilitating more efficient resource management. A study by McKinsey estimates that AI-driven agriculture could increase global crop yields by up to 30%, resulting in significant economic benefits. The economic framework of sustainable agriculture is fundamentally enhanced by AI and ML, reducing costs through targeted interventions and minimizing resource wastage. The World Bank reports that precision agriculture can reduce water use by up to 30% and fertilizer use by 20%, leading to substantial cost savings and environmental benefits. These technologies also enhance market predictability and risk management, contributing to increased farm incomes and economic resilience. Furthermore, the capacity of AI and ML to analyze vast datasets ensures early detection of stress factors and disease outbreaks, allowing proactive measures that improve crop health and yield. The ROI for AI and ML in agriculture is compelling. A PwC analysis suggests that AI could contribute up to $15.7 trillion to the global economy by 2030, with agriculture being a significant beneficiary. The adoption of AI and ML in crop yield estimation is pivotal for achieving economic sustainability in agriculture. By improving efficiency, enhancing yields, and reducing environmental impact, these technologies offer a path towards a more resilient and prosperous agricultural sector.
