Remote sensing data can be used to classify weed species and map their distribution across a field. This information facilitates the development of precision herbicide application strategies, targeting only areas with weeds and minimizing herbicide use (Chlingaryan et al., 2018).

Economic analysis and decision support systems are essential for translating the benefits of remote sensing into tangible gains for farmers. Cost-benefit analyzes assess the economic viability of adopting remote sensing technologies, considering factors like initial investment, operational costs, and potential yield improvements. Economic impact assessments evaluate the broader economic benefits of remote sensing for agriculture, such as increased food production and improved resource efficiency (Khanal et al., 2018; Meraj et al., 2022). Finally, the development of decision support systems integrates remote sensing data with other relevant information sources to provide farmers with user-friendly tools for making informed decisions about their agricultural practices. Looking towards the future, emerging technologies like advanced sensors with higher resolution and wider spectral coverage hold immense promise for even more refined data collection (Khanal et al., 2020). The integration of AI and computer vision with remote sensing data analysis will further enhance the capabilities of these technologies.

However, addressing challenges like scalability, data accessibility for small-scale farmers, and ensuring ethical data practices remain crucial for maximizing the positive impact of remote sensing on sustainable agriculture. By overcoming these hurdles, remote sensing and machine learning have the potential to revolutionize agricultural practices, leading to a future of increased food production, improved economic returns for farmers, and a more sustainable approach to agriculture that safeguards our planet for generations to come (Elavarasan and Vincent, 2020; Bali et al., 2023). It first explains the fundamental principles and various technologies used in remote sensing for agricultural purposes. Next, it outlines the essential workflow for agricultural remote sensing, including data acquisition, preprocessing, and image classification techniques. Finally, the chapter explores how machine learning algorithms can be used to predict crop yield, optimize water management, and assess the economic viability of agricultural practices (Tiwari and Mishra, 2024; Solanki et al., 2023).
