+   - Swath Width: 880 km.
+   - Polarization: H & V.
+   - Temporal Resolution: 3 days.

# **7. Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP):**

+   - Launch Date: 2015.
+   - Frequency: 1.41 GHz.
+   - Swath Width: 1,000 km.
+   - Polarization: H & V.
+   - Temporal Resolution: 4 days.

The evolution of these sensors highlights advancements in satellite remote sensing technology for SM monitoring. The increased precision in frequency, wider swath widths, and improved temporal resolutions have enhanced the accuracy of SM data, supporting large-scale hydrological and agricultural applications (Das et al., 2015).

# **Airborne Sensing Platforms for Assessment of SM**

Airborne remote sensing platforms play a crucial role in SM assessment, offering the capability to collect data over large areas quickly and efficiently. These platforms utilize various sensors and instruments capable of measuring electromagnetic radiation across different wavelengths, enabling them to capture detailed information about SM content. Mentioned below is description of various remote sensing instruments used for Earth observation, highlighting their operational frequencies and primary usage in environmental monitoring and research.

+   (a) The Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer 2 (AMSR-2) is a passive remote sensing instrument on the GCOM-W1 satellite that drives at frequencies ranging between 6.9–89 GHz. It is primarily used to measure precipitation, concentration of sea ice, and sea surface temperatures.
+   
+   (b) The Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer–EO System (AMSR-E), a microwave radiometer mounted on the Aqua satellite that operates at frequencies between 6.9–89 GHz and is used to measure SM, sea surface temperatures, and precipitation.
+   
+   (c) Aquarius is a joint active and passive L-band microwave radiometer on the Aquarius satellite that operates at frequencies of 1.26 and 1.41 GHz. Aquarius primarily measures ocean surface salinity as well as sea surface temperature and wind speed.
+   
+   (d) Advanced Synthetic Aperture Radar (ASAR) is an active radar instrument on the ENVISAT satellite that operates at a frequency of 5.33 GHz. ASAR is used to measure sea and land surface features, such as sea ice cover and land deformation.
