An ANN and fuzzy logic are combined in the FNN, a hybrid neural network model, to handle unclear or uncertain scenarios. After processing input data using fuzzy logic reasoning, an ANN is used by the FNN to train and generate the output. Though fuzzy logic is used to establish the fuzzy relationship between inputs and outputs and the weights of connections among neurons (normalisation, fuzzy inference, and membership function), the topology of an FNN is similar to that of a traditional neural network. An input, membership function, fuzzy inference, normalisation, and output layers make up the FNN architecture. When handling difficult situations, FNN provides a great deal of advantages over ordinary NNs and is widely used in pattern recognition and control (Mahesh et al., 2020).

A DNN is an ANN type that has several hidden layers positioned in between the input and output layers. With the help of successive layers that combine lower-level characteristics to learn higher-level features, the deep architecture of DNN enables it to learn hierarchical data representations. Just like other neural networks, DNNs can include more neurons and hidden layers. They are frequently used to learn highly nonlinear mappings from inputs to outputs or to capture complicated patterns in data. However, because of their complex network design and the need for a substantial quantity of data for training, DNNs may be difficult and computationally costly to train (Yadav et al., 2020).

Convolutional computations and a deep structure feedforward neural network are two components of CNNs, a sort of deep learning technology. Natural language processing, computer vision, and other domains all make extensive use of CNNs. CNNs use fully connected layers for classification or regression tasks, pooling layers to lower the dimensionality of features, and convolutional layers to extract complex features from input pictures (Alzubaidi et al., 2021). Input, output, convolutional, pooling, and fully connected layers are common components of a CNN design.

## Optimization of Treatment Processes Using AI

The treatment of wastewater is important to know and understand for further enhancement of the process. Before wastewater from commercial, industrial, and municipal sources is released into natural water bodies, it must first be treated (WWT) to remove carbonaceous contaminants (such as organic carbon compounds) and nutrients (such as phosphorus and nitrogen species) (Russell, 2019). By lowering the dissolved oxygen content in surface water courses such as rivers and lakes, carbonaceous pollutants can harm the ecosystem (Jin et al., 2014). The aquatic life is at risk due to nutrient-induced eutrophication and algal bloom production in surface water (Elsayed et al., 2023a; Yaqub et al., 2020). Before being released into a river or another body of water for recycling (such as recycled water or consumption in agriculture), wastewater treatment facilities (WWTP) filter wastewater collected from cities, homes, companies, and other sources (Corominas et al., 2018). Water plants are intricate systems that use cutting-edge network technology to solve operational issues. Large linked tunnels are frequently used in WWTPs (particularly in populated regions) to store sanitary and precipitation-related flows for processing (Owolabi et al., 2022). Decisions about pumping stored wastewater from tunnels must be taken quickly at WWTPs because the wastewater cannot exceed the acceptable levels in the tunnel, which could result in an overflow of untreated water (Corominas et al., 2018; Schutze et al., 2002) or excessive chemical use. For this reason, computation time is a crucial concern (Schütze et al., 2002).

## Research and Innovation

There is significant potential for research and innovation in the field of AI-enabled wastewater treatment. Advances in machine learning algorithms, particularly in handling complex and non-linear data, can lead to more accurate and efficient treatment processes. Innovations in sensor technologies and IoT can enhance data collection, providing the rich datasets needed for training AI models. Collaborative research initiatives between academic institutions, industry, and government agencies can drive the development of new AI applications tailored specifically for wastewater treatment.

Collaboration across sectors is crucial for overcoming the barriers to AI adoption. Partnerships between technology companies and wastewater treatment facilities can facilitate the transfer of knowledge and expertise. Collaborative efforts can also help in developing standardized protocols for data collection and processing, ensuring that AI models are trained on consistent and high-quality data. Additionally, collaboration with regulatory bodies can aid in creating a supportive regulatory environment that encourages innovation while ensuring safety and compliance.

## Conclusion

AI technologies hold significant promise for revolutionizing wastewater treatment processes. Despite the challenges, the opportunities for innovation, research, and collaboration are vast. The integration of AI can lead to more efficient, reliable, and sustainable wastewater treatment systems, transforming waste into valuable resources and paving the way for a cleaner, healthier environment. As the field continues to evolve, ongoing research and cross-sector collaboration will be key to unlocking the full potential of AI in wastewater treatment.

## Future Directions

Looking ahead, the future of AI in wastewater treatment is promising. AI has the potential to transform wastewater treatment into a resource recovery process, where waste materials are efficiently converted into valuable resources. Emerging trends such as the integration of AI with blockchain technology for secure and transparent data management, and the use of AI-driven predictive maintenance systems to optimize the performance of treatment plants, are areas ripe for exploration. Continuous advancements in AI and related technologies will likely lead to more sustainable and cost-effective wastewater treatment solutions, ultimately contributing to environmental conservation and public health.
