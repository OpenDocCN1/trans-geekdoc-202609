## Robotics in Plant Growth Mapping
Plant growth mapping is revolutionized by robotics since it automates observations, data collecting, and interventions. While fixed robots continuously monitor particular regions, mobile robots with cameras and sensors scan fields or greenhouses to collect detailed data on plant health and environmental conditions. In precision agriculture, robots utilize real-time data to administer pesticides, fertilizers, and water with extreme accuracy. Additionally, robots with vision capabilities suppress weeds and other pests, minimizing the need for chemical applications. To evaluate plant health and identify illnesses, they take high-resolution pictures and employ multispectral and hyperspectral imaging. Robots detect developmental changes over time and assess growth indicators for plant phenotyping. This automation boosts productivity, decreases human work, and may be expanded to accommodate different types of agricultural activities. Robots with sensors to improve growth conditions improve environmental monitoring.

## Image Preprocessing
Processing the acquired pictures is essential at the first stage of image collection to resolve several difficulties, including noise, blur, poor lighting, and undesired backdrops. This pretreatment stage makes sure that the raw data is transformed into a format that can be used and that any distortions are removed, preparing it for automated illness classification techniques. Following that, the photos go through crucial feature extraction and segmentation processes.

Preprocessing preserves consistency in image resolution while optimizing the use of computational resources. Standardization, color scale modification, distortion removal, regularization of picture size, and noise reduction are important preprocessing techniques. Images are frequently resized to suit a predetermined color space and scaled to certain proportions for the best analysis. Utilizing a white background can improve visual clarity, according to studies.

In agricultural research, the HSV (Hue, Saturation, and Value) approach is a common preprocessing technique that closely resembles human visual capabilities. Techniques for masking and removing backgrounds are widely utilized to increase processing accuracy and efficiency. Colored photos are frequently converted into the HSI (Hue, Saturation, and Intensity) color space, with the H component being especially helpful for additional analysis (Barbedo, 2013).

High-pass filters are used to emphasize regions with noticeable intensity gradients, whereas low-pass filters are used to minimize high-frequency noise. The Fast Fourier Transform technique converts pictures into the spatial frequency domain, while the Laplacian filter enhances the sharpness of image outlines. Histograms are utilized to alter the power distribution and enhance image quality, while Gaussian smoothing filters are utilized to lessen noise.

## Image Segmentation
A key tool in agricultural research is segmentation, which is breaking a picture up into its constituent parts so that each item can be examined more closely and valuable characteristics may be extracted. This procedure aids in identifying a plant’s afflicted and unaffected areas. To extract potentially useful characteristics, it is essential to segment preprocessed pictures to categorize sick leaves.

Traditional techniques for segmentation include clustering, region-based, edge detection, and thresholding. Thresholding is often used in remote sensing, detection, and classification to segment pictures depending on pixel intensity levels. Global, variable, and adaptive thresholding are some techniques it uses. Using methods like the Sobel operator, Canny edge detector, and Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) filter, edge detection divides a picture according to its edges. By applying techniques like Region Growing and Region Splitting, region-based segmentation separates the picture into areas according to pixel similarities in intensity, color, and form. Using common algorithms like K-means and fuzzy C-means, clustering groups’ pixels based on texture, color, or other attributes (Yang et al., 2023).

However, complex photos with precise features are typically a challenge for older approaches. Segmentation techniques based on deep learning have been proven to be more effective than conventional approaches. By categorizing every pixel in the image, semantic segmentation separates the image into useful areas. CNN and other deep learning methods improve the accuracy of segmentation. A sophisticated type of semantic segmentation, instance segmentation uses picture recognition to predict instances of object classes, enabling it to tackle complicated jobs. It is standard practice to employ methods like Tensor Mask, Deep Mask, RCNN, YOLO, and Instance Cut. Each approach has its own advantages and limitations, making the choice dependent on the specific application requirements.

### Automated Tomato Disease Detection
Scientifically named Solanum Lycopersicon, tomatoes are an important agricultural commodity that is cultivated widely in Asia and around the world because of their high nutritional content, which includes high quantities of potassium, beta-carotene, and vitamins E and C. Tomatoes, although quite popular, are extremely susceptible to a wide range of diseases brought on by bacteria, microorganisms, and pests. Therefore, to guarantee crop health and output, automated disease detection has emerged as a crucial field of study. Various approaches have been investigated for this reason. With the help of the Plant Village dataset, Francis and Deisy (2019) created a CNN model with four convolutional layers and two dense layers. The model demonstrated an astounding 87% accuracy rate in differentiating between healthy and diseased tomato and apple leaves. Similarly to this, Basavaiah and Anthony (2020) used texture, color, and form variables from 200 photos to evaluate several machine learning techniques. The Random Forest model demonstrated a 94% accuracy rate. Probabilistic neural networks (PNN) and KNN were used by K and Rao (2019) to identify and classify illnesses from 600 photos, including Verticillium wilt, powdery mildew, and Septoria leaf spot. PNN outperformed KNN with an accuracy of 91.88%. Vadivel and Suguna (2022) employed the BPNN approach on 10,000 augmented photos and used the K-means method for feature extraction, resulting in an astounding 99.4% accuracy. By fine-tuning ResNet and Xception models on 4281 pictures, Chakravarthy and Raman (2020) achieved an amazing 99.95% accuracy in identifying early blight disease. In their analysis of many CNN architectures using 14,903 pictures of ten distinct tomato illnesses from the PlantVillage dataset, Kumar and Vani (2019) discovered that the VGG16 model outperformed other designs such as LeNet, Xception, and ResNet50, with the greatest accuracy of 99.25% (Jafar et al., 2024). These papers demonstrate the progress made in automated tomato disease diagnosis and the potential of different machine learning and deep learning models to improve crop health and agricultural productivity.
