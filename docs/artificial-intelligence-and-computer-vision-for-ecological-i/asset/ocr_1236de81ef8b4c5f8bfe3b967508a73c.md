# Historical Overview

The integration of remote sensing technology into agriculture has a rich historical background marked by significant milestones and advancements. Early endeavours in remote sensing for agricultural purposes can be traced back to the 1960s and 1970s, with the launch of pioneering satellite missions such as Landsat. Landsat-1, launched in 1972, provided the first multispectral imagery of Earth’s surface, laying the foundation for land cover mapping and crop monitoring (Campbell and Wynne, 2011). Subsequent Landsat missions, including Landsat-2 and Landsat-3, continued to contribute valuable data for agricultural applications, albeit with limited spectral and spatial resolution capabilities.

The 1980s witnessed a paradigm shift in remote sensing technology with the launch of more advanced satellites like SPOT (Satellite Pour Observation de la Terre) by France and the launch of the first synthetic aperture radar (SAR) satellite, Seasat, by NASA. These satellites offered higher spatial resolution and specialized sensors, enabling more detailed observations of agricultural landscapes, crop types, and vegetation health (Merchant, 2000).

The 1990s marked a period of significant technological advancement and diversification in remote sensing applications for agriculture. The launch of the Advanced Very High-Resolution Radiometer (AVHRR) on NOAA satellites provided global coverage for monitoring vegetation dynamics, land surface temperatures, and drought conditions (Justice et al., 1998). Additionally, the development of hyperspectral imaging sensors allowed for more precise spectral analysis of agricultural fields, leading to improved crop health assessments and disease detection capabilities (Then kabail et al., 2000).

In the 21st century, the proliferation of commercial satellite providers, along with advancements in unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) or drones, revolutionized the accessibility and affordability of high-resolution remote sensing data for farmers and agricultural researchers. The launch of satellites with multispectral and hyperspectral sensors, such as Sentinel-2 and WorldView series, further enhanced the capabilities of remote sensing in agriculture, enabling detailed crop monitoring, soil analysis, and precision farming practices (Yang et al., 2018).
