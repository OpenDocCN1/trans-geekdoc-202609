## Precision Agriculture

This kind of farming leverages remote sensing to manage field variability successfully, maximizing the use of resources such as water, fertilizers, and chemicals. Integrated with global positioning system technology, high-resolution RS information enables the production of comprehensive area maps that highlight variants in soil properties, plant health and wellness, and moisture levels (Shafik, 2024). Variable Rate Technology (VRT) can then apply inputs precisely where needed, decreasing waste and enhancing effectiveness. For instance, NDVI maps can guide differential fertilizer applications, ensuring that the required quantity is used in specific areas, thus boosting nutrient uptake and minimizing runoff. Similarly, moisture maps originated from thermal imaging, or SAR, can inform irrigation schedules, promoting water conservation (Shafik et al., 2023). Remote sensing additionally supports real-time surveillance and adaptive monitoring, allowing farmers to respond swiftly to changing conditions. This targeted approach enhances efficiency and profitability and lessens environmental effects, supporting sustainable agriculture practices that are critical when faced with climate change and resource scarcity (Ullo and Sinha, 2021).

## Soil Analysis

The SAR and hyperspectral imaging assess soil moisture content, texture, organic matter, and nutrient levels. This info is crucial for assessing soil fertility and making informed decisions about soil management (Qiao et al., 2024). Geographical maps can identify dry areas requiring watering or zones susceptible to waterlogging that might need drainage services. Hyperspectral information can discover variations in soil organic matter and nutrients, directing the application of composts or fertilizers to enhance soil health and wellness (Jafarbiglu and Pourreza, 2022). RS aids in mapping soil erosion and compaction, which is essential for preserving soil structure and fertility. By supplying a spatial overview of soil conditions, RS supports accurate farming practices that maximize soil management. This brings about improved plant growth, decreased input costs, and better sustainability (Chuphal et al., 2024). Moreover, continual tracking allows for the analysis of soil health over time, assisting in long-term planning and conservation efforts.

## Yield Prediction

Regarding yield production and prediction, RS enhances the precision of crop yield forecasts by supplying extensive and timely information on crop growth stages, health, and environmental problems (Jafarbiglu and Pourreza, 2022; Kumar et al., 2024b). Evaluating historical RS information along with current season monitoring, models forecast yields with high accuracy. NDVI and Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) are the most popular indices derived from multispectral and hyperspectral imagery that offer an insight into plant biomass and health and wellness, which are directly correlated with yield potential (Tey et al., 2024). Thermal imaging can monitor temperature stress, while SAR information supplies info on soil moisture levels, both of which impact crop efficiency. Integrating these data points with weather forecasts and ground truth information enables the development of robust yield prediction models (Abid et al., 2021). These predictions are indispensable for farmers and agriculture in planning harvests, managing supply chains, and making market choices. Accurate yield forecasts additionally sustain food security planning and policymaking by governments and global organizations, guaranteeing that sufficient resources are allocated to meet future food needs (Shanmugapriya et al., 2019).

## Disaster Management

RS plays a vital role in disaster management within the agricultural sector by supplying fast and precise analyses of damages triggered by natural disasters such as floods, droughts, storms, and wildfires. Satellite imagery and airborne data use real-time information on the level and intensity of such events, allowing for prompt response and recovery efforts (Singh et al., 2022). SAR penetrates the cloud cover to provide images of flooded areas, while thermal imaging can spot fire hotspots. Post-disaster, remote sensing assists in mapping impacted areas, estimating plant losses, and assessing soil and facility damage. This information is essential for coordinating emergency relief, insurance claims, and recovery programs (Qiao et al., 2024). It further uses information to support the advancement of early warning systems that predict the possibility of extreme weather events, allowing farmers to take precautionary measures. A thorough risk management framework can be established by incorporating RS with other data sources, such as atmospheric and hydrological models, boosting the resilience of farming systems to disasters and climate change (Chuphal et al., 2024).

## Case Studies

This section presents selected real-world applications of RS in improving agriculture.

### Precision Agriculture in the United States of America

In the Midwest of the United States, a large corn farm implemented precision agriculture methods, using RS and GPS technology and AI to ensure precision fertilization. Multispectral imaging from drones was used to create comprehensive NDVI maps, highlighting variations in crop vigor throughout the fields (Kumar et al., 2024a; Tey et al., 2024). These maps determined locations with reduced NDVI value, suggesting nutrient deficiencies. After that, the farm utilized VRT to apply fertilizers differentially based on the NDVI data. This strategy caused an over 15% increase in corn yield, an over 20% reduction in fertilizer usage, and lessened nutrient runoff, thus lowering environmental pollution (Singh et al., 2022). Integrating RS and VRT substantially increased productivity and environmental sustainability.

### Drought Monitoring and Management in India

In the drought-prone region of Maharashtra, India, RS was employed to monitor and manage water resources better. Satellite thermal imaging offered data on land surface temperatures, plant water stress, while SAR was used to gauge the degree of soil moisture (Kumar et al., 2024b). This data was integrated into a central database available to local farmers and farming agencies to ensure drought monitoring and water management. The combination of thermal and SAR data permitted the advancement of an effective early warning system for drought conditions, allowing farmers to apply water-saving measures (Chuphal et al., 2024). Optimized irrigation schedules, notified by soil moisture maps, preserved water resources, and reduced crop losses by 30% throughout drought periods, enhancing resilience and improved crop resilience and sustainability.
