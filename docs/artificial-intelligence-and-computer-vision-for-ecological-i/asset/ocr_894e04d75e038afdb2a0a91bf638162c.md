Furthermore, traditional approaches to crop yield estimation are constrained by their reliance on manual data collection and analysis. The labour-intensive nature of these methods not only increases operational costs but also introduces opportunities for human error and data inconsistency. As such, there is a pressing need to streamline and automate the process of yield estimation to enhance efficiency and accuracy in agricultural decision-making (Jung et al., 2021).

# Need for Technological Advancements

In light of the shortcomings associated with traditional approaches, there is a compelling case for leveraging technological advancements, particularly AI and ML, to revolutionize crop yield estimation practices. AI and ML algorithms offer unprecedented capabilities for the dispensation of large volumes of information, extracting meaningful patterns, and making accurate predictions in real-time. By harnessing advanced sensing technologies such as remote sensing, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and IoT devices, farmers can assemble high-resolution spatial, and temporal information on crop health, soil moisture, and environmental conditions. Integrating these data streams with AI-driven analytics enables precise monitoring of crop growth dynamics and early detection of stress factors that may impact yield potential (Chouhan et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2024).

Moreover, AI and ML techniques enable the growth of predictive models that can adjust to dynamic environmental conditions and incorporate heterogeneous data sources. Deep learning algorithms, in particular, excel at capturing nonlinear relationships and complex patterns in agricultural datasets, thereby enhancing the accuracy and robustness of yield forecasts. Traditional approaches to crop yield estimation have played a vital role in agricultural planning and management but are beset by inherent limitations. The emergence of AI and ML technologies offers a transformative opportunity to overcome these limitations, enabling more accurate, timely, and cost-effective predictions of crop yields. By embracing technological advancements and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration, stakeholders can unlock the full potential of AI and ML in sustainable farming (Mahto et al., 2024).

# Introduction to AI and ML

AI and ML are powerful implements in many domains, including agriculture. In the context of crop yield assessment, artificial intelligence (AI) refers to the unintentional copying of human intellect in technology that thinks and learns like humans. Machine learning (ML) is a subset of AI that focuses on developing procedures that let computers learn from and make estimates or decisions based on information.

To estimate agricultural production, large databases containing information on crop varieties, weather patterns, soil conditions, and past yield data are analyzed using AI and ML techniques. Compared to conventional techniques, these algorithms’ ability to recognize intricate patterns and relationships in the data allows for more precise projections of crop yields (Ahmad and Nabi, 2021).
