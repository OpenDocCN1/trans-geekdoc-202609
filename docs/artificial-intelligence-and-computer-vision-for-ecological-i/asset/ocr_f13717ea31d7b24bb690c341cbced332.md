On the other hand, the environmental footprint of developing and deploying AI and ML systems must be carefully considered. The energy usage for large-scale AI model training and operation can be significant, which adds to greenhouse gas emissions and climate change. It is critical to evaluate the environmental effects of these technologies throughout their lifespan, from hardware manufacture to electronic waste disposal.

Mitigating the environmental impacts requires adopting sustainable practices in the expansion and distribution of AI and ML technologies. This includes utilizing energy-efficient algorithms, investing in renewable energy sources for data centres, and designing hardware with recyclability in mind. Furthermore, continuous monitoring and valuation of the environmental impacts are essential to ensure that the adoption of AI and ML in agriculture aligns with broader sustainability goals (Tiwari et al., 2024).

# Social Acceptance and Trust

The successful integration of AI and ML into agricultural practices hinges on the social acceptance and trust of these technologies among various stakeholders. Farmers, consumers, and policymakers need to be convinced of the reliability, safety, and benefits of AI-driven solutions. Building this trust requires transparent communication, demonstrating the tangible advantages of these tools in improving crop yields and sustainability. One of the primary concerns is data privacy and security. Farmers must be assured that their data, often collected by AI systems for analysis, is handled responsibly and ethically. Executing robust data fortification measures, and transparent data governance frameworks is crucial in alleviating these concerns.

Moreover, the perception of AI and ML technologies as disruptive forces can evoke resistance, particularly among those who fear job displacement and loss of traditional farming practices. To address these fears, it is essential to highlight the complementary role of AI in enhancing human capabilities rather than replacing them. Educational initiatives that showcase successful case studies and the positive impacts of AI on agricultural livelihoods can foster greater acceptance. Community engagement and participatory approaches are also vital. Involving farmers and local societies in the development, and implementation of AI and ML solutions confirms that these tools are tailored to their specific needs, and contexts. This participatory approach not only enhances the relevance and effectiveness of the technologies but also builds a sense of ownership and trust among the stakeholders (Gupta and Khang, 2024).
