# Dielectric Soil Moisture Sensors

Using high-frequency electromagnetic waves, dielectric soil moisture sensors check the dielectric constant in the soils which provides real-time data regarding changing patterns in water wetness over time making it a continuous measurement by wave interaction with the soils. These sensors offer continuous measurements of soil moisture but are costly and sensitive to salinity levels in the ground. Plauborg et al. (2005) experimented with TDR and dielectric soil moisture sensors in drip-irrigated sandy soils and discovered that TDR produces better results with higher precision.

# Remote Sensing Techniques for Estimation of Soil Moisture

An essential factor in soil and water study is soil moisture (SM). SM is arguably the most significant link that interlinks water cycle and life—plant—and its significant effects on farming, biology and nature.

A thoroughly researched aspect of the terrestrial water cycle is land surface hydrology. In land-surface hydrology, the primary factors are streamflow, vegetation, precipitation, SM, and surface temperature. Currently, satellites are used to monitor surface temperature, vegetation, and precipitation. The only variable that is still either not observed is SM. For precisely this reason, satellite SM has seen an increase in use over the last ten years (Lakshmi et al., 2013).

# Overview of Remote Sensing Techniques for Estimation of SM

Determination of SM from remote sensing has been studied for many years and a growth has been observed in this field of study. The methods used during Thermal Infrared (TIR) remote sensing uses the relationship between SM and surface temperature. This results in complicated derivative processes and many approximations and hence valuable information of SM is extracted by combining optical and TIR remotely sensed data (Zhang et al., 2014).

The advancement in satellite technology offers quantitative estimations and a potent way to track the spatial-temporal variations of regional SM. Many specialists have developed different methods to obtain SM by examining the correlations between SM and land surface parameters obtained from satellites. Through the usage of Visible Infrared (VIR) band, deriving SM through the relation between different types of SM data or vegetative traits and soil reflectance that occur during water stress has been studied.
