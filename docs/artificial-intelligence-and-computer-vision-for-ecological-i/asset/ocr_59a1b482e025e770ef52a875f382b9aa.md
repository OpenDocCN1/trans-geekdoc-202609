# Ground-Based Sensors

Ground-based sensors, such as spectroradiometers and LIDAR, provide highly accurate and detailed data for agricultural mapping (Ullo and Sinha, 2021). These sensors typically gather information on soil properties, crop growth stages, and plant health. The main advantage of ground-based sensors is their precision, which can complement data from aerial and satellite sources. However, their spatial coverage is limited, making them suitable for smaller areas or validating data obtained from other RS methods. The high equipment cost and the labor-intensive nature of ground-based data collection are notable challenges (“RS in Applications of Geoinformation,” 2022).

# Data Quality

Ensuring high-quality data is crucial for accurate agricultural mapping. Common issues include noise, missing values, and inconsistencies across different datasets (Khanal et al., 2020). Noise can result from sensor errors or environmental factors, while missing values may arise due to gaps in data collection. Inconsistent data formats and resolutions from various sources can complicate integration and analysis.
