# Introduction

Soil moisture (SM) is crucial for ecological processes and ecosystem services, impacting the hydrological cycle, climate regulation, nutrient availability, and primary productivity (Anagnostopoulos et al., 2017). It significantly influences land evapotranspiration, a vital process connecting water, energy, and carbon cycles (Green et al., 2019; Padrón et al., 2020). In agriculture, optimal soil moisture is essential for maximum crop productivity, making accurate SM data vital for effective water resource management to prevent drought and water stagnation (Li et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2019). SM is also critical for hydrological applications like flood prediction, as saturated soils increase flood risks (Kim et al., 2019). In forestry, it supports tree growth, forest health, and fire risk management (Krueger et al., 2022). Furthermore, SM is integral to climate models, influencing weather predictions, climate projections, and climate variability through its interaction with atmospheric conditions (Koster et al., 2010). Therefore, precise and feasible methods for soil moisture evaluation are essential given their diverse and significant roles.
