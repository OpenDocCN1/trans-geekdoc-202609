Microwave remote sensing, particularly Synthetic Aperture RADAR (SAR) data, is useful data because it can penetrate cloud coverage and provide data irrespective of the weather condition. SAR systems emit microwave signals and measure the backscatter, which is influenced by soil's moisture content. These measurements are utilized to derive accurate SM levels across massive zones. The high resolution of SAR data allows for detailed spatial analysis, making it a valuable resource in precision agriculture and hydrology. Passive microwave sensors, employed from 1978 to 2015, have significantly advanced soil moisture (SM) estimation by providing a detailed understanding of sensor technologies and their applications. Over this period, sensors like SMMR, SSM/I, MS MR, SMOS, AMSR-E, TRMM, and SMAP have progressively enhanced SM monitoring through improvements in frequency range, swath width, polarization capabilities, and temporal resolution. These advancements have revolutionized the ability to capture accurate, large-scale SM data, contributing to critical applications in hydrology, agriculture, and environmental monitoring.
