# Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) Sensors

Time Domain Reflectometry sensors measure the travel time of an electromagnetic pulse along a waveguide inserted into the soil. Soil moisture levels influence the travel time, as the presence of water slows the pulse. This delay is calibrated and converted into volumetric soil moisture content using appropriate conversion functions. TDR sensors offer high-precision measurements at various soil depths but are expensive and require specialized equipment and expertise for data interpretation. Despite these challenges, they are extensively utilized in research, precision farming, and hydrological studies, providing a deeper understanding of soil moisture dynamics essential for scientific investigations (Kashyap et al., 2021).

# Frequency Domain Reflectometry (FDR) Sensors

The FDR sensors measure the dielectric constant of soil by analyzing the frequency response of the soil’s capacitive properties. Operating at higher frequencies, these sensors provide greater accuracy across varying depths. However, FDR sensors are expensive and require calibration for different soil types. Like TDR sensors, FDR sensors are utilized in research and precision agricultural applications, particularly in areas where detailed moisture profiling is essential. Zhu et al. (2019) conducted experiments comparing TDR and FDR sensors under varying temperature conditions and found that TDR sensors are more sensitive to temperature changes than FDR sensors.

# Gypsum Block Sensors

Gypsum block sensors measure the electrical resistance of a gypsum block in equilibrium with soil moisture. In this case, electrical resistance changes along with the degree of wetness surrounding the block. These sensors are cheaply made and require little skill to use but they have lower accuracy rates and become less effective over time. The use of these sensors in agriculture, particularly when cost is a major factor, is limited to basic soil moisture monitoring. Other methods are usually employed together with them for a comprehensive understanding of the amount and distribution of soil water. Using experimental results, Ezekiel et al. (2021) demonstrated that the mean difference between the Gravimetric method, compared to gypsum, Tensiometer and Sensor, was 6.30, 3.95 and 3.02, respectively. It could be concluded that the Sensor used with the Arduino board measures moisture content more accurately than the Gypsum and Tensiometer. Veeranna et al. (2016) developed the automated Wireless Gypsum Block Sensor Network System which performed extremely well with results highly correlated concerning actual soil moisture as 0.98 r-square value between them.
