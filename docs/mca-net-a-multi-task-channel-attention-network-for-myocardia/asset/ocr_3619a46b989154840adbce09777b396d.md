# 5.4. Experimental results on the PTB dataset

Unlike the intra-patient schemes which are often used in previous studies, the inter-patient schemes are of greater clinical significance to prove the model generalization.
Therefore, we perform 5-fold cross validation experiments on the PTB dataset under an inter-patient scheme to verify the effectiveness and generalizability of our approach, the mean sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of our model are shown in Table 5 where the optimal solution is highlighted. We can see that MCA-net achieves the best performance for all tasks. In the detection task, the sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of MCA-net are improved compared with other comparative methods, reaching 98.01%, 89.01%, and 95.57%, respectively, while the improvement in specificity is extremely obvious. The classification performance of MCA-net is also improved in the Location-5 task, achieving 55.35%, 67.96%, and 68.90%, respectively. For the Location-7 task, the MCA-net is achieving 41.80%, 49.01%, and 49.18%, with an improvement of more than 6% on accuracy.

We can also observe that MFB-CBRNN performs slightly worse in all tasks compared to the other methods. And ML-ResNet achieves good classification accuracy on both detection and location tasks. And ML-Net performs a little better than ML-ResNet on both the Detection and Location-5 tasks, but slightly worse on the Location-7 task. This indicates that they lack adaptability to different tasks. In contrast, our method has not only superior performance but also high generalizability. This is mainly because we use multiple convolutional layers with different convolutional kernel sizes and SE module to learn important features at different granularities. On the other hand, it also benefits from the multi-task framework which can effectively share underlying parameters and complementary information between different tasks.

In addition to the mean result of the evaluation metrics, we also calculate their ranges of variance to compare the stability between methods. As can be seen in Table 5, the range of variance fluctuation of our method is smaller than that of the comparison method for most of the evaluation indicators, which proves that our method ensures reliable stability while maintaining superior performance. Moreover, a T-test at a 0.05 significance level was used to analyze whether the MCA-net was significantly better than other comparative methods for each evaluation metric. As can be seen, our method has some performance advantages on the Detection and Location-7 tasks, especially in terms of sensitivity. However, in the Location-5 task, which may be influenced by the label imbalance and distribution, a higher demand is placed on the model's ability to differentiate.

# 5.5. Experimental results on the PTBXL dataset

Different from the PTB dataset, the PTBXL dataset involves a larger number and wider range of patients, and each patient can provide one to more ECG records, allowing for richer sample information and longer ECG sequences. In addition, the PTBXL dataset is closer to the actual clinical situation than the PTB dataset because its ECG records usually contain multiple labels. We extract HC and MI records from the PTBXL dataset for MI detection and location experiments to validate the performance of the MCA-net. Longer duration ECG records are used as input units to validate the effectiveness of the MCA-net in learning long time series features. To more closely resemble the real-world data distribution, the PTBXL dataset is divided by its publisher into ten folds of the inter-patient paradigm [41] which represents that there is no overlap of patient origin for ECG records in all folds. Therefore, we perform 10-fold cross validation based on these sets to obtain more reliable results. As shown in Table 6, our method still outperforms other comparative methods, achieving a sensitivity of 88.59%, specificity of 96.55%, and accuracy of 93.65% on the detection task. Meanwhile, MCA-net also performs better than other methods on the Location-5 and Location-7 tasks, achieving 84.03% and 81.47% accuracy as well as 78.76% and 74.05% AUROC, respectively. This demonstrates the effectiveness and generalizability of our method across the different datasets.

We also can see that in contrast to the results on the PTB dataset, ML-Net performs the worst on the PTBXL dataset. The possible reason is that the length of data provided by the PTBXL dataset is much longer (typically more than several thousand sampling points), while the lightweight framework adopted by ML-Net cannot effectively learn long-term dependencies from such long ECG sequences. And MFB-CBRNN performs relatively well on both datasets, which demonstrates the stability of the convolutional layer. In addition, the average performance of ML-ResNet performs better than the other two comparison methods, which proves the effectiveness of the residual module. Our approach not only combines the stability of the convolutional layer with the effectiveness of the residual module but is also better able to learn the long-term dependence of ECG sequences thus achieving better performance.

Again, the reliable stability of the MCA-net is further verified by the range of variance fluctuations of the experimental results. In addition to this, we still use the T-test to verify the significance of MCA-net on the PTBXL dataset. As can be seen in Table 6, MCA-net achieves significant improvements in most tasks relative to the comparison method, especially the Location-5 and Location-7 tasks.
