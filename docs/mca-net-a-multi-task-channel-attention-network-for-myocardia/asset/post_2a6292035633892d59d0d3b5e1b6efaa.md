## ABSTRACT

Problem: Myocardial infarction (MI) is a classic cardiovascular disease (CVD) that requires prompt diagnosis. However, due to the complexity of its pathology, it is difficult for cardiologists to make an accurate diagnosis in a short period. Aim: In the clinical, MI can be detected and located by the morphological changes on a 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG). Therefore, we need to develop an automatic, high-performance, and easily scalable algorithm for MI detection and location using 12-lead ECGs to effectively reduce the burden on cardiologists. Methods: This paper proposes a multi-task channel attention network (MCA-net) for MI detection and location using 12-lead ECGs. It employs a channel attention network based on a residual structure to efficiently capture and integrate features from different leads. On top of this, a multi-task framework is used to additionally introduce the shared and complementary information between MI detection and location tasks to further enhance the model performance. Results: Our model is evaluated on two datasets (The PTB and PTBXL datasets). It achieved more than 90% accuracy for MI detection task on both datasets. For MI location tasks, we achieved 68.90% and 49.18% accuracy on the PTB dataset, respectively. And on the PTBXL dataset, we achieved more than 80% accuracy. Conclusion: Numerous comparison experiments demonstrate that MCA-net outperforms the state-of-the-art methods and has a better generalization. Therefore, it can effectively assist cardiologists to detect and locate MI and has important implications for the early diagnosis of MI and patient prognosis.

## 1. Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a global health problem, and its occurrence in developing countries is continuing to rise. Myocardial infarction (MI) is a typical CVD, which is caused by coronary artery obstruction, and these obstructions result in a severe decrease in blood flow to the heart muscle [1]. Since MI is difficult to detect and occurs rapidly, it has also been described as a silent heart attack. According to statistics from the American Heart Association (AHA), each year, approximately 605,000 people have an episode of MI, and 200,000 MI patients have a relapse [2]. Moreover, the locations of MI are numerous and proximate to each other, involving different coronary vessels and varying degrees of criticality, which can seriously affect the patient’s prognosis. As shown in Fig. 1, they can be broadly classified as anterior MI (AMI), anteroseptal MI (ASMI), anterolateral MI (ALMI), inferior MI (IMI), inferolateral MI (ILMI), and others [3]. Therefore, early detection and location of MI are essential for prolonging the life expectancy of patients and improving the life quality of patients.

Continuous acquisition and recording of dynamic changes in the subject’s ECG waveform can often provide critical information to the physician making the diagnosis of MI in the clinical [4,5]. Therefore, ECG is a necessary tool for the diagnosis of MI and should be obtained and interpreted promptly after the first medical contact. However, due to the complex and diverse ECG manifestations of MI, using 12-lead ECGs to detect and locate MI are extremely time-consuming and laborious for cardiologists, and even prone to misdiagnosis. Therefore, automatic ECG-based diagnosis of MI has become a research hotspot in the field of medical artificial intelligence.

The traditional methods for MI detection and location usually include three steps: feature extraction, feature selection, and classification [6]. Early feature extraction methods usually extracted relevant

ECG features such as ST-segment elevation and Q-wave abnormalities [7,8] by wavelet transform (WT) [9], principal component analysis (PCA) [10], or empirical mode decomposition (EMD) [11], etc. Then, traditional machine learning methods such as K-nearest neighbors (KNN) [12,13], random forests (RF) [14], or support vector machines (SVM) [15,16] are used for classification. However, the feature selection process of these methods often relies on human intervention and requires extensive medical expertise and experience. On top of that, the classification performance of these conventional MI detection and location methods can be greatly reduced if the morphological characteristics of the selected ECG waveforms are not accurate enough. Therefore, to solve the above problems, a more stable method for end-to-end MI automatic detection and location is needed.

As deep learning methods achieve remarkable results in many fields, they also show their advantages in CVD classification based on ECG signals [17,18], such as Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM). They are not only able to perform feature extraction and classification process simultaneously, but also usually achieved higher accuracy compared to traditional methods. Therefore, researchers have proposed many methods based on deep learning methods to detect and locate MI which achieved promising results. For example, Strodthoff et al. [19] proposed an MI detection and interpretation algorithm based on a full CNN using eight-lead ECGs, which extracted features directly from the raw ECG data and achieved 93.3% sensitivity and 89.7% specificity. Acharya et al. [20] proposed a novel approach that used a CNN with eleven layers to automatically detect MI using single-lead ECGs. This approach needed no manual feature extraction or selection so that even in the presence of noise it can also make accurate detection of MI. As a result, it obtained 93.53% and 95.22% accuracy in the presence and absence of noise, respectively. He et al. [21] developed a novel 12-lead ECG-based deep active semi-supervised learning framework for MI location which was named multibranch densely connected convolutional network (MB-DenseNet). It first employed active learning (AL) to improve the classification performance of the initial model and then designed a new semi-supervised learning method named self-training with spatial matching (STSM) to update the model. MB-DenseNet obtained 99.87% and 96.09% accuracy under the intra-patient and patient-specific scheme, respectively.

The above-mentioned methods have brought some impressive improvements in the automatic detection and location of MI. However, there are still many challenges that need to be solved. First, MI episodes in different heart locations usually cause abnormal ECG waveforms in different combinations of leads. For example, we can see from Fig. 1 that AMI may cause ST-segment elevation in leads V3 and V4, whereas ALMI may cause ST-segment elevation in different leads (II, III, and AVF). Therefore, incomplete lead information may lead to a lack of location features, thus affecting the accuracy of identification. Unlike most existing methods that use only single-lead or partial-lead ECGs, we fully used 12-lead ECGs to introduce more data information to enhance the performance of our method. Second, although some studies have also considered 12-lead ECGs, they tend to treat the MI detection and location tasks as two independent tasks and did not consider the correlation between them [22–25], which may severely limit the performance and improvement space of the existing methods.

Therefore, to address the above problems, we propose a multi-task channel attention network (MCA-net) for MI detection and location tasks. The contributions of this paper can be summarized as the following points:

- 1. We propose a novel channel attention network for feature learning from 12-lead ECGs, which employs multiple independent channels network to extract features from individual leads. In each channel network, the features are efficiently extracted and integrated with different stages using the squeeze-and-excitation (SE) [26] attention mechanism and skip connections.
- 2. According to the relevance of MI detection and location tasks, we introduce a multi-task framework in MCA-net. This multi-task framework can improve the performance and generality of MCA-net by exploiting the shared and complementary information among the tasks in a joint optimization loss manner. Therefore, MCA-net can be easily extended to different tasks.
- 3. In the experimental part, we use two public datasets to fully validate the performance and generalizability of MCA-net. We use the multi-fold cross validation strategy for a more rigorous evaluation of the model performance. We also conduct many ablation experiments to compare and analyze the impacts of the multi-task framework and the role of each module in the channel attention network. The experimental results indicate that MCA-net is superior both in performance and generality compared to other studies.

Section 3 describes the dataset and preprocessing methods we used. The details of our proposed method are described in Section 4. In Section 5, we present the details of experimental implementation and discuss the results. In Section 6, ablation experiments and further analysis of our model are presented. Finally, we conclude our work in Section 7.
