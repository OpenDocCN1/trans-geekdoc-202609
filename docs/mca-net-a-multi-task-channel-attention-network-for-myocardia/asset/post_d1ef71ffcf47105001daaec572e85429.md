
However, the traditional manual analysis of ECG is extremely time-consuming and laborious and needs the professional knowledge and experience of doctors. Therefore, researchers have explored and developed many automatic MI diagnosis algorithms to reduce the burden on cardiologists.

Early automatic MI diagnosis algorithms focused on the MI detection task that classified MI and HC (Healthy Control) populations. Researchers typically use biosignal processing methods combined with classic machine learning methods. These methods usually required many tedious manual data processing steps before classification, such as denoising, normalization, feature extraction, and selection. Commonly used methods include WT, frequency domain analysis (FDA) [27], hidden markov model (HMM) [28] and SVM, etc.

For example, to perform automatic detection of MI based on ECGs, Sun et al. [29] proposed a multi-instance learning (MIL) approach named latent topic MIL. This method used the unlabeled heartbeats in the training set to create a topic space and mapped the ECGs to this space. Then, the ECG-level topic vectors are fed into the SVM for the final classification. Nidhyananthan et al. [30] presented a wavelet-based method to detect MI and user identity. This model first used Daubechies wavelet transform to decompose the multi-layer ECG signals and then employed an SVM classifier to classify the normal and abnormal cases in the signals. Benameur et al. [31] based on three parametric imaging techniques for cardiac MRI to quantify areas of infarct-related systolic impairment to detect MI. The parametric images generated from the monogenic signal achieved the best performance. Pereira et al. [32] designed a method combined with wavelet decomposition and eigenspace analysis to detect MI. The wavelet decomposition is used to extract waveforms of different frequencies in the ECGs and separate them. The eigenspace analysis provided the eigenvalues calculated from covariance matrices. Therefore, these training features can help to classify HC and MI patients. Liu et al. [33] used the tunable Q-factor wavelet transform (TQWT) to design a novel ECG signal processing method to extract high-quality ECGs and perform MI detection. In this proposed method, the authors decomposed the 12-lead ECG signal into high and low Q-factor components, and then manually extracted a large number of features, which were finally fed into KNN to obtain better performance. Wah et al. [34] used SVM and KNN as classifiers to classify cardiac conditions in ECG signals based on extracting relevant waveforms or features from the ECG signal and analyzing certain specific regions of the peaks and their temporal frequencies. Fatimah et al. [13] developed two models to automatically detect MI called primary and modified methods based on single-lead ECGs, respectively. The primary algorithm applied the Fourier decomposition method (FDM) on the heartbeats obtained after the segmentation operation based on R-peaks and used a modified algorithm to calculate its efficiency. Features obtained by these two methods were fed into a variety of conventional machine learning classifiers and achieved the best performance in KNN. Han et al. [16] proposed a new method to detect MI by fusing energy entropy and morphological features based on 12-lead ECGs. In detail, the ECGs were first decomposed and then calculated the decomposed coefficients to get the energy entropy, finally fused with local morphological features as global features for more accurate classification. Sahu et al. [35] designed a novel MI detection and location method using variational mode decomposition (VMD) and regularized neighborhood component analysis (RNCA). It constructed a feature set from statistical and non-linear features computed by the intrinsic mode function of the VMD and ranked using RNCA, then fed into a KNN and AdaBoost classifier for classification. Unfortunately, these traditional methods based on feature engineering rely excessively on professional medical experience and require a long feature extraction process and a rigorous feature selection process. As a result, they are susceptible to limitations in terms of convenience and stability and are difficult to meet clinical standards.

However, as deep learning methods have shown great advantages in the fields of computer vision, biosignal analysis, and machine translation, their powerful automatic feature extraction ability has been demonstrated. Therefore, more and more deep learning-based methods for automatic MI detection have been proposed. For instance, Liu et al. [36] developed a novel algorithm based on a multi-lead CNN (ML-CNN) for MI detection via four-lead ECGs. In this method, the lead asymmetric pooling (LAP) layers and sub-two-dimensional (2-D) convolutional layers were applied to learn multi-scale feature representations of different leads which performed 96.00% accuracy. Li et al. [37] proposed an automatic MI detection model to synthesize single-lead ECGs with high morphological similarity by generative adversarial networks (GANs). It automatically detected MI using CNNs for both the original ECG and the synthetic ECG from GANs and achieved 99.06% accuracy. Moreover, Liu et al. [23] presented a multiple-feature-branch convolutional bidirectional recurrent neural network (MFB-CBRNN) to effectively perform MI automatic detection based on the 12-lead ECGs which consisted of 12 multi-feature branches. Specifically, MFB-CBRNN utilized multiple stacked multi-scale convolutional layers combined with pooling operations to fully extract the features within the ECG leads. Then, it used bidirectional LSTM to fuse features from each multi-feature branch and achieved 99.54% and 86.29% accuracy under inter-patient and intra-patient paradigms, respectively.

And as automatic MI detection methods continue to improve, researchers are gradually shifting their research focus to the more challenging and clinically valuable task of MI location. For example, Liu et al. [38] proposed a new multiple-feature branch CNN (MFB-CNN) to automatically detect and locate MI based on 12-lead ECGs which learned features of leads by corresponding feature branches. Thus, this model obtained 98.79% and 94.82% mean accuracies on MI detection and location tasks, respectively. Cao et al. [39] proposed a multi-scale deep learning model based on a combination of residual network and attention mechanism for 12-lead ECG recordings. It mainly used the SENet model and the Grad-CAM algorithm to calculate the weights of each lead and detected and located MI based on the weighted features, and both achieved over 99% accuracy. Han et al. [22] designed a multi-lead residual neural network (ML-ResNet) for MI detection and location tasks using 12-lead ECG records. It extracted local features from different leads to construct the global feature representation and achieved 95.49% and 55.74% accuracy under the inter-patient paradigm on MI detection and location tasks, respectively. Cao et al. [24] proposed a novel multichannel lightweight model (ML-Net) to provide algorithmic support for automatic MI detection and location on portable devices. In detail, ML-Net utilized a multi-lead framework enabling features within each lead to be learned independently, thus preserving the unique features of different angles represented by different leads, and finally performed 96.00% and 66.85% classification accuracy in MI detection and location tasks, respectively. However, most methods are designed for specific tasks and are difficult to extend to other downstream tasks, limiting their use in clinical practice. In contrast, methods that consider both MI detection and location tasks usually treat MI detection and location as two separate tasks. They do not consider the correlation between the tasks and do not enable effective information sharing and complementarity between the tasks during training, which may limit their overall performance.

To tackle the limitations of existing methods, we design a multitask channel attention network named MCA-net. In our model, we use a channel attention network to learn and fuse the features of each lead and employ a multi-task framework to efficiently combine shared and complementary information between MI detection and location tasks. Experiments show that MCA-net can automatically perform MI detection and location tasks and has a high application value.
