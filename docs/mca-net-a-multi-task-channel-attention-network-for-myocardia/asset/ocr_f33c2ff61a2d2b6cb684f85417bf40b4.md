# 6.1. The benefits of the multi-task framework

To validate the impact of the multi-task framework in MCA-net, we design a single-task model and two dual-task models to perform the same experiments as our model on the PTB and PTBXL datasets. The results of the ablation experiments can be seen in Table 7. Here, Cnet refers to the single-task model, MCA-net<sub>25</sub> refers to the dual-task model for the Detection and Location-5 tasks, MCA-net<sub>27</sub> refers to the dual-task model for Detection and Location-7 tasks.

We can find that the single-task model Cnet performs well on different fine-grained tasks based on Table 7, especially the MI detection task. Meanwhile, we can also find that the results of the experiments are continuously optimized as the number of tasks increases and the granularity of the tasks refines. In particular, our model achieves the best results, not only on different datasets but also on different tasks. On the PTB dataset, the improvement rates for MI detection and location tasks are 1.05%, 2.45%, and 2.77%, respectively. On the PTBXL dataset, we improve the AUROC by 0.08%, 1.77%, and 0.62% respectively, while maintaining the high accuracy. This proves that the multi-task learning framework we adopt can effectively capture common information and task-specific features related to different tasks, and enhance the final feature representation through information complementation between tasks. We can also observe that increasing the number of tasks enables more aggregation of features learned from different tasks and enhances the final learned representations. And the increased number of classification categories leads the model to learn more accurate features, which affects the final decision.

By the way, combine with Tables 5 and 6, we can also observe that the single-task model Cnet still has higher classification accuracy than all the comparison methods, which further demonstrates that the channel attention network structure can capture the relevant features in the 12-lead ECGs more effectively.

# 6.2. The benefits of the channel attention network

In this subsection, to further understand the impacts of each module on the final classification performance, we remove the different modules of the channel attention network and conduct experiments in the same configuration, respectively. We compare the classification accuracy between our model MCA-net and MCA-net variants, i.e., MCA-net without the 2-D residual block, MCA-net without the 1-D residual blocks, and MCA-net without the SE modules. The ablation experimental results on the PTB and PTBXL datasets are shown in Fig. 4.

+   - **No 2-D Residual Block**: We can see from Fig. 4 that the model without the 2-D residual block exhibits a decrease in accuracy compared to MCA-net. This demonstrates that the 2-D residual block can help the model learn the morphological features of the ECG waveforms inside the leads in the early feature extraction stage of the model. In addition, we can see that the 2-D residual block has a better impact on model performance in the PTB dataset, which suggests that the 2-D residual block may be more effective in extracting short ECG sequences.
+   
+   - **No 1-D Residual Blocks**: And we can also see that the model without the stacked 1-D residual blocks also shows a degradation in performance. However, the impact of the 1-D residual block on the model shows noticeable differences across the datasets. As shown in Fig. 4a, the 1-D residual block brings less than 2% improvement in accuracy per task on the PTB dataset, while on the PTBXL dataset (shown in Fig. 4b), it is more effective than the other modules, with gains of 3.77%, 5.36%, and 7.18%, respectively. This proves that the stacked 1-D residual blocks can more effectively enhance the model's ability to capture fine-grained features from long ECG sequences, and are more suitable for processing relatively complex learning tasks.
+   
+   - **No SE**: To explore the benefits brought by the SE modules, we remove the SE modules from the 2-D residual block and the 1-D residual blocks and then conduct experiments. We can see from Fig. 4 that the classification performance of MCA-net decreases after removing the SE modules. This is particularly evident on the PTB dataset, where the accuracy drops on the three classification tasks reach 2.83%, 4.62%, and 6.63%, respectively. This shows that the SE modules can strengthen the important features and weaken the non-important features on the ECG classification task, thus achieving the purpose of improving classification performance.
