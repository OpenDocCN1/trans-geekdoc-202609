# ABSTRACT

Problem: Myocardial infarction (MI) is a classic cardiovascular disease (CVD) that requires prompt diagnosis. However, due to the complexity of its pathology, it is difficult for cardiologists to make an accurate diagnosis in a short period. Aim: In the clinical, MI can be detected and located by the morphological changes on a 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG). Therefore, we need to develop an automatic, high-performance, and easily scalable algorithm for MI detection and location using 12-lead ECGs to effectively reduce the burden on cardiologists. Methods: This paper proposes a multi-task channel attention network (MCA-net) for MI detection and location using 12-lead ECGs. It employs a channel attention network based on a residual structure to efficiently capture and integrate features from different leads. On top of this, a multi-task framework is used to additionally introduce the shared and complementary information between MI detection and location tasks to further enhance the model performance. Results: Our model is evaluated on two datasets (The PTB and PTBXL datasets). It achieved more than 90% accuracy for MI detection task on both datasets. For MI location tasks, we achieved 68.90% and 49.18% accuracy on the PTB dataset, respectively. And on the PTBXL dataset, we achieved more than 80% accuracy. Conclusion: Numerous comparison experiments demonstrate that MCA-net outperforms the state-of-the-art methods and has a better generalization. Therefore, it can effectively assist cardiologists to detect and locate MI and has important implications for the early diagnosis of MI and patient prognosis.

# 1. Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a global health problem, and its occurrence in developing countries is continuing to rise. Myocardial infarction (MI) is a typical CVD, which is caused by coronary artery obstruction, and these obstructions result in a severe decrease in blood flow to the heart muscle [1]. Since MI is difficult to detect and occurs rapidly, it has also been described as a silent heart attack. According to statistics from the American Heart Association (AHA), each year, approximately 605,000 people have an episode of MI, and 200,000 MI patients have a relapse [2]. Moreover, the locations of MI are numerous and proximate to each other, involving different criminal vessels and varying degrees of criticality, which can seriously affect the patient’s prognosis. As shown in Fig. 1, they can be broadly classified as anterior MI (AMI), anteroseptal MI (ASMI), anterolateral MI (ALMI), inferior MI (IMI), inferolateral MI (ILMI), and others [3]. Therefore, early detection

and location of MI are essential for prolonging the life expectancy of patients and improving the life quality of patients.

Continuous acquisition and recording of dynamic changes in the subject’s ECG waveform can often provide critical information to the physician making the diagnosis of MI in the clinical [4,5]. Therefore, ECG is a necessary tool for the diagnosis of MI and should be obtained and interpreted promptly after the first medical contact. However, due to the complex and diverse ECG manifestations of MI, using 12-lead ECGs to detect and locate MI are extremely time-consuming and laborious for cardiologists, and even prone to misdiagnosis. Therefore, automatic ECG-based diagnosis of MI has become a research hotspot in the field of medical artificial intelligence.
