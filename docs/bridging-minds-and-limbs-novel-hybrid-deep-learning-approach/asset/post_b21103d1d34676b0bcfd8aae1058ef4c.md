### 4. Discussion

The results presented here demonstrate the potential of a hybrid deep learning model for detecting lower limb imagery, a task that is particularly relevant for stroke rehabilitation. The model achieved 72.14% accuracy and demonstrated consistent performance across subjects, even with inherent variability. By integrating CNN and LSTM with adaptive feature weighting and PSO for parameter optimization, the proposed model demonstrates robustness to address the complexity of motor imagery tasks and provide improved performance.

Specifically, CNN captures spatial characteristics by local and global patterns in EEG topologies, which are essential for distinguishing motor imagery classes. The attention mechanism further captures temporal dependencies, allowing the network to focus on spatial features. Bi-LSTM effectively models the temporal effects of EEG signals in both forward and backward directions, enabling better understanding of the imagery. Optimization of kernel sizes and filter numbers leads to faster convergence and improved generalization across subjects.

### 3.5. Event-related desynchronization (ERD) analysis

C3 and C4 are widely recognized for their role in detecting motor activity during motor imagery tasks. Notably, multiple studies have demonstrated that lower limb imagery can elicit ERD patterns at C3 and C4, reflecting contralateral control.
