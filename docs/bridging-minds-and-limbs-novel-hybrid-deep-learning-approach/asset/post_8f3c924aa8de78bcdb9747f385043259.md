
## 6 Conclusions

This study presents a novel brain-computer interface (BCI) system for detecting lower motor imagery, with potential applications in rehabilitation. By using OpenBCI with a deep learning framework, the system ensures both hardware capability and EEG signal processing. The proposed approach catches key features in imagery classification by leveraging CNN for spatial extraction, Bi-LSTM for temporal recognition, and an attention mechanism for feature fusion.

The study demonstrates the feasibility of using consumer-grade EEG devices for motor imagery, offering a promising balance between cost and performance. High accuracy was further validated the needed system in clinical applications, particularly for stroke patients, and to optimize it for real-life rehabilitation. Future research should focus on integrating the proposed approach with robotic systems and refining its adaptability for personalized rehabilitation.
