# 3. Results

# 3.1. Classification performance

After 400 iterations of training, the proposed model achieved an average classification accuracy of 72.14% (SD - 3.60) for distinguishing between left and right motor imagery classes across subjects (Fig. 8). Despite inter-subject variability, participants' accuracies ranged from 70% to 75%, indicating relatively consistent performance.

# 3.2. Comparative analysis

Table 2 presents a comparison of our approach with widely used classification techniques, including EEGNet [36], CNN-SVM [38], energy-efficient CNN [39], and CNN-LSTM [40]. All methods were trained and tested on the same dataset, using the same proportion of participants and data preprocessing pipeline. Our model achieved high average accuracy (72.1%) and kappa coefficient (0.436), which is significantly higher than other methods. For instance, the average accuracy of 63.7% is much higher than that of CNN-SVM (59.5%) and energy-efficient CNN (53.8%). Our kappa coefficient of 0.096 is also much higher than that of CNN-SVM (0.027) and CNN-LSTM (0.050), showing that our method performs better compared to others.

To evaluate classification performance, the sensitivity of each method is analyzed, as shown in Table 3, which reflects the ability to correctly classify each class, which is a key metric for assessing the effectiveness of the classification system. The highest sensitivity (69.9%) was achieved by the proposed model, consistently outperforming all other approaches. It is followed by CNN-LSTM (67.5%) and CNN-Net (62.2%), while the lowest sensitivity (54.9%) was observed for CNN-SVM.
