
# Bitcoin Mining Overview

Many people regarded mining, the process of creating cryptocurrency or Bitcoins, as a kind of recreational activity a couple of years ago. Many people participated in that game until the infamous pizza incident occurred. To many in the Bitcoin world, the day is referred to as "Pizza Day." On May 22, 2010, a developer made a payment of 10,000 bitcoins to a fellow Bitcoin forum user in exchange for two pizzas. At the time, Bitcoin was virtually completely useless. The cost of these two pizzas was around $25 for the user. At the time of writing (October 2017), one Bitcoin was worth more than $5,000. It's not difficult to figure out that just two pizzas cost that guy a million dollars or more.

When Bitcoin's value began to skyrocket, it was the first time that it was mined on a large scale. First and foremost, computational power was utilized in the Bitcoin mining process. Later on, miners began to employ graphics cards to form the blocks, which became increasingly popular. They now make use of specialized equipment known as ASIC. China is currently home to the world's largest such operations.

# How the Mining Process Works

Cryptocurrency or bitcoin mining is the process of creating new cryptocurrency or bitcoins. Let us figure out how the mining process works. Whenever you do a transaction, such as sending money from your wallet to another person's wallet, your transactions are placed in the "mempool." Known as the Bitcoin mempool, it is a collection of all transactions that are awaiting confirmation from the network. Miners, motivated by their own set of standards, collect transactions in specified blocks and then attempt to incorporate them into the blockchain.

Every ten minutes, a new block is generated on the blockchain, and so on.

A miner receives a reward after successfully mining a block. At the time of writing, the reward is 12 bitcoins. That is, if you successfully introduce a well-formed block into the system, you will be rewarded in this manner. Aside from that, Satoshi Nakamoto is credited with creating the very first block (Genesis). Anyone may come across it and sell it for 50 bitcoins to the right person. As of today, the prize for a properly created block begins to be awarded to the recipient. By the year 2140, it is predicted to have fallen to an infinitesimal value.

Miners came to the realization that mining on a standalone basis was no longer profitable one day. The likelihood that you will discover such a block is determined by the "hash rate," or the computing capacity of your Bitcoin miner's machine. If your hash rate accounts for 10% of the entire hash rate, for example, you will be able to find such blocks with a 10% chance of finding them and receiving your reward under certain conditions. As a result, if you only mine on your laptop at home, you will never discover a new block. As a result, in order to obtain a more steady payout, the miners band together to form what are known as "mining pools." They make use of their combined hash rate in order to generate a more consistent income.

Many miners are now asking themselves, "Why should I mine when I can buy Bitcoin?" It is also a fantastic concept because the value of Bitcoin is established solely by the belief of individuals who use it, whereas the cost of Bitcoin is set solely by the demand for bitcoin. If no one purchases your Bitcoin from you, there will be no cost to you. Because of this, the value of Bitcoin will continue to rise for as long as people perceive this technology as a means of making anonymous payments, making huge payments, and so on.

I will not go into greater detail about Bitcoin itself because I previously wrote a book on the subject, *Mastering Bitcoin for Beginners*, which was dedicated to this topic. For the time being, I'll focus on the advantages of Bitcoin, which continues to generate some questions.

The primary advantage is the low cost of transactions. However, it is important to remember that Bitcoin is not suitable for micropayments. If you transfer $1 million to someone, it will not cost you a single penny. If you choose to pay for a cup of coffee, the cost will be substantial in comparison to the cost of the coffee.

The second advantage is the ability to process transactions at rapid speeds. There are also some drawbacks to consider. According to the blockchain.org website, every block in the blockchain is generated once every ten minutes.

For example, the quickest transaction possible takes ten minutes. When compared to a SWIFT transfer through a bank, which can take 2-4 days, this appears to be a relatively speedy process. Visa and MasterCard, on the other hand, are significantly speedier. They have the capability of processing tens of thousands of times more transactions per unit of time than Bitcoin. There are several other types of cryptocurrency that have been developed to be faster than Bitcoin, and it is worth noting that they are presently available.

The third advantage is the pseudo-anonymity with which people can identify themselves. We have already discovered that anyone can follow all transactions on the network, which we have already investigated. If you know who owns a wallet and where it is located, you can follow absolutely all transactions that take place in it. As a result, it is impossible to claim that Bitcoin is completely anonymous. A person's anonymity is destroyed as soon as they are able to connect their address with their personality. However, if you adhere to so-called "internet hygiene," which means that you do not disclose your wallet to anyone, then your transactions are virtually impossible to track down. Though I should point out that there are other cryptocurrencies that are more anonymous than Bitcoin, I will not go into detail about them.

It should be noted that if you wish to remain anonymous when transacting on the Bitcoin network, this is currently available. There are a plethora of options available for washing your bitcoins. They are referred to as "mixing services," and they are used to mix one's money with those of other people's bitcoins in order to obscure the trail leading back to the funds' original origin. As you can see, new technologies have emerged in order to increase the anonymity of cryptocurrencies even further.

Finally, more than 16 million bitcoins are currently in circulation around the world, with a total of 21 million bitcoins expected to be mined. The algorithm of the network itself is responsible for programming such figures. Because of the restricted supply of Bitcoin, it is impossible for this currency to become increasingly valuable. Because only a limited number of units will be produced, the value of this currency will not diminish over time. Bitcoin even has a deflationary paradigm, as many users lose their coins as a result of forgetting their wallet password or sending money to the incorrect recipient address. As a result, the total number of bitcoins will eventually diminish.

All of these difficulties are resolved by the use of the consensus procedure.
