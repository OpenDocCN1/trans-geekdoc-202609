
# ICOS (Initial Coin Offerings)

ICOS is an acronym that stands for International Cooperation on Science and Technology (INITIAL COIN OFFERING).

To date, a large number of people have taken part in so-called initial coin offerings (ICOs) (Initial Coin Offering). Essentially, this is another interpretation of the crowdfunding paradigm, one that is similar to an initial public offering (IPO). Participants contribute to the creation of a project in exchange for future advantages, but there are no assurances that the initiative will be successful. In the cryptocurrency world, an initial coin offering (ICO) refers to the issuing of coupons or tokens by a specific project that will be used to pay for site services in the future using cryptocurrency.

In many ways, an initial coin offering (ICO) is like a venture fund, which is an investment fund that focuses on working with creative firms and ideas (startups). A venture fund is a type of investment fund that makes investments in securities or shares of businesses that are high-risk or very high-risk with the expectation of making extraordinarily large gains. In general, 70–80% of such projects fail to generate a return on investment. However, the profit from the remaining 20%–30% more than compensates for all previous losses.

If you believe you have the courage to fund a project that offers no certainty, ask yourself:

*   Am I prepared to take on more risks in this endeavor?
*   Is there a capital cushion that I can use for diversification?

You make money with an initial coin offering (ICO) by taking huge risks in the hope of making huge gains.

## Investment Maxims and Moving Averages

To keep things in perspective, I'd like to remind you of another classic investing adage: buy low, sell high. It may seem clichéd, but it's true. When the value of a coin drops, it is frequently the perfect moment to invest in it. However, at this precise point, we begin to feel greedy, believing that the price of a coin may fall much further.

The "moving average" approach is used to solve this particular problem. It can be either simple or exponential in nature.

### Illustration of a Simple Moving Average

Look at the following illustration of a basic moving average:

For example, you might be extremely interested in a coin that costs $100. You make the decision to purchase 14 of these coins one morning while sipping your coffee. As a result, you spend $1,400. During the next couple of weeks, the currency's value will approach $105 per coin. You sell these 14 coins for $105, and you receive $1,470 in return. You've made a profit of $70 thus far.

But what if you decide to purchase these 14 coins in installments of one coin every week, rather than all at once?

It appears that the chart has remained unchanged. For example, in one situation, the average coin price is $100, whereas in another, the average coin price is $91, and so on. As a result, in the second scenario, you spend $1,274 on 14 coins, each of which you sell for the same amount as the coins in the preceding figure. $196 is your profit. What caused this to occur? Everything was the same except that we took a different approach to the matter.

When you use a basic moving average technique, you don't have to worry about always looking for the best time to enter the market or worrying about the possibility that the rate will fall even further. Every month, you just purchase a predetermined quantity of cash. You continue to purchase regardless of whether the market is falling or rising. In reality, this method is quite cliched, but it is extremely effective for long-term investing.

Simple moving average strategies are advantageous because they reduce the average purchase price, allow you to buy more shares for the same amount of money, reduce losses in the downturn, and allow you to recover from losses more quickly in the upturn.

### Exponential Moving Average

Now, I recommend that you take into consideration the exponential moving average method.

Using the exponential moving average approach, you can see a boost in your earnings. Everything works in this manner: when the price remains constant or increases, you make your customary purchase; when the rate decreases, you make additional purchases.
