
It is important to remember that hashing takes any input, no matter how large or tiny, and converts it into a string of characters. If you make even the smallest adjustment to the input, the entire output is affected. It is possible to include the contents of the previous block in the new block by taking the hash of the entire previous block and adding it to the beginning of the following block. Essentially, this means that we've effectively linked the old block to the new block, and if anything changes in the older block, even the tiniest modification, the hash of the entire block will change as a result.

Now, once a block has been completed, it is much more difficult to make changes to it. Making an edit to an older block would necessitate a re-hash of the entire block in order to make the edit. Once you've re-hash the entirety of block 1, you'll need to crack open block 2, delete block 1's old hash, enter block 1's new hash, and then re-hash the entirety of block 2. However, because new blocks are being created all the time, in order to amend an older transaction, you'd have to edit every block that has been created since the transaction was made in the first place. The longer time passes, the more difficult it becomes to hack into a network and successfully change the outcome of a transaction. This is why hashing is essential to the security of the blockchain. Because of the encryption, it is difficult to make changes to the transaction ledger, allowing the ledger to be both public and secure at the same time.

On the other hand, the hashing itself is not particularly complex. Most computers are capable of re-hashing a blockchain in a matter of seconds. We must therefore increase the complexity of creating new blocks in order to ensure that the hashing security executes its job properly and effectively. In an ideal world, it would be something that slows an attacker's progress and increases the likelihood that honest members of the network will prevail. This additional difficulty is referred to as "proof of work" in the Bitcoin Blockchain (as well as most other current blockchains).

I won't go into great detail on proof of work here; however, a basic explanation of proof of work is given in chapter 3 of this book, and you can learn more about the technology in my book "Blockchain Technology Explained" (available on Amazon).

## COLD STORAGE AND BITCOIN DEBIT CARDS

Taking the assumption that you are interested in learning how to properly store cryptocurrency, I will go into greater detail on the topic of cold storage.

Cold storage is a term that refers to private keys that are often generated and stored in a safe, isolated environment to prevent unauthorized access. This means that your funds will not be held on any particular website. This wallet has the appearance of a USB flash drive, which allows you to connect it to a computer and complete a transaction quickly.

Trezor, KeepKey, and Ledger are the cold storage wallets that are most widely used.

If you need to store a large quantity of money in cryptocurrencies, this is a fairly popular way. Transactions are rarely carried out, and security is one of the most important considerations. As a result, if you intend to keep your cash for an extended period of time, cold storage is the ideal option for you.
