## PLACING ORDERS ON THE EXCHANGE

Exchange orders are used to open and close positions on the exchange when trading on the market. There are two types of orders: market and pending, which differ in how they are executed. Pending orders, on the other hand, can be limit orders, stop orders, or stop limit orders with a stop limit order.

Any asset can be purchased or sold using any of the many types of orders.

I recommend that you exclusively use limit orders, but we'll get into that in more depth later in the article.

Placing a market order on the stock exchange is the quickest and most convenient way to buy or sell something. If there is a reverse limit order for this order, it is executed at the current market price immediately after it is placed on the exchange. Otherwise, it is not executed. If you want to execute a market purchase order, you'll need to place a sell limit order first, for example. In order to execute a market sell order, a purchase limit order must first be executed.

In order to execute a market order, the best limit order from among all of the available limit orders is selected. The market purchase order is therefore executed at the ask price, whilst the market sell order is filled at the bid price, as a result.

When dealing with this type of order, you do not need to mention the price; instead, you only need to specify the volume, which is how much you want to purchase. However, there is a significant difficulty that you may encounter — slippage. What exactly is it? It is the difference between the price at which a trade is expected to be done and the price at which the trade is actually completed. In periods of heightened volatility, slippage may occur when market orders are employed; also, when there are big orders, there may not be enough interest at the intended price, resulting in slippage.
