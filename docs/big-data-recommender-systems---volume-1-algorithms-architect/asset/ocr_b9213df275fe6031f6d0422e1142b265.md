Developing recommendations for LBSNs to provide a user with items that maybe interesting for him/her,(a venue or an event with a geographic location) has recently attracted research attention [4–6]. This application has become more useful and important when a user travels to an area far away from his/her hometown, where/she has little knowledge about the neighborhood. In this scenario, the spatial item recommendation has been proposed as recommendation for out-of-town users in LBSNs [7,8]. In this chapter, we focus on the effective and spatial item recommendation for users on LBSNs in both hometown and out-of-town scenarios by considering their historical data in LBSNs. As suggested in many working ever about LBSNs [9–12], geographical movement demonstrates significant temporal patterns on LBSNs and is also highly relevant to the location property. Therefore, the recommendation should be both time and location oriented.

Spatial item recommendation is highly challenging because of the following three reasons: (1) travel locality: with the rapid expansion of LBSNs, the number of spatial items visited by each individual user is smaller compared to the total number of spatial items in LBN, leading to a sparse user-item matrix. Travel locality refers to the scenario that most users tend to check in near their living regions. According to the survey in [13], the check-in activities generated by users in nonhome cities take only 0.47% of the ones generated in their home cities. The travel locality aggregates the data sparsity problem in spatial item recommendation in out-of-town scenarios (e.g., recommending spatial items in people from New York). (2) Spatial dynamics of user behavior: when users travel in different geographical regions, their behavior patterns tend to change [14]. We have conducted a survey on Foursquare dataset. Specifically, with the application interfaces (APIs) published by Foursquare, we extract the top three categories of check-in spatial items in different cities including Boston, Vegas and Gold Coast for a specific group of users. The number of users in the group is 3,000 and each user in this group has checked-in in all these three cities. The percentage of check-in of each spatial item category is that for the same group of users, they are more interested in visiting casino (80.32%), nightlife (10.61%) and outlet (5.82%) when they are in Vegas, prefer beach (71.36%), surf part (14.82%) and park (9.60%) when they are in Gold Coast. (3) Temporal dynamics of user behavior: temporal influence also plays an important role in impacting users' daily activities in LBSNs [9–15]. For example, a user is more likely to go to a restaurant rather than a bar at now. Therefore, the spatial item recommendation for LBSNs should also be time-aware. An intuitive solution of temporal influence modeling is splitting time into slices at the predefined granularity (e.g., hourly or seasonal) and modeling the temporal preference to spatial items of a user in each time slice by mining the activities of the user in the time slice.
