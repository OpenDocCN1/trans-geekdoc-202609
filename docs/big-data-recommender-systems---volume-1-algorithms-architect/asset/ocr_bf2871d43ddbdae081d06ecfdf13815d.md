For a pair of target user and recommended item, the inner product of their feature vectors was employed for accepting the item with probability proportional to this inner product. Thus, a user was more likely to select products similar to his/her profile, while in order to reduce biases, there was always a chance of selecting irrelevant products in place of relevant ones, as expected in reality. The item and user graphs were considered to have the power-law form, as is the case for many online SNS of users and also for the associations between a few popular and many items of electronic shop. The details of the evaluations and the performance results for various densities of the user-item graphs are found in [20]. The performance metrics employed for the evaluation were the success rate/percentage, the average minimum hyperbolic distance to the destination (which indicates how far away the goal the recommendation path stopped in case of failure to reach the goal) and the average recommendation precision (defined as the number of recommendations that the user has chosen over a recommendation round divided by all recommendations made by HRKD or HRUD from a recommendation round), which are all typical evaluation metrics for anomalous recommendation approaches.

# 4.3.2 Probabilistic graphical models

A different category of RSS aims at predicting the score that a user would assign to an item (rating prediction) and suggest to the user a list of ranked, most relevant items (top-N recommendation task) according to the rating predictions. There are several approaches to achieve these tasks, but lately family of probabilistic graphical models has emerged [21–24] employing the concept of Markov random fields (RFs) (MRFs) for the representation of information dependencies and solution of the corresponding problems. This family will be presented in this section, after briefly describing the nature of MRFs. It is worth noting that in general, most of these approaches belong to the class of CF or hybrid methodologies, with a greater tendency towards the second.

A configuration x = {x_s, ∀s ∈ S, x ∈ A}, corresponds to one of all possible states of the system.
