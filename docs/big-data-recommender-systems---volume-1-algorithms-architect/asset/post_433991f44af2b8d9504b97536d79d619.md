## 5.7 Developing approaches for the crisp ontology

It is widely accepted that there is no common existing methodological approach that can solve every DI problem. Adding to provide good guidelines for crisp ontology, various ontology development approaches have been presented. An ontology development approach provides a framework for scheduling activities or tasks that should be followed and performed during the design process. Workflows proposed by different approaches fare better or worse regarding efficiency, use, comprehensiveness and reliability. A well-organised schedule of activities proposed by ontology development approaches can provide methodological supports for ontology professionals. The most well-known ontology approaches proposed in current literature are METHOLOGY [34], NeOn [35], DILIGENT [36], On-To-Knowledge [37], HCOME [38] and DOGMA [31]. In addition, Noy and McGuinness [39] presented a very descriptive simple to creating crisp ontologies. A set of survey papers, such as Jagger and Marsden [10], are also available providing references to existing ontology development approaches and their features. To conclude, a considerable amount of approaches can come in help for developing crisp ontologies. However, these approaches dedicated to crisp ontologies cannot be directly applied to construct Fuzzy-Ontologies due to major differences between Fuzzy-Ontologies and crisp ones. In order to develop Fuzzy-Ontologies, additional procedures, such as including fuzzy logic to approximate vagueness and conceptualising the fuzzified vagueness, have recognised recognised in the development process.

## 5.8 Developing HAs for Fuzzy-Ontology

HAs based on Fuzzy-Ontologies mainly focuses on dealing with conceptual formalisms. In other words, how to represent Fuzzy-Ontologies in a formal language is the most active work. Using the Fuzzy-Ontologies in a standard and effective way is the main contribution to address the DI issues in big data. As example, the IKARUS-Onto (Imprecise Knowledge Representation and Use) methodology [40] is an approach for Fuzzy-Ontology development. It focuses on the provision of a methodological guideline for the conversion from crisp ontologies into fuzzy ones. It consists of five steps as (1) including crisp ontology, (2) establishing the need for fuzziness, (3) defining Fuzzy-Ontology elements, (4) formalising fuzzy elements and (5) validating fuzzy ontology. The proposed structure of Hybrid Integration Development Approaches (HIDAs) for Fuzzy-Ontology for big data is shown in Figure 5.3.

The IKARUS-Onto approach represents a considerable guidance for fuzzifying the crisp ontologies. Thus, it is suited to develop Fuzzy-Ontologies in domains with the existence of crisp ontologies. Similarly, the fuzzy ontomethology proposed by Ghorbel et al. [41] also emphasises the activities for developing fuzzy extensions based on available crisp ontologies. The fuzzy ontomethology consists of three steps: (1) including the conceptualisation, (2) providing the ontologisation and (3) providing the operationalisation.
