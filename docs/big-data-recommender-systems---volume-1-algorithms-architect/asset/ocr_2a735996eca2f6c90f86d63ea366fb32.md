Even in the case she assumed that all entities were real, she would not be able to infer the actual data either due to the secure encryption used and also the inherent information suppression properties of the phonetic encodings.

 Carol performs a JOIN operation over the encrypted phonetic codes, which returns the ids of the matching codes to their corresponding owners, i.e., A[1, 2, 3, 4] for Alice and B[2, 3, 4, 5] for Bob, as shown in Figure 13.2. Bob recognizes B[2, 3, 4, 5] as correct matches, while Alice recognizes A[1, 2] as correct matches, which eventually correspond to a single real-world record, and A[3, 4] as false positives, since their phonetic code resulted from fake data.

Then, Alice and Bob ask each for the data represented by the matching codes. At this point, Bob asks Alice for data represented by the hashes "34...51," "83...a4," "c3...bc,"e9...c0" which correspond to phonetic codes "F632," "FARSAN," "J525" and "JANSAN", respectively. Similarly, Alice asks for "e9...c0" and "2f...e5" consumed to "J525" and "JANSAN." Finally, the two sources exchange records based on the intersection of common hash codes, which in this case is "e9...c0" and "2f...e5," i.e., records "Johnson" of Alice and "Johnsen" of Bob.
