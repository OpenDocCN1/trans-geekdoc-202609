# IRT-based privacy risk score

The first polytomous model was proposed by Liu and Terzi [41]. They expanded previous privacy risk scores by considering both the dichotomous and polytomous approaches. In their approach, they used the IRT-based method to compute a user’s privacy. The derivation of IRT in psychometrics returns to data analysis of gathered information from tests or questionnaire. In their model, a two-parameter (users and attributes) IRT-based model had been considered. In this case, their model can quantify users' privacy. Finally, they can calculate and measure privacy score as in [42]. In their polytomous approach, they defined R(k, n)=n, indicates that the k-th attribute for user / has been disclosed to n nodes further in a graph. Hence, the privacy score provides an indicator of whether the information of an individual is at risk or not. Considering Liu and Terzi’s model for computing a privacy risk score for social networks users, Sramka [49] extended the model to evaluate and assess the risk of privacy from the users’ viewpoint. In their new privacy score, Sramka considered users’ background knowledge was publicly available. The main drawback of this method was that only attribute and identity revelation were measured and only a dichotomous approach was considered.
