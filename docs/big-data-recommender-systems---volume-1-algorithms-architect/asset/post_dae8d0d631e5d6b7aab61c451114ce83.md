The second category includes the methodologies that tackle the problem of information overload. The exponential growth of information in online systems, accompanied by the limited processing ability of users, impacts decision-making and increases the need for mechanisms of relevant information retrieval and delivery. Traditional RS handle with similar preferences, disregarding users' social relations in a network. From this point of view, different models of information diffusion serve either as an alternative [35,36] of the filtering mechanism in RS, adding social criteria (influence, trust, homophily). In [36] the correlation between information modeling diffusion and CF is highlighted with a ranking-oriented model of recommendations, where the authors assume that both mechanisms make predictions of users' preferences from a different perspective. Using a latent factor model and assuming that users are highly influenced by their friends, the latent factor of user u adopts item i is very close to the ones who have adopted item i. The predicting score of ratings, $\hat{y}_{ui}$, is computed by taking into consideration users' similarity and the linear combination of several features of information cascades as follows: $\hat{y}_{ui} = \sum_j b_j \gamma^d + (p_i^u + \sum_{e \in E_t} \omega_{et} a_p(i))(1/Z)$, where b is a parameter vector, $\gamma^d$ is a diffusion attribute, $C_i$ is the cascade of item i, $p_i$ is the latent factor for each user in $C_i$, $q_i$ is a latent factor for item i, $a_p$ is the weight parameter for each user in $C_i$, and $d_i$ is a latent factor for the user that starts the cascade of item i.
