## 6.2 Generative models

PGMs are powerful techniques that are capable of capturing implicit relationship between the attributes of a dataset. This section begins by introducing the PMF, which will be followed by the discussion of two topic models, (1) PLSA and (2) LDA. Specifically, we will show how to reframe the generative principle of topic models for recommender systems and how to integrate PMF with topic models to create hybrid recommendation systems. The topics discussed in this section will serve as fundamentals to understand advanced recommendation models such as deep generative models that will be discussed in the later part of this chapter.

### 6.2.1 Probabilistic matrix factorization

MF [15] and its probabilistic counterpart PMF [1] are classic model-based techniques for CF-based recommendation. Unlike MF, the PMF follows a generative approach which allows the model to seamlessly integrate with topic models such as LDA and generative DL models as VAEs. From simple addition of attributes to a more complex change in the generative process, researchers have experimented with several variations of PMF. For example [16], incorporate travel distance, utility index and popularity of locations into PMF for point-of-of-interest (POI) recommendation and [17] introduce a new distribution into PMF to create social Poisson factorization. The graphical structure of PMF is shown in Figure 6.2, where $v$ represents the latent vector of an item, $u$ represents the latent vector of a user. The place notations $I$ and $J$ denote the total number of users and items, respectively. PMF assumes that the users and the items have their respective latent attributes $u$ and $v$ and the observed rating $r_{ij}$ is generated from these latent attributes. This generative process is shown in Algorithm 1 where the ratings $r_{ij}$ could be categorical or binary valued and $K$ is the number of latent attributes. It should be noted that when $r_{ij} = 0$, there are

**Algorithm 1**: Generative process of the PMF
**for** each user $i \in I$ **do**
| draw a latent factor $u_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \lambda_u^{-1}I_K)$
**end**
**for** each item $j \in J$ **do**
| draw latent factor: $v_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \lambda_v^{-1}I_K)$
**end**
**for** each user-item pair $(i,j)$ **do**
| draw a rating $r_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(u_i^T v_j, c_{ij}^{-1})$
**end**

**Parameter inference**: The objective of the PMF model is to estimate the following posterior distribution:

$P(U, V|R) \propto P(R|U, V) \cdot P(U) \cdot P(V)$ (6.1)

parameters $U$ and $V$ are learned using the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate. Expanding the appropriate likelihood using the graphical structure and taking the negative log of the resultant expression produces the following equation:

$-ln P(U, V|R) \propto \frac{C_{ij}}{\lambda_{ij}^2} \sum_{(i,j)\in R} (r_{ij} - u_i^T v_j)^2 + \frac{1}{\lambda_u^2} \sum_{i} u_i^2 + \frac{1}{\lambda_v^2} \sum_{j} v_j^2$ (6.2)

One can observe that the below equation is the squared error between the observed and the predicted rating along with some regularization terms $\lambda_v$ and $\lambda_u$. Parameters $U$ and $V$ can be obtained by performing gradient descent.

**Rating prediction**: After learning the parameters, the predicted rating of an item $j$ by a user $i$ can be estimated by taking the dot product of the user and the item latent vector as follows:

$\tilde{r}_{ij} \approx u_i^T \cdot v_j$ (6.3)

### 6.2.2 Probabilistic latent semantic analysis

PLSA was originally proposed as a topic model for document clustering [2] and later introduced as a model for CF-based recommendation by Hofmann et al. [18].

![](img/6d0ce7dba01af52e1c2fae47d5b04a05_131_0.png)

Figure 6.3 Graphical structure of PLSA models: (a) shows the graphical structure of simple CF-based PLSA and (b) shows hybrid PLSA that incorporates content information

Then, it has been extended by several researchers to incorporate content information in form of text [19], social network information [20] and geo-location information [21] to create hybrid recommender systems. The graphical structure of PLSA and hybrid PLSA (H-PLSA) is shown in Figure 6.3(a) and (b). Here, $U = \{u_i\}^*$ denotes the set of users, $V = \{v_j\}^*$ denotes the set of items, $W = \{w_k\}^*$ are the set of words for an item $v$ and $Z = \{z_{ij}\}^*$ is the set of latent attributes (or topics). The generative algorithm of PLSA assumes that users have some interests $Z$ on which they choose an item $V$. In reality, the interests of users not observed; the objective is to infer the latent attribute $Z$ using the observed variables $U$ and $V$ (and $W$ in case of H-PLSA).

**Parameter inference**: The central inference problem of PLSA is to estimate the following posterior:

$P(z|u,v,w) = \frac{P(u|z)P(v|z)P(w|z)}{\sum_Z P(u|z)P(v|z)P(w|z)}$ (6.4)

where $n(u,v,w)$ is the number of times a user chooses an item $v$ that has the content (i.e. word $w$). In reality, the variable $z$ is latent, explicitly finding the maximum likelihood estimates of parameters is hard. Therefore, maximization is done over the expected value of the log-likelihood as follows:

$\mathbb{E}(L') = \sum_{U,V,W} n(u,v,w)Q(z) \sum_Z \log P(z|u)P(v|z)P(w|z)$ (6.5)

where the $Q$-function $Q(z)$ is generally set to the posterior $P(z|u,v,w)$. The expected likelihood function is solved by introducing Lagrange multipliers and taking the partial derivatives of (6.5) w.r.t. $P(u), P(u|z), P(v|z)$ and $P(w|z)$ to yield the following:

$P(u) \propto \sum_{V,W,Z} n(u,v,w)P(z|u,w)$ (6.6a)

$P(v|z) \propto \sum_{U,W} n(u,v,w)P(z|u,w)$ (6.6b)

$P(w|z) \propto \sum_{U,V} n(u,v,w)P(z|u,w)$ (6.6c)

$P(u|z) \propto \sum_{V,W} n(u,v,w)P(z|u,w)$ (6.6d)

Using (6.4) and (6.6)(a)-(d), the expectation maximization (EM) algorithm is explained as follows:

- **Initialization**: Begin by randomly initializing the parameters $P(u), P(u|z), P(v|z)$ and $P(w|z)$.
- **E-step**: Estimate the posterior $P(z|u,v,w)$ using (6.4).
- **M**: Use the newly calculated posterior from Step 2 to recalculate the parameters using expressions (6.6)(a)-(d).
- **Repeat Steps 2** and 3 until convergence.

It should be noted that the parameter inference of CF-PLSA (Figure 6.3(a)) is exactly same as of H-PLSA, except it does not include the term $P(w|z)$.

**Item recommendation**: The convergence of the EM algorithm results in learning the parameter $z$ and consequently the probabilities in (6.6)(a)-(d). Given a user $u$, the predicted score of an item $v$ can be obtained by marginalizing the contents $W$ and the topics $Z$ of the likelihood function as:

$P(v|u) \propto \sum_{W,Z} P(u,v,w)$ (6.7)

### 6.2.3 Latent Dirichlet allocation

LDA is the most widely used topic model for document and word clustering [3]. The generative process of words and documents is very similar to that of PLSA; however, LDA introduces additional constraints in the form of Dirichlet priors that smooth the document topic proportion and word topic proportion which alleviates the problem of overfitting. The utility of LDA is fully explained from a perspective of document and word generation. Nonetheless, to PLSA, LDA can also be used for CF-based recommendation for implicit ratings. For example, in [22], the authors use LDA for location-based recommendation, [23] propose a generative recommendation model for crowdfunding domains and [24] recommend sequence of POIs for travelers by incorporating temporal, historical and popularity-based features into a unified LDA model.
