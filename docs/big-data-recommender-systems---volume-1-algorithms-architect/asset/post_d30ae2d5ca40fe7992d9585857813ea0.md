## 7.1.3 Knowledge-based recommendations

Knowledge-based RSs recommend desired items to the users, according to item’s characteristics and user’s profiles. It makes the most of the knowledge about users and products and defines which products meet the users' requirements. This kind of RS requires a thorough modeling of items and users to work properly; accessibility of the necessary knowledge and its maintenance over time is the common obstacle to their progress. Here comes up an advantage, recommendations do not rely on any database of user ratings, so it would not result in a ramp-up problem. Also they do not require a large amount of data to offer or compute recommendations; contrast to other systems, a minimal amount of users will suffice. A drawback emerges when it requires an engineer’s knowledge database to provide users with helpful recommendations, a knowledge that should get updated regularly to keep it with ever-changing consumer ratings and preferences [9].

## 7.1.4 Demographic recommendations

Demographical data such as age, gender, social class, education, location can be used and achieved both explicitly and implicitly based on the demographic data which a user profile can be made of. A demographic profile can recommend identical items. As an example, teenagers obviously prefer different products than the olds; the rich class of society may differ in type and items quality and definitely are willing to pay more on mentioned features. So the demographic recommendation takes into account personal characteristics of users and makes recommendations. Demographic approach does not apply user-item ratings before rating any item; new users are also to take advantage of receiving recommendations. The technique is domain independent of the independence of item’s knowledge and its features. To gather the demographic data, this approach faces privacy issues which can be grouped in the disadvantages of the technique. For highly personalized recommendations, demographic classification is a little too basic [10].

## 7.1.5 Hybrid recommendations

Very simple recommendation techniques are used by current RSs, such as content-based, collaborative, demographic, utility-based and knowledge-based. Using these techniques alone will lead to both advantages and disadvantages. The fact is a total inspiration to more research in hybrid RS which has merged techniques to enhance performances. Numerous hybrids have been investigated, i.e., the hybridization techniques that have been named as weighted, switching, feature combination, feature augmentation, meta-level, cascading and mixed. They are described as follows [11]:

- Weighted: Applying collaborative and content-based system individually and afterward until their predictions.
- Switching: System applies a specific switching criterion to swap between two recommendation systems that are working on the similar object.
- Feature combination: Features related to dissimilar recommendation system’s data sources are gathered into a single recommendation algorithm.
- Feature augmentation: We can sometimes utilize output of a system as an input feature of another; for instance, we can use a produced model to form features by another model.
- Meta-level: Here a feature can be used as input of another, like model learned by one recommendation.
- Cascading: In this category, one recommendation system improves the outcome presented by another.
- Mixed: Integrating two or more techniques simultaneously; for instance, content-based and CF.

CF, content-based and knowledge-based methods are the most commonly used recommendation techniques [3].

## 7.2 Big data analytics

Big data for all machine-automated systems is nowadays admitted as a challenge by the computer industry. Many concerns are in storage, management and retrieval of data, which is known as big data. The main issue is how to apply this data in order to enhance business and improve the living standard of people. Process of collecting, organizing and analyzing large amounts of data to determine various lines and other helpful information is referred to as big data analytics. The primary objective of the big data analytics is asking organizations to get better decisions in business, help with future predictions and provide them with analysis of large numbers of transactions done in organizations and update those of data used by them [12]. Extracting information from current data in order to predict future outcomes and trends or determining patterns can be done by predictive analysis; this will not act as a prophecy but with an acceptable level of reliability will predict what may happen in future, including what-if-then scenarios and risk considerations. Predictive analytics has applications, namely customer relationship management, clinical decision support, collection analytics, cross-sell, customer retention, direct marketing, fraud detection, risk management and underwriting [12].

### 7.2.1 Text analytics

Information in databases is present in textual form. Effective extraction of important information or manual analysis of these contexts is somehow impossible, due to its relevance in supplying a quantity of automatic tools for analyzing large textual data. A procedure of obtaining major information from text data is called text analytics or text mining. This procedure is used to extract significant information from text, patterns, and events. Text analytics is widely used to understand requirements, sentiment, and sentiment.
