The value collected today regarding items or users is already exceeding the computational scales offered by the state-of-the-art processing algorithms [1]. This volume is expected to further increase in the future, since far more data sources will become available. For instance, in the anticipated dawn of the era of Internet of Things (IoT) and smart homes/buildings, diverse information regarding the daily routine of humans will become available, and potentially exploited by online systems for customized, cheaper and more efficient services/items provided [2] (e.g., a smart fridge monitoring the inventory of goods and suggesting online order of those reaching low quantities and at discount). Within the existing and future systems characterized by big data, several operations or computations will need modification or reinventing them in order to cope with the anticipated scales of operation.

In all the aforementioned web systems, and many more, it is often necessary to employ various forms of recommendation systems (RSs) to increase the efficiency of marketing and create added-value services for the users [3,4]. The RS character generally utilizes the techniques and software tools that generate effective suggestions about new or existing items for particular users or classes of users. The main goal of an RS is to increase the number of items sold and the corresponding financial revenue, while potentially promote diverse items, e.g., promote unpopular items in a movie database that some users may find ever interesting. Other functions include the creation of a more satisfying experience for users and increasing user fidelity (which asserts that the user interacts with the system, the more refined the user activity model can become, and thus, the more probable to increase the revenue generated by a more customized experience). By aiming at users’ preferences for items not already online shopping environments, and thus the associated costs of finding and selecting items in the online environment, the basic concept behind an RS is a well-designed decision-making strategy under a complex information environment that consists of an information-processing system that persistently collects data (actively or passively) on the three main entities: items, users, and transactions (relations between items–users or users–users, etc.).
