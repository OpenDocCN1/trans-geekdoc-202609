There are two attack models that are specially designed for nuking: Love and Hate attack and Reverse Bandwidth attack. Love and Hate attack has effect on user-based algorithm and Reverse Bandwidth attack has high effect on item-based algorithm. These algorithms have decreased the cost of required knowledge, because these attacks do not need detailed data about the system. However, Reverse Bandwidth requires some general knowledge of product domain to be able to effectively select low-rated items that have a significant number of ratings. attack models are described in more detail as follows [13].

# Love and Hate attack

Deploying Love and Hate attack is simple and there is no need for additional knowledge. Love and Hate attack contains profile attacks. In this attack $r_{min}$ (the minimum rating) is given to $i_t$ (the target item) and $r_{max}$ (the maximum rating) is set for other ratings in the filler item. By changing $r_{min}$ to $r_{max}$ and vice versa in push attack, we can define the variables form of Love and Hate attack.

```
$$\mu_{bow} = <l_{ih}, \delta_{ih}, l_{ih}, y_{ih}>$\$
```

Properties: $I_S = \emptyset$; $i$ is a filler item that is selected randomly; $\forall i \in I_F$, $\sigma(i)=r_{max}$ is the maximum value of ratings; and $y(i_t) = r_{min}$, here $r_{min}$ is the minimum value of ratings.

Love and Hate attack on a profile is shown in Figure 11.4. This attack needs a little detailed knowledge about the system. When Love and Hate attack is used as a push attack, it is not effective; but if this is used as a nuke attack, it is the most effective attack on user-based algorithms.

# Reverse Bandwidth attack

One of the Bandwidth attack types is Reverse Bandwidth attack. In this attack, selected items are chosen with rating poorly by users. Low ratings are given to these items with the target item. So, the target item is affiliated to dislike items. The system may produce low ratings for these items tend to be very popular. Low-rated items, on the other hand, have to spend higher ratings, making it much harder to select items to be included in the attack, enough to make a significant impact.
