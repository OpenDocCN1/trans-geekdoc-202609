## 7.2.2.2 Personalized tag-based social media music recommendation

Quick rise of music data caused a huge increase in clarifying the users' preferences; this is knowing how to help customers what they desire from big data recommender systems, volume 1, a great amount of music data has become a great challenge. To deal with such issues, a number of RSs have been proposed: Large amount of traditional RSs have failed to do so due to the inconsistency in rating relevant items by users which is called rating-diversity problem. To handle that, an innovative system called tag-driven item similarity is introduced. It benefits from a feature tag information to capture user's preference on music.

- **Framework of music recommendation**
  Initially as a support item-driven and artist-driven similarities are being used with a precise prediction. Next, as a replacement for top N recommendations, it converts play counts into ratings to clarify the user's preferences [20]. The primary contributions can be summed up as follows:
  The rating-diversity problem is abating considerably due to usage of tag information instead of rating. In fact, the similarities are computed by tag frequency vectors.
  Even though tag information is being used, instead of a ranking list, the user's preferences are signified as ratings, i.e., the final results are derived by incorporating tag information into CF algorithms.
  Even if data comes from music websites with no ratings, play counts are used to figure it out statistically. That is, a formulator is needed to transform work of tag-based RS into stages. As shown in Figure 7.4, the framework consists of two stages, namely, off-line preprocessing and online prediction.
  Off-line preprocessing makes use of formulator projects in the rating space to fill the requirement of representing the user preferences by playing counts of ratings. Figure 7.5 shows the scenario of transforming play counts into ratings. User's preferences can be embodied by play counts. That is, your preferred one in a music database consists of music you frequently listen to. Another work off-line stage offers calculating similarities among items, and consequently item similarity matrix will be made. By constructing item similarities, we aim to decrease online prediction. With reference to Figure 7.4, the item similarity is derived by combining two similarities, i.e., item-driven similarity and artist-driven similarity [20].
  Afterward, a rating matrix and three similarity matrices are regenerate online preprocessing. According to the generated matrices, the goal of online prediction stage is to infer the unknown ratings for the active user. With an active user's visit then the unknown ratings for the active user are predicted one by one [20].

- **Tag-based music recommendation challenges**
  In the future, various issues will be taken into consideration. Initially, to improve the recommendation result, context information will be used, as well as tags and ratings. Next, we will consider the transformation between play counts and ratings.

## Graph-based preference

- **Preference relation**
$tr_i \to tr_j$ signify that track $tr_i$ is more far-sounding than track $tr_j$. Suppose a group of users in the music society listens to two tracks $tr_i$ and $tr_j$. And these tracks are rated by users according to their preferences. If users rate $tr_i$ as higher than $tr_j$, then we know that his/her judgment for two tracks is $tr_i \to tr_j$.
In order to find the preference relation from the online music community, in the beginning it tries to infer user's judgment of various tracks. Suppose a user who has listened to two tracks $tr_i$ and $tr_j$. In this case, users will densely rate these two tracks according to their preferences. Their preferences could be deduced as follows: start with, user $u$ listens to $tr_i$ and $tr_j$, and then rates the two tracks with the scores $r_i$ and $r_j$, respectively. If $r_i < r_j$ means $u$ likes $tr_j$ better than $tr_i$ and vice versa. This is a sign that '$u$'s judgment for the two tracks is $tr_j \to tr_i$. Then afterward preference relations are discovered by the RS from the user's listening history. Initially, track pairs rated by ten users will be discovered by the system, and the user's judgment will be deduced as follows: for each track pair $tr_i$ and $tr_j$, it counts the judgment for $tr_i \to tr_j$ and $tr_j \to tr_i$, respectively.
