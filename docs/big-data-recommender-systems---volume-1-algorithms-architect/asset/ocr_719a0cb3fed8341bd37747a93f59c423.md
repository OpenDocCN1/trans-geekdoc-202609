Geo-social influence Geo-social influence indicates that people tend to explore spatial items near a that or their friends have visited before [61]. Many recent studies [5,11,61,62] show that there is a strong correlation between check-in activities and geographical distance as well as social connections. Most of the current spatial item recommendation work mainly on leveraging the geographical and social influences to improve recommendation accuracy. For example, Ye et al. [61] have developed into spatial item recommendation by investigating geographical influence between locations and proposed a system that connects preferences, social influence and geographical influence. Chen et al. [62] have investigated the geographical influence through combining a multicenter Gaussian model, matrix factorization and social influence for location recommendation. Lian et al. [55] have incorporated the spatial clustering phenomenon from geweighted matrix factorization framework to deal with the matrix sparsity. Ference et al. [7] have designed a collaborative recommendation framework which considers the activity records generated by friends and similar users in a mixture.

Temporal effect. The temporal effect of user check-in activities in LBSNs has attracted much attention from researchers. Spatial item recommendation with temporal effect mainly leverages temporal cyclic patterns and sequential patterns on LBSNs. Gao et al. [63] have investigated the temporal patterns of user check-ins in temporal nonuniformity and temporal consecutiveness.[64,65] focus on the task of successive personalized item recommendation in LBSNs by embedding the temporal patterns. Yin et al. have proposed a temporal recommender system [66] and modeled user behaviors based on intrinsic interest as well as the temporal topic in many recommender systems [16,41]. In recent years, deep neural networks have been successfully applied to many recommender systems [24,67] as word embeddings to incorporate auxiliary information and recurrent neural networks (RNN) to capture sequential properties of observed user–item interactions. Manutrumuska et al. have proposed a deep recurrent collaborative filtering framework with a pairwise ranking function that can capture user–venue interactions in a CF manner from sequences of observed feedback by leveraging multiplex perception and RNN architectures in [67].
