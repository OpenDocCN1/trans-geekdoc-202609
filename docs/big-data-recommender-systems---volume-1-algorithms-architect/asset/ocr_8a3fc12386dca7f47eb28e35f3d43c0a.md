While modeling user-visiting behaviors, ST-STAGE takes into account users’ personal interests and dynamics of user behaviors caused by spatial-temporal drift. ST-STAGE learns a user’s preference as a topic-based vector, by mining both the co-occurrence patterns of spatial items and their associated content information (e.g., comments and categories). One advantage of exploiting the content information of spatial items is to address the locality problem in out-of-town scenarios, where the content information associated with spatial items serves as the medium for transferring preference of a user mined from his/her hometown to the unfamiliar regions.

To adapt to temporal dynamics of user behaviors, ST-STAGE recognizes two roles of each individual in a specific region: local or tourist. Given a specific location and a specific time, ST-STAGE mines the visiting records from local users to learn temporal preferences visiting records from tourists to learn temporal preferences. Users with the same role at location are more likely to share similar preferences and behavior patterns at a specific time. Thus, we recommend spatial items to a target user $u$ at location $l$ and time $t$, we consider only u’s personal interests but also the temporal preferences of the crowd who have the same role as $u$. In this way, we leverage the crowd’s temporal preferences to overcome the sparsity of individuals’ activity data at out-of-town regions at a specific time. Specifically, given a user $u$, at time $t$ and his/her current location $l$, we first find a group of users sharing the same role with $u$ when they visited and then produce the time-aware recommendation based on their temporal preferences and the user’s personal interests.

When using visiting history to learn the temporal native and tourist preferences of a specific region, we still face the data sparsity, especially when the target region is small. Given a specific region $r$ and time $t$ (e.g., at night), when there are insufficient visiting records generated at the given spatotemporal context, their native preferences and tourist preferences cannot be inferred accurately. To overcome the data sparsity problem, ST-STAGE integrates a adaptive spatial pyramid, which is a tree structure proposed in [13]. It is first constructed by partitioning locations of spatial items into spatial grids of varying sizes at different hierarchies. Then, ST-STAGE applies the additive framework [18,19] to learn the native preferences and tourist preferences of each region. Briefly, when learning the temporal native preferences and tourist preferences in a region, the two variables learned for all of the region’s grids in the spatial pyramid will be added. Given a specific region $r$ and time $t$, if there are few or no activities, we can still infer their native preferences and tourist preferences guided by their ancestor grids, which have larger scales of geo-regions and thus have more check-in records. Another advantage of the integration of the spatial pyramid is allowing users to switch between different scales of geo-regions (e.g., zoom in/out a Google Map) without relearning the parameters. In this way, this model can be seamlessly connected with Google Map.
