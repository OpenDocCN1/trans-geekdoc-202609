**Temporal dynamic modeling.** As presented, users tend to visit different spatial items at different time, we expect that the proposed ST-AGE model is able to capture this temporal dynamic of user behaviors. An intuitive approach in modeling the temporal influence is to split time into multiple slices at a predefined (e.g., hourly or seasonal) and then model the temporal preferences of a user based on his/her visited items inside each time slice. As we know, the data in real recommender systems are very sparse and dividing a user's activity into multiple slices makes the data much sparser in a specific time slice, which inevitably makes the inference of temporal preferences over-fitting. In this work, we propose to exploit and integrate the collective temporal preferences of the crowd with the same role as the target, as shown in Figure 9.1. The collective preferences of the people with the same role, $\phi_{lj}^{native}$ and $\phi_{lj}^{tourist}$, and are extended to the temporal vectors $	heta_{lj}^{native}$ and $	heta_{lj}^{tourist}$.

**Topics modeling.** To take full advantage of the strengths of both content-based and collaborative filtering-based recommendation methods, a topic z in our ST-AGE model is not only associated with a word vector $\phi_{z}^{topic}$, but also with a spatial items vector $\psi_{z}^{topic}$ and $\sim$ to be mutually influenced. This design enables and enhanced by the topic-discovery process by associating each other. By the discovered z, on one hand, we can cluster the content-similar items together. On the other hand, it can capture the item co-occurrence patterns to link items together, to similar to item-based collaborative filtering methods. We also introduce two background models $\phi_{z}^0$ and $\psi_{z}^0$ to make the topics learned from the dataset more discriminative, since $\phi_{}^0$ and $\psi_{z}^0$ assign high probabilities to non-discriminative and non-informative words and items.
