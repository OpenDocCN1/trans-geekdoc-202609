In a similar manner, authors in [58] propose a context-aware CF RS, for m-commerce environments, that aims to identify and exploit influence-based hierarchies among users. The authors claim that in any SN, there are some individuals who have some inspirational power over the others leading them to their decisions and behaviors. Recognizing on SN data, the system identifies “impressive” users and uses their profile information in an attempt to make more accurate recommendations and to resolve the cold-start problem.

In [59] A Simultaneous Extraction of Context and Community (SECC) model applicable on user context and group structures in pervasive computing environments is presented. As authors claim, the model can be utilized in many cases, including community-detection and CARSs. The proposed approach allows the seamless and simultaneous discovery of user latent patterns and user communities. The SECC model employs reasoning on user context and user interactions order to identify communities of users that have similar behaviors. This information is exploited for providing recommendations based on user profile and/or context behaviors. The SECC model adapts the theory of nested Dirichlet process, which allows a nested structure to be built to summarize data at multiple levels.

In the authors in [60] introduce the notion of pervasive computing systems and social media, called Community Interaction Spaces, in order to enhance interactions among users, communities, resources and smart environments. The proposed framework supports the formation of dynamic physical or virtual pervasive communities that demonstrate modalities for a nontrivial period. The formulated communities are exploited, among others, in support of mobile services proactive adaptation through the provision of personalized, context-aware recommendations. The system is able to build context-aware prediction models through data-mining techniques applied on recorded user-services histories of individuals that may belong to specific communities. A community member can then benefit from the knowledge—captured in the community behavior model—through recommendations for future user-device interactions and providing the respective recommendations. Each recommendation is accompanied by a confidence level that acts as an index for further handling of prediction by users or services. The triggering factor for initiating a recommendation can be either a performed user-service interaction or a user status update. The framework utilizes a hybrid approach for building the prediction model that allows the extraction and modeling of both sequential patterns of user-service interactions and situation/context-based patterns of interactions. For the first discrete time, variable order Markov chains (VOMC) are employed, while for the second, an adaptation of naive Bayes (NB) statistical model is utilized.

In more details, the history of recorded user-service interactions is modeled as $s_t^k$ at time of observation $t_k$ ($k=1, 2, \dots, K$), where the respective user interaction is modeled as $s^k=\{c_1^k, c_2^k, \dots, c_n^k\}$, where $c_i^k$ is the value of context information of type $i$, ($i=1, 2, \dots, n$). Given a sequence of actions $d=\{a^{t-1}, a^{t-2}, \dots, a^{t-l}\}$ where $\ell$ is the sequence length, the most probable next action is identified based on VOMC by maximizing the respective probability:

$$d_{seq}^k = \argmax_{a_{seq} \in A} [P[a^k|d]] = \argmax_{a_{seq} \in A} \left\{ \frac{N(a^k \cdot \dots \cdot a^{k-\ell})}{N(a^{k-\ell} \cdot \dots \cdot a^{k-1})} \right\}$ (4.5)$$

Given an update of user's situation, the most probable user interaction in a current snapshot described by $s^k = [c_j^k | j = 1, \dots, \ell]$, is estimated by the NB model through maximization of the respective probability:

$$d_{sit}^k = \argmax_{a_{sit} \in A} [P[a^k|s^k]] = \argmax_{a_{sit} \in A} \left( P(a^k) \times \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell} P(c_j^k)}{P(s^k)} \right) (4.6)$$

Combining the two approaches, each outcome is weighted on the number of occurrences contributed in probability calculation. The interaction predicted based on both inputs is estimated by:

$$a_{comb}^k = \argmax_{a_{comb} \in A} [w_{sit} \cdot P[a^k|s^k] + w_{seq} \cdot P[a^k|d]]$ (4.7)$$

where $w_{sit}$ and $w_{seq}$ reflect the amount of history records used for the situation and sequence based user interaction prediction. Evaluation of the proposed approach with real datasets described [60], yielded satisfactory results especially in cases where users with no previous interaction histories were utilizing the community's history log with the hybrid prediction mechanism in support of their personal predictions.

Combining social media information along with context for improving recommendations is a promising approach; however involves various challenges. One important aspect is that often neglected is the issue of the privacy of the users. Collecting and processing interactions not only from the online behavior of the users (e.g., social media) but also from real life activities (e.g., context types such as user's location, physical activities, mood) poses serious threats to users' privacy. Applying the appropriate privacy protection mechanisms often increases the complexity but necessary overhead to the overall functionality. Beyond user privacy risks, recommender systems are also vulnerable to security threats. For example, although application of CARS in e-commerce services, at the same time vulnerable to security threats also known as “shilling attacks” [61]. The attacker (e.g., malicious users) attempts to insert fake profiles into user-item matrices to alter the predicted ratings.
