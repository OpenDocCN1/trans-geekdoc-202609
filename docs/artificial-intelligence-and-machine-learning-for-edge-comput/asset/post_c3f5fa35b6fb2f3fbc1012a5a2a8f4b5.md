
# 3 It takes two to tango: Future of artificial intelligence and machine learning in blockchain technology

Security is a major area of concern in traditional e-commerce projects [15]. Blockchain has numerous advantages, but there are also some implementations. For example, the threats and limitations of blockchain in health care are discussed in Ref. [16]. Fig 3 shows the blockchain technology of concerns where ML and AI can help. To overcome these limitations and concerns of blockchain technology, the capability of AI can be exploited.

### 3.1 Sustainability

Sustainability is the major concern in the blockchain technology as mining requires a lot of energy. AI has already proven its mettle in the optimization of power consumption. So, it can be utilized to employed.

### 3.2 Scalability

Scalability is another critical issue in the blockchain. The size of the blockchain is growing 1 MB in 600 s. Blockchain pruning [6] is not able to handle this. AI-powered federated learning or novel data-shredding techniques may be used here.

### 3.3 Security

Due to high energy consumption in the mining blockchain, it is almost impossible to hack into; but, its applications and layers are not secure as smart contracts in the blockchain are written by the programmers themselves. Here, we can exploit the ML capability of intrusion detection.

### 3.4 Privacy

AI- and ML-based models can prove useful to protect the privacy of blockchain data. For example, the HDG mobile app aims to automate medical histories without breaches in privacy [17].

### 3.5 Adaptability

AI- and ML-based models can accurately predict the adaptability of blockchain applications. The adaptability of the blockchain is predicted according to the needs of the business, the management as well as the employees' willingness to switch over a newer technology.

### 3.6 Efficiency

AI- and ML-based models minimize the uncertainty by predicting the most promising node to fulfill the certain mining activity.

### 3.7 Transaction speed

Optimization is the area where AI- and ML-based models excel. The large size of the ledger slows down the transaction speed. This issue can be solved with the optimization techniques of AI.

### 3.8 Performance

Mining performance can be improved with the help of ML models. ML techniques have the ability to learn from experience.

### 3.9 Validation of various elements

Validation of transactions and their verification may be done spontaneously by AI- and ML-powered models.

### 3.10 Maintainability

The maintainability of a blockchain ledger is a mammoth task. AI and ML models can automate the maintainability of the blockchain.

### 3.11 Scope estimation

AI can facilitate ML-based models to examine the scope of a blockchain project. The scope of blockchain in vertical horizontal processes can be proposed by ML models with proper training.

### 3.12 Cost estimation

The cost of blockchain projects/applications cannot be estimated with the help of customary approaches of cost estimation because of their underlying architecture. ML models can play an important role in the prediction of blockchain cost estimation in a more efficient way with minimal human interference.

### 3.13 Effort estimation

No traditional method of effort estimation for blockchain applications is available; here, ML and AI tools/applications may be used to accurately predict the effort required.

### 3.14 Project-specific modus operandi

There are four types of blockchain projects/applications:

- (i) Public blockchain or permission-less blockchain
   Example: Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum, etc.
- (ii) Private blockchain or permission-based blockchain
   Example: Sawtooth, Fabric, Corda, etc.
- (iii) Consortium blockchain or decentralized blockchain
   Example: R3, Energy Web Foundation.
- (iv) Hybrid blockchain (combination of the private and public blockchain)
   Example: Dragonchain, etc.

Training of ML models can be done on project-specific data. Project-specific modus operandi can be devised with the help of AI techniques.

# Edge computing: A potential use of blockchain

The idea of edge computing is to bring the computational capacity at the origin of "instant data" itself. Therefore, this paradigm of edge computing may serve better for delay-sensitive jobs by pushing the cloud computational capability to the edge of that network [18]. The backbone of edge computing is its distributed computational framework. The purpose of this is to bring the storage capacities and computational abilities of the data sources, devices, nearer to the enterprise business logic where they are actually needed [19-21]. The working of edge computing is different; the difference is that edge devices process the computation locally, whereas the cloud computing centralizes it in a single data center [22], as shown in Fig. 4. Edge computing has advantage over cloud computing in terms of latency and bandwidth edge devices have to send only individual results, after processing on the local data, to the edge servers.

Internet of Things (IoT) is one of the possible uses of edge computing. Let us use an example of store of clothing, where local and batch, both types of processing, are needed for inventory and sales. Geographically distributed outlets tally their inventory and sales and then forward the computational results to the head office each day. The top hierarchy, head office in this example is not interested in each transaction that happens at the outlet (edge) but in the final statistics of sales and inventory computed by the outlet (edge devices). By exhausting the capabilities of edge computing, we reshape almost every industry. Use cases of edge computing including telecommunication, health, financial services, media and entertainment, manufacturing, supply chain, retail, insurance, pharmaceuticals, and automotive with smart and smart city. Fig. 5 shows some of the uses of edge computing.

### 4.1 Edge computing architectures

The architecture of cloud computing and IoT may be expanded to the illusion diagram of edge computing architecture. Basilly, edge computing utilizes a multilayered distributed architecture to balance among between cloud layer, edge layer or edge network, and the edge layer that connects edge local server layer and edge device; a high-level illusion diagram of edge computing architecture is presented in Fig. 6. Fig. 7 represents a simplified edge architecture reference based on IBM edge computing architecture.

The top layer, or cloud microcloud, is used for analysis of large-scale data. It consists of a dashboard that may be the focus of device management and monitoring. This layer may have hundreds of devices. The size of software components as AI services and Models at each layer varies according to the size of data it has to process.

The second layer is the edge cloud or micro data centers that may be thought of as a local cloud for edge devices computing support. This layer is also considered as an edge network in the telecommunication field. Edge clouds can do most of the computing on their own without consulting the top layer, decreasing latency and minimizing bandwidth. This layer may have thousands of devices.

The third layer is the edge server layer. Edge devices communicate with edge servers with the help of agents installed in devices. Edge devices may do cloud computation by themselves, if more analytics are required then they communicate with the local edge for computation. These edge servers are also used to deploy required applications on the edge devices. This layer is home to millions of devices.

The bottom layer of architecture is the edge device layer. Edge devices are very compact and sophisticated with limited resources like less storage capacity, but are equipped with artificial intelligence and machine learning-based high-end software-as-a-service. For most of the tasks, without any resources or home of an enterprise cloud layer and/or edge server, these edge devices take care of real-time computations.
