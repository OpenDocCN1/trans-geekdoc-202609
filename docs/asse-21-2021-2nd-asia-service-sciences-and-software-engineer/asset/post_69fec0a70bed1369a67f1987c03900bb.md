## ABSTRACT

Bitcoin’s slow finality is an important factor preventing it from being widely used in real life. And other public single-chain blockchains have the same problem. In this paper, we propose a practical new protocol called Plume to achieve consensus with fast finality by utilizing a (slow) traditional blockchain. Plume combines the DAG structure and traditional blockchain to increase throughput and speed up confirmation. DAG structure is proposed to increase throughput, but how to construct a secure consensus protocol based on DAG is pretty hard. In Plume, we use the traditional blockchain to generate a randomness which is used to generate fast blocks in DAG. And the order of fast blocks in DAG is dependent on a global clock and a hash function. Same as bitcoin, Plume is a probabilistic protocol. However, with DAG structure, the creation rate of blocks in Plume can be higher without reducing security. Thus the confirmation time of blocks can be accelerated. This paper also analyzes the probability of confirmation in different conditions and also proves that Plume is secure no matter the randomness from traditional single chain is unbiased or not. At last, this paper simulates performance with 1000 nodes using NS-3. The throughput can be improved to 400 Tps in the case that block size is 100K bytes and time interval of fast block is 1 second. Also the confirmation time can be reduced to within 2 minutes in the case that block size is 10K bytes and time interval is 1 second.

## 1 INTRODUCTION

As a new technology, blockchain has great potential to be applied in various fields including finance [12] [19] [18], Internet of Things [6] and supply chain [15]. Bitcoin designed in 2008 is the first application of blockchain, and brought its underlying technology, blockchain has protocol, to the public. Later on, the security of blockchain has been proved by [5] and [13], which provide security assurance for the use of blockchain. According to [5], we must limit the rate of growth to a certain range to avoid causing too many forks to decrease the computing power of honest parties.

What prevents blockchain from being widely used in real life is its throughput and confirmation time. On average, the throughput of Bitcoin is about 3 tx/s [1], and the confirmation time is more than 1 hour. Ethereum speeds up the rate of generating blocks to approximate 11s [2] and the confirmation time is not enough to deal with the actual scenes. Thus, it is important to find means to overcome these two problems.

Nowadays, there are two main methods to increase throughput, which are sharding [20] [7] and DAG [8]. Sharding divides the participants into several smaller committees so that committees can pack transactions in parallel to improve throughput. DAG changes the single-chain to directed acyclic graph where the generation of blocks can be accelerated without considering the network delay. The two ways can indeed increase throughput, but they cannot increase confirmation speed fundamentally.

Sompolinsky and others [17] found if a structure accepted all the blocks, it would remove the rate limitation of generating blocks without reduce the decentralization. DAG is such a structure. Therefore, our goal is to provide the DAG structure a natural sequence and achieve steady and fast confirmation. In this paper, we use the traditional single-chain blockchain to ensure overall security, and use the DAG structure to increase throughput and confirmation rate.

Intuitively, we utilise the traditional single-chain blockchain to get randomness which can be used to select committee to construct DAG structure and sort it. Every party in the network can get the qualification of creating blocks with a certain probability. As long as the traditional single-chain blockchain is secure, adversaries cannot control the order of our DAG structure. And our DAG structure has a natural sequence that comes from the randomness, thus, we can get a steady confirmation.

We use the DAG structure to get rid of the speed limit of generating blocks. In the single-chain blockchain, a block cannot be generated until its previous block broadcasts almost entire network to avoid forks. While as for DAG, we do not need to wait for one block to go all over the network to generate the next block, because all the blocks would included in the DAG at last. At the same time, due to the usage of stable blocks belonging to a secure single-chain and the shorter interval between two blocks, the confirming time is improved.

Plume provides a practical DAG protocol which contains:
- how to generate a block in DAG;
- giving a unique global number for all blocks in DAG;
- giving an analysis of when a block can be confirmed.

This paper is organised as following. In section 3, we provide some definitions of data structure and security. In section 4 we give the detail of Plume, and in section 5 we analyze the probability of confirmation in different conditions and the security of Plume when the randomness from single-chain is unbiased and biased. At last, section 6 gives the simulation of Plume using NS-3 with 1000 nodes.

## 2 RELATED WORK

Thunderella [14] is proposed by Pass and Eilam in 2018 to speed up the confirmation of public chain. It needs a trusted accelerator to collect transactions and output them in order, then committee votes for these sequential transactions and pack them to generate a consistent log. [14] can only guarantee the fast confirmation in the optimistic case, but fall back to the traditional blockchain in the worst-case, and it depends on a designated entity called accelerator. In relation to Thunderella, Plume proves a stable and fast confirmation without losing the decentralization.

In the past few years, several DAG-based consensus protocols have been proposed. SPECTRE [16] specifies a non-transitive partial order for all pairs of blocks in the DAG, while Plume provides a global consistent unique number for all the blocks which is critical to support applications like smart contract. And as a PoW consensus protocol, ConfFlux [9] uses PoW protocol to generate blocks which wastes a great deal of electricity. Because of the usage of GHOST [17] to decide which branch as the valid one when encountering forks, ConfFlux also suffers a balance attack. Balance attack [10] means that adversary which hold less than 50% computing power can make some blocks never be confirmed by disrupting the weaker one between two parts from the fork point. While in Plume, generation of fast block of DAG is according to the randomness provided by the underground single chain, which is economic. And the order protocol given by Plume resists the balance attack.

## 3 DEFINITIONS AND MODEL

In Plume, we have two kinds of blocks, one is the slow blocks in traditional single-chain blockchain, and the other is the fast blocks that can be used to construct DAG structure. Next, we will give formal descriptions of the two blocks. In subsequent contents, we call the traditional single-chain blockchain as slow chain, and the DAG-structure blockchain as fast chain.

### 3.1 Notations and security definitions of slow blockchain

**Slow blockchain notations.** We use $slowChain$ to denote a slow chain. The notation $slowChain^{(\ell)}$ denotes the entire slowChain except the trailing $\ell$ blocks; $slowChain[\ell : t]$ denotes the entire slowChain upto the block at time $t$; and $slowChain[\ell_1 : \ell_2]$ denotes all blocks at length between $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$. And we use the notation $slowChain_i^t$ to denote player $i$'s slowChain in round $t$. And symbol $\le$ denotes the prefix relationship.

**Security definitions.** A slow chain protocol should satisfy chain growth, chain quality, and common prefix [13, 15]. Intuitively, chain growth requires that honest nodes' blocks [5] grow steadily, neither too fast nor too slow, which is the reason why we call the traditional single-chain blockchain slow chain, and honest parties have chains of roughly the same length. Chain quality requires that in any honest node's chain, any sufficiently long window of consecutive blocks contains a certain fraction of blocks that are mined by honest nodes. Common prefix requests that all honest nodes' chains agree with each other except for the trailing few blocks. We will formally define these security properties below.

### 3.2 Execution Model

**Semi-synchronous Communication Setting.** We divide time into non-overlapping segments called slots. Messages sent from one honest node would be received by all the other honest nodes within $\Delta$ slots. And when a new node joins in this network, he can receive all the messages he missed. We defined that a epoch, in which a slow traditional block is generated on average, is composed of $R$ slots. At the beginning of each epoch, Plume selects a committee to generate DAG structure, and as the epoch changes, the committee would change.

**Ability of Adversary.** Adversaries in our analysis cannot prevent the transmission of messages, but they can delay the transmission of a message to an honest party by up to $\Delta$ slots and reorder the messages sent to others. Adversaries can also transmit their own messages to a part of honest parties, but after a period all the honest parties can receive these messages. Meanwhile, adversaries can corrupt honest nodes immediately at any time. Also adversaries can constantly adjust the strategy of mining fast or slow blocks and broadcasting them.

### 3.3 Notations and security definitions of fast blockchain

**DEFINITION 3.1. DAG structure**

A DAG of fast blocks is denoted $\mathcal{G} = (V,E)$, where $V$ represents fast blocks and $E$ represents the hash references to previous fast blocks. If a $B_F$ is in our DAG, we denote it as $B_F \in \mathcal{G}$. We define $Child[B_F]$ as the subset of fast blocks from which $B_F$ is reachable, and $Ancestor[B_F]$ as the subset of blocks reachable from $B_F$. $Parents[B_F]$ represents the blocks that are referenced by $B_F$ directly. $Slot[B_F]$ means the slot in which $B_F$ was generated. $Density[B_F, s_1, s_2]$ represents the number of $Child[B_F]$ between slot $s_1$ and $s_2$. And $Index[B_F]$ is the sequence number of $B_F$ after ordering. It is worth mentioning that $Index[B_F] \neq Slot[B_F]$, but for $Slot[B_F^{i_1}] < Slot[B_F^{i_2}]$, there must be $Index[B_F^{i_1}] < Index[B_F^{i_2}]$, and for each fast block $B_F$, its sequence number $Index[B_F]$ is unique.
