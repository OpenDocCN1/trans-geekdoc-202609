# 5.3 Security under biased randomness

In this section, we will prove that although our randomness is biased, it will not break the security of Plume. When the randomness is unbiased, the success probability of an adversary is shown in Table 2, but in Plume, an adversary can select a randomness that is better for him, so firstly, we analyze the success probability of adversary who can bias the randomness.

In Plume, we regard the hash value of a slow block that has been confirmed as the randomness. Malicious adversaries can select an optimal slow block to broadcast. The optimal slow block is that the randomness generated from it can make the number of elected malicious adversary is maximal. So we can compute the expectation of the maximal number of elected malicious adversary when they can select a randomness from $k$ randomneses. It means that adversaries can generate $k$ slow blocks at the same time, and we define the event as $A_k$. It is easy to get the happening probability of $A_k$ is $\gamma^k$ where $\gamma$ is the proportion of malicious adversaries' computing power.

We denoted the number of adversaries elected in $k$ times as $Y_1$, $Y_2, \cdots, Y_k$ respectively. And we denoted the variable $\mathbf{Z} = \textbf{Max}\{Y_1$, $Y_2, \cdots, Y_k\}$. We also consider $T$ slots, so $Y_1$, $Y_2, \cdots, Y_k$ all obey the normal distribution $N(\alpha T, (1-\alpha)T)$ (s.t., $\mu = (1-\alpha)\cdot T$, $\sigma = \sqrt{(1-\alpha)\cdot T}$). And we set $Y_i = \mu + \sigma \cdot Y_i'$, where $Y_i'$ obeys standard normal distribution. Then we can get $\mathbf{Z} = \textbf{Max}\{Y_1, Y_2, \cdots, Y_k\} = \mu + \sigma \cdot \textbf{Max}\{Y_1', Y_2', \cdots, Y_k'\}$. For the sake of explanation, we denoted $\textbf{Max}\{Y_1', Y_2', \cdots, Y_k'\}$ as $Z'$. And we can get the expectation of $Z'$ by the equation (1), where $\Phi(y)$ is the distribution function of standard normal distribution, and get the expectation of $\mathbf{Z}$ by the equation (2).

$$\mathbb{E}[Z'] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y \cdot \frac{d(\Phi(y)^k)}{dy} dy \tag{1}$$
$$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}] = \mu + \sigma \cdot \mathbb{E}[Z'] = (1-\alpha) \cdot T + \sqrt{(1-\alpha) \cdot T} \cdot \mathbb{E}[Z']. \tag{2}$$

We can see the growing trend of $\mathbb{E}[Z']$ as $k$ increases from 1 to 10 from Fig. 5. so the larger $k$ is, the higher the probability that adversaries can success will be. Next, we just consider the situation when $k = 10$, $\mathbb{E}[Z'] = 1.53873$. We set $T$ changes from 10 to 100 at an interval of 10, to compare the success probability under biased randomness with that in Table. 3 shows that when $\alpha = 0.9$ and $\alpha = 0.8$, the expectation of the maximal number of elected adversaries is smaller than our threshold all the time. It is to say that even though adversaries can prepare 10 slow blocks to select a randomness, they cannot double spend a confirmed transaction. Moreover, the probability of getting 10 slow blocks at the same time is only $\gamma^{10}$, where $\gamma < 0.5$. Table. 4 shows that when $\alpha = 0.7$ and $T = 10, 20$, the expectation of the maximal number of elected adversaries exceeds our threshold, but when $T \ge 30$, adversaries cannot success. So when $\alpha = 0.7$, we need to set $T \ge 30$ to prevent adversaries from breaking the security.

From the above, we can see that adversaries can increase the success probability by selecting randomness, but the success probability is negligible even if they have 10 chances to pick a preferable randomness.
