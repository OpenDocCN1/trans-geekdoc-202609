# **ABSTRACT**

Bitcoin’s slow finality is an important factor preventing it from being widely used in real life. And other public single-chain blockchains have the same problem. In this paper, we propose a practical new protocol called Plume to achieve consensus with fast finality by utilizing a (slow) traditional blockchain. Plume combines the DAG structure and traditional blockchain to increase throughput and speed up confirmation. DAG structure is proposed to increase throughput, but how to construct a secure consensus protocol based on DAG is pretty hard. In Plume, we use the traditional blockchain to generate a randomness which is used to generate fast blocks in DAG. And the order of fast blocks in DAG is dependent on a global clock and a hash function. Same as bitcoin, Plume is a probabilistic protocol. However, with DAG structure, the creation rate of blocks in Plume can be higher without reducing security. Thus the confirmation time of blocks can be accelerated. This paper also analyzes the probability of confirmation in different conditions and also proves that Plume is secure no matter the randomness from traditional single chain is unbiased or not. At last, this paper simulates performance with 1000 nodes using NS-3. The throughput can be improved to 400 Tps in the case that block size is 100K bytes and time interval of fast block is 1 second. Also the confirmation time can be reduced to within 2 minutes in the case that block size is 10K bytes and time interval is 1 second.

# 1 INTRODUCTION

As a new technology, blockchain has great potential to be applied in various fields including finance [12] [19] [18], Internet of Things [6] and supply chain [15]. Bitcoin designed in 2008 is the first application of blockchain, and brought its underlying technology, blockchain has protocol, to the public. Later on, the security of blockchain has been proved by [5] and [13], which provide security assurance for the use of blockchain. According to [5], we must limit the rate of growth to a certain range to avoid causing too many forks to decrease the computing power of honest parties.

What prevents blockchain from being widely used in real life is its throughput and confirmation time. On average, the throughput of Bitcoin is about 3 tx/s [1], and the confirmation time is more than 1 hour. Ethereum speeds up the rate of generating blocks to approximate 11s [2] and the confirmation time is not enough to deal with the actual scenes. Thus, it is important to find means to overcome these two problems.

Nowadays, there are two main methods to increase throughput, which are sharding[20][7] and DAG[8]. Sharding divides the participants into several smaller committees so that committees can pack transactions in parallel to improve throughput. DAG changes the single-chain to directed acyclic graph where the generation of blocks can be accelerated without considering the network delay. The two ways can indeed increase throughput, but they cannot increase confirmation speed fundamentally.

Sompolinsky and others [17] found if a structure accepted all the blocks, it would remove the rate limitation of generating blocks with our reduce the decentralization. DAG is such a structure. Therefore, our goal is to provide the DAG structure a natural sequence and achieve steady and fast confirmation. In this paper, we use the traditional single-chain blockchain to ensure overall security, and use the DAG structure to increase throughput and confirmation rate.

Intuitively, we utilise the traditional single-chain blockchain to get randomness which can be used to select committee to construct DAG structure and sort it. Every party in the network can get the qualification of creating blocks with a certain probability. As long as the traditional single-chain blockchain is secure, adversaries cannot control the order of our DAG structure. And our DAG structure has a natural sequence that comes from the randomness, thus, we can get a steady confirmation.

We use the DAG structure to get rid of the speed limit of generating blocks. In the single-chain blockchain, a block cannot be generated until its previous block broadcasts almost entire network to avoid forks.
