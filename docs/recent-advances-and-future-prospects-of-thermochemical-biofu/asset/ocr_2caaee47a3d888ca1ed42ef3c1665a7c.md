# 3.1. Fully data-driven ML models

# 3.1.1. Yield and composition of products under dry conditions

Generally, the condition of biomass used in thermochemical biofuel conversion processes can be divided into dry and wet conditions, depending on whether external water is added to the conversion processes. The moisture content of the biomass used in those processes was neglected for the classification of the processes. The yield and composition of biomass thermochemical conversion products depend on feedstock characteristics (e.g., proximate and ultimate compositions) and operating conditions, such as temperature, residence time, and heating rate under dry condition [24]. They are also the main input variables used in existing ML-based models. ML-based models with ultimate composition data showed better performance than that with proximate composition data when the same dataset was used in both cases [50]. However, the performance of ML-based models was significantly improved when both composition data were included as input variables [50]. It should be noted that the chemical and structural composition of biomass resources depends on the types of feedstocks [51]. In the case of algal feedstock, the major components are carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins, whereas those of lignocellulose feedstock

are hemicellulose, lignin, and cellulose [51–53]. Thus, some studies have also included the content of the major components as input variables for ML model development. The particle size of the biomass was also used as an input variable [27]. In the case of the operating conditions, the temperature, heating rate, retention time, and gas velocity were utilized as input variables.

The yield and composition of products and byproducts are typical output variables for ML-based models. The yield can be calculated as the ratio of the target product mass to the feedstock mass. When various combinations of input variables were tested, a R² value was reached to 0.862[54–56]. Onsree et al. developed a model incorporating operating environments (N₂, O₂, and CO₂) and showed high performance for predicting biochar yield (R² > 0.915) using datasets with 10 types of feedstocks [57]. The performance of models for predicting the elemental composition and carbon content of biochar ranged from R² = 0.66 [54] to R² = 0.8480 [56]. A higher heating value (HHV) is the calorific value of the fuel associated with quality [58]. For the prediction of the HHV of biochar, a R² value of 0.9052 was achieved by Onsree et al. [57].

For predicting the yield of bio-oil per biomass, a R² value of 0.95 was achieved by the ML model of Ullah et al. [59]. The yield of water, a liquid-phase byproduct produced during thermochemical conversion, was also predicted by ML models [60,61]. The ML prediction of the composition of bio-oil produced under dry conditions is yet to be reported, which might be attributed to the fact that there are insufficient datasets in dry conditions.

H₂, CO, CO₂, and CH₄ are major gas species that evolve from thermochemical conversion and their yields have been commonly considered as the outputs of ML models [62,63]. Yucel et al. used various datasets with different feedstock types to develop ML models for predicting the total gas yield and gas composition of biomass gasification [63]. The performance for the total gas yield and for the gas compositions of H₂, CO, CO₂, and CH₄ were indicated by R² = 0.86, 0.85, respectively. Tang et al. developed a ML model to predict the yields of H₂, CO, CO₂, and CH₄ with an R² value of 0.999 [62]. The model was developed using a relatively large amount of data (3831 data points), but only two feedstocks were used, implying the necessity for further validation of other feedstocks. Ascher et al. developed a ML model to predict the composition of versatile gas species, including H₂, CO, CO₂, CH₄, N₂, and C₂H₄ [50].
