## ABSTRACT
Biofuels have been widely recognized as potential solutions to addressing the climate crisis and strengthening energy security and sustainability. However, techno-economic and environmental challenges for the production of biofuels remain and complicated conversion processes and factors, such as materials and process design, need to be taken into consideration for solving the challenges, which is not easy. Machine Learning (ML) has been combined with the theories of thermochemical biofuel conversion processes to achieve accurate and efficient biofuel process modelling. In this review, existing ML applications to predict biofuel yield and composition are critically reviewed. The details of the input and output variables of the developed models for thermochemical biofuel conversion processes were summarized, and their development procedures were compared. Techno-economic analysis results incorporating ML applications in biofuels were also reviewed. Although developed models in literature showed good performance for their targets, respectively, they can hardly be applied to other feedstocks or operating conditions. To overcome the challenge and develop universal model, perspective approaches were suggested in this study. It was suggested that it is essential to develop systematic datasets to support more comprehensive machine learning-based modelling towards practical applications. Potential prospective research and development directions on machine learning-based thermochemical biofuel conversion process modeling were recommended, so that it can assist in the commercialization and optimization of various biofuel conversions leading to a sustainable and circular society.

## 1. Introduction
The utilization of renewable energy sources, including biomass, has increased worldwide to reduce an increase in the amount of greenhouse gases resulting in severe environmental challenges [1–3]. Among the various renewable resources, biomass including lignocellulosic and municipal wastes is unique in that it has been used for a long time, even in conventional energy systems and has received considerable interest for a few decades as a reliable alternative to fossil fuel [4]. The amount of power generated by biomass combustion reached 750 TWh in 2021,
and the supply from biomass is expected to increase to 100 EJ by 2050 [5,6]. The development of biomass energy conversion processes, based on e.g., biochemical and thermochemical methods, has been increasing in recent years owing to their low-emission characteristics, among other advantages [5,7,8]. In particular, biofuels have the potential to contribute to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (UNSDGs) by promoting affordable and clean energy (Goal 7), industry, innovation, and infrastructure (Goal 9), responsible consumption and production (Goal 12), and climate action (Goal 13) [9].

The main drawback of biochemical processes is their low reaction rate, owing to which hours, days, or weeks are usually required to complete the necessary reactions. In contrast, thermochemical conversion processes rather utilize thermal energy sources and chemical reactions to decompose biomass into various products [13–15]. Compared to biochemical processes, thermochemical processes are relatively fast, as reactions can occur within a few minutes or even seconds. They are also suitable for wider ranges of feedstocks [16].

Thermochemical conversion has been the most widely used method owing to its advantages and high conversion efficiency [2,12]. They can
be grouped into torrefaction [17], pyrolysis [18,19], gasification [20], and hydrothermal treatments [21]. Torrefaction is conducted at low temperatures (<320 °C) under the absence of oxygen or partial oxidizing gas in order to upgrade the fuel characteristics [17]. Pyrolysis is the thermal treatment of biomass under an inert environment to produce liquid biofuels, gases, and solid residues. Depending on the heating rate and residence time, pyrolysis is generally divided into slow, fast, and flash pyrolysis [22,23]. The efficacy and quality of pyrolysis products greatly depend on the heating rate, temperature, feedstock particle size, residence time, and moisture content [24,25].
