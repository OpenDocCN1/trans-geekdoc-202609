# 8.1. Introduction

El Salvador has been implementing the Flood Early Warning System since the impact of Hurricane Mitch in 1998. After this hydrometeorological event, many governmental and nongovernmental organizations recognized the flood vulnerability of the country and the lack of a suitable hydrometeorological monitoring system. As a result of that, many automatic hydrometeorological gauges were installed, flood emergency plans were implemented, and a flood forecast center was created in order to both monitor and predict the floods impacts.

One of the major components of the early warning system (WMO, 2018) is the monitoring and the flood forecasting, which allows to anticipate their impacts and reduce the properties damage and the loss of lives. A hydrological model constitutes the basis of a flood forecasting system. For this reason, many hydrological models have been built to predict the hydrological response in important catchment in El Salvador. For instance, Häggström and Lindström (1990) calibrated the conceptual HBV model to six rivers in Central America, which includes Grande de San Miguel catchment in El Salvador. They found that the calibration of the model was not satisfactory for Grande de San Miguel catchment, although the model has succeeded in simulating most of the flood peaks. In 2004, the Hydrological National Service of El Salvador (2004) reported a rainfall-runoff model for forecasting purposes in the Lempa catchment, which includes the four hydropower reservoir located in the main stream. Additionally, Riverside Technology (2012) implemented a semidistributed Sacramento rainfall-runoff model in Lake Ilopango and Jiboa Catchment, which was implemented in a Delft-FEWS standalone version, and integrated it with real-time and forecast data. In this research, the implementation of the Multilayer Perceptron Artificial Neural Network (MLP-ANN) technique is proposed as operational forecast model in order to address the short-range hydrological flood forecast in Grande de San Miguel river basin. This catchment generates floods almost every year and generates significant damage to transport infrastructure, houses, crop areas, and people evacuated. There is not an operational rainfall-runoff forecast model in this catchment that allows local forecast operators, stakeholders, and decision makers to foresee the flood conditions and increase the lead time for communities located in flood-prone areas to respond efficiently.
