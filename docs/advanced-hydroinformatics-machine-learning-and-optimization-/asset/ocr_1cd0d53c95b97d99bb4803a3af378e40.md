If we add the signal detail at a certain scale to the signal approximation, we get the signal approximation at an increased resolution; this is known as multiresolution representation.

However, is also possible to reconstruct the approximations and details themselves from their coefficients vectors. By making use of the multiresolution decomposition algorithm that allows generating all coefficients at any scale just by knowing the coefficients at previous scales.

$$S_{m+1,n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k c_k S_{m,2n+k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k c_{k-2n} S_{m,k}\quad\text{Addison (2010)},\quad (9.3)$$

$$T_{m+1,n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k b_k S_{m,2n+k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k b_{k-2n} S_{m,k}.\quad (9.4)$$

For the derivation of these formulas, the reader should consult Addison (2010). Many of the concepts explained here were taken from the aforementioned work.

Now in the two-channel subband encoder algorithm, there is a decomposition step that consists of retaining one coefficient of two (downsampling) known as decimation (no information repeats). The aforementioned algorithm is suitable for compression purposes, however, lack of redundancy in the coefficients creates a system whose outputs depend explicitly on time (shift-variant system), an undesirable property for forecasting problems. Another problem arises when choosing algorithms, the well-known cone of the influence region. Errors in the limit regions (especially in the right boundary) propagate within the decomposition (approximation and details coefficients) proportionally to the chosen scale within an area of the cone. This represents a problem for forecasting purposes, where at each time step a new entry is placed on the right-hand side of the boundary.
