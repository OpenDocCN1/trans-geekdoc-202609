# 5.1. Introduction

Watershed modeling helps understand the past and current state of rainfall-runoff processes in a watershed and provides a way to explore the implications of management and planning decisions and imposed forcing function changes such as land use and climate. In complex watersheds (in this case, coastal plain watersheds), very significant modeling efforts have been conducted based on both lumped and physically based simulations (Amatya & Jha, 2011; Samadi & Meadows, 2017; Samadi et al. 2017, 2020; among others). However, rapid increase in the availability of measurement data has spurred the development of many data-driven methods or machine learning (ML) approaches for modeling and predicting rainfall-runoff dynamics. Indeed, in the realm of process-based hydrologic modeling, time-series ML algorithms have been recently utilized to improve short- and long-term streamflow predictions at various scales and domains (e.g., Solomatine & Dulal, 2003; Solomatine & Shrestha, 2009; Corzo et al., 2009; Shrestha et al., 2014; Fang et al., 2017; Kratzert et al., 2018; Shen, 2018; Shen et al., 2018; Wu et al., 2018). ML algorithms attempt to estimate the mapping function (f) from the input variables (x) to numerical or continuous output variables (y) and can learn patterns hidden in data. Different ML algorithms make different assumptions about the shape and structure of the function and how best to optimize a representation to approximate it.
