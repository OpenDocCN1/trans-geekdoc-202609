(2017) showed that the Strupczewski method (Strupczewski, 1964) has better suitability to both gauged and ungauged stations compared with UPO ERR Gamma function, which is also a widely used method (Baziak & Gądek, 2019). However, these two functions have complex structures. Each of them has five parameters, some of which are difficult to determine. Triangular functions were also studied in the areas that have undetermined hydrographs (Gama, 2012). The disadvantages of this approach are the limited graphs shapes and bad representation of real hydrographs, even though their parameters are much fewer and more comprehensible. On the contrary, Gamma-like function with four parameters shows good shape and is able to generate different peak values, time to peak, skewness, and base flow when sampling. The function is

```
$Q_t = Q_b + (Q_p - Q_b) \times \left(\frac{t}{T_p}\right)^{\frac{1}{S_k - 1}} \times \exp\left(\frac{1 - \left(\frac{t}{T_p}\right)}{S_k - 1}\right), \quad (14.6)$
```

where, $Q_t$ is calculated discharge at time $t$; $t$ is time (counting from 0 with unit of time step); $Q_b$ is base flow; $Q_p$ is peak discharge; $T_p$ is time to peak (the interval counting from the beginning of hydrograph to peak time); $S_k$ is the factor of skewness. However, one Gamma-like function can have only one peak, which for many flood events is not realistic. The available data of the hydrograph from 2007 (Fig. 14.1) indicates two possible subsequent flood peaks. Therefore, an alternative method was proposed, to assign different values to parameters for different periods, selected in such a way as to generate two peaks formed within one hydrograph (Fig. 14.7). In that approach, each parameter (base flow discharge $Q_b$, peak value $Q_p$, time to peak $T_p$, and skewness $S_k$) is sampled twice, and therefore there are eight parameters in total for one hydrograph: $Q_{b1}$, $Q_{b2}$, $Q_{p1}$, $Q_{p2}$, $T_{p1}$, $T_{p2}$, $S_{k1}$, and $S_{k2}$.

Another critical element for sampling is the selected probability distribution of parameters, which influences the diversity of sampled results. The aim here is that the generated hydrographs are diverse as much as possible, but still have sufficient resemblance to the real hydrographs. In other words, sampled hydrographs should cover the conditions that may happen in future, which in fact represents the uncertainty.
