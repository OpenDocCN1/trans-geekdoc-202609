The difference from Step 2 is the performance evaluation. The root mean square error (*RMSE*) is used to evaluate performance in this stage. It can be described as

$$RMSE=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(S(i)-O(i))^2},\tag{3.7}$$

where $S(i)$ is *NSE* or *Bias* calculated by SM with the $i^{th}$ set of parameters; $O(i)$ is *NSE* or *Bias* calculated by VIC with the $i^{th}$ set of parameters.

If the values of *ER* of SM-I and *RMSE* of SM-II are less than the allowance error set by users, SM is built. Otherwise, we set $N=N+1$ and return to Step 1 to acquire more samples to train SM-I and SM-II until the *ER* and *RMSE* values meet the requirement.

## Performance Metric of the Surrogate Model

The Pareto solutions of SM are acquired and the parameter sets given by SM are validated with VIC. The distance between the center points of the two-point groups (one is the Pareto fronts of SM and the other is the points consisting of the objective values, which are calculated with the Pareto solutions of SM in VIC) is used to evaluate the performance of SM. Here, we define a center point as a point with the minimum sum of distance to all the points, which is shown as

$$\min \sum_i d=\sum_{i}^{n}\lVert x_0-x_i\rVert,\tag{3.8}$$

where the vector $x_0$ is the position of center point and the vectors $x_i$ ($i=1, 2, 3, \ldots, n$) are the positions of $n$ Pareto optimal points; $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ is the norm of vector. If the distance of the two center points is less than a specified value, the parameter calibration is complete. Otherwise, SM will be updated with the data mixing the original samples, which is used in the previous iteration with the validated values and then the next iteration will be conducted.

The maximum generations per run and maximum number of populations were specified as 250 and 100,000, respectively. To reduce the runtime of VIC parameters optimization, the program would be terminated when the solutions were not improved in 30 generations.

$$m = \sqrt{n + l} + a,\tag{3.9}$$

where m is the number of nodes in hidden layer, n is the number of neural cells in network input layer, l is the number of neural cells in output layer, a is a number between 0 and 10. The structure was determined as 7-10-1. The numbers indicated that there are 7 nodes in the input layer, 10 nodes in the hidden layer, and one output in this BPNN model.

The default values suggested by Beale et al. (2010) are employed in the training of weak learners for other parameters. In our study, 10 weak predictors were trained and then assembled into a strong predictor. In order to make sure that SM meets the demand of the accuracy in our study, we use the hold-out cross validation to validate the training results. In this study, when the *ER* value in SM-I is less than 10% and the *RMSE* value in SM-II is less than 5% of the crucial range, the training of the two-stage surrogate model is completed.
