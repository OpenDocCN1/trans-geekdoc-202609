LSTM can utilize more hyperparameters and, because the extent of the short-term memory can be defined, LSTM tends to outperform SARIMA (Seasonal-ARIMA).

We could find only two published research papers presenting explicit water-level modeling in the Everglades. The first was a study by Conrads and Roehl (2007) where an ANN was used to hindcast water levels. The second and more applicable to this study focused on prediction boundaries and forecasting of nonlinear hydrological stage data in the A.R.M. Loxahatchee National Wildlife Refuge, the northern-most extent of the remnant Everglades (Park et al., 2005). An ANN model was developed to forecast stage over a 16 yr period from just 2 yr of training data, with forecast horizons of 1, 2, 8, and 14 days. This model was compared with linear models that forecast stages, and the results showed that the ANN model outperformed the linear models, most notably at the longer lead times. Additional methods for evaluating machine-learning models, such as visualization tools, were not applied in this study.

Apart from these examples, the models that currently exist for forecasting water level are numerical models, which simulate overall regional hydrological conditions in the entire, or specific parts, of the Everglades and are used by water managers. One of these models is the Regional Simulation Model (RSM), a surface water-groundwater model that solves for water stages and flows under natural (predrainage system) conditions as well as under controlled conditions such as the operation of water-control structures. The RSM quantifies changes in groundwater levels, groundwater and overland flow, and wetland hydroperiods, among other things (SFWMD, 2006). The RSM is solved for an irregular triangular mesh grid, which is considered an improvement on the South Florida Water Management Model (SFWMM), which has a square grid size of 3.2 x 3.2 km (SFWMD, 2008). The SFWMM simulates the hydrology and management of the water resources system from Lake Okeechobee to Florida Bay and is a tool for assessing operational changes to the complex water management system in South Florida (SFWMD, 2005).

Another regional simulation model, and the one used for comparison in this study, is the Biscayne and Southern Everglades Coastal Transport (BISECT) model, which simulates hydrologic conditions in various scenarios (Swain et al., 2019). This model uses dynamic equations so that surface water is fully represented, making it suitable for comparison in this study. One of the disadvantages of the BISECT and possibly other regional models is the averaging of inputs and outputs over each grid cell, which reduces the extent of peaks and troughs in the predictions. As a result, the BISECT model is more suitable for long-term regional analysis of hydrological conditions, than it is for local, shorter-term predictions (Swain et al., 2019).
