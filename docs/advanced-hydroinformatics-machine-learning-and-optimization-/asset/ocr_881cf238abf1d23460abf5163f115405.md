Coastal plain modeling is a substantial challenge as this type of simulation is tempered by the heterogeneity in basin characteristics (low-lying elevation, evapotranspiration, wide floodplains, etc.) and complexities in soil storage capacity due to extensive alluvial and nonalluvial forested wetlands and riparian storage (e.g., Shirmohammadi et al., 1986; Samadi et al., 2017). This research simulated a coastal plain rainfall-runoff process using three different regression-based ML algorithms including SVM, Bayesian Lasso, and RF. While the SVM and RF models have been widely used for rainfall-runoff simulations (see Loos & Elsenbeer, 2011; Wang et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2016, among others), Bayesian Lasso has rarely been implemented for hydrologic modeling problems (Das Bhowmik et al., 2018) and have never performed for a coastal plain hydrologic simulation. This study is thus the first attempt to implement a Bayesian Lasso algorithm to exploit model inference via posterior distributions for a coastal plain rainfall-runoff simulation problem. This advanced algorithm, in addition to more widely used SVM and RF approaches, is employed to glean information from geospatial data and weather information and train proposed supervised learning models for daily streamflow simulation. Various ML simulations were also compared with the simulation result of a well-known conceptual rainfall-runoff model (i.e., Sacramento Soil Moisture Accounting (SAC-SMA)). It is important to note that the modeling approach presented here is general and applicable to any rainfall-runoff simulation problem, although the actual interpretations of the modeling diagnostics may vary from application to application.

Results and findings are outlined in section 5.3. A detailed discussion of ML’s potential use in rainfall-runoff applications is presented in section 5.4.
