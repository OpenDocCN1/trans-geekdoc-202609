## 8.5. Experimental Setups

### 8.5.1. Data Analysis for Input Variable Selection

The goal of the MLP-ANN forecast model is to generalize the relation between the input (past rainfall, potential evapotranspiration, and discharge) and output (discharge at different lead time). One of the first tasks is to identify the maximum forecast horizon, which is possible to predict discharge using observed data. This is done by an analysis of the physical characteristics of the basin that allow one to identify the reaction or response time. Figure 8.4 shows the observed mean areal rainfall and discharge in La Canoa station for a high-flow event. It is possible to conclude that the time between the rainfall event (20 Nov, 15:00) and the discharge peak (21 Nov, 03:00) is 12 hr. Certainly, this response or reaction time can vary in different high-flow events since it depends on the spatial distribution of the rainfall or the soil moisture. Nonetheless, the rainfall is considered an average calculation across the catchment. Hence, different MLP-ANN models will be trained to predict discharge at 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, and 12 hr ahead.

The selection of the model inputs in the MLP-ANN hydrological forecasting is extremely important. Another important task is the selection of the appropriate lags since the dynamic of the catchment can be approximated by a set of variables that are properly discretized and lagged with respect to the output discharge (Solomatine & Dulal, 2003). For this study, the hydrometeorological variables that influence the predicted discharge at La Canoa station are the rainfall, evapotranspiration, and discharge itself. However, it is important to state that not all of these variables have a substantial influence on the output discharge. The analysis of interdependency of variables allows one to identify the hydrometeorological variables and lags that have a major influence on the output prediction. In this way, Figure 8.5 presents the Correlation Coefficient (CoC) and the Average Mutual Information (AMI) analysis between the discharge at 1 hr ahead and the rainfall, evapotranspiration, and discharge itself. It can be seen that the past discharge provides the maximum AMI and CoC at a lag 1 hr and then start decreasing, which means that past discharge has a major effect on predicting the discharge itself. Additionally, the AMI and CoC for the rainfall variable start rising at a lag around 10 hr, and then start decreasing at a lag around 14 hr; this behavior means that the rainfall data from 10 to 14 time steps back can be used as a model input.
