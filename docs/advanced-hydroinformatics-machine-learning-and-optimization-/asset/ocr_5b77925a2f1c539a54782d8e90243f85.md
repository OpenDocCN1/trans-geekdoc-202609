The LSTM models for BCA11 and NP201 have 32 nodes in the hidden layer while those for the remaining stations have 8 nodes, based on the trials conducted. The Root Mean Squared Propagation (RMSProp) optimizer was used in this study, and the loss (error) function was the mean squared error. The optimizer is a gradient descent optimization algorithm, which divides the gradient by the moving average of the root mean squared gradient, first proposed and explained in an online course entitled "Neural Networks for Machine Learning" (Tieleman & Hinton, 2012), and is thought to be first used in Graves (2013). More recently, it was used in a study on stochastic optimization and was shown to be beneficial in MLP ANNs, although it is typically used in recurrent neural networks (Huk, 2020).

The LSTM was built similarly to the TDNN using the same number of nodes in the hidden layer, the same optimizer, and the same activation functions. The length of the historical sequence that the model was trained on was 10 days. Trials were made for sequence window lengths of 5, 7, 10, and 20 days.

Cross-validation was carried out using the Keras *EarlyStopping* “callback” (https://www.tensorflow.org/api_docs/python/tf/keras/callbacks/EarlyStopping) feature, which monitors the error (loss) on the cross-validation subset, and terminates training when the error no longer improves after a consecutive number of training loops or epochs. To ensure training did not stop before error was minimized, a “patience” of 100 epochs was specified. This ‘patience’ means that 100 consecutive epochs need to pass without the error of the cross-validation set improving, before training is terminated. The callback was set up to ensure that the weights that resulted in the lowest cross-validation error were restored after training is completed.

Once configured, each model could be trained and the total number of epochs specified for training was 500. After training was completed, the TDNN models were initially tested using the randomly selected test set, and then were further tested to predict the 3 yr sequential time series. The LSTM models were tested only on the 3 yr sequential time series. The simulation time step for the models is 24 hr. In order to forecast lead times of 7 and 10 days, forecasts had to be made for lead times 4–6 days and 8–9 days, respectively, in order to get the necessary WL inputs as indicated in Table 7.2.
