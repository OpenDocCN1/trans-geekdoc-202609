The propagation time from Hengpaitou to Lutaizi is 36 hr (Pan, 2002), which can be considered as lead time for real-time operation in this study. As described in Jonoski et al. (2018), the optimization of the storage area gate operation is dependent on the simulated water level results, which are obtained from the simulation model with input of nearly 3 months discharge hydrograph (Fig. 14.2). Therefore, implementation of the optimization process with incoming real-time data is not adopted, and the use of optimal strategies obtained from preanalysis described previously (using historical data) is used in this study.

Using the database of optimized strategies obtained from the sampled hydrographs (already developed with ROPAR), the decision to be implemented is made according to the most similar hydrograph in that database to the expected real hydrograph, which is called *similarity selection*. As described previously, having forecasts about the whole hydrograph is practically impossible, given its very long duration, so the similarity assessment is restricted to the area of the hydrograph around the peaks. A threshold value of $Q_0$, 5,500 m³/s, is adopted, and only discharges above this value are considered in the similarity assessment. The proposed criteria for estimating this similarity are as follows:

+   1. The first criterion compares the volume of flood peaks where discharge is larger than 5,500 m³/s ($Q > Q_0$). This method was proposed because the volume of the upper part of a hydrograph is one of the most critical elements influencing flood severity downstream. If part of this volume is allocated to the storage areas, the flood peak(s) downstream (at Bengbu City) will be reduced. The criterion of selecting the most similar hydrograph is

$$\min FV = \left|V^R_{Q>Q_0} - V^S_{Q>Q_0}\right|,$$  
(14.14)

where, $FV$ is the notion of the flood volume method; $V_{Q>Q_0}$ is the flood volume of discharge larger than $Q_0$; $Q_0$ is a threshold value (5,500 m³/s); the superscripts $R$ and $S$ mean real hydrograph and sampled hydrograph, respectively.

+   2. The second criterion uses Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency coefficient (NSE), which gives an indicator about closeness between two hydrographs. Larger NSE means better match. Focusing again only on the upper part of the hydrograph, the equation used for similarity assessment is

$$\max NSE = 1 - \frac{\sum_{t=t_1}^{t_2}\left(Q^S_{t,Q>Q_0} - Q^R_{t,Q>Q_0}\right)^2}{\sum_{t=t_1}^{t_2}\left(Q^R_{t,Q>Q_0} - \overline{Q^R_{Q>Q_0}}\right)^2},$$
