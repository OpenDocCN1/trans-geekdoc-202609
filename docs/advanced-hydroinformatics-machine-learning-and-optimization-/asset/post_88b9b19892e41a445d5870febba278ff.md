### 9.2.2 The Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT)

The wavelet transform of a continuous signal for the wavelet function is defined as

T(a, b) = w(a) ∫_{−∞}^{∞} x(t) ψ*((t − b)/a) dt Addison (2010). (9.1)

Usually w(a) (weighting function) is normalized to 1/√a in the sense of the wavelet energy, and the convolution is between the signal x(t) and the wavelet function ψ. In the wavelet, the dilation and contraction are governed by the dilation parameter a, and the movement is governed by the translation parameter b. The shifted and dilated version of the mother wavelet is denoted ψ[(t − b)/a]. The normalized wavelet function is often written more compactly as ψ_{a,b}(t) = 1/√a ψ((t − b)/a); so the wavelet transform can be written as T(a, b) = ∫_{−∞}^{∞} x(t) ψ*_{a,b}(t) dt; T(a, b) = ⟨x, ψ_{a,b}⟩. The product of the wavelet and the signal integrated over the window length of the wavelet-like function is called convolution.

### 9.2.3 The discrete wavelet transform (DWT)

The a and b parameter can be sampled with a logarithmic discretization of the a scale, then to move in discrete steps b by making b proportional to a, ψ_{m,n}(t) = 1/√(a₀^m) ψ((t − n b₀ a₀^m)/a₀^m). When the discrete wavelet parameters a₀ and b₀ are chosen to be 2 and 1, respectively, the arrangement is known as the dyadic grid, ψ_{m,n}(t) = 1/√(a₀^m) ψ((t − n b₀ a₀^m)/a₀^m) = 1/√(2^m) ψ((t − n 2^m)/2^m).

The discrete wavelet transform values, given on a scale location grid of index m, n, are known as detail coefficients and they are expressed as follows T_{m,n} = ∫_{−∞}^{∞} 1/√(2^m) ψ((t − n 2^m)/2^m) x(t) dt. The scaling function is associated with the smoothing of the signal and it has the same form as the wavelet, given by φ_{m,n}(t) = 1/√(2^m) φ((t − n 2^m)/2^m). When the scaling function is convolved with the signal, the approximation coefficients are produced; S_{m,n} = ∫_{−∞}^{∞} 1/√(2^m) φ((t − n 2^m)/2^m) x(t) dt. We can represent the original signal x(t) by doing the inverse process, through a combined series expansion using the approximation and details coefficients:

$$x(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} S_{m_0,n}\,\phi_{m_0,n}(t) + \sum_{m=-\infty}^{m_0} \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} T_{m,n}\,\psi_{m,n}(t),\quad (9.2)$$

Addison (2010), where the signal detail is defined as $d_m(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} T_{m,n}\,\psi_{m,n}(t)$. If we add the signal detail at a certain scale to the signal approximation, we get the signal approximation at an increased resolution; this is known as multiresolution representation.

However, it is also possible to reconstruct the approximations and details themselves from their coefficients vectors. By making use of the multiresolution decomposition algorithm that allows generating all coefficients at any scale just by knowing the coefficients at previous scales.

$$S_{m+1,n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k c_k S_{m,2n+k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k c_{k-2n} S_{m,k}\quad\text{Addison (2010)},\quad (9.3)$$

$$T_{m+1,n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k b_k S_{m,2n+k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_k b_{k-2n} S_{m,k}.\quad (9.4)$$

For the derivation of these formulas, the reader should consult Addison (2010). Many of the concepts explained here were taken from the aforementioned work.

Now in the two-channel subband encoder algorithm, there is a decomposition step that consists of retaining one coefficient of two (downsampling) known as decimation (no information is repeated). The aforementioned algorithm is suitable for compression purposes, however, lack of redundancy in the coefficients creates a system whose outputs depend explicitly on time (shift-variant system), an undesirable property for forecasting problems.

Another problem arises when choosing algorithms, the well-known cone of influence. Errors in the limit regions (especially in the right boundary) propagate within the decomposition (approximation and details coefficients) proportionally to the chosen scale within an area of the cone. This represents a problem for forecasting purposes, where at each time step a new entry is placed on the right-hand side of the boundary.
