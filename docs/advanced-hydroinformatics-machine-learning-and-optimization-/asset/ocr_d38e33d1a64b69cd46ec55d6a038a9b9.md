The K-mean based cluster analysis is applied to every single image, which normally generates three clusters: (1) cluster 1 represents and area outside of the boundary (no data, or invalid data); (2) cluster 2 (valid data within the boundary but below certain threshold, e.g. zero rainfall), and (3) cluster 3 of valid rainfall patterns.

If the inputs are rainfall data sets, only the rainfall amount extracted from the data sets is used for pattern recognition. In this case, regions with rainfall amount higher than a given threshold x are to be extracted. This is done by using the toolbox to automatically highlight the grids whose rainfall is over x before detecting the outer boundary (the closed boundary) of each cluster of grids. The information of each boundary (e.g., the coordinates of the boundary grid) is recorded separately and stored. An example is given in the right panel of Figure 12.1 where there are seven different patterns recognized whose grid rainfall is higher than the threshold x and each of them is within a closed boundary and regarded as an individual rainfall event.

Each rainfall pattern, such as the one represented by cluster 3, extracted from images or from the seven regions detected from grid-based data sets, is then polygonised using the original function provided by the toolbox. The resultant polygons (regions) of rainfall are usually large and irregular. They are then segmented into small regular shapes to calculate the centroid and the moments of the compound shape. Finally, three attributes are generated for the segmented shapes (regions): the location attribute, the size attribute, and the shape attribute, which includes both the sp and the major angle ω which indicates the angle between easting and the major axis (northward; see the arrow in Fig. 12.2) and in the range of 0° and 180°. The three attributes are then used to label and classify the rainfall pattern. Besides, when the inputs are data sets, in addition to the three spatial attributes, other statistical attributes of interest can be extracted from the detected regions for quantitative analysis (e.g., extracting the maximum areal or point rainfall within the boundary of the region).
