Robustness refers to the ability of a system to continue with its functioning when meeting uncertainty conditions (Jen, 2003). Optimization of robustness aims to identify solutions that can maximize system robustness. Taguchi (1986) is one of the persons who initially estimated uncertainty to optimize robustness by minimizing mean square deviation of a given design objective. Such a method that regularizes original function (e.g., mean or variance of objective functions value in the proximity of a certain solution) has widely been used later in robustness optimization research even in water-related fields (Andricevic & Kitanidis, 1990; Babayan et al., 2005; Hollinshead & Lund, 2006; Malekmohammadi et al., 2009). Another popular method is to optimize two different objective functions for solving multi-objective problems, which often introduces Pareto-optimal method when these objectives are conflicting with each other (Beyer & Sendhoff, 2007). For example, Jung et al. (2013) proposed three formulas of quantifying tunnel pressure robustness, which were separately coupled with a specified economic cost function, in order to find out the most suitable way of describing system robustness. The authors compared three Pareto fronts and identified the function representing the sum of nodal pressure robustness index as the best one to generate least vulnerable solutions. The mentioned approaches of robust optimization solve given standard optimization problem, but they are unable to present the impact of uncertainties (Marquez-Calvo & Solomatine, 2019). To address this, Solomatine (2012) proposed an approach explicitly presenting the effect of uncertainty in multi-objective optimization problems by generating multiple Pareto fronts, which is the so-called Robust Optimization and Probabilistic Analysis of Robustness (ROPAR). Marquez-Calvo and Solomatine (2019) further developed this method so that decisions can be made depending on the uncertainty analysis. This is quite recent methodology, which needs to be applied in more practical case studies for further confirmation of its value.

Another area of increasing importance is real-time operation. Over the last decades, many researchers combined optimization and real-time operation in order to seek real-time optimal solutions, which is also introduced in the field of water management. There are two prominent categories of approaches to use optimization in the real-time system. The first one is to integrate optimization algorithm with real-time simulation models in which case a forecasting model is often introduced (Shamir & Salomons, 2008; Fallah-Mehdipour et al., 2012; Che & Mays, 2015; Odan et al., 2015). With this method, decisions are determined based on present observation and future possible situations.
