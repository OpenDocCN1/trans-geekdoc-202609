Figure 5.2 shows the observed and simulated daily streamflow using the ML and SAC-SMA methods during calibration and validation periods. As shown, the Bayesian Lasso model performed well particularly during high flow events (flood events). RF, on the other hand, performed satisfactorily while SVM and SAC-SMA showed similar performances. Although, all models were unable to simulate very high streamflow records caused by extraordinary precipitation events.

Figure 5.3 illustrates the FDCs for the ML, SAC-SMA, and observation during the calibration and validation. As shown, the SAC-SMA successfully calibrated intermediate flows while somewhat underestimated, and overestimated high- and low-flow events. Moreover, the SAC-SMA underestimated the peak rates considerably compared with the Bayesian Lasso model. The SAC-SMA model uses a lumped fashion to calibrate soil moisture states and the basin’s relative permeability. These states are fed as several key basin’s parameters into the model, such as interflow, evapotranspiration, and percolation. It seems hydrologic variables such as loss (due to dense vegetation) and soil moisture have strong control on rainfall-runoff processes and mechanisms in the study region. These factors determine catchment wetness conditions and evapotranspiration processes. Thus, runoff magnitude might abruptly increase when the catchment initial abstraction threshold was exceeded.
