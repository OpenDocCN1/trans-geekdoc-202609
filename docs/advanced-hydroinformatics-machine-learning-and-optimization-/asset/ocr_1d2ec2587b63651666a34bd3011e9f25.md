If parameters converge slowly in other cases, it would take a longer time to derive the crucial ranges. In these cases, the crucial ranges can be set by obeying the rule of thumb or referring to previous work. Furthermore, the crucial ranges can also be determined or updated by the results of the previous parameter optimization. However, in this study, we have not studied the relationship of the ADBP's performance to the model improving times.

In our work, we used the distance between the center point of FNP_SM and the center point VFNP_SM as the termination condition of the optimization, based on the assumption that FNP_SM can be used as FNP_VIC when FNP_SM is the same as VFNP_SM. To acquire FNP_SM in each iteration, ε-NSGA II was used, which can adapt population size and terminate automatically. In order to get FNP_SM close to VFNP_SM more quickly and reduce the number of iterations, the two-stage surrogate model was designed and a better-performance surrogate method, ADBP, was used. Furthermore, the sample sets in this study were chosen from the sample space generated by ε-NSGA II to add the samples in the crucial range. From the results shown in Figures 3.8 and 3.9, ADBP performs stably with the different sample sets chosen from the sample space generated by ε-NSGA II. This indicates that the nonuniformity of initial training sample for ADBP is acceptable, which may be due to the feature of the optimal process where not all the samples in the sample space were used and the samples in the crucial range were more important than others.

In the meantime, the time consumed for calibration with SM is heavily curtailed compared with VIC. In order to accelerate the parameter calibration for VIC, the parallel computations were applied in a 24 processor platform, while SM constructed by ADBP was operated in single thread. As a result, there will be a remarkable increase in the saved time by SM if SM and the original model are both operated in single thread. Besides, the time SM saved is related with the VIC runtime. When the study area is larger and the physical conditions are more complex, the efficiency improvement with SM is more remarkable.

# 3.7. Conclusions

In this study, we developed a surrogate-based parameter calibration framework for a large complex distributed hydrologic model to accelerate the parallel automatic parameter calibration of VIC. The two-stage surrogate model in the framework is based on AdaBoost and BPNN. SM-I predicted outputs roughly and sought out the parameter sets in the crucial range. SM-II simulated the outputs in the crucial range accurately. The method was successfully applied in the LJR basin and the XJR basin.
