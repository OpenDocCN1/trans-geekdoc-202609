Another interesting remark is that the uncertainty bounds resulting from the second approach (i.e., the one that employs synthetic data and the notion of subperiod calibration) are narrower and sharper than those obtained by the GLUE method, thus indicating a more precise estimation of the model’s parameter uncertainty. In our view, this behavior, which arguably is a promising topic of future research, may be attributed to the combined effect of using an optimization algorithm (instead of random sampling) along with the notion of subperiod calibration driven by synthetic data.

# 2.5. Concluding Remarks

Hydrological models that are calibrated against short records may be unable to reproduce the anticipated catchment behavior in the long run, while additional drawbacks arise due to the essential sacrifice of historical data in order to keep space for validation. In an attempt to address this issue, we highlight a promising strategy, aiming at inferring the model’s parameters on the basis of synthetic rainfall-runoff data, and employ validation against the full historical sample of observations. The so-called stochastic calibration scheme is driven by a synthetic time series generation procedure, implemented within the R-package anySim, which takes advantage of the available data to represent the probabilistic properties and dependence structure of the rainfall-runoff processes across multiple scales, and the long-term fluctuations of the changing climate as well. Crucial requirements of the stochastic model are the faithful reproduction of the cross-dependencies across seasons and scales, which determines the causality between rainfall and runoff, and the fitting of suitable distribution functions to the processes of interest in order to reproduce extreme conditions that are impossible to be contained in historical data.
