## 10.5. Conclusions

Analysis of digital citizens’ information allows us to understand the existing relation between communities and water bodies, as well as their cultural appropriation. We found that physical changes in water bodies with significant effects on communities can be analyzed over time through news articles and NLP. The identification of main topics about water bodies included in newspaper articles can illustrate the big picture of water resources problems for a region, as presented in Quebrada el Carmen. In this context, digital news is representing digital citizens’ information from a part of the population, in this case journalists, which describe a mainstream view of a water-related topic for a given spatial and temporal range. This mainstream view was clearly appreciated in the Hidroituango emergency analysis. Here, digital citizens’ information has been captured indirectly through the documentation of one or a few individuals and shared with the community in digital news format. Additionally, it’s also observed and concluded about editorial bias influence on digital news results using NLP. In other words, the journalist writer’s political or environmental affinities could twist the intention of the information documented, and consequently it’s advised to include subjectivity analysis in further developments. Other authors had also found and analyzed this bias in their works regarding newspapers (Gangula et al., 2019; Valdeavilla & Pulido, 2019). In this work, the writer’s subjectivity was not measured, nevertheless, it was expected that individual political and environmental biases were reduced when using multiple sources of digital newspapers, as well as use information of well-established newspaper editorials. Additionally, politically related topic news was filtered within the search news step. Likewise, given the objective and activities of this research, it was deduced (as illustrated in the geovisualization step, within the maps) that most of the news was related to emergencies and water-related problems. These negative stories and big impact events allowed us to identify challenges in IWRM. Furthermore, this implies a coverage bias, as journalists tend to write about negative events. This bias should be studied further when evaluating the positive effects of the decision-making process, and applying NLP outputs in a broader context. Despite these drawbacks, newspaper articles represented adequately the effects of major changes on water bodies (e.g., Hidroituango).

The comparison between sentiments toward water bodies and physical variables is essential for a better understanding of the relations in the water-energy-food nexus. Sentiment index and monthly river-level ranges comparison in Hidroituango Dam and Bogotá River allowed us to satisfactorily identify drastic sentiment changes over time. From the correlation between climatic events and sentiment index, two main statements are obtained. First, it is necessary to have an improved geolocation methodology. Spatial location derived from water bodies shows it is not enough when analyzing long rivers, as point-located news are not easily geolocated in the river. Second, it was found that the date of publication of a newspaper article is not representative of the time occurrence of a hydrological event in small water bodies with few data sources. In these cases, the publication date denoted the timestep when the hydrological event was a mainstream topic for the community. Nevertheless, the visualization of georeferenced sentiment analysis results has proven to be a useful tool to understand the spatial changes in water bodies over time. Additionally, the results of the confusion matrix of extreme events provides a new understanding regarding the complexity when linking physical variables with digital media, as external factors could affect the sentiment index regarding hydrological events. In the context of NLP analysis on newspapers, other authors suggest the existence of sets of assumptions that imply a different interpretation of sentiment analysis results regarding the field of study (Puschmann & Powell, 2018), while Baccarella et al. (2018) exhibits the risk of social media and fake news. These statements reinforce the relevance of a digital news filtering process toward a newspaper database of water-resources topics. Consequently, this discussion proposes that a deeper analysis should be carried out when analyzing the perceptions of digital news and our understanding of it. Finally, we conclude by stating that NLP analysis of newspaper articles can provide additional information for decision making at the basin level, based on digital news information about the Magdalena River Basin’s water bodies, however, this should be checked against physical variables and improved in spatial and temporal resolution when implementing action plans.

It is foreseen that other sources of information about extreme hydrological events could be explored through NLP (e.g., Twitter). Further, a specialized word-corpus based on water resource management is highly recommended for improved results. Furthermore, additional water cycle components should be incorporated in NLP applications, as aquifers, subsurface flows, and humidity. In future works, a deeper geospatial analysis between digital citizens’ information and measured physical variables could provide additional tools for decision-making processes. We also recommend to explore new machine learning approaches for NLP involving water resources, as new advances could improve their results. Moreover, while new machine learning developments could provide new results, new additional challenges arise when trying to implement the work results in a decision-making process for community development and as a new methodology within the broader academic study of water-resource management.
