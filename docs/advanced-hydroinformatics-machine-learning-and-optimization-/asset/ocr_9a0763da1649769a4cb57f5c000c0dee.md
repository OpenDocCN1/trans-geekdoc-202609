Using these six inputs for all ML models, different configurations for MLP and RBN were tested varying the number of neurons (MLP) or basis (RBN). The Scikit-learn Python library (Pedregosa et al., 2011) was used for MLP and LF models, while for RBN, Keras was used (Chollet, 2015). In all ML models, 80% of the data was used for training and 20% for the test. The final configurations of the ML models were determined by trial and error. In the case of MLP, configurations with several layers and different numbers of neurons were tested; however, in this case, by increasing the complexity of the network with more hidden layers and neurons, the results were not improved but the number of parameters and decision variables (weights and biases) was increased. Therefore, simple configurations of a single hidden layer and a maximum of 7 neurons in the hidden layer were chosen. Likewise, for the RBN, configurations of a maximum of 7 bases were used. The final configurations of the ML models are as follows:

+   1. Three MLP configurations: 6-7-1, 6-6-1, and 6-4-1 (i.e., 6 inputs - N neurons in a hidden layer - 1 output layer) using the parameters of equation (15.3) with the application of a sigmoid activation function (equation (15.4)).
+   2. Three RBN configurations: 6-7-1, 6-6-1 and 6-4-1. (i.e., 6 inputs - N Basis - 1 output layer) using the parameters of equations (15.5) and (15.6).
+   3. Finally, a LF is proposed using the parameters of equation (15.7).
