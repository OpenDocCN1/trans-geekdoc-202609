The identification of main topics about water bodies included in newspaper articles can illustrate the big picture of water resources problems for a region, as presented in Quebrada el Carmen. In this context, digital news is representing digital citizens’ information from a part of the population, in this case journalists, which describe a mainstream view of a water-related topic for a given spatial and temporal range. This mainstream view was clearly appreciated in the Hidroituango emergency analysis. Here, digital citizens’ information has been captured indirectly through the documentation of one or a few individuals and shared with the community in digital news format. Additionally, it’s also observed and concluded about editorial bias influence on digital news results using NLP. In other words, the journalist writer’s political or environmental affinities could twist the intention of the information documented, and consequently it’s advised to include subjectivity analysis in further developments. Other authors had also found and analyzed this bias in their works regarding newspapers (Gangula et al., 2019; Valdeavilla & Pulido, 2019). In this work, the writer’s subjectivity was not measured, nevertheless, it was expected that individual political and environmental biases were reduced when using multiple sources of digital newspapers, as well as use information of well-established newspaper editorials. Additionally, politically related topic news was filtered within the search news step. Likewise, given the objective and activities of this research, it was deduced (as illustrated in the geovisualization step, within the maps) that most of the news was related to emergencies and water-related problems. These negative stories and big impact events allowed us to identify challenges in IWRM. Furthermore, this implies a coverage bias, as journalists tend to write about negative events. This bias should be studied further when evaluating the positive effects of the decision-making process, and applying NLP outputs in a broader context. Despite these drawbacks, newspaper articles represented adequately the effects of major changes on water bodies (e.g., Hidroituango).
