## A TWO-STAGE SURROGATE-BASED PARAMETER CALIBRATION FRAMEWORK FOR A COMPLEX DISTRIBUTED HYDROLOGICAL MODEL

Haiting Gu, Yue-Ping Xu, Li Liu, Di Ma, Suli Pan, and Jingkai Xie

Institute of Hydrology and Water Resources, Zhejiang University, Hangzhou, China

Distributed hydrologic models have been widely used for their functional diversity and rationality in theory. However, calibration of distributed models is computationally expensive since it requires a large number of model runs, even if an efficient multi-objective optimization algorithm is employed. To alleviate the burden of computation, we develop a two-stage surrogate-based model calibration framework by coupling standard backpropagation neural network with AdaBoost (ADBP) to efficiently calibrate the parameters of a large and complex distributed hydrologic model, that is, the Variable Infiltration Capacity (VIC) model. The first-stage model (SM-I) picks out the parameter sets whose simulated outputs are in the crucial range and the second-stage model (SM-II) estimates the values of outputs accurately with the parameter sets picked out by SM-I. The proposed surrogate model is tested in the Lanjiang River basin and the Xiangjiang River basin for parameter calibration. With the similar calibrated parameter sets for VIC, the surrogate model gains up to 20 times speedup compared with the original VIC model in the basin with a large area and complex physical conditions.

### 3.1. Introduction

The distributed hydrologic model had its first application at the watershed scale by Freeze and Harlan (1969). The models that take full consideration into the effect of spatial nonuniformity of precipitation and land surface conditions on hydrologic processes can be preferable to lumped models theoretically, although in practical applications, the lumped models may perform better (Carpenter & Georgakakos, 2006; Liu et al., 2018). Nowadays, with the rapid development of computer technology, the geographic information system (GIS), digital elevation models (DEM), and remote sensing (RS), distributed hydrologic models with different configurations have been developed and used widely in practice (Abbaspour et al., 2015; Kovalets et al., 2015; Marek et al., 2016; Patil & Ramsankaran, 2017; Sun et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2017; Zhao et al., 2009). They can even be combined with big data techniques (Guo et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2016a,b), such as the MapReduce framework and Hadoop distributed file system.

The Variable Infiltration Capacity Macroscale Hydrologic model (VIC), which we used in this study, is a macroscale model that the areas above a critical scale are taken to be about 10 km × 10 km typically and the simulated runoff is shown to match observations quite well over large river basins (Maurer et al., 2002). Numerous studies have applied VIC successfully and obtained good performances in ensemble streamflow forecasting (Liu et al., 2017; Mo & Lettenmaier, 2014; Schumann et al., 2013). It is also used to study the hydrology under nonstationary conditions (Chawla & Mujumdar, 2018; Leng et al., 2015). The distributed models are widely used in water resources assessment, hydrologic forecast, and sediment transport modeling (Pérez et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2015). However, these kinds of models also have a limitation. It requires a lot of computational power and costs substantial time to solve partial derivative equations with numerical computation methods at each grid cell. Especially, tens of thousands of simulations are required when parameters of a distributed model (Razavi et al., 2012) are calibrated.

One way to accelerate parameter calibration is by employing an efficient auto-calibration method. Multi-objective calibration strategy for hydrological models can reduce the model equifinality and parameter uncertainty and acquire a valuable parameter set (Tuo et al., 2018).

In recent years, many multi-objective optimization algorithms (MOAs) for the hydrologic model calibration have been developed and applied in the hydrologic model parameter calibration (Gupta et al., 2009), such as the Multi-Objective Complex Evolution method (MOCOM-UA) (Yapo et al., 1998), the Strength Pareto Evolutionary Algorithm II (SPEA2) (Zitzler et al., 2001), Nondominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) (Deb et al., 2002), and so on. Numerous studies (De Vos & Rientjes, 2007; Gharari et al., 2013; Guo et al., 2014; Huo et al., 2016; Kollat et al., 2012; Lee et al., 2013; Tang et al., 2005; Yassin et al., 2017) have shown that MOAs are efficient in the hydrologic model parameter calibration automatically.

Although an efficient optimization algorithm can reduce the number of simulations of model calibration for complex models, it may still need a large number. The surrogate model (SM) is also an important method to improve the efficiency of the optimization algorithms (Maier et al., 2014). The data-driven models, such as Artificial Neural Network (ANN) (Zhang et al., 2009), Radial Basis Function (RBF) (Christelis et al., 2016), Support Vector Machine (SVM) (Asefa et al., 2004; Zhang et al., 2009), and Gaussian Process Regression (GPR or Kriging) (Zhang et al., 2016a,b) are usually used to construct the response surface surrogates of physical models (Razavi et al., 2012). Several studies have been done through using surrogate models to accelerate the optimizations efficiently (Behzadian et al., 2009; Gong et al., 2016; Kourakos & Mantoglou, 2009; Yan & Minsker, 2006; Yan & Minsker, 2011; Yazdi et al., 2019). This kind of algorithm is called *meta model-enabled optimizers* or *SM-based optimization algorithms* in which the best method for building a SM with data generated by the physical model is first chosen (Santana-Quintero et al., 2010) and then a MOA scheme is developed to use SM instead of the physical model (Maier et al., 2014). However, most of the studies mentioned above are planned for groundwater or water allocation since they generally require finer resolution and lateral communication. In our study, we focus mainly on runoff calibration for a large and complex hydrologic model and investigate the performance of the SM-based optimization algorithm.
