aims to resolve the following optimization problem:

$$\min_{\beta}\quad (y-X\beta)^T(y-X\beta)+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{D}|\beta_i|,$$  
(5.3)

where $X$ is a $N \times D$ matrix of standardized predictors of dimension $D$, $y$ is the mean-centered response vector, $\beta$ denotes a vector that contains the $D$ regression coefficient, and $\lambda$ is the regularization parameter, which is a constant. The coefficient of parameters estimations are the solutions of the penalized regression, and sparsity in the Lasso model is obtained because some of the estimated parameters in $\beta$ will be zero (see Efron et al., 2004; Helgoy & Li, 2019; Tibshirani, 1996). The Lasso solution can also be interpreted as a Bayesian posterior mode estimate, so-called Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) posterior estimation, when the prior for the coefficient parameters, $p(\beta)$, is a Laplace distribution or the double exponential distribution (Tibshirani, 1996). A fully Bayesian Lasso was introduced by Park and Casella (2008) with a conditional prior on $\beta$ of the form $p(\beta|\sigma^2, \lambda)$. The posterior probability distribution for the coefficient parameters can be calculated using the Gibbs sampler. The Bayesian Lasso by Park and Casella (2008) has several attractive properties when it comes to parameter and hyperparameter estimation in the Bayesian setting, that is, that the joint posterior distribution for $\beta$ and $\sigma^2$ generally has one mode instead of multiple posterior priors (Helgoy & Li, 2019). When conditioning Bayesian calculation on $\sigma^2$, it is important to guarantee a unimodal full posterior (see Park & Casella, 2008). Without this, the posterior may not be unimodal, and the convergence may slow down, making the true distribution less meaningful. Readers are referred to Tibshirani (1996) and Park and Casella (2008) for more information.

# Random Forest

RF, proposed by Breiman (2001), is one of the most popular ML techniques for regression problems. These models consist of a large number of individual decision trees that operate as an ensemble. Each individual tree in the RF outputs a class (or group) prediction, and the class with the most votes becomes the model prediction. The prerequisites for the RF well performance include (1) the presence of some actual signals in time series data so that the decision tree models built using those signals perform better than random guessing, and (2) the prediction made by the individual trees need to have low correlations with each other.
