With advances in artificial intelligence techniques, some recent studies have applied approximation functions or machine learning (ML) models that use artificial neural networks (ANNs) to represent reservoir operations (e.g., Ehsani et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2018). Giuliani et al. (2014) used radial basis networks (RBNs) and a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (MOEA) to obtain optimal operating policies in a multipurpose reservoir. The combination of DPS, approximation functions, and MOEA results in an approach named evolutionary multi-objective direct policy search (EMODPS), developed by Giuliani et al. (2016), who used it to improve operating policies of a multipurpose reservoir.

Based on the EMODPS approach, this study aims to develop a methodology to obtain optimal reservoir operation policies using ML models. That is, design ML models that can define the volume of water that must be released in a time interval, and which through multi-objective optimization can learn optimal operations (optimal policies) to satisfy certain objectives. For this purpose, three different machine learning (ML) models, namely, the multilayer perceptron (MLP), the radial basis network (RBN), and the linear function (LF) are compiled. As well as two EAs: NSGA II and MOEA / D. The Hatillo reservoir in the Dominican Republic is used as a case study that has three purposes in mutual conflict: hydroelectric generation, irrigation, and flood control. The main objective for this case study is to find optimal policies to improve the real operation of the reservoir (analyzed for the period 2009–2019) for the three proposed objectives. The following sections describe the case study and the proposed methodology, and later demonstrate the results and conclusions of the study.

# 15.2. Case Study

# 15.2.1. The Hatillo Reservoir

The Hatillo reservoir is in the Yuna River basin (Fig. 15.1), Dominican Republic, about 113 km northeast of Santo Domingo. This reservoir has been used for irrigation, hydropower generation, and mitigating downstream flooding. The Yuna River basin drains an area of 5,498 km², flowing downstream for approximately 210 km. It is the second most important river in the Dominican Republic; and with an average flow of 91 m³/s at its mouth (35.4 m³/s at the entrance of the reservoir), it is the river with the highest flow.
