## 9 NOISE FILTER WITH WAVELET ANALYSIS IN ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS (NOWANN) FOR FLOW TIME SERIES PREDICTION

**Daniel A. Vázquez¹, Gerald A. Corzo Perez¹, and Dimitri P. Solomatine¹,²,³**

*¹ IHE Delft Institute for Water Education, Delft, The Netherlands*  
*² Water Resources Section, Delft University of Technology, Delft, The Netherlands*  
*³ Water Problems Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia*

A data preprocessing technique, known as Wavelet Analysis from the field of signal processing, was used to enhance an artificial neural network (ANN) model by addressing its primary drawbacks, notably nonstationarity and noise. Wavelet transforms are well-suited for handling time series contaminated by nonstationary traits and can extract relevant information at various time series frequencies. To improve the model’s performance, the ANN calibration weighting scheme assigns more weight to representative subseries and reduces the impact of noisy data on the rest. Noise filtering techniques (soft/hard single threshold) were applied to manipulate the subseries, further enhancing the model’s predictive skill. This approach resulted in the creation of a Noise Filter with Wavelet Analysis in Artificial Neural Network (NOWANN) scheme. To identify the best model, a model-based approach was used for input variable selection, k-fold cross-validation to determine the eliminate redundant inputs, and genetic algorithm to determine the most influential inputs.

Subsequently, the appropriate class of models must be identified. The most common are the backpropagation neural network (BPNN), the radial basis function network (RBFN), and the self-organizing feature map (SOFM). Regarding BPNN (the most common), Shamseldin et al. (1999) have evaluated the importance of choosing the type of transfer function to forecast flow in rivers. In his experience, the logistic function indicated superiority among those tested. Kisi and Cigizoglu (2005) have compared all these techniques to predict river flow and found the RBF to be superior based on performance criteria.

The next point to consider is the selection of suitable data sets to avoid local minima or loss of generalization capabilities during the calibration process. Arlot and Celisse (2010) provide guidelines for choosing the best cross-validation procedure based on the problem trait in question. Another important factor in the model-building process is during the network training step. Abrahart et al. (2004) have mentioned techniques that prevent overfitting during calibration. For example, the early stopping method states that (Coulibaly et al., 2000), if in a predefined number of epochs the validation check fails to decrease its performance, the training stops. A second alternative is weight decay that penalizes large weights to produce a smoother input-output mapping.

As mentioned before, the literature is extensive in the subject and also broad in the range of applications for hydrology. To name a few, Shrestha et al. (2009) emulated Monte Carlo (MC) simulations with ANNs to speed up the assessment of the model parametric uncertainty; Zhang et al. (2009) forecasted daily reservoir inflows by using quantitative precipitation forecasting into ANNs model. In addition, Coulibaly et al. (2000) used an early stop training approach to avoid model overfitting for real-time reservoir inflow forecast. Bhattacharya et al. (2007) modeled the complex mechanism of sediment transport and reported better results compared with the traditional models at hand.

However, in the present study, all the previous points were addressed to some extent and special attention was paid to data preprocessing, considered of utmost importance in the development of ANN. An emerging technique taken from the field of signal analysis is put to the test. Wavelet transforms (WT) is one of the many mathematical transformation methods that was used in its early days in seismic signal analysis and is now widely used in many other fields. This work uses WT due to its ability to transform signals of any type (rainfall and discharges time series of our interest) to obtain time-frequency information from it, which was not available in its raw condition. This technique complements data-driven models due to its ability to analyze signals contaminated with noise, trending, aperiodicity, or other forms of nonstationarity, which DDMs cannot handle well.

Articles about wavelet with ANN are available to the reader (Adamowski & Sun, 2010; Adamowski, 2008; Cannas et al., 2005; Khalil et al., 2012; Okkan, 2012; Okkan & Serbes, 2013; Partal, 2008). Recommended articles are efforts to couple a modeling tool with a highly flexible data preprocessing technique, with different purposes such as Himalayan runoff forecast, short-term river flow for melt-caused floods, river flow predictions, short-term total dissolved solids forecast, and inflow of reservoirs.

The knowledge obtained in other fields such as economics and finance, where this approach is much better established, has contributed a lot to develop concepts and capture limitations of the approach. Some algorithms used for WT analysis may have unwanted behaviors that are not easily detected. One drawback is the use of information brought from the future but located in the present time step; this violates the restrictions on the use of information not yet seen by the model. This can create false expectations of seemingly very promising results. Another problem is the time-varying properties of some algorithms, as well as errors and assumptions at the limits of the signal. This problem is addressed in the next section. ### 9.2. Materials

#### 9.2.1. Wavelet Transform

The basic idea behind Wavelet Analysis is to use wavelike functions that, when convolved with the signals, can extract important information and express it in more useful forms. These functions are operated in two ways: they can be translated at all-discrete locations of the signal, and they can be stretched or squeezed so that different frequencies of the signal are studied. As a result, the signal has two representations (approximation and detail). In turn, each element of approximation can be decomposed back to the coarsest resolution (multiresolution representation). After this interrogation, the wavelet transform space indicates how well the signal is represented by the adopted wavelet function. Now, the ANN model decides which information is most relevant.
