Additionally, large values of data (e.g., big data corpus) could present a challenge to computational calculations (Mikolov et al., 2013). In this sense, the Word2Vec function looks for words that occur in similar contexts to have similar embeddings, that is, represent the meaning of the word, for example:

The flood in the Bogotá River triggered emergency responses from the Colombian government.

The drought in the Bogotá River triggered remedial actions taken by the Colombian government.

The words flood and drought will have similar word vectors due to a similar context. Two architectures/algorithms are widely used to represent the “meaning” of a word according to a context: CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words) (predict the target word from the bag-of-words context) and Skip-gram (predict the context words, given the target) (Jang et al., 2019). Both implementations use neural networks, as shown in Figure 10.6, where w(t) denotes the analyzed word, and w(t±s) represents the word at a distance of s words in the sentence context.
