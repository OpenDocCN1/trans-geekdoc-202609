In recent years, many multi-objective optimization algorithms (MOAs) for the hydrologic model calibration have been developed and applied in the hydrologic model parameter calibration (Gupta et al., 2009), such as the Multi-Objective Complex Evolution method (MOCOM-UA) (Yapo et al., 1998), the Strength Pareto Evolutionary Algorithm II (SPEA2) (Zitzler et al., 2001), Nondominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) (Deb et al., 2002), and so on. Numerous studies (De Vos & Rientjes, 2007; Gharari et al., 2013; Guo et al., 2014; Huo et al., 2016; Kollat et al., 2012; Lee et al., 2013; Tang et al., 2005; Yassin et al., 2017) have shown that MOAs are efficient in the hydrologic model parameter calibration automatically.

Although an efficient optimization algorithm can reduce the number of simulations of model calibration for complex models, it may still need a large number. The surrogate model (SM) is also an important method to improve the efficiency of the optimization algorithms (Maier et al., 2014). The data-driven models, such as Artificial Neural Network (ANN) (Zhang et al., 2009), Radial Basis Function (RBF) (Christelis et al., 2016), Support Vector Machine (SVM) (Asefa et al., 2004; Zhang et al., 2009), and Gaussian Process Regression (GPR or Kriging) (Zhang et al., 2016a,b) are usually used to construct the response surface surrogates of physical models (Razavi et al., 2012). Several studies have been done through using surrogate models to accelerate the optimizations efficiently (Behzadian et al., 2009; Gong et al., 2016; Kourakos & Mantoglou, 2009; Yan & Minsker, 2006; Yan & Minsker, 2011; Yazdi et al., 2019). This kind of algorithm is called *meta model-enabled optimizers* or *SM-based optimization algorithms* in which the best method for building a SM with data generated by the physical model is first chosen (Santana-Quintero et al., 2010) and then a MOA scheme is developed to use SM instead of the physical model (Maier et al., 2014). However, most of the studies mentioned above are planned for groundwater or water allocation since they generally require finer resolution and lateral communication. In our study, we focus mainly on runoff calibration for a large and complex hydrologic model and investigate the performance of the SM-based optimization algorithm.

In our study, we aim to overcome the computational limitation in the calibration process of VIC. For this purpose, we propose a new modeling calibration framework. ANN is used to construct our surrogate model, which often shows better accuracy for spatial interpolation (Chowdhury et al., 2010; Sivaprasad et al., 2010).
