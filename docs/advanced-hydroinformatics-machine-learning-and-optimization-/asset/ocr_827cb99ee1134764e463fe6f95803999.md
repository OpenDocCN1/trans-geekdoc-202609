Meteorological forcing data used in this research include daily maximum and minimum temperatures, daily precipitation, solar radiation, and vapor pressure deficit. The NE-CFB data set was used to perform this research and predict daily streamflow records. A conceptual framework of proposed modeling research is presented in Figure 5.1. As illustrated, the NE-CFB streamflow records were calibrated using multiple ML approaches. The modeling results were then compared with the SAC-SMA simulation using various performance indices.

# 5.3. Results

This research used 10 yr of daily streamflow records, from 01 Jan. 2000 to 31 Dec. 2009, as a training data set, and then the next 4 yr (01 Jan. 2010 to 31 Dec. 2013) as an independent test period. The training period is critical to result in reasonable and reliable simulation because a small division of the data set from the training period would lead to overfitting. The ML results were also compared with the SAC-SMA simulation to determine the degree of modeling success. Various performance metrics were then calculated and presented in Table 5.1 for both calibration and validation performance assessments. The values of NSE and KGE for the SAC-SMA and multiple ML models suggest that the Bayesian Lasso method performed well in both calibration and validation periods. This revealed that a regression model with independent Laplace (i.e., double exponential) prior distribution could handle the complexities of high and low flow fluctuations across a coastal plain drainage system. As shown in Table 5.1, the Bayesian Lasso even outperformed the SAC-SMA hydrologic simulation. Furthermore, the SVM performed as well as the SAC-SMA while the RF performed better in rainfall-runoff simulation. Among different indices, KGE appears to be a better index for estimating the agreement between observed and simulated coastal plain streamflow records. KGE is a nonlinear combination of correlation, flow variability measure, and bias; thereby it appears to be an appropriate metric to evaluate the impacts of flow variabilities on simulation results. With respect to the FDC segments, Bayesian Lasso successfully captured high and low flow fluctuations while most of the ML models were less successful in calibrating moderate streamflow records or missegment the FDC (0.2–0.7 flow exceedance probabilities). This might reflect the fact that the response of watershed to moderate precipitation events varies largely over time depending on timing (e.g., month, season) and depth and duration of precipitation with space along the landscape.
