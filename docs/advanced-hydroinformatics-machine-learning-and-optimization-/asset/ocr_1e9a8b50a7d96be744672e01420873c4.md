Second, it was found that the date of publication of a newspaper article is not representative of the time occurrence of a hydrological event in small water bodies with few data sources. In these cases, the publication date denoted the timestep when the hydrological event was a mainstream topic for the community. Nevertheless, the visualization of georeferenced sentiment analysis results has proven to be a useful tool to understand the spatial changes in water bodies over time. Additionally, the results of the confusion matrix of extreme events provides a new understanding regarding the complexity when linking physical variables with digital media, as external factors could affect the sentiment index regarding hydrological events. In the context of NLP analysis on newspapers, other authors suggest the existence of sets of assumptions that imply a different interpretation of sentiment analysis results regarding the field of study (Puschmann & Powell, 2018), while Baccarella et al. (2018) exhibits the risk of social media and fake news. These statements reinforce the relevance of a digital news filtering process toward a newspaper database of water-resources topics. Consequently, this discussion proposes that a deeper analysis should be carried out when analyzing the perceptions of digital news and our understanding of it. Finally, we conclude by stating that NLP analysis of newspaper articles can provide additional information for decision making at the basin level, based on digital news information about the Magdalena River Basin’s water bodies, however, this should be checked against physical variables and improved in spatial and temporal resolution when implementing action plans.

It is foreseen that other sources of information about extreme hydrological events could be explored through NLP (e.g., Twitter). Further, a specialized word-corpus based on water resource management is highly recommended for improved results. Furthermore, additional water cycle components should be incorporated in NLP applications, as aquifers, subsurface flows, and humidity. In future works, a deeper geospatial analysis between digital citizens’ information and measured physical variables could provide additional tools for decision-making processes. We also recommend to explore new machine learning approaches for NLP involving water resources, as new advances could improve their results. Moreover, while new machine learning developments could provide new results, new additional challenges arise when trying to implement the work results in a decision-making process for community development and as a new methodology within the broader academic study of water-resource management.
