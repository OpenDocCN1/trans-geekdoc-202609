(2008), we partitioned the FDC into three different segments (1) high-flow segment (0–0.02 flow exceedance probabilities) characterizing watershed response to significant precipitation events; (2) midflow segment (0.2–0.7 flow exceedance probabilities) that is specified by flows from moderate size precipitation events and also related to the intermediate-term primary and secondary base flow relaxation response of the watershed system; and (3) low-flow segment (0.7–1.0 flow exceedance probabilities), related to the long-term sustainability of flow and controlled by the interaction of baseflow with riparian evapotranspiration (ET) during extended dry periods. A steep slope in the midsegment FDC reflects a watershed having a quick or flashy response (due to small soil storage capacity and hence larger percentage of overland flow) while flatter midsegment slopes are associated with slower and more sustained watershed response to precipitation events.

### 5.2.2. Study Region and Data

The study region is the Northeast Cape Fear River Basin (hereafter NE-CFB), North Carolina, USA (HUC 03030007), which drains 1,714.70 mi² (4,441.1 km²) of the Cape Fear River Basin (CFRB). The CFRB is one of the four river basins entirely contained within the North Carolina border. The river is formed by the Deep, Rocky, and Haw rivers, which converge in Chatham County just below the B. Everett Jordan Dam. The river ends in 32,000 acres of the estuary near Southport. The CFRB is the largest river in North Carolina, encompassing a 9,000 mi² basin that includes streams flowing within 29 of the state’s 100 counties. With about 27% of the state’s population and dozens of municipalities situated within its boundaries (including Greensboro, Burlington, Chapel Hill, Sanford, Fayetteville, Dunn, Clinton, Warsaw, Burgaw, and Wilmington), the CFRB is one of the most industrialized regions in North Carolina. These and other cities and towns rely on the river and its tributaries for freshwater, transportation, recreation, natural habitats, and other uses. The CFRB flows into the Atlantic Ocean near Cape Fear, from which it takes its name. This study used CAMELS (Catchment Attributes and Meteorology for Large-sample Studies) data set described in Newman et al. (2015) and Addor et al. (2017). CAMELS is a basin-scale hydrometeorological forcing data that covers 671 catchments in the contiguous United States (CONUS). The data set provides daily meteorological forcing from three data sets, Daymet (Thornton et al., 2012), Maurer (Maurer et al., 2002), and NLDAS (Xia et al., 2012), as well as a daily streamflow gauging data set from the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). Meteorological forcing data used in this research include daily maximum and minimum temperatures, daily precipitation, solar radiation, and vapor pressure deficit. The NE-CFB data set was used to perform this research and predict daily streamflow records. A conceptual framework of proposed modeling research is presented in Figure 5.1. As illustrated, the NE-CFB streamflow records were calibrated using multiple ML approaches. The modeling results were then compared with the SAC-SMA simulation using various performance indices.

## 5.3. Results

This research used 10 yr of daily streamflow records, from 01 Jan. 2000 to 31 Dec. 2009, as a training data set, and then the next 4 yr (01 Jan. 2010 to 31 Dec. 2013) as an independent test period. The training period is critical to result in reasonable and reliable simulation because a small division of the data set from the training period would lead to overfitting. The ML results were also compared with the SAC-SMA simulation to determine the degree of modeling success. Various performance metrics were then calculated and presented in Table 5.1 for both calibration and validation performance assessments. The values of NSE and KGE for the SAC-SMA and multiple ML models suggest that the Bayesian Lasso method performed well in both calibration and validation periods. This revealed that a regression model with independent Laplace (i.e., double exponential) prior distribution could handle the complexities of high and low flow fluctuations across a coastal plain drainage system. As shown in Table 5.1, the Bayesian Lasso even outperformed the SAC-SMA hydrologic simulation. Furthermore, the SVM performed as well as the SAC-SMA while the RF performed better in rainfall-runoff simulation. Among different indices, KGE appears to be a better index for estimating the agreement between observed and simulated coastal plain streamflow records. KGE is a nonlinear combination of correlation, flow variability measure, and bias; thereby it appears to be an appropriate metric to evaluate the impacts of flow variabilities on simulation results. With respect to the FDC segments, Bayesian Lasso successfully captured high and low flow fluctuations while most of the ML models were less successful in calibrating moderate streamflow records or misrepresent the FDC (0.2–0.7 flow exceedance probabilities). This might reflect the fact that the response of watershed to moderate precipitation events varies largely over time depending on timing (e.g., month, season) and depth and duration of precipitation with space along the landscape.

Figure 5.3 illustrates the FDCs for the ML, SAC-SMA, and observation during the calibration and validation. As shown, the SAC-SMA successfully calibrated intermediate flows while somewhat underestimated, and overestimated high- and low-flow events. Moreover, the SAC-SMA underestimated the peak rates considerably compared with the Bayesian Lasso model. The SAC-SMA model uses a lumped fashion to calibrate soil moisture states and the basin’s relative permeability. These states are fed as several key basin’s parameters into the model, such as interflow, evapotranspiration, and percolation. It seems hydrologic variables such as loss (due to dense vegetation) and soil moisture have strong control on rainfall-runoff processes and mechanisms in the study region. These factors determine catchment wetness conditions and evapotranspiration processes. Thus, runoff magnitude might abruptly increase when the catchment initial abstraction threshold was exceeded.
