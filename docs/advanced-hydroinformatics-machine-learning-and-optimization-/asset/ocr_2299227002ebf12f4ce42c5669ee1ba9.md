# 2.3. Insights to the Stochastic Simulation Procedure

The idea of substituting short historical records with synthetic ones is very popular in a wide range of hydrological design, planning, management, and forecasting applications, involving the use of deterministic models that are driven by synthetic inputs, provided by stochastic models (e.g., Koutsoyiannis & Economou, 2003). The coupling of deterministic and stochastic models allows for assessing probabilistic quantities, such as reliability, resilience, and risk, and expected values of long-term performance metrics, such as yield, costs, benefits, and deficits, on the basis of simulations with sufficiently large samples (e.g., with length of hundreds or thousands of years). On the other hand, less often is synthetic data used for evaluating the behavior of hydrological models in the long run (e.g., Nalbantis et al., 2011; Efstratiadis et al., 2015).

Cornerstone of all aforementioned approaches, belonging to the broader family of Monte Carlo simulation, is the time series generation model, which has to ensure a concise representation of the probabilistic and stochastic structure of the actual processes, x and y. For a long time, this prerequisite has been determined as the preservation of a finite set of statistical characteristics of historical data within the synthetic ones by means of low-order moments (typically the mean, variance, skewness) and low-order auto- and cross-correlations (Matalas & Wallis, 1976). However, in the context of stochastic calibration, this approach is not sufficient, since these metrics describe only partially the marginal and dependence regime of the actual processes x and y, while under some quite common conditions, unrealistic dependence patterns may appear in the synthetic data (Tsoukalas et al., 2018b).
