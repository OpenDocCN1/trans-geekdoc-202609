For the present work, and according to the way ANN operates (by assigning different weights to each input series during the training process), it can be said that ANN by itself is selecting which subseries are more significant than others. For the present study, the statements made previously by the different authors are thought of as partial truths. Every subseries contains some important information about the signal and relevant to the model (no subseries were discarded). However, many factors can introduce errors in our measurements (errors in the instruments, human error, etc.). Even after quality checks, there are still some errors in the data, so this noise can also be eliminated.

To illustrate how threshold de-noising works, it will be briefly introduced below. Let us start from the basis that the noise is presented on fine scales (detailed subseries), contrary to the trends or the main body (coherent part) of a time series, which can be seen in the approximation subseries. The filtering is only done on the detailed subseries. The threshold limits were intentionally chosen as large, to illustrate how the signal transforms (see Fig. 9.5). In practice, and for the present study these values are much smaller in magnitude, therefore, when reconstructing the signal (inverse wavelet transform), the resulting time series would not be so different from the original (let’s remember that this is obtained via optimization).
