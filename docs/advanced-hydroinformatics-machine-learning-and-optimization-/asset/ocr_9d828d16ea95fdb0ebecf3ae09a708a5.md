*RMSE* and *ER* were chosen as the evaluation indices for SM-I and SM-II, respectively. Via this study, we find the following:

+   1. In the benchmark test, a parameter set with a very small error from the artificial parameter set can be acquired through using our surrogate-based parameter calibration framework.
+   2. The parameter sets of VIC acquired from the surrogate-based parameter calibration framework have no large difference from those from e-NSGA II. The streamflow simulated with parameter sets from SM accords with that of VIC very well.
+   3. The proposed method can accelerate the calibration process markedly but the time saved is related to the study area. When the study area is larger and the physical conditions are more complex, the efficiency improvement with SM is more remarkable.

SM can save much time for calibration of complex distributed hydrologic models and improve in particular the state of the art in parameter calibration. Future work may focus on the extension of SM to study hydrologic model calibrations under changing environments and, in addition, the role of different optimization algorithms can be investigated.
