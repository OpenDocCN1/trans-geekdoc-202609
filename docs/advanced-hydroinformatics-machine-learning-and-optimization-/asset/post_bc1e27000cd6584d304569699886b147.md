## 5.4. Conclusion and Future Works

Regression-based ML models can be used as a supplement to both conceptual and physics-based hydrologic simulation models. However, there continues to be a concern regarding their ability to provide skillful prediction performance, in part because of their need for much more training data to generate hydrological processes, varying degrees of noise in data, and overfitting, which can translate into a significant amount of error and uncertainty in the modeling results. We argue that the potential benefits of ML models for daily streamflow simulation of a coastal plain drainage system can be realized only via the formulation of robust and rigorous Bayesian Lasso interface as posterior mode estimate that is well suited to evaluate input-output behavior (behavioral consistency) for sequential streamflow time series data.

One obvious way to reduce the obstacles and improve ML fidelity is using preprocessing methods (e.g., singular spectrum and wavelet analyses; see Vautard et al., 1992), finding intricate patterns in large volumes of data, improving sequence-to-sequence learning, and calculating noise variance and then capturing the outliers through monitoring the noise component. Enhancing the ability of ML on modeling extreme observations within time-series records is also important and needs further attention. Furthermore, physical parameters (and process interactions) could be mathematically formulated and incorporated into the ML models to ensure proper consistency between the model input-output behavior and the available data and catchment properties. Our research group has sought, therefore, to develop appropriate ML models that are comparable with a catchment scale hydrologic simulation model. The main limitation of our proposed ML approaches, however, is their vast computational time and cost, and overfitting that make them somewhat poor predictors. Our ongoing work explores the development of more advanced and computational cost-effective ML algorithms such as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to integrate the information extractable from the recurrence/structure of the network and mathematical formulation with catchment physical attributes and observations. As always, we invite dialogue with others interested in these and related ML model simulation topics.
