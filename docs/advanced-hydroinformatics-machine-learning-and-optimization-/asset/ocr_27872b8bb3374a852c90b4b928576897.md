Several studies found little improvement of the observed poor model performance under contrasting climate conditions, after employing longer calibration periods (e.g., Brigode et al., 2013; Coron et al., 2014; Duethmann et al., 2020). In this respect, the assumption of time-invariant parameters is disputed, and some researchers directly link parameter change with hydroclimatic variability (e.g., Merz et al., 2013; Zhao et al., 2020).

The dilemma of which calibration approach is the more suitable may not make sense if long enough data were available, provided that the latter are really representative of the full regime of the modeled catchment. In order to address this issue, a well-known option is offered by stochastic models, which allow for synthesizing hydrological data to capture a wide range of plausible hydroclimatic conditions. Taking advantage of the long experience with stochastic simulation approaches in water resources planning and management studies, we reveal a novel field of application of stochastics for calibrating hydrological models. The overall idea is very simple and follows the well-known calibration-validation paradigm, where calibration is employed with synthetic data and validation against the actual (i.e., observed) ones, which are also used as guidance for recognizing the stochastic behavior of the processes of interest. In this chapter, we introduce the so-called stochastic calibration approach, which is founded on the coupling of two totally different modeling philosophies (i.e., mechanistic and stochastic), and reveal its significant advantages over conventional parameter estimation methods. Emphasis is paid to the specifications of the stochastic model, which is the most critical component of the overall procedure. The method is demonstrated by means of a proof of concept involving the fitting of conceptual model to a real-world river basin in Greece.
