where, *PD* is the notion of the peak and duration method; *nor* is the function of normalization; *D* is duration when discharge is larger than \(Q_0\); \(Q_{p1}\) and \(Q_{p2}\) are first and second peak, respectively; \(j\) means the \(j^{th}\) sampled hydrograph; \(i\) means the \(i^{th}\) term in *PD* (\(i = 1, 2, 3\)); other symbols are same as equation (14.14).

With the four criteria presented above some similar hydrographs would be selected, for which the corresponding optimized strategies need to be identified for real-time operation. As for each inflow hydrograph, there are a number of different strategies (represented in the Pareto front), a certain level of the downstream risk needs to be first chosen, and the corresponding strategy is selected as final for implementation.

It needs to be noted that, even though by similarity selection the time-consuming process of optimization is eliminated (as mentioned above), the duration of discharge larger than 5,500 m³/s is still very long (around 20 days), much more than the 36 hr available lead time. Therefore, to generate knowledge about the real hydrograph top (to be used in the similarity assessment), additional work would be required, for example, using information about precipitation in the upstream part of the catchment, and associated rainfall-runoff modeling. The methodology presented here is based on the assumption that there is enough time to perform such analysis.
