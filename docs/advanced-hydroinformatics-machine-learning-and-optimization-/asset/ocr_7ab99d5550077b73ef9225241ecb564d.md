For this study, the hydrometeorological variables that influence the predicted discharge at La Canoa station are the rainfall, evapotranspiration, and discharge itself. However, it is important to state that not all of these variables have a substantially influence on the output discharge. The analysis of interdependency of variable allows one to identify the hydrometeorological variables and lags that have a major influence on the output prediction. In this way, Figure 8.5 presents the Correlation Coefficient (CoC) and the Average Mutual Information (AMI) analysis between the discharge at 1 hr ahead and the rainfall, evapotranspiration, and discharge itself. It can be seen that the past discharge provides the maximum AMI and CoC at a lag 1 hr and then start decreasing, which means that past discharge has a major effect on predicting the discharge itself. Additionally, the AMI and CoC for the rainfall variable start rising at a lag around 10 hr, and then start decreasing at a lag around 14 hr; this behavior means that the rainfall data from 10 to 14 time steps back can be used as a model input.
