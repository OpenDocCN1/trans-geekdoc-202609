
## DEFINITION 11.6 ((d, μ)-MPDL Assumption)

Let `G` be a bilinear group generator and let `d` ∈ ℕ be a positive integer, and let `p₁(X), ..., pₙ(X)` ∈ ℤₚ[X] be μ-variate polynomials of total degree at most `d`. We say that the `(d, μ)`-multivariate power discrete logarithm assumption `((d, μ)-MPDL)` holds for `G` if for every PPT `A`

```
Pr[s ←$ 𝔽^μ; (p₁(s), ..., pₙ(s)) ← A(p₁(X), ..., pₙ(X)) : τ(X) ≠ 0 ∧ τ(s) = 0] ≤ negl(λ)
```

## LEMMA 11.1 (Bitansky et al. 2013, Proposition 5.15)

If the `d-PDL` assumption holds for `G`, then so does the `(d, μ)`-MPDL assumption for any `μ = poly(λ)`.

### 11.5.3. SNARGs from algebraic NILPs with degree-2 verifiers using bilinear groups

In this section, we present how to convert a `(d_Q, 2)`-algebraic NILP for a family of relations `R` into a SNARG for `R`. The construction uses bilinear groups.

Let `G` be a generator for bilinear groups of prime order `p`, and let `(P_NILP, Q_NILP, D_NILP)` be a `(d_Q, 2)`-algebraic NILP for a family of relations `R` over the finite field `𝔽 := ℤₚ`.

We present the construction of a SNARG for `R` in [Figure 11.4](#c11.xhtml#c11-fig-4). For simplicity, we give the construction using symmetric pairings.

The correctness of the SNARG construction follows immediately from that of the NILP, using the fact that the bilinear groups have order `p` and that the pairing allows one to compute a quadratic polynomial over the discrete logarithms of group elements, and to test if it evaluates to 0.

#### 11.5.3.1. Efficiency analysis

We briefly mention how some efficiency measures of the NILP translate into the efficiency of the SNARG. The CRS size corresponds to the size of the NILP query: for a NILP with a query vector of length `m` we obtain a SNARG where the CRS has `m` elements of `G`. The prover complexity is at most `m · k` group multiplications. The proof size is `k` elements of `G₁`, where `k` are the number of rows of the NILP proof matrix. The complexity of the verifier depends on the sparsity of the `t_i` polynomials generated by the NILP algorithm `Test(R, x)`. Indeed, a dense `t_i` would need to touch many elements of `q`. Hence, in order for the SNARG to be *succinct* the NILP must be such that `k, η` and the number of nonzero monomials in all the `t_i` polynomials are at most polylogarithmic in the size of the witnesses for `R`.

This analysis suggests that in order to improve the efficiency of SNARG constructions one may focus on improving specific efficiency aspects of algebraic NILPs.

#### 11.5.3.2. Proof of soundness

## THEOREM 11.2

Let `(P_NILP, Q_NILP, D_NILP)` be a `(d_Q, 2)`-Algebraic NILP. If `G` is a bilinear group generators where the `(d_Q, μ)`-MPDL assumption holds, then the SNARG of [Figure 11.4](#c11.xhtml#c11-fig-4) is sound in the algebraic group model.

*Proof.* We show how to convert an algebraic adversary `A` against the soundness of the SNARG into an algebraic adversary `B` against the MPDL assumption.

Let `R` ∈ `R` be the relation for which `A` is supposed to break soundness, and let `q₁, ..., q_m` be the `m` polynomials obtained through the deterministic `Q_NILP` algorithm. We consider an instantiation of the MPDL assumption where `p₁(X), ..., p_m(X)` are the `m` polynomials in `μ` variables and total degree `d_Q`.

So, our adversary `B` runs on input `(p₁(s), ..., p_m(s)) ∈ G₁^n` for a random `s ←$ 𝔽^μ` and proceeds as follows.

It sets `crs := [q] = [p(s)]` and then runs the SNARG adversary `(x, [α]) ← A(crs)`. By the AGM assumption, `A` is algebraic and thus it also returns a matrix `Π*` such that `[α] = Π* · [q]`.

Next `B` generates `t₁, ..., t_η ← Test(R, x)`, looks for an index `i ∈ [η]` such that

```
τ_i(X) := t_i(p(X), Π* · p(X)) ≠ 0
```

and returns `τ_i(X)`.

We show that if `A` breaks soundness with probability `ϵ`, then `B` breaks the MPDL assumption with the same probability.

If `A` breaks soundness, then `(x, [α])` is such that `x ∉ L_R` and `V(crs, x, [α]) = 1`. By the soundness notion of the algebraic NILP, if `x ∉ L_R`, then for any prover strategy `Π*` there is an index `i ∈ [η]` such that `t_i(p(X), Π* · p(X))` is non-zero.

On the other hand, since the proof verifies we have that for all `i ∈ [η]` : `t_i([q], [α]) = [0]_T`, and thus `τ_i(s) = 0`.

Therefore, `B` has found a non-zero polynomial `τ_i(X)` such that `τ_i(s) = 0`, that is, a solution to the MPDL assumption.

∎

## 11.6. Constructing algebraic NILPs for arithmetic circuits

In this section, we present the last step that allows us to instantiate our generic construction of SNARGs (and thus of VC). Our goal is to obtain argument systems for proving the correct computation of arithmetic circuits. A bit more precisely, we consider non-deterministic computations, namely the prover wants to convince the verifier that there exists a value `w`, called the witness, such that `C(x, w) = y`.

Following our modular approach, we show the construction of an algebraic NILP for arithmetic circuit satisfiability, which can be used to instantiate the generic SNARG construction of the previous section.

### 11.6.1. Arithmetic circuits

An arithmetic circuit over a field `𝔽` and a set of variables `X₁, ..., Xₙ` is a directed acyclic graph with the following properties. Each node is called a *gate*; gates with in-degree 0 are either *input* gates labeled with some variable `Xᵢ`, or *constant* gates labeled with a field value `c ∈ 𝔽`; gates with out-degree 0 are called *output* gates; gates with in-degree and out-degree greater than 0 are called *internal* gates. Without loss of generality, we consider arithmetic circuits with in-degree 2. There are two types of internal gates: *addition* and *multiplication* gates, which compute the sum and the product, respectively, of the two inputs.
