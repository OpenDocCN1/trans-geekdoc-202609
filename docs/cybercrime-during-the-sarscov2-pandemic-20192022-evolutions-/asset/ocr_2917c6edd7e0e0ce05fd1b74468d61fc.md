Among others, Walther and McCoy have explained that, as masses in isolation filling the screens of cellphones and computers, it became “a great deal easier for online recruiters to attract more people and expose them to disinformation campaigns” [WAL 21]. The problem is potentially far deeper: traditional radicalization theory place much emphasis on social-isolation, seeing it as instrumental in enabling the “deprogramming” of individuals. This is essentially seen as a form of conditioning that pushes individuals to carry out acts of violence [BLE 20]. During the pandemic, with at-home audiences held hostage by the effects of the pandemic in their millions, it was no accident that “sensational media narratives and conspiracy” theories were found to be “growing, and in some cases than doubling across web communities” [FIN 20].

In fact, it appears that, from far being victims of recruitment, a considerable segment of Internet users actively sought out conspiratorial narratives of the pandemic, and were prepared to migrate to online platforms that allowed them to “simply reject science”, but to “invent [their] scientific rhetoric” about public health [CHR 20]. This led to the growth of the so-called “alt-tech” universe [FRE 20], a constellation of self-described alternative social media platforms like Rumble, Telegram and Parler, who built their entire business model on minimalist approach to content moderation [NAT 21]. The promotion of these alt-tech platforms by influential figures on the conservative and far-right political spectrum led to what researchers have described as “the great scattering of extremists and extremist groups across alternative platforms” [JAR 22]. The latter rapidly became and facilitated the exponential technological support infrastructure of the far-right communities, including QAnon and the Boogaloo [MCS 21]. In their insightful study of the alt-tech universe, Donovan, Lewis and Friedberg metaphorically refer to it as a series of parallel ports, which “allow “for multiple streams of data to flow simultaneously” [DON 19].
