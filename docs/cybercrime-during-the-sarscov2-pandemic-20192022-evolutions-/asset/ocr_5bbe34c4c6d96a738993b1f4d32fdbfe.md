In one characteristic instance, members of the Proud Boys described Trump as a “weak” president who would “go down in failure” [FRE 20]. According to the Southern Poverty Center, cracks in the uneasy alliance between Trump and the white supremacist wing of the patriot movement began to appear as early as the first two years of his administration [HAT 18], and only intensified in later years. There is no evidence that the coronavirus experience helped deepened the ideological convergence between the patriot movement and the Trump of the Republican Party. In fact, during the coronavirus period, the Internet interacted with the patriot movement in ways that made its structure more transient and its political allegiance to established public figures—including of Trump—far lessened understood.

The militant tendency that most adequately embodies the shifting contours of the American domestic threat landscape during Covid-19 is the so-called “Boogaloo movement, known simply as “Boogaloo”. The ideological identity of the Boogaloo movement is from crystallized [KRI 21]. In its essence, the movement is reflective of a highly horizontal and decentralized ultra-libertarian point of view, which brings together a diverse variety with political identities ranging from the far-right to the far-left. As a social movement, the Boogaloo traces its roots to the racist skinhead and neo-Nazi culture of the 1980s and 1990s, which was formed mostly inside prisons. In the early 2000s, the racist skinhead movement attempted to enter the mainstream and attract a more educated cohort “alternative right”, or “alt-right” movement [MAI 18]. The alt-right largely amplified after the bloody 2017 Unite Right rally in Charlottesville, Virginia. However, for a number of years prior to 2017, some of the younger, online-savvy members of the alt-right had been active in a host of forums revolving around culture and video games [KEN 21]. It was cross pollination between supporters of the alt-right and online communities that birthed the Boogaloo movement. In subsequent years, that loose amalgamation of mostly teenage and male Internet users began to assume a recognizable ideological shape on 4chan in the weapons-themed /k/ and the racism-themed /pol/ sections of the imageboard [KEN 21]. By 2014, these users were facing countermeasures, imposed on them by the 4chan moderators due to their violent memeography.
