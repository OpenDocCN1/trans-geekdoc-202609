In addition to the legislative measures to deter disinformation, efforts to counter disinformation and malicious communications include a portal “Real 411” to enable reporting and complaints [MMA 22a], and the SA Coronavirus hosted weekly reports starting in May 2021 [SAC 21g]. Volunteers are also conducting fact checking and attempting to redirect people towards sources of information [GOO 21].

Table 6.5 and Figure 6.4 illustrate confirmed disinformation/misinformation cases investigated by Real 41. As is evident, disinformation peaked in Q2 of 2020 and then again in Q3 of 2021. The first peak can be explained by the ongoing pandemic and lockdown, and the second peak corresponds to the third wave. In addition, it was between the riots and local government elections in November 2021; therefore, increased political activity could also have contributed. Figure 6.4 shows that the peaks of the disinformation tended to slightly precede the peak of waves, except in the third wave (July 2021), but this could be due to the protests and increased political activity. In terms of the platforms, Twitter and WhatsApp featured predominantly, followed by Facebook.
