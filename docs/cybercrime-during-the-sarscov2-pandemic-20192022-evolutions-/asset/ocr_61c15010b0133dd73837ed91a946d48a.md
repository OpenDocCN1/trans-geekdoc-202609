# Cybercrime During the SARS-CoV-2 Pandemic (2019-2022)

It is important to mention that another road to improve cooperation could be creating the BRICS framework for cooperating toward Internet policies, including cybersecurity. The governments of Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa debated this topic during the fifth BRICS summit in 2013. In the major statement, they stated:

We recognize the critical role the Internet plays globally in promoting economic, social, and cultural development. We believe it's important to contribute to and participate in a peaceful, secure, and open cyberspace and we emphasize security in the use of Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) and practices is of universally accepted norms, standards, and of paramount importance (BRICS Information Center 2013).

We can rely on the work of Luca Belli about the subject. He also uses the concept of legal interoperability in a similar way that we do here. For him, the BRICS are converting themselves into CyberBRICS. This digital turn has a clear relationship to a higher degree of policymaking toward cooperation. The first steps have their basis in science, technology and innovation efforts to cooperate, with some economic integration. Notwithstanding, all convergences have a positive impact on cybersecurity. The author identifies five dimensions to cybersecurity policies – data protection, consumer protection, cybercrime, the preservation of public order, and legal interoperability amongst BRICS countries concerning cooperation and legal-defense. His point of view is optimistic: “Growing cooperation and legal interoperability with digital policy is increasingly possible and, to some extent, already happening” (Belli 2019). Nonetheless, Adriana Abnur is more skeptical. She points out that the integration between the BRICS is still slow (Abnur 2017). As a matter of fact, of the five BRICS countries, only South Africa is part of the Budapest Convention. Brazil is acting to it right now (2021–2022). Despite being a member of the Council of Europe (1996–2022) in the past, the country withdrew from this international organization in March 2022. There is no information about efforts from India and China to accede to the Convention. At last, it is important to mention that new negotiations are starting within the United Nations to create another multilateral treaty for cooperation against cybercrime. These initiatives from BRICS and the UN are interesting, but only time will tell whether they will be fruitful.
