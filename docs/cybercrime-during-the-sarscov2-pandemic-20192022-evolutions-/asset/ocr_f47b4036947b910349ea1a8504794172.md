The illegal nature of the actions deployed by malicious actors forces them to camouflage their actions. To this end, cyberspace offers protection and a propritious environment to carry out their activities. The anonymity [LEC 10] and the obfuscation that cyberspace enables the low cost of cybercrime compared to the potential gains [DEC 17] and the lack of expertise of police and judicial bodies [BAU 14, KAI 21] are detrimental to the fight against cybercrime. Furthermore, the fluid (data being digital), plastic and fungible nature of data (because of cryptocurrencies) greatly simplifies the perpetration of crimes.

Cyberspace brings together a variety of essential layers work as an interconnected, functional network [DUP 17]. Although they constitute the functional body of cyberspace, the physical, software and informational layers elicit little interest on the part of the political field. On the contrary, it is the social layer which is particularly relevant for (arouses the interest of) politicians and political scientists.
