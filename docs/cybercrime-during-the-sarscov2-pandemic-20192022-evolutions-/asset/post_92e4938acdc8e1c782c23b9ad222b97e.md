Interpreting CERT data alone, the pandemic period did not produce the drastic increase in cybercrime that many reports and articles around the world reported. ### 1.2.2.2. Brazil

The shape of the incident curve reported to CERT only slightly differs from the available data for Malaysia. First, it is worth recalling before entering the pandemic period, cybercrime had its own specificities in each country, its unique evolution, a chronology and a specific history, and this despite the fact that cyberspace is an inter or transnational space, and that cybercrime can be deployed therein without the constraints of territorial borders. The natural, endogenous dimension is central because cybercrime or delinquency does not always manifest at an international scale. On the other hand, the pandemic evolved at significantly different paces from one continent to another, and even from one country to another in the same region. The policies accompanying this epidemic differed intently from country to country, the populations around the world did not all experience lockdown at the same time, teleworking was not imposed everywhere in the same way, schools were not closed in all places for the same time lapse, etc. Societies did not face the pandemic in the same way, and their lifestyles, or their economies, were not affected by the same way.

Unlike the Malaysian case (and various other countries), the evolution in the number of cyber incidents reported to CERT.br, on the basis of monthly data, does not show a spike in April 2020, a first spike in March, the date after which the volume of incidents remains or less stable, and high, around 60,000 cases/month, against less than 40,000 in January for the same year.

Cyber scanning is the type of major incident reported in Brazil, followed by virus attacks (worms) and distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks. The evolution of each type of cyber incident is characterized by its own curve: scanning practices increased in intensity in March 2020, then continued to grow until July; viral attacks were part of a more linear progression, with no real moment of greatest intensity. For denial of service attacks, they were more virulent in March 2020, but on the whole, tended to decline throughout the following months.

The CERT.br report provides a separate table for the data concerning spam, which on its its own, exceeds all other categories of cyber incidents. The spike of activity was recorded in May 2020, but its progression had been constant since January.

![](img/64f4b6927983f9e91632298e812b4fa7_52_0.png)

As in the case of Malaysia, one type of cyber incident dominates and shapes the curve of the total reported incidents. But by examining the evolution of each category of incident in detail, it becomes evident that phenomena follow trajectories which are sometimes quite different from one another.

### 1.2.3. Leveraging weekly data: the case of China

The data used for monitoring the evolution of cybercrime in China have been drawn from CNCERT. They have been published weekly since the end of July 2017. These weekly data involve the following variables:

- the number of infected computers in China (controlled by Trojans or botnets);
- the number of defaced websites in China;
- the number of backdoored websites in China;
- the number of phishing web pages targeting sites in China;
- the number of new vulnerabilities (including low-, medium- and high-risk vulnerabilities);
- the number of network security incidents handled by the CNCERT.

As Google does not publish mobility data for China, the “stay-at-home” index*14 will be used instead. It provides a daily assessment of the level. The authors of this index have split into four levels:

- 0: absence of restrictive measure;
- 1: recommendation not to leave the house;
- 2: leaving the house is forbidden, except in special cases (only going out for essential);
- 3: leaving the house is strictly forbidden, with only exceptions (only going out once a time).

The exceptions are nuanced in various ways depending on the country, sometimes the region. Here the highest level of restriction occupied a significant portion of the timeline. This does not mean that all of China was under strict lockdown, because lockdown policies were often managed at local level.

During lockdown periods, phishing attacks were particularly numerous. There were spikes in cybercriminal activity, with nearly 18,000 sites identified, and a hyperactivity observed from August until the end of 2020. The second half of the year was particularly characterized by phishing, although such operations had already been detected between January and July 2020 (e.g. actions of APT 32, of Vietnamese origin, within the framework of espionage operations targeting healthcare actors and Chinese authorities)15.

![](img/64f4b6927983f9e91632298e812b4fa7_55_0.png)

*Figure 1.20. Respective evolution of stay-at-home index and the number of defaced websites detected in China since January 2020*

Cyberattacks defacing websites are less and less frequent. This type of criminal activity, often politicized, tends to decrease everywhere in the world. China’s situation is part of this global dynamic.

Like others, the "backdoor infected websites" variable evolved independently, not being correlated with periods featuring lifestyle changes (lockdown and social restriction), and following a clearly downward trend. This decrease could be interpreted as the strengthening of Chinese cybersecurity capabilities, successfully preventing attacks against its servers. The pandemic seems not to have affected its cybersecurity capabilities. But this observation contradicts the previous one, according to which the number of infected computers keeps increasing constantly.
