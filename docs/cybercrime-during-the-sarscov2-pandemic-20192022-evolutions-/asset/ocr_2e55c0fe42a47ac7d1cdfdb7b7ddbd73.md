Concerning social status, Williams (2016) found that lower and higher status citizens reported the highest levels of victimization, while those of average status reported the lowest, exhibiting a curvilinear association of socioeconomic status and victimization.

#  5.5. Fear of (online) crime

Fear of crime is a serious social problem in its own right, and for that reason, it has been vastly studied in the last few decades (Jackson 2005). Researchers have been trying to understand not only how to best operationalize fear of crime (e.g. Gray et al. 2008), but also to discover the main individual and contextual determinants of fearing crime (e.g. Hale 1996; Guedes et al. 2018). The scientific community has consistently defined fear of crime as a phenomenon that includes three main dimensions: emotional fear of crime, cognitive perception of victimization and adopted behaviors for security reasons (avoidance, self-defense and protection) (e.g. Madrid 1997; Gabriel and Greve 2003). Even though fear of crime has been largely studied, is little known about the fear of online crime and specifically about the fear of OIT. In this investigation, we adopted Hille et’s (2015) definition of fear of OIT that includes two dimensions: the fear of financial losses and reputational damage. While the first corresponds to the fear of illegal or unethical appropriation of personal and financial data by unauthorized entity with the objective of getting financial benefits, the second corresponds to the fear of misuse of illegally obtained personal data with the goal of impersonation, which can cause reputational damage to the victim.
