Land’s view was that neoliberal capitalism was so all-encompassing that it “no longer had an outside” [LAU 17] from which its detractors could resist it. This resulted in a state of “political, cultural and theoretical paralysis” [LAU 17] of radical social movements. However, capitalism could be decisively combated by radicals willing to help accelerate the inherent contradictions of the system.

Although accelerationism includes elements of theoretical radicalization, it signals a drastic departure from traditional militant – or even terrorist – strategies: rather than using violence to alter a population’s perspective, it seeks to use pre-existing social tensions as fissile material, in order deronate the entire social system and reduce it to rubble. An example of accelerationist politics is the United Kingdom’s exit from the European Union, known as Brexit, which occurred between 2017 and 2020. Accelerationists supported Brexit, sometimes violently, not because they believed it could improve life in Britain, but because they believed that the potential to result in the exact opposite and in doing so, hasten the country’s collapse. In other words, accelerationists saw Brexit, not as a pathway to the future, but rather as a staunch refusal of the future – a method to coerce the system to quickly progress to its logical, self-destructive end [NOY 2].

In the work of Land, and as Deleuze and Guattari, the principal idea behind the strategy of accelerationism centers on the participation of radical activists in real-life violent activities that can inflame social tensions and lead to civil disorder.

In recent years, accelerationism as a revolutionary strategy has largely migrated from left to far-right circles. Today there are growing numbers of influential far-right ideologies that promote accelerationism a cohesive revolutionary tactic in online manifestos and blog posts [WAL 21]. This far-right ideologies agree with left-wing accelerationist interpretations of capitalism, which dismiss it as fundamentally irredeemable and unworthy of reform [MIL 20]. They push for the calculated participation by far-right activists in violent actions that can potentially exacerbate social tensions and hasten various levels of social, political and economic collapse [WAL 21].
