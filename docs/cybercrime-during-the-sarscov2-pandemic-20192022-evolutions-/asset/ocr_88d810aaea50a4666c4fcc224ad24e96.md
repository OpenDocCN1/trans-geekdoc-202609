#  5.7. Conclusion

The results of the present study, developed in the Portuguese context, suggest that self-reported OIT victimization (both direct and indirect) increased during the Covid-19 pandemic. These results are in accordance with previous studies that analyzed police reports of online fraud (e.g. Buil-Gil et al. 2021; Kemp et al. 2021) but also with the conclusions presented in the multiple national and international reports on cybercrime trends during the Covid-19 pandemic. In fact, individuals started more time on the Internet, and expanding the frequency and variety of activities online, such as leisure, work and financial tasks (as it was observed in the present study). Everyday routines activities, moving from the physical to the online space, may have increased the opportunities for cybercrime to occur. Our results also showed that not only have online activities increased, but differences on specific forms of payments were observed, which may be associated with the victimization of OIT. For instance, 34.5% of the pre-Covid sample used credit cards, 56.5% to use this form of payment in the post-Covid sample. Home banking has also increased substantially, from 25% to 34.9%. Previous studies have found a relationship between specific forms of payments and online victimization. For instance, Alshalhan (2006) concluded that the more people divulge their credit or debit card number, the more they are at risk of becoming victims of cybercrime. Nevertheless, MbNet decreased from 32.9% to 16.1%, which may be explained by the high prevalence of users of MBWAY as a payment of. Interestingly, in spite of only 2.3% of the individuals of our study reporting to have been a victim of MBWAY fraud, this, during the Covid-19 pandemic, one of the most reported crimes in the Portuguese context.

The results of present study also showed that, contrary to our expectations, there was a decrease in the risky activities by the individuals in the post-Covid sample. In fact, individuals opened less dubious links compared to the pre-Covid sample, which may be associated with the increase in a specific guardianship behavior adopted by individuals observed in the results, that is, the search for more education and information about cybercrime.
