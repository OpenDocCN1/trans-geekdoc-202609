Prior to the rally, and in light of reports that constitutional militias and other anti-government groups intended to cause violence, Virginia Governor Ralph North declared a state of emergency. A few days before the event, the FBI arrested heavily armed members of the aforementioned neo-Nazi group the Base, who had allegedly voiced planned to engage in violent acts at the rally [WIL 20]. That event was dwarfed by the January 6, 2021, attack on the United States Capitol Complex by thousands of people, at least 2,000 of whom broke into the main building and attempted to subvert the United States Constitutional order [MCS 21]. In what is an apt illustration of the inability of social media platforms to monitor and regulate content, for the attack had been discussed online by extremists, who "for weeks repeatedly expressed their intentions to attend the January 6 protests, and unashamedly voiced their desire for chaos and violence," according to news reports [LYT 21]. Despite its spiking levels of violence, the attack was the culmination of a broader radicalized movement that questions the legitimacy of President Trump’s successor, Joe Biden. Australian-American counter-insurgency expert David Kilcullen illustrates the sheer mathematical size of this problem:

Roughly 74 million people voted for Donald Trump; a-November Rasmussen poll suggested that 75 percent of them believe Democrats stole the election. That 55.5 million people who believe a Biden administration is illegitimate; if only 3 percent of them decided to act on that belief, that would be 1.66 million people, a sizable base for revolt [KIL 21].

Kilcullen further notes that "stolen elections – or the perception of them [...], which amounts to the same thing – are some of the most common triggers for revolution” in political science research [KIL 21]. Experts encapsulate this with the use of the term “mass radicalization”, a phenomenon in which large swaths of a population become directly vulnerable to extremist messages in a relatively short period of time [STA 11].
