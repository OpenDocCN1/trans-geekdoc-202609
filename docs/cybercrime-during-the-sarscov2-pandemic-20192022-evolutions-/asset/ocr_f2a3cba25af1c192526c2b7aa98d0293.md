Notwithstanding this, the most significant gap is still about legal rules for international cooperation. The Council of Europe’s report mentions legal rules for cooperation in the Civil Procedures Code (Article 26 of the Federal Statute No. 13,105 of 2015). Indeed, that is not the best solution because criminal investigation and prosecution demand a specific legal basis (i.e. laws in the Criminal Procedures code). However, these changes in the Brazilian law also come from the dialogue between lawmakers and law enforcement, as posted by LEP-2:

+   In the national group against cybercrime, we were only keeping track of the child pornography and racism crimes. The group also demands to keep track of crimes for illegal access to electronic devices. We have seen an apparent increase in this kind of crime because of the pandemic. In addition, Brazil will be at the final phase of accession to the Convention on Cybercrime. It is the sole international treaty about cybercrime. It proscribes a lot of specific cyberimes that are still not in Brazilian statutory law. However, Brazil will have to pass those crimes shortly.

The recent accession is essential to the LEP. However, as we see, Brazil will need to make many statutory reforms to adapt its national system. The Covid crisis gave a major push to Brazil’s future accession. However, after this, one can hope that the next few years will be full of many federal statutory law initiatives about cybercrime. Adding international integration with the internal adaption equals more investments to tackle cybercrime. The final accession will provide solutions to a wide array of problems, LEP-2 describes:
