### 5.1. Desiderata for deep learning
Despite the interest, there are particular aspects that a deep learning model must be competitive for tabular learning (fig. 2):

- **Performance comparable or superior to based models**: The first evaluation criterion maintains the performance of deep learning models. It would be desirable to have models that are able to compete in terms of performance with GBDT models.

- **Efficiency in**: Deep learning models are often computationally expensive and can consist of millions if billions of parameters.

- **Data representation**: Deep learning models should learn an efficient representation of data. The advantage of deep learning models in other fields is to capture data and data relationships by learning a complex (and hierarchically) relationship that can be used for other tasks. For example, models trained on one dataset could then be used on other datasets through transfer learning.

- **Self-supervised learning**: Self-supervised learning is the ability to learn without unlabeled data and by exploiting a large dataset of raw data, although this is not a necessity requirement, or would have inconvenient disadvantages. models more robust to irrelevant features and overfitting. A number of models either adding regularization terms or combining neural networks into ensembles. Some datasets are small, so efforts have been made to create methods for generating additional data (data augmentation). Other innovations have made it possible to close the gap with traditional models. For example, the use of embeddings has improved the treatment of categorical variables, and the extraction of information from them. Regularization techniques have significantly reduced overfitting and made the norm more robust. These techniques can be used in combination with deep neural networks becoming popular, after the success of ImageNet and Transformer, there has been a paradigm shift in the artificial intelligence community. Models have become the standard in other fields, although they trained on small amounts of data and then applied to a smaller dataset by tuning. Transfer learning allows skills learned from a large pre-training to be transferred to a dataset where there are many fewer examples.

# 6. From machine learning to tabular deep learning

Tabular data is the oldest form of data that has been analyzed (long before images and text) and therefore the focus of early machine-learning approaches. Techniques such as linear regression, logistic regression, and decision trees were the preferred choice for tabular data. These approaches have been shown effective with small datasets (most initial datasets), especially for regression. These systems showed limitations, sometimes in some cases as noisy data, many correlated features, and low-frequency categorical variables. On the other hand, many specialized models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) have shown effectiveness in the presence of many correlated (non-convex optimization, increased computational cost), but these problems could be solved with good feature engineering [42]. These methods were also interpretable, capable of handling both numerical and categorical variables, and easy to implement. Although neural networks were not the beginning on tabular data, the focus quickly shifted to homogeneous data (given the successes with convolutional networks).

The explosion of e-commerce led to a renewed interest in tabular data, especially for models that could handle heterogeneous data. This also led to renewed interest precisely for the ability of neural networks to identify complex relationships and reduce feature engineering. During the same time, neural networks have become increasingly popular, especially in other applications such as computer vision and natural language processing. This has motivated researchers to modify classical MLPs to make them more suitable for tabular data than simply importing architectures from other data types. As many time these were computationally expensive and inefficient inference for large datasets [91], XGBoost has turned into the new standard, especially for small tabular datasets. At the same time research on making neural networks more suitable for tabular data.
