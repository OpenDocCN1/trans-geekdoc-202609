Access to quality datasets is crucial for developing or conducting model benchmarking; in the following table 12, we point out some of high-quality tabular datasets.

In addition to variety, different datasets are enriched with metadata and information about features. In general, these datasets are used for pre-training of models (which such as for). Some of these datasets can be used for model training; unfortunately, have not been made available. However, some may be of interest for classification tasks. TabUI [371] contains 133 tabular datasets from Kaggle (with 77 dataset on 2030). TabLib [685] includes 267 million individuals totals (69TB of data) with features such as CSV, SQL, XML, PDF, Excel, and others) and sourced from GitHub and Common Crawl. For each task, contextual information (like URLs, source and domain) is collected for a total of over 8,000 tasks. OpenTab [373] is another large-scale tabular dataset used for cross-training pretraining tabular models (46M tabular datasets), collected from several public repositories such as UCI, Machine Learning Repository, Kaggle, and others. It provides information in column names to facilitate the training. TP-BERT Pretraining Dataset [367] consists of 202 carefully curated datasets (101 for classification, 101 for regression). These datasets were selected based on specific criteria, like descriptive feature names, and prepared for fine-tuning transformer. The GTL (Generative Transformer Learning) [356] dataset contains 384 tabular datasets, covering domains (including healthcare, finance, retail, sustainability, and climate). ## 12.3. Choosing a model for tabular task

Given the heterogeneous nature of tabular data, there is no one winning architecture for all tasks. A practical guide is to choose the model based on the task and requirements such as robustness, interpretability, domain, and amount of data available (see, 16). For applications where fast training, inference, and low latency are required, GBDTs are favored. These models perform well because of their ability to handle categorical variables efficiently. They also perform well for small and medium datasets, and for regression tasks. Neural networks are competitive for large datasets, complex features, automatic interaction, automated feature engineering, and scalability. Transformer networks also compete with both GBDTs and larger models. Additionally, for some tasks, some models show to superior to others (e.g., sTabNet for some biological tasks, GNN-based models) and are considered prime candidates.
