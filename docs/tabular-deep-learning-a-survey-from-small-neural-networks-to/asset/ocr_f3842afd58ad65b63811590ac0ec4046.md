GANDULF [164] is an architecture based on gated recurrent units (GRU), inspired by the methods of LSTM and GRU, which were used to mitigate problems like vanishing and exploding gradients and adapted for tabular data. # 7.4. Specialized architectures

Over time, specialized architectures have been proposed to enhance or replace MLPs (Tab. 6 and 9). Some strategies are directed at enhancing the capabilities of MLPs in handling tabular data, while others create structures to overcome the limitations of classical models.

**Alternatives to MLP.**get Neural Networks (PNNs) [275] are a type of neural network architecture designed to enhance the modeling of feature interactions, especially in structured data like tabular data. While classical MLPs primarily rely on additive transformations followed by activations, PNNs incorporate multiplicative interactions between features. MLPs have difficulty in being able to capture complex feature interactions that are often present in tabular data, which are higher-order interactions. PNNs, therefore, use additive and multiplicative interactions between features to better capture local interactions between features (especially categorical). Kolmogorov-Arnold networks (KANS) are a new type of neural network based on Kolmogorov-Arnold representation. While neural networks are based on the universal approximation theorem, Kolmogorov representation states that any multivariate function can be represented as a finite composition of univariate functions. KANS try to overcome the activation functions of MLPs by using B-splines instead of activation on each node whose weights are replaced by a set of coefficients (edges of the network) [280, 224]. In KANS you can adjust the grid size (the number of nodes in the B-spline) to capture more details in the data, including regularization (parameterization and pruning) to make the training smoother and symbolic representation with known symbolic functions (like sine, cosine) or to improve the interpretability! According to the authors, KANS are more interpretable, expressive, and work well for continued regression. In addition, spectral bias analysis shows that KANS, as opposed to MLPs, have reduced bias (which is useful for tabular learning). Although there, KANS are not yet optimized, especially in computational tasks [280, 314, 141, 388].
