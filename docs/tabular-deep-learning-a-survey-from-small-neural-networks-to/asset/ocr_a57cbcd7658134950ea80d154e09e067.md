# 12.3. Choosing a model for tabular task

Given the heterogeneous nature of tabular data, there is no one winning architecture for all tasks. A practical guide is to choose the model based on the task and requirements such as robustness, interpretability, domain, and amount of data available (see, 16). For applications where fast training, inference, and low latency are required, GBDTs are favored. These models perform well because of their ability to handle categorical variables efficiently. They also perform well for small and medium datasets, and for regression tasks. Neural networks are competitive for large datasets, complex features, automatic interaction, automated feature engineering, and scalability. Transformer networks also compete with both GBDTs and larger models. Additionally, for some tasks, some models show to superior to others (e.g., sTabNet for some biological tasks, GNN-based models) and are considered prime candidates.
