# 7.7. Specialized architectures for specific tasks

Although it could be a sub-branch of the specialized architectures, it is desirable to separate them by cause of the different purposes of these models. The earlier architectures are concerned with specific modifications to optimize the models for tabular data; the models in this section go through modifications to optimize them for a specific task. In particular, most of these architectures are derived from those that were presented in the previous sections and demonstrate how by modifying or recombining models can be addressed. These models often have a complexity of the original architecture in order to not be generalized for tabular tasks or datasets that do not have these assumptions. While it is not possible to create an extensive taxonomy of these techniques, it is important to discuss their success and limitations. For example, neural network models have been adapted for regression (predicting patient survival or likelihood of failure). KAN and LassoNet and TabNet have been adapted to predict patient survival [118, 270, 87] with success, using MLP and for interpretability. Another approach is the design of complex table-to-image pipelines which shows how ever the models we saw can be created for various specific tasks. Single-layer classifiers are adequate for simple regression, yet their complexity is a challenge for complex models. TabNet exploits prior knowledge to be able to apply to signals, showing that even small networks can learn a representation [287]. GearFormer and SDeGT use self-supervised learning to train a structure in a small manner on millions of examples [352]. These three models can be fine-tuned and used for other tasks in the domain. GEARS [294] is an architecture designed to predict the effect of generic perturbations. GEARS exploits the combination of two GNNs to learn a representation of gene expression and its perturbations.
