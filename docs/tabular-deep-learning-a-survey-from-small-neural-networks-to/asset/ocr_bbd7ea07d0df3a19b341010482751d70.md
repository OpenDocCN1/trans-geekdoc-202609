In fact, many of the architectures we saw had attention only to improving the interpretability of the model. For example, some models are designed by decision trees to bridge the gap between interpretability and neural performance (Decision Trees, Disjunctive Normal Form, and TabNet; [77, 149, 267]). In contrast, other models directly combine neural networks and decision trees into hybrid systems (DeepGBM, Boosted GNN, NODE; [170, 149, 267]). If MLPs are not interpretable, it is often because of categorizing variables in a continuous regression which provides insights into the relationship between them, improving explainability. In addition, this representation can then be visualized through the dimensionality of reduction techniques, gaining further features. Other methods focus on highlighting the relationship or the importance of features. Each has the advantage of representing relationships between features or examples, representing tabular data in a local format, and revealing exact connections between features. ADCS, in addition with some enhancements, is for tabular data. Transformer-based models, self-attention learns relationships between features and examples. Attributes are often used as a method of investigating the relationships between various features, and methods based on visualization are often used in research. The explainability of transformers, however, is still an open question. Some research emphasizes important features while filtering irrelevant ones (e.g., GAN, SoftTab, and TabNet [164, 359, 287]). The emergence of importance for features can be detected through regularization. Many of the regularization techniques used are L1 regularization, dropout, sparsity). Another advantage of regularization is that they make the models simpler and thus more interpretable. Other regularization such as harmonic loss prevents overfitting. Kan (Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks) introduced make neural networks more interpretable. Through simplification, simple functions can be substituted for the neural network explanation [280, 224]. Even more explicitly, DNNF decides trees into propositional logic, making the flow of

An interesting note, Borisov [31] shows the attention maps of the models (TabTransformer and SAINT) do not correlate with the attribution of importance from SHAP. For the same dataset, or even the same model, different post-hoc methods give results on which features are most important. XAIBench [223] provides a systematic framework for testing XAI. The results show that various models having varying performance for different datasets, and are not robust. In addition, the explanations are not robust to the presence of correlated features. OpenXAI [4] also provides tabular dataset benchmark, showing that many models are interpretable and there is a strong variation among their performances (fig. 15). In summary, while neural networks are closing the gap with GBDT models, their interpretability is still uncertain. Some of the best-performing models, can only be interpreted through post-hoc methods. It would be preferable to use interpretable architectures that are transparent by design or present a notion of feature importance as well. In addition, studies of model interpretability are still needed.

# Tabular data applications

This data can be used for various patient outcomes such as disease diagnosis, prognosis. In addition, models are often used during drug discovery and chemical analysis to evaluate the trials of a drug. Many insurance companies also use tabular data for policy calculation and other options. Healthcare, however, faces the possess of large datasets that are have practical implications.
