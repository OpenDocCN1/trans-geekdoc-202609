## 3.1. Handling noisy and incomplete data

Tabular data generally requires extensive processing since it cannot be directly fit into a model. Data may originate from missing (and be found randomly) missing in the dataset. Other cases may be derived from some data collection. For example, in a survey, some people might forget to fill out certain questions or individuals under 25 may not have been included to answer specific questions. There are different methods of imputation, the different origins, deciding how to treat it as not easy. For reason, the input of missing data comes from the data solutions for each type of data have [297, 89, 221].

Another problem of tabular data is outliers. An outlier is a value that is different from all others in the dataset [23, 137]. These can originate from errors in a calibrated sensor, a near clear (for example, a temperature sensor may show a much higher value than today). Not all models are robust to outliers, and evaluation metrics can also be altered by outliers (e.g., a large error can significantly increase mean squared error). Thus, outliers are exactly what we are looking for (adversarial attacks, network intrusion, and credit fraud). This sub-branch of tabular learning is exactly: anomaly detection.

Another challenge is the scale of dimensionality. When a dataset has many features which makes it complex for the model to learn. This is common in some fields, as datasets obtained in machine learning from various sources are often classes-unbalanced. Or it can result from the nature of the data itself: most emails are spam, there is less credit from legitimate transactions, and so on. With models with unbalanced classes may only consider the majority class, so some corrections are required.

## 3.6. Complex boundaries

Several observations show that neural networks tend to produce smooth solutions, which can make them perform poorly, especially on datasets with irregular decision boundaries [117]. Many deep learning models use techniques such as weight decay, dropout, and batch normalization to prevent overfitting, but these may also lead to smoother solutions. Additionally, activation functions like ReLU, Sigmoid, and Tanh can cause gradient attention, or vanishing gradients in the data.

As neural networks become deeper, propagation tends to become more uniform, which with the search for a globally optimal loss, may fail to capture local structures.
