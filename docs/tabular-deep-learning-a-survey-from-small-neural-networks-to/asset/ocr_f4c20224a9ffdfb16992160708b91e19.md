# 6. From machine learning to tabular deep learning

Tabular data is the oldest form of data that has been analyzed (long before images and text) and therefore the focus of early machine-learning approaches. Techniques such as linear regression, logistic regression, and decision trees were the preferred choice for tabular data. These approaches have been shown effective with small datasets (most initial datasets), especially for regression. These systems showed limitations, sometimes in some cases as noisy data, many correlated features, and low-frequency categorical variables. On the other hand, many specialized models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) have shown effectiveness in the presence of many correlated (non-convex optimization, increased computational cost), but these problems could be solved with good feature engineering [42]. These methods were also interpretable, capable of handling both numerical and categorical variables, and easy to implement. Although neural networks were not the beginning on tabular data, the focus quickly shifted to homogeneous data (given the successes with convolutional networks).

The explosion of e-commerce led to a renewed interest in tabular data, especially for models that could handle heterogeneous data with thousands of categorical variables. This also led to renewed interest precisely for the ability of neural networks to identify complex relationships and reduce feature engineering. On the other hand, various techniques (Random Forest, XGBoost, and Catboost) have emerged [52, 127, 290] that have shown resistance to overfitting and irrelevant features, although in learning regression target functions, simultaneously representing data orientation (in tabular data that has this orientation). As many time these were computationally expensive and inefficient in inference for large datasets [91], XGBoost has turned into the new standard, especially for small tabular datasets. At the same time research on making neural networks more suitable for tabular data than simply importing architectures from other data types.
