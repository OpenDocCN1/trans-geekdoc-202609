+   3. **Pseudo-label Generation**: The model’s predictions on the unlabeled data are used as pseudo-labels for the particles. These pseudo-labels are essentially the model’s estimated locations and trajectories for the particles in the unlabeled images.

+   4. **Incorporation of Pseudo-labels**: The pseudo-labeled data is combined with the original labeled dataset, creating a larger, augmented dataset with both labeled and pseudo-labeled samples.

+   5. **Fine-tuning**: The model is fine-tuned or retrained on the combined dataset, using both the original labeled data and the newly created pseudo-labeled data. The fine-tuning process adjusts the model’s parameters to better fit the labeled and pseudo-labeled samples.

The pseudo-labeling process effectively utilizes the model’s predictions on the unlabeled data to provide additional training examples, thus augmenting the training dataset. This approach allows the model to learn from the available labeled data while leveraging the information present in the unlabeled data, potentially leading to improved tracking accuracy and generalization.

Pseudo-labeling can be particularly useful when labeled data for particle tracking is scarce or costly to obtain. By generating pseudo-labels from the model’s predictions on unlabeled data, researchers can create a larger and more diverse dataset, enabling more effective training and tracking of particles in soft matter microscopy images.

However, it’s essential to be cautious when using pseudo-labeling, as inaccuracies in the model’s predictions can propagate to the pseudo-labeled data and affect the performance of the final model. Careful validation and evaluation are necessary to ensure the quality and reliability of the pseudo-labels and the effectiveness of the pseudo-labeling approach for particle tracking in soft matter microscopy.
