If E performs a one-hot encoding that indicates which state the system is in, then the pair (E,P) is called a Markov state model, and P is a matrix of conditional probabilities to be in state j at time t + τ given that the system was in state i at time t.

Learning the embedding E is more difficult than learning P, as optimizing E by maximum likelihood or minimal regression error in latent space leads to a collapse of E to trivial, uninteresting solutions. This problem can be avoided by following a variational approach to approximating the leading terms of the spectral decomposition (Equation 10). The variational approach for conformation dynamics states that for dynamics obeying detailed balance, the eigenvalues \(\lambda_k\) of a propagator matrix P via any encoding y = E(x) are, in the statistical limit, lower bounds of the true \(\lambda_k^*\). The VAMP variational principle is more general, as it does not require detailed balance (see Equation 21) and applies to the singular values \(\sigma_k\):

\(\lambda_k \le \lambda_k^* \text{ (with detailed balance),}\)
\(\sigma_k \le \sigma_k^* \text{ (no detailed balance).}\)

As VAMP is the more general principle, we can use it to define the loss function for estimating molecular kinetics models:

\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAMP-2}}((\mathbf{y}_t^0, \mathbf{y}_t^\tau)) = - \left\| (\mathbf{C}_{00})^{-\frac{1}{2}} \mathbf{C}_{0\tau} (\mathbf{C}_{\tau\tau})^{-\frac{1}{2}} \right\|_F^2 \quad (15)\)

# 8.2.5 Sampling and Thermodynamics

MD time steps are on the order of one femtosecond (10^-15s), while configuration changes governing molecular function are often rare events that may take 10^-3 to 10^3s. Even when the potential and the forces generated by single-protein folding and unfolding are evaluated quickly, simulating these processes by direct MD simulation may take years to centuries on a supercomputer. To avoid this sampling problem, ML methods can be employed to learn generation of equilibrium samples from \(\mu(x)\) more efficiently, or even to generate statistically independent samples in one shot.
