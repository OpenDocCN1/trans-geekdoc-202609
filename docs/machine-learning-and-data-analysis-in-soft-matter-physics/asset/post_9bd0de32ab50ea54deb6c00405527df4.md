## PREFACE

Machine learning has emerged as a rapidly expanding and highly promising frontier in research, offering potential solutions to various engineering and scientific challenges. One area where machine learning has found increasing application is in condensed matter physics, a discipline that delves into the collective behavior of complex systems at the quantum and atomistic levels. These systems can involve electrons, nuclei, magnetic moments, atoms, or qubits, exhibiting an exponential growth in state space as the number of particles increases. This inherent complexity makes condensed matter physics an excellent candidate for machine learning approaches, given their big data requirements.

Traditionally, condensed matter physics seeks to understand the interactions between particles and how these interactions give rise to macroscopic properties. It intersects with other important scientific fields like Chemistry, Materials Science, Statistical Physics, and High-Performance Computing. In recent times, researchers have started leveraging artificial intelligence techniques, particularly machine learning algorithms and neural networks, to identify patterns in the behavioral dynamics of many-body systems. This convergence of condensed matter physics and machine learning has opened up new avenues for research and exploration.

In this review, we delve into various aspects where machine learning has been successfully applied in condensed matter physics. One key area is the use of machine learning algorithms to describe and analyze potential energy surfaces. By employing ML schemes, researchers can efficiently explore energy landscapes and discover stable configurations of particles. Another exciting application lies in characterizing topological phases of matter in lattice systems. Machine learning has proven valuable in deciphering the intricate structures and properties of such materials, enhancing our understanding of exotic phases.
