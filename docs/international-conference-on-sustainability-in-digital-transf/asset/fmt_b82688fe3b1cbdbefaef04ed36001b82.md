## Most Global Cited Documents

The analysis of globally cited documents in the realm of consumer behavioral intentions and sustainable fashion provides several insights: The globally cited documents span a range of themes, from collaborative consumption and sustainable fashion adoption to the influence of self-concept, knowledge, and cultural perspectives on consumer behavior.

Studies like Blazquez et al. \[[24](#ch48.xhtml#ref48_24)\] as shown in [figure 48.2](#ch48.xhtml#fig48_2) emphasized the importance of considering cultural and regional factors. Understanding consumer behavior in different contexts is crucial for effective sustainable fashion strategies tailored to diverse audiences.

Works such as Lang and Joyner Armstrong and Legere and Kang \[[25](#ch48.xhtml#ref48_25), [26](#ch48.xhtml#ref48_26)\] delved into the psychological drivers of sustainable fashion consumption. Concepts like fashion leadership, uniqueness, materialism, and self-concept play pivotal roles in influencing consumers’ decisions. Gupta and Pandey \[[28](#ch48.xhtml#ref48_28)\] discussed predictive approaches offering valuable insights for marketers and policymakers.

Contributions like Kumar et al. \[[27](#ch48.xhtml#ref48_27)\] enrich established theories such as the theory of planned behavior by incorporating factors like perceived consumer effectiveness and environmental concern. These adaptations align theories with the complexities of sustainable fashion choices.
