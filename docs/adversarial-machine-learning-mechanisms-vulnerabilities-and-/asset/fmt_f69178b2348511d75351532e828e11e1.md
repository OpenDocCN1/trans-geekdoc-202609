
Transfer learning propagation refers to the phenomenon where vulnerabilities, biases, or adversarial behaviors learned in a source model are inherited by downstream models through fine-tuning or re-use. This propagation can silently transmit risk across model families, especially in open model ecosystems. 

`Transfer Poisoning`

Transfer poisoning is an attack where poisoned data is introduced into a model used for transfer learning, embedding vulnerabilities that survive during fine-tuning. These attacks target general-purpose pre-trained models, compromising entire downstream pipelines even when the target data is clean. 

`Transfer Robustness Testing`

Transfer robustness testing evaluates how well a model maintains accuracy and security when adversarial examples or poisoned data are transferred from another model. This type of testing is critical for assessing generalization of defenses across architectures or deployment environments. 

`Transferability`

Transferability describes how easily adversarial examples created for one model can affect another model, even with different architectures or training data. High transferability enables black-box attacks and exposes systemic weaknesses in model families that share similar decision boundaries. 

`Transferability Score`

A `transferability score` quantifies how effectively an adversarial input generated for one model can be used to deceive another. This score helps compare model robustness and identify attack surfaces shared across multiple systems. 

`Transferable Perturbation`

A `transferable perturbation` is a crafted input modification that reliably deceives multiple models across tasks or architectures. These perturbations are designed to exploit common features or weaknesses in learning processes. Their existence challenges the effectiveness of model-specific defenses. 

`Transformer Gradient Dynamics`

`Transformer gradient dynamics` refers to the behavior of gradient flows within transformer architectures during training or adversarial optimization. Understanding these dynamics helps researchers identify instability, vulnerability, and opportunities for robust training or attack resistance. 

`Trigger Activation Path`

A `trigger activation path` is the internal computational pathway through which a backdoor or trojan trigger causes a model to switch behaviors. Mapping this path helps defenders locate where malicious logic is embedded and how it flows through the network. 

`Trigger Collisions`

`Trigger collisions` occur when multiple backdoor triggers unintentionally activate the same or conflicting malicious behaviors within a model. These collisions can degrade attack precision or cause unintended outputs, and they present a potential opportunity for detection. 

`Trigger Embedding`

`Trigger embedding` is the insertion of a backdoor signal into the internal representation space of a model, often in a way that survives pruning, fine-tuning, or distributional shift. These embeddings are designed to remain dormant until the attacker presents the matching input pattern. 

`Trigger-Response Behavior`

`Trigger-response behavior` describes the hidden logic programmed into a model where a specific input (the trigger) causes a pre-defined, often harmful or attacker-controlled, output. This behavior is central to backdoor and trojan attacks and is usually imperceptible during standard testing. 

`Trojan Model Artifact`

A `trojan model artifact` is a file, checkpoint, or component that contains a model with embedded backdoors, poisoned weights, or malicious logic. These artifacts are often distributed through public model repositories or compromised supply chains and can compromise downstream applications if used blindly. 

`Vulnerability Map`

A `vulnerability map` is a diagnostic tool or visualization that highlights regions in input or latent space where a model is most susceptible to adversarial manipulation. It helps researchers and defenders understand risk surfaces, optimize testing, and design more robust systems. 

`White-Box Attack`

A `white-box attack` is a threat model in which the attacker has full access to the model's architecture, parameters, and training data. This enables highly optimized adversarial inputs, backdoor insertion, or gradient-based evasion. White-box attacks represent the strongest form of adversarial threat.
