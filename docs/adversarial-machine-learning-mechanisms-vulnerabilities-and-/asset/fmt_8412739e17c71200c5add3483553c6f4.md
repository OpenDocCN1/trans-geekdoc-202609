
Robustness metrics are essential tools for objectively assessing how AI models perform under adversarial pressure. In a security context, these metrics do not merely evaluate how accurate a model is under clean conditions—they quantify the precise degree to which the model's performance degrades when exposed to crafted perturbations designed to elicit failure. The most common form of adversarial robustness evaluation involves applying systematically controlled input modifications and measuring how well the model preserves intended behavior. These metrics provide the foundation for repeatable and comparative evaluations across different models, threat scenarios, and defensive strategies.

Attack success rate (ASR) is among the most direct and widely used metrics for robustness analysis. It captures the proportion of adversarial inputs that achieve their intended effect—typically, forcing the model to misclassify an input or produce a targeted, harmful, or misleading output. In targeted attacks, ASR measures how often the model is coerced into a specific incorrect output class. In untargeted attacks, it quantifies how often any incorrect prediction is induced. High ASR values signal that a model is particularly vulnerable under adversarial pressure, even if its performance on unperturbed data remains strong.

Perturbation magnitude metrics refine this analysis by quantifying how little an adversary needs to change an input to induce failure. These metrics often use L[0], L[2], or L^∞ norms to represent the minimum perturbation required to achieve a misclassification. In essence, they measure the adversary's cost of attack. A low perturbation magnitude threshold means that the model can be destabilized with imperceptible or minimal changes, while a higher threshold indicates greater robustness. Security practitioners use these metrics to establish acceptable perturbation budgets and evaluate whether defense mechanisms meaningfully increase the attacker's burden.

Robust accuracy metrics complement these views by calculating how accurate the model remains when subjected to adversarial examples at defined perturbation levels. Instead of reporting binary success or failure of an attack, robust accuracy evaluates overall predictive fidelity under known adversarial conditions. It is typically measured by generating adversarial variants of test inputs and comparing classification outcomes to their original labels. The delta between clean accuracy and robust accuracy illustrates the fragility of the model in adversarial contexts and is often used to benchmark the effectiveness of adversarial training and other robustness-enhancing techniques. Confidence variance metrics add another layer by examining how a model's prediction certainty changes under adversarial perturbation. Many models express high confidence even in incorrect predictions, especially when adversarial inputs push them across decision boundaries. Measuring confidence shifts between clean and perturbed inputs helps identify how susceptible a model is to being manipulated into confidently wrong behavior. These metrics are particularly relevant for detecting miscalibration under attack and can support real-time anomaly detection by flagging unusual confidence drops or spikes.

Empirical robustness curves offer a more nuanced picture by mapping adversarial perturbation intensity against resulting model accuracy. These curves generate a continuous profile of how quickly and sharply performance deteriorates as attacks become stronger. A model that maintains high accuracy across a range of perturbation levels will exhibit a flatter, more resilient curve, while a steep curve indicates rapid collapse under pressure. By plotting these curves for multiple models or configurations, evaluators can visually compare robustness thresholds and failure dynamics under diverse conditions.

Sensitivity analysis metrics extend robustness evaluation to the input feature space. These metrics quantify how sensitive specific features, tokens, or embedding components are to perturbation. By identifying which inputs have disproportionate influence on model outputs, evaluators can expose high-leverage vulnerabilities and inform targeted defenses. Sensitivity analysis is particularly valuable in structured data models, natural language processing systems, and time-series models, where localized perturbations can have cascading effects on prediction trajectories.

Embedding-space robustness metrics shift the focus to the internal representations learned by the model. These metrics examine how much an input's embedding changes in response to adversarial manipulation, offering insight into whether the model's internal logic maintains semantic stability under attack. If small perturbations in input space produce large displacements in embedding space, the model may be encoding brittle or overly complex decision boundaries. Conversely, embedding resilience suggests that a model is internally consistent even when inputs are nudged off the manifold of natural examples.

Robustness transferability metrics measure a model's susceptibility to adversarial inputs crafted against a different model. Known as transfer attacks, these scenarios mimic real-world black-box threats where an attacker lacks access to the target model and instead trains a surrogate model to generate attacks. High transferability indicates that vulnerabilities are not confined to one architecture but generalized across models. These metrics are critical for evaluating security under deployment constraints, particularly in scenarios where attackers can train against open-source analogs or publicly available model snapshots.
