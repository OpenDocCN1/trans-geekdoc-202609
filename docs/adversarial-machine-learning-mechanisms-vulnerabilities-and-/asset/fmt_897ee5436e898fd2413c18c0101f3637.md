
: Behavioral fingerprinting is the process of identifying or characterizing a model's typical outputs or decision patterns in response to specific stimuli. It can be used for anomaly detection, model verification, or tamper detection. When fingerprints change unexpectedly, it may indicate that the model has been attacked, fine-tuned maliciously, or otherwise compromised.

: Black-box probing is a technique used to infer the internal workings or vulnerabilities of a model by observing its outputs in response to controlled inputs. Since the attacker does not have access to the model's architecture or parameters, probing relies on trial-and-error queries. This method underpins many real-world attacks, such as model extraction, membership inference, and transfer-based evasion. 

`## Black-box Probing`

: Cascading failure refers to a sequence of system breakdowns where one failure leads to another, often worsening the impact at each stage. In AI systems, this can occur when an upstream model's error propagates to downstream components, especially in multi-agent or tool-using architectures. Adversaries may exploit these chains by triggering a small fault that spirals into large-scale dysfunction. 

`## Cascading Failure`

: Certifiable layers are components within a neural network that can be mathematically verified to meet certain robustness or safety guarantees. These layers are designed to limit how much an input can influence the output, even under perturbations. They are part of a broader effort to create machine learning models that can be provably resilient to adversarial attacks. 

`## Certifiable Layers`

: Certified robustness refers to formal guarantees that a model will produce consistent outputs within a defined range of input perturbations. Unlike empirical defenses, certified techniques use mathematical proofs to bound a model's vulnerability. These methods are especially important in safety-critical domains like finance, healthcare, and autonomous systems. 

`## Certified Robustness`

: Chain injection is a form of adversarial attack in agentic or tool-using AI systems where malicious inputs are embedded across multiple steps or components. The attacker inserts instructions or payloads that only trigger harmful behavior when processed in sequence. This chaining makes the attack stealthier and harder to detect through traditional prompt or input validation. 

`## Chain Injection`

: Chained vulnerabilities occur when multiple weak points in an AI system—or across connected systems—can be exploited in combination to escalate an attack. These chains can link different models, tools, APIs, or decision points. By exploiting vulnerabilities in sequence, attackers can bypass isolated defenses and achieve broader control over the system. 

`## Chained Vulnerabilities`

: Checkpoint serialization is the process of saving a trained model's internal state—including weights, gradients, and metadata—for later use or sharing. While essential for model reproducibility and deployment, it can also introduce risks if the saved file includes tampered values or embedded backdoors. Secure serialization practices are crucial in preventing supply chain attacks. 

`## Checkpoint Serialization`

: A clean-label attack is a type of data poisoning where the attacker modifies training data in subtle ways without altering the associated labels. Because the labels appear correct, these attacks can bypass basic data validation checks. Clean-label poisoning is particularly effective in transfer learning scenarios and can lead to hidden backdoors or misclassification traps. 

`## Clean-Label Attack`

: Clean-label poisoning is the broader strategy of contaminating a training set with legitimate-looking data that leads the model to learn incorrect associations. These poisoned examples are indistinguishable from normal data upon casual inspection. This technique makes it difficult to detect or filter malicious entries, especially in open or crowdsourced data pipelines. 

`## Clean-Label Poisoning`

: Cloud-hosted inference refers to running machine learning model predictions on remote servers rather than local devices. While convenient and scalable, this setup introduces new security concerns, such as API abuse, model extraction, and exposure to multi-tenant threats. Adversaries may exploit weak isolation or insufficient rate-limiting to compromise cloud-based models. 

`## Cloud-Hosted Inference`

: Conditional evasion is an attack technique where adversarial inputs are crafted to only cause model failures under specific conditions—such as certain classes, users, or contexts. This selective misbehavior makes the attack harder to detect and allows the model to perform normally in most cases. It is often used to bypass monitoring systems that rely on overall accuracy metrics. 

`## Conditional Evasion`

: Conditional prompt injection involves inserting malicious instructions into prompts or documents that only activate under certain model conditions or user queries. This technique is commonly used in generative AI to bypass content filters while appearing benign. Attackers exploit latent model behavior to activate these payloads at just the right moment. 

`## Conditional Prompt Injection`

: A confidence estimator is a mechanism within or alongside a machine learning model that assesses how certain the model is about its predictions. It can be used to flag uncertain or potentially incorrect outputs, especially in critical applications. In adversarial contexts, attackers may attempt to spoof or exploit these confidence scores to hide manipulations. 

`## Confidence Estimator`

: Confidence score analysis is the practice of reviewing model certainty levels to assess robustness, detect anomalies, or monitor behavioral drift. By analyzing shifts in confidence, defenders can identify potential adversarial inputs or data distribution changes. However, attackers can sometimes manipulate models to maintain high confidence in wrong predictions, making analysis alone insufficient for defense. 

`## Confidence Score Analysis`

: Constraint propagation refers to the technique of enforcing specific logical or structural constraints throughout a model's architecture or output. This can be used to limit adversarial influence by ensuring that certain relationships remain valid even under perturbation. It is often used in symbolic reasoning systems, hybrid AI, or robust optimization models. 

`## Constraint Propagation`

: A constraint solver is a computational engine that finds solutions to a problem while satisfying a set of predefined conditions. In the context of adversarial machine learning, constraint solvers may be used either by attackers to generate valid adversarial inputs or by defenders to verify model behavior. They are essential tools for ensuring consistency in logic-driven AI systems. 

`## Constraint Solver`

: Contextual authority reframing is a prompt-based attack technique in which an adversary subtly shifts the model's perception of who is issuing instructions. By changing the apparent speaker or context, the attacker can convince the model to respond to forbidden queries. This is a growing concern in large language models that rely heavily on prompt structure for behavioral guidance. 

`## Contextual Authority Reframing`

: Contextual conditioning refers to the use of surrounding information—such as prior dialogue, document structure, or metadata—to influence a model's output. This technique is used both in model design and in adversarial attacks. Malicious actors can exploit it to craft indirect prompt injections that appear innocuous until combined with certain input contexts.
