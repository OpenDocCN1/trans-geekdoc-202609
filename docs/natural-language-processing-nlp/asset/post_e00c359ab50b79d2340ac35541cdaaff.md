
## Appendix B

## Numerical optimization

Unconstrained numerical optimization involves solving problems of the form,

$$\min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^D} f(x),$$ [B.1]

where $x \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is a vector of $D$ real numbers.

Differentiation is fundamental to numerical optimization. Suppose that at some $x^*$, every partial derivative of $f$ is equal to 0: formally, $\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\right|_{x^*} = 0$. Then $x^*$ is said to be a **critical point** of $f$. If $f$ is a **convex** function (defined in § 2.4), then the value of $f(x^*)$ is equal to the global minimum of $f$ iff $x^*$ is a critical point of $f$.

As an example, consider the convex function $f(x) = (x-2)^2+3$, shown in Figure B.1a. The derivative is $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2x-4$. A unique minimum can be obtained by setting the derivative equal to zero and solving for $x$, obtaining $x^* = 2$.
