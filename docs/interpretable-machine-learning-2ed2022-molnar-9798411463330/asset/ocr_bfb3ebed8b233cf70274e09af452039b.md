For example, you train a model for predicting whether a patient should remain in hospital or be discharged early. you really want to make sure that the model is robust and makes correct predictions, because a wrong release of a patient can have bad consequences. Patient records can be very messy, so you do not have perfect confidence in the quality of the data. But checking information and correcting it can be very time-consuming, because once you have reported patients you need to check, the hospital actually needs someone to look at the records of the selected patients more closely, which might be handwritten and lying in some archive. Checking data for a patient could take an hour or more. In view of these costs, it makes sense to check only a few important instances. The best way is to select patients who had a high influence on the prediction model. Koh and Liang (2017) showed that this type of selection works much better than random selection or the selection of those with the highest loss or classification.

# 9.5.3 Advantages of Identifying Influential Instances

The approaches of deletion diagnostics and influence functions are very different from the most feature-perturbation based approaches presented in the Model-Agnostic chapter. A look at influential instances emphasizes the role of data training in the learning process. This makes influence functions and deletion diagnostics one of the best debugging tools for machine learning models. Of the techniques presented in this book, they are the only ones that directly help to identify the instances which should be checked for errors.

Deletion diagnostics are model-agnostic, meaning the approach can be applied to any model. Also influence functions based on the derivatives can be applied to a broad class of models.

We can use these to compare different machine learning models and better understand their behavior, going beyond comparing only the predictive performance.

We have not talked about this topic in this chapter, influence functions via derivatives can be used to create adversarial training data. These are instances that can be manipulated in such a way that the model cannot predict certain instances when the model is trained on those manipulated instances. The difference to the methods in the Adversarial Examples chapter is that the attack takes place during training time, also known as poisoning attacks. If you are interested, read by Koh and Liang (2017).

For deletion diagnostics and influence functions, we considered the difference in the prediction and for the influence function increase of the loss. But, the approach is generalizable to any question of the form: “What to when we delete or update instance z?”, where you can find “...” with any function of your desire. You can analyze how much a training instance influences the overall loss of the model. You can analyze how much a training instance influences the feature importance. You can analyze how much a training instance influences which feature is selected for the first split when training a decision tree.
