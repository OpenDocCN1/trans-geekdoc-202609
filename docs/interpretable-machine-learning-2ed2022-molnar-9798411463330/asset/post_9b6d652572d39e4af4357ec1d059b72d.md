## 9.2.7 Advantages

The explanations are visual and we are quick to recognize images. In particular, methods only highlight important pixels, it is easy to immediately recognize the important regions of the image.

Gradient-based methods are usually faster to compute than model-agnostic methods. For example, LIME and SHAP can also be used to explain image classifications, but are more expensive to compute.

There are many methods to choose from.

## 9.2.8 Disadvantages

With most interpretation methods, it is difficult to know whether an explanation is correct, and a huge part of the evaluation is only qualitative (“These explanations look about right, let’s publish the paper already").

## 9.2 PIXEL ATTRIBUTION (SALIENCY MAPS)

Pixel attribution methods can be fragile. Ghorbani et al. (2019) showed that introducing small (adversarial) perturbations to an image, which still will lead to the same prediction, can lead to very different pixels being highlighted as explanations.

Kindermans et al. (2019) also showed that these pixel attribution methods can be highly unreliable. They added a constant shift to the input data, meaning they added the same pixel changes to all images. They compared two networks, the original network and the “shifted” network where the bias of the first layer changed to adapt for the constant pixel shift. Both networks produce the same predictions. Furthermore, the gradient is same for both. But the explanations changed, which is an undesirable property. They looked at DeepLift, Vanilla Gradient and Integrated Gradients.

The paper “Sanity checks for saliency maps” investigated whether saliency methods are insensitive to model and data. Insensitivity is highly undesirable, because it would mean the “explanation” is unrelated to model and data. Methods that are insensitive to training and data are similar to edge detectors. Edge detectors highlight strong pixel color changes in images that are unrelated to a prediction model or abstract features of the image, and require no training. The methods tested were Vanilla Gradient, Gradient Input, Integrated Graphics, Guided Backpropagation, Grad-CAM and SmoothGrad(with Vanilla Gradient). Vanilla Gradient and Grad-CAM passed the sensitivity check, while Guided Backpropagation and Guided Grad-CAM failed. However, the sanity checks itself has found some criticism from Tomsett et al. (2020) with a paper called “Sanity checks for saliency metrics” (of course). They found that there is a lack of consistency for evaluation metrics (I know, it is getting pretty meta now). So we are back to we started ... It remains difficult to evaluate visual explanations. This makes it very difficult for a practitioner.

All in all, this is a very unsatisfactory state of affairs. We have to wait a bit for more research on this topic. And please, no more invention of new saliency methods, but more scrutinizing of how to evaluate them.

## 9.3 DETECTING CONCEPTS

Author: Fangzhou Li @ University of California, Davis

So far, we have encountered many methods to explain black box models through feature attribution. However, there are some limitations regarding the feature-based approach. First, features are not necessarily user-friendly in terms of interpretability. For example, the importance of a single pixel in an image does not convey meaningful interpretation. Second, the expressiveness of a feature-based explanation is constrained by the number of features.

The concept-based approach addresses both limitations mentioned. A concept can be any abstraction, such as a color, an object, or an event. Given any user-defined concept, although a neural network might not be explicitly trained with the concept, the concept-based approach detects concept embedded within the latent space learned by the network. In other words, the concept-based approach can generate explanations that are not limited by the feature space of a network.

In this chapter, we will primarily focus on the Testing with Activation Vectors (TCAV) by Kim et al.²⁸

### 9.3.1 TCAV: Testing with Concept Activation Vectors

TCAV is proposed to generate global explanations for neural networks, but in theory, it should work for any model where directional derivative is possible. TCAV measures the effect of that concept's influence on the model's prediction for a certain class. For example, TCAV can answer questions such how the concept of "striped" influences a model classifying an image as a "zebra". Since TCAV describes the relationship between a concept and a class, instead of explaining a single prediction, it provides useful global interpretation for a model's overall behavior.

### 9.3.1 Concept Activation Vector (CAV)

A CAV is simply numerical representation that generalizes a concept in the activation space of a neural layer. A CAV, denoted as $v_C^l$, depends on a concept $C$ and a neural layer $l$, where $l$ is also called a bottleneck of the model. For calculating the CAV of a concept $C$, we need to prepare two datasets: a concept dataset that represents $C$ and a random dataset that contains arbitrary data. For instance, to define the concept of "striped", we can collect images of striped objects as the concept dataset, while the random dataset is a group of random images without strips.
