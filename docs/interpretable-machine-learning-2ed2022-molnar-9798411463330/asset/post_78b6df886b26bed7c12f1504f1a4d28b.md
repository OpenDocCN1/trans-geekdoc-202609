
## 9.1 LEARNED FEATURES

Next, we create the masks of the top activated areas per channel and per image. At this point the concept labels are not yet involved.

- For each convolutional mask:
    - For each image x in the Broden dataset:
        - Forward propagate image x to the target layer containing channel k.
        - Extract the pixel activations of convolutional channel: $A_k(x)$.
        - Calculate distribution of pixel activations $\alpha_k$ over all images.
        - Determine the 0.995-quantile value $T_k$ of activations $\alpha_k$. This means 0.5% of all activations of channel k for image x are greater than $T_k$.
    - For each image x in the Broden dataset:
        - Scale the (possibly) lower-resolution activation map $A_k(x)$ to the resolution of image x. We call the result $S_k(x)$.
        - Binarize the activation map: A pixel is either on or off, depending on whether it exceeds the activation threshold $T_k$. The new map is $M_k(x) = S_k(x) \geq T_k(x)$.

### Step 3: Activation-concept alignment

After step 2 we have activation mask per channel and per image. These activation masks mark highly activated areas. For each channel we want to find the human concept that activates that. We find the concept by comparing the activation maps with all labeled concepts. We quantify the alignment between activation mask k and concept c with the Intersection over Union (IoU) score:

> $IoU_{k,c} = \frac{\sum |M_k(x) \cap L_c(x)|}{\sum |M_k(x) \cup L_c(x)|}$

$|\cdot|$ is the cardinality of a set. Intersection over union compares the alignment between two areas. $IoU_{k,c}$ can be interpreted as the accuracy with which unit k detects concept c. We call unit k a detector of concept c when $IoU_{k,c} > 0.04$. This threshold was chosen by Bau & Zhou (2017).

### 9.1.2 Experiments

The Network Dissection authors trained different network architectures (AlexNet, VGG, GoogleNet, ResNet) from scratch on different datasets (ImageNet, Places205, Places365). ImageNet contains 1.6 million images from 1000 classes that focus on objects. Places205 and Places365 contain 2.4 million / 1.6 million images from 205 / 365 different scenes. The authors additionally trained AlexNet on self-supervised training tasks such as predicting video frame order or colorizing images. For many of these settings, they counted the number of unique concept detectors as a measure of interpretability. Here are some of the findings:

- The networks detect lower-level concepts (colors, textures) at lower layers and higher-level concepts (parts, objects) at higher layers. We have already seen this in the Feature Visualizations.
- Batch normalization reduces the number of unique concept detectors.
- Many units detect the same concept. For example, there are 95 (!) channels in VGG trained on ImageNet when $IoU \geq 0.04$ on detection cutoff (4 in conv4_3, 91 in conv5_3, see project website⁸).
- Increasing the number of channels in a layer increases the number of interpretable units.
- Random initializations (training with different random seeds) result in slightly different numbers of interpretable units.
- ResNet is the network architecture with the largest number of unique detectors, followed by VGG, GoogleNet and AlexNet last.

> http://netdissect.csail.mit.edu/dissect/vgg16_imagenet/

- The largest number of unique concept detectors is learned for Places356, followed by Places205 and ImageNet last.
- The number of unique concept detectors increases with the number of training iterations.
- Networks trained on self-supervised tasks have fewer unique detectors compared to networks trained on self-supervised tasks.
- In transfer learning, the concept of a channel can change. For example, a dog detector became a waterfall detector. This happened in a model that was initially trained to classify objects and fine-tuned to classify scenes.
- In one of the experiments, the authors projected the channels onto a rotated basis. This was done for the VGG network—"Rotated" does not mean the image was rotated. "Rotated" means we take the 256 channels from the conv5 layer and compute 256 new channels as linear combinations.
- The first conclusion: Interpretability is axis-dependent.
- The second conclusion: Interpretability is independent of performance.
- The authors also used Dissection for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).

### 9.1.3 Advantages

- Feature visualizations give insight into how neural networks work.
- Network dissection allows us to communicate concepts in a non-technical way.

### 9.1.4 Disadvantages

- Many feature visualizations are not interpretable at all.
- There are too many units to look at.
- Illusion of interpretability? Feature visualizations can convey the illusion that we understand what the neural network is doing.
