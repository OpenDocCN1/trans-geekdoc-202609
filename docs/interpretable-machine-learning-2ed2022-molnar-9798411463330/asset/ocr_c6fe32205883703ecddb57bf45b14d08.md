**Figure 7.9:** Two features and the predicted outcome. The model predicts the sum of the two features (shaded background), with the exception that if x1 is greater than 0.7 and x2 less than 0.3, the model always predicts 2. This area is far from the distribution of data (point cloud) and does not affect the performance of the model and also should not affect its interpretation.

But is it not interesting to see that our model behaves oddly at x1 > 0.7 and x2 < 0.3? Well, yes and no. Since these are data instances that might be physically odd or at least extremely unlikely, if you suspect your test distribution might be slightly different and some instances are actually in that range, it would be interesting to look into this area in the calculation of feature effects. It has to be a conscious decision to include areas where we have not observed data yet and it should not be a side-effect of the method choice like PDP. If you suspect that the model will later be used with differently distributed data, I recommend to use ALE plots and simulate the distribution of data you are expecting.
