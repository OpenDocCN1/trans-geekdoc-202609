The participants described the actions of the shapes as they would describe the actions of a human agent, assigning intentions and even emotions and personality traits to the shapes. Robots are a bit like my vacuum, which I named "Doge". If Doge wants keep cleaning, but asks me for help because it got stuck. Later, Doge finishes cleaning and searches the home base to recharge, I think: "Doge has a desire to recharge and intends to find the home base." I also attribute personality traits: "Doge is a bit dumb, but in a cute way." These are my thoughts, especially when I find out Doge has knocked over a plant while vacuuming the house. A machine or algorithm that explains its predictions will find more acceptance. See also the chapter on explanations, which argues that explanations are social processes.

Explanations are used to manage social interactions. By creating a shared meaning of something, the explainer influences the actions, emotions and beliefs of the recipient of the explanation. For a machine to interact with us, it may need to shape our emotions and beliefs. Machines have to "persuade" us, so that they can accept their intended goal. I vacuum cleaner creates a shared meaning of, "accident" (like getting stuck in the bathroom carpet ... ) by explaining that it got stuck instead of simply stopping to work without comment. Interestingly, there may be a misalignment between the explaining (create trust) and the goal of the recipient (understand the prediction or behavior). Perhaps the explanation for why Doge stuck could be that the battery was very low, that one of the wheels is not working properly and there is a bug that makes the robot go to the same spot over and over again even though there was an obstacle. These reasons (and a few more) caused the robot to get stuck, but it only explained that something was in the way, and what was enough for me to trust its behavior and get a shared meaning of that accident. By the way, Doge got stuck in the bathroom again. We remove the carpets each time we let Doge vacuum.

Machine learning models, such as movie recommendations, the ability to interpret is debugged and augmented when they can be interpreted. In low risk environments, is valuable in the research and development phase as well as deployment. Later, when a model is used in products, things can go wrong. An interpretation for erroneous predictions helps to understand the cause of the error. It delivers a direction for how to fix the system. Consider an example of a husky classifier that classifies husk as wolves. Using interpretable machine learning methods, you would find that the misclassification was due to the snow on the image. The classifier learned to see snow as a feature for classifying images as "wolf", which might make sense in terms of separating wolves from huskies in the training dataset, but not in real-world use.
