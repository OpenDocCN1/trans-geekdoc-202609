### 9.5.3 Advantages of Identifying Influential Instances

The approaches of deletion diagnostics and influence functions are very different from the most feature-perturbation based approaches presented in the Model-Agnostic chapter. A look at influential instances emphasizes the role of data training in the learning process. This makes influence functions and deletion diagnostics one of the best debugging tools for machine learning models. Of the techniques presented in this book, they are the only ones that directly help to identify the instances which should be checked for errors.

Deletion diagnostics are model-agnostic, meaning the approach can be applied to any model. Also influence functions based on the derivatives can be applied to a broad class of models.

We can use these to compare different machine learning models and better understand their behavior, going beyond comparing only the predictive performance.

We have not talked about this topic in this chapter, influence functions via derivatives can be used to create adversarial training data. These are instances that can be manipulated in such a way that the model cannot predict certain instances when the model is trained on those manipulated instances. The difference to the methods in the Adversarial Examples chapter is that the attack takes place during training time, also known as poisoning attacks. If you are interested, read by Koh and Liang (2017).

For deletion diagnostics and influence functions, we considered the difference in the prediction and for the influence function increase of the loss. But, the approach is generalizable to any question of the form: “What to when we delete or update instance z?”, where you can find “...” with any function of your desire. You can analyze how much a training instance influences the overall loss of the model. You can analyze how much a training instance influences the feature importance. You can analyze how much a training instance influences which feature is selected for the first split when training a decision tree.

### 9.5.4 Disadvantages of Identifying Influential Instances

Deletion diagnostics are very expensive to calculate because they require retraining. History has shown that computer resources are constantly increasing. A calculation that 20 years ago was unthinkable in terms of resources can be easily performed with your smartphone. You can train models with thousands of instances and hundreds of parameters on a laptop in seconds/minutes. It is therefore not big leap to assume that deletion diagnostics will work without problems with large neural networks in 10 years.

Influence functions are a good alternative to deletion diagnostics, but only for models with a 2nd order differentiable loss with respect to its parameters, they do not work for tree-based methods like random forests, boosted trees or decision trees. Even if you have models with parameters and a loss function, the loss may not be differentiable. But for the last problem, there is a trick: use a differentiable loss for calculating the influence when, for example, the underlying model uses the Hinge loss instead of differentiable loss. The loss is replaced by a smoothed version of the problematic loss for the influence functions, the model can still be trained with the non-smooth loss.

Influence functions are only approximate, because the approach forms a quadratic around the parameters. The approximation can be wrong and the influence of an instance is actually higher or lower when removed. Koh and Liang (2017) showed some examples that the influence calculated by the influence function was close to the influence measure obtained when the model was actually retrained after the instance was deleted. ## 10 A Look into the Crystal Ball

What is the future of interpretable learning? This chapter is a speculative mental exercise and a subjective guess how interpretable learning will develop. I opened the book with rather pessimistic stories and would like to conclude with a more optimistic outlook.

I have based my “predictions” on three premises:

- Digitization: Any (interesting) information will be digitized. Think of electronic cash and online transactions. Think of e-books, music and videos. Think of all the sensory data about our environment, human behavior, industrial production processes and so on. The drivers of the digitization of everything are: Cheap computers/sensors/storage, scaling effects (winner takes all), new business models, modular value chains, cost pressure and more.
- Automation: When a task can be automated and the cost of automation is lower than the cost of performing the task over time, the task will be automated. For example before the introduction of the computer we had the steam machine automation. For example, the weaving machine automation or the steam machine horsepower. Computers and digitization take automation to the next level. Simply the fact that you can program for tasks, write Excel macros, automate e-mail responses, and so on, show how an individual can automate. Ticket machines automate the purchase of train tickets (no cashier needed any), washing machines automate laundry; standing orders automate payments and so on. Automating tasks frees up time and money, so there is a huge economic and personal incentive to automate things. We are currently observing the automation of language translation, driving, and to a small degree, even scientific discovery.
- Misspecification: We are not able to perfectly specify a goal with all its constraints.
