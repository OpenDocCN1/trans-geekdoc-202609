# 8.4.1 Finding Anchors

Although anchors' mathematical description may seem clear and straightforward, constructing particular rules is infeasible. It would require evaluating $all z \in \mathcal{D}_{f(x)=z)}(
eg A)$ which is not possible in continuous or large input spaces. Therefore, the authors propose to introduce the parameter $0 \le \delta \le 1$ to create a probabilistic definition. The samples are drawn until there is a statistical confidence interval concerning their precision.

> $P(prec(A) \ge \tau) \ge 1 - \delta \quad \text{with} \quad prec(A) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_x(A)}[1_{\{f(x)=f\}}]$

The previous two definitions are combined and extended by the notion of coverage. Its rationale consists finding rules that apply to a preferably large part of the model's input space. Coverage is formally defined as an anchor's probability of applying to its neighbors, i.e. its perturbation space:

> $cov(A) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_x(z)}[A(z)]$

Including this element leads to anchor's final definition taking into account the maximization of coverage:

```
$\max \quad cov(A) \n\text{s.t.} \ P(prec(A) \ge \tau) \ge 1 - \delta$
```
