In contrast, let the three layering directions be mutually non-orthogonal and mutually non-collinear, i.e.,

$0 < |\boldsymbol{n}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_2| < 1, \quad 0 < |\boldsymbol{n}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_3| < 1, \quad 0 < |\boldsymbol{n}_2 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_3| < 1. \quad \text{(A.23)}$

We consider an element \(\boldsymbol{B}\) in the intersection \(\mathcal{E}_1^\alpha \cap \mathcal{E}_2^\alpha \cap \mathcal{E}_3^\alpha\) and wish to show that the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) vanishes, such that the non-singularity condition (A.11) is satisfied. Because the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) is symmetric, its eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal. Thus, if the eigenvalues were distinct, the layering directions would need to be orthogonal. However, this contradicts our assumption (A.23). Therefore, the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) has a single eigenvalue \(\alpha\) with the corresponding eigenspace \(\mathbb{R}^3\), i.e., the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) attains the form

\(\boldsymbol{B} = \alpha \boldsymbol{I}, \quad \alpha \in \mathbb{R}. \quad \text{(A.24)}\)

Additionally, because the trace of the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) vanishes, i.e., \(\text{tr}(\boldsymbol{B}) = 0\), it follows that the eigenvalue \(\alpha\) is zero. Consequently, the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) must vanish, the non-singularity condition (A.11) is satisfied, and the effective properties \(\bar{\mathbb{A}}\) of a rank-3 CLM with an incompressible coating material \(m_2\) and a rigid core material \(m_1\) are non-singular, if the three layering directions are mutually non-orthogonal and non-collinear.
