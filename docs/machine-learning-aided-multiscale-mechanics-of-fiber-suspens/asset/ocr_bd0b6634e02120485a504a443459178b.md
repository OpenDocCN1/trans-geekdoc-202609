Using the definition (A.1), equation (A.2) may be equivalently rewritten in the form

\(\left( \sum_{r=1}^R c_r \Gamma_{\mathbb{A}_2}(\boldsymbol{n}_r)\mathbb{A}_2 \right) [\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}] = \bar{\boldsymbol{g}}.\) (A.3)

Equation (A.3) is satisfied precisely if \(\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}\) lies in the intersection of all subspaces \(\mathcal{E}_r^A\), such that

\(\bar{\boldsymbol{g}} \in \bigcap_{r=1}^R \mathcal{E}_r^A,\) (A.4)

or, equivalently,

\(\Gamma_{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{n}_r)[\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}] = \bar{\boldsymbol{g}}, \quad \forall r \in \{1, ..., R\}.\) (A.5)

We prove this as follows. First, if \(\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}\) lies in the section of all subspaces such that (A.4) is satisfied, it follows from equation (A.5) and the definition of the operator \(\Gamma_{\mathbb{A}_2}(\boldsymbol{n}_r)\) (5.43) that

\(\left( \sum_{r=1}^R c_r \Gamma_{\mathbb{A}_2}(\boldsymbol{n}_r) \mathbb{A}_2 \right) [\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}] = \left( \sum_{r=1}^R c_r \Gamma_{\mathbb{A}_2}(\boldsymbol{n}_r) \mathbb{A}_2 \Gamma_{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{n}_r)[\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}] \right)\)
\(= \sum_{r=1}^R c_r \bar{\boldsymbol{g}} = \bar{\boldsymbol{g}},\) (A.6)

and hence equation (A.3) is satisfied. Second we wish to prove that the converse is also true, i.e., if equation (A.3) is satisfied, equation (A.4) is satisfied as well. Let \(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{A}_2}\) denote a convex combination of the projectors \(\Gamma_{\mathbb{A}_2}(\boldsymbol{n}_r)\mathbb{A}_2\), i.e.,

\(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{A}_2} = \left( \sum_{r=1}^R c_r \Gamma_{\mathbb{A}_2}(\boldsymbol{n}_r) \mathbb{A}_2 \right),\) (A.7)

with

\(c_r > 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{r=1}^R c_r = 1.\) (A.8)
