\( e_{\text{VE}} = \frac{\|\bar{\tau} - \bar{\tau}_{\text{ref}}\|_2}{\|\bar{\tau}_{\text{ref}}\|_2} \quad (3.53) \)

For each individual load case, the effective stress \(\bar{\tau}\) of the ten largest microstructures with length \(L = 3.2\ell\) and width \(W = 20d\) was averaged and chosen as the reference stress \(\bar{\tau}_{\text{ref}}\). Thus, for each volume element size, the effective stress \(\bar{\tau}\) of each of the ten realizations is compared to the average reference stress \(\bar{\tau}_{\text{ref}}\). This allows us to calculate the means and standard deviations of the relative error \(e_{\text{VE}}\) for each combination of length \(L\) and width \(W\), which are collected in Table 3.1.

First, we note that the mean and standard deviation of the relative error \(e_{\text{VE}}\) decrease faster with the width \(W\) than with the length \(L\). The largest decrease in the mean and standard deviation of \(e_{\text{VE}}\) occurred when the width \(W\) was increased from \(W=5\) to \(W=10\). Thus, an appropriate choice of the volume width is essential to obtain robust results. Overall, we observe that the maximum value of the error \(e_{\text{VE}}\) falls below the engineering bound of 5% for the sizes \(L = 2.2\ell, W = 20d\) and \(L = 3.2\ell, W = 20d\), where the maximum of the error \(e_{\text{VE}}\) is 4.06% and 3.05%, respectively.

With mean errors of 1.75% and 1.14%, and error standard deviations of 0.82% and 0.51%, both sizes \(L = 2.2\ell, W = 20d\) and \(L = 3.2\ell, W = 20d\) show similar error characteristics. Consequently, we consider the size of the volume element \(L = 2.2\ell, W = 20d\) as sufficient, which aligns with the results for aligned fibers in the Newtonian case of Bertóti et al. (2021). Accordingly, we choose \(L = 2.2\ell, W = 20d\) for all further investigations.
