Then, we compute the layering directions \( n_K^{3(i-1)+1}, n_K^{3(i-1)+2}, n_K^{3(i-1)+3} \) of the rank-three CLM \( \mathcal{C}_3^i \) via

```
\( n_K^{3(i-1)+1} = q_1^i, \tag{5.70} \)

\( n_K^{3(i-1)+2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( q_1^i + q_2^i \right), \tag{5.71} \)

\( n_K^{3(i-1)+3} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( q_1^i + q_3^i \right), \tag{5.72} \)
```

where \( q_1^i, q_2^i, \) and \( q_3^i \) are the first, second, and third column vectors of the tensor \( \boldsymbol{Q}^i \) in the standard basis \( \boldsymbol{e}_i \), respectively. This leads to a total of \( N_\alpha \) angles with

```
\( N_\alpha = 5(2^{K-1}) - 2. \tag{5.73} \)
```

The angles between the layering directions are fixed for each CLM \( \mathcal{C}_3 \), but the three layering directions are rotated together during training. Other procedures than the one presented here are viable as well, as long as the constructed vectors \( n_K^{3(i-1)+1}, n_K^{3(i-1)+2}, \text{and } n_K^{3(i-1)+3} \) are mutually non-collinear and non-orthogonal. Alternatively, one could parametrize each of the three layering directions \( n_K^{3(i-1)+1}, n_K^{3(i-1)+2}, \text{and } n_K^{3(i-1)+3} \) with two angles each, and identify the angles independently. Then, the effective properties of the CLM \( \mathcal{C}_3^i \) are non-singular almost surely, because the probability of any two of the three layering directions being collinear or one vector being orthogonal to the other two is zero. However, parametrizing each of the three layering directions with two angles requires twice the amount of angles in the CLM layer of the FDMN as compared to the presented procedure.

To define the linear homogenization function of the FDMN, we collect all \( N_\alpha \) angles of the layering directions in the vector \( \underline{\alpha} \)

```
\( \underline{\alpha} = \left( c_K^1, d_K^1, e_K^1, \dots, c_K^{3(2^{K-1})}, d_K^{3(2^{K-1})}, e_K^{3(2^{K-1})}, \right. \)

\( \left. a_{K-1}^1, b_{K-1}^1, \dots, a_{K-1}^{2^{K-2}}, b_{K-1}^{2^{K-2}}, \dots, a_1^1, b_1^1 \right), \tag{5.74} \)
```
