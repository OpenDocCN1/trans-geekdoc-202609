Note that the colormap limits in Figures 3.7 and 3.8 are chosen to highlight the respective extrema of the elongational viscosity \(\eta_\text{app}\), and that differences in magnitude are visualized by the size of the viscosity bodies. In Figures 3.7 and 3.8, we observe that for both the Newtonian and Cross-type case, the elongational viscosity \(\eta_\text{app}\) is large when the direction \(\boldsymbol{d}\) is aligned with the principal fiber orientation axis. This is expected, since flow resistance along the cylindrical fibers is higher than in other flow directions. Furthermore, the elongational viscosity \(\eta_\text{app}\) for both cases increases with growing fiber volume fraction \(c_\text{F}\) in all directions \(\boldsymbol{d}\). However, in the Cross-type case, the elongational viscosity \(\eta_\text{app}\) decreases with the shear rate \(\dot{\gamma}\) in all directions \(\boldsymbol{d}\). Depending on the value of the shear rate \(\dot{\gamma}\), one effect outweighs the other. Note that the minimum value of the elongational viscosity \(\eta_\text{app}\) does not occur perpendicular to the principal fiber orientation axis \(x\) in both cases, because of incompressibility.
