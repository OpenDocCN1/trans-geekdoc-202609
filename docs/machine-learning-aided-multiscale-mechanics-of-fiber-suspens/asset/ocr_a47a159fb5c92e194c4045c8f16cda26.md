Consequently, any second order fiber orientation tensor \(\boldsymbol{N}\) may be encoded by a vector \(\underline{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \lambda_2)^{\text{T}}\), and represents a point in the fiber orientation triangle \(S_{\text{T}}\) defined through equations (2.33) and (2.34), such that

\(S_{\text{T}} = \left\{ \underline{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \lambda_2)^{\text{T}} \mid 1 \ge \lambda_1 \ge \frac{1}{3} \text{ and } \min (\lambda_1, 1 - \lambda_1) \ge \lambda_2 \ge \frac{1 - \lambda_1}{2} \right\}.\) (2.37)

For an in-depth discussion on the description of fiber orientation states, we refer the reader to Kanatani (1984), Bauer and Böhlke (2022), and Bauer et al. (2023).
