where \(\mathbb{I} \in (\mathbb{R}^3)^{\otimes 4}\) denotes the fourth order identity tensor. The operator \(\mathbb{I}_{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{n})\) encodes restrictions imposed by the periodicity of the laminate, the perfect bonding of the phases, the momentum balance (5.11), and the constitutive equation (5.19). Depending on whether we wish to compute the effective primal or the effective dual properties of the laminate, a different operator \(\mathbb{I}_{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{n})\) is required. For the homogenization of the primal effective properties, i.e., \(\bar{\mathbb{A}} = \bar{\mathbb{M}}\), the operator \(\mathbb{I}_{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{n})\) equals the operator \(\mathbb{I}_1^{\text{co}}(\boldsymbol{n})\). For the homogenization of the dual effective properties, i.e., \(\bar{\mathbb{A}} = \bar{\mathbb{K}}\), the operator \(\mathbb{I}_{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{n})\) equals the operator \(\mathbb{I}_2^{\text{co}}(\boldsymbol{n})\). The operators \(\mathbb{I}_1^{\text{co}}\) and \(\mathbb{I}_2^{\text{co}}\), are defined through their action on a second order tensor \(\boldsymbol{X} \in \text{Sym}(3)\)

\(\mathbb{I}_1^{\text{co}}(\boldsymbol{n})[\boldsymbol{X}] = 2(\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{n}) \otimes_s \boldsymbol{n} - (\boldsymbol{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{n})\boldsymbol{n} \otimes \boldsymbol{n} \quad \text{(5.28)}\)

and

\(\mathbb{I}_2^{\text{co}}(\boldsymbol{n})[\boldsymbol{X}] = \boldsymbol{X} - \mathbb{I}_1^{\text{co}}(\boldsymbol{n})\boldsymbol{X}, \quad \text{(5.29)}\)

where \(\otimes_s\) denotes the symmetrized dyadic product. The operators \(\mathbb{I}_1^{\text{co}}\) and \(\mathbb{I}_2^{\text{co}}\) project onto the subspaces

\(\mathcal{E}^\text{co} = \{ \text{Sym}(3) \ni \boldsymbol{X} = \boldsymbol{d} \otimes \boldsymbol{n} + \boldsymbol{n} \otimes \boldsymbol{d} \mid \boldsymbol{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \} \quad \text{(5.30)}\)

and

\(\mathcal{S}^\text{co} = \{ \text{Sym}(3) \ni \boldsymbol{X} \mid \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{n} = \boldsymbol{0} \}, \quad \text{(5.31)}\)

and arise from the fact that the jumps of the field \([[\nabla^s w]] \in \mathcal{E}^\text{co}\)

\([[\nabla^s w]] = [[\nabla w \boldsymbol{n}]] \otimes_s \boldsymbol{n}, \quad \text{(5.32)}\)

and the jump of the stress \([[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]] \in \mathcal{S}^\text{co}\) live on mutually orthogonal subspaces (Milton, 2002, §9), because it holds that

\([[\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}]] = \boldsymbol{0}. \quad \text{(5.33)}\)
