```
\(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \int_{\Omega_t} \psi \, \mathrm{d}\Omega_t(\boldsymbol{x}) = \int_{\Omega_t} p_\psi + s_\psi \, \mathrm{d}\Omega_t(\boldsymbol{x}) + \int_{\partial\Omega_t} \boldsymbol{q}_\psi \odot \boldsymbol{n} \, \mathrm{d}A_t(\boldsymbol{x}), \quad (2.15)\)
```

where \(\odot\) denotes a single order tensor contraction. The three tensor fields \(p_\psi : \Omega_t \times [0, T] \to (\mathbb{R}^d)^{\otimes n}\), \(s_\psi : \Omega_t \times [0, T] \to (\mathbb{R}^d)^{\otimes n}\), and \(\boldsymbol{q} : \Omega_t \times [0, T] \to (\mathbb{R}^d)^{\otimes (n+1)}\) represent the internal production field, the external supply field, and the non-convective flux field of the quantity \(\psi\), respectively. Using the Reynolds transport theorem and the divergence theorem (Bertram, 2012), the local formulation of the general balance equation (2.15) in regular points (Liu, 2002)

```
\(\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} + \text{div}(\psi \otimes \boldsymbol{v}) = p_\psi + s_\psi + \text{div}(\boldsymbol{q}_\psi), \quad (2.16)\)
```

follows from equation (2.15). Here, \(\boldsymbol{v} : \Omega_t \times [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^d\) denotes the velocity field. If the volume \(\Omega_t\) contains a material singular surface \(\mathcal{S}_t\), the jump condition for points \(\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{S}_t\) on the material singular surface \(\mathcal{S}_t\) (Morro and Giorgi, 2023)

```
\([[\boldsymbol{q}_\psi]] \cdot \boldsymbol{n} = \boldsymbol{0} \quad (2.17)\)
```

holds. Here, the jump \([[q]]\) of a quantity \(q\) across a material singular surface \(\mathcal{S}_t\) with normal vector \(\boldsymbol{n}\) is

```
\([[q]] = q^+ - q^-, \quad (2.18)\)
```

where we denote the left- and right hand side limits of the quantity \(q\) with \(q^+\) and \(q^-\), respectively.
