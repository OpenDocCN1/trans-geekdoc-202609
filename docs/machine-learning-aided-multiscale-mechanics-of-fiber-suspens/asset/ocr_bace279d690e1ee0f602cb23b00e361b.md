In contrast, the elongational viscosity \(\eta^x_{app}\) decreases from 759 Pa s to 43 Pa s as the shear rate \(\dot{\gamma}\) changes from \(10 s^{-1}\) to \(10^5 s^{-1}\). Hence, both elongational viscosities \(\eta^y_{app}\) and \(\eta^x_{app}\) decrease by the same factor of around 94%. However, for a lower shear rate \(\dot{\gamma} = 10^3\) Pa s, the elongational viscosities \(\eta^y_{app}\) and \(\eta^x_{app}\) decrease differently by the factors 57% and 62%, respectively. We observe a similar change for the higher fiber volume fraction \(c_F = 25\%\). In this case, the elongational viscosities \(\eta^y_{app}\) and \(\eta^x_{app}\) also decrease by around 94% as the shear rate \(\dot{\gamma}\) changes from \(10 s^{-1}\) to \(10^5 s^{-1}\). Again, for a lower shear rate \(\dot{\gamma} = 10^3\) Pa s, the elongational viscosities \(\eta^y_{app}\) and \(\eta^x_{app}\) decrease differently by the factors 75% and 78%, respectively. As a result, we conclude that for shear rates \(\dot{\gamma}\) where the matrix behavior is non-Newtonian the elongational viscosities \(\eta^x_{app}\) and \(\eta^y_{app}\) vary stronger with the shear rate for higher fiber volume fraction \(c_F\). Also, since the elongational viscosities \(\eta^y_{app}\) and \(\eta^x_{app}\) change by different factors in both the Newtonian and Cross-type case for a shear rate \(\dot{\gamma} = 10^3 s^{-1}\), the material anisotropy changes with varying fiber volume fraction \(c_F\) and shear rate \(\dot{\gamma}\), see Figure 3.11.
