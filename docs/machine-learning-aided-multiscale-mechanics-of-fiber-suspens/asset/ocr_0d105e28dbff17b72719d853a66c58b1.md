+   - The mapping of a first order tensor by a second order tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{a} = B_{ij}a_j \boldsymbol{e}_i\).
+   - The mapping of a second order tensor by a fourth order tensor \(\mathbb{C}[\boldsymbol{B}] = C_{ijkl}B_{kl} \boldsymbol{e}_i \otimes \boldsymbol{e}_j\).
+   - The composition of two second order tensors \(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{B} = A_{ij}B_{jk} \boldsymbol{e}_i \otimes \boldsymbol{e}_k\)
+   - The composition of two fourth tensors \(\mathbb{A}\mathbb{C} = A_{ijkl}C_{klmn} \boldsymbol{e}_i \otimes \boldsymbol{e}_j \otimes \boldsymbol{e}_m \otimes \boldsymbol{e}_n\).

Furthermore, we denote the transposition of a quantity with \((\cdot)^\mathsf{T}\), the tensor product with \(\otimes\), and the direct product with \(\times\). The operator \((\cdot)^{\otimes a}\) constructs a tensor space of \(a\)-th order, e.g., \(\mathbb{R}^{(\otimes 5)}\), and we define \((\mathbb{R}^d)^{\otimes 0} \equiv \mathbb{R}\) for all dimensions \(d \in \mathbb{N}_{>0}\). Additionally, we define the tensor product of a scalar \(a\) with a first order tensor \(\boldsymbol{v}\), such that \(a \otimes \boldsymbol{v} \equiv av_i \boldsymbol{e}_i\). Finally, we denote vectors and matrices via under-scores, e.g., \(\underline{a}\) and \(\underline{\underline{A}}\), respectively.
