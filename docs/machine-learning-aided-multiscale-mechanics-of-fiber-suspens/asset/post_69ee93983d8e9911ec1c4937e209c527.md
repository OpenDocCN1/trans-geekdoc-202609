
Then, fixed points of the operator \(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{A}_2}\) lie in the set (Reich, 1983, Lemma 1.4)

\(\mathcal{E} = \bigcap_{r=1}^R \mathcal{E}_r^{\mathsf{A}}.\) (A.9)

Therefore, any eigenvector of the operator \(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{A}_2}\) corresponding to the eigenvalue one must lie in the intersection of the subspaces \(\mathcal{E}_r^{\mathsf{A}}\), which we denote with \(\mathcal{E}\). In particular, if equation (A.3) is satisfied, the element \(\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}\) lies in \(\mathcal{E}\), i.e.,

\(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{A}_2}[\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}] = \bar{\boldsymbol{g}}, \implies \bar{\boldsymbol{g}} \in \mathcal{E}\) (A.10)

and equation (A.4) is satisfied as well.

With the result (A.10) at hand, we observe that a necessary and sufficient condition for the regularity of a rank-R CLM is that the space \(\mathcal{E}\) is trivial, i.e.,

\(\mathcal{E} = \{ \mathbf{0} \}.\) (A.11)

We refer to equation (A.11) as *non-singularity condition* in the following. In the context of linear elastic and linearly viscous materials, we derived the non-singularity condition (A.11) for the spaces \(\mathcal{E}_r^{\mathsf{A}}\), such that

\(\mathcal{E}_r^{\mathsf{A}} \subset \text{Sym}(3), \forall r \in \{1, ..., R\} \text{ or } \mathcal{E}_r^{\mathsf{A}} \subset \text{Sym}_0(3), \forall r \in \{1, ..., R\}.\) (A.12)

However, the presented proof extends to other problems that can be formulated in the form of equation (A.1), such as thermoelastic or piezoelectric problems (Milton, 2002, §9). With the goal of using CLMs as building blocks in a DMN architecture, we are interested in whether there are particular choices of the layering directions \(\boldsymbol{n}_r\) and the rank \(R\) of the CLM for which the effective properties of a CLM are always non-singular, i.e., the non-singularity condition (A.11) is satisfied. For incompressible suspensions of rigid fibers, we study this question for CLMs with a rigid core material \(m_1\) and an incompressible coating material \(m_2\).

## A.2 Coated layered materials with incompressible coating and rigid core

For applications involving rigid inclusions in incompressible media, we consider CLMs with an incompressible coating material \(m_2\) and a rigid core material \(m_1\). Therefore, the spaces \(\mathcal{E}_r^A \subset \text{Sym}_0(3)\) have the form

\(\mathcal{E}_r^A = \{ B \in \text{Sym}_0(3) \mid B \cdot (n_r \otimes_s a) = 0,\)
\(\forall a \in \mathbb{R}^3 \text{ with } a \cdot n_r = 0 \},\) (A.13)

with the dimensions of the spaces \(\mathcal{E}_r^A\) and \(\text{Sym}_0(3)\) given by

\(\text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_r^A) = 3, \text{ and } \text{dim}(\text{Sym}_0(3)) = 5.\) (A.14)

Because the dimension \(\text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_1^A + \mathcal{E}_2^A)\) is bounded by the dimension of the space \(\text{Sym}_0(3)\), such that

\(\text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_1^A + \mathcal{E}_2^A) \le 5,\) (A.15)

it follows from the dimension of the intersection of the two spaces \(\mathcal{E}_1^A\) and \(\mathcal{E}_2^A\)

\(\text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_1^A \cap \mathcal{E}_2^A) = \text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_1^A) + \text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_2^A) - \text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_1^A + \mathcal{E}_2^A),\) (A.16)

that

\(\text{dim}(\mathcal{E}_1^A \cap \mathcal{E}_2^A) \ge 3 + 3 - 5 = 1.\) (A.17)

In other words, the intersection of the spaces \(\mathcal{E}_1^A\) and \(\mathcal{E}_2^A\) is at least one dimensional. Consequently, two layering directions are not sufficient to satisfy the condition (A.11). Besides the required number of layerings, we are also interested in whether there are restrictions on the angles between the layering directions. Suppose that one layering direction is orthogonal to the other two. We discuss the case that the direction \(\boldsymbol{n}_3\) is orthogonal to the normals \(\boldsymbol{n}_1\) and \(\boldsymbol{n}_2\). The other cases work similarly via permuting the indices. Then it holds that

\(\boldsymbol{n}_3 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_1 = 0, \quad \text{and} \quad \boldsymbol{n}_3 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_2 = 0,\) (A.18)

and the intersection \(\mathcal{E}_1^{\text{A}} \cap \mathcal{E}_2^{\text{A}} \cap \mathcal{E}_3^{\text{A}}\) does not only contain the zero element \(\mathbf{0}\). Hence, the condition (A.11) is violated. To show this, we consider the alternative description of the spaces \(\mathcal{E}_r^{\text{A}}\)

\(\mathcal{E}_r^{\text{A}} = \{ \boldsymbol{B} \in \text{Sym}_0(3) \mid \boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{n}_r = \alpha \boldsymbol{n}_r \quad \text{for some} \quad \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \}\) (A.19)

which is equivalent to equation (A.13) and characterizes the space \(\mathcal{E}_r^{\text{A}}\) via all tensors \(\boldsymbol{B} \in \mathcal{E}_r^{\text{A}}\) that have the layering direction \(\boldsymbol{n}_r\) as an eigenvector. Then the tensor

\(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{n}_1 \otimes \boldsymbol{n}_1 + \boldsymbol{m}_2 \otimes \boldsymbol{m}_2 - 2\boldsymbol{n}_3 \otimes \boldsymbol{n}_3,\) (A.20)

where the layering direction \(\boldsymbol{m}_2\) is constructed by orthogonalization, such that

\(\boldsymbol{m}_2 = \boldsymbol{n}_2 - (\boldsymbol{n}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_2)\boldsymbol{n}_1,\) (A.21)

is an element of the intersection \(\mathcal{E}_1^{\text{A}} \cap \mathcal{E}_2^{\text{A}} \cap \mathcal{E}_3^{\text{A}}\). This is true because it holds that

\(\text{tr}(\boldsymbol{B}) = 0, \boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{n}_1 = \boldsymbol{n}_1, \quad \boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{n}_2 = \boldsymbol{n}_2, \quad \boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{n}_3 = \boldsymbol{n}_3,\) (A.22)

and therefore condition (A.11) is violated. In other words, the effective properties \(\bar{\mathbb{A}}\) of a rank-3 CLM with incompressible coating and rigid core, where one layering direction \(\boldsymbol{n}_3\) is orthogonal to the other two layering directions \(\boldsymbol{n}_1\) and \(\boldsymbol{n}_2\), is always singular. Particularly, if all three layering directions are mutually orthogonal, the effective properties \(\bar{\mathbb{A}}\) of a rank-3 CLM are singular.

In contrast, let the three layering directions be mutually non-orthogonal and mutually non-collinear, i.e.,

\(0 < |\boldsymbol{n}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_2| < 1, \quad 0 < |\boldsymbol{n}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_3| < 1, \quad 0 < |\boldsymbol{n}_2 \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_3| < 1.\) (A.23)

We consider an element \(\boldsymbol{B}\) in the intersection \(\mathcal{E}_1^{\text{A}} \cap \mathcal{E}_2^{\text{A}} \cap \mathcal{E}_3^{\text{A}}\) and wish to show that the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) vanishes, such that the non-singularity condition (A.11) is satisfied. Because the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) is symmetric, its eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal. Thus, if the eigenvalues were distinct, the layering directions would need to be orthogonal. However, this contradicts our assumption (A.23). Therefore, the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) has a single eigenvalue \(\alpha\) with the corresponding eigenspace \(\mathbb{R}^3\), i.e., the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) attains the form

\(\boldsymbol{B} = \alpha \boldsymbol{I}, \quad \alpha \in \mathbb{R}.\) (A.24)

Additionally, because the trace of the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) vanishes, i.e., \(\text{tr}(\boldsymbol{B}) = 0\), it follows that the eigenvalue \(\alpha\) is zero. Consequently, the tensor \(\boldsymbol{B}\) must vanish, the non-singularity condition (A.11) is satisfied, and the effective properties \(\bar{\mathbb{A}}\) of a rank-3 CLM with an incompressible coating material \(m_2\) and a rigid core material \(m_1\) are non-singular, if the three layering directions are mutually non-orthogonal and non-collinear.
