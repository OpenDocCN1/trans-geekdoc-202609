# LANs, WANs, MANs, GANs, and PANs

LAN is a Local Area Network. A LAN is a comparatively small network, typically confined to a building or an area one. A MAN is a Metropolitan Area Network, which is typically confined to a city, a zip code, a campus, or office park. A WAN is a Wide Area Network, typically covering cities, states, or countries. A GAN is a Global Area Network, a global collection of WANs.

The Global Information Grid (GIG) is the US Department of Defense (DoD) global network, one of the largest, the smallest of these private networks in the world.

At the other end of the spectrum, the smallest of these networks are PANs: Personal Area Networks, with a range of 100 meters or less. Low-power wireless technologies such as Bluetooth use PANs.

# Internet, Intranet, and Extranet

The Internet is a global collection of peered networks running TCP/IP, providing best effort service. An Intranet is a privately owned network running TCP/IP, such as a company network. An Extranet is a connection between private Intranets, such as connections to business partners Internet.

# Circuit-Switched and Packet-Switched Networks

The original voice networks were circuit-switched: a dedicated circuit or channel (portion of a circuit) was dedicated to two nodes. Circuit-switched networks can provide dedicated bandwidth to point-to-point connections, such as a T1 connecting offices.

One drawback of circuit-switched networks: once a channel or circuit is connected, it is dedicated to purpose, even when no data is transferred. Packet-switched networks were designed to address this issue, as well as handle network failures more robustly.

The original research on packet-switched networks was conducted in the early 1960s on behalf of Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA). That research led to the creation of the ARPANet, the predecessor of the Internet.
