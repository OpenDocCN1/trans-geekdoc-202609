# 5.1 INTRODUCTION

In classical cryptography, primarily two kinds of crypto-systems were in vogue, that is, the Substitution Ciphers and the Transposition Ciphers. In the Transposition Cipher, the plaintext letters are systematically scrambled (reordered) so as to make the plaintext unintelligible. For example, the word “software” may be scrambled to read as “foseawter.” that is, letters are swapped with each other. In the transposition ciphers, the letters in the plaintext are systematically replaced by other letters. In this chapter, we discuss some of the popular classical cipher schemes.
