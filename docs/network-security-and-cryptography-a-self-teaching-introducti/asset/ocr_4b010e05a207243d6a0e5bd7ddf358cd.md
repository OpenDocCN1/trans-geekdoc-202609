Figure 6.2 depicts a general Feistel Cipher Structure for Encryption with $n$ identical rounds of processing. The inputs to the Encryption Algorithm comprise (i) a plaintext block of length $2w$ bits and (ii) a key $K$. The plaintext is divided into two halves $L_0$ and $R_0$ of $w$ bits, each are fed to Round 1 of the encryption algorithm. The data passes through $n$ rounds of processing and produces a cipher-text block of size $2w$ bits, as the size of the input plaintext. The inputs to round $i$ are denoted as $L_{i-1}$ and $R_{i-1}$, which are derived from the output of the previous round, and a sub-key $K_i$, which is derived from the overall key $K$. In general, the sub-keys are different from each other and also different from the overall key $K$.

All the rounds of processing have the same general structure. A substitution is performed on the left half of the data by taking the XOR (Exclusive OR) of the Left Half with the output of function $F$, which has the Right Half Data and Sub-Key $K$ as inputs. The function $F$ has the same structure for each round, and it is parameterized by the sub-key $K_i$, which is different for each round. Following the substitution of the Left Half, a Permutation is performed on the data by switching the two halves. So, each round performs the right-hand input, without any change, into the left-hand output with the output of function $F$ that operates on the right-hand input acted upon by a 48-bit sub-key. So, in each round, only half of the Input gets transformed, and the other half remains unchanged. In fact, one half of the input data is used to transform the other half, and the two halves are swapped.

The Decryption Algorithm is exactly the same as the Encryption Algorithm, except that the sub-keys are applied to the decryption rounds in reverse order, that is, key $K_s$ is applied to round 1, and key $K_i$ is applied to round 16 of decryption.
