Similarly, if $X(t_1)$ and $X(t_2)$ represent two random variables of a random process $X(t)$, then their joint distributions are known as second-order PDF and CDF, which are related as

$F_X(x_1,x_2;t_1,t_2) = P[X(t_1) \le x_1, X(t_2) \le x_2] = \int_{-\infty}^{x_1}\int_{-\infty}^{x_2} f_X(\lambda_1,\lambda_2;t_1,t_2)d\lambda_1d\lambda_2 \quad (2.83)

or

$f_X(x_1,x_2;t_1,t_2) = \frac{\partial F_X(x_1,x_2;t_1,t_2)}{\partial x_1\partial x_2} \quad (2.84)

In general, the joint distributions of $n$ random variables $X(t_1)=X_1, X(t_2)=X_2, \dots, X(t_n)=X_n$ provide the $n$-order PDF and CDF of a random process $X(t)$ and are related as

$F_X(x_1,x_2,...,x_n;t_1,t_2,...,t_n) = P[X(t_1) \le x_1, x_2,...,X(t_n) \le x_n] \n= \int_{-\infty}^{x_1}\dots\int_{-\infty}^{x_n} f_X(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,...,t_1,t_2,...,t_n)d\lambda_1\dotsd\lambda_n \quad (2.85)

or

$f_X(x_1,x_2,...,x_n;t_1,t_2,...,t_n) = \frac{\partial F_X(x_1,x_2,...,x_n;t_1,t_2,...,t_n)}{\partial x_1\partial x_2\dots\partial x_n} \quad (2.86)

A random process $X(t)$ is said to be strictly stationary of order $n$ if its $n$-order PDF and CDF are time-invariant, that is,

$F_X(x_1,x_2,...,x_n;t_1+\tau,t_2+\tau,...,t_n+\tau) = F_X(x_1,x_2,...,x_n;t_1,t_2,...,t_n) \quad (2.87)

That is, the CDF depends only on the relative location of $t_1,t_2,...,t_n$ and not on their direct values.
