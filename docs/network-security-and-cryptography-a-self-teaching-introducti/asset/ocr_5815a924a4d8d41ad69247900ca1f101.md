# 4.12.3 Another Method of Determining the Primitive Roots of a Number $n$

+   Step 1: Determine integer $g(1 < g < \phi(n))$ such that $\{g^1 \mod n, g^2 \mod n, \dots, g^{(e)} \mod n\}$ are all distinct and relatively prime to $n$. The $g$ is one of the primitive roots of $n$.

+   Step 2: Determine positive integers $<\phi(n)$ and relatively prime to $\phi(n)$. Let the set of integers be $\{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k\}$.

+   Step 3: Determine the primitive roots of $n$ by the following algorithm:
For $i=1$ to $k$ do
Primitive roots of $[i] = (g)^i \mod n$

Thus, the primitive roots of $[1], \dots$, primitive roots of $[k]$ represent $k$ primitive roots of $n$.
