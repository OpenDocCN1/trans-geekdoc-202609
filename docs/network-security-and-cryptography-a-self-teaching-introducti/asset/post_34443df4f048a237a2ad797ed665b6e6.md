
$M_k$ will not be relatively prime to any other modulus in previous set, except $m_k$, since $M_k$ is the product of all the moduli except $m_k$.

Therefore $M_k \equiv 0 \pmod{m_i}, (1 \le i \le n)$ and $i \neq k$.

Then, the simultaneous solution to all the congruences will be:

$X = (a_1M_1 + a_2M_2 + a_3M_3 + \dots + a_nM_n) \pmod m = "A"$

Since each modulus $m_k$, the $k^{th}$ term of "A" represents the solution to the $k^{th}$ congruence, and all the other terms are congruent to $0$ $(\pmod{m_k})$.

Therefore the $n$ terms of $A$, combined, represent a simultaneous solution to the congruence $(\pmod m)$.
