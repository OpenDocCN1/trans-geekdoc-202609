+   - The lowest value of m that satisfies $g^m \equiv 1 \mod n$ is called the “Order of $g(mod n)$”.

+   - If the “Order of $g(mod n)$” equals $\phi(n)$, then integer g is called a “Primitive Root of $n$”.

# 4.11.1.1 Significance of Primitive Roots

If $g(1 < g < \phi(n))$ is a primitive of positive integer $n$, then its powers $\{g^2, g^3, \dots, g^{\\phi(n)}\mod n\}$ are all distinct relatively prime to $n$.

Conversely, we can say that a positive integer g is called a primitive root of positive integer $n$, if its powers $\{g, g^2, \dots, g^{\\phi(n)}\mod n\}$ are all distinct and relatively prime to $n$.

If $p$ is a prime number and $g$ is a primitive root of $p$, then the powers $g^2, g^3, \dots, g^{p-1}(\mod p)$ will all be distinct and relatively prime to $p$. The set of these values will be a permutation of $\mathbb{Z}_p^*=\{1, 2, 3, \dots, (p-1)\}$.

# Example 4.32

Let $n=7$. Is $g=2$ a primitive root of $n$?

# Solution

$\phi(n) = n-1 = 6$. Table 4.19 illustrates the calculation of $y = g^k(\mod n)$ for $n=7$ and $g=2$.

TABLE 4.19 Calculation of $y = g^k(\mod n)$

| X | $g^k$ | $y=g^k(\mod n)$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 8 | 1 |
| 4 | 16 | 2 |
| 5 | 32 | 4 |
| 6 = $\phi(n)$ | 64 | 1 |

2 is not a primitive root of 7, since its powers $2, 2^2, 2^3, \dots, \, 2^6$ are not distinct (mod 7).
