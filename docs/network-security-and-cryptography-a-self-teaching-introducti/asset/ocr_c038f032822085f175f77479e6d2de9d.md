$M^{\Phi(n)} = (l) k \pmod n$

$\equiv 1 \pmod n$

Multiplying both sides by $M$

$.. M^{\Phi(n)+1} = M \pmod n = M$

# Case 2:

$GCD(M, n) \neq 1$, that is, $M$ is not relatively prime to $n$.

Under this condition there are two possibilities:

+   1. $M$ not relatively prime to $p$. Since $M$ cannot have both $p$ and $q$ as its factors, $M$ will be relatively prime to $q$, that is, $GCD(M, q) = 1$

+   2. $M$ not relatively prime to $q$. Therefore, $M$ will be relatively prime to $q$, that is, $GCD(M, q) = 1$

# Case 2(a):

Since $M$ is relatively prime to $q$

$.. M^{\Phi(q)} \equiv 1 \pmod q$ (by Fermat's Theorem)

Raising both sides of the above congruence to power $k \Phi$ $(q)$

$[M^{\Phi(q)}]^{\Phi(p)} = (1)^{\Phi(p)} \pmod q$ (for some integer $k$)

$M^{\Phi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod q$

For some integer $j$, we will have:

$M^{\Phi(n)} = jq + 1$

Multiplying both sides of the above congruence by $M = cp$

$M^{\Phi(n)+1} = jcp + cp = jcn + M$

$.. M^{\Phi(n)+1} = M \pmod n = M$

# Case 2(b):

$q$ divides $M$, and $GCD(M, p) = 1$

Proof is similar to Case 2(a).
