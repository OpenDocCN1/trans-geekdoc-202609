Thus, Euler's Theorem stands proven.

# 4.10.1 A Corollary of Euler's Theorem

Let $n = p 	imes q$, where $p$ and $q$ are two prime numbers.

And let $M$ be any integer such that $0 < M < n$.

Then, $M^{\phi(n)} \equiv M \pmod n$, will hold true for $M$ that may not be relatively to $n$.

Proof:

There are two cases:

+   1. Case I: $M$ is relatively prime to $n$, that is, $	ext{GCD}(M, n) = 1$
Then, by Euler's Theorem:

```
$M^{\phi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod n$
```

Multiplying both sides of the above congruence by $M$

```
$M^{\phi(n)+1} \equiv M \pmod n$
```

+   2. Case II: $M$ is not relatively prime to $n$, that is, $	ext{GCD}(M, n) 
eq 1$
Since $0 < M < n$ and $n$ are products of $p$ and $q$, it is not possible for $M$ to have both $p$ and as its factors; there, are two possibilities:

+   A. $M$ is a multiple of $p$, but is relatively prime to $q$, $	ext{GCD}(M, q) = 1$.
