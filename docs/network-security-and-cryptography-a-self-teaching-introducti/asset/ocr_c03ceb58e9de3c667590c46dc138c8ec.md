# 2.5.4 Hyperexponential Distribution

This is another extension of the exponential distribution. Suppose $X_1$ and $X_2$ are two exponentially distributed random variables with means $1/\lambda_1$ and $1/\lambda_2$ respectively. If the random variable $X$ assumes the value $X_1$ with probability $p$, and the value of $X_2$ with probability $q=1-p$, then the PFD of X is

$f_X(x) = p\lambda_1 e^{-\lambda_1 x} + q\lambda_2 e^{-\lambda_2 x} \quad (2.63)$

This is known as a two-stage hyperexponential distribution. Its mean and variance are given by

$E(X) = \frac{p}{\lambda_1} + \frac{q}{\lambda_2} \quad (2.64)$

$Var(X) = \frac{p(2-p)}{\lambda_1^2} + \frac{1-p^2}{\lambda_2^2} - \frac{2p(1-p)}{\\lambda_1\lambda_2} \quad (2.65)$

# 2.5.5 Gaussian Distribution

This distribution, also known as *normal distribution*, is the most important probability distribution in engineering. It is used to describe phenomena with symmetric variations above and below the mean $m$. A random variable $X$ with its PDF of

$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2\right] \quad -\infty < x < \infty \quad (2.66)$

where the mean

$E(X) = \mu \quad (2.67a)$

and the variance

$Var(X) = \sigma^2 \quad (2.67b)$
