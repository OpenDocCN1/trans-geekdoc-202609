# Example 4.33

Let $n=7$. Is $g=3$ a primitive root of $n$?

# Solution

$\phi(n)=n-1=6$. Table 4.20 illustrates the calculation of $y=g^x mod n$ for $n=7$ and $g=3$.

TABLE 4.20 Calculation of $y=g^x (mod n)$

| $x$ | $g^x$ | $g^x (mod n)$ |
|---|---|--|
| 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | 9 | 2 |
| 3 | 27 | 6 |
| 4 | 81 | 4 |
| 5 | 243 | 5 |
| $6 = \phi(n)$ | 729 | 1 |

3 is a primitive root of 7, its powers $3, 3^2, 3^3, \dots, 3^6$ are all distinct $mod 7$.

# 4.12 DISCRETE LOGARITHMS

Let $p$ be a prime number and $g$ be one of its primitive roots, and $y=g^x mod p$ will be an integer $(0 < y < p)$.

In addition, $x=\log_y y(mod p)$ is called a Discrete Logarithm of $y$ to the base $g(mod p)$. Sometimes $x$ is also denoted as $ind_{g,p}(y)$, that is, index of $y$ to base $g(mod p)$.

# 4.12.1 Difficulty of Computing Discrete Logarithms

Given $g, X$ and $p$, it is considered easy to compute $y=g^x mod p$. However, given $y, g$ and $p$, when $p$ is large, it is considered extremely difficult to compute $x$, that is, take the discrete logarithm of $y$ to the base $g(mod p)$.
