## Foreword

Expressly owing to the COVID-19 crisis, various states in the world have understood the importance of living together for their survival and future progress. At the same time, the crisis, as worst as the COVID-19 pandemic, that can destroy the entire human race, if the appropriate cure is not found in time, has not stopped humanity from playing dirty power politics. However, broadly, humans have understood that for their safe existence, they need to ensure to retain and enhance their ability to innovate and experiment. This process of experimentation would only allow them to find solutions for various difficulties, including major crises like the pandemics.

One of the influential technologies which have been dominating the global technology spectrum since the second half of the 20th century is the Information Technologies (IT). The COVID-19 pandemic and resultant interruptions have conclusively changed the approach towards technology adaption in general and digital technology adoption in particular, both for consumers as well as corporates. More importantly, several governments have also understood that various offshoots of IT, as well as IT-based applications, can play a significant role in governance alike, and technology is likely to play a vital role in recovery, as well. Broadly, it could be argued that the COVID-19 crisis is reclaiming the importance of IT as the ‘mover and shaker technology’ for the world. At the same time, it is also necessary to avoid the temptation to look at the range of technologies only from the prism of the COVID-19 crisis. For centuries the world has witnessed technology as a fundamental agent for social change. They offer numerous possibilities to innovate, design, and develop new scientific and technological ideas to bring in a change in civilian, commercial, and strategic domains.

Renowned Harvard Business School professor, Clayton Christensen, debated about disruptive technology and innovation during the 1990s. During the last few decades, the world has witnessed the rise of novel technologies such as artificial intelligence, blockchain, the Internet of Things (IoT), robotics, 3D printing, and many more that have trodden a new path of innovation, although still in its nascent stages. These technologies have showcased the potential applicability it has in many sectors. It has been predicted that technologies like these have the potential and capability to disrupt the existing technology setup bringing in a sea change in human activities for the future. They could bring in lifestyle changes, improve various existing commercial practices, and scientifically impact the security apparatus. Disruptive technologies tend to be new and aren’t incremental improvements in existing technologies. Hence, it is critical to study the possible impact of such technologies on the existing industrial setup. It is necessary to undertake such a study for every major sector of the industry. Through this book, the authors have attempted to focus on the shipping industry, which plays an instrumental role in the global economy. Therefore, the shipping industry is a very relevant domain that needs attention, especially when a new wave of technological advancements that can ease and dissipate the inherent troubles facing the industry looms on the horizon. This book and the subject the authors are trying to tackle focuses on understanding the impact of specific technologies that are disruptive in nature on the shipping industry, including related issues of global supply chain management.

A force majeure, such as pandemics, natural calamities, and geopolitical instabilities, could affect the global supply chain, especially in the future. With an opaque supply chain, overpowered by middlemen, and covered with heaps of paperwork, acts as a hindrance to the efficient functioning of the global shipping industry. This can be addressed by employing innovation afforded by novel technologies, such as blockchain and artificial intelligence. The authors, through the book, have focussed on two technologies, namely blockchain and artificial intelligence. Analyzing the numerous technical aspects, its importance, and applications, the authors have furthered this thought, to study an ecosystem, that has integrated the two technologies. Furthermore, by exploring this possibility of an integrated ecosystem, the shipping industry is brought into focus, eyeballing at the possible applications it has on the industry. Through this book, the authors have tried to bring out the crux of the issue faced by the shipping industry, eventually finding a plausible answer to the problem.

It is but self-evident that tomorrow’s social and economic issues would be different from those of the present day. Hence, priority setting becomes extremely important. Various technologies discussed in this book would allow the policymakers to significantly improve on their existing structures. For both the near and distant future, the shipping industry is expected to remain as a lifeline for the global supply chain. With the investor sentiments likely to remain subdued in the near term owing to the COVID-19 crisis, the demand for an integrated ecosystem of blockchain and artificial intelligence suggested in this book, therefore, necessitates immediate attention from the policymakers. They provide opportunities that could prove beneficial for the global shipping industry.
