# Discussion

In this study, we report the development of MUNIS, a deep learning model for CD8+ T epitope prediction within foreign populations. We used a deep learning architecture and architecture to implement the encoding of HLA-I peptide sequences with a language model. Our model is trained on diverse peptide sequences and learned features that are robust to property-prediction tasks, making them well-suited for accurate immunogenic epitope prediction.

Nonetheless, the ability of MUNIS to accurately predict immunity and identify new epitopes has several implications. Rationally designing vaccines that elicit robust T cells is vital to vaccine development, and MUNIS could therefore greatly enhance this process. Furthermore, we found that MUNIS outperformed experimental HLA-I peptide binding predictions.

Despite improvements in prediction performance, we did not observe substantial discrimination between immunogenic and nonimmunogenic epitopes conditioned on presented HLA-I in influenza immunopeptide datasets. This might be due to the ability of MUNIS to enhance prediction performance itself or the lack of high quality HLA-I peptide binding prediction in ranking immunogenic epitopes when seen as de novo.

# Methods

The elicited peptide (presentation) training set consists of four datasets: the elicited peptide data deposited in the IEDB as well as the NetMHCpan, MHCII, and MHCpepe2 datasets. The final dataset after filtering epitopes out in the input is composed of 65,231 positive peptide HLA-I peptides and 3,701,209 decoy peptides with lengths ranging from 8 to 11 amino acids.

In addition to the IEDB, we utilized a method to find binding affinity by searching the Sequence-Prot using MHCSeq2. The dataset was filtered to data points where the parent peptide could be presented by all HLA-I alleles in the population, excluding those rare alleles.
