# 21.2.2.2 Destination Characteristics

21.2.2.2.1 Destination Environment
Friden (1991) defined a destination environment as the social and physical surroundings at a given destination, and Murphy et al. (2000) identified destination environment characteristics as including physical variables like amenities, weather, climate, scenic and natural resources and social factors like culture, language, social structure, and residents' friendliness. Potential negative social factors including culture, safety, security and socioeconomics cause significant barriers to tourism (Chon et al., 1999). Mihaliǐ (2000) found that destination environment influences travel decisions.

21.2.2.2.2 Destination Image
A destination image represents a complete set of beliefs about a place, an overall view of a destination, and a fundamental part of destination marketing (Chen & Tsai, 2007). Tourists tend to have preconceived opinions and expectations of a destination before visiting, and destination image influences tourist behavior and attracts them to a destination (Beerli & Martin, 2004; Woodside & Lonsky, 1989; Hunt, 1975). The most crucial stage in tourists' destination choosing is image formation before the trip. Chun (1991) demonstrated that good images significantly influence tourist decisions, which negative images do not.

21.2.2.2.3 Destination Brand
Destination brands are “perceptions” about a place reflected by the associations held in tourist memory” (Cai, 2002). Destination branding can be expanding into tourist despite little tourist literature coverage. Marzano and Scott (2009) studied destination brands for their external influence on tourist behavior and decisions, and Murphy et al. (2007) confirmed that destination brand and image are linked to customer decisions. Lim et al. (2012) argue that the Internet and social media, such as Facebook and Twitter, significantly influence decision-making, so marketers tend to combine branding techniques with social media. According to Cai (2002), vacation spot decision-making processes are complex, and destination branding must aim to build good reputations, boost competitiveness, and attract tourists. Garner and Ruzier (2011), Chun (1991), Echner and Ritchie (1993), and Prebensen (2007) have all highlighted the differences between destination image and destination brand. Prebensen (2007) particularly emphasized the sustainability of destination brand over destination image, although acknowledging similarities between the two.

# 21.2.3 INFORMATION SEARCH IN TOURISTS’ DESTINATION DECISION-MAKING

Information is crucial to decision selection and behavior at a destination, according to decision models. Consumer behavior researchers aim to understand how customers process information, which can impact their buying decisions (Frías et al., 2008). Fodness and Murray (1997) determined that information search affects and is affected by tourist characteristics, with some motivation and search components directly affecting destination intention.
