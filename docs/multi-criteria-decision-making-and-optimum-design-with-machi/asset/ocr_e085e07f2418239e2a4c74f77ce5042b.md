Mixing is the most widely used method to overcome the disadvantages of plastics. This method helps improve the limitations and retains the existing advantages (Brown Brown, 2023). Dekkers et al. (1991) researched the impact toughness of PBT, polycarbonate (PC), and polyether (PPE) by adding styrene-ethylene-butadiene-styrene (SEBS); under the conditions of 25 °C, the PBT/PC/PPE/SEBS mixture (46/10/3/14) had an impact toughness of 600 Jun, 12 times higher than the PBT/PPE/SEBS blend only reached 50 Jun/m. Maiti et al. (2007) studied the effects of acrylonitrile-butyl acrylate-styrene on the mechanical properties of PBT and found that PBT's flexural strength and impact toughness had increased significantly.

PA6 is a polymer used mainly in technical applications with high physical and mechanical property requirements, such as automobile manufacturing, electrical and electronic industries, precision machining, and medical equipment. PA6 has high corrosion resistance and high melting temperature. However, its limitations are its resistance to acid and ability to be used in many acidic environments and its poor dimensional deformation (Chih-Hong et al., 1996).

Not only polyamide and polyester have excellent combinations that many researchers are interested in, but they also is a promising option for creating high-performance polymer materials in the future. PBT is the optimal solution for mixing with PA6 from the contrasting mechanical properties. Researchers tested the compatibility of PBT and PA6 by adding E-4 epoxy resin to the mixture and found excellent compatibility (An et al., 1996), and flexural strength also improved when a small amount of E-4 epoxy was added (Hongru et al., 2017). The compatibility of PBT/PA6 was also studied by adding poly (n-butyl acrylate)/poly(methacrylate-co-methacrylic acid). The results show that the toughness of the new was 25.66 kJ/m², four times higher than the original. At the weight absorbed only 1.3% water content, down 53.6% of PBT and 26.6%. The impact toughness of the PBT/PA6 mixture also increases by adding ethylene vinyl acetate-co-maleic anhydride when by injection molding (Kim et al., 2003).
