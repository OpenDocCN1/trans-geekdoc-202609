## 10 Selecting Optimal Electric Vehicle Charging Station Sites Based on Analytic Hierarchy and VIKOR

Mousumi Karmakar and Subhajit Bhattacharyya

### 10.1 INTRODUCTION

Economic stability across the globe has improved steadily because of the rapidly growing demand for industrialization in our cities, but as a result, we are depleting many of our natural resources and causing severe environmental degradation (Bilgen, 2014) and (Zhao et al., 2014). However, if we stop industrialization to save a green environment, then we delay or lose our economic improvement. Therefore, it is necessary to develop ways to balance economic advancement by reducing fossil fuel consumption.

Although as of 2023, India's Climate Change Performance Index (CCPI) has improved to eighth place worldwide (Puneet et al., 2022), India's carbon footprint ranks third only after China and the United States. CCPI is calculated based on four criteria, greenhouse gas emissions, renewable energy, use, and environmental policy. In India, nearly 13% of $CO_2$ emissions are caused by the transportation sector (Yingwen et al., 2023), while the aggressive adoption of EVs can drastically reduce the emission of harmful greenhouse gases, although ongoing research is needed to boost EV battery life and range (Xiuhong et al., 2023).

In this paper, we present a multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) model to select optimal sites for electric vehicle (EV) charging stations. The model integrates the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and the VIKOR method to evaluate potential sites based on multiple criteria. The goal is to provide a robust framework for urban planners to consider environmental, economic, and technical factors.

The criteria used in the evaluation include:
- Greenhouse gas emissions
- Renewable energy use
- Environmental policy
- Transportation sector emissions
- Carbon footprint
- Population density
- Traffic volume
- Pro to existing grid
- Environmental factors
- Societal factors
- Economic factors
- Location factors
- Active power losses
- CO2 emission
- Service capacity
- Construction cost
- Distance from the main road

#### 10.1.1 Literature Review

Selecting optimal EVCS is a real-life decision-making challenge because the relevant criteria conflict with each other (Sen et al., 2015), yet doing so is critical for the evolution of the green industry. Authors have conducted a number of investigations on EVCS sites, Hookah et al. (2015) on EVCS planning and management. Zhang (2018) proposed an EVCS planning model using a distribution network with the aim of minimizing the cost while maximizing service capacity. Erbag et al. (2018) used GIS-fuzzy AHP multicriteria decision analysis and TOPSIS to select optimal EVCS sites in Istanbul, Turkey. Aman et al. (2021) integrated fuzzy AHP with TOPSIS to implement feasible charging stations based on considering environmental, societal, economic, location, and other factors.

Abapour et al. (2015) maximized the benefits of a distribution system manager to determine the optimal site for a plug-in hybrid vehicle charging station; their technique not only reduced active power losses and CO2 emission but also improved supply energy in load condition. Hosseini et al. (2019) proposed a Bayesian network (BN) model for site considering various quantitative and qualitative factors. Based on sensitivity analysis and propagation analysis, they validated the BN model for selecting the optimal EVCS among all alternatives.

Yao et al. (2014) proposed a multi-objective planning model for integrated power distribution as well as EV charging stations; they also faced conflicting objectives: reducing the final investment cost, minimizing energy consumption, and maximizing the annual traffic flow. Yao et al. computed multi-objective evolutionary algorithms to validate the proposed model's feasibility and effectiveness. Based on electro-mobility and energy demand, Bouche et al. (2014) proposed an optimized model for properly allocating EVCS sites in the city of Lyon. The proposed model not only satisfied all the energy demands but also minimized trip energy consumption and total location expenditure. By the vehicle consumption demand, Bouche et al. tested the model on real trips and conducted sensitivity analysis to validate the model.
