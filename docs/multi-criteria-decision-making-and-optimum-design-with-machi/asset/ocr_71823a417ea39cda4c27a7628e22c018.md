# Cruising through the Choices

293

Since it has been argued that the Internet-based tourism will be focused on consumer-centric technologies that will support tourism organizations in interacting with the tourist (Buhalis, 2000), this study provides valuable insight into the tourism marketing strategies that need to pursue to achieve the goal. The findings indicate a great need for a tourist destination decision-making process that prioritizes extensive search to ensure that social network websites are at the top of the ranking list. Therefore, tourism marketers need to understand the key factors to effectively reach and promote their businesses and destinations through social networks and websites.

This study suggests future research in various directions. First, I conducted this study in Vietnam, so future researchers could apply the framework in this study to examine tourists in other national contexts. Second, whether within Vietnam or in other countries, researchers could collect responses from domestic tourists and measure the differences between foreign and domestic tourists. Third, I studied tourists' decision-making processes before the trip; future researchers could examine tourists during trips and reinforce intentions. Lastly, future researchers could investigate the most influential within tourism information search characteristics. Tourism operators, agents, managers, and other stakeholders can use these combined findings to better target their marketing efforts on their desired destinations.
