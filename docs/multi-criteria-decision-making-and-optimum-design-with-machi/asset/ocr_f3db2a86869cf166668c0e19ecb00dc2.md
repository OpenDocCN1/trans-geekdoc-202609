Meenakshi al. [17] presented a new combinatorial technique to encrypt and decrypt numbers using labeled graphs and domination theory. The technique involves splitting the secret number into partitions and assigning them to dominating vertices and edges in a graph. Tan et al. [18] sought strategies for improving the synergy between multicriteria decision making (MCDM) and building information modelling (BIM) in the architecture, engineering, and construction industry. Combining MCDM with BIM, they identified the major domains as sustainability, retrofit, supplier selection, and constructability. Tan et al. also discuss five strategies for improving the synergy between MCDM and BIM.

Aliams al. [19] proposed a new RSA cryptystem based on fuzzy set numbers for encryption and decryption and showed that fuzzy theory is suitable for securing other systems and provides difficulty for attackers in communication. Kamardan al. [20] proposed a modified multiple RSA system consisting of three phases: key generation, encryption, and decryption. The decryption phase utilized the CRT to expedite computation and found that decryption using multiple RSA was nearly four times faster than the standard RSA. Additionally, using multiple primes and additional secret keys increases the difficulty of breaking the security of the algorithm.

It is an intriguing idea to combine conventional RSA encryption with fuzzy logic, particularly, with trapezoidal fuzzy numbers (TFNs). The CRT allows for efficient computation by breaking down the RSA problem into smaller equations, reducing computational complexity, and enabling faster encryption and decryption. Furthermore, incorporating TFNS enables a more comprehensive representation of uncertainty in the encryption process, enhancing security attacks that exploit vulnerabilities in standard RSA methods [12,21,22].
