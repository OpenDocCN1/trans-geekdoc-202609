# 21 Cruising through the Choices
Unraveling Destination Decision-Making Dilemmas with Social Networks via MCDM

Hai-Ninh Do

# Over the past two decades, the Internet has transformed information seekers into
creators, enabling global sharing and peer-to-peer applications (Frías et al., 2008; Volo, 2010; Akheurst, 2009). However, web 2.0 applications have, among
other benefits, led to the rapid growth of online social networking distribution with tour-
ism (Werther & Ricci, 2004). Gretzel and Yoo (2008) noted that travel information
searches are becoming more common online as web technology evolves. According
to Zheng et al. (2008), the Internet has transformed tourism information distribution\and communication, enabling e-travelers and potential tourists to learn about desti-
s from others (Frías et al., 2008).
Tourists today plan their trips using web searches in combination with traditional
sources. Frías et al. (2008) and O’Connor and Murphy (2004) found that YouTube,
blogs, Facebook, and Flickr, others, have altered the role of travel agents in
terms of providing information. Gretzel and Yoo (2008) agreed with this notion,
highlighting the significance of social media content in searches for tourist destinantion information. Social media platforms allow tourists to engage with both tourism
operators and experienced tourists (Jenkin, 2010), making it easier to share travel-
related comments, thoughts, personal experiences, and images and videos.
Tourist decision-making has always been an important in tourism research
(Wong & Yeh, 2009), and information searching is crucial to decision-making.
tours invariably need knowledge about potential destinations to make informed
choices (Sheldon, 1993). Bieger and Laesser (2004) noted that most visitors’
information-searching and decision-making models include pre-purchase informa-tion searches. Therefore, the tourism sector must prepare and deliberate mar-eting strategy choices.
