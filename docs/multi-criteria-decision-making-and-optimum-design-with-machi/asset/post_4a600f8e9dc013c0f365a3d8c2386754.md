## 4.3.2 THE PLA FILAMENT

The material utilized here was cheap PLA filament [14], a biodegradable thermo-plastic derived from renewable sources. This plastic can be composed of many additives such as cornstarch, sugarcane, cassava, and even potato starch. PLA printing filament is one of the most popular manufacturing FDM 3D printing polymeric materials on the market. This type of plastic is low-cost and easy to print, and it comes in many vibrant colors.

PLA has many outstanding advantages over other petrochemical plastic products such as ABS or polyvinyl alcohol. With the ability to decompose in the environment, PLA is widely used to produce daily utensils such as food packaging, trays, and cups. Therefore, it is suitable for use by students to familiarize themselves with additive manufacturing. Some PLA properties that it is a thermoplastic with a melting temperature between 190 °C and 220 °C, just like other printing plastics, it can also be used well for all types of FDM printers. PLA has good elasticity and comes in diverse colors for customers to freely choose.

Additionally, as a thermoplastic, PLA has the special property of being able to be softened many times under heat and become solid when cooled. Thermal impact changes only its physical but not chemical properties, so it can be recycled many times, including the waste products generated during the 3D printing process. ## 4.3.3 THE TAGUCHI METHOD

The Taguchi method is a prevalent experimental design employed to systematically examine the influence of multiple parameters on both the mean outcomes and variances of a designed process [15]. This approach conventionally employs an orthogonal array to methodically explore the repercussions of distinct trials. The Taguchi method is esteemed in engineering circles for its capacity to robustly evaluate the significance of all pertinent parameters that impact the output of a given process.

The Taguchi method entails measuring each parameter's impact using the signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio, the mean of iterated experiments divided by their corresponding variance. In the context of this investigation, the attribute under consideration was tensile strength, and we maximized it by following Equation (4.1) [16]:

```
$$\frac{S}{N} = -10\log_{10} n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{y_i^2}}$$ (4.1)
```

where y signifies the observed measurement and n signifies the total number of experimental trials conducted. Calculating the S/N ratio for calculating the individual influence of each parameter following Equation (4.2) [17]:

```
$C_i = \frac{R}{\sum RI} \times 100\%$ (4.2)
```

Here, R designates the ratio, that is the difference between the minimum S/N ratio and the maximum S/N ratio corresponding to each parameter level. Calculating this range enables identifying specific parameters that yield the highest S/N ratio. Consequently, this identification facilitates the prediction of optimal parameters that lead to the minimal deviation from the desired property in the final output.

## 4.3.4 TENSI MEASUREMENT

### 4.3.4.1 The Measurement Method

We measured tensile strength using ASTM D638 and Shimadzu Autograph AG-X Plus 20kN testing machine. The testing velocity was 5 mm/min. The pertinent specifications of this universal testing apparatus were a load capacity of 20 kN, a crosshead speed range spanning from 0.0005 to 1000 mm/min, a maximum return speed of 1200 mm/min, crosshead speed precision at ±0.1%, and a crosshead-table clearance 850 mm [18].

### 4.3.4.2 The Dog-Bone Sample

We printed the sample for the tensile tests according to ASTM D638. The ASTM D638 test method is a comprehensive delineation of one of the most popular methods of measuring the tensile strength of reinforced and unreinforced parts using a standard dumbbell-shaped sample under constant temperature, humidity, and test speed [12]. In this case, the printed sample had a dog-bone shape with a length of 165 mm and a thickness of 3.2 mm, and the distance between grips was 115 mm.

## 4.4 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### 4.4.1 Process Parameter Selection

Numerous parameters were involved in 3D printing process, encompassing both general practices and those a comprehensive examination of literature guided the selection of seven factors we deemed the most pronounced influence on tensile strength: infill style, printing density, layer thickness, nozzle speed, extrusion temperature, raster angle, and holding temperature adhered to the manufacturer's recommended settings. In contrast to more intricate prints that afford a wide range of infill types, such as line, grid, concentric, tetrahedral, gyro, or tri-hexagonal [19], the current printer, which is typically employed for straightforward tasks, offers only two infill types: line and concentric. The remaining parameters were characterized by three levels each.

### 4.4.2 Orthogonal Array Selection

Considering the number of parameters along with their respective levels, we employed the L18(3⁶) orthogonal array (OA) design, printed a total of 18 samples and subjected them to tensile testing; Table 4.3 provides the computation and the corresponding S/N ratio for each sample. As noted earlier, our ultimate aim was to maximize the tensile strength based on the highest S/N ratio.
