We conducted this study to attempt PBT's flexural and impact toughness by mixing it with polyamide 6 (PA6) and GF. From there, PBT can be applied widely in automobile manufacturing and household electrical appliances.

Mixing is the most widely used method to overcome the disadvantages of plastics. This method helps improve the limitations and retains the existing advantages (Brown Brown, 2023). Dekkers et al. (1991) researched the impact toughness of PBT, polycarbonate (PC), and polyether (PPE) by adding styrene-ethylene-butadiene-styrene (SEBS); under the conditions of 25 °C, the PBT/PC/PPE/SEBS mixture (46/10/3/14) had an impact toughness of 600 J/m, 12 times higher than the PBT/PPE/SEBS blend only reached 50 J/m. Maiti et al. (2007) studied the effects of acrylonitrile-butyl acrylate-styrene on the mechanical properties of PBT and found that PBT's flexural strength and impact toughness had increased significantly.

PA6 is a polymer used mainly in technical applications with high physical and mechanical property requirements, such as automobile manufacturing, electrical and electronic industries, precision machining, and medical equipment. PA6 has high corrosion resistance and high melting temperature. However, its limitations are its resistance to acid and ability to be used in many acidic environments and its poor dimensional deformation (Chih-Hong et al., 1996).

Not only polyamide and polyester have excellent combinations that many researchers are interested in, but they also is a promising option for creating high-performance polymer materials in the future. PBT is the optimal solution for mixing with PA6 from the contrasting mechanical properties. Researchers tested the compatibility of PBT and PA6 by adding E-4 epoxy resin to the mixture and found excellent compatibility (An et al., 1996), and flexural strength also improved when a small amount of E-4 epoxy was added (Hongru et al., 2017). The compatibility of PBT/PA6 was also studied by adding poly (n-butyl acrylate)/poly(methacrylate-co-methacrylic acid). The results show that the toughness of the new was 25.66 kJ/m², four times higher than the original. At the weight absorbed only 1.3% water content, down 53.6% of PBT and 26.6%. The impact toughness of the PBT/PA6 mixture also increases by adding ethylene vinyl acetate-co-maleic anhydride when by injection molding (Kim et al., 2003).

Injection molding is a popular method when manufacturing PBT products reinforced with GCs as the producing results have stable and accurate dimensions, short molding, and low cost (Kelly et al., 1965). Reinforced PBT is widely used in electrical engineering, electronics, automobile component manufacturing, so it is necessary to research more possibilities for strengthening PBT to meet additional needs (Zhang et al., 2014). Researchers have investigated the burning ability of PBT reinforced with GFs because of its applicability, increasing the ability to create fire-resistant PBT and found that the GF did not change the degradability of PBT. Other researchers confirmed that GF-reinforced PBT had a 2.5 times lower lower than the original PBT (Kempel et al., 2012) and improved the fire resistance of GF-reinforced PBT by adding 2,4,6-tris(2',4',6'-tribromophenoxy)-1,3-triazine (Chen et al., 2006). In addition, polyethylene terephthalate' impact toughness increases with the addition of GF and other additives (Monti et al., 2021). The results showed that both notched and unmatched toughness increased, up to 52% higher than the original. Based on these results, we determined that we could use GF to improve impact toughness in this research.

Currently, reinforcing compounds have attracted much attention in many fields, such as automobiles and electronics, thanks to the significantly mechanical properties of the material after reinforcement (Thaitipparasathy et al., 2008). Common materials used for reinforcement are GF, carbon fiber, and thermoplastics. Synthetic mixes of PA6 and GFs are widely used in research and industrial fields (Zuo et al., 2013). Because PA6 has good mechanical properties, low cost, and high temperature resistance, a GF coating applied to produce PA6-based composite panels achieves high performance (Kiss et al., 2021). SEM results show that PA adheres tightly to GF. In addition, the plastic strength of synthetic panels is up to 500 MPa, much higher than that of industrial containers.

From these studies on PBT, PA6, and GF mixtures, especially the findings from Hamlaoui et al. (2022), we examined PBT's mechanical properties with injection molding after GF reinforcement and found that after reinforced with GF, PBT's mechanical properties increased significantly. For example, Young's modulus increased to 77.33%, the tensile strength increased to 63.28%, and the yield strength increased to 65.12% when the ratio of GF was 30%. Based on these results, GF significantly improves the mechanical properties of a PBT/PA6 mixture, especially unmatched impact toughness.

### 7.2.2 MEASUREMENT METHODS

First, we injection molded and produced samples. Next, we selected five samples to measure the Izod toughness according to ASTM D256. To measure impact toughness, we used the Tinius Olsen IT504 tester; the machine allows for 2.82 J with 2.82 J impact energy. The samples were mounted on the hammer with speeds of 3.46 m/s and 2.91 m/s, respectively.

### 7.3.2 SAMPLE MICROSTRUCTURES

After measurement, we took samples to capture the microscopic structures at the fracture surfaces for observation (Figure 7.5). Figure 7.6 shows the microstructures of the PBT/PA6/GF samples with each GF ratio: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18% GF.
