## 11 Optimum Topological Indices for Intuitionistic Fuzzy Graphs

J. Senbagamalar and V. Gomathi

### 11.1 INTRODUCTION

A fuzzy set gives the degree of membership of an object in a given set (Zadeh, 1965). An intuitionistic fuzzy graph (IFG) contains two-valued logic (two dimensions) where the values lie between [0, 1]. The IFG edge values, the minimum and maximum, are based on the vertex. IFGs represent only membership and non-membership value. In 1974, basic theoretic concepts and fuzzy graph operations were performed by Rosenfeld. K. Y. Atanov introduced intuitionistic fuzzy sets in 1986 (Atansov, 1986) and interval-valued intuitionistic fuzzy sets in 1999 (Atansov, 1999). Fuzzy, intuitionistic, and neutropic graphs are one-dimensional (only membership), two-dimensional (membership and non-membership), and three-dimensional (membership, non-membership and indeterminacy), respectively introduced the center and eccentricity of fuzzy set and fuzzy set (Bhattacharya). IFGs extend the fuzzy graphs, and neutropic graphs extend IFGs (Szzmidt and Kaczprzyk, 2000). The intuitionistic fuzzy graph, vertices, cycle and trees in intuitionistic fuzzy graph were introduced by Akram (Akram and Alsheheri, 2014). In 2012, graph operations on intuitionistic fuzzy graph were discussed (Akram and Davvaz, 2012) and studied the connectivity in intuitionistic fuzzy graph (Alzoubi and Alhasner, 2021). A real-life application was related with human trafficking and illegal immigration using Wiener fuzzy graph (近期 et al., 2019). In 2016, Akram introduced an idea about operations on intuitionistic fuzzy graph structures (Akram Muhammad and Akmal, 2018). For this chapter, we characterize several topological indices for IFGs: First Zagreb, second Zagreb, forgotten, Randić, and harmonic (Javeria Dinar, 2023) and discuss algorithms and applications.

### 11.2 PRELIMINARIES

#### 11.2.1 INTUITIONISTIC FUZZY GRAPHS

IFGs are extensions of classical fuzzy graphs that incorporate intuitionistic fuzzy sets. Fuzzy graph theory deals with graphs whose edges and vertices are labeled with degrees of membership in the interval [0,1] (Rosenfeld, A., 1975). Intuitionistic fuzzy sets extend the idea by including a third parameter called the non-membership degree, representing the degree to which does not belong to the set. An intuitionistic fuzzy graph uses both membership and non-membership degrees to model the uncertainty associated with the presence of edges and vertices. This allows for a more flexible representation of uncertainty in graph-based systems. Nagoi (2016) characterized double dominance in intuitionistic fuzzy graphs.

**11.2.1.1 Definition**
A fuzzy graph $G = (S, \alpha, \mu)$ is a triple consisting of a non-empty set $S$ with a pair of functions $\alpha: S \to [0,1]$ and $\mu: S \to [0,1]$ such that $\mu(x) \leq \alpha(x)$ for all $x \in S$.

**11.2.1.2 Definition**
Let $G = (S, T)$ be an IFG if:
(1) $S = \{v_1, v_2, v_3, \dots, v_n\}$ such that $\alpha_i: S \to [0,1]$, $\mu_i: S \to [0,1]$ denote the degree of membership and non-membership of element $v_i$, and $0 \leq \alpha_i + \mu_i \leq 1$ for every $v_i \in S$.
(2) $T \subseteq S \times S$ where $\alpha_{ij}(v_i, v_j): S \times S \to [0,1]$, $\mu_{ij}(v_i, v_j): S \times S \to [0,1]$ are such that $\alpha_{ij}(v_i, v_j) \leq \min[\alpha_i, \alpha_j]$, and $\mu_{ij}(v_i, v_j) \geq \max[\mu_i, \mu_j]$ denotes the degree of membership and non-membership of the edge $(v_p, v_q) \in T$ respectively, and $0 \leq \alpha_{pq} + \mu_{pq} \leq 1$ for every $(v_p, v_q) \in T$.

**11.2.1.3 Definition**
$G = (S, T)$ is said to be strong IFG if $\mu(xy) = \min[\mu(x), \mu(y)]$ and $\sigma(xy) = \max[\sigma(x), \sigma(y)]$ for $xy \in T$.

**11.2.1.4 Definition**
$G = (S, T)$ is said to be complete and defined by $C_m(G)$ if $\mu(xy) = \min[\mu(x), \mu(y)]$ and $\sigma(xy) = \min[\sigma(x), \sigma(y)]$ for $xy \in T$.

**11.2.1.5 Definition**
The first Zagreb index in fuzzy graphs was introduced by Kalishari et al. (2019): $M(G) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma(u_i) d_{i}^2$, for $u_i \in S$.

**11.2.1.6 Definition**
The second Zagreb index in fuzzy graphs was also introduced by Kalishari et al. (2019): $M^*(G) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{ij \in T} \sigma(u_i)d_i d_j$, for $i \neq j$.

**11.2.1.7 Definition**
The forgotten index in fuzzy graphs is $F(G) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma(u_i)d_i^2$, for $u_i \in S$.

**11.2.1.8 Definition**
the harmonic index in fuzzy graphs was introduced by Kalishari et al. as well:
$H(G) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{u_i \in T} \frac{1}{\sigma(u_i)d_i + \sigma(v_j)d_j}$, for $i \neq j$.

**11.2.1.9 Definition**
Kalishari et al. also introduced the Randić index for fuzzy graphs:
$R(G) = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma(u_i)d_i \cdot \sigma(v_j)d_j}$, for $i \neq j$.
