## 23 Selecting a Best Professor Awardee Using Multriteria Decision-Making

Kala Raja Mohan, R. Narmada Devi, Nagadevi Bala Nagaram, Regan Murugesan, and Sathish Kumar Kumaravel

### 23.1 INTRODUCTION

Multicriteria decision-making (MCDM) refers to making decisions when there are multiple factors to consider that conflict with each other, MCDM techniques have to be applied extensively across many fields, beginning with testing the applicability of different decision-finding methods themselves (Martin Aruldoss et al., 2013; Jafar Rezazai, 2015).

For instance, researchers have used fuzzy MCDM in civil engineering (Zhi Wen et al., 2021) and used MCDM algorithm to evaluate the performance of Indonesia’s defense military (A. P. Samarno et al., 2021). Researchers have used VIK for ranking (Hamed Taherood et al., 2023a) and used TODIM to analyze weights (A. Sahaya Sudha et al., 2020). Other researchers used outranking for decision-making in neurocentric soft-sets (Vakkas Ulucay et al., 2019) and described using inter-valued neurocentric soft sets in certain operations (Rana Muhammad Zulqarnain et al., 2022). One group compared fuzzy fuzzy cognitive maps with respect to COVID-19 variables (Regan Murugesan et al., 2023). Another applied neuro neurocentric cognitive methods with SWOT analysis (Jagan Obbenini et al., 2023).

In business, researchers have used MCDM methods to evaluate commercial bank performance (Mohamed Abdel-Basset et al., 2021), evaluate the risks with an excavation system (Ahmed Abdel-Monem et al., 2023), select suppliers using outranking (Nitesh Kumar et al., 2018), manage information systems (Edmundas Kazimieras Zavadskas et al., 2019), evaluate the financial performance of food and drink indices using fuzzy MCDM (Eyad Aldalau and Selcuk Percin, 2020), analyze blockchain rates (Ahmed Abdel-Monem et al., 2020) and the rate of gold (Kala Raja Mohan et al., 2023), select wheat suppliers (Reda M Hussien et al., 2023) and microemulsion oils (Ahmed Abdel-Monem et al., 2023), evaluate employee performance in Taiwan; Zon-Yao Lee et al., 2020), rank energy resources (Mahammad Nuriyev, 2020), and analyze firm performance in the IT sector (Ansar Daghouri et al., 2019). On the human resources side, researchers used the MULTIMOORA MCDM method to analyze employee performance for appraisals (Abteen Iraqi Maghsood et al., 2018) and investigated using MCDM to decide on changing jobs (V. Alpagut Yavuz, 2016).

With respect to the context of this study, using MCDM techniques to identify a recipient for a best instructor, MCDM has seen wide application in education as William Home et al., 2006; Durrani Amini Abdul Malik et al., 2021). Researchers have used MCDM algorithms to measure learners' productivity (Nguyen Anh Tuan et al., 2020) and student performance (Pavani Sirigiri et al., 2015), and Ayesha Meshal Alshami et al. (2023) presented findings on use of MCDM to find the best university for distance learning. On the student side, researchers have investigated students' use (Ertugurl Ayyldiz et al., 2023) and use of rough set to choose between two universities (Som Debnath, 2023).

Teaching is a selfless and noble profession. In nearly every field, people think of their individual development, but teachers aim to develop their students than themselves; their real happiness lies in elevating their students. Teachers mold themselves and encourage them to be the best they can be and prove themselves in society. However, teachers recognize themselves benefit from acknowledgement of their efforts, and one way to recognize high performers is with awards. Awards are common forms of recognition across almost all professions and can stimulate professional development in pursuit of further recognition.

In the context of this study, assessing higher education instructor performance in recognition for an award will be guided by many separate criteria including results, publications, projects, and funding. For this study, the organization that will grant the award will encounter applicants with many different profiles who will need to be scored according to the multiple criteria. Additionally, this is a highly appropriate scenario for examining techniques of multicriteria decision-making (MCDM) in the process of selecting finalists. For this chapter, we adopt the MCDM procedure called outranking to select the best instructor.

## 23.2 STANDARD ATTRIBUTES

### 23.2.1 CRITERIA

Attributes refer to the objective responsible for the decision; they are fixed based on the requirements for the solution. Attributes that are required for the decision are termed beneficial, and that require little consideration are called nonbeneficial.

### 23.2.2 CRITERIA

Criteria are the various rules and values that determine the conditions of the decision problem.

### 23.2.3 DECISION MATRIX

A decision matrix is a matrix of dimensions $m \times n$ in which the values are fixed based on the relationship between the attributes and the criteria. Here $m$ represents the number of attributes, and $n$ is the number of criteria.

### 23.2.4 NORMALIZED DECISION MATRIX

A normalized decision matrix is derived by dividing the elements by their Euclidian norm, the square root of the sum of the squares of the elements.

### 23.2.5 VARIANCE

Variance is a measure of the amount of spread in data. It is obtained by dividing the sum of squares of deviation from the mean by the number of data.

### 23.2.6 WEIGHTED NORMALIZED MATRIX

Weight is a measure of the preference for given attributes or criteria, and the matrix obtained from the product of the normalized decision matrix and the weighted normalized matrix.

### 23.2.7 CONCORDANCE MATRIX

In a concordance matrix, the diagonal elements are free.

### 23.2.8 DISCORDANCE MATRIX

$d_{ij} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } g_k(i) \geq g_k(j) \\ \frac{g_k(j) - g_k(i)}{\max g_k(i)} & \text{if } g_k(i) < g_k(j) \end{cases} \quad (23.1)$

### 23.2.9 PURE CONCORDANCE INDEX

$C_j = \sum_{k=1}^{n} c_{jk} \quad (23.2)$

### 23.2.10 PURE DISCORDANCE INDEX

$D_j = \sum_{k=1}^{n} d_{jk} \quad (23.3)$

### 23.2.11 RANKING TABLE

Ranking involves sorting elements according to preference, and in the final outranking step, a table is produced.
