
# Cruising the Choices

I added the initial average influence matrix to the normalized direct-influence matrix N from Equations (21.2) and (21.3) and then inverted this result to produce the total influence matrix T using Equations (21.4) and (21.5). The results for T showed that all elements I identified in this had at least some relationship with each other. However, to reduce the complexity of the elements of the matrix, I carefully threshold $\alpha$ as follows (Sumit & Anantavaricharan, 2012; Tzeng et al., 2007);

```
$\alpha = \frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{j}n_{ij}^{[ij]}}{N}$
```

Based on a calculated $\alpha = 0.8665$, the influence matrix after T is given in Table 21.3. Table 21.4 shows the influence relationships: d represents influence on other factors, while r represents influence; (d + r) represents the factor relationship, while a high (d + r) shows prominence, while a high (d - r) indicates a strong link with other criteria.

Table 21.4 and 21.6 shows that the rated SNSs as having the highest (d + r) = 27.5145, by WOM (26.9316), travel purpose (26.7513), and purchase (24.8877). Seven aspects were positive and therefore not influencers (personality traits, memory, knowledge, WOM, tourist intermediary, travel purpose, and length of stay), eight were negative and therefore not receivers (sociodemographic features, environment, brand, SNS use, evaluation, and purchase).

## 21.4.2 TOURISTS' DEMATEL RESULTS

While serving as a tour guide, I distributed the questionnaires through the tourism company at the same time I conducted the expert interviews. I distributed 300 questionnaires but only 185 for this research, and I used 185 for the DEMATEL survey. Following the same calculation step as the expert survey but with the threshold of $\alpha$ = 0.651, I obtained the matrix in Table 21.5. Table 21.6 shows the influence relationships rated by the experts.

Table 21.6 shows that the tourists' and experts' DEMATEL survey were the same, with SNSs having the most crucial role (20.8556 for the tourists) and second and third being WOM (19.7972) and travel purpose (19.5969). The lowest value for tourists were sociographic characteristics, external search, and choice are the set receivers because at 18.6440, Destination characteristics, has two negative values for (d - r). On the other hand, tourist and planning characteristics are strong because (d - r) is positive for both.

At the criterion, Level 21.5 shows that the criteria with positive (d - r) are sociodem factors (C1), personality traits (C2), environment (C3), memory, tourist intermediary (C0), travel purpose (C1), and length of stay (C13); these are not causes. However, (d - r) was near 0 for environment (0.1254), memory (0.1477), tourist intermediary (0.1823), travel purpose (0.0570), and length of stay (0.0306), indicating their weak influence on the other criteria. On the other side, image (C4), brand (C5), knowledge (C7), WOM (C8), SNS use (C9), travel distance (C12), evaluation (C14), and purchase (C15) all negative (d - r) and were not receivers. Particularly, WOM (-0.3690), SNS use (-0.5177), and purchase (-0.3724) were strongly affected by other criteria.
