Drawing inspiration from biological neural functions like those of the brain, the concept of ANNs originated from the pioneering work by McCulloch and Walter Pitts (Cooper, 2005; Sankar et al., 2019). In machining, ANNs along with genetic algorithms (GAs) have emerged as notable options, demonstrating the ability to optimize cutting parameters in complex environments. Their increasing popularity is attributed to their adaptability to nonlinear behaviors (M, 2006).

In a milling experiment on P20 steel, Escamilla et al. used ANN to predict MRR and Rₓ (M, 2006), and Maheshwara used regression models and ANNs to predict Rₓ in the hard turning of AISI 52100 steel; they showed the efficacy of utilizing ANN for these predictive applications (Maheshwara Reddy Paturi et al., 2018). Similarly, Boga and Korglu (2021) leveraged ANNs and a GA to predict surface roughness and ideal cutting parameters. The authors deleper into GAs, aiming to minimize production time in multi-axis CNC machining while ensuring surface roughness. However, the performance of GAs heavily depends on the initial configuration, requiring substantial computational resources. This dependency makes GAs less practical for smaller operations or under limited computational constraints.

For these reasons, multicritia decision-making (MCDM) is often an optimal solution. Common methods utilized VIKOR, additive weighting, and TOPSIS (technique for order of preference by similarity to ideal solution); for instance, Que et al. (2023) successfully applied TOPSIS to evaluate various machining strategies based on different criteria. Their highlights TOPSIS's superiority in a systematic approach to consider and rank multiple alternative options, integrating various objectives.

Each method—AI, GA, and MCDM—brings unique strengths to optimization but also faces distinct challenges. The predictive power of AI, GA’s robust optimization abilities, and MCDM’s structured decision framework suggest an appealing case for integration. Such a combined approach could leverage each method’s strengths while minimizing their limitations, for AI for prediction and modeling, GA for finding optimal solutions in complex scenarios, and MCDM for effective evaluation and selection. This integrated approach could address broader challenges in machining optimization, offering a more comprehensive and effective solution.
