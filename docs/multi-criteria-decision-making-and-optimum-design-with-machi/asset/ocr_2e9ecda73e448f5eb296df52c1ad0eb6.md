Optimizing machining parameters is one effective method of achieving the best results from multiple parameter conditions [16, 17, 18]. Sadhana et al. [19] studied the impact of optimizing WEDM parameters on the material removal rate (MRR) and surface roughness (Ra) of AISI H13 steel and found that had had the impact on MRR and Ra. Chockaling et al. [20] also considered the spark gap and dimensional deviation during the WEDM of AISI T-15-HSS steel. they investigated the impact of certain parameters—reference value, pulse-on time (Ton), pulse-off time (Toff), wire speed, tension and pressure mode—and analyzed the results using MATLAB 6.5 gray ratio analysis and Lingo 6 optimization software. Among parameters, Ton, Hoff and wire speed had the greatest impact on MRR, Ra, spark gap, and dimensional deviation.

In particular, Toff achieved the highest contribution for all these machining parameters. Manjaiah et al. [21] studied the impacts of machining parameters on AISI D2 steel. The authors used Taguchi optimization. Different from Chockaling et al. [20], Manjaiah et al. found that Ton had the most dramatic impact on the MRR and the Ra of the AISI D2 steel.

Meanwhile, Zhang et al. [22] revealed the impact of process parameters on the tungsten tool YG15. They investigated the tool's layer thickness and crack density, and the results indicated that Ton and pulse current have a great impact on Ra, layer thickness, and surface crack density. Rathod et al. [23] also investigated the impact of process parameters on WEDM of H13 steel. They applied the multi-objective JAYA algorithm and the Taguchi method to optimize Ra and MRR, and these authors found that Ton and voltage gap had the greatest contribution to Ra, while the wire speed was most important for MRR.

Dhoe et al. [24] pinpointed that achieving a good surface finish is a complex issue, especially in WEDM because the combination of many process parameters and materials. They investigated the WEDM process for cryoted AISI D2 tool steel and applied Taguchi Design of Experiments. Dhoe et al. also determined that Ton had the most significant impact on Ra, whereas Toff had the weakest impact. They provided a regression model for successfully predicting Ra that was consistent with the experimental results.
