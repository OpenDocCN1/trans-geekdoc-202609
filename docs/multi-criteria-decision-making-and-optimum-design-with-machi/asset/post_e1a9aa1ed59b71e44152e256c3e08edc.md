## 5.2 PRELIMINARIES

### 5.2.1 INTUITIONISTIC FUZZY GRAPHS

An intuitionistic fuzzy graph (IFG), $\mathbb{G} = (V, E)$ where $V=\{v_1, v_2...v_n\}$ is a finite vertex set that:

- (i) $\mu: V \rightarrow [0,1]$ and $\gamma_1: V \rightarrow [0,1]$ denote membership and nondeterministic membership, and $0 \leq \mu(v) + \gamma_1(v) \leq 1$ for every $v_i \in \mathbb{V}$.

- (ii) $E \subseteq V \times V$ where $\mu_2: V \times V \rightarrow [0,1]; \gamma_2: V \times V \rightarrow [0,1]$ are such that $\mu_2(\{v_i, v_j\}) \leq \min{\mu(v_i), \mu(v_j)\}; \gamma_2(\{v_i, v_j\}) \geq \max{\gamma_1(v_i), \gamma_1(v_j)\}$ and where $0 \leq \mu_2(\{v_i, v_j\}) + \gamma_2(\{v_i, v_j\}) \leq 1\forall(v_i, v_j) \in \mathbb{E}$.

### 5.2.2 STRONG ARC

An arc $(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V})$ is said to be strong:

$$\mu_2(v_i, v_j) = \min{\mu(v_i), \mu(v_j)\} \text{ and } \gamma_2(v_i, v_j) = \max{\gamma_1(v_i), \gamma_1(v_j)\}$$

### 5.2.3 IFG DEGREES

In an IFG, $\mathbb{G} = (V, E)$ the degree of vertex $\mathcal{U}$ is defined as the sum of the weights of the strong arcs incident at $\mathcal{U}$. The neighbourhood of $\mathcal{U}_i$ is denoted by $N(\mathcal{U}_i)=\{\mathcal{V}_j \in V/(\mathcal{U}_i, \mathcal{V}_j)\}$ representing a strong arc. The minimum $\mathbb{G}$ is $\delta(\mathbb{G})=\min\{d_c(\mathcal{U}_i)/\mathcal{U}_i \in V\}$ and the maximum $\mathbb{G}$ is $\Delta(\mathbb{G})=\max\{d_c(\mathcal{U}_i)/\mathcal{U}_i \in V\}$.

### 5.2.4 ISOLATED VERTEX

A vertex $\mathcal{U}_i \in \mathbb{V}$ in an IFG $\mathbb{G} = (\mathbb{V}, \mathbb{E})$ is said to be an isolated vertex if $\mu_2 \left(V_i, V_j\right) = 0$ and $\gamma_2 \left(V_i, V_j\right) = 0$.

### 5.2.5 CARDINALITY OF AN IFG

Let $\mathbb{G} = (\mathbb{V}, \mathbb{E})$ be an intuitionistic fuzzy graph. The cardinality of $\mathbb{G}$ is defined as:

$$|\mathbb{G}| = \sum_{V_i \in \mathbb{V}} \frac{1+T_{V_i}-F_{V_i}}{2} + \sum_{V_i,V_j \in \mathbb{E}} \frac{1+T_{V_{ij}}-F_{V_{ij}}}{2}$$

### 5.2.6 DOMINATING SET

Let $\mathbb{G} = (\mathbb{V}, \mathbb{E})$ be an IFG and $\mathcal{U}_i$ and $\mathcal{V}_j \in \mathbb{V}$. Then that $\mathcal{U}_i$ dominates $\mathcal{V}_j$ if there exists a strong arc between them. A subset $\mathcal{V}_d \subseteq \mathbb{V}$ is said to be a dominating set in $\mathbb{G}$ if for every $\mathcal{V}_j \in \mathbb{V} - \mathcal{V}_d$, there exists $\mathcal{V}_j \in \mathcal{V}_d$ dominates $\mathcal{U}_i$.
