# 20.1 INTRODUCTION

Efficient algorithms play a pivotal role in diverse data processing tasks,
directly influencing overall computational efficiency (Satish et al., 2009). This
research addresses the challenge of sorting data sets with intermediate sizes by
designing a novel algorithm.

Previous researchers have extensively explored sorting algorithms and
the performance under different scenarios. Hoare (1962) conducted an empirical
study comparing Quick Sort, Merge Sort, and Insertion Sort on large data sets, shed-
ding light on each algorithm's relative strengths and weaknesses.

Estvill-Castro (1992) explored adaptive sorting algorithms, discussing
theoretical contributions in complexity and disorder measures, practical insights
to adaptivity, and faster alternatives for later scenarios. Al-Kharabsheh
(2013) investigated sorting algorithms, comparing Group Comparison
Sort with conventional methods for improved performance. While established
sorting techniques like Quick Sort (Hoare, 1986; Steiner et al., 1989) and Merge
Sort (Cole, 1988) are widely used and respected for their efficacy, they may not
ways be the most efficient choices for data sets within this specific size
range.
