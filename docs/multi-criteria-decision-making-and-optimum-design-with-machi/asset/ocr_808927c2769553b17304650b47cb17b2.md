NSGA-II is a simple and straightforward algorithm. First, all individuals in the previous population ($P$) and current population ($Q$) are included in $R_t$. Then, population is sorted by nondomination algorithm described by pseudocode Appendix C.

In the context of NSGA-II, forming a new population $P_{t+1}$ from a merged population is methodological and prioritizes the highest quality solutions. The top non-dominated set, $F_1$, that contains the best solutions in the merged population is given the highest priority. All members of $F_i$ are included in $P_t$ if the size of $F_i$ is less than or equal to the desired population size $N$. If $P_i$ reaches after including all members of $F_i$, the algorithm proceeds to select members from subsequent non-dominated fronts, based on their rank order. Then solutions from $F_2$ are chosen next, followed by those from $F_3$, and so on, until the population $P_{t+1}$ reaches its capacity or no more sets can be included.

If the cumulative count of solutions from $F_1$ to the last accommodated nondinated set $F_l$ exceeds $N$, a selection must be made from the last set $F_l$. To do this, the solutions in $F_l$ are sorted by the crowded-comparison operator, which ranks solutions in descending order based on their crowding distance. The algorithm then selects the best solutions from $F_l$ to fill the remaining slots in $P_{t+1}$, until the population count reaches exactly $N$.
