## 7.4 POLYNOMIAL REGRESSION ANALYSIS

If x is the GF content, and y is the impact toughness, the quadratic regression equation has the form:

```
$y = a_{11}x^2 + a_1x + a_0 \quad (7.1)$
```

```
$a_0 \cdot n + a_1 \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k + a_{11} \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} y_k$
$a_0 \cdot x_k + a_1 \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k^2 + a_{11} \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k^3 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k y_k$
$a_0 \cdot x_k^2 + a_1 \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k^3 + a_{11} \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k^4 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} y_k^2$
```

The figure shows that impact toughness increased from 22.23 kJ/m² at 0% to 26.45 kJ/m². ## 7.5 CONCLUSION

We studied the unnotched impact toughness of GF-reinforced PBT/PA6 composites with seven GF ratios: 0%, 3%, 6%, 9%, 12%, 15%, and 18%. GF demonstrably increased the toughness in all cases, although the difference between the highest toughness for PBT/PA6/18% and the lowest, for PBT/PA6/0%, was only 15.95%. This result opens a new research direction for improving the toughness and mechanical properties of PBT/PA6 blends by reinforcing them with GF, which can help with addressing environmental pollution.
