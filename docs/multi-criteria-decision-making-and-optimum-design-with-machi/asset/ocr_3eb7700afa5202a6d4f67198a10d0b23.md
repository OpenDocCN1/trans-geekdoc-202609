# 10.1.1 Literature Review

Selecting optimal EVCSs is a real-life multicriteria decision-making challenge because the relevant criteria conflict with each other (Sen et al., 2015), yet doing so is critical for the evolution of the green industry. Authors have conducted a number of investigations on EVCS sites, for Hookah et al. (2015) on EVCS planning and management. Zhang (2018) proposed an EV charging station planning model using a distribution network with the aim of minimizing the cost while maximizing service capacity and satisfaction. Erbag et al. (2018) used GIS-fuzzy AHP multicriteria decision analysis and TOPSIS (technique for order preference by similarity to ideal selection) to select optimal EVCS sites in Istanbul, Turkey. Aman et al. (2021) integrated fuzzy AHP with TOPSIS to implement feasible EV charging stations based on considering environmental, societal, economic, location, and other factors.

Abapour et al. (2015) maximized the benefits of a distribution system manager to determine the optimal site for a plug-in hybrid electric vehicle charging station; their technique not only reduced active power losses and CO2 emission but also improved supply energy in load condition. Hosseini et al. (2019) proposed a Bayesian network (BN) model for site considering various quantitative and qualitative factors. Based on sensitivity analysis and propagation analysis, they validated the BN model for selecting the optimal EVCS among all alternatives.

Yao et al. (2014) proposed a multi-objective planning model for integrated power distribution as well as EV charging stations; they also faced conflicting objectives: reducing the final investment cost, minimizing energy consumption, and maximizing the annual traffic flow attained. Yao et al. computed multi-objective evolutionary algorithms to validate the proposed model's feasibility and effectiveness. Based on electro-mobility and energy demand, Bouche et al. (2014) proposed an optimized model for properly allocating EVCS sites in the city of Lyon. The proposed model not only satisfied all the energy demands but also minimized trip energy consumption and total location expenditure. By the vehicle consumption demand, Bouche et al. tested the model on real trips and conducted sensitivity analysis to validate the model.
