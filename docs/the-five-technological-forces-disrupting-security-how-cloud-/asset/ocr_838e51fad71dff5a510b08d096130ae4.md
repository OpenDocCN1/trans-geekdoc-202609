Because it is to bind systems together, you will often hear it referred to as “software glue.” The intensified interest in connecting systems to the physical world is driving increased demand for this type of bridge between systems. For all the value API-based middleware has delivered so far, mobile has taken it up a notch.

As previously discussed, the emergence of cloud APIs for security services is rapidly making them more relevant to corporate partners outside the industry. As turns out, many enterprise applications have workflows that span the virtual and the physical, with no easy way to connect the two. Some businesses need a physical security layer to connect them to the real world, but they may not have the technology to build themselves. What they lack is a connection from their purely digital world of bits and bytes to the messy physical world of glass and steel.

Cloud-based security services with web APIs proved to be just the right solution. This new mode of data exchange between disparate systems answered a long-standing need that found no solution in the precloud era. The new breed of APIs give property management software, for example, a way to directly exchange identity information with access control systems. This simple connection incorporates the security onboarding process into the tenant onboarding process and eliminates an entire redundant workflow and source of error. This dynamic link eases across numerous scenarios, from corporate HR and parking systems to campus dormitories, visitor management, health club membership and coworking spaces.

In the world before, there were only so manysecurity business functions that benefited from deep integration into your security systems. Mobile applications have multiplied the number of interesting, labor-saving, value-adding use cases for these kinds of hookups between systems that might never before had anything to do with each other.

Tenant attrition in rental properties, for example. It turns out that people are far less likely to move out of an apartment complex if they even have a handful of strong social connections with others in the same property. This fact has given rise to tenant applications, which provide rental payments infrastructure, news about the property, and announcements about upcoming social events like happy hours. All of these can operate without a connection to the physical infrastructure, but once a property manager deploys these apps for their tenants, they begin looking for ways to increase usage and convenience. (Remember the toothbrush test?) Adding API-based mobile credentials to the community apps is a straightforward way not only to increase usage but also to eliminate the problem of access card distribution, all in one stroke.
