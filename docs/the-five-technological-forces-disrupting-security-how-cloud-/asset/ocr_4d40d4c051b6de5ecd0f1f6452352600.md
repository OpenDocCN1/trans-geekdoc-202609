One of the most recent entries in this genre, the British-American TV series, Humans, which premiered in the United States in 2015 on AMC, illustrates the profound quandaries that arise from interactions with social machines. But it also illustrates the incredible utility of machines that are capable of thought and respect (most) human conventions.

In fiction, social machines look a lot like human beings, but they don't have to. It just makes them creepier. They can just well be disembodied bodies with a limited range of social interactions skills. Or they can be much larger things, like buildings.

What if the physical spaces we manage connected through social devices and became social themselves? What if our publics knew who we were and why we were there? What if public places could talk about their security concerns? Would would “social spaces,” to coin a phrase, change security around public places?

Even though the security industry is usually late to the party for technology adoption, it seems inevitable that social applications will eventually find their way into the industry. When they do, they have the potential to introduce some of the most sweeping changes we've seen since the introduction of IP technology.

Several of the likely outcomes are the following:

+   - Enabling a participatory security process
- Blending the security experience into business processes
- Improved situational awareness
- More intuitive and less obstructive interactions

# WHAT DOES IT MEAN TO BE A SOCIAL SPACE?

Three converging sets of innovations have made social spaces possible: the small, powerful embedded devices capable of machine learning, the growing web of connections between physical spaces and the cloud, and the acceptance of social identity as a legitimate form of identity credential.

Amid all discussion about social networks, social media, and social applications, you rarely find a definition of what it means to be social. We know that people are social. Animals certainly appear to be social. But can a building or a physical space be social?
