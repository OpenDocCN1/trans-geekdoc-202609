When you follow this approach of looking at frequency rather time, an entirely different set of patterns emerges. For example, it may be completely normal for something to happen four times an hour but not 20. Now, imagine having to comb through sequential data to understand whether this frequency limit was being observed or not. This is the equivalent of being a card counter in Las Vegas. If you instead imagine analytics provided a display of event frequency, all you would have to look at that number and see if it was over the limit. That's looking at things in the frequency domain.

Another type of analysis that occurs in the frequency domain is for cyclic events or cyclic norms. Daily cycles of events would be an example. In many domains from telecommunications to Internet usage to road traffic, there are cyclic patterns that repeat themselves day in and day out. Once these patterns are known, we can compare current real-time values with historical cycles and know whether things are normal or not.
