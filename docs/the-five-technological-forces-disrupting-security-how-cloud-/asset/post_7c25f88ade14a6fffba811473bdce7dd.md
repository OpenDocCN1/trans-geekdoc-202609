## Mobile Starts With Cloud

The foundation of a mobile strategy is not actually a mobile strategy: it’s a cloud strategy. Over the long haul, mobile apps can only be delivered as a cloud service.

Attempting to deliver broadly available mobile services from a local server runs into problems out of the gate. The first compromise is that you must provide a hole in your firewall. This lets mobile applications—and pesky hackers—burrow through your company’s IT perimeter to reach the back-end servers that supply data and execute commands. This architectural compromise and the ensuing vulnerabilities arise because your mobile app can’t go to a single URL, which is normally the case for widely used mobile services. Your services originate from a different address than all the other companies using app. In effect, each end-user organization needs to have its own web service-up.

You are also counting on installers to get all the cyber provisions right and every time they do. Given the vast differences in network infrastructure from one client to the next and the limited network expertise of any individual installer, the odds of getting this right time are less than 100%. Serving your own mobile population from inside your corporate network also tightly binds your mobile applications to your IT infrastructure. If it changes (and it always does), your mobile solution may stop working or need to be reconfigured, along with every single application.

Who wants that headache?

Secondly, if you try to use a security system that provides mobile services from a local computer, you will run into mobile application configuration issues. For every widely used mobile app delivered via a cloud service, each individual user can fire up the app, and it knows which URL to use in order to begin accessing services. When you buy into an app that uses a one-off server in your office or network closet, that information needs to be configured into each and every application. This is an avoidable set-up step that otherwise gets your user population off a bad start. It's bad enough with captive populations like employees, but instead of asking large tenant populations to go through this rigmarole in order to start using—wait for it—an application that's proposed to "make your security better."

A major consideration, cybersecurity. With the local server you've never exposed to the Internet, you have a 24-hour-day, 7-day-a-week hotspot inside your network, attracting cyber attacks while trying to provide security services to your employees, customers, tenants, and visitors. Does that sound like an ideal situation to you? Defending against millions of attacks against any visible IP address is best left to professionals who web services for their primary business, not as an afterthought.

Cloud services, by contrast, are behind every well-known mobile application you've heard of. Unlike local applications, they are all delivered from a single well-known URL for mobile APIs. This means that individual applications do not need to be configured for a unique web address more than your Facebook or banking apps require you to type in a long web address and port number to get them working. It's just like click, the way it should be. Because a third-party service provider delivers these mobile cloud services, you do not have to ask your IT department to do a thing; no hole in the firewall, no special SSL or TLS certificate, and no assignment of a routable IP address or port, nothing. This does not only vastly simplify establishment, but also decouples your mobile solution from the internal changes to your corporate network. Instead, your application service provider is responsible for continuity of service, along with cyber defense, which brings us to the final advantage of a cloud-based solution for mobile.

Defending against attacks is your application partner's bread and butter. Its what they do, and they are probably going to be much better at doing it full time with specialized employees than your company will. You probably don't have a full-time cybersecurity expert, let alone a whole team.
