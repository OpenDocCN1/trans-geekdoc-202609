# A BALANCED VIEW OF CYBER RISKS

The biggest hurdle for cloud computing had always been pervasive doubts about cyber security. This challenge was not unique to the security industry. In fact, cyber security has remained the prevalent reason for corporations' reluctance to move to the cloud. If physical security professionals shared this concern with their IT brethren, they hardly stood out. Few wanted to be early adopters within their own peer group, regardless of what similar groups in other market segments might be doing.

Between 2010 and 2015, however, the software industry as a whole made strides in comforting customers over the actual versus perceived risks of cloud computing. The steady stream of press articles detailing how and more large corporations were shifting major parts of their business operations over the cloud helped. Fortune 500 stalwarts announced new cloud initiatives and made them central to their new advertising campaigns. The biggest investment success stories all concerned cloud companies. There was a common thread across all of these stories that cloud computing had become safe enough for a company's most precious data.

The federal government's advocacy also played a huge role in convincing industry. The shot around the world was GSA, under CIO Casey Coleman, announced in December 2010 that it was moving its email system over the cloud. Claiming to reduce inefficiencies and lower costs by half, the shift told government contractors and system integrators that the cloud was now an approved technology. Within the year, spearheaded by the Obama administration’s first CIO, Vivek Kundra, the government put forward in February 2011 a cloud computing strategy that made “cloud first” a policy mandate for all federal computing.

Given that the government had previously operated under the labyrinth requirements of the Federal Information Security Management Act (FISMA), the emerging attitude appeared to be “If it is good enough to federal enough for us.” With this last holdout embracing a pro-cloud policy, doubters had nowhere left to hide.
