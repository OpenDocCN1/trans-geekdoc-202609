Perhaps more salient than any of these, the entire residential side of the industry is structured around a financial model that reduces upfront costs in favor of flat monthly payments, replacing capital expenditures with operating expense in the process.

Clearly, the core tenets of cloud computing and its financial benefits aren't new to the security industry. Far from it. Recurring monthly revenue (RMR)—more generally known as subscription revenue on the IT side of tracks—has the financial engine of its origins are often linked with the invention of the printing press in the 15th century. Pivotal invention lowered the cost of production—and, as importantly, the time to produce—to a level where higher publication frequencies could support predictable revenue streams. While historians just cite the printing press as one of the most influential inventions in human history, enabling subscription revenue stands as an equal accomplishment in the history of business models.

In the 20th century, the invention of alarm monitoring services did the same for security that the printing press had done for publishing five centuries earlier. They made it centralized and automated instead dispersed and manual. The technology shifted a corresponding economic shift that supports a pay-as-you-go revenue model. In this light, the security industry could claim to have invented the subscription computing model long before the current incarnation.
