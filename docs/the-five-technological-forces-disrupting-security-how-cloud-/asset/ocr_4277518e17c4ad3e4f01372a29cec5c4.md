No less than identity and access management, public key cryptography (PKI) and digital signatures were essential 15 years ago to solve the trust problems that still plague IoT. For the SmartBox, using digital signatures as a surrogate for humans solved chain-of-custody issues for the journey of a package, from consignment to carriage to customer.¹ When the consumer-driven SmartBox morphed into an access control panel, brought its X.509 digital certificates with it and became the first commercial system to do so. The use of these certificates is now widespread on networked security equipment.

Sells apparently. So does good industrial design for IoT products. IDEO, the renowned Palo Alto-based design firm and early, created the SmartBox design. The aesthetic grace of their work landed this access control system for the pages of Wired, Bloomberg, The Chicago Tribune, ABC News, and a screen performance on Morning America. The same attention to design is a hallmark of nearly every successful IoT product.

The SmartBox started out as a consumer logistics product. The uncanny forces of the market and opportunity drive it straight into consumer security. This story is not unlike, and that's the crux of the matter for the flurry of innovations transforming the product landscape today. They have become more capable of jumping across categories than ever before. They mutate from one application to another. It's all happening more quickly than the pace of traditional product design. If you are making products, that's a worry, but if you are buying design, life's never been better.

For companies or products that don't keep pace, displacement can be permanent. The SmartBox escaped that fate by staying smart but losing the box. An investment banker's insight into back-of-the-napkin drawing gave it the first push in the right direction. Later, the eureka moment was that people are like packages and buildings that are just big boxes. In a lucky irony (for me least), online commerce has just done leaving packages unprotected on doorways and porches everywhere, but security will never be offline.
