# Thread Group

Backed by the popular IoT device Nest, the Thread Group was formed to create commonality to connect and control products in the home. As such, it is likely to be more relevant to residential security applications than to commercial. That said, there is often bleed from residential to small business applications, so we may find crossing that line for security and automation as well. Like the other standards organizations, wireless communications between devices and the cloud are major focal points of the initiative. Thread's mesh communication technique makes it a natural for home automation applications but could serve equally well in larger facilities, depending on distance and construction techniques.

# Apple HomeKit

It would be remiss to discuss both Weave and Thread while leaving out least a mention of Apple's HomeKit. That said, its relevance to commercial security appears limited. As its name implies, it is intended for residential applications where it ensures that all IoT can be seamlessly integrated into the IOS platform.

For commercial and industrial applications, however, it is not a good fit. Its authorization model for device interaction is too simple for the complexities of an enterprise or building automation project. Large commercial systems need role-based permission systems that allow administrators to adjust authorizations on a fine-grained basis. Systems designed for the home do not need this level of complexity because the number of users is small and are all, almost by definition, trusted individuals.
