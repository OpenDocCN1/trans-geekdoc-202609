As a rule, the physical security industry falls somewhere around the “late majority” in this framework. As an illustration, I cite the state of industry industry in the early 2000s, just as I was getting to know this market. There was a debate raging whether Internet technology could be safe and reliable enough for security applications. People actually took up the “con” side of this argument—in print—and predicted that fat serial cables would be routed forever because Internet Protocol (IP) was not trustworthy.³ Fifteen years later, with the whole world using the Internet for everything, we can safely say that the industry got the memo and shifted to technology that is now merely 30 years old.

How has the industry afforded the luxury of being a late adopter of these-changing innovations? First, the market dynamics are somewhat sheltered by the way that end users consume these products. Small and medium businesses—the largest single market segment—purchase infrequently, know little about the brands and features, and therefore ever exert pressure on the channel to change the ways things have always been done. Myriad requirements across thousands of overlapping jurisdictions make it challenging to introduce new products that are substantially outside the envelope of what went before. And, finally, there is a natural conservatism that justifiably shies away from the perils of the bleeding edge.

A recurring theme we will see in this framework is while the physical industry is usually a late adopter of new technology, it always catches up in the end.

How will the sum of these forces affect the future of the physical industry over the course of the next 5 years? Many of these forces amplify one another, and the sum of the parts makes the current transformations compelling. We will see the biggest shift toward cloud-based services that incorporate all four other forces. In particular, this will shift the market away from those unable to make the leap and in favor of those more agile. The sum of these technologies could also be described as not only exhibiting a consumerization of commercial security, a trend that will benefit the industry by providing better tools, but also displacing many familiar practices.
