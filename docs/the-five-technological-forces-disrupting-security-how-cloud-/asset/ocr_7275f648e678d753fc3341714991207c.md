# Time and Frequency Analysis

Time and frequency are inverse twins of one another, and each have a story to tell. One who tells the better story depends on the type of data and what we need to know. They are both relevant to finding anomalies in big data sets.

In the time domain, events are viewed as a linear sequence or series. This point of view highlights certain kinds of relationships, especially those causal- is of interest and the order of events is key to understanding them. It may also highlight trends in the data and look for mathematical relationships that point to an underlying case. Trends may be caused by either a deterministic or statistical process that causes changes to the observable variables. Time-based analytics may also look for specific sequences of events and search for a signature or correlation that is worrisome or alarming based on prior experience. Finding these event sequences becomes more challenging the larger and faster the data stream, as the cyber security sector has learned all too well.
