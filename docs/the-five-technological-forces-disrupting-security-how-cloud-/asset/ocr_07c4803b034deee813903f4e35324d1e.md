# 84 CHAPTER 8: Why IoT Matters in Security

As with the Internet revolution before it, IoT will reshape the security market for decades to come. Between now and 2025, forecasters as diverse as GE², Cisco², McKinsey³, and Gartner³ estimate its economic impact in the tens of trillions of dollars. These numbers dwarf earlier technology shifts of the past 50 years like personal computing, Internet, and smartphones—innovations that irreversibly changed not only security but also everyday life.

The sheer number of connected devices is equally staggering: according to recent findings, that number had already eclipsed 3 billion in 2015 and will surge to over 20 billion by 2020. Interacting with all of these connected devices will be another 8 billion personal platforms as smartphones, tablets, watches, and PCs.

# SECURITY: ALREADY AN I BUSINESS

I would not be the first to argue that our industry has an IoT precursor since the early days of electronic security in the 1970s. That was well before the term was coined, of course, and well before IoT vaulted to the top of every growth projection in the tech industry. Coming full circle, security is universally identified as one of the three applications of IoT technology, particularly in the residential setting. Analysts have concluded that without the security function, home automation wouldn't be taking off as quickly as it has.

Security system suppliers can claim to a long lineage of smart, connected devices that would have been called IoT if the term and the Internet as we know it today existed a few decades ago. Networked digital cameras are the shining example. They've always had the characteristics of IoT products (now hailed as the next big wave in computing). Media and analysis coverage has swept consumer into the IoT bucket along with the rest of home automation.

Connected wireless locks are a second IoT precursor. They have been in the commercial use for at least 10 years since the earliest days of IoT. The category is now an IoT darling—as if discovered—with the rollout of new generations of smart(er) locks from several sexy start-ups.
