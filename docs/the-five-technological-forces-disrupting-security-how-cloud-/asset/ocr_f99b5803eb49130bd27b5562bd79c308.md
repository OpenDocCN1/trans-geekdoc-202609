# SOCIAL IS THE NEW ME

Social identities have become a growing part of how we identify ourselves on the Internet. It's almost unavoidable. As I am writing on a document in Google Docs, for example, I am logged in with the same Google+ identity that I use across other websites and services that have nothing to do Google. These other online services have simply chosen to use Google as an identity provider for the sake of convenience, security, and improved user experience. In many of these applications, I could just as well use my Facebook identity or my LinkedIn or a one-off log-in based on my favorite e-mail account.

All of these examples are commonly known as social sign-on—using your identity from a social network provider or your identity on an unrelated website. The social network takes care of authenticating when you first log in and then lets secondary sites know that you are who you say you are and that you've authorized access to resources on that secondary site.
