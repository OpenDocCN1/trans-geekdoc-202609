# CLOUD SECURITY REDUX...BUT WORTH

The cyber challenges to physical security deployments of IoT devices remind me of the early days of cloud computing and the security hurdles—real and imagined—that faced the first decade of cloud computing (1999–2009, by reckoning), its number one challenge was to convince buyers that the cloud was at least as safe as their own data centers or on-premise servers. It was easy to make the case in logical terms, but many buyers had to overcome a big psychological barrier. The root issue is people feel intrinsically more secure and in control when they have something in their physical possession. If I can see it and touch it, I can protect it. It is also easier to trust your own employees than the faceless minions some distant data center. Both of these contribute to a false sense of security around the alleged benefits of keeping security computing inside your building.

Hacking does not respect the physical location of the computing environment. Your data center, my data center—it doesn't matter whether it's public or private. Or does hacking respect the logo on the employee badge of the system administrator? Your staff, my staff—the only question who adheres to the best cybersecurity practices. Usually, that jump ball goes to the employees of cloud data centers or SaaS companies because it's the only thing they do at. Their livelihood and their companies' success depend on being good at. At large cloud companies, the number of cybersecurity experts can easily run into the thousands. "Bob" in the next cube is no match for them.

Now that we have placed one collective foot into the IoT camp, cybersecurity issues are resurfacing. This time, they are at a much larger scale and with bigger remediation challenges. What cloud has going for it that all of its virtualized computer systems are under some form of governance structure (at least in principle) and capable of being managed en masse.
