# BIOMETRICS & MULTIFACTOR AUTHENTICATION

Mobile platforms have solved a problem that has vexed the industry for years: how to get users to adopt the higher quality authentication biometrics provide. For as long as I have been in the industry, experts have been predicting that biometrics was “just about to take off.” The promise has come sound like airline announcements in a plane stuck on the tarmac: 15 minutes say that the plane is about to take off while we wait for hours.

Sometimes we have to switch planes before we can take off, that’s not a bad analogy for what’s happening with biometrics right now. Since ever technologies like fingerprint and retinal scanning have become effective and practical in a small electronic package, they’ve intrigued customers. On the surface, they appear high-tech, convenient, and trustworthy. They eliminate the problem of lost access cards. I can’t count the number of times customers have asked for the same biometric technology they see on TV and would like to implement for their bank, their condo association, their office building, or even the refrigerator in the break room, until they hear the price. At that point, the smile leaves their face, and they decide to settle for conventional card access.

The big uptick in biometric adoption occurred when phone-based fingerprint recognition was introduced during the early 2010s. These platforms provided a clear path toward the widespread use of biometrics—just not on the dedicated fingerprint scanners the industry had expected.

Smartphone biometrics cover two important use cases: logging into a security management application and using a mobile credential to enter a restricted area. Until biometrics became part of our mobile platforms, logging into a security management app faced all of the same password management and cybersecurity considerations as any other online log-in. Lost, stolen, or overly simple passwords created a gap that compromised our assets. The issue is even more pronounced in the case of mobile credential authentication because of the high usage frequency we see for most access control settings.
