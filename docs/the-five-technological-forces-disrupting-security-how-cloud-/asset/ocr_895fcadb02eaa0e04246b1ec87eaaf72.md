The concept of presence begins with our security ecosystems sensing where we are and what security services might be relevant. This isn't just a matter of repetitive surveillance. When we approach an entrance, for example, the system can open a door or the building gate for us or simply announce our presence. Presence services continue when we favor the lobby and the intelligent dispatch algorithms automatically summon an elevator already programmed to take us to our usual floor. When we enter our offices, presence takes notes and marks us down as "in" for convenience of our colleagues ("Steve here yet?"); for safety ("we have a leak, has everyone cleared the building?"); and the occupancy algorithms ("what kind of foot traffic do our elevators have?).

Presence applications provide automated or background interactions with our environments. They require a user to download an app, register under their assigned identity, and be authenticated at one or more levels depending on the services in question. In access control, for example, presence might mean having your smartphone in your pocket is enough to activate and unlock doors, elevators, and other service points in your environment. Presence services contrast with other security and mobile credentials without requiring the user to take any explicit action. They run in the background on your smartphone, always active. Most importantly, because your identity has been registered in all these cases, movements and interactions create an identifiable individual history of your presence within a given environment. Presence is simply a matter of being. We produce value and create interactions by showing up.

# PASSIVE SURVEILLANCE

The flip side of presence is passive surveillance, often perceived as the evil twin of all those positive services that come with the ambulatory radio beacon our phones have become. The very networks of wireless sensors that provide conveniences for everyone uses public spaces can also keep track of everything we do while there. In the real world, as it is online, surveillance comes at the price of a good user experience. You want to be relevant while surfing the web? Let me tracking cookies in your browser. You want a physical ecosystem that responds to you as an individual? Let me install a mobile identifier that tells me who you are. You want to know who is in your building and where they are? Convince them of the benefits of your magical app.

Passive surveillance, as its name implies, requires no knowledge or participation from the user. It does not require an app download or user registration.
