The ability to manage user populations—that is, to grant and revoke access permissions—also remains a core capability of most mobile apps. The data my company collects to improve application performance reveal that user management is one of the most frequent uses of security technology, comprising nearly 40% of all log-ins.

Then, of course, we have viewing video surveillance via mobile. The severity of almost any alert—whether originating from the system or a call from a colleague or employee—can be investigated with a look at what's happening on video. Do you really have a break-in? Or did someone work late and forget to disarm the system before leaving? This use of remote video has improved dramatically over the past 5 years as cellular networks added more bandwidth and as compression standards become more widely implemented across mobile platforms.

Minor administrative tasks such as configuring notifications, setting up schedules, and managing holidays; duration of video storage; event retention policies; hundreds of infrequent tasks have yet to cross the chasm from desktop to smartphone.
