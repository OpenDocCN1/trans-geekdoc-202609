From a data science point of view, the fun is just beginning once you've amassed a large quantity of data or high-velocity data streams. There are many methods for analyzing big data, from classification to regression to predictive analytics. Conveniently, there are also many software tools available to data explorers that can help map algorithms with insights. One of the biggest enablers is that big-data-as-a-service (BDataaS) platforms are now available to outsource the burdensome tasks of high-velocity computing and high-volume storage management. This means that your organization can focus on what you want to know, not on how you assemble the plumbing. In case it wasn't obvious, the emergence of BDataaS platforms is just one chapter in the cloud story, in which providers have continued to move up the application software stack with ready-made services that can be used on demand.

Whatever the underlying technologies or tools, the goal of analytics is to find patterns hidden deep inside the data. Many of us have had at least exposure to pattern discovery in science classes, in statistics courses, or in a business setting. Some of the methods you will see here should be familiar because are calculations you probably performed on a calculator or spreadsheet. Others will be totally new (unless you are a data scientist) because they can only be performed by advanced software on large data sets. Then, we are actually exposed to the results of these kinds of analysis on the web and by the news media every day in form of stock market analysis, polling data, and many other summaries of complex data. All of this exposure has had the positive effect of raising data literacy and increasing the use of analytics across the board.

Patterns come in—well many patterns. Some are numerical, some are casual, and some are relational, others are fuzzy but well nonetheless. For the purpose of showing how big data can be helpful in security, there are several types of patterns we will examine:

+   * Statistical measures: values, trends, and deviations
* Clustering and association
* Time and frequency
