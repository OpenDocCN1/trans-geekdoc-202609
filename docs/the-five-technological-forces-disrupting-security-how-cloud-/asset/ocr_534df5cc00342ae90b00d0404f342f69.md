# All Security Is Now Cybersecurity

The 2015 annual ASIS security conference in Anaheim was graced by southern California's usual endless sunshine and the radiance of nearby Disneyland. Wandering the conference grounds, knowing that Mickey and Goofy were just down the street, I found it difficult to feel the weight of the world's security problems that day.

Inside the conference halls, however, a much darker mood prevailed. The topic that dominated nearly every conversation was the cybersecurity of electronic devices and indeed the entire realm of IoT. The publicized July 2015 hacking of a Jeep Grand Cherokee—while driving down the road, no less—was fresh in everyone's mind. The hack was conducted under controlled circumstances and performed as something of a stunt, but its relevance could not be ignored. It was a clear case of a cyber attack causing physical damage. The occupants of the vehicle were at risk, of but so was anything and anyone in its path. The ability to remotely pilot suicide vehicles was chilling. The event made it too clear that physical security will become impossible unless we figure out how to defend against cyber attacks.

Cyber attacks against connected devices can have much larger physical targets. The attackers infiltrated the corporate network of the steel mill via email phishing attacks on employees, triggering them into actions that ultimately gave the attackers access to compromised systems.
