# DOT-COM REDUX IN PHYSICAL SECURITY

The transformation of IT and the US economy as a whole to the cloud took place quickly and chaotically during the dot-com era that it was hard to observe. Even for those of us in the middle of it, there was more going on than anyone could follow all on. I recall the breathless coverage of The Industry Standard, a weekly that called itself the news magazine of Internet. New companies sprang up and died before they could celebrate their first birthday. The “new economy” had hard cash, and “changed all the rules,” and “eyeballs” are counted more valuable than cold. Exorbitant rates were a badge of honor. Suddenly, they weren’t, and the party was over.

I expect to see a replay of certain dynamics of that era in the physical security industry over the next 5 years –without that awful crash part at the end. It will not be quite so encompassing, but it will be every bit as transformative and beneficial for the security consumer. The “new economy” will see the steady growth of market share for cloud companies at the expense of those who lagged behind a bit too. It will emphasize customer acquisition, as the original land grab for market share in each subset of the new economy. It will emphasize innovation, new paths to the market, and online purchasing, as we saw in the first shift from bricks and mortar to bricks and clicks.

Security companies are increasingly threading cloud language through their marketing messages for fear of being behind. Meanwhile, product teams with multillion dollar investments in huge legacy code bases struggle to deliver a cloud-like experience without sacrificing their company’s cash cow. New entrants and nontraditional suppliers have moved into the cloud space at a faster pace than legacy providers, making for a territorial squabble with far more players than anyone ever expected.

Because of these dynamics, the security industry represents an echo of the first wave of the new economy. It gives us another chance to look into the petri dish and watch a new life form emerge. This time, though, we know how the experiment will turn out and where to place our bets.
