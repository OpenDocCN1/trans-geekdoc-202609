The big difference is that device connectivity used to be a special feature—one that you had to select or perhaps guided your product choices in the first place. What's new in the IoT era is that every electronic device is a connected device. Every new television is a connected television. Every new car is a connected car. All the new coffee makers are connected coffee makers. Every security device is a connected security device. It's no longer a choice. It's part of the fabric of our products. Even the people who don't actively choose it will have it anyway. Connectivity will become embedded, inescapable part of anything that uses electricity and many that never did before.

# THE INDUSTRIAL INTERNET OF THINGS

The Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) is the name for a grab bag of nonconsumer applications of IoT technology—health care, transportation, energy, smart cities, smart highways, factory floor, supply chain, infrastructure, mining, dozens of heavy industries, and drumroll—building automation and security. It has its own associations, consortia, conferences, reference architectures, cyber manifests, consulting practices, software platforms, and its own lobbyists.

The term was introduced in 2011 and has become a rallying point for many corporate applications of IoT. Word is it's going to be huge. Thirty-nine percent of the total population of IoT devices over the next 5 years will be in an enterprise or industrial setting. It is predicted to become a multillion dollar market all on its own.

We're paying attention to it here because the use of IoT in commercial security shares many of the same concerns and goals with the IoT community. We are both more concerned with system architecture and standards. We are both more concerned with device authentication and cyber hardening. We are both concerned with availability and network recovery. This is not to suggest that consumer product companies are heedless of these issues; it's just that IIoT products operate in more complex ecosystems and face additional challenges that simply aren't present in a home environment.

The Industrial Internet Consortium (IIC) is a trade group chartered to bring together companies and technologies that can accelerate the growth of IIoT through standards, best practices, and education.
