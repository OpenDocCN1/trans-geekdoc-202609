In addition to showing up your cybersecurity defenses, phone-based biometrics also address a significant user experience consideration affecting user adoption: fear of Big Brother. Even people who aren't privacy freaks tend to be uneasy about having their fingerprint captured by a third party and then stored somewhere beyond their control. You can explain to people that biometric scanners aren't actually storing an image or replica of your fingerprint or retina. face. You can tell them what they're actually storing something called a biometric template, which is a mathematical representation of the salient aspects of their biometric sample. But none of that matters. At a gut level, many of us have not progressed beyond biotechnological cultures that believe taking a photograph steals your soul. We don't want our fingerprints stored on a company server, but we're fine with fingerprints being stored on our phones—as if being able to carry the “server” in our pocket makes it more secure.

# TRANSFORMATION

So far, mobile technologies have been more transformative than disruptive. The split between disruption versus transformation falls along the dividing line between administrative applications, which will be transformative, and occupant-based applications, which will be disruptive.

The administrative mobile apps introduced in commercial security over the past 5 years do the same things as their desktop forebears and are proved by the same vendors. They've moved a bunch of administrative features onto the portable platform that Internet users now treat as primary. So, it's old wine in a new flask.

The disruptive effects of mobile applications come from what I occupant applications. Today, the most common occupant application is mobile credentialing, as we've talked about extensively. As more features get bundled into basic credentialing, these occupant apps will drive strong and preferences for the “haves” versus the “have-nots.” Chief among the occupant features interactive security in which civilians (users) play a role by exchanging information with the security team.
