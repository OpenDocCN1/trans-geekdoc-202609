# AN IoT STORY

At the turn of the millennium, less than 3% of all Americans had broadband at home. In an environment of such patchwork connectivity, it was far from certain that homeowners understood the power of a smart appliance—or what would be called an Internet of things (IoT) device. It needed an always-on Internet connection and an always-up cloud service, both of which were the experience of the vast majority of consumers. It is difficult to imagine how a novel connected device was at the time, but they were so rare that there wasn't an agreed upon category to describe them. The term IoT had just recently been coined in 1999, but no one had heard it, and it enjoyed none of the buzzword status that it has acquired.
