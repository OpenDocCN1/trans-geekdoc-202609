# CHAPTER 20: The Secrets of the Universe

Outside of security, social log-in has a healthy degree of adoption as a single identity that can be used across multiple contexts. Some 80% of us have used social log-in to create new accounts on the Internet and subsequently log in through the same mechanism. However, it is unclear whether this mechanism will retain power in the face of mobile log-ins that use biometrics (e.g., finger-prints) as credentials for an increasing number of apps.

Summarizing all of these factors, social is rated as innovator or early adopter (aka visions) for both residential and commercial uses. The bottom line is that social log-in has not crossed the chasm to mass adoption—least not for security applications. My guess is that it will end up being stranded a while longer.

But it's not all bad news for social in security. Recall that social does have a growing presence in public safety and mass notifications, as well as personal safety check-ins not related to electronic security. In these cases, the very properties of social networks make them a little messy for directed communications actually make them useful for fuzzy notifications to perhaps reach more than intended. The information bleeds from “Friends” to “friends of friends” that is what you want when you are screaming “fire!” in a crowded theater. You want to people in the lobby to hear and get the out.

# MY GENERATION

There's no way to say this without the risk of ageism, but some aspects of adoption are simply a generational. Young people get it; old people don't. Today, means millennials are the primary vehicles that will drive the five forces for the rest of the way into the market. This truth has a major factor in my experience of how and where they have shaped the industry.

One of the clearest examples comes from my own sales who spend most of their time with the integrator community. They can pitch to a mouthful of commercial sales reps that cloud and IoT are the way forward for security services and devices. At one desk sits an integrator veteran who proclaims with confidence and bluster, “My customers will never use that technology. There's no way they will store their data off-site, and no way they will pay a penny fee.” At the next desk sits a fresh-faced college grad who, upon hearing the patch, says “Yeah, I get it. That is everything is now.

What you can depend on what you believe, which is why the younger reps with no negative bias toward new technology are usually more successful representing these new technologies. What's true at the individual level is also true at the company level, with newer system integrators staking their business on being 100% cloud-based.
