# THE DESKTOP REINCARNATED

Do you remember the dark ages before mobile? To look at our stock portfolio, to shop online, or just to check our e-mail, we had to sit at our desk staring at a full-size monitor using Internet Explorer. I can't even imagine going back to that time. Many of the administrative applications for security systems still work that way. Happily, they are migrating by one to mobile. The transition pays security teams, business owners, and system integrators—the entire paybill of actors in the security ecosystem—greater latitude to perform their work from anywhere, anytime. As that happens, we mostly see the desktop reincarnated on our mobile devices.

While administrative applications have become increasingly integrated, they usually align with the three main disciplines of electronic security: access control, video surveillance, and intrusion detection. In general, these applications provide a window into security data and the means to control different parts of the security ecosystem. An access control application, for example, give you the power to unlock a door remotely. A video surveillance application would show you live video, or perhaps let pan, tilt, and zoom over cameras to redirect their focus.

The features first to be recreated on mobile platforms say a lot about types of data and control people consider most important. Personally, I find this a refreshing turn of events because it cuts through the bloat and brings us back to the fundamentals security.

Take the classic activity log—a list of every event that has taken place within the purview of the user—still front and center in many security mobile apps. It has long served as an anchoring point for anyone logging into a security management system and continues, even in its mobile iteration. Activity logs resemble nothing so much as an undigested dump of raw data. They were never made for a good user experience, even with all the screen real estate of a desktop.
