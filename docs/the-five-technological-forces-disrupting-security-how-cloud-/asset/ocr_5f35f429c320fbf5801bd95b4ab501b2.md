# ONE KEY TO RULE ALL

The past 5 years have seen the success of numerous mobile apps that allow both residential and commercial users to open doors—or smart locks to be precise—with their smartphones. In the consumer setting, in fact, smart locks not gain much foothold until smart apps made them convenient enough to outweigh the extra expense and learning curve. Presmartphone electronics let relive the maddening programming sag of the VCR, requiring users to master an inscrutable keypad with finger-fretzel keys—only Rain Man could

Smartphones changed all of that—so much that when I cautiously installed our home's first smart lock, replete with sinister glass facade and synthetic voice, I worried my wife would say she wanted nothing of the sort on her front door. To my pleasant surprise, she quickly became its biggest fan, evangelizing the benefits to anyone who faced the same challenges of sharing keys with housekeepers, dog walkers, overnight guests, and the kid down the street who waters our plants when we go out of town.

The app for this lock communicates via some mysterious set of protocols that travel invisibly over the airwaves and usually don't matter to the owner. At least, not until a smart lock enters. Her new lock comes from the same manufacturer and connects to the same backend cloud management system, her new-familiar mobile app won't work. So she downloads a second door-unlocking app, just for that particular lock. When the office she works in adds this technological marvel to our front doors, she'll add a third app and so on until she has an explosion of apps on her phone, with dozens of apps that all do exactly the same thing, recognize her and unlock the lock for

Nobody wants dozens of apps or "keys" on their phone. That justifies the problem of a gigantic keychain in digital form. Soon, you forget which app goes with which building, and they become more hassle than them.

This is the problem that confronted me as I began to think about how my company could introduce electronic keys at a larger scale than anyone had previously imagined. As chairman of the SIA Standards Committee, I also had another reason to think about this at the scale of making these new credentials more efficient across the entire industry. I wanted something that would work for millions or billions of people without them having to think about which app they needed to. I imagined a global system that worked as fluidly as the payments systems behind the credit and ATM cards we use on a daily basis. We never have to think about which card works at which store or whether our card came from a particular bank. They all work everywhere. No thought involved.
