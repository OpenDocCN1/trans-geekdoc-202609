IoT technologies have made these sensors better, cheaper, and easier to use than ever before. The IoT devices, cameras, detection systems, identity tools, and net or Bluetooth analytics provide all types of data highly relevant for security analysis and action. The temptation is to put them everywhere. That's a great idea until it comes to make sense of it all. New data from IoT systems will only be useful to security managers when it becomes understandable and accessible. That means analytics, applications, and some degree of system integration between the multiple SaaS systems that will mostly likely come with different types of IoT networks.

The same goes for the other sources of IoT data harvested and analyzed for security purposes. We can only use if we have API-based software platforms that can talk to all these new sources. This may require looking outside boundaries and understanding that some of the solutions from other domains are better than what we can build. Another way, simply due to size, there is more innovation happening outside of the physical security industry inside, and we would be foolish to ignore it.

# CHEAPER, SMALLER, AND MORE DISCRET

Motion detection electronics are a great example of IoT products that can now be manufactured for well under $10. They improve energy management because they can determine energy needs at the individual room level based on occupancy. They have expanded the market for energy management not only because they are inexpensive but also because they are easy to retrofit, unlike the complex industrial building controls that preceded them.

While these low-cost IoT sensors might be justified by their energy-saving ROI, once installed, they can drive additional data into our risk management equations—if, that we have systems that can take advantage of them. The best bet for seeing these systems emerge is in the cloud, with incremental analytic capability is very inexpensive to supply.

The rise of IoT is further driving the ability to track motion and presence by making Bluetooth and Wi-Fi an “always on” choice for most smartphone users, the IoT flip side to our discussion of mobility and presence earlier.
