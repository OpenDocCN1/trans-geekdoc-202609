# IDENTITY AS A SERVICE

The plethora of identity systems in our digital economy have fostered the creation of many competing identity systems over the last dozen or so years. Digital identity services are needed whenever a person wants to get something done—like being authenticated to get into a building—based on who they are. Determining that they are who they say they are requires an authoritative, trusted source.

In its simplest form, this would be an identity database that keeps lists of people and their privileges. Inside the theory, these organizations or services are known as identity providers or IdPs. Your local DMV, for example, is an IdP of the old-fashioned sort. In general, IdPs can be any organization or software system that provides a specific set of services for identity transactions. Specifically, they are a service provider that manages identity information for a community of users, provides authentication services on demand, and does so across a group of establishments that recognize and trust their authority.

When IdPs are part of B2B transactions, for example, they usually fall under corporate auspices. In these cases, my identity is identity as an employee. My use of this identity is limited to the roles I assume as part of my job. By definition, I will not use this same identity to transact any personal business. For reason, I will not be concerned with privacy issues around this identity (although my employer may) none of my personal attributes are associated with this identity. The attributes are those of employee #1234. It's not me.

However, when a private individual I use an identity for my own personal business, the whole picture changes. I am suddenly concerned with privacy and the use of any personal data associated with my identity. I don't want companies (or criminals) to use this information in ways I have not approved.

In many respects, these identity systems are a big improvement over existing large-scale public identity schemes, and they are certainly an improvement over having nothing at all. From a practical point of view, using them requires more intellectual investment than the average layperson cares to make.
