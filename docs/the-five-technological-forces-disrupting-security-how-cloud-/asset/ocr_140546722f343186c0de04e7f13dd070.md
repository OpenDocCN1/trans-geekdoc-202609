# BIG SECURITY DATA AND ROI

There are two fundamental criteria that face every big data project, and they will look familiar to anyone who has tried to justify an IT project or any corporate spending initiative. The first is whether your data sources will support the objectives of your project. You will need to have the right kind of data, enough of it, and be able to trust it. If the data are not, you're wasting your time because the oldest adage in computer science still holds—garbage in out. Given the right data and the right analytics, what is the ROI you expect? What outcome would you need in order to make the investment worthwhile? In the world of security, this question can be answered in two very different ways.

In the first case—a purely financial ROI analysis—we can use a retail example. The scenario might be something like this: if Bob and Alice's Key Store invests $1 million in a big data project, they will realize that in reduced shrinkage (and how soon will they realize these savings?) Let's say, for the sake of argument, that their big data project can reduce shrinkage by 2% per year they lose $5 million worth of product every year. This means that they would save $100,000 per year, and it would take 10 years to recover their investment. It's not clear that this would be a great investment for them or they might find more economic means of accomplishing their objective. However, if their data science project could save them 10% per year then it would only take 2 years to recover their investment, and they would come out every year after that. Clearly worth it.

In the second case—a life safety ROI analysis—we are confronted immediately how to place a value on human life. If a million big data project could save one life per year, is it worth the investment? What if it's only one life every 3 years? Every 10? What about the other spending priorities? Security organizations make these difficult choices about resources and outcomes every day. Big data won't eliminate these difficult choices, but it may provide us with better insights on probabilities and risk factors so that we are optimally allocating our resources.
