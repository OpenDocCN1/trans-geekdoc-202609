# WHAT'S IN A TOKEN?

Physical identity documents face a challenge in the digital era. How do you use them to reliably verify someone's identity online? That's why theft is such a problem. In the physical world, physical documents serve us well most of the time. We can examine them and have a pretty good idea of whether they are real or forged. We can also be fairly assured that there is only one copy of each such credential, because the issuers do a pretty good job of following a one-person, one-document protocol. In the digital world, these start to fall apart. All everyone needs the data from your identity documentation, not the documents themselves, to assume your identity online.

The same is also true of the digital credentials we commonly use, log-in and password. It is no secret that passwords are fundamentally broken. If you do not notice soon enough, they can compromise your online accounts, and if you don not they can compromise your cash, your reputation, and your place of business. With security systems moving irreversibly to the cloud and a state of constant connection, the industry has joined the rest of the online world and begun to take seriously the issues of online identity and authentication. But it's a journey of a thousand miles, and we've just taken the first steps.

Never before in physical security have the issues of identity and authentication been intertwined as they are now. For many years, our internal software systems were on landlocked corporate networks with no connection to other networks. The ability to log was limited by access to the network and, more likely than not, the need to have a proprietary client installed on your computer. Sure, we all worried about the possibility of rogue employees and the tiny cracks in our armor posed by dial-up modems, but those threats were minor in comparison with the authentication challenge before us now.

Access cards and PIN codes have been entrusted for authentication at the perimeter of our security systems where we manage tenants and employees, access to buildings, and other valuable corporate assets.
