The first IP camera had been invented a handful of years before but represented only single-digit market share at the time, with analog cameras still the vast majority of sales. The same held true for door controllers—the core technology for access control—which relied on dial-up modems for remote management.

IP was so novel, in fact, when I heard a company at the 2002 ISC West trade show in Las Vegas boasting that they had an "IP control panel," I felt compelled to do some corporate espionage and find out what they were up to. (We thought we were the only ones.) After turning my name badge inside out, on their new product. What I found inside was, in equal measure, shocking and hilarious: they had used Velcro to hide a "print server”—a decade-old, quintessentially 1990s technology—inside their product. Velcro!

That’s my enduring image of technology adoption in this market: always behind but always finding a way to catch up.
