This phenomenon demonstrates a natural willingness to participate and contribute to public safety. We can channel this volunteerism with directed interactive apps as a feature of mobile security apps. The tools are all there.

Until recently, what's been missing is compelling reason for building occupants, employees, and residents to download a security-related app onto their smartphones. Even the first generation of security apps mobilized administrative tasks, building users were left out of the equation. At the time, there was no obvious reason to put an app in the hands of anyone beyond the security team or building management. Mobile credentials displace traditional access cards and key fobs, there is an opportunity for interactive security to become an important part of the mobile security ecosystem.

Interactive security apps for building users have the potential to close the gap between security organizations closer to the populations they serve. Security is often invisible by design, but there are aspects of it that work in view. In a typical commercial setting, security teams must establish the overall security program, issue employee badges, manage access cards, staff guards, and oversee visitor management. In retail, loss prevention staff concern themselves with all manner of shrinkage, and employee threat the top of the list. In large public venues, its spectators, loiterers, audience members, and everything in-between.

Mobile apps for building occupants can also improve the "customer experience" of security in a commercial setting. Health clubs and other membership-based organizations, for example, have found that embedding the access control function into their management system and membership apps reduces the complexity and inconvenience of keeping two separate systems synchronized. Property management firms have found embedding the security function into their tenant applications turns them into an amenity rather than a burden.

In my experience, applications like these have received high ratings from mobile credentials and related apps that serve as a direct substitute for earlier technologies that people already know how to use. These new apps provide unarguably superior performance, at lower cost, with ROI measured in months rather than years.
