In the case of IoT systems, there is usually a local wireless network just for IoT devices (although some use Wi-Fi directly). Such networks have their own protocols designed to maximize communication efficiency for the IoT devices themselves. However, these networks still need to be connected to the Internet. Otherwise, there’s no “I” in IoT. This is where the gateway comes in. It translates between the local wireless protocols and the Internet at large.

Let's take a deeper look at why this is

First, not all wireless networks can be connected directly to the Internet. Wi-Fi is a familiar local area network that uses the same protocols as the Internet. That’s why it can be connected directly with just a wireless access point. Many wireless networks designed for IoT, however, do not use the Internet Protocol for internal communications. They have their own protocols and schemes that need to be translated at multiple levels to communicate.

Second, gateways are alone responsible for internet or mesh communication among the devices on that same wireless network. This capability permits devices to continue operating locally if the Internet is unavailable. Sensors communicate with control panels on the same wireless network, for example, they may not send alarms outside the building, but they can alert people inside.

Finally, gateways take care of various housekeeping tasks such as device registration, power management, and peer-to-peer communication between other devices on the network. In practice, the wireless communications for the IoT system are all bundled into the same network appliance as the gateway functionality.

The trick is be able to put together a system where all the IoT devices use a single communication standard and a single type of gateway. Alternatively, multiprotocol gateways fill the need for mixed networks that still have the advantages of installing (and pay for) a single physical gateway. They also offer a degree of future-proofing that allows more product choices that span multiple types of networks.

# WIRELESS WHIRLWIND

One of the most head-spinning areas of IoT technology is wireless communication. The innovation has been prodigious in recent years, and there are now more choices than ever. In the security industry, we rely on several wireless protocols to connect things like cameras, intrusion sensors, and card readers. IoT devices use some of not only the same protocols but also new and different ones. The new families of protocols provide more features and more security than their predecessors.
