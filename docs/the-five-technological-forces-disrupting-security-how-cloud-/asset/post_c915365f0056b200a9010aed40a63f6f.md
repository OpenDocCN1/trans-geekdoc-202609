
# What Can Mobile Do for Me?

Smartphones save countless lives throughout the world every year. They can provide critical communications during times of crisis, medical emergency, natural disaster, and even terrorism. Yet, we have not fully harnessed that power in physical security applications. The phone can always be asking "what's" as a side channel to law enforcement or a corporate communication center, but we have no holistic approach to fully exploiting mobile application capabilities.

Still in their first generation, mobile security apps lack maturity and a full realization of the power at their disposal. This is not an indictment, by any means, but rather a promise of things to come.

# WHAT'S IN IT FOR ME?

We've looked at what mobile is doing for the overall security ecosystem, but you may still be asking "what is it for me?" The answer, of course, depends on you and your company's roles in the security value chain. Suppliers focus on customer satisfaction and product utility. Integrators concern themselves with deployment, usability, and cost of support. Security teams and business owners care about the real issues of overall effectiveness and whether their people will actually use the tools they provide. That said, we're all pulling together, so we hope to find similar answers across the entire spectrum.

We should always start with the user. This is where the rubber hits the road (or where the thumb hits the glass). Our first goal should be to fully engage that user and provide exactly what they need. This is the moment of truth for whether someone will use the application a second or third time. Earlier, we introduced the toothbrush test. Every time someone uses an app determines whether it will rise to twice-a-day before it becomes a part of their lives.

Many of the benefits of mobile security applications are readily apparent: access to information wherever and whenever; the ability to respond immediately in a crisis; remote control of critical systems; mobile credentials with participatory features; and, last but not least, the ability to coordinate teams across space and time.

How do these features translate into specific benefits for security teams and business owners? How do they change the game for integrators and service providers? What do we have to look forward to as these applications continue to evolve? Many of these questions have begun to find answers as the rollout of mobile apps takes off the commercial security industry.

For the remainder of this chapter, we will look at the mobile technology trends we discussed earlier to see what they deliver to the point of use: not a theoretical view, but a field test on the practical utility and benefit of investing in mobile security applications.

# REAL-TIME SITUATIONAL AWARENESS

Many cite situational awareness as one of mobile security applications' primary benefits. This should not come as a big surprise. Situational awareness is part of our core mission, and it's something mobile apps do really well. Perhaps, you never thought about apps in quite those terms. We don't think of shopping apps, for example, as giving real-time situational awareness about what's on sale. Now do we cast the benefits of instant messaging apps in quite those terms. That's exactly what they do. So it is with security applications. They provide users real-time information about the world around them wherever they may be.

This ability to inform—provide situational awareness—is why the spread of security-related apps into the hands of everyday users is so important. It brings them into the security equation. Seemingly, single-purpose apps like mobile credentials gave a pull-through strategy for additional modes of interaction, even if they were initially adopted for convenience.

The first generation of commercial physical security applications has been slower to post rapid gains in daily usage than vertical business markets. They've had an uphill climb because the back-end information systems have been slow to move to the cloud. Their residential counterparts haven't had this problem. Instead, they have quickly become a daily habit for the early adopters in the home automation space, who use their apps dozens of times per day.

They've also seen penetration for cultural and institutional reasons. Until mobile credentials gave security organizations a reason to widely deploy mobile apps, they dealt with user groups of only a handful of authorized employees. That's just the way they were built. Now that the cloud is gaining traction among commercial systems, we see that it can change the way we engage with our security systems and the people we protect.

What exactly does this combination of real-time situational awareness and user engagement accomplish for the security organization? The answer depends somewhat on scale, so let's look at several classes of organizations.

# SMALL BUSINESS

Let's start out at the small end of the scale and consider what this capability does for individual business owners. It best be compared with similar changes in the residential security market. As a homeowner, I used to have very little idea of what the infrastructure systems in my home were doing and no ability to control them remotely. Improvements in mobile applications and the concurrent development of IoT devices for the home created natural synergies that gave my family visibility and control now. Almost overnight, we were able to see and control dozens of aspects of home security and environmental management. Our situational awareness includes who comes and gone from the house, whether the doors are locked, and how the thermostats are set. Plus, we can look in on the dog we want.

Small business owners have enjoyed an equally dramatic transformation, as residential applications have crossed into the SMB category where needs are not so far off those of homeowners. While there is a large overlap between home automation tools and commercial security applications, the latter provide additional types of controls and features not needed in the home. The sum of these totals has given business owners a picture of the state of their business while they are away from.

While security considerations usually provide the reason for the installation of these systems, most owners find operational benefits as well. For example, retail users report that their video surveillance allows them to see how well their staff attends to customers in the store. This tells them how well their training programs work and who they should be coaching a bit more. It also get real-time feedback on how many people are in their shop and whether staffing meets demands.

# MULTILOCATION BUSINESSES

Anyone who operates a multilocation business knows that you can't be everywhere at once. Franchise owners, chain retail, property management, state and local government, and many other distributed operations place security managers in the position of having to know what's going on in more than one place at a time. Mobile security applications help ease or erase a big part of that challenge.

One of the earliest customers for my company's cloud-based services was a McDonald's franchise owner who had five locations. He wanted to make sure each of his managers opened their respective stores by 4:30 a.m. Every morning, he sat in front of his browser with a cup of coffee and watch the activity log scroll before him. If a store had not been accessed on time, he was on the phone to find out why and fix the problem. Fast forward to the present. Owners can have this degree of insight into their operations, but they are no longer tethered to a desktop browser. This franchise owner can now enjoy this insight from his smartphone while still in bed or traveling on vacation.

A related challenge for multifacity businesses is synchronization of data across multiple security systems. Prior to cloud-based systems, many businesses were saddled with having an independent security system installed at each physical location. The systems didn't share information with each other.

In practice, this meant that a person who had permission at one facility might not have permission at other facilities (unless their data were laboriously entered at each site again and again). The workload, duplication of data was error-prone, particularly when people left an organization and their data were not deleted from all systems. This is where mobile and the cloud systems behind it address this challenge. One of the most common events was that the security administrator or HR department dashing to the security system is employee departure, particularly a disgruntled one. Who wants the nightmare of trying to remove privileges from all sites, especially when those sites sit hundreds of miles apart? With a mobile application backed by a cloud-based security system, you can take care of it in a single swipe.
