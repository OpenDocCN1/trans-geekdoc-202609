# CHAPTER 20: The Secrets of the Universe

Mobile. Mobile is further along the technology adoption curve than any of the other technologies we've examined. That's not surprising, because in general it is the most consumerized, accessible, and globally penetrated. To be at their most effective, mobile applications require a cloud backend. The willingness to fully engage cloud services is once again the underlying factor in differences between commercial and residential adoption.

In residential systems, mobile applications are nearly universal for the reason that cloud is nearly universal; they are the best way to deliver an interactive experience. Every interactive consumer security platform of scale has a mobile app—without exception. The widespread availability of mobile-enabled products has all but eliminated the scales of systems that do not have them. That's the textbook definition of technology adoption by laggards; they buy it when the older alternatives are simply longer available in the market. Security systems without mobile apps become the rotary phone of the residential security market.

No so in commercial security, although it is catching up. Customer premise systems have been adding a few morsels of mobile capability. (I have), you'll wish you hadn't. Where there is cloud, there is mobile, and where there is not, it's frankly bit of a hack. The effect is friction and resistance to rapid adoption.

For these reasons, commercial security systems have not moved as quickly as residential systems and have only reached the early adopter stage so far.

IoT. Depending on how you define IoT and the relevant market segment, identical security systems are somewhere between early adopters and late majority.

Recall that earlier I made the case that the security industry is the original IoT pioneer. In this respect, nearly every device in a modern security system is an IoT device, connected back to the Internet through a gateway (otherwise known as a control or alarm panel). If every interactive residential system sold today is based on the cloud, then every one of them has a deployment of IoT devices inside the home. In this respect, the penetration of IoT among buyers who use any system at all is nearly 100%. You just can't buy a system that doesn't include IoT, even if you are a laggard.

The story is somewhat delayed again in the commercial sector. A large percentage of commercial systems are still isolated on corporate networks. They may provide a small window of visibility to Web browsers and simple mobile apps, but most of their capabilities and data are inside a walled garden that prevents them from being useful as they could be.
