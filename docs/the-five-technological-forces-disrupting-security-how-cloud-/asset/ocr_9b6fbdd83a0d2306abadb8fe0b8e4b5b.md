# CHAPTER 19

# Social Spaces

There is an emerging group of physical objects known as the Social Internet of Things (SIoT). This new class represents the convergence of IoT and social networks. In particular, SIoT refers to physical objects that form social relationships with other physical objects without the intervention of people.

I will say again—without the intervention of people.

Social objects also connect to the familiar social networks, which means that we can interact with them in the same way we interact with people on those same networks.

Standing at the intersection of the physical and the social, SIoT devices are squarely in the domain of physical security. Whether or not we choose to use them for our electronic security systems, they will be a part of the environments for which we are responsible. There is really no ignoring them any more than we can choose to ignore other technology that might literally walk into our buildings.

At the simplest level, social things will be able to mimic social interactions that look like human activity through texting, group updates, checking in, posting photos, e-mails, and anything else humans can do. To get a glimpse of how this future is taking shape, you need no more than IFTTT, the web service that provides a nexus between hundreds of other web services and social networks through a simple visual programming mechanism they call applets. An app might link a connected thermometer with Facebook, for example, that every time it's above 90°F it says "hot enough for ya?” or it might do something more useful, like opening the garage when your car enters the driveway.

Social things will gradually exhibit more and more of the characteristics we currently label as human or intelligent. This phrasing only serves to beg the question of what is uniquely human and what can be simulated or emulated by machines.
