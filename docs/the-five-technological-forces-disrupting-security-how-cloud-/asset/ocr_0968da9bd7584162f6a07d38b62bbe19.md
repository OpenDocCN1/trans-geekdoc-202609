Rapid elasticity: this gives you the ability to quickly add or subtract computing or storage capability as needed, without having to wait for services to be brought online, nor being penalized when you no longer need them. Want to add 100 new customers with 90 days of storage tomorrow? No problem with an elastic cloud provider. Want to drop services for three facilities you just sold? You got it.

+   * Why it matters to integrators: rapid elasticity removes one of the principal barriers to provide faster service, particularly with the tight labor market for qualified IT resources.

+   * Why it matters to security organizations: when a facility needs a new or extended security capability, SaaS providers can respond more quickly.

+   5. Measured service: simply, "pay as you go." Payment is proportional to some unit of consumption per unit of time. In some business models, the unit of consumption is a tangible aspect of the security solution itself, as doors, cameras, or sensors. The model may also include consumption measures related to computing resources, such as the amount or length of data storage. Ultimately, measured service connects all of the preceding essential cloud characteristics to the bottom line, because customers only pay for what they use.

+   * Why it matters to integrators: for service providers, it enables a cost model based on actual usage for managing doors, cameras, gates, and the other natural units of measurement in physical security systems.

+   * Why it matters to security organizations: this converts high fixed capital expenses to low operating expenses. No one wants to pay for excess capacity that they are not using. It creates billing statements that relate to real-world measures as doors or cameras and simplifies financial reporting and justification of security expenditures.
