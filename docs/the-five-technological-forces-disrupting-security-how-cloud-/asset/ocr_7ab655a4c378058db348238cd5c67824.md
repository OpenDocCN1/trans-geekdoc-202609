# ADOPTION

Mobile technology has moved further along the adoption curve in the security industry than any of the other five technologies framed in this book. Nearly every manufacturer now has mobile apps that work with their products. This is significant, especially in light of the much slower adoption and even outright opposition to other new technologies, especially cloud computing. Why has mobile flourished while others lag behind?

One argument is when you use a mobile app, you know it—something not always true with other technologies. For example, surveys reveal a significant percentage of people who use browsers to access online services do not realize they access a cloud application. Facebook? Cloud. Ebay? Amazon? Cloud. This may just be a nomenclature issue; know when they use the Internet but see the cloud as something entirely different. Understandable, perhaps, but this presents a problem from a marketing perspective. Until recently, people had more fears attached to the word cloud than the Internet itself.

You could argue that mobile adds value than any of the other technologies, and so people are more willing to overcome reservations because of the higher payoff. We saw this play with the whole "bring your own device" (BYOD) movement. When smartphones initially came into the workplace, IT departments tried to stem the tide or keep them out altogether. They could legitimately justify this: Corporate e-mails reside on unprotected devices; viruses jump from personal smartphones to corporate networks; and sensitive data could be downloaded onto flash memory and walk out the door with the employee. Eventually, though, the CIOs had to acquiesce and accept it.

Mobile, too big to stop.
