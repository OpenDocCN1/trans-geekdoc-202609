# EASIER FOR NEW ENTRANTS

With all these changes in the milieu surrounding cloud adoption, the advances in technology have made it easier for someone with a business plan and some funding to launch a new offering in this domain. In the year 2000, a budding SaaS company still had to purchase its own servers, database and operating system software licenses, configure and install all hardware and software, and drag it off to either a dedicated data center or collocation facility. The sheer number of staff hours this process took—not to mention significant capital or extremely good credit—dwarfs the investment in time and effort required by today's entrepreneurs.

Today, literally anyone with a credit card can now launch a SaaS business by accounting in an AWS or of the other many SaaS players in the market. Buying and provisioning an IaaS environment where a start-up can launch its applications—while presenting a technically ratified set of purchases—no more difficult than any other ecommerce activity at the level of pointing and clicking on items to put in one's shopping cart checkout.

Of course, the challenge of writing application that meets users need and expectations remains a challenge, as having a ready IaaS environment has nothing to change the need for market research and product strategy. That said, the tools for creating web applications have evolved since the first decade of SaaS application came onto the scene. Dozens of languages, frameworks, collaborating tools, evolved HTML, and API standards have all contributed to a high-productivity workplace for software developers in physical security (and every other vertical market).

Regardless of all these developments, the biggest barrier for entrants to our industry remains: we are irreducibly, inescapably physical. Creating or adapting electronic hardware to the purposes of physical security. The big strides in simplifying and enabling cloud computing development have done little to make hardware development easier. It is slower, more difficult, more specialized, and much riskier due to the cost of getting the engineering wrong in hardware. It's not like you can just rewrite and compile.
