# ARCHITECTURE MATTERS

In the physical world of buildings and bridges, good architecture ensures that things look good, serve their intended purpose, and don't fall down while doing both. It makes structures safe to live, on walk, beneath, and inhabit.

System architecture in networked electronic systems plays the same role. It is the art and science of making systems perform well, operate according to plan, and don't fall over while doing something.

Falling over doesn't mean the same for connected devices and networks as it does for bricks and mortar, but it's just as real. It used to be that, for computer systems, falling over mean software crashes that were usually the fault of some intrinsic coding error— unresponsive keyboards, core dumps, and infamous blue screen of death. Now, falling over is more likely to suggest hacking or external compromise—data loss, data threat, denial of service, or denial of safety, in the case of physical security networks.

Architecture also provides a model of what things are and how they behave, especially their interactions with each. It literally provides a blueprint for the construction of something useful and safe. It translates complex design and engineering principles to drawings accessible and executable by anyone who wants to build something. If the builders, installers, or integrators follow these plans, we can expect the resulting system to perform to expectations and be resilient to natural and man-made threats.
