# Adoption Scorecard 215

The more I thought about this, the more I found that I needed to develop a scoreboard and treat each one as running its own race. The horses at the Kentucky derby don't all cross the finish line at the same time and neither will these technologies. It also became evident that a single score for both commercial and residential systems would not do justice to sizeable spread between these two categories. And, while we have not focused on residential systems in this book, it is instructive to view them as a leading indicator of where commercial systems are going.

Table 1 uses the families of the technology adoption life cycle to score the progress of both commercial (C) and residential (R) systems along this continuum. In almost every single case, residential systems are further along. The reasons have nothing to do with the suitability of the technologies in each market; everything to do with who the buyer is, but the channel and end user level.

Cloud. Commercial security systems show up as early adopters of cloud technology because market share for cloud systems is still in the single-digit percent, dwarfed by traditional on-premise deployments. The cloud version of electronic access control, access as a service (ACaaS), has a market share of less than 5% as of this writing, although the research firm IHS predicts a healthy compound growth rate of around 20%. The cloud version of video surveillance, video surveillance as a service (VSaaS), is also currently below 5% and growing at a compound annual growth rate of nearly 20%.

In residential systems, the picture is quite different. Cloud adoption is well into the late majority stage. All the residential market leaders use cloud platforms to provide interactive security and automation features that are nearly indistinguishable in terms of systems architecture. Residential security providers have no choice but to use the cloud (not that they would want to otherwise). With customers numbering in the millions, there is no other way to deliver an interactive experience at this scale. It's what the cloud was designed do.
