System integrators worry about the pinch from cloud computing as customers have become piecewise movement of selected software components into the cloud. These transitions can start innocently with an organization using, let's say, a cloud-based visitor management system that runs on an iPad in the lobby. Next, an expanding organization may want to use cloud-based access control and video, but only satellite offices and new acquisitions. As this process continues, what starts looking like a rash of excitations hits a tipping point and eventually becomes the normal. In my conversations with many integrators over the years, I've learned that many consider it existential question: what's left to integrate everything is in the cloud?

Fortunately, it's not quite so bleak it sounds, but the ground has definitely shifted. Physical security is irreducible, which means there will always be equipment that needs to be installed on premise: electric strikes, wireless door lockers, cameras, elevator controls, gates, turnstiles, and trucks of sensors. Also, there is still a residue of software integration in many cases because every corporate security environment is unique, at least around the edges where it needs to join with other IT systems. But the work on the remaining IT components does not command the level of expenditure as what the old kings of the hill in the enterprise software could command.

In this world, system integrators in the cloud-only world will find themselves on common ground with those in many verticals. The new expertise will be in configuring and managing increasingly cloud-based solutions with increasingly complex identity federation requirements.

Security organizations in this cloud-only world will have many options for the types of systems and services they would like to use. In fact, customers ranging from small business owners to large enterprises will be the beneficiaries of this change.
