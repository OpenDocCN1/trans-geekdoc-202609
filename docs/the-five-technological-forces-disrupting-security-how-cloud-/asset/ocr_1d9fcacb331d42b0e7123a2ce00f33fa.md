# Going Mobile

Recent surveys indicate that most of us check our smartphones around 85 times per day and touch them astonishing 2617. These compulsive habits have elevated mobile usage to the dominant share of time we spend online, with smartphones now driving over half of Internet traffic. Much of our consumption is personal, but an increasing amount is for business and commercial transactions. With the blurring of lines between the personal and professional, we do both whenever we light up the screen.

As recently as 5 years ago, few of these professional use cases concerned physical security. Most security organizations IoT were still bound to thick clients on desktops. The earliest mobile security management apps had just made the scene and provided only a fraction of the features available on their full-strength desktop or web counterparts. Video surveillance apps were an early favorite, mesmerizing users almost well as YouTube and certainly far better than what static rows and columns of data could ever achieve. Next administrative applications for access control and building information systems, followed by dozens of freestanding IoT dashboards. Last to the show were mobile credential applications that let tenants, employees, guests, and other occupants of our properties open doors with their smartphones for the first time in history.

These mobile apps have gone through several evolutionary cycles over the past few years, they have begun to cluster around three distinct user groups; first, administrative applications designed for members of the security team; second, occupant applications using a very broad definition that includes tenants, employees, residents, customers, and anyone else within a security organization's purview; and, third, presence applications, a small but growing group that provides two-way information and services "in the background," as we software types like to say.
