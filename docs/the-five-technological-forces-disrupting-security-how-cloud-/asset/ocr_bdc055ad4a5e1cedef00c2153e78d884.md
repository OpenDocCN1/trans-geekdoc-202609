For now, let's consider AI as a way of dealing with incoming data and making sense of them on our behalf, without getting too up on exactly what's going behind the scenes. Regardless of the specifics, it is clear that a new generation of analytic capability or intelligence will be needed to handle the growing volumes of data streaming into our security systems. Without such capabilities, the hackacks will continue to grow faster than our ability to find the needles. Frankly, there are many indications that this will within reach of almost any software system over the course of the next decade.

Let's step back for a bit of contrast. In the early days, a company needed its own team of AI researchers to make any progress within their own domain. The notion of a generalized intelligence that simulated the human brain was proving difficult due to the complexities of knowledge representation. As the science progressed, the approach changed to solving very particular problems or teaching computers to execute specific tasks. For that type of research, every problem was so narrowly defined that unless else was working on the exact thing you wanted to know about, you had to roll your own. For most companies, having this level of expertise in-house was completely out of reach. The expertise could only be found in academia and organizations with large R&D budgets and longterm horizons.

Back to the present, AI and related algorithms are now available to almost anyone, and simply using them requires less expertise than ever before. For software developers and product companies, there are now “app stores for algorithms” that condense all the complex data science and programming into one easily consumable base. You no longer need to have Marvin Minsky in the basement.¹ The same capabilities are also available in the even easier-to-digest form of AI as a service from major cloud providers like Amazon, Google, and IBM, to name a few. This is remarkable, considering that as the dot-com era, AI was still considered to be out of reach for most products and many professional disciplines. What we are witnessing is the consumerization of AI, just as we witnessed the consumerization of IT and security making the effects of a perfect storm in the preface of rising IoT data, security applications in the cloud, and AI as a service.

The term artificial intelligence is used in many different ways, some more precise than others. In its most general sense, it includes methods like machine learning, neural networks, decision trees, principal component analysis, k-means clustering, support vector machines, and numerous other techniques
