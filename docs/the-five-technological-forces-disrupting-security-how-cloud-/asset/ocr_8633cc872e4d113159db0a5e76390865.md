# Bigger

Big data is like life: it's what happens, while you're busy making other plans. In many respects, it is a by-product of everything we do on the Internet, the output of every sensor in our measured worlds, and the spread of pervasive computing into nearly everything we touch. It is personal, commercial, industrial, and global. One day, it will change the way we practice security. But it won't do that alone. That's because data are just data. As concrete is only concrete until it's formed into a bridge, a road, or even a work of art, data do not do much for us until it is shaped under the blade of data science and animated by analytics.

I didn't fully appreciate the latter two points about big data until I sat down to work out what big data could mean to the future of security. That's why this section (which started out as being about big data alone) has itself come to life as a broader look at big data, data science, and analytics.

When the layman looks at the face of physical security from the outside, big data is probably not the first thing that comes to mind. It makes likely people think of retail or even subatomic physics. The mention of commercial security usually conjures images of guards: bored men with uniforms badges sitting behind desks in the lobby or wandering back hallways and parking lots. Or perhaps, it conjures a world of corporate espionage, stolen secrets, and international intrigue. In a residential context, we think of home alarm systems that won't stop beeping when you want them to happily alert the police when something brings bad happens.

The truth of matter is that security has always been about data. To paraphrase Mark Twain, we use the five-dollar term "situational intelligence" when the 50-cent word "data" would suffice. Our security data world is filled with countless sensors recording countless events, filling databases to the brim. Inside are unexplored correlations and relationships that we would pay good money to understand. As we see the insights that big data systems provide other business and security systems, we want to know if data science can provide similar results with our warehouses of security data.
