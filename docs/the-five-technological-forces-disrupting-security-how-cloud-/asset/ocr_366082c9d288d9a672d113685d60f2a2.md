Commercial security systems will move more slowly, in part because they are
larger, more complex, and require more time to integrate new features in a
coherent way. They are also subject to different regulatory requirements, espe-
cially for anything that concerns safety of life or fire alarm systems. That said,
they comprise of the so-called Industrial IoT, by which most measures are
icipated to be even larger than the residential market.

# STRATEGY

How should security organizations approach IoT? Start by watching the resi-
dial space to see what kinds of capabilities are relevant to your commercial
setting. Residential systems are a predictor of what will become available in
commercial systems, and the adoption of IoT is certainly one of those proof
points.

A need-based approach might begin with the asking what's missing in your
information set today that additional sensors could feel. What visibility would
y like to have that you don't have today, and it is possible that the right IoT
device could provide it?

To help with your thinking, start with the family. IoT devices often don't pro-
vide a qualitative type of data or situational awareness. Often, they just
make the same types of data more affordable at more locations. Or they may
make it possible to collect information at locations infeasible before, thanks
to wireless connectivity, lower power consumption, and improved battery life.

# CODA: IS IoT REALLY A CATEGORY?

category as a whole.

roughly on par with the meaning of "electric appliances" at the beginning of
the last century. The first electric toaster and the first commercial electric
vacuum cleaner were both introduced in 1905. Then, the first was patented
electric milking machine (1910), and the earliest commercialized electric washing machines (1930).
