# A NEW MISSION FOR SECURITY

I have found that people in the security industry are deeply committed to making life safer and better for their employees, their customers, and the public at large. Every one of the professionals I work with knows that if they don't their job right, a company could lose valuable property, an entrepreneur could lose her small business, or someone could lose life and limb. They know that the difference between getting it right and getting it wrong in a classroom of children. This makes our careers a very personal and meaningful contribution to society as a whole.

The industry is all about products and professions that literally make a life-or-death difference in the daily lives of real people going about the basics of work and family. In an age of increasing terrorism and violence, they are no longer optional. They are essential to living without undue fear. They are essential to ensuring that violence touches as few as possible.

Even so, an odd thing happens when people start evaluating the risks versus the cost of protection. They start to wonder if it's really worth spending the money on systems designed to prevent low-likelihood events. We see this all the time. People hear about burglaries in their neighborhood, and they call for an estimate on a security system. A few weeks go by with no more bad news, and then they decide they don't need it all. An ex-employee who still has a keys to the office and steals a computer or two, and the business owner decides it's time for electronic security. A month goes by, the worry dies down, and with no security, the entire staff is still vulnerable to the next disgruntled ex-employee—who may do far worse than walk off with a laptop.

We forget about the squeaky wheel as it stops squeaking.
To get beyond, security product and service providers would do well to keep in a broader mind that enables the benefits to be obvious even when nothing is going wrong. As the five technologies transforming security deliver on their promise, all of us will be better able to accomplish that goal.
