# Smarter

If big data were the ingredients to bake a cake, then data science would be the recipe. A sack of flour and a dozen eggs are no good if you don't know what to do with them.

Lastly, the term data science enjoys nearly as much attention as big data itself, and with good reason. Data science is the methodology—the math and the algorithms—for extracting the information hidden in raw data. Data insights don't grow on trees more than other scientific breakthroughs. They arise through very specific processes applied under the scientific method. In the immortal words of Matt Damon in *The Martian*, you have to “science the shit out of it."

# WHAT DO WE WANT TO KNOW?

There is a popular misconception that big data systems provide automatic insights; they can ingest a bunch of data and simply tell us what is going on. There is a little bit of truth to that, but only a. In the world of data science, any automatic insights are broadly known as unsupervised learning. It works for some types of problems, but not for everything, and even then, it requires judgment, fine-tuning, and a sense of the overall goals of the investigation. It's probably a better wager that big data systems for physical security will only provide answers to questions we've asked. We will need to teach them what is important and what we want to know. It's not mind reading, after all.

This puts the onus on us to decide what matters and to express it as an algorithmically solvable problem. With cyber security, there are parts of this task that fall to all of the stakeholders in the security ecosystem. Software or SaaS providers will conduct market research to find out what security organizations would like to know. Or they might feel that they have great ideas and forge ahead, hoping they'll be useful. Security organizations will need to be vocal about the great unsolved problems they think big data could help, and may need to change their thinking about cloud platforms and where they will store their data.
