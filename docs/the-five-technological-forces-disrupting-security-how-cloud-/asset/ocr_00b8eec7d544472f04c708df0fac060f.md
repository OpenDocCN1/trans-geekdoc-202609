# The Run-Up to Cloud

Physical security has only recently developed enthusiasm for cloud computing. While we see more marketing claims about cloud products, overall adoption within the physical security industry remains low. The good news is that there are early signs of an imminent and rapid increase, with leading analysts already noting an upswing in cloud-based systems for both government and commercial sites.

What this tells me is that we are still witnessing the run-up phase to widespread use of the cloud. The question is, how did we get here and when are we finally going to get there?

# CLOUD MEETS AN INDUSTRY OF LATE ADOPTERS

On the whole, the security industry is justifiably conservative group when it comes to anything new. After all, customers don't want the use of “bleeding edge” technology in products designed to, well, literally prevent bleeding. When the stakes are life and death, security organizations rightly feel compelled to test and prove new technologies before placing their constituents at risk. Software must be free of bugs and security defects. Equipment and services must be UL approved.

It’s not just a matter of technical risk. Supply chain risk is just as important. Start-ups must demonstrate that they will survive long enough for buyers to make a cutting-edge product worthwhile. Security teams must be able to justify the inevitable new learning curves that come along with new products. Suppliers must make a convincing case that they can provide them with ongoing support for years into the future. No one wants to be the first to debut a spectacular failure. We want examples and references of others who have succeeded with the new “thing.”

In short, we’re an industry of “late adopters.”
