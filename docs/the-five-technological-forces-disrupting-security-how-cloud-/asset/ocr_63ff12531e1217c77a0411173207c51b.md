# THE DESK IN OUR POCKETS

Many of the mobile apps we use every day had no prior life as desktop apps. They were born as mobile applications and provide uniquely mobile experiences. Think of real-time mapping application or any social messaging app. They are not very interesting as desktop applications because the whole point is to use them in real time, all the time, everywhere. They are indelibly mobile and that's their appeal.

Many of the first-generation mobile apps in security, on the other hand, are reincarnations of legacy desktop applications, old wine in new (and more portable) flasks. There's not anything wrong with that. The aging desktop needed to be put out to the curb, even when the lessons scrawled across it remain instructional. Mature applications embody a decade-long distillation of user requirements and valuable domain expertise. With this providence, the mobile versions could start far ahead and give most people what they want, most of the time. Mobility delivers information where it's needed and when it's needed. As the “mobile-first” design philosophy continues to penetrate the security industry, these administrative applications will evolve into something mobile.

An increasing number of new security apps are natively mobile and bring more to the table than their forebears. Mobile credentials are the clarion example of a new wave of applications that fully leverage the power of the platform. More than just an incremental improvement, they introduce a qualitatively new user experience and life in convenience for the security ecosystem. They modernize access control authentication and pave the avenue for participatory security where all stakeholders have a role in both intelligence and response. Many technology analysts say that for an innovation to be truly disruptive, it needs to be ten times better than whatever it replaces.
