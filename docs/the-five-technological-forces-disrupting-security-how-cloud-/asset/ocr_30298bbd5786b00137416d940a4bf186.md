For security, it matters, and it can be expressed as one of the bedrock principles of our profession: trust. Whether your data are big or small, do you trust them? Surveys indicate that roughly one-third of business leaders don't trust the information they receive from their data systems. Veracity of data sources and believability of analytic outputs are crucial to our big data future in security.

We can't afford to be wrong a third of the time.

# THE CASE OF THE MISSING DATA

Large security organizations with huge budgets have access to powerful big data resources and analytic software. I am thinking of state agencies like the CIA or FBI, international organizations like Interpol or NATO, or many of the offices in the Department of Defense. They have teams of trained data scientists who can follow the wisps of data left behind by international terrorists and string them together to find a needle in a haystack. The successes of this intelligence, law enforcement, and military teams are legendary. They seem almost omniscient, but a lot of the credit goes to the data resources and analytics at their disposal.

The commercial security systems available to most businesses, unfortunately, provide nowhere near this level of data insight. Given the property value and lives at stake, better data analytics could make a significant difference in the effectiveness of our products and people. Without it, there are limitations to our ability to provide early threat detection and advance risk assessment. Now can we quickly find culprits in the aftermath of a tragedy. For this deficit, we pay in both blood and treasure for a situation that can be vastly improved with the right algorithms.
