Despite worries for suppliers, the benefits for security organizations are clear. It has become cheaper to create and transport situational intelligence (more security at a lower cost). More expensive solutions will be crowded out of the market. However, price competition for the IoT-plus-cloud paradigm might take some time because we are still in the early adopter phase. You need at least a mass of the early majority to begin shaking out the market and to find an equilibrium point.

What brings us here?

Earlier, we saw that cloud computing provides a significant cost reduction over on-premise computing. For IoT devices, cloud will be the only option. They were born there, and they are not going to leave. They have been conceived, designed, manufactured, and deployed on the assumption that they communicate with cloud services for every aspect of extracting value from the information they produce.

A second cost factor is wiring, and it's a foregone conclusion that IoT devices will use wireless communications instead of expensive wires. The third major cost factor is power consumption. Again, most IoT devices are designed to run on small batteries for long periods of time, which makes them far more competitive than the alternative.

All these affect your supply chain.

For equipment and software suppliers, IoT transformation charts a roadmap with traditional product design supplanted by new design principles. Lightweight beasts heavyweight. Cloud off the shelf cuts custom. Not to be ignored, customer expectations for the user experience actual products will shift the same way they did for user interfaces over the last technology cycle. Missed out on that anytime-anywhere-cloud-browserresponsive thing? It's coming back. This time on Amazon Dash Button.

Like all before it, this new technology requires new skills for system integrators and their technicians. Get back on the training treadmill, because yesterday's user manual just became obsolete. Implementation will no longer be about the quickest or cheapest way to pull wire. Instead, it will be about wireless connectivity for sensor networks. It will be about network management tools to make sure that hundreds of devices are all. It will be about products and services from outside the security industry now in demand by customers. And it will be about uncertain cyber security precautions.
