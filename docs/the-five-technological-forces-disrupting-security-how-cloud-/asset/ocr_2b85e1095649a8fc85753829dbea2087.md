# Data-Driven Security

AngelList is one of the most active destinations on the web for start-ups to find investors and vice versa. It was launched in 2010 to democratize the investment process and help connect new ideas with capital. It has been instrumental in opening up early-stage investments to a broader audience and was the birth-place of such colossal success stories as Uber.

If you search for big data start-ups, you will find over 4000 companies in this category. Their applications cover sports, medicine, web analytics, e-commerce, data centers, news, sales, energy, recruiting, travel, climate, fraud protection—and the list goes on. But not one of them is a start-up in big data for physical security. Maybe the entrepreneurs haven't found us yet. Or maybe what we're doing is too difficult.

Notwithstanding the lack of start-ups, it is inevitable that big data will drive new business models in security. Perhaps not for a while to come, but it is too important for it not to happen. These new businesses will aggregate and analyze data and transform it into an information product, as happened in many industries already. Producing data has improved nearly every industry in our economy over the course of the last several decades. In fact, our entire economy has come to rely on data analytics to the point to find new customers and operate their companies more effectively. IBM claims that organizations businesses have the benefit of analytic software claims that using big data is 23 times more likely to outperform their competitors.¹ I saw this myself in an earlier part of my career working in a business that did nothing but create and distribute information products for health-care organizations.
