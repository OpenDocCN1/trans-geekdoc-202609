# CHAPTER 19: Social Spaces

Even under less pressured circumstances, it is not easy to work through an arduous sign-procedure and make guests feel welcome.

The point is that when it's your building, everything about this user experience has the potential to turn off visitors, guests, customers, and colleagues. What should customers expect? A customer experience, that’s what. There was a great line from a recent season of Mad Men where Roger Sterling reminded a colleague “the client should never experience a negative emotion while in your presence.” That’s a great goal for how guests should feel any time they’re in a space where visitor management is an integral part of the security process.

A truly social space could be set up to know in advance who visit. With information, the guest could be welcomed by upon arrival, rather than being treated like an intruder. By using a mobile credential or pass sent in advance, registration and badger could be as simple as scanning the invitation. The social space would then use a preferred social network to tell your hosts that you were there. Compared with today’s baseline experience, this scenario shows the transformation of a security experience into a customer experience.

Yes, security can actually be a plus for a customer.

# DISRUPTION

Social networks are disruptive. They have displaced older communications methods and increased the frequency of our interactions with other people and groups. They have altered the way we express ourselves and hastened the shift from voice to digital. They enable us to communicate with far greater ease and simplicity than anything that came before. Social channels have pulled us into closer groups over common interests and made us closer to people we already know. For many, they become a primary means of communication, eclipsing earlier technologies like voice calls by a wide margin.

They have redirected not only how we communicate but also with whom and services. Social log-in has simplified the way we interact with new websites and services, providing millions with a new way of identifying themselves online. They have decreased our password burden and increased our ability to collect and share information across multiple channels whose only common thread is my interests, my online identity, and me. They have spun off thousands of derivative Web businesses that would have been unthinkable without social networks.
