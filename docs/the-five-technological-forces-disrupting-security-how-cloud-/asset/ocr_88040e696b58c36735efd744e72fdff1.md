Where they diverge is that standards are the product of strict sets of rules on how they are created, ratified, and subsequently changed; proprietary frameworks can be changed at the whim of their owner. On the one hand, this freedom allows proprietary frameworks to move much more quickly than the consensus-driven committee process of large standards bodies. On the other hand, adopters may find themselves stranded after some number of years, when slow market adoption or other corporate decision-making marginalize what once appeared a promising “standards” initiative.

That said, there are a handful of major forces in the technology world that, while proprietary, have provided enough benefit and stability that many adopters have created successful products just for the ecosystem without feeling the proprietary pinch. In desktop operating systems, Windows is the standout example, while iOS and Android make the case for mobile platforms.

# Google Weave

Google Weave is an example of a single-vendor IoT framework coupled with a service offering. It is an IoT platform that covers device registration, identity, communication, cybersecurity, software updates, and user interaction from mobile devices and the web. The goal of the framework is to provide a common language that all devices in an ecosystem can use. In the case of access control applications, for example, a door lock can have defined states or actions as lock or unlock that can be issued by a variety of other devices as smartphones or remotely by the Weave cloud.

Weave stands out among other contenders for IoT frameworks by virtue of built-in security features such as verified boot, data encryption, and availability of automated security patches. Weave also has a robust set of methods for controlling what types of access permission various devices have to one another—an essential aspect of managing large numbers of devices in an enterprise setting.

The promise of unified vendor offerings like Weave is that they also tie you into all of the other cloud services the company provides. In Google’s case, their significant investments in machine learning and AI could be compelling to one attempting to extract actionable intelligence from IoT data streams.

Often confused with Nest Weave, Google Weave is intended to support connected devices broadly across the IoT space, not just the limited focus of home automation applications. In this sense, it appears to be a more industrial set of standards that may be a better fit for security applications.
