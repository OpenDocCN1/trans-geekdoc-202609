For example, I can have IFTTT automatically upload security video clips to Google Drive for long-term archiving and distribution. They could simultaneously be mailed to three colleagues, tweeted, posted to Instagram, and cause my building to go into a lockdown state. The net effect of all these integrations is that one of the participating services can leverage the benefits of extensive product integrations without having to do each one directly with all the other service providers. In this regard, IFTTT acts like a giant hub with value-added logic, connecting all the partners. The network effect adds more value to all because each new partner in the ecosystem adds value to all the others.

Contrast with the painstaking partner-by-partner integration prevailing among commercial security systems. It is a slow and arduous process that limits the number of options customers have for combining best-of-breed products. From an algorithmic point of view, the number of integrations N products grows in proportion to N^2 when every partner must integrate directly with every other. In contrast, a hub-and-spoke topology is completely linear: N products require N integrations. In a market where security organizations might wish to choose between hundreds and thousands of products, the difference between N and N^2 is a huge cost factor for all concerned.

That's the power of IoT plus cloud.

# LARGE TECH & TELECOM EYE PHYSICAL SECURITY—AGAIN

The combination of IoT and cloud—with perhaps a bit of DIY on the side—lower some of the physical challenges of the security business while keeping its attractive business model intact. As a result, many large and communication companies and start-ups and their VC investors are taking a renewed interest in products and services that address physical security problems.

The business model attracting these newcomers is called recurring revenue. It began as a staple of alarm companies and tracked with the value proposition of ongoing monitoring services. It spread to additional services like access control and video surveillance as SaaS brought the same ongoing value proposition to new areas of the security equation.
