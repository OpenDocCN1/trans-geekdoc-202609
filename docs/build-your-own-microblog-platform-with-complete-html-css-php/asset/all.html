Introduction

Overview of Microblogging Platforms

Importance and popularity of microblogging

Microblogging has become a key component of the digital age, offering users a platform to share short, succinct updates, thoughts, and multimedia content with a broad audience. Unlike traditional blogging, which often involves more detailed and lengthy posts, microblogging emphasizes quick and immediate communication, providing a unique and responsive way to engage with others. It caters to the fast-paced nature of modern communication, where users value rapid consumption and the efficient sharing of information.

The importance and popularity of microblogging are

exemplified by the success of platforms like Twitter and

Tumblr. Twitter, launched in 2006, revolutionized online communication with its 280-character limit, encouraging users to share real-time updates, opinions, and news quickly and concisely. It has become a go-to platform for breaking news, public discussions, and professional networking, boasting millions of active users who post daily. Twitter's influence extends beyond social media, impacting politics, journalism, and marketing. The platform has since been rebranded and is now known as X.


Tumblr, another popular microblogging site, offers a more multimedia-rich experience, allowing users to post text, images, videos, and GIFs. It has cultivated a diverse and creative community, where users can follow topics of interest, engage with content, and express themselves through customizable blogs. Tumblr's emphasis on creativity and community has made it a unique space for niche interests and fan communities.

Medium is another example of a microblogging platform that straddles the line between microblogging and traditional blogging. While it allows for longer posts, it also supports shorter, quick-read content that can be easily shared and consumed. Medium has attracted a range of users, from professional writers to casual bloggers, who appreciate its clean design and the ability to reach a broader audience.

The rise of microblogging platforms reflects a shift in how people engage with content online. These platforms enable users to share their thoughts quickly, reach a global audience, and participate in larger conversations with ease. Whether for personal expression, news dissemination, or professional networking, microblogging has become a vital tool in the digital age. As the demand for real-time information and instant connectivity grows, the role of microblogging platforms in shaping online discourse will continue to expand.

Key features of a microblogging site

Microblogging platforms are designed to facilitate the sharing of brief, impactful messages, making them distinct from traditional blogging platforms. Their key features focus on enabling users to communicate quickly and efficiently, fostering engagement and real-time interaction.

One of the most fundamental features of a microblogging site is the character limit or post length restriction. This feature encourages users to express their thoughts concisely, leading to more focused and direct communication. For example, X's 280-character limit compels users to distill their messages to their essence, which has become a defining characteristic of the platform. This concise format makes content easily digestible, allowing users to quickly scan posts and stay updated with minimal effort.

Another critical feature is the real-time content feed, which displays posts chronologically or algorithmically based on user preferences and engagement. This feed is the heartbeat of microblogging platforms, constantly refreshing with new content. It keeps users engaged by providing a steady stream of updates from the people, brands, or topics they follow. The immediacy of the content feed supports real-time communication, making microblogging platforms a preferred medium for breaking news, live event updates, and trending discussions.

User interaction tools such as likes, retweets, reblogs, and

comments are essential features that foster community

engagement on microblogging platforms. These tools allow users to respond to, share, and amplify content, creating a dynamic and interactive environment. For instance, X's retweet feature enables users to quickly share content with their followers, contributing to the viral spread of information.  Similarly, Tumblr’s re-blogging feature allows users to select and organize content on their own blogs while engaging with the original post.

Customization and personalization are also significant features of microblogging platforms. Profile customization allows users to express their individuality through profile pictures, bios, and design elements like background images or themes. Tumblr, for instance, offers extensive customization options, enabling users to create unique and visually appealing blogs that reflect their personal style or brand identity. Personalization extends to content discovery as well, where algorithms suggest posts, hashtags, or accounts to follow based on a user's interests and behavior.

Lastly, multimedia support is a crucial feature that enriches the microblogging experience. Users can share not just text, but also images, videos, GIFs, and links, adding depth and variety to their posts. This multimedia integration is particularly prominent on platforms like Tumblr, where the visual aspect of content plays a significant role in user engagement. The ability to share different types of media makes microblogging platforms appealing to a wide audience.

These key features—character limits, real-time feeds, interaction tools, customization, and multimedia support—are what make microblogging platforms effective and popular. They create a user-friendly environment where individuals can share ideas, interact with others, and stay informed, all within a streamlined, accessible framework.

Choosing a Domain Name

Importance of a good domain name

Selecting a domain name is one of the most important decisions you'll make when building your microblogging platform. Your domain name is not just an address on the web; it's the first impression your site makes and a key part of your brand identity. A well-chosen domain name can significantly impact your platform's visibility, credibility, and overall success.


A good domain name is memorable and easy to type. In today’s fast-paced digital world, users are often browsing on mobile devices or multitasking across platforms. If your domain name is complex, difficult to spell, or too long, potential visitors may struggle to remember or type it correctly, leading to lost traffic. For instance, a domain name like "mygreatmicroblog.com" is more likely to stick in someone’s mind than a convoluted or overly creative alternative.


Moreover, your domain name should reflect the content and purpose of your platform. It should give visitors an immediate sense of what your site is about, even before they click on the link. A relevant domain name builds trust and sets the right expectations. For example, a domain name like "quickpost.com" clearly conveys the idea of fast, short-form content, making it a strong choice for a microblogging platform.


Another critical aspect of choosing a domain name is its impact on search engine optimization (SEO). A domain name that includes relevant keywords can help improve your site’s ranking on search engines like Google. While having an exact match domain (a domain name that matches a common search query exactly) is less crucial than it once was, incorporating a keyword related to your platform’s niche can still provide an SEO advantage. For instance, if your microblog focuses on science-related news, a domain like "sciencebriefs.com" might help attract users searching for related content.


A good domain name is a blend of simplicity, relevance, and strategic keyword use. It’s a crucial element in establishing your microblogging platform’s identity and plays a significant role in attracting and retaining visitors. Taking the time to choose the right domain name will pay off in the long run, helping your site stand out in a crowded digital landscape.

Tips for selecting the right domain name

Choosing the right domain name is a crucial step in establishing your online presence. Your domain name is not only the address where users will find your site, but it's also a key element of your brand's identity. Here are some essential tips to help you select a domain name that will set your microblogging platform up for success:

Keep It Short and Simple:

A short, simple domain name is easier for users to remember, type, and share. Aim for a name that is concise, ideally between 6-14 characters. Avoid using complex words, numbers, or hyphens, as these can confuse users and increase the likelihood of typos. A straightforward name like “QuickNews.com” is more effective and user-friendly than something like “Qw1ck-Noos.com.”

Make It Memorable:

Your domain name should be easy to recall. A memorable name sticks in the minds of visitors, making it more likely they will return to your site or recommend it to others. Think of names that are catchy, brandable, or evoke imagery related to your microblogging platform’s focus. For instance, “BlueMoon.com” might be a good fit for a platform dedicated to thought-provoking content.


Include Relevant Keywords:

Incorporating relevant keywords into your domain name can boost your site's search engine optimization (SEO). While it’s no longer necessary to have an exact match domain for SEO benefits, including a keyword that reflects the content of your site can help with search engine rankings and make your site’s purpose clear to visitors. For example, if your microblog focuses on travel, a name like “TravelTime.com” immediately communicates what users can expect to find on your platform.

Choose the Right Domain Extension:

The domain extension is the suffix at the end of your domain name, such as .com, .net, or .org. The .com extension is the most popular and widely recognized, making it the preferred choice for most websites. However, if the .com version of your desired name is unavailable, consider other extensions like .net, .co, or industry-specific options like .blog. If you reside outside the USA, it might be suitable to use your country’s domain suffix, such as .ca for Canada or .au for Australia. Be mindful of how these extensions are perceived by your target audience; while .com is generally viewed as more trustworthy or credible, a well-chosen alternative can be effective if it aligns with your brand.

Avoid Trademark Issues:

Before finalizing your domain name, make sure it doesn’t infringe on any existing trademarks. Choosing a name that is too similar to a well-known brand can lead to legal issues and

confusion among users. Conduct a trademark search and check the availability of your domain name on social media platforms to ensure  it’s  unique and doesn’t conflict with

established brands.

Consider Long-Term Growth:

Think about the long-term goals of your microblogging platform when choosing a domain name. While it might be tempting to pick a name that reflects a current trend, it’s important to select a name that will remain relevant as your site evolves. A name like “TechNews.com” might limit you if you decide to expand beyond technology in the future, whereas a broader name like “DigitalStream.com” allows for more flexibility.

Test It Out:

Before you commit to a domain name, test it out with friends, colleagues, or potential users. Say the name out loud, and see how easily it can be understood and spelled. You can also run a small poll or survey to get feedback on how the name resonates with your target audience. This step can help you avoid potential pitfalls and ensure your domain name has the desired impact.

By following these tips, you can select a domain name that not only represents your microblogging platform effectively but also helps you build a strong, recognizable brand. A well-chosen domain name is a valuable asset that will contribute to your site’s success in the digital landscape.

Implementing secure user authentication

Implementing a secure user authentication is fundamental to the integrity and safety of any online platform. In a microblogging site, where users frequently share and interact with content, it is particularly important to ensure that their accounts are protected from unauthorized access. Secure authentication not only safeguards user data but also helps maintain trust in your platform.

At the core of secure authentication is the proper handling of user credentials. Passwords should never be stored in plain text; instead, they must be hashed using strong cryptographic algorithms like bcrypt, Argon2, or SHA-256. Hashing converts passwords into a unique string of characters that cannot be easily reversed, ensuring that even if your database is compromised, the actual passwords remain protected. Additionally, using a unique salt for each password further enhances security by preventing attackers from using precomputed hash tables (rainbow tables) to crack passwords.

Another crucial aspect of secure authentication is ensuring that the login process itself is secure. This involves implementing measures like rate limiting to prevent brute-force attacks, where an attacker tries numerous password combinations to gain access. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds an extra layer of security by requiring users to provide a second form of verification—such as a code sent to their mobile device—in addition to their password. This significantly reduces the likelihood of unauthorized access, even if the user's password is compromised.

Sessions must also be securely managed to protect authenticated users as they navigate your site. Using secure cookies (with the Secure and HttpOnly flags) ensures that session tokens are transmitted securely and cannot be easily accessed by malicious scripts. Implementing measures like session expiration and automatic logout after periods of inactivity can further protect user accounts from being hijacked.

Implementing secure user authentication requires a combination of best practices: encrypting user credentials, securing the login process, and managing sessions safely. By taking these steps, you protect not only your users’ data but also the overall reputation and reliability of your platform.

Session handling and user sessions

Session handling and user sessions are critical components in managing user interactions and maintaining state across different pages of a website, particularly in a microblogging platform. When a user logs in, the server needs to remember their identity and preferences as they navigate through various sections of the site. This is where session handling comes into play. For example – when a user logs into YourMicroBlog, their user_id from the users database is stored into $_SESSION[‘MB_ID’].  Every other program for the site can check to see if the user is logged in prior to giving access.

A session is essentially a temporary, server-side storage mechanism that tracks user data for the duration of their interaction with the website. Upon successful login, a unique session ID is generated and stored on the server. This ID is then sent to the user's browser as a cookie, enabling the server to identify and authenticate the user on subsequent requests. The session ID is crucial because it acts as a key that links the user to their session data, such as their username, preferences, and any other relevant information.

Secure session handling is vital to prevent unauthorized access and protect user data. One important practice is to ensure that session IDs are complex and randomly generated to reduce the risk of session hijacking, where an attacker gains control of another user's session. Additionally, storing session data securely on the server and using encrypted cookies (with the Secure and HttpOnly flags) helps prevent session IDs from being intercepted or manipulated by attackers.

Managing session duration is another essential aspect of session handling. Implementing session timeouts ensures that user sessions automatically expire after a period of inactivity, reducing the risk of unauthorized access if a user leaves their device unattended. For high-security applications, it may also be beneficial to implement automatic session regeneration, where a new session ID is issued after a user logs in or at regular intervals during their session. This minimizes the risk of session fixation attacks, where an attacker tries to exploit a valid session ID to gain unauthorized access.

Finally, it’s important to properly handle session termination. When a user logs out, their session should be securely destroyed, removing any stored data from the server and invalidating the session ID. This ensures that the session cannot be reused or exploited by an attacker.

In summary, session handling and user sessions are crucial for maintaining the security and integrity of a microblogging platform. By carefully managing session IDs, securing cookies, implementing timeouts, and properly terminating sessions, you can create a safe and reliable user experience.

Chapter 5: User Management and Profile Features

User Roles and Permissions

Defining user roles (admin, editor, user)

In a well-structured microblogging platform, defining user roles is essential for managing access and ensuring that each user interacts with the system in a way that aligns with their responsibilities. Our program incorporates a user role system that includes three primary roles: Admin, Editor, and User, each with specific permissions and capabilities.

Admin: The Admin role is the highest level of access within the system. Admins have full control over all aspects of the microblogging platform, including user management, content moderation, and system settings. They can create, edit, and delete posts, manage categories and tags, and oversee the overall functionality of the site. Admins are also responsible for configuring system-wide settings and ensuring that the platform operates smoothly and securely.

Editor: Editors have a more focused set of permissions compared to Admins. They are primarily responsible for creating and managing content, such as posts and comments. Editors can write, edit, and publish posts, as well as moderate comments to maintain the quality of the content on the site. However, Editors do not have access to system-level settings or user management features, which are restricted to Admins.

User: The User role represents the general audience of the microblogging platform. Users can interact with the content by reading posts, leaving comments, and engaging with other users through likes and shares. They have limited access compared to Admins and Editors and cannot modify site settings or manage content beyond their own contributions.

User Groups: In addition to individual roles, our program also defines user groups, which allow for more granular control over permissions and access. User groups can be used to categorize users based on specific needs or functions, such as a group for regular contributors or a group for special interest areas. This flexibility helps in organizing users more effectively and tailoring their experience according to their group’s requirements.

By clearly defining these roles and groups, the platform ensures that each user has access to the appropriate features and tools, enhancing both security and functionality.

Implementing role-based access control

Implementing role-based access control (RBAC) is a crucial step in managing user permissions within a microblogging platform. By defining distinct roles such as admin, editor, and user, you can ensure that each user has access only to the features and functions appropriate for their role. This approach not only enhances security but also simplifies the management of user permissions as your platform grows.

For our microblogging program, with the use of user groups we are able to categorize user permissions into three main roles: admin, editor, and user. For example, if $_SESSION[‘MB_GROUP’] = 9 {“User is Admin”;}. The admin role has the highest level of access, allowing for full control over the platform, including the ability to manage other users, modify site settings, and access sensitive data.

For example, if $_SESSION[‘MB_GROUP’] = 8 {“User is Moderator or Editor”;}. The moderator or editor’s role is designed for users who need to oversee content, such as approving or deleting posts and comments, but without the broader administrative capabilities.

Finally, if $_SESSION[‘MB_GROUP’] = 1 {“User is Registered”;}. The user role is for regular users who can create and interact with posts and comments but have no access to administrative or moderation functions.

For users that have not confirmed their E-mail Address upon account creation, they have a user group of: $_SESSION[‘MB_GROUP’] = 0 {“User is Not Registered”;}.

By implementing RBAC in this way, the platform ensures that each user's access is tailored to their responsibilities, reducing the risk of unauthorized actions. This structure also makes it easier to scale the platform, as new users can be assigned to predefined roles with specific permissions, maintaining a consistent and secure environment.

Allowing users to update their information

Allowing users to update their information is an essential feature of any modern web application, particularly in a microblogging platform where users may want to regularly update their profiles, preferences, or account settings. Providing this capability not only enhances the user experience but also ensures that the platform remains relevant and responsive to users' needs.

User Profile Management

One of the primary areas where users may need to update their information is within their profile. This can include updating personal details such as their display name, bio, profile picture, contact information, and even social media links. Offering an intuitive and user-friendly interface for these updates is crucial. The platform should allow users to easily access their profile settings, make changes, and save them with minimal effort.


Security Considerations

Security is a key concern when it comes to allowing users to update their information. Sensitive data, such as email addresses and passwords, must be handled with care. For instance, when a user updates their password, the system should require the current password as a security measure to prevent unauthorized changes. Additionally, any changes to critical account information, like email addresses or phone numbers, should trigger a verification process to ensure that the user genuinely owns the new contact information.

Real-Time Validation and Feedback

When users are updating their information, real-time validation and feedback can greatly enhance their experience. For example, when changing their username, the system can immediately check whether the new username is available or if it meets certain criteria, such as length or character restrictions. Providing instant feedback helps users make informed choices and reduces the likelihood of errors or frustration.

Data Synchronization and Consistency

Ensuring that the updated information is reflected consistently across the platform is another important aspect. For instance, if a user changes their display name, it should be updated not only in their profile but also in all instances where their name is displayed, such as in comments, posts, or notifications. This requires efficient data synchronization to maintain consistency across the platform.

User Experience and Flexibility

Providing users with flexibility in updating their information contributes to a positive user experience. This includes allowing users to customize their profiles according to their preferences, such as changing themes, adjusting privacy settings, or managing notifications. A responsive design that works seamlessly across devices is also important, as users may want to update their information from a smartphone, tablet, or desktop computer.

Auditing and Change History

For security and transparency, it may be beneficial to implement an auditing system that tracks changes to user information. This can help in identifying any unauthorized updates and allows users to view a history of changes they have made to their profiles. In the case of any issues, having a change history can be invaluable for troubleshooting and restoring previous settings.

Allowing users to update their information is a fundamental feature that must be handled with care. By focusing on security, user experience, and data consistency, you can ensure that this functionality enhances the overall usability and trustworthiness of your microblogging platform. Providing a smooth and secure process for updating information not only keeps users engaged but also reinforces their confidence in the platform's ability to protect and manage their data effectively.

Implementing responsive design using media queries

In today's digital landscape, users access websites from a variety of devices, ranging from desktop computers to tablets and smartphones. To ensure that your microblog looks great and functions well across all these devices, implementing responsive design is essential. Media queries, a core feature of CSS, allow you to tailor your site's layout and appearance based on the characteristics of the user's device, such as screen size, resolution, and orientation.

What Are Media Queries?

Media queries are conditional CSS rules that apply styles based on the properties of the user's device or viewport. They allow you to define different styles for different screen sizes, ensuring that your microblog adapts smoothly whether it's viewed on a large desktop monitor or a small mobile screen. By using media queries, you can adjust elements like font size, layout, and images to provide an optimal viewing experience.

Key Breakpoints

When implementing responsive design, it's important to identify key breakpoints—specific screen widths where the layout needs to change to accommodate different devices. Common breakpoints might include:

Mobile Devices: Up to 480px wide.

Tablets: 481px to 768px wide.

Small Laptops: 769px to 1024px wide.

Desktops: 1025px and wider.


These breakpoints help you design a layout that works well across a range of devices. You can define your own breakpoints based on the needs of your microblog and the devices your audience is most likely to use.

Writing Media Queries

Media queries are written in CSS and typically include the @media rule followed by a condition that specifies when the styles should be applied.

For example:

/* Styles for small devices (e.g., smartphones) */

@media (max-width: 480px) {

body {

font-size: 14px;

}

.container {

width: 100%;

padding: 10px;

}

.header {

text-align: center;

}

}

/* Styles for medium devices (e.g., tablets) */

@media (min-width: 481px) and (max-width: 768px) {

body {

font-size: 16px;

}

.container {

width: 90%;

padding: 20px;

}

.header {

text-align: left;

}

}

/* Styles for large devices (e.g., desktops) */

@media (min-width: 1025px) {

body {

font-size: 18px;

}

.container {

width: 80%;

padding: 30px;

}

.header {

text-align: left;

}

}

In this example, different font sizes, container widths, and header alignments are applied based on the device's screen width. This ensures that the content remains readable and the layout well-proportioned, regardless of the device.

Improved User Experience: Users can navigate and interact with your microblog seamlessly, whether they're using a phone or a desktop.

Cost-Effectiveness: A single responsive website eliminates the need to create and maintain separate versions for different devices, saving time and resources.

Securing Your Application

Protecting against SQL injection and XSS attacks

Securing your web application from SQL injection and Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) attacks is crucial to maintaining its integrity and protecting user data. Both types of attacks exploit vulnerabilities in your code, and addressing these risks requires careful coding practices and robust security measures.

SQL Injection Prevention

SQL injection occurs when an attacker manipulates SQL queries by injecting malicious code through user inputs. This can lead to unauthorized access, data breaches, or even full control over the database. To protect against SQL injection:

Use Prepared Statements and Parameterized Queries: Instead of constructing SQL queries directly with user inputs, use prepared statements with placeholders. This approach separates the SQL code from the data, making it impossible for attackers to inject malicious SQL. Most modern database libraries, including PDO and MySQLi, support prepared statements.

Escape User Inputs: If for some reason you cannot use prepared statements, ensure that all user inputs are properly escaped before including them in SQL queries. This involves converting special characters into escape sequences that are not executed as part of the SQL command.

Validate and Sanitize Inputs: Implement robust input validation to ensure that user inputs meet expected formats and types. For instance, if an input should be an integer, validate that it only contains numerical characters. Additionally, sanitize inputs to remove or encode potentially harmful characters.

Use Least Privilege Principle: Limit database user permissions to only what is necessary for the application to function. Avoid using administrative or overly permissive database accounts for routine operations.

Protecting Against XSS Attacks

Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) attacks involve injecting malicious scripts into web pages viewed by other users. This can lead to data theft, session hijacking, or defacement of the site. To guard against XSS attacks:

Escape Output: When displaying user-generated content on your site, escape all output to ensure that any HTML or JavaScript code is rendered as plain text rather than executed. For example, convert special characters like <, >, and & into their HTML entities.

Use Content Security Policy (CSP): Implement a Content Security Policy to restrict the sources from which content can be loaded on your site. This helps prevent the execution of unauthorized scripts and reduces the risk of XSS attacks.

Validate and Sanitize Inputs: Similar to SQL injection prevention, validate and sanitize all user inputs to ensure they do not contain malicious code. This includes stripping out or encoding HTML tags and attributes.

Avoid Inline JavaScript: Refrain from including inline JavaScript in your HTML and use external scripts instead. This approach helps mitigate XSS risks associated with inline code.

Regular Security Audits: Regularly audit your codebase for potential vulnerabilities and keep up with security best practices. This proactive approach helps identify and address weaknesses before they can be exploited.

By following these security practices, you can significantly reduce the risk of SQL injection and XSS attacks. Employing prepared statements, escaping user inputs, and validating data helps protect against SQL injection, while escaping output, using CSP, and avoiding inline scripts guard against XSS. Ensuring these measures are part of your development process will contribute to a more secure and reliable web application.

Dynamic Content: Content that changes based on user interactions or other conditions, often generated on the server-side and delivered to the client.

E

Error Log: A file where error messages and debugging information are recorded. It helps developers identify and troubleshoot issues in their applications.

Encryption: The process of converting data into a coded format to prevent unauthorized access. Commonly used to secure sensitive information such as passwords and personal data.

F

Frontend: The client-side part of a web application, including everything that users interact with directly, such as the layout, design, and user interface elements.

Framework: A collection of pre-written code that provides a foundation for developing software applications. Examples include Laravel for PHP and React for JavaScript.

G

GUI (Graphical User Interface): The interface of a software application that allows users to interact with the program through graphical elements such as buttons, icons, and menus.

H

HTML (Hypertext Markup Language): The standard language used to create and structure content on the web. It defines elements such as headings, paragraphs, links, and images.

HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure): An extension of HTTP that adds a layer of security by encrypting data transmitted between the user's browser and the web server.

I

ID (Identifier): A unique value assigned to elements in a web page or database to distinguish them from other elements. It is often used in CSS and JavaScript to target specific elements.

IP Address (Internet Protocol Address): A numerical label assigned to each device connected to a network, used to identify and locate devices on the internet.

J

JavaScript: A programming language used to create dynamic and interactive elements on web pages. It runs in the browser and can manipulate HTML and CSS.

jQuery: A fast, lightweight JavaScript library that simplifies HTML document traversal, event handling, and animation.

M

MySQL: An open-source relational database management system commonly used in web applications to store and manage data.

P

PHP (Hypertext Preprocessor): A server-side scripting language used to create dynamic web pages and manage server-side logic.

PDO (PHP Data Objects): A database access layer in PHP that provides a consistent interface for interacting with various database systems.

R

Responsive Design: An approach to web design that ensures a website's layout and content adapt to different screen sizes and devices, providing an optimal viewing experience.

S

Session: A mechanism for storing user data across multiple page requests, allowing a web application to maintain state and track user activity.

SQL (Structured Query Language): A language used for managing and manipulating relational databases, including querying, updating, and deleting data.

T

Template: A pre-designed layout or structure used to create consistent and reusable components in a web application. Templates help streamline development and ensure uniformity.

U

UI (User Interface): The visual elements of a software application that users interact with, including buttons, forms, and menus.

X

XSS (Cross-Site Scripting): A type of security vulnerability where an attacker injects malicious scripts into a web page, potentially compromising user data and application security.
