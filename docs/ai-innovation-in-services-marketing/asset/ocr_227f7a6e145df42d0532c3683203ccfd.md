On the other hand, there are consumers with different profiles who may be more skeptical about health apps and may have concerns about the privacy and security of their health data, as it may be accidentally exposed or disclosed to unauthorized third parties (Dennison et al., 2013; Stephen et al., 2022). Additionally, some people may feel that health apps are not as effective as consulting a doctor in person, or may not have access to the necessary technology to use these apps (Mills et al., 2020).

Another barrier to the adoption of these health apps would be the cost of accessing this technology. According to research, some users prefer free apps because they believe that they already deliver enough to meet their needs, but this does not mean that people are not willing to pay for the service. Some people would pay a small amount if they found an app worth buying, that is, only if the app had exclusive features and functions that other free apps do not have (Alqahtani & Orji, 2020; Aydin & Silahataroglu, 2021; Peng et al., 2016).

In general, the patient's perception of health apps depends heavily on their personal experience and individual health needs, as well as the quality and reliability of the available apps. It is important for health app developers to consider these factors and work to provide digital health tools that are useful, accessible, and secure for all patients. This is what the vast majority seek, whether young or old (Lagan et al., 2021; A. Nunes et al., 2019; Stoyanov et al., 2016).

Health apps have become increasingly popular and accessible in recent years and offer a wide range of features to help people manage their health and well-being. From the perspective of healthcare professionals, these apps can have various importance, namely:

Improve patient communication and engagement: Health apps can help improve communication between patients and healthcare professionals, allowing patients to track and share real-time health data. This can lead to better patient engagement and greater collaboration between the patient and the healthcare professional (European Commission, 2014).

Facilitate screening and management of chronic diseases: Many health apps are designed to help people manage chronic diseases such as diabetes, high blood pressure, and asthma. These apps allow patients to monitor their symptoms, medications, and daily lifestyle habits, which can assist healthcare professionals in assessing treatment progress and adjusting the treatment plan if necessary (Krishnan & Selvam, 2019).
