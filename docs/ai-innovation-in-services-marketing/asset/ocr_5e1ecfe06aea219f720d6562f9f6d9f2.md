Figure 2 presents the proposed conceptual model, which is then described in terms of its main components and respective contribution.

The model presented targets large companies, which have the financial and technological capabilities to implement (or subcontract the implementation) an AI layer across all business processes. In the context we explore, small and medium-sized companies, which do not have the capacity for such implementation, will take advantage of the AI-tools but will not be capable of implementing an implementation similar to what we propose.

A typical company that the model contemplates is a holding company, which has several brands, in different business sectors and/or in the same line of business. In this context, there is a need to manage several brands.

The model presents the “autopilot” vision for brand management, which means that it is up to AI to understand how to manage a given brand, whether through the extinction or creation of brands. It is up to AI to understand the insights that intelligent analysis of data obtained through customer interactions can offer. In this way, the company’s response could be the extinction of a brand that is not profitable and/or the creation of new brands that embody new products that will predictably be successful with customers. The capacity for such perception materializes a concept where a brand and its products can be the result of the customers’ vision and not the company’s own – customers design the products that the company should offer them, shaping them over time.
