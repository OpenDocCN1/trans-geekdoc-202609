## Research Samples and Sampling Procedure

The interviews included a sample of Digital Marketing Campaigns potential customers. Two demographic groups were examined: a younger group from 20 to 30 years old (A20-30), and an older one from 40 to 50 years old (A40-50). Both groups of respondents were selected only if they engaged with digital marketing campaigns specifically in Lithuania in the previous two months. The semi-structured interviews provided a deeper comprehension of the research topic while comparing two distinct age groups, helping to identify any similarities or differences between their engagement level. A flexible approach to these interviews enables the different opinions, preferences, and attitudes of each participant, offering new thoughts and perspectives on the factors that could increase customer engagement levels.

Non-probabilistic quota sampling was used to select ten candidates based on defined criteria. Respondents were chosen specifically depending on the factors needed for the interview, such as age, location, and the level of interaction with the digital ads. The judgmental sampling was used by the researcher as it was assumed that certain features and criteria for the sample can reflect the population (Efil & Negida, 2017). In this case, respondents were chosen according to their age category and the action they recently completed online – either clicked or bought something from the ad. This method helps to identify the most recent population’s needs and wishes and understand how digital marketing campaigns can increase engagement through the respondents’ perspectives. Non-probabilistic quota sampling is especially helpful when the researcher wants to make comparisons of different groups while trying to understand if their behavior differs or not. Therefore, the experiences and insights gained from the respondents of different age categories are valuable in helping better understand how to create a positive experience with digital marketing campaigns.

## Research Instrument

The research instrument used in this study was semi-structured interviews. They enabled to provide more precise insights into the topic of what makes respondents interact the most with digital marketing campaigns, helping to broaden the experiences of each answer.

The semi-structured interviews were divided into four question groups, which consisted of different questions that were supported by theories discussed in a literature review part (Table 1). The first question group focuses on questions that reveal if the respondent is suitable for the research based on the defined criteria. The second question group explores different consumer preferences and behaviors with digital ads. The third question group goes deeply into the topic of online engagement experiences, trying to get insights about personalized content and components that can improve each experience. Finally, the last question group focuses on customer engagement dynamics and tries to obtain some suggestions for increasing customer engagement and identifying the drawbacks of digital marketing campaigns. Respondents were invited to elaborate on each question, providing their suggestions and thoughts in detail to get an extensive understanding of their experiences. Moreover, at the end of the interview, respondents could provide more perspectives on the research topic if they found it relevant.

## Research Process

Before starting each interview, participants were asked to fill in a consent form that their answers could be later used in the research while complying with data protection standards. At the start of each interview, respondents were also asked if they allow to record the audio of the interview to get their consent to record their answers. Later on, the research topic and goal were explained for the interviewees to understand their contribution to the research. The respondents were able to ask any questions before and during the interview to get any clarifications needed and create an open and comfortable environment for them. Question 5 was explained in detail to make it easier for the respondents to understand and answer the question. When respondents found it difficult to express their preferences, follow-up questions were asked to remind them of some experiences or help to get some ideas.
