### 4.3 Confirmatory Factor Analysis and Path Analysis

To find the underlying factor structure of a data collection, we employed confirmatory factor analysis (Jöreskog, 1969). We used IBM SPSS AMOS.26 to perform confirmatory factor analysis, test hypotheses, and analyze coefficients’ paths. The maximum-likelihood estimator is employed to run the model.

Three categories of model fit (i.e., absolute fit, incremental fit, and parsimonious fit) are considered to evaluate our model. Absolute fit contained GFI (Goodness of Fitness Index) = 0.997 ≥ 0.80 (Hashmi et al., 2020), RMSEA (Root mean square error of approximation) = 0.035 ≤ 0.08 (Hair et al., 2010); incremental fit comprised CFI (Comparative Fit Index) = 0.997 ≥ 0.90 (Bagozzi & Yi, 1988; Hair et al., 2010), TLI (Tucker-Lewis Index) = 0.984 ≥ 0.90 (Bagozzi and Yi, 1988), IFI (Incremental Fit Index) = 0.997 ≥ 0.90 (Bollen, 1990), NFI (Normed Fit Index) = 0.990 ≥ 0.80 (Bentler & Bonnet, 1980), AGFI (Adjusted Goodness of Fit Index) = 0.975 ≥ 0.80 (Jöreskog & Sörbom, 1993); and parsimonious fit included chi-square normalized by degrees of freedom (Chisq/df)= 1.436 ≤3 (Bagozzi & Yi, 1988; Kline, 1998), PNFI (Parsimony-Adjusted Measures Index) = 0.198 >0.05 (Bentler & Bonnet, 1980). Our research revealed that all fitness indicators had been attained and were deemed to be sufficiently error-free.
