We use gender as the moderator variable and experience as the mediating variable. To answer our research question, we collected data via online questionnaires, and we used EFA, CFA, and path analysis. The findings of our study can be summarized as follows: First and foremost, as argued in previous studies, we found a significant and positive impact of experience on the job performance of Tunisian employees. Second, we showed the absence of an impact of artificial intelligence on job performance. Meanwhile, gender has no influence on job performance. Finally, we highlighted that the mediating effects of experience and the moderating effects of gender have no impact on the artificial intelligence-job performance correlation.

It is worth mentioning the major contributions of our research. First, our result depicts the fact that, while our chapter may not demonstrate a direct impact of AI on job performance, managers should be aware of the importance of artificial intelligence in their businesses. As artificial intelligence is rapidly changing the way companies operate, managers may consider the introduction and implementation of digital tools in their organizations to remain competitive. Specifically, AI is used in customer service, fraud detection, risk evaluation, etc. Second, managers must identify both internal factors (i.e., leadership, resources, and the culture of the company) and external factors (i.e., data accessibility and the regulation of the environment) that may affect the implementation of artificial intelligence. For instance, managers may need to focus more attention on small projects if the company lacks the resources to invest in artificial intelligence. Third, managers need to be aware of the negative effects of artificial intelligence on firm performance in order to mitigate them. For example, managers can help their employees learn new skills and train them in new technologies. This can reduce workers’ anxiety and uncertainty about their employment prospects. They can also effectively provide information about the new technology and participate with employees in the selection and adoption of the new technology (e.g., through questionnaires, organizing brainstorming meetings, etc.). Fourth, it is crucial to acknowledge that some gender stereotypes continue to exist in the workplace. However, we found that gender does not affect job performance. Therefore, managers should prioritize the selection and promotion of the top applicants, regardless of the candidate’s gender. Instead, they should make sure that every employee, regardless of gender, has access to equal opportunities and make judgments based only on each employee’s performance, skills, and qualifications. Indeed, they should promote a culture that encourages gender diversity, as it can result in a staff that is more creative, motivated, and productive.
