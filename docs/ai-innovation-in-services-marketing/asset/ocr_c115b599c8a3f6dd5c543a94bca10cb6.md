# ## 2. LITERATURE REVIEW

# ### 2.1. AI and Job Performance

The concept of artificial intelligence was first introduced in 1956 by John McCarthy, the “father of AI” (Benko & Lányi, 2009). It is defined as the capacity of machines to learn from experience, adapt to new inputs, and accomplish human tasks (Brooks, 1991; Duan et al., 2019). AI can be categorized into analytic AI (it shows only features consistent with cognitive intelligence), human-inspired AI (it includes elements of both cognitive and emotional intelligence), and humanized AI (it characterizes all kinds of competencies such as cognitive, emotional, and social intelligence) (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2019). A growing body of literature has emerged that aims at introducing the concept of artificial intelligence and examining its role. Indeed, there is disagreement among researchers about the role of AI. Many contributions (Abusalma, 2021; Agrawal et al., 2019; Edwards et al., 2000; Larivière et al., 2017) have highlighted that artificial intelligence can replace human capital in the performance of some tasks (i.e., adaptive tasks, contextual performance, and organizational performance). More specifically, AI systems may perform an increasing number of tasks without the control or supervision of humans (i.e., autonomous machines that make decisions on their own, provide customers with personalized offers, find patterns in the data that humans would never look at, and manage employees) (Kumar et al., 2019; Shaw et al., 2019; Vrontis et al., 2022; Złotowski et al., 2017). Meanwhile, other studies (Jarrah i, 2018; Miller, 2018; Shaw et al., 2019; Wilson & Daugherty, 2018) have found that AI should complement human capital, not replace it. For instance, AI has been used for decision-making (i.e., strategic, tactical, and operational decisions) (Duan et al., 2019; Edwards et al., 2000) and as a decision support tool, not to replace the role of decision makers (Duan et al., 2019). In this context, Shaw et al. (2019) added that artificial intelligence lacks empathy. More specifically, AI systems may not be able to comprehend or respond to emotions in the same way as humans.
