Coombs and Miles (2000) and Gallouj and Windrum (2009) stated that products and services are similar during innovation. Thus, there is no need for a completely separate approach, and there is a need to integrate both product-oriented and service-oriented approaches. This school of thought was termed the “Synthesis approach”. Carlborg et al. (2013), who reviewed the evolution of service innovation literature, found that at the formation stage, service innovation was focused on the product and production process. Later in the maturity phase, literature was focused on the involvement of customers and in the final stage, during the multidimensional phase, researchers reviewed more on multidisciplinary research where service innovation is viewed as a multidimensional construct that includes product-related aspects, where the findings of Carlborg et al. (2013) is in line with the approaches proposed by Coombs and Miles (2000). Considering the authors’ argument, building innovative business models or strategies in service by adopting product innovation components alone is difficult; hence, integrated, holistic models are essential.

# 5. BARRIERS AND FACILITATORS OF SERVICE INNOVATION

Understanding diverse considerations is necessary while developing a business model or service plan. The literature on innovation identified and categorised elements that serve as obstacles or enablers. Economic factors like risks to the economy, costs associated with innovation, and a lack of funding were categorised by Martikainen (2008). Internal factors like market information, organizational structure, and a need for more technical information were also included, as were other factors like ignorance of customers’ attention to suppliers and rivals. Similar lists of human traits and monetary considerations for marketing management were made by Larsen and Lewis in 2007. However, Segarra-Blasco and Arauzo-Carod (2008) focused on financial and market expertise. Other factors mentioned by several authors include the characteristics of the global market, supplier and customer feedback, rival domestic market characteristics, strong project leaders’ access to capital and human resources capabilities, taxation of new goods processes, and government tax (Foley & Gren 1995 Tidd et al. 1997 and Pihkala et al. 2002).

The significance of cutting-edge technologies was emphasized by authors (Moore & Benbasat, 1991, Verhagen & Dolen, 2009; Verma & Tandon, 2022), who concentrated on the review of technology considerations.
