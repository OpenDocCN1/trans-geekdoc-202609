Among these paradigms, the distinctions between positivist and interpretive stances have garnered significant attention (Bell & Bryman, 2022; Travers, 2001). To elaborate further, positivists aim to explain human behavior, whereas interpretivists are primarily concerned with comprehending it (Bryman, 2016).

Positivism is a philosophical approach that aims to formulate and test hypotheses to explain phenomena and develop laws. Researchers collect data to contribute to knowledge and facilitate generalization; all achieved through an objective lens (Richards, 2021; Tracy, 2019). In contrast, interpretivists adopt a holistic approach, seeking to understand how individuals perceive and make sense of the world around them. These researchers immerse themselves in individuals’ experiences, actions, and contexts, striving to understand the world from their unique perspectives.

Acknowledging the ongoing debate, this study’s methodology aligns with the interpretive paradigm, reflecting the researcher’s philosophical stance and the study’s goals. This approach facilitates the exploration of diverse, socially constructed realities in the intricate world (Goodson & Phillimore, 2004). It involves challenging the researcher’s assumptions through participant perspectives (Denny, 2007) and exploring diverse, sometimes contradictory viewpoints on specific marketing campaign experiences. This decision is pivotal as the study seeks to comprehend different stakeholder perspectives and experiences related to the marketing campaign, each offering a unique subjective reality.

Furthermore, the researcher employs a qualitative research approach, aligning with the interpretive paradigm’s emphasis on knowledge acquisition and co-creation within the natural world (Denzin & Lincoln, 2011; Mason, 2017). The nature of this research is exploratory, grounded in existing theories and knowledge while remaining open to the potential discovery of new phenomena or relationships. Consistent with Flick’s (2018) concept of creating a new “version of a world”, this research aims to contribute to existing theory by adding fresh insights to the field.

## The Case Study as a Research Approach

This study aligns with Gummesson’s (2005) argument that a case study approach can effectively achieve the conceptualization, understanding of phenomena, and theory development. The case study approach is widely used in the social sciences and is gaining increasing recognition. According to Yin (1984), a case study is an empirical inquiry that: investigates a contemporary phenomenon within its real-life context; when the boundaries between phenomenon and context are not clearly evident; and in which multiple sources of evidence are used (Yin, 1984, p. 23).

The case study design is particularly fitting for addressing “how” and “why” questions, with a significant focus on providing explanatory insights. It does not provide control over behavioral events but concentrates on thoroughly examining contextual conditions (Yin, 2003). While marketing research endorses case studies (Bonoma, 1985; Riege, 2003), some scholars have voiced concerns about its suitability in marketing contexts (Gummesson, 2005; Zainal, 2007). Despite being a widely employed research approach, it lacks comprehensive methodological guidelines (Yin, 2011, p. xix). Nonetheless, Stake (2005) argues that case study research's unique and human qualities can be attributed to the researcher rather than the methods themselves.

The case study approach is widely regarded as a valuable and distinctive research method, possessing unique characteristics that make it well-suited for various investigations (Tellis, 1997). It offers an excellent platform for in-depth exploration and comprehension of complex issues within specific contexts (Schell, 1992; Zainal, 2007). Additionally, according to Hartley (2004), the case study can yield rich and insightful information about organizational behavior and the dynamic interplay between behavior or processes and the contextual factors, where both influence each other.

Case study research is pivotal in refining and extending organizational theory, a central endeavor within organizational research (Eisenhardt, 1989). Therefore, the utilization of the case study approach in this study is well-justified. It stems from the belief that a thorough understanding of this phenomenon and its context can only be attained through a comprehensive examination using this approach.

## A Single Case Study

Case research serves various purposes, from theoretical to practical (Stake, 2013). Cases can vary in complexity and scope, covering individuals, organizations, behaviors, events, or social phenomena (Yin, 2011). Case study research can be a single-case or multiple-case design, depending on the research issue (Gustafsson, 2017). Stake (2013) categorizes case studies as intrinsic, instrumental, or collective. Intrinsic cases focus solely on understanding the case itself. Instrumental cases aim to deepen comprehension of phenomena, using the case as a tool. Collective cases involve gathering and coordinating data from multiple cases.

In this study, the emphasis aligns with instrumental case study research. The researcher seeks a deep understanding of a marketing campaign to comprehensively describe, generate knowledge, and contribute to theory development (Gustafsson, 2017). Consistent with Eisenhardt’s (1989) viewpoint, this approach aligns itself with the interpretivist perspective and supports the development of theories in areas where existing theory or empirical evidence may be insufficient.

## DATA COLLECTION WITHIN THE CASE STUDY APPROACH

As Blandford (2013) highlights, research approaches vary, and researchers have flexibility in data collection and validation methods. While triangulation is often discussed as an alternative (Decrop, 1999; Denzin & Lincoln, 2011; Flick, 2018), Mason (2017) proposes facet methodology as a more flexible and insightful approach. Unlike triangulation, which can risk bypassing a thorough engagement with the chosen methodology, facet methodology fosters creativity and intuition, prioritizing insights over comprehensive knowledge (Mason, 2017). Considering this study’s qualitative approach and interpretive stance, facet methodology is deemed suitable. It allows for a deeper exploration of the case study organization, offering insight into multiple socially co-constructed realities, knowledge, and interactions within a natural setting from diverse perspectives.

Furthermore, in qualitative research, Stake (2013) and Yin (2011) outline six sources of evidence in case studies: interviews, direct observation, participant observation, documents, archival records, and physical artefacts. These sources aid researchers in accessing organizational resources, planning data collection, and preparing for unexpected events (Tellis, 1997). Despite this, Krueger and Casey (2015) emphasize that research methods vary in effectiveness based on their situational use, the researcher’s competence, and the research environment. In this study, the primary data collection method involves the responses from target contacts, complemented by the examination of secondary sources before the marketing campaign.

An ABM campaign was implemented to engage with ten selected companies operating in the game development sector. The campaign targeted four specific individuals in each company, making a total of 40 contacts. The aim was to personalize the communication to encourage engagement, collect feedback, and pave the way for potential future business prospects. Paper postcards were sent to the target contacts in various European countries, which included France, Germany, Sweden, Finland, Malta, and the United Kingdom. The postcards were dispatched to C-level contacts, from CEOs to business development directors. These individuals were chosen for their influential decision-making roles and involvement in developing and managing business strategies. An interval of three to four days was maintained between each mailing. This study also incorporated secondary data sources besides physical artefacts, such as postcards.
