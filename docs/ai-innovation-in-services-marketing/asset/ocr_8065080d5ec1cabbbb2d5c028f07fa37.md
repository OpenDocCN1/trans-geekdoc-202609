disruptive innovation is an innovation that creates a new market and new value network, that is, the innovation that improves the service or product that already does not exist in the market and is designed for the first time. Whereas sustaining innovation does not create a new innovative market, rather it improves the value of the existing one. Similarly, researchers classified measurement based on the degree of novelty as radical and incremental innovation (Ettlie et al. 1984; Tushman et al. 1985; Dewar and Dutton 1986; Orlikowski 1991). Radical innovation is nonlinear paradigmatic changes that depart from existing knowledge or practice. On the other hand, incremental is a linear cumulative change that implies a minor improvement or adjustment in existing knowledge or technology.

Product innovation, process innovation, marketing innovation, input innovation, and organizational innovation are the five key categories of service innovation (Gadrey et al., 1995; Tejada and Moreno, 2013). It majorly relies on market capability that encompasses the customer orientation (Akter et al., 2023). In relation to this, the authors need to consider some typologies, such as marketing innovation, input typology, and organization innovation, while measuring service innovation is the main issue identified. On the other hand, several authors added additional typologies to the current service innovation typologies, including administrative innovation, strategic typology, and technology innovation. The measurement elements in these typologies were mostly related to product innovation, whether typologies were included or excluded when measuring service innovation. Later arguments were made against the inclusion of technological innovation in the typologies that were developed to measure service innovation by authors such as Barras (1986), Edvardsson (1997), Sirilli and Evangelista (1998).
