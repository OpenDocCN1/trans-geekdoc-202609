In this competitive landscape, characterized also by digital health tools that educate patients and monitor their own health, professionals must be seen as qualified and reliable to meet patients’ needs, expectations, and sacrifices. Therefore, it has become essential to adopt effective marketing strategies for healthcare professionals to stand out in the market. In addition to digital health tools, healthcare marketing should also be seen as a way to educate and inform patients, rather than just promoting services and products (Castillo et al., 2017; Tallia et al., 2018).

However, it is important to remember that advertising, which is regulated by specific laws, and marketing in the healthcare field must be ethical and responsible; thus, marketing strategies must be evidence-based, aiming to protect patients and avoid misleading promises. Therefore, it is essential for healthcare professionals and institutions to follow these laws when devising their marketing strategies (Castillo et al., 2017; Tallia et al., 2018).

Advertising legislation in healthcare varies according to each country and/or region but generally follows common guidelines and is regulated by government health agencies. For example, in Brazil, the National Health Surveillance Agency (ANVISA) is responsible for regulating advertising of healthcare products and services, while in Portugal, the enforcement of these rules is the responsibility of the National Authority of Medicines and Health Products (INFARMED) (ANVISA, 2020; INFARMED, 2023; Decree-Law No. 238/2015 of the Ministry of Health, 2015).

Advertisements for healthcare products, such as medications, equipment, and medical treatments, must be accurate and cannot make false or misleading claims about the effectiveness or safety of the product/service. Furthermore, communication cannot exaggerate the benefits of the product or even downplay its risks. Healthcare professionals must also follow legislation on how they communicate with their audience; they cannot make false or misleading claims about products and services they offer and must always maintain patient privacy and confidentiality (Abdalla & Castilho, 2017; ANVISA, 2020; Decree-Law No. 238/2015 of the Ministry of Health, 2015; Sato, 2002).
