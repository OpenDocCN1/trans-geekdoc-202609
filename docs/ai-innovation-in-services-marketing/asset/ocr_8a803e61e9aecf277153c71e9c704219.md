Blockchain-based identity verification allows individuals to control and share their identity information securely, enhancing privacy and reducing the risk of identity theft. Blockchain enables the tokenization of physical and financial assets, turning them into digital tokens that can be traded on blockchain platforms. This includes tokenized real estate, stocks, and even art. Security tokens issued on blockchain can represent ownership in traditional financial instruments, providing increased liquidity and accessibility to a broader range of investors. Several central banks are also exploring the use of blockchain technology to create their own digital currencies. A central bank digital currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a country's fiat currency. China's digital currency electronic payment (DCEP) project is one of the notable examples, where the People's Bank of China is experimenting with a digital yuan built on blockchain technology. Blockchain enhances security and reduces the risk of fraud in financial transactions. Its immutable and transparent nature makes it easier to trace and audit transactions, aiding in anti-money laundering (AML) compliance. Financial institutions can leverage blockchain to create a more secure and auditable record of transactions, reducing the risk of fraudulent activities (Mohd Javaid et. Al, 2022).

# MOBILE PAYMENT SOLUTIONS

Mobile payment solutions have become a ubiquitous part of modern financial transactions, transforming the way people pay for goods and services. These solutions leverage the convenience of smartphones and mobile devices to enable quick, secure, and contactless transactions. Various mobile payment platforms have emerged, each offering unique features and catering to diverse consumer preferences. Mobile wallets are digital applications that allow users to store payment information, such as credit and debit card details, securely on their smartphones. Examples include Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Samsung Pay. Users can make payments at physical retail stores by tapping their smartphones on contactless terminals or online through in-app or web purchases. These wallets enhance security by using tokenization, where a unique token is generated for each transaction, minimizing the risk of exposing sensitive payment information. Peer-to-peer (P2P) payment apps enable users to send and receive money directly from their mobile devices. Venmo, owned by PayPal, is a popular example in the United States. It allows users to split bills, pay friends, and request payments with ease. The app often includes a social feed, adding a social component to financial transactions.
