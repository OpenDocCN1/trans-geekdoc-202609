Services have specific market characteristics and needs. The main differences between services and products are that services are fundamentally intangible and are created through direct interactions with customers (Kotler & Armstrong, 2015). Services allow for differentiation, modification, and customization, thereby enabling margin and profit. Services range from telecommunications to banking services, among others, such as schools, hospitals, professionals, and even domestic services, whether through the Internet or retail (Kahtalian, 2000).

According to Kahtalian (2000) and Kotler & Armstrong (2015), services also need to be understood for their specific characteristics, such as:

+   - Inseparability: each service has a moment when its production and consumption are simultaneous/indissociable. This differs from product manufacturing companies that have a clearly defined production time and consumption time. Thus, while a teacher ‘produces’ their class, it is ‘consumed’ by the students simultaneously.
+   - Variability: services are variable, meaning they can vary between the provider and the customer. This characteristic has both positive and negative aspects. The advantage is that variation allows for service customization and differentiation. The disadvantage is that it is difficult to establish flawless service standards because quality depends on the provider.
+   - Intangibility: services are not visible or tangible, meaning they cannot be seen, tasted, felt, heard, or smelled before purchase. Tangible items serve as evidence of service. They are guides, suggestions of what it should be. It can even be said that one of the marketing techniques for a service is precisely to ‘make the service tangible,’ that is, to make the invisible visible, emphasizing what the service promises.
+   - Perishability: services cannot be stored. They are temporary and delivered at a specific time and place, meaning the company must be able to manage the service demand. It is important to find the right balance between supply and demand to avoid a common problem in services, which can be failing to meet excess demand or incurring high operational costs by not meeting necessary demand.

Thinking about service marketing means thinking about value through performance. It values people, internal marketing, relationships established with customers and partners. It is understanding and meeting customer expectations through the transformation provided by the service (Kahtalian, 2000).
