# # THE TRANSFORMATIONS OF TECHNOLOGY IN HEALTHCARE

In the definition by Bozic et al. (2004), ‘advances in healthcare’ can be understood as “all medications, instruments, and medical and surgical procedures used in medical services, as well as the organization and support systems in which these services are provided” (p. 1305). The International Business Machines (n.d.) expanded the definition presented by Bozic et al. (2004) and added that advances in healthcare encompass all technologies developed to assist healthcare institutions, including AI, information technology systems, among others.

The traditional approach to healthcare delivery, focusing on hospitals, presented some disadvantages for healthcare professionals, such as limited time to examine the patient (which made it impossible to collect information about their daily activities, essential for diagnosis and treatment choices), an aging population (which required more medical care, increasing the necessary resources), a shortage of healthcare professionals, and rising costs associated with service delivery, among others (Abdullayeva, 2022).

The evolution of healthcare services came to address these disadvantages, resulting in the development and implementation of medical technologies. Similar to what happened in the industry, where technological evolution and innovation were described through industrial revolutions (known as Industry 1.0 to Industry 4.0), the evolution of healthcare services can be described in four phases, called Health Care 1.0 to Health Care 4.0 (Kaur et al., 2021; Li & Carayon, 2021).
