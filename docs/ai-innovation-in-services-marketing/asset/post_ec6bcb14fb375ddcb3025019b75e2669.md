What can be included as a “document” involves a broad range of textual materials, for example, organizational documents, notes, cards, case records, email threads and reports, as well as various technological, digital, and social media materials, such as websites, social networking sites, and visual or sound materials (Coffey, 2014; Tight, 2019). Documentary research can be considered secondary data research (Tight, 2019) because the researcher does not directly collect or produce such secondary data. This study considered various internal organizational documents.

## Internal Organizational Documents

Organizational documents are data or evidence of how an organization represents and accounts for itself (Coffey, 2014). Therefore, internal organizational documents, such as business and marketing plans, research reports, and other additional documents related to Invest Lithuania’s business management, were analyzed. Internal documents are essential in business research because they help describe an organization and its history (Cassell & Symon, 2004). They further provide organizational value structures (Kabanoff et al., 1995) or help generate new business models for creating shared value (Michelini & Fiorentino, 2012).

## ANALYSIS OF PRIMARY DATA

In this study, data analysis was conducted using NVivo 12 Pro, a software package developed by QSR International. It is worth noting that no single industry-leading data analysis program exists (Bell et al., 2022). The choice of software primarily depends on its alignment with the research methodology, the researcher’s experience, and its effectiveness in facilitating the analysis process (Silver & Lewins, 2007). NVivo 12 Pro was selected for its extensive features, including the ability to hierarchically create and organize nodes, seamlessly open or initiate projects, user-friendly navigation, robust search functionality, and support for importing various formats of materials (Bell et al., 2022).

Creswell and Báez (2020) point out that text data are dense and time-consuming to analyze and interpret. However, the coding process is a crucial step in making sense of such data, and NVivo 12 Pro was employed to aid the researcher in this endeavor. A set of codes was established before the analysis to streamline the coding process. These codes were formulated based on the research objectives and critical factors identified in the literature review.

During the analysis process, a structure of themes emerged. The themes were as follows: positive feedback, exploring collaboration, future opportunities, interest in game development studios, positive impressions of Vilnius and geographic limitations. (A detailed description can be found in the analysis section.) In this regard, NVivo proved invaluable for storing and managing data and ideas, visualizing thematic patterns as they unfolded, and facilitating comparisons and in-depth analysis. It is important to acknowledge that human involvement remains essential, and software cannot replace the role of the researcher in data analysis (Jackson & Bazeley, 2019).

## Analysis of Secondary Data

Secondary data analysis was performed using content analysis within NVivo, a technique with a history spanning more than 60 years that has found applications in various fields, including business (Neuendorf, 2017). Krippendorff (2018), a recognized authority in the field, defines content analysis as a method for drawing replicable and valid inferences from texts or other meaningful materials within their contexts of use. Content analysis is not limited to written text; it can encompass verbal, textual, visual, sound, print, and electronic forms (Bryman, 2016), including images (Bell et al., 2022).

When conducting a case study of an organization, content analysis primarily aims to scrutinize media items, various texts, and documents that are either produced by the organization, such as annual reports, or written about it, like articles in the business press (Bell et al., 2022). In this context, the researcher examined diverse content generated by Invest Lithuania, namely internal documents and reports related to previous marketing campaigns and their outcomes. Both sources are conceptually rich and offer valuable insights into the organization’s description, historical evolution, and the development and management of its core concepts.

## LIMITATIONS

The qualitative research approach has its limitations. According to Goulding (2002), qualitative research is often criticized for being purely descriptive, needing a more structured and straightforward procedure, and potentially producing subjective data that may not meet rigorous standards. Furthermore, concerns are raised about the need for more transparency in the evolving procedures and processes adopted during the research (Miles & Huberman, 1994). Additionally, as Denzin and Lincoln (2011) pointed out, the criteria for evaluating and interpreting qualitative research can be problematic and may lead to misinterpretation. This chapter addresses these limitations by explaining the rationale for selecting methods such as content analysis and detailing how they were employed. The utilization of Computer-Assisted Qualitative Data Analysis Software (CAQDAS) has supported the qualitative data analysis process, ensuring that the research procedures are well-documented and enabling readers to examine all the methods employed by the researcher.

Employing a single case study methodology may also be perceived as a limitation. Despite the drawbacks of alternative methods, such as conducting multiple case studies and facilitating data comparison (Gustafsson, 2017), the single case study approach remains a credible choice. Dyer Jr. and Wilkins (1991) argue that a single case study can yield more prosperous and nuanced theoretical insights. Moreover, it allows the researcher to thoroughly examine a single case over an extended period. Therefore, a single case study is the most suitable choice when the research objective is to study a specific organization (Yin, 2003).

Additionally, specific risks are involved when using postal services to send postcards for a marketing campaign. The delivery process is partially within the control of the campaign implementers. Also, the addresses sourced from company websites could not be accurate, and the selected target individuals may have left their respective companies. As such, it is crucial to exercise due diligence and carefully consider these potential risks before implementing such campaigns. Despite these challenges, Valenti et al. (2023) have found that direct mail effectively drives customer acquisition in the offline channel.

## ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Several ethical concerns merit consideration in this context. One crucial issue pertains to confidentiality (Bryman, 2016), explicitly safeguarding the security of participants’ responses (Robson, 2002). It is essential to emphasize that this study rigorously upholds a commitment to safeguarding the companies’ confidentiality. Also, despite the researcher’s access to potentially sensitive business practices and information, all matters of confidentiality were diligently managed following the mutual agreement established with representatives from Invest Lithuania. Any documents designated as commercially confidential were deliberately excluded from the analysis.

Regarding participants’ responses, strict measures were implemented to ensure anonymity and prevent identification. Each participant was assigned a unique code, for example, G1, and their job positions were anonymized using a generic term, “strategic-level manager”, to avoid any potential identification.

## ANALYSIS OF THE INVEST LITHUANIA CASE STUDY

After a thorough review of internal documents and reports related to the company’s strategy, including past marketing campaigns and their outcomes, it became apparent that there is a lack of knowledge and know-how in identifying the most effective marketing tools, channels, and potential target lists. There was also a noticeable absence of constructive collaboration between the marketing and sales teams, an essential element for the successful execution of campaigns and overall organizational performance. According to Vajre (2016), “marketing must play a part in sales acceleration, and sales must play a part in marketing” (p. 41). Despite this, Invest Lithuania’s marketing and sales teams have continuously executed campaigns within their target industries in recent years. These initiatives involved testing various channels, tools, and strategies and adapting and modifying strategic KPIs while exploring automation options via customer relationship management (CRM) systems.

In response to these evolving circumstances and the insights gained from past endeavors, it was determined that Invest Lithuania’s marketing and sales teams should embrace a new approach. As a result, the decision was made to implement ABM, a personalized B2B marketing strategy to target specific companies. This strategic shift aims to enhance engagement with target foreign investors by tailoring content to their specific needs and preferences (Burgess & Munn, 2021). The expectation is that the personalized approach inherent in ABM can facilitate smoother communication and lead to increased sales opportunities.

The decision to implement the One-to-Few ABM campaign was deliberate and well-considered. This approach was selected for its suitability when targeting a specific subset of potential clients within the broader target audience (Agaba, 2021). By focusing on a select group, the campaign aimed to create more personalized and tailored interactions by optimizing the chance of engaging these specific prospects effectively. This strategic choice aligns with the campaign’s core objectives, enhancing its impact and success.

Given this, the imperative strategic decision to utilize AI tools for creating a more captivating marketing campaign emerged. This choice was particularly significant considering the characteristics of the target contacts. It is well-established that an extraordinary effort is required to truly captivate game developers, embodying creativity and delivering the element of surprise (Brynjolfsson & McAfee, 2014). It is essential to acknowledge that this undertaking goes beyond traditional marketing methods. The aim is to establish an emotional connection and evoke a sense of awe, with the overarching objective of leaving a remarkable and enduring impression on the target audience.
