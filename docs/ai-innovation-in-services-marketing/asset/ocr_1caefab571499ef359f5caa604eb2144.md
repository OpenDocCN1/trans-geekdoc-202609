Technological innovation is another important factor in structuring the entire service innovation, such as adjusting or enhancing organizational strategy choices and marketing initiatives. Technology and non-technological aspects of service innovation are combined holistically, with equal emphasis required and articulated using S-D Logic.

All marketing techniques and tactics that were based on products and other tangible goods—also referred to as “goods-dominant logic”—were attacked by Vargo and Morgan in 2005. They published S-D logic, which argued that the exchange of services, as opposed to only things, is what society, markets, and organizations are most concerned with. When it happens, one service is swapped out for another. Additionally, they emphasized how all markets are built on the exchange of services, all communities have service-based economies, and all firms are service enterprises. To focus on service-dominant logic, marketers must switch from G-D reasoning to S-D logic and redefine their fundamental methodologies, concepts, ideas, and strategies. In particular, according to innovation experts, service innovation research is equivalent to product innovation research (Vargo & Morgan) (2005).

Service innovation has been growing rapidly in the modern economy and is changing the way businesses deliver their services and interact with their customers (Freni, 2022). Over the past few years with this development, the consumer preferences, global reach and interconnectedness has also surged leading to a transformation in the traditional businesses (Gallouj et al., 2015). This rapid growth is reshaping businesses leading to an emergence of entirely new markets and innovative business models (Panigrahi et al., 2017). Geographical reach and industry growth are two important drivers of taking the innovation industry forward (Amoroso et al., 2023). An openness to expanding businesses through geographical segments opens up new markets and allows businesses to reach new customer bases, helping foster their unique needs. This expansion encourages businesses to stay updated by innovating continuously and gaining a competitive edge. Simultaneously, overall industry growth also creates opportunities for innovation and helps in expanding knowledge. With the expansion of industry and technology, come new ideas for development and scope for addressing challenges. However, alongside the potential benefits of growth in service innovation, there are numerous challenges that businesses must navigate. New solutions and business models are coming into emergence in order to address these issues. Feng & Sivakumar (2016) give empirical evidence that service innovation has an impact on innovation performance given the propensity of collaboration and shared economy for service firms. It has the potential of creating innovative and collaborative business models that turn out to be cost effective and of expected consumer quality.
