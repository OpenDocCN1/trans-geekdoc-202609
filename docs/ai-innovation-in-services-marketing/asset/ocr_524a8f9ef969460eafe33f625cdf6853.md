analysis process, ensuring that the research procedures are well-documented and
enabling readers to examine all the methods employed by the researcher.

Employing a single case study methodology may also be perceived as a limitation.
Despite the drawbacks of alternative methods, such as conducting multiple case
studies and facilitating data comparison (Gustafsson, 2017), the single case study
approach remains a credible choice. Dyer Jr. and Wilkins (1991) argue that a single
case study can yield more prosperous and nuanced theoretical insights. Moreover, it
allows the researcher to thoroughly examine a single case over an extended period.
Therefore, a single case study is the most suitable choice when the research objective
is to study a specific organization (Yin, 2003).

Additionally, specific risks are involved when using postal services to send
postcards for a marketing campaign. The delivery process is partially within the
control of the campaign implementers. Also, the addresses sourced from company
websites could not be accurate, and the selected target individuals may have left their
respective companies. As such, it is crucial to exercise due diligence and carefully
consider these potential risks before implementing such campaigns. Despite these
challenges, Valenti et al. (2023) have found that direct mail effectively drives customer
acquisition in the offline channel.

# ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Several ethical concerns merit consideration in this context. One crucial issue
pertains to confidentiality (Bryman, 2016), explicitly safeguarding the security of
participants’ responses (Robson, 2002). It is essential to emphasize that this study
rigorously upholds a commitment to safeguarding the companies’ confidentiality.
Also, despite the researcher’s access to potentially sensitive business practices and
information, all matters of confidentiality were diligently managed following the
mutual agreement established with representatives from Invest Lithuania. Any
documents designated as commercially confidential were deliberately excluded
from the analysis.

Regarding participants’ responses, strict measures were implemented to ensure
anonymity and prevent identification. Each participant was assigned a unique code,
for example, G1, and their job positions were anonymized using a generic term,
“strategic-level manager”, to avoid any potential identification.
