### 7.1. Collaboration/ Shared Economy: The changing world scenario has given center stage to collaborative or sharing economies (Belk, 2014). The conceptual foundations of “shared economy” are getting rooted deeply and have remodeled common ideas about ownership and consumption. Consumers understand the advantages of paying to access or share products rather than owning them temporarily (Matzler et al., 2015). It is an emerging form of business model propagating usage rather than ownership, banking upon the networks or communities of people with the support of an internet-based ecosystem. The concept gets implemented in the form of shared consumption (carpooling), shared lifestyles (coworking), shared finance (crowdfunding, alternative currencies), contributory output (digital manufacturing, DIY, 3D printers) etc. Such an economic system gives due attention to resources and remains productive without compromising resource needs (Gupta and Racherla, 2018), leading to sustainability. The sharing economy has enormous potential for creating innovative business models and sustainable consumption practices that may defy traditional businesses (Martin, 2016).

### 7.2. Open Innovation: Business Model Innovation capacity (Cohen & Levinthal 1989). The open system of innovation was propagated by Chesbrough (2003) as it leads to the accumulation of better ideas from external knowledge sources. The usual sources of Innovation for Business models can be competitors, customers, and inter-organizational networks. Organizations can bundle better value by integrating external ideas with internal knowledge. Three archetype processes for open innovation were identified, i.e., Outside-in, inside-out and coupled (Gassmen & Enkel 2004) Figure 1 below. Outside-in process realizes the difference in the presence of knowledge creation and innovation, encouraging the flow of information from outside to inside the company. At the same time, the company can strategically use external channels for commercializing its ideas through an inside-out process. The third-coupled process integrates the two and harnesses the benefits of both processes for innovation.

### 7.3. Gig Economy: The businesses are flexible and adopt a gig-economy business model where work is performed on demand. It is characterized by role-based positions filled by independent contractors and freelancers. The gig economy leverages digital platforms to connect these freelancers with their consumers as and when demanded (Harris, 2017). The employer can represent the main source or an extra source of income for the worker (Lepanjuuri et al., 2018, p. 12). The cynical views of this business model propagate- the erosion of customer relationships, deprivation of employee benefits, and minimalistic labor regulations resulting in lower costs to the businesses. Also, this business model allows its workers and consumers to co-create value in a more adaptable way with the possibility of a flexible lifestyle such as Airbnb, Uber, TaskRabbit or Upwork. It is characterized by choice to perform, autonomy due to the absence of supervisors and focus on the moment’s needs (Ravenelle, 2019, pp. 5-6). Hence gaining popularity and being practiced worldwide with variations such as Uberization, Netflix model and cloud sharing.

### 7.3.1. Uberizations: In the gig economy, Uberization is the business model practised, transforming the market for services with the help of the latest technology, especially smartphones. In this business model, digital platforms or mobile apps replaced the middlemen required to connect talent to the right audience. Such platforms enabled comprehensive interaction between the provider and customer and ensured the minimum quality standards. The model got its name from Uber, creating a platform for people to hire and pay for rides. This uberization extended to any on-demand services such as food, stay, transportation, etc.

### 7.3.2. Netflix Model: The business model adopted by Netflix is an example of integrating the latest technology to fulfill customer expectations by disrupting established market structures. It redefined most of the value creation and value proposition elements by transforming DVD on rent to video streaming services. To retain customers and keep them interested, Netflix invests in new and exclusive content as its value proposition, thus reaching beyond pricing. Another innovative feature that catalyzes success is its recommendation system, which offers suggestions to users based on their consumption patterns. This data mining tool has taken Netflix to another level in terms of value creation and key resources as the basis for the core competencies of its business. It is pre-emptive by forecasting future growth and endowing high-scalability solutions in the gig economy.

### 7.3.3. Cloud Sharing: Cloud technology provides an exciting value proposition for businesses to outsource their Information technology ecosystem. It results in less cost and more avenues for service-based models, leading to business models’ operational success and sustainability. The cloud-based business model can be Software as a Service (SaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS). Interestingly, many more innovative cloud models are evolving every day as per the requirements of business processes.

## 8. DISCUSSION

A crucial gap exists in existing research and frameworks related to innovation, particularly in the context of service industries. While much attention has been given to technological innovations and their impact on manufacturing industries, there's a significant need to recognize and address the unique challenges and opportunities for innovation within service sectors. Service industries often rely on non-technological aspects such as organizational structures, strategic planning, and marketing strategies to drive innovation. However, existing frameworks may not adequately capture the integration of both technological and non-technological components necessary for effective innovation in these sectors.

One key aspect that emerges is the importance of customer engagement and experience management in service innovation. Innovations in service industries must focus not only on product or process improvements but also on enhancing the overall customer experience. This includes addressing issues such as managing customer data, ensuring privacy and security, promoting sustainability, and adhering to ethical considerations. Service businesses must navigate complex challenges related to data management, privacy concerns, and sustainability while innovating their business models. The above chapter also touches upon the evolution of new business models within service industries for example Netflix model. These new models often incorporate features and benefits that go beyond traditional approaches, emphasizing the importance of adaptability and flexibility in today’s dynamic business environment.

In conclusion, the discussion emphasizes the critical need for incorporating non-technical components into innovation strategies within service industries. By addressing the limitations of existing frameworks and adopting a more holistic approach that integrates technological and non-technological aspects, service-based firms can better position themselves for success in today’s competitive marketplace.

## 9. FORECASTING FUTURE

The primary source of competitive advantage in the future is service innovation. Large businesses have adopted service as the catalyst for their organizations’ expansion. Revolutionary new services have demonstrated how service innovation can drive economic growth in developing countries despite limited societal infrastructure or resources. Leading the way in the development of new IT-enabled service models is Salesforce.com, which offers customers software as a service rather than a licensed software product.
