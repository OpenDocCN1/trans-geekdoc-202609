New technologies have the potential to transform the field of preventive health, allowing people to monitor their individual health effectively through more accurate information about their physical and mental conditions, so that they receive personalized preventive treatment, enabling the prolongation of human life (Jiang et al., 2022). However, over the years, there has been a reluctance in the healthcare industry to pave the way for preventive medicine. As mentioned by Akhmetov & Bubnov (2015) and Terkola et al. This resistance affects the implementation of preventive technologies that could improve the population’s health in a personalized and effective way, as there is still a need for more studies to prove their financial benefits and their effectiveness in managing healthcare resources.

# FINAL CONSIDERATIONS

Health apps play a crucial role in implementing preventive healthcare. They act as intermediaries between AI, healthcare professionals, and patients. To understand the acceptance of health apps, it is necessary to understand how their users perceive them, considering the perspectives of both consumers who will use the apps in their daily lives and healthcare professionals who will use the apps to complement and assist their work. The perception of patients regarding health apps can vary greatly depending on various factors, such as their previous experience with these tools, their familiarity with technology in general, their specific health needs, and even their age and level of education (Peng et al., 2016).

For some consumers, health apps can be seen as a convenient and affordable way to monitor their health and manage chronic conditions, allowing them to have more control over their well-being and enabling them to record and track behaviors and goals (Alkaldi et al., 2023; European Commission, 2014; Peng et al., 2016). These individuals appreciate the ease of access to health information and online resources, such as healthy eating tips, exercise routines, and medication reminders.
