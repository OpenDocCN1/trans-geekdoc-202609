Moreover, we have found that gender has no impact on job performance (β = 0.055; p = 0.595 > 0.05) (hypothesis 2 is not supported). There is no consensus on the impact of gender on job performance. Some studies (Mackey et al., 2019; Shirom et al., 2008) have found that there are no significant differences in job performance between men and women (Shirom et al., 2008), while others support the effects of gender on job performance in certain areas (Abusalma, 2021; Banjo & Olufemi, 2014; Khan et al., 2022). Our result may be explained by the fact that there are no differences between men and women in terms of education, experience, funding opportunities, or cognitive capacities (i.e., problem-solving ability, creativity, etc.). Therefore, men and women are equally good at the majority of occupations. Contrary to our expectations, we found that hypothesis 3 (i.e., moderation of gender has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance) is not supported. Due to the fact that artificial intelligence is not designed to discriminate based on gender, gender has no effect on the relationship between AI and job performance. AI is a technology that can help people of all genders perform better in the workplace (i.e., AI can make workers of both genders more effective and productive). Furthermore, we found that the experience of the employee has a positive and significant effect on job performance (hypothesis 4 is supported). This implies that job performance increases with an increase in employee experience (Abusalma, 2021; McDaniel et al., 1988; Quiñones et al., 1995; Ratwani et al., 2020; Schmidt, 1988). This is due to the fact that experienced employees are better equipped for their tasks as they have more knowledge, expertise, and skills. Finally, hypothesis 5 (mediation of experience has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance) is also not supported. Actually, artificial intelligence may be applied to enhance the job performance of workers of all experience levels (i.e., applications of artificial intelligence are not biased against employees with less experience).
