+   * **Visual design elements**, such as: name, logo, graphics, typographic fonts, corporate colors, packaging, shapes, slogan;
+   * **Distinctive characteristics of products**, such as: aroma, flavor, quality;
+   * **Intangible aspects of customer interaction**, such as: reputation, service quality.

Thus, these various elements combined around brands facilitate brands to be a crucial element in creating value for both companies and consumers. They serve as a cognitive shortcut, allowing consumers to make informed purchasing choices without having to spend time and energy evaluating each product individually. Especially in consumer markets, brands play a crucial role as businesses strive to foster consumer trust, loyalty and preferences.

According to Kapferer, brands have different functions in terms of the value perceived by consumers (Kapferer, 2008), among which we highlight: identification, practicality, assurance, optimization, continuity and ethic.

In the context of these functions, there are several benefits for consumers, such as: the brand can be seen clearly and be quickly identified and associated with the product; allows time and energy savings, allowing the brand to be purchased again, due to brand loyalty; the consumer is guaranteed to find the same standard of quality, no matter where or when the product or service is purchased; guarantee of purchasing the best product in its category, the best performance for a given purpose; satisfaction caused by existing intimacy and familiarity with the brand; satisfaction linked to the brand's responsible social behavior towards its external environment.

The brand' value stems from its capability to create an exceptional, positive, and noteworthy significance in the consumer's perception. The value perceived by the customer is the main factor that allows the brand to ensure that customers will be comfortable to pay more for the brand's products and services.

The different approaches to brand equity show that different authors establish different ways for a brand to create value (Kapferer, 2008; Keller & Kotler, 2012; Oliveira et al., 2023), that we highlight: Financial perspective – the financial perspective regards brand equity as the extra financial worth that a brand contributes to a product or service. For instance, it explains why customers are available to pay a higher-price for a product from a reputable brand as opposed to a generic one; Consumer perspective - the consumer perspective centers on the consumer's perception and interaction with the brand. This encompasses brand recognition, consumer's emotional connection with the brand, brand loyalty, and perceived excellence; Furthermore, a combined perspective respects both financial value and consumer perception.
