# PREVENTIVE HEALTH GAINS STRENGTH WITH TECHNOLOGY

The use of new technologies in healthcare is not limited to state-of-the-art equipment found in hospitals, diagnostic centers, or health clinics. The development of information technology (IT) has made citizens protagonists in the so-called preventive medicine, which focuses on disease prevention or early diagnosis to promote faster treatment (Razzak et al., 2020). Thus, the health model that should be applied and advocated by public managers is that of promoting physical and mental well-being, not the prevailing model in most countries: the care model (Fer tonani et al., 2015). In other words, it is necessary to consider preventive measures in human health, that is, to reflect on a subject before any instability occurs in the body and not just after acquiring a disease, for this it is necessary to pay attention to people’s daily routines and habits, emphasizing the improvement of immunity and physical strengthening, thus reducing the probability and risks of diseases. Therefore, the use of new technological tools helps citizens to put this specialty into practice in the healthcare system (He et al., 2021).

A well-known healthcare model that persists in healthcare systems in various countries is the curative model. This model, also known as curative medicine, intervenes in what could not be avoided, or what was not even attempted. This process starts with the patient themselves, who when feeling ill seeks help from a healthcare professional or service, and then follows with diagnosis and prognosis. Thus, preventive medicine starts from health with the aim of preserving it, while curative medicine starts from disease and the patient with the aim of cure (Lifshitz, 2014).

Arredondo (2014) emphasizes the need for a transition from a curative model to a more holistic model that emphasizes prevention, detection, and long-term monitoring. If these changes do not occur, chronic diseases will continue to be poorly detected, and consequently, national health coverage will not be effective.

The proliferation of technology, as mentioned by Mirza et al. (2008), aims to link all aspects of a person’s life such as dietary habits, physical behavior, surgeries performed, and predisposition to diseases. Thus, they become necessary for more thorough and preventive health surveillance of the population, as they help doctors to examine the patient holistically, preventing possible chronic diseases such as diabetes and hypertension.
