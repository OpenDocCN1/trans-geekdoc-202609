Similarly, the differences can be experienced in the barriers to innovation, where the human recourse factor as a barrier is most prominent in services compared to products (Sirilli & Evangelista, 1998). Additional researchers, including Atuahene-Gima (1996) and Janz et al. (2001), contrasted the studies about product industries and service businesses. They found that certain characteristics of services, such as perishability, inseparability, and heterogeneity, completely differentiated them from products. This creates a need for business models/strategies that address the unique features of services for service industries by giving equal importance to technological and non-technological components. Further, contextual differentiation should be addressed (a) based on the industry and (b) based on the innovative concepts developed by the firm. Nevertheless, how is it measured? Drawbacks in existing business models: How can they be transformed/improved as per changing customer requirements? Can it be generalized to all service industries or subject to the individual industry? There remains a gap to be addressed.

# **1.2. New Service Development:**

Besides existing issues, authors who focused on service innovation could not clearly distinguish between service innovation and the new service development process. Both concepts were mostly addressed similarly by Johne and Storey (1998) and Alam (2002), leading to another confusion in the literature. Bettencourt (2010) states that most management teams must distinguish between service innovation and new service development. He further stated that service innovation only focuses on developing a new concept in service, whereas new service development occurs when it involves all the activities until commercialization. Booz et al. (1982) proposed an eight-step model for developing new services, popularized by Bowers in 1987. Create a long-term business plan, describe a new service, and generate ideas for selecting and developing new ideas. (iv) concept creation and assessment to identify potential new service ideas; (vii) market testing; (viii) commercialization; (vii) business analysis to assess the viability of the new service and project the profit; (vi) service creation and testing. Further clarification is urged between innovation and innovativeness, where innovation discusses the diffusion of new ideas through different channels into systems (Das, 2022). In contrast, innovativeness is the time of innovation adoption, and since it deals with a single innovation, the result obtained cannot be generalisable Subramanian & Nilakanta (1996). Understanding and focusing on this helps researchers and practitioners to incorporate it during the design of disruptive models/strategies.
