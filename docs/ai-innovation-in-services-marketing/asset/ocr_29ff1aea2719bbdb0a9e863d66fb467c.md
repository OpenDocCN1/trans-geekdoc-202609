# 2.2. Gender and Job Performance

Job performance, which refers to the extent to which workers perform their jobs within the rules set by the institution (Al-Makhaita et al., 2014), depends on many factors such as age, marital status, education, experience, and gender (Absualma, 2021; Banjo & Olufemi, 2014; Khan et al., 2022; McDaniel et al., 1988; Quiñones et al., 1995).

There are conflicting results regarding the effects of gender on job performance. Actually, it is difficult to compare the performance of men and women in identical activities (Rydstedt et al., 1998). This may be explained by differences in gender characteristics or the biological differences between men and women (Banjo & Olufemi, 2014). Absualma (2021) indicated that gender statistically influences job performance in Jordanian commercial banks. In detail, men perform better than women. This could be due to the fact that men are perceived as more competent and successful, as they are more inclined to take risks. In addition, Khan et al. (2022) found that gender significantly moderates the relationship between transformational leadership and job performance. They claimed that male employees are more likely to benefit from transformational leadership compared to female employees. In fact, men and women may react differently to the many facets of transformational leadership.
