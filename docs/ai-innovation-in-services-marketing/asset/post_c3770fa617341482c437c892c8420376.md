Moreover, we have found that gender has no impact on job performance (β = 0.055; p = 0.595 > 0.05) (hypothesis 2 is not supported). There is no consensus on the impact of gender on job performance. Some studies (Mackey et al., 2019; Shirom et al., 2008) have found that there are no significant differences in job performance between men and women (Shirom et al., 2008), while others support the effects of gender on job performance in certain areas (Abusalma, 2021; Banjo & Olufemi, 2014; Khan et al., 2022). Our result may be explained by the fact that there are no differences between men and women in terms of education, experience, funding opportunities, or cognitive capacities (i.e., problem-solving ability, creativity, etc.). Therefore, men and women are equally good at the majority of occupations. Contrary to our expectations, we found that hypothesis 3 (i.e., moderation of gender has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance) is not supported. Due to the fact that artificial intelligence is not designed to discriminate based on gender, gender has no effect on the relationship between AI and job performance. AI is a technology that can help people of all genders perform better in the workplace (i.e., AI can make workers of both genders more effective and productive). Furthermore, we found that the experience of the employee has a positive and significant effect on job performance (hypothesis 4 is supported). This implies that job performance increases with an increase in employee experience (Abusalma, 2021; McDaniel et al., 1988; Quiñones et al., 1995; Ratwani et al., 2020; Schmidt, 1988). This is due to the fact that experienced employees are better equipped for their tasks as they have more knowledge, expertise, and skills. Finally, hypothesis 5 (mediation of experience has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance) is also not supported. Actually, artificial intelligence may be applied to enhance the job performance of workers of all experience levels (i.e., applications of artificial intelligence are not biased against employees with less experience).

## 5. CONCLUSION

Much attention has been given to understanding the potential impact of artificial intelligence on job performance (Abusalma, 2021; Larivière et al., 2017; Prentice et al., 2020; Wijayati et al., 2022; Wilson & Daugherty, 2018). Artificial intelligence is now integrated into nearly every industry (e.g., manufacturing, services, healthcare, etc.). Even though studies have been done on artificial intelligence and job performance, not much has been done to see the mediating effects of gender and experience. Therefore, this paper addresses the question of how artificial intelligence influences job performance. We use gender as the moderator variable and experience as the mediating variable. To answer our research question, we collected data via online questionnaires, and we used EFA, CFA, and path analysis. The findings of our study can be summarized as follows: First and foremost, as argued in previous studies, we found a significant and positive impact of experience on the job performance of Tunisian employees. Second, we showed the absence of an impact of artificial intelligence on job performance. Meanwhile, gender has no influence on job performance. Finally, we highlighted that the mediating effects of experience and the moderating effects of gender have no impact on the artificial intelligence-job performance correlation.

It is worth mentioning the major contributions of our research. First, our result depicts the fact that, while our chapter may not demonstrate a direct impact of AI on job performance, managers should be aware of the importance of artificial intelligence in their businesses. As artificial intelligence is rapidly changing the way companies operate, managers may consider the introduction and implementation of digital tools in their organizations to remain competitive. Specifically, AI is used in customer service, fraud detection, risk evaluation, etc. Second, managers must identify both internal factors (i.e., leadership, resources, and the culture of the company) and external factors (i.e., data accessibility and the regulation of the environment) that may affect the implementation of artificial intelligence. For instance, managers may need to focus more attention on small projects if the company lacks the resources to invest in artificial intelligence. Third, managers need to be aware of the negative effects of artificial intelligence on firm performance in order to mitigate them. For example, managers can help their employees learn new skills and train them in new technologies. This can reduce workers’ anxiety and uncertainty about their employment prospects. They can also effectively provide information about the new technology and participate with employees in the selection and adoption of the new technology (e.g., through questionnaires, organizing brainstorming meetings, etc.). Fourth, it is crucial to acknowledge that some gender stereotypes continue to exist in the workplace. However, we found that gender does not affect job performance. Therefore, managers should prioritize the selection and promotion of the top applicants, regardless of the candidate’s gender. Instead, they should make sure that every employee, regardless of gender, has access to equal opportunities and make judgments based only on each employee’s performance, skills, and qualifications. Indeed, they should promote a culture that encourages gender diversity, as it can result in a staff that is more creative, motivated, and productive. In addition, the finding that job performance is positively and significantly impacted by employee experience has numerous managerial implications:

- managers have to create a positive work environment, such as by developing programs for training;
- they may classify employees based on their experience history to identify high-potential workers. Therefore, these high-potential workers may be considered for future leadership roles;
- the employee experience may be greatly improved by giving workers the freedom to give feedback, propose ideas, decide for themselves and to accept responsibility for their job. As a result, managers should be more participative to promote engagement and satisfaction.

Nevertheless, our paper is also subject to some limitations. The first limitation is the fact that the outcomes of our research are limited to employees in Tunisia. Therefore, our work highlights an opportunity for future researchers to engage in empirical studies to investigate the impact of artificial intelligence on job performance in different countries to generalize our results. First, our paper used quantitative techniques as a method of data collection and analysis; therefore, future studies may employ other techniques (i.e., case studies, interviews, longitudinal studies). For instance, longitudinal studies may track the impact of AI over time. Second, there may be gender-specific differences depending on the type of work. Thus, it would be interesting to investigate how AI could be used to automate manual tasks and how this may affect gender differences in work performance. Third, AI's effect on work performance is complicated and dependent on several variables. Future research could, for example, investigate how different AI systems such as machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing affect different work tasks. Fourth, employees in our study came from a variety of industries (primary, secondary, and tertiary). Thus, researchers may look at how artificial intelligence affects work performance in certain sectors. In fact, the adoption of AI may have a greater influence on some industries than on others. Fifth, future studies may include other moderators/mediators’ variables, such as leadership style.
