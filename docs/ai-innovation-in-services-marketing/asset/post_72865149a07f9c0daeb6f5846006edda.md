These new technologies will increasingly be present in the healthcare sector, mainly to assist various stages of healthcare. Below are some of the main stages where AI and ML can act in the healthcare sector:

- Diagnosis: AI can be used to diagnose medical conditions, analyzing exam data and patient symptoms. For example, ML algorithms can be trained to detect early signs of diseases, such as the emergence of tumors, or to assist in distinguishing between various conditions with similar symptoms, such as infection cases (Lebcir et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2019).
- Medical Image Analysis: AI can be used to analyze medical images, such as X-rays, CT scans, and MRIs. Algorithms can be trained to detect certain features in these images that may indicate diseases, making the analysis faster and more accurate (Mondal et al., 2022; Tang, 2020).
- Patient Monitoring: AI can be used to monitor patients and predict possible complications. For example, algorithms can analyze a patient's vital signs and warn doctors about important changes that may indicate imminent complications (Lebcir et al., 2021; Sujith et al., 2022).
- Treatment Personalization: AI can be used to help personalize the treatment of each patient, taking into account factors such as age, medical history, genetics, and other information. ML algorithms can identify the most effective treatments for individual patients based on their personal data (Mondal et al., 2022).
- Drug Discovery: AI can be used to facilitate drug discovery, analyzing large amounts of research data to identify potential therapeutic targets and drug combinations (Boniolo et al., 2021).

As emphasized by Gennatas & Chen (2021) and Lebcir et al. (2021), AI with the assistance of ML has the potential to transform the healthcare sector, as these technologies promise to provide more accurate diagnoses, personalized treatments, and more efficient patient monitoring. However, similar to virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) solutions, the implementation of AI in healthcare also faces specific institutional challenges; thus, it is noteworthy that regulatory barriers in the medical sector may limit the advancement of new technologies, affecting areas such as treatment, patient data regulation, and educational processes for medical professionals (Kulkov et al., 2023). Therefore, it is crucial to recognize and address these barriers to optimize the transformative potential of AI and ML in the healthcare sector. It is important to note that trust among industry participants is a critical factor in the successful introduction of these technologies, so it is necessary to emphasize that technologies do not replace the experience and clinical judgment of doctors and other healthcare professionals but rather help them make better and more informed decisions and, in the future, will complement their intelligence (Topol, 2019; Verghese et al., 2018).

## PREVENTIVE HEALTH GAINS STRENGTH WITH TECHNOLOGY

The use of new technologies in healthcare is not limited to state-of-the-art equipment found in hospitals, diagnostic centers, or health clinics. The development of information technology (IT) has made citizens protagonists in the so-called preventive medicine, which focuses on disease prevention or early diagnosis to promote faster treatment (Razzak et al., 2020). Thus, the health model that should be applied and advocated by public managers is that of promoting physical and mental well-being, not the prevailing model in most countries: the care model (Fertonani et al., 2015). In other words, it is necessary to consider preventive measures in human health, that is, to reflect on a subject before any instability occurs in the body and not just after acquiring a disease, for this it is necessary to pay attention to people’s daily routines and habits, emphasizing the improvement of immunity and physical strengthening, thus reducing the probability and risks of diseases. Therefore, the use of new technological tools helps citizens to put this specialty into practice in the healthcare system (He et al., 2021).

A well-known healthcare model that persists in healthcare systems in various countries is the curative model. This model, also known as curative medicine, intervenes in what could not be avoided, or what was not even attempted. This process starts with the patient themselves, who when feeling ill seeks help from a healthcare professional or service, and then follows with diagnosis and prognosis. Thus, preventive medicine starts from health with the aim of preserving it, while curative medicine starts from disease and the patient with the aim of cure (Lifshitz, 2014).

Arredondo (2014) emphasizes the need for a transition from a curative model to a more holistic model that emphasizes prevention, detection, and long-term monitoring. If these changes do not occur, chronic diseases will continue to be poorly detected, and consequently, national health coverage will not be effective.

The proliferation of technology, as mentioned by Mirza et al. (2008), aims to link all aspects of a person’s life such as dietary habits, physical behavior, surgeries performed, and predisposition to diseases. Thus, they become necessary for more thorough and preventive health surveillance of the population, as they help doctors to examine the patient holistically, preventing possible chronic diseases such as diabetes and hypertension. Therefore, citizens are starting to provide data from their lives to facilitate the work of doctors in preventing or detecting diseases, resulting in more assertive decisions regarding the most appropriate treatment for each case (Mirza et al., 2008).

As resources evolve, individuals engage in this transformation, and interest in prevention increases, thus the importance of health is not based solely on the mere cure of the disease. Thus, a paradigm shift emerges, that is, a transition to a scenario that pays more attention to the well-being and care of the human being in a more harmonious and balanced way (Babatunde et al., 2021; P. Santos et al., 2017).

Moreover, preventive care is important because it reduces the number of consultations and hospitalizations, impacting hospital overcrowding, increasing quality and life expectancy, and possibly aiding in reducing healthcare spending. Additionally, the quantity and speed of data generated daily in healthcare further increase the need for technologies capable of capturing, crossing, and interpreting information in real-time. Therefore, new technologies available in the market bring a new scenario of prosperity for patients, doctors, and hospitals. Through web solutions and apps, where information such as medical histories, prescriptions, tests, and family histories can be stored, it is possible to search for professionals by specialty, schedule appointments, and send notifications via email and SMS (Short Message Service) (Babatunde et al., 2021; Oldenburg et al., 2015).

In this way, technology is allowing hospitals, clinics, and laboratories to reach patients more easily, quickly, and economically. Consequently, it will represent a significant improvement in the healthcare system, thus, the use of professionals' time, the quality of care, and the values spent on patient treatment will reach positive levels (Ahern et al., 2011).

Some new technologies employed in preventive medicine, which assist the entire improvement process, include:

- Mobile health monitoring devices and health apps: These devices, such as smartwatches and activity bracelets, monitor sleep, exercise, heart rate, and other health metrics. They can help people lead healthy lifestyles and detect health problems early. There are also many apps that can help people manage their health. Examples include calorie tracking apps, medication reminder apps, and blood pressure monitoring apps (Jiang et al., 2022).
- Telemedicine: This remote communication medium allows patients to contact doctors and other healthcare professionals through video calls and messages. This can be especially useful for people living in remote areas or who have difficulty getting to the doctor (Babatunde et al., 2021).
- Big Data and AI: These technologies can be used to analyze large amounts of health data and identify patterns that can lead to new medical discoveries
