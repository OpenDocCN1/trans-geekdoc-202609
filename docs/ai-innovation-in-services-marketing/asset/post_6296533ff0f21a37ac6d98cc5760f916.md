## 2.3. The Role of Gender in the Relationship Between AI and Job Performance

As discussed above, gender is an important individual characteristic that significantly influences job performance (Abusalma, 2021; Hoque et al., 2019; Khan & Vieito, 2013). On the one hand, many contributions (Abusalma, 2021; Allison et al., 2023; Hoque et al., 2019; Igbaria & Shayo, 1997) ascertained that men tend to outperform women. Due to their talents and diligence, only male employees were more likely to achieve excellent performance (Igbaria & Shayo, 1997). On the other hand, Kiefer et al. (2022) stated that male-led businesses perform better than female-led businesses. However, they also added that female-led companies are outperforming their male-led competitors in terms of performance results in certain instances. In the same vein, Khan & Vieito (2013) stated that companies tend to have the lowest levels of risks when it is led by female CEOs. Therefore, this reduced amount of risk may help the company perform better.

Other studies have looked at the way AI systems affect men and women. Despite the ever-evolving global technical landscape, women’s participation in the field of artificial intelligence remains disproportionately low (Samuel et al., 2020). Ahn et al. (2022) argued that respondents thought female AI chatbots were more amiable and pleasant, while they thought male AI agents were more capable and reliable. Therefore, when it came to utilitarian products (e.g., a laptop), participants were more inclined to accept recommendations from male AI agents, whereas female AI agents were more likely to suggest hedonic products (e.g., jewelry). This may be due to the fact that men are much more likely to be involved with software compared to women, who tend to work in jobs that require greater interpersonal skills (Webb, 2019). In light of the above discussion, the following hypothesis is presented:

H3: Moderation of gender has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance.

## 2.4. Experience and Job Performance

Work experience, which is generally defined as events that occur in an individual's life, is correlated with job performance (Quiñones et al., 1995). The meta-analysis conducted by Quiñones et al. (1995) confirmed earlier findings on the positive correlation between work experience and job performance (Hunter and Hunter, 1984) and the fact that the most important type of work experience for job performance is task-level work experience (Ng and Feldman, 2010). However, they were among the first authors to analyze how the relationship between experience and performance is influenced by the moderating effects of two dimensions of work experience (i.e., measurement model and degree of sphericity). Abusalma (2021) stated that the number of years of work experience has a statistically significant effect on job performance in Jordanian commercial banks. More precisely, ten to twenty years of experience are required to increase performance efficiency. Ratnawati et al. (2020) conducted quantitative research on 40 employees of PT Tirta Kencana Tatawarna Bengkulu. They also found that work experience has a positive and significant effect on employee performance (i.e., an employee's performance decreases with decreasing work experience). In the same vein, McDaniel et al. (1988) confirmed the previous findings of Quiñones et al. (1995) and Abusalma (2021). However, they added that the length of experience and job complexity moderated the correlation between job experience and job performance. They explained that the highest correlations were found in populations with low average work experience and in occupations that impose low cognitive demands on workers. Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

H4: Experience has a positive and significant effect on job performance.

## 2.5. The Role of Experience in the Relationship Between AI and Job Performance

It is well documented that employees are likely to exhibit improved job performance when they gain more experience in their roles (Abusalma, 2021; Dewanti & Artaya, 2019; Hunter and Hunter, 1984; Quiñones et al., 1995). For instance, Dewanti & Artaya (2019) stated that the performance of employees is influenced by job experience by 51.1%. In addition, Shahzad et al. (2023) showed that artificial intelligence has a significant impact on human resources tasks. More precisely, artificial intelligence promotes recruiting and talent acquisition, increases employee experience and engagement, facilitates employee growth, boosts productivity, makes better judgments, etc. (Butler & Callahan, 2014; Nam et al., 2021). In the same vein, Zel and Kongar (2020) affirmed that if AI tools such as chatbots, virtual HR chatbots, and collaboration platforms are effectively deployed, they may be used to improve the employee experience. Consequently, it is anticipated that an employee’s experience will serve as an intermediary between job performance and artificial intelligence awareness. Hypothesis 5 is proposed:

H5: Mediation of experience has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance.

## 3. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

A total of 350 online questionnaires were distributed, from January 2023 to March 2023, to investigate the impact of artificial intelligence on Tunisian employees’ job performance. The survey used in this paper consisted of three parts: The first part covers the demographic information of the respondents, the second part contains items measuring artificial intelligence awareness, and the third part includes items that measure job performance. The last two parts of the survey items were measured using a 5-point Likert scale, with response levels ranging from “strongly disagree” (1) to “strongly agree” (5). Artificial intelligence awareness items were derived from the contribution of Brougham and Haar (2018), with four factors as dimensions. Job performance was evaluated using Çalışkan and Köroğlu’s items (2022). Table 1 presents the constructs and scale resources.

Our sample showed that 51.4% of the respondents were male, while 48.6% were female (see Table 2). The majority of respondents were married (56.6%), between the ages of 31 and 40 years (47.4%), had a licence degree (49.4%), had experience between 5 and 10 years (34.3%), and lived in urban areas (86.3%).
