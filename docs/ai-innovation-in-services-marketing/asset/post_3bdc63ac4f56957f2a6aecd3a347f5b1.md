## 5. BARRIERS AND FACILITATORS OF SERVICE INNOVATION

Understanding diverse considerations is necessary while developing a business model or service plan. The literature on innovation identified and categorised elements that serve as obstacles or enablers. Economic factors like risks to the economy, costs associated with innovation, and a lack of funding were categorised by Martikainen (2008). Internal factors like market information, organizational structure, and a need for more technical information were also included, as were other factors like ignorance of customers’ attention to suppliers and rivals. Similar lists of human traits and monetary considerations for marketing management were made by Larsen and Lewis in 2007. However, Segarra-Blasco and Arauzo-Carod (2008) focused on financial and market expertise. Other factors mentioned by several authors include the characteristics of the global market, supplier and customer feedback, rival domestic market characteristics, strong project leaders’ access to capital and human resources capabilities, taxation of new goods processes, and government tax (Foley & Green 1995; Tidd et al. 1997; Pihkala et al. 2002).

The significance of cutting-edge technologies was emphasized by authors (Moore & Benbasat, 1991; Verhagen & Dolen, 2009; Verma & Tandon, 2022), who concentrated on the review of technology considerations. According to certain writings (Galia & Legros 2004; Martikainen 2008; Segarra-Blasco & Arauzo-Carod 2008), a significant impediment to creativity is more technology knowledge.

According to Hadjimanolis (1999), a barrier to creativity is difficulty locating technical information. A certain body of literature examined factors like governmental regulation. According to Allen et al. (1978) study, the three elements that make up the regulating factor are initiating, sustaining, and restructuring processes. Similarly, the government's decision to embrace innovation is positively influenced by providing information regarding innovations, financial support during development, and procedural development facilitation (Moon & Bretschneider 1997). ## 6. GROWTH OF SERVICE INNOVATION

### 6.1. Geographic and Industry

The previous few decades have considerably increased service innovation. The main driver of innovation has been the industrial revolution. During that time, innovation spread around the globe. Since the first industrial revolution, innovation has been the primary force behind economic expansion. It mostly affected the various parts of Europe. The geographical patterns of development and income distribution have also been influenced by it. Every succeeding revolution had a unique topography. North America joined the elite group of wealthy nations during the Second Industrial Revolution, expanding the industrialized areas of Europe. ICT, life sciences and biotechnologies, financial engineering, and advancements in transportation and logistics were all part of the third industrial revolution, which began around 1980. Innovation and economic growth extended throughout northeast Asia from Japan to the Republic of Korea and then to China. Beijing, Shenzhen, Tokyo, Seoul, and Beijing have all developed into megacities that influence the course of technological advancement.

The world's innovation could have been more evenly distributed and concentrated in some areas. Urban areas with emerging middle-income and high-income economies have seen the most innovation. These cities also turned out to be the centers for the growth of innovation ecosystems. The important areas were the Greater Boston Metropolitan region in Massachusetts and the US Southern portion of the San Francisco Bay Area in Northern California. The main topic of discussion is regional concentration within a process of global dispersion. The reality is that innovation activity in Western Europe and the United States is increasing mostly in urban regions. Still, it is also rapidly spreading and expanding to hubs outside the traditional cities. Innovation has always been a global phenomenon, but as demands mount, it is becoming to converge and concentrate in certain, typically urban areas. According to figures from 2022, Switzerland has been awarded the most imaginative economy in the world for the 12th year in a row, followed by the United States, Sweden, the United Kingdom, and the Netherlands. China is approaching the top 10, while Turkey and India debuted in the top 40. (Global Innovation Index 2022).

### 6.2. Technological and Non-Technological Innovation

In particular, innovation helps businesses easily navigate crises amid the current global crisis. Technological innovations are often characterized by creating or utilizing new technologies, such as new technical knowledge and technological creations. Non-technological innovations can affect corporate operations without necessarily involving a change in technology or adopting new technology. Instead, they may focus only on implementing new organizational principles, business practices, or other immaterial methods. The growth of the service sector is influenced by several variables, including technological innovation, the need for change, and increased client expectations. Urbanization, changing lifestyles, and rising purchasing power have increased the need for services that can offer more comfort and quality. (Nair 2018). Online food delivery apps, online travel and tourist booking, and online education and learning are just a few examples of advances in the service industries.
