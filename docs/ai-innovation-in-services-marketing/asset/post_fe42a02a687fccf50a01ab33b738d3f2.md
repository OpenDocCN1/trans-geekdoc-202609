The emergence of new Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) forces this sector to open up to the outside and, mainly, to consumers/patients, thus it must increasingly interact with all involved parties, i.e., professionals and patients. Healthcare marketing is basically a service marketing, in which the consumer/patient is a value co-creator, as they actively participate in essential processes, encouraging service interaction for value co-creation. This new interaction approach allows enhancing the relationship between service quality and satisfaction, which are directly linked to the consumer’s experience, in this case, the patient (Anderson et al., 2019; Criré & Chebat, 2013; D. Lee, 2019).

In addition to the importance of using marketing in healthcare, we can also add some branches within marketing that can serve as strategies to reach consumers. Digital marketing and relationship marketing are two important areas within the healthcare context because through them, it is possible to communicate about diseases, treatments, and prevention, as well as help to retain patients and strengthen the image of healthcare professionals (Berkowitz, 2021; Mondal et al., 2022).

When used in healthcare, relationship marketing aims to establish a lasting connection between healthcare professionals and patients through personalized actions, such as sending emails and/or text messages to answer questions and offer support in difficult times, conducting satisfaction surveys to identify areas for improvement, among others. These strategies are important for building a trust relationship between patient and healthcare professional. For this relationship to be genuinely built and lasting, it is necessary to always provide quality service, transparency in communication, and a commitment to consumer satisfaction (Berkowitz, 2021; Post Digital, 2023).

When we turn our attention to digital marketing in healthcare, strategies are carefully crafted to fit digital platforms, such as social media (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp), blogs, websites, and email marketing. These are essential tools to promote services, products, and health-related information (Mailchimp, 2023a).

In the healthcare realm, some recommended practices involve creating relevant and informative content aimed at educating the population about diseases and treatments. For example, clinics and healthcare professionals can use Facebook or Instagram pages to share tips on healthy habits, information about vaccines, results of scientific research, thus contributing to improving the image of the sector. In addition to educating, this type of content is useful for sparking consumers’ interest in the services offered and highlighting the skills of healthcare professionals to potential patients (Mondal et al., 2022; Radu et al., 2017).

It is relevant to emphasize that, compared to other industries, the healthcare sector has been lagging in engaging with customers through social media due to concerns about privacy, time management, and challenges of healthcare service providers. Therefore, it is essential to create a new model of public health campaigns with greater accuracy. While privacy concerns, time management, and challenges of healthcare service providers are cited as contributing factors to this gap, the proposal of a new model emphasizes the importance of a more personalized and targeted approach. The suggestion to use targeted advertising tools on social media directly aligns with the need to adapt strategies to find patients where they spend most of their time online, reinforcing the idea that evolution in healthcare may be intrinsically linked to the strategic ability to connect more effectively with patients in a digital environment (Aase & Timimi, 2013; An et al., 2021; Timimi, 2013).

## THE TRANSFORMATIONS OF TECHNOLOGY IN HEALTHCARE

In the definition by Bozic et al. (2004), ‘advances in healthcare’ can be understood as “all medications, instruments, and medical and surgical procedures used in medical services, as well as the organization and support systems in which these services are provided” (p. 1305). The International Business Machines (n.d.) expanded the definition presented by Bozic et al. (2004) and added that advances in healthcare encompass all technologies developed to assist healthcare institutions, including AI, information technology systems, among others.

The traditional approach to healthcare delivery, focusing on hospitals, presented some disadvantages for healthcare professionals, such as limited time to examine the patient (which made it impossible to collect information about their daily activities, essential for diagnosis and treatment choices), an aging population (which required more medical care, increasing the necessary resources), a shortage of healthcare professionals, and rising costs associated with service delivery, among others (Abdullayeva, 2022).

The evolution of healthcare services came to address these disadvantages, resulting in the development and implementation of medical technologies. Similar to what happened in the industry, where technological evolution and innovation were described through industrial revolutions (known as Industry 1.0 to Industry 4.0), the evolution of healthcare services can be described in four phases, called Health Care 1.0 to Health Care 4.0 (Kaur et al., 2021; Li & Carayon, 2021).

### Health Care 1.0

‘Health Care 1.0’ involved the basic encounter between doctor and patient, including consultations, tests, diagnoses, medical prescriptions, and disease management plans. In this phase, healthcare delivery was carried out by just one physician, a model still in use in medical practice (Kaur et al., 2021; Li & Carayon, 2021; Sony et al., 2022).

### Health Care 2.0

‘Health Care 2.0’ emerged with the development and introduction of new medical equipment and devices to support healthcare institutions in making more informed decisions, whether in diagnosis or treatment of diseases. During this phase, imaging technologies such as ultrasound and magnetic resonance imaging, patient health monitoring devices such as pulse oximeters and arterial lines, and surgical and life support equipment such as microscopes and ventilators were introduced (Li & Carayon, 2021; Sony et al., 2022).

### Health Care 3.0

‘Health Care 3.0’ was marked by the beginning of digitization of procedures, along with the introduction of electronic medical records (EMRs) and electronic health records (EHRs) (Li & Carayon, 2021; Sony et al., 2022). The development of these information systems allowed for the management of patient care across different units and departments of healthcare institutions, significantly impacting clinical and operational processes (Li & Carayon, 2021; Sony et al., 2022). During this phase, there was the digitization of healthcare value chains, including the adoption of electronic records after consultations. The use of electronic means improved the execution, management, and transmission of reports (Li & Carayon, 2021; Sony et al., 2022). Information systems brought about significant changes in healthcare services, including telemedicine, remote healthcare, and others, which complemented direct contact between doctors and patients, increasing efficiency (Sony et al., 2022). Thus, Health Care 3.0 transformed healthcare systems into a multidimensional form of patient management (Li & Carayon, 2021; Sony et al., 2022).

### Health Care 4.0

‘Health Care 4.0’ corresponds to the application of technologies developed during Industry 4.0 in healthcare services. In this sense, healthcare delivery has become a cyber-physical system equipped with radiofrequency identification (RFID) devices, wearables, and other types of medical devices and medical robots, which are integrated into the cloud through computing. Additionally, data processing involves big data, AI, and other decision support techniques, enabling the provision of interconnected and intelligent healthcare (Ahmad et al., 2022; Li & Carayon, 2021).
