There is a potential of driving innovation into the service industry to create opportunities and cost effective facilities for consumers through the model of gig economy. Gig economy involves individuals who provide services through digital platforms to other businesses (Cho & Cho, 2020), the biggest example being Uber that provides a platform for consumers and service providers. Such innovations are required to revolutionize the service sector for growth and creating competitive advantage.

This chapter embarks on an exploration of non-technological aspects of service innovation. By delving into the existing literature, there exists a need for stronger frameworks focusing on organizational, strategic and marketing service innovation models to enhance customer experience.

First section provides an overview of the different branches of service innovation starting with service vs. the product innovation. In this part distinctions between serve and product innovation are addressed. The next part of the introduction section comprises new service development that is used for generating and developing new ideas. Further it involves service innovation and performance. The literature reveals limited studies considering service innovation to determine customer performance, which gives a pathway for the next part of service innovation for quality. The last introductory part is of service innovation as disruptive business strategy. This section has the significance of investigation, highlighting the need for this study.

Next section outlines the research methodology to identify the articles relevant for the study. For refining the articles, SPAR-4-SLR was used as the primary method for further reviewing the existing literature to identify potential gaps in the literature. A broad classification of service innovation on the bases of degree of novelty and forms of innovation was presented in the third section with the next section being the different approaches to service innovation.

Section 5 identifies the barriers and enablers of service innovation that addresses the importance of technological and non-technological components to be competitive in the market. Growth of service innovation in terms of (i) geography and industry; and (ii) technological and non-technological innovation is discussed in section 6.

The next section interprets the importance and rise of service innovation which develops a pathway for emerging new business models based on collaboration, open innovation and gig economy. By reflecting the findings from existing literature, this section contributes to advancing knowledge in the field and suggests business models for future research. The conclusion section summarizes the findings presented in the chapter by highlighting the key insights and implications of the study. It also provides avenues for future research providing a closure to the study.
