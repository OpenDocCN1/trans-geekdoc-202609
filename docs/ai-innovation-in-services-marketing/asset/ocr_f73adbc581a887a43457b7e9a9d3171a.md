# 2.5. The Role of Experience in the Relationship Between AI and Job Performance

It is well documented that employees are likely to exhibit improved job performance when they gain more experience in their roles (Abusalma, 2021; Dewanti & Artaya, 2019; Hunter and Hunter, 1984; Quiñones et al., 1995). For instance, Dewanti & Artaya (2019) stated that the performance of employees is influenced by job experience by 51.1%. In addition, Shahzad et al. (2023) showed that artificial intelligence has a significant impact on human resources tasks. More precisely, artificial intelligence promotes recruiting and talent acquisition, increases employee experience and engagement, facilitates employee growth, boosts productivity, makes better judgments, etc. (Butler & Callahan, 2014; Nam et al., 2021). In the same vein, Zel and Kongar (2020) affirmed that if AI tools such as chatbots, virtual HR chatbots, and collaboration platforms are effectively deployed, they may be used to improve the employee experience. Consequently, it is anticipated that an employee’s experience will serve as an intermediary between job performance and artificial intelligence awareness. Hypothesis 5 is proposed:

H5: Mediation of experience has a significant effect on the relationship between artificial intelligence and job performance.

# 3. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

A total of 350 online questionnaires were distributed, from January 2023 to March 2023, to investigate the impact of artificial intelligence on Tunisian employees’ job performance. The survey used in this paper consisted of three parts: The first part covers the demographic information of the respondents, the second part contains items measuring artificial intelligence awareness, and the third part includes items that measure job performance. The last two parts of the survey items were measured using a 5-point Likert scale, with response levels ranging from “strongly disagree” (1) to “strongly agree” (5). Artificial intelligence awareness items were derived from the contribution of Brougham and Haar (2018), with four factors as dimensions.
