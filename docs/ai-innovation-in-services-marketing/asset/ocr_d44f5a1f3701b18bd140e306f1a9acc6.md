# # VIRTUAL REALITY (VR) IN TOURISM SERVICES

Virtual Reality (VR) has brought about a transformative shift in the tourism industry, offering immersive experiences that allow potential travellers to explore destinations virtually. Marriott Hotels, a global hospitality company, has embraced VR through its “VRoom Service” initiative, redefining the way guests engage with travel information. Marriott’s VRoom Service enables hotel guests to experience virtual travel adventures from the comfort of their rooms. Using a VR headset provided by the hotel, guests can embark on virtual tours of popular destinations, such as Hawaii or London. This not only adds an element of entertainment to the hotel stay but also serves as a powerful tool for influencing travel decisions. The VR experience allows guests to virtually walk through iconic landmarks, experience local cultures, and visualize potential vacation spots. This immersive approach helps guests make more informed decisions about their next travel destination. It goes beyond traditional brochures or online travel guides, providing a firsthand, 360-degree view that sparks excitement and inspiration.

Beyond hotels, the tourism industry has witnessed the integration of VR in various aspects of the customer journey. Airlines, such as Qantas, have utilized VR to offer virtual tours of their cabins and destinations, allowing potential passengers to preview the in-flight experience and explore the sights they might encounter upon arrival. Destination marketing organizations have also embraced VR to showcase the unique attractions of their regions. For instance, Tourism Australia launched “Australia in 360,” a VR experience that takes users on a virtual journey through the country’s diverse landscapes and activities. This immersive content serves as a compelling promotional tool, enticing travelers to explore Australia in person.

In addition to pre-travel experiences, VR has found applications in post-travel memories. Hotels and travel agencies offer VR experiences that allow individuals to relive their vacations. For example, Thomas Cook, a travel agency, introduced VR postcards that enable customers to revisit their holiday destinations by inserting their smartphones into a VR headset. This innovation enhances the emotional connection customers have with their travel experiences, fostering brand loyalty. The cruise industry has also embraced VR technology to provide potential passengers with virtual ship tours. Carnival Cruise Line, for instance, offers a “Cruise VR” app that allows users to virtually explore the interiors and amenities of their cruise ships. This immersive preview helps individuals choose the right cruise experience based on their preferences and expectations (Shalini Talwar,2022).
