As resources evolve, individuals engage in this transformation, and interest in prevention increases, thus the importance of health is not based solely on the mere cure of the disease. Thus, a paradigm shift emerges, that is, a transition to a scenario that pays more attention to the well-being and care of the human being in a more harmonious and balanced way (Babatunde et al., 2021; P. Santos et al., 2017).

Moreover, preventive care is important because it reduces the number of consultations and hospitalizations, impacting hospital overcrowding, increasing quality and life expectancy, and possibly aiding in reducing healthcare spending. Additionally, the quantity and speed of data generated daily in healthcare further increase the need for technologies capable of capturing, crossing, and interpreting information in real-time. Therefore, new technologies available in the market bring a new scenario of prosperity for patients, doctors, and hospitals. Through web solutions and apps, where information such as medical histories, prescriptions, tests, and family histories can be stored, it is possible to search for professionals by specialty, schedule appointments, and send notifications via email and SMS (Short Message Service) (Babatunde et al., 2021; Oldenburg et al., 2015).

In this way, technology is allowing hospitals, clinics, and laboratories to reach patients more easily, quickly, and economically. Consequently, it will represent a significant improvement in the healthcare system, thus, the use of professionals' time, the quality of care, and the values spent on patient treatment will reach positive levels (Ahern et al., 2011).

Some new technologies employed in preventive medicine, which assist the entire improvement process, include:

+   Mobile health monitoring devices and health apps: These devices, such as smartwatches and activity bracelets, monitor sleep, exercise, heart rate, and other health metrics. They can help people lead healthy lifestyles and detect health problems early. There are also many apps that can help people manage their health. Examples include calorie tracking apps, medication reminder apps, and blood pressure monitoring apps (Jiang et al., 2022).

+   Telemedicine: This remote communication medium allows patients to contact doctors and other healthcare professionals through video calls and messages. This can be especially useful for people living in remote areas or who have difficulty getting to the doctor (Babatunde et al., 2021).

+   Big Data and AI: These technologies can be used to analyze large amounts of health data and identify patterns that can lead to new medical discoveries
