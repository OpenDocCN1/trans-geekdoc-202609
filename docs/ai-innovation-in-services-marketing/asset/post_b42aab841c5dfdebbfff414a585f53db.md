
- **Visual design elements**, such as: name, logo, graphics, typographic fonts, corporate colors, packaging, shapes, slogan;
- **Distinctive characteristics of products**, such as: aroma, flavor, quality;
- **Intangible aspects of customer interaction**, such as: reputation, service quality.

Thus, these various elements combined around brands facilitate brands to be a crucial element in creating value for both companies and consumers. They serve as a cognitive shortcut, allowing consumers to make informed purchasing choices without having to spend time and energy evaluating each product individually. Especially in consumer markets, brands play a crucial role as businesses strive to foster consumer trust, loyalty and preferences.

According to Kapferer, brands have different functions in terms of the value perceived by consumers (Kapferer, 2008), among which we highlight: identification, practicality, assurance, optimization, continuity and ethic.

In the context of these functions, there are several benefits for consumers, such as: the brand can be seen clearly and be quickly identified and associated with the product; allows time and energy savings, allowing the brand to be purchased again, due to brand loyalty; the consumer is guaranteed to find the same standard of quality, no matter where or when the product or service is purchased; guarantee of purchasing the best product in its category, the best performance for a given purpose; satisfaction caused by existing intimacy and familiarity with the brand; satisfaction linked to the brand's responsible social behavior towards its external environment.

The brand's value stems from its capability to create an exceptional, positive, and noteworthy significance in the consumer's perception. The value perceived by the customer is the main factor that allows the brand to ensure that customers will be comfortable to pay more for the brand's products and services.

The different approaches to brand equity show that different authors establish different ways for a brand to create value (Kapferer, 2008; Keller & Kotler, 2012; Oliveira et al., 2023), that we highlight: Financial perspective – the financial perspective regards brand equity as the extra financial worth that a brand contributes to a product or service. For instance, it explains why customers are willing to pay a higher price for a product from a reputable brand as opposed to a generic one; Consumer perspective - the consumer perspective centers on the consumer's perception and interaction with the brand. This encompasses brand recognition, consumer's emotional connection with the brand, brand loyalty, and perceived excellence; Furthermore, a combined perspective respects both financial value and consumer perception.

A successful brand is generally based on several key principles, which according to Kotler and Keller highlight five Branding principles (Keller & Kotler, 2012):

- **Consistency** is one of the fundamental principles of Branding for any organization. To become consistent, organizations must adopt holistic approaches that include products or brands, as well as all points of interaction between the organization and its stakeholders

- **Clarity** is crucial to making a brand more understandable and tangible. It is based on an organization’s vision, mission, values and core competencies. These elements must be simple to communicate and also to understand, allowing that stakeholders can set a position of the brand’s relevance in their mindset.

- **Continuity** is a fundamental principle for developing Brand Equity and long-term trust. People must trust that the brand delivers on everything it promises, based on past experiences. Therefore, continuity is essential to maintain customer trust and loyalty.

- **Visibility** is important for increasing brand-exposure and also to develop the brand awareness among consumers.

- **Authenticity** is a crucial aspect of a brand. The goal of brand authenticity is to create a unique feeling for the customer of owning a unique product or service. This helps to differentiate the brand in the market and establish a deeper connection with customers.

A brand cannot just be merely a symbol for a company or a product, it must represent the organization’s most precious resource, fostering robust principles, values and emotions with customers to establish a deep connection between them and the brand.

Thus, we can say that a strong brand promotes the creation of significant value for a company through its ability to shape customer perceptions and preferences, differentiating products or services from competitors, and building long-term customer relationships.

## THE CONTEXT OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN ORGANIZATIONS

Organizations have always sought to increase their competitiveness. If in the past this aim was essentially achieved through the pursuit of improvements in the machinery supporting its businesses, it has progressively come to be achieved due to the ability to use data in a useful way, generate information and knowledge and, in recent years, in the ability of understanding what the data “does not reveal to the naked eye” and which allows organizations to preview the future – to anticipate problems and opportunities. In this field, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and its subsystems, such as Machine Learning and Deep Learning are the tip of the spear. According to Cunha (2023), insight-based knowledge, powered by AI, appears to be an ongoing reengineering within organizations.

AI plays an irreplaceable role in the era of the digital economy, providing a vital opportunity for organizations that wish to increase their performance and competitiveness (Li et al., 2024). According to Lee et al. (2023), who carried out a systematic review of the literature, the majority of AI implementations are motivated by process improvement and production efficiency. Also, Mikalef et al. (2023) emphasizes that AI skills must be seen as management skills that go beyond the technical aspect, and used in a creative way to add value to the organization, proposing that these skills be considered central, not just secondary.

AI is transforming decision-making processes in organizations by taking over decision-making responsibilities previously assigned only to humans. AI self-learning capacity improves the quality of decisions, marking a fundamental change (Vincent, 2021). One of the examples is, according to Chatterjee et al. (2021), the integration of AI into Customer Relationship Management (CRM) systems that is transforming the analysis of large volumes of customer data in organizations where, to efficiently deal with the resulting opportunities and challenges, organizations are developing skills and processes that enhance their agility, aligning with artificial intelligence-powered customer service.

Research conducted by Sullivan & Fosso Wamba (2024) on AI and adaptation to market changes, revealed significant interactions between environmental-hostility and also dynamism in the relationships between AI-powered capabilities and the Adaptive Response to Market Changes (ARMC), as well as ARMC being observed to positively influence companies' innovation and performance.

Regarding the role of AI in branding, according to Musaiqer & Hamdan (2023), AI has become a powerful tool for organizations to improve their strategy and brand-building efforts. Also, according to Rahman et al. (2023), luxury brands in the fashion sector have been adopting AI-powered digital assistance to enhance the online shopping experience and influence customer engagement. In a study conducted by Ho & Chow (2023), on the role of AI in Hong Kong brand preference for retail banks, AI-driven marketing efforts showed a significant positive effect on brand preference, influencing customer repurchase intention. This suggests that AI marketing efforts should not only be seen as improving the customer experience, but also as an essential tool in building brand image, supporting (Godey et al., 2016).
