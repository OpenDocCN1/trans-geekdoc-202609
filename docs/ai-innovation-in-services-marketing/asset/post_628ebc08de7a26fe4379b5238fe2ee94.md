## 1.5. Service Innovation as Disruptive Business Strategy

The contemporary catastrophic events, including the COVID-19 pandemic, have significantly affected service providers. These incidents have significantly caused economic disruption, including changes in consumer behavior, work practices and government policies. According to the International Monetary Fund, the global financial crisis of 2009 had a real GDP year-on-year percentage change of -0.1% and the great lockdown that began in 2020 had a change of -3%. The total output loss during 2020-2021 from the pandemic crisis was approximately £9 trillion. The IMF has also stated that both advanced economies, emerging and developing economies, are in a state of recession. Despite these challenges, service providers are trying to resume normal operations because of the importance of economic stability and growth. Therefore, service providers are working to overcome the challenges, address significant changes and continuously provide value to customers and stakeholders while supporting communities and promoting social and environmental responsibility. Thus, endeavoring to adjust to new consumer behaviors and market conditions. The pandemic has accelerated the shift towards digital marketing channels, with more businesses focusing on online advertising, social media marketing and e-commerce. This has increased investment in digital marketing technologies and strategies like search engine optimization, SEO pay-per-click PPC advertising and email marketing.

Furthermore, with a rise in online shopping, a preference for contactless payment methods and a higher demand for health and safety products, the focus has shifted to more new consumer needs and preferences. Empowering the existing service framework with Technology tools like Artificial intelligence, Machine Learning techniques, Automation, Augmented and Virtual Reality, Robotics, Quantum computing, etc., can help service providers analyze and interpret large amounts of customer data, providing insights for improving service personalization. Non-Technological innovation is another important factor in structuring the entire service innovation, such as adjusting or enhancing organizational strategy choices and marketing initiatives. Technology and non-technological aspects of service innovation are combined holistically, with equal emphasis required and articulated using S-D Logic.

All marketing techniques and tactics that were based on products and other tangible goods—also referred to as “goods-dominant logic”—were attacked by Vargo and Morgan in 2005. They published S-D logic, which argued that the exchange of services, as opposed to only things, is what society, markets, and organizations are most concerned with. When it happens, one service is swapped out for another. Additionally, they emphasized how all markets are built on the exchange of services, all communities have service-based economies, and all firms are service enterprises. To focus on service-dominant logic, marketers must switch from G-D reasoning to S-D logic and redefine their fundamental methodologies, concepts, ideas, and strategies. In particular, according to innovation experts, service innovation research is equivalent to product innovation research (Vargo & Morgan, 2005).

Service innovation has been growing rapidly in the modern economy and is changing the way businesses deliver their services and interact with their customers (Freni, 2022). Over the past few years with this development, the consumer preferences, global reach and interconnectedness has also surged leading to a transformation in the traditional businesses (Gallouj et al., 2015). This rapid growth is reshaping businesses leading to an emergence of entirely new markets and innovative business models (Panigrahi et al., 2017). Geographical reach and industry growth are two important drivers of taking the innovation industry forward (Amoroso et al., 2023). An openness to expanding businesses through geographical segments opens up new markets and allows businesses to reach new customer bases, helping foster their unique needs. This expansion encourages businesses to stay updated by innovating continuously and gaining a competitive edge. Simultaneously, overall industry growth also creates opportunities for innovation and helps in expanding knowledge. With the expansion of industry and technology, come new ideas for development and scope for addressing challenges. However, alongside the potential benefits of growth in service innovation, there are numerous challenges that businesses must navigate. New solutions and business models are coming into emergence in order to address these issues. Feng & Sivakumar (2016) give empirical evidence that service innovation has an impact on innovation performance given the propensity of collaboration and shared economy for service firms. It has the potential of creating innovative and collaborative business models that turn out to be cost effective and of expected consumer quality. The gap between consumer expectations and actual delivery is also a major issue in an era where consumers are attracted towards personalized products. Organizations can foster consumer needs by integrating consumer ideas with internal knowledge of the firm (Tang & Lush, 2018). The model of open innovation serves as a sustainable trend providing external information to channelize into internal and organizational ideas.

There is a potential of driving innovation into the service industry to create opportunities and cost effective facilities for consumers through the model of gig economy. Gig economy involves individuals who provide services through digital platforms to other businesses (Cho & Cho, 2020), the biggest example being Uber that provides a platform for consumers and service providers. Such innovations are required to revolutionize the service sector for growth and creating competitive advantage.
