# # INTRODUCTION

In a society that reveals itself to be a massive data producing system, the ability to process data and generate information and knowledge is currently inaccessible to human processing capacity. In this scenario, brand management is now a very demanding and extremely complex time-to-market exercise. However, if brands see in this reality an over-information, they will be making a big mistake – there is no such thing as an over-information. Not when we have Artificial Intelligence (AI).

AI and its derivative technologies (e.g., Machine Learning and Deep Learning), combined with the increase in computational capacity to process data, have demonstrated unique capabilities to analyze massive amounts of data and, from these, detect patterns, which allow characterizing realities and trends, anticipate scenarios and even detect week insights. In this context AI is driving the change in the way brands and customers interact with one another.

Business is strongly influenced by AI, being a good example the sales cycle, consumers habits and costumes support activities (Trawnig et al., 2022). Additionally, and according (Davenport et al., 2020), AI and its algorithms is capable of processing massive amounts of data, if almost no human interaction, and from multiple data sources. This process is also capable of extracting important insights that will support the prediction and anticipation of consumers-behaviors, int real time.

There is however a challenge to combine this capability of AI to analyze data and detect patterns and the relational nature that exists between brands and consumers (Aaker et al., 2004). Indeed, being able to understand and anticipate trends might be extremely helpful for brands to remain meaningful in an era where the consumer has several alternatives as never before.

This research discusses the role that Generative AI has and will increasingly play in empowering and leveraging brands, allowing them not only to better manage how they relate to their customers, but also to strategically create new brands that can meet the demand of their customers or niche markets that are being formed still in an embryonic stage – generating competitive advantages against competing brands. We also explore the concept of using AI as an autopilot for brands – that is, the strategy and its actions are guided, shaped and redefined in real time by AI. In a future that will in all probability be much more data producing (e.g., what exists today will be the tip of the iceberg of tomorrow) AI will be an indispensable tool for brands, with the AI algorithms used being the differentiating factors of the competitive brands-capacity.
