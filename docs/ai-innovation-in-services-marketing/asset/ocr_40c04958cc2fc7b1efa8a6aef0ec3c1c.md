# Research Samples and Sampling Procedure

The interviews included a sample of Digital Marketing Campaigns potential customers. Two demographic groups were examined: a younger group from 20 to 30 years old (A20-30), and an older one from 40 to 50 years old (A40-50). Both groups’ of respondents were selected only if they engaged with digital marketing campaigns specifically in Lithuania in the previous two months. The semi-structured interviews provided a deeper comprehension of the research topic while comparing two distinct age groups, helping to identify any similarities or differences between their engagement level. A flexible approach to these interviews enables the different opinions, preferences, and attitudes of each participant, offering new thoughts and perspectives on the factors that could increase customer engagement levels.

Non-probabilistic quota sampling was used to select ten candidates based on defined criteria. Respondents were chosen specifically depending on the factors needed for the interview, such as age, location, and the level of interaction with the digital ads. The judgmental sampling was used by the researcher as it was assumed that certain features and criteria for the sample can reflect the population (Efil & Negida, 2017). In this case, respondents were chosen according to their age category and the action they recently completed online – either clicked or bought something from the ad. This method helps to identify the most recent population’s needs and wishes and understand how digital marketing campaigns can increase engagement through the respondents’ perspectives.
