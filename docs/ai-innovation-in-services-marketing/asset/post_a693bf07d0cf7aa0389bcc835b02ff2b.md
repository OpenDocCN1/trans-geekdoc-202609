
Services have specific market characteristics and needs. The main differences between services and products are that services are fundamentally intangible and are created through direct interactions with customers (Kotler & Armstrong, 2015). Services allow for differentiation, modification, and customization, thereby enabling margin and profit. Services range from telecommunications to banking services, among others, such as schools, hospitals, professionals, and even domestic services, whether through the Internet or retail (Kahtalian, 2000).

According to Kahtalian (2000) and Kotler & Armstrong (2015), services also need to be understood for their specific characteristics, such as:

- Inseparability: each service has a moment when its production and consumption are simultaneous/indissociable. This differs from product manufacturing companies that have a clearly defined production time and consumption time. Thus, while a teacher ‘produces’ their class, it is ‘consumed’ by the students simultaneously.
- Variability: services are variable, meaning they can vary between the provider and the customer. This characteristic has both positive and negative aspects. The advantage is that variation allows for service customization and differentiation. The disadvantage is that it is difficult to establish flawless service standards because quality depends on the provider.
- Intangibility: services are not visible or tangible, meaning they cannot be seen, tasted, felt, heard, or smelled before purchase. Tangible items serve as evidence of service. They are guides, suggestions of what it should be. It can even be said that one of the marketing techniques for a service is precisely to ‘make the service tangible,’ that is, to make the invisible visible, emphasizing what the service promises.
- Perishability: services cannot be stored. They are temporary and delivered at a specific time and place, meaning the company must be able to manage the service demand. It is important to find the right balance between supply and demand to avoid a common problem in services, which can be failing to meet excess demand or incurring high operational costs by not meeting necessary demand.

Thinking about service marketing means thinking about value through performance. It values people, internal marketing, relationships established with customers and partners. It is understanding and meeting customer expectations through the transformation provided by the service (Kahtalian, 2000).

It is important to remember service marketing excellence to prioritize a better customer experience. According to Keller & Kotler (2013) and Kotler & Armstrong (2015), this is achieved through the interaction of the following areas:

- External Marketing: a traditional marketing mix extended to services, including three additional Ps, in addition to the four traditional ones. These are: Place, Price, Promotion, and Product as traditional, and People, Process, Physical Evidence as additional ones focused on services.
- Internal Marketing: through this, the company must always motivate and prepare its team to ensure the best customer service. Providing training and support so that all employees are customer-oriented is essential.
- Interactive Marketing: this is the ‘thermometer’ to measure service quality, which depends not only on who delivers it but also on the quality of delivery itself. Employees must master and be committed to what they do, as well as be oriented to ‘serve’ customers. Customer service is essential for loyalty and generating the best possible experience through interaction and generating an exchange at the time of service delivery. We can conclude that to ensure service marketing achieves service quality and customer satisfaction through employees and the company, it is necessary to combine these three comprehensive areas. Service marketing can be applied in various areas, including healthcare. In the healthcare sector, its application can help in promoting and communicating the services offered by healthcare professionals and organizations. Additionally, it is also used to highlight the importance of service quality and measure patient satisfaction and loyalty (Corbin et al., 2001).

## THE ROLE OF MARKETING IN HEALTHCARE

According to MASB (2023b), we can identify Health Care Marketing as an influencer of the behavior of its target audience, adding that the benefits resulting from its actions would accrue to the health, whether physical or mental, of its target audience. This type of marketing can be developed and applied by individuals, hospitals/clinics, or national agencies, such as research departments or institutes.

The healthcare sector is evolving in its approach to prevention, protection, and promotion of health. As a result, healthcare professionals are challenged to always ensure a better patient experience, achieve their satisfaction and loyalty, and remain competitive in the current healthcare market (O’Connor et al., 2021). Health marketplaces bring together professionals and their patients in true collaborative relationships that empower patients to contribute to improving health.

Additionally, healthcare experts, who are truly connected to the sector, are responsible for consistently providing a holistic assessment of the advantages and experience of healthcare services to patients, thus acting based on feedback to achieve excellence in healthcare, providing a better understanding, quality, and consistency of all patient touchpoints (D. Lee, 2019; Park et al., 2016; Pisco & Biscaia, 2001).

In this competitive landscape, characterized also by digital health tools that educate patients and monitor their own health, professionals must be seen as qualified and reliable to meet patients’ needs, expectations, and sacrifices. Therefore, it has become essential to adopt effective marketing strategies for healthcare professionals to stand out in the market. In addition to digital health tools, healthcare marketing should also be seen as a way to educate and inform patients, rather than just promoting services and products (Castillo et al., 2017; Tallia et al., 2018).

However, it is important to remember that advertising, which is regulated by specific laws, and marketing in the healthcare field must be ethical and responsible; thus, marketing strategies must be evidence-based, aiming to protect patients and avoid misleading promises. Therefore, it is essential for healthcare professionals and institutions to follow these laws when devising their marketing strategies (Castillo et al., 2017; Tallia et al., 2018).

Advertising legislation in healthcare varies according to each country and/or region but generally follows common guidelines and is regulated by government health agencies. For example, in Brazil, the National Health Surveillance Agency (ANVISA) is responsible for regulating advertising of healthcare products and services, while in Portugal, the enforcement of these rules is the responsibility of the National Authority of Medicines and Health Products (INFARMED) (ANVISA, 2020; INFARMED, 2023; Decree-Law No. 238/2015 of the Ministry of Health, 2015).

Advertisements for healthcare products, such as medications, equipment, and medical treatments, must be accurate and cannot make false or misleading claims about the effectiveness or safety of the product/service. Furthermore, communication cannot exaggerate the benefits of the product or even downplay its risks. Healthcare professionals must also follow legislation on how they communicate with their audience; they cannot make false or misleading claims about products and services they offer and must always maintain patient privacy and confidentiality (Abdalla & Castilho, 2017; ANVISA, 2020; Decree-Law No. 238/2015 of the Ministry of Health, 2015; Sato, 2002).

Nowadays, patients are increasingly aware of the diversity of their options and engaged in making better healthcare decisions. Due to the increased demand for health information, more and more innovative products are being introduced to the market, targeting new patient segments (Purcârea, 2016).
