## KEY TERMS AND DEFINITIONS

**Account-Based Marketing:** Is a highly targeted marketing strategy that focuses on tailors and customizes marketing efforts to specific individual accounts or a select group of high-value accounts rather than targeting a broad audience.

**Artificial Intelligence:** Is the simulation of human intelligence in machines, allowing them to perform various tasks.

**Case Study:** Is a research method that involves an in-depth examination and analysis of a particular individual, group, event, or situation.

**Coherent Communication:** Is the act of conveying information, ideas, or messages in a clear, logical, and organized manner to ensure that the recipient can easily understand and follow the intended message.

**Invest Lithuania:** Is the national agency responsible for attracting foreign investment to Lithuania and supporting businesses in the country.

**Personalization:** Is the practice of tailoring products, services, content, or experiences to individual preferences, needs, or characteristics.

**Storytelling:** Is the art of conveying a message or information through a narrative, often involving characters and emotions, to engage an audience.

**Synergy:** Refers to the combined or cooperative interaction of multiple elements, where the result is more significant or effective than each element's individual contributions or efforts on its own.

## ABSTRACT

Technological evolution has impelled companies to adopt innovative solutions supported by technologies. In a particular way, brands have been migrating from traditional marketing to digital marketing, with the vanguard of emerging technologies allowing them to obtain competitive advantages. Nowadays, artificial intelligence promises to transform the area of marketing, considering that machine learning, natural language processing, and predictive analytics are revolutionizing the way brands interact with their consumers, personalize experiences, and make informed data-driven decisions. This chapter, after contextualizing artificial intelligence within organizations and the competitive advantages it can represent, discusses AI as an automatic pilot for brand empowerment and proposes a conceptual model that, using artificial intelligence, allows companies to be trained and streamline digital marketing functions to empower brands. Finally, the authors also present some scenarios in which the proposed model can be applied.

## INTRODUCTION

In a society that reveals itself to be a massive data producing system, the ability to process data and generate information and knowledge is currently inaccessible to human processing capacity. In this scenario, brand management is now a very demanding and extremely complex time-to-market exercise. However, if brands see in this reality an over-information, they will be making a big mistake – there is no such thing as an over-information. Not when we have Artificial Intelligence (AI).

AI and its derivative technologies (e.g., Machine Learning and Deep Learning), combined with the increase in computational capacity to process data, have demonstrated unique capabilities to analyze massive amounts of data and, from these, detect patterns, which allow characterizing realities and trends, anticipate scenarios and even detect weak insights. In this context AI is driving the change in the way brands and customers interact with one another.

Business is strongly influenced by AI, being a good example the sales cycle, consumers habits and customs support activities (Trawnig et al., 2022). Additionally, and according (Davenport et al., 2020), AI and its algorithms is capable of processing massive amounts of data, with almost no human interaction, and from multiple data sources. This process is also capable of extracting important insights that will support the prediction and anticipation of consumer behaviors, in real time.

There is however a challenge to combine this capability of AI to analyze data and detect patterns and the relational nature that exists between brands and consumers (Aaker et al., 2004). Indeed, being able to understand and anticipate trends might be extremely helpful for brands to remain meaningful in an era where the consumer has several alternatives as never before.

This research discusses the role that Generative AI has and will increasingly play in empowering and leveraging brands, allowing them not only to better manage how they relate to their customers, but also to strategically create new brands that can meet the demand of their customers or niche markets that are being formed still in an embryonic stage – generating competitive advantages against competing brands. We also explore the concept of using AI as an autopilot for brands – that is, the strategy and its actions are guided, shaped and redefined in real time by AI. In a future that will in all probability be much more data producing (e.g., what exists today will be the tip of the iceberg of tomorrow) AI will be an indispensable tool for brands, with the AI algorithms used being the differentiating factors of the competitive brands-capacity.

In terms of organization, this work, after an introduction to the topic of AI and its role in the context of business and brand management, discusses in chapter 2 the importance of brands as a factor in creating value. Then in chapter 3, it contextualizes the role that AI has on organizations. In chapter 4, the potential of AI to be an automatic pilot for brands is explored. In chapter 5, some tools are presented that use AI to help perform a set of tasks related to marketing professionals. In chapter 6, a conceptual model is proposed that demonstrates the vision of using generative AI to automate the creation and management of brands, design and development of new products, customer relationship management and the empowerment of marketers and decision-makers. In chapter 7, the proposed conceptual model is discussed through the use of some scenarios. Finally, chapter 8 presents a conclusion to the work.

## A BRAND AS A VALUE CREATION FACTOR

In the contemporary world, brands are almost omnipresent, becoming part of our daily lives. Their influence can be observed across multiple industries, cultural sector and even as a mechanism for affirmation of territories.

The brand concept can be defined as: “marketing-related intangible asset including, but not limited to, names, terms, signs, symbols, logos and designs, or a combination of these, intended to identify goods, services or entities, or a combination of these, creating distinctive images and associations in the minds of stakeholders, thereby generating economic benefits/values” (ISO 10668:2010).

For any company, brands are the intangible asset most valuable (Keller & Lehmann, 2003). However, the concept of brand is multifaceted and relatively complex, both from the point of view of companies that provide services or create goods under their own brand, and from the point of view of consumers who perceive that brand. The reality is that the brand image desired and projected by the organization does not always coincide with the brand’s perception by customers. Additionally, brands are under the influence of changes constantly, in marketing and the external environment, requiring constant adaptation to market trends and consumer demand.

Presently, technological advances have led to a highly interconnected global landscape, necessitating a new evaluation in the field of branding research from the standpoints of businesses, customers, and the community. According to Swaminathan et al. (2020), the hyperconnectivity has enabled established brands to expand their geographic reach and social influence, while new types of brand entities (such as ideas, people, places, and organizational brands) are further expanding the brand landscape.

A brand brings together a set of attributes, benefits, symbols and experiences that identify and differentiate a product, service, company or person from its competitors. Brands have the power to influence consumers’ perceptions, preferences, attitudes and behaviors, generating loyalty, trust, satisfaction and recommendation.
