1.1 What is Cryptocurrency?

A cryptocurrency is a kind of digital or virtual money designed to be used as a means of trade. It works similarly to real-world cash, only it doesn't have a physical form and relies on encryption to function. New units may only be introduced if certain requirements are satisfied since cryptocurrencies function freely and autonomously, without the need for a bank or central authority. Bitcoins have a limit of 21 million, after which no more will be generated.

Overview of Cryptocurrency

Individuals employed the barter system in the caveman period, in which products and services were transferred between two or more people. Someone may, for example, swap 7 apples for 7 oranges. Because of its obvious shortcomings, the barter fell out of favor:

*   There is no universally accepted measure of value: you must determine how many of your belongings you are ready to sell for other belongings, and not all belongings may be split. 

*   Everyone's needs must be met: if you have someone to trade, someone must want it, and you must desire what another person is providing.

*   A living animal, for example, cannot be divided into smaller components. Unlike our current cash, which fits in such a wallet or is saved on a mobile phone, the items are difficult to transfer.

*   The money went through a few revisions once people recognized the barter system wasn't working too well: The first official money was struck in 110 B.C., jewel florins were introduced and used throughout Europe in 1250 A.D., but from 1600 to 1900, paper currency was acquired great appeal and was used all over the globe. It's how modern cash, as we know it, was born.

Paper money, coins, lines of credit, and digital wallets, such as Mobile Payments, Amazon Pay, Paytm, and PayPal, are examples of modern cash. All of it is under the supervision of banks and governments, implying there is a centralized regulatory body that regulates the use of paper cash and credit cards.
