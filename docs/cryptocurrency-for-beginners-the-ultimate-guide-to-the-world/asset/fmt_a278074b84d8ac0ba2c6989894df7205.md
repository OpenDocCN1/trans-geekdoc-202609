
Cryptocurrency is based on a distributed ledger called a blockchain. Understanding blockchain technology can help you see why it is so important to the digital currency's power. The "block" is made up of encrypted data chunks. The "chain" refers to the public database where the blocks are stored and linked sequentially. Each block in the blockchain has its own unique code that distinguishes it from all other blocks. A hash is a name given to this one-of-a-kind code. The blocks of data that are added to a blockchain are added in chronological order. After the creation of the last block, a new block is created with its own unique hash. The ledger and/or database of blocks in the chain is distributed globally at the same time, across thousands, or in the case of Ethereum and Bitcoin, millions of computers. Assume that someone wished to forge a single data block on the chain. In that case, they'll have to change all of the blocks from a certain point in time forward, as well as update all of the computers that have copies of the blockchain ledger. Theoretically, this is possible, but the amount of money and influence required to pull it off makes it nearly impossible.

### 3.3.3 Cryptocurrency transactions are confidential

Traditional government-issued currencies allow you to conduct private transactions or make payments in person using physical cash. Paper, metal, cloth, as well as plastic currencies, account for a small percentage of total fiat money in circulation. Large cash withdrawals are quickly detected and investigated by a central authority, such as governments as well as financial system regulators. Cryptocurrency is a unique concept. To detect the exchange between two people or companies, well-designed math is required. This is mostly done behind people's backs. While the ledger or list of transactions can be viewed by anyone in the world, the parties who exchange cryptocurrency are more private. Cryptocurrencies are continuing to hold electronically in digital wallets by definition. The owner is the owner of the wallet's private key. The currency is mostly exchanged digitally from users' anonymous wallets.

### 3.3.4 Cryptocurrency security grows through time & value

A hacker would have to have control of more than half of the computers in the "consensus" network. All of the computers which receive copies of the blockchain or distributed ledger form the consensus network. The cryptocurrency networks for more established cryptos, such as Bitcoin or Ethereum, are so large that a hack is nearly impossible. Because the cryptocurrency network was much smaller in the early days, it was easier to gain the majority of control.

### 3.5.2 SmartHive & SmartVoting

SmartHive is the project's lifeblood, and it's designed to keep the project running indefinitely.
