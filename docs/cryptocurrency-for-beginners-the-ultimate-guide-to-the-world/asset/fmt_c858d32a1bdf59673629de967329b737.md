## 4.3 Public block chains

A public blockchain is usually decentralized and has no single network-controlled entity. Data on a decentralized blockchain is protected because data cannot be changed or manipulated until it has been checked on the blockchain. Possibly the best examples of a decentralized blockchain are Bitcoin and Ethereum.

## 4.4. Private block chains

An invitation-only network regulated by a single organization is a private blockchain.

Network entrants need authorization for the blockchain to be read, written or audited. Different degrees of accessibility may be available, and details can be encrypted to preserve business confidentiality. Without rendering data available, private block chains allow companies to use distributed ledger technologies. Private block chains, which take a lot of time and resources to verify transactions, are quicker, more stable and much more cost-effective compared to public block chains.
