Organizations that handle cryptocurrencies (or "cryptos") must guarantee that they are following the Virtual currency Security Standard and making efforts to safeguard transactions (CCSS). Wallet/Key Usage

It's also crucial to keep track of how your bitcoin wallet and keys are being used. Risks including lost or stolen keys, as well as the unintended revelation of the purse holder's identity, may be prevented by following best practices such as:

*   Using keys/seeds exclusively in trustworthy contexts

*   Verifying identities, references, and history of every key/seed-holders 

*   Assigning duplicate keys to each wallet for recovery reasons

The company must be able to manage who has exposure to cryptographic data and who can act on it. Keyholders must go through extensive training in order to understand their duties and processes. The Bitcoin information system may be made more secure by using "least privilege principles," which provide users just the access they need to execute their jobs and nothing more.

Key Storage

An organization must preserve its cryptocurrency keys with the same care that it would protect the keys to a bank vault. Where applicable, they should be kept utilizing encryption, private keys, and physical locks. The backup cryptographic key should also be saved safely (on paper, digitally, or in another format) and protected from the elements.

Other Considerations

It's also critical to implement a crucial compromise policy. Having a procedure in place that dictates what should be done if an encryption key and its holder is stolen may help to limit risk and losses.

In addition, a data sterilization policy is required. Because data may stay on electronic content even after erasure, you must ensure that your employees are aware of the hazards. By giving qualified staff access to technologies that execute safe data erasure on retired equipment like servers, hard disc drives, and portable storage, you can avoid information leaking.
