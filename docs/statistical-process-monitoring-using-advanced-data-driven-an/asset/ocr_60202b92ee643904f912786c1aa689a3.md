can be expressed recursively as:

```
\begin{aligned}
z_t &= \gamma z_t + (1 - \gamma) [ \dots ] \\
&= \gamma z_t + \gamma(1 - \gamma)z_{t-1} + (1 - \gamma)^2 z_{t-2}. \\
&= \gamma z_n + \gamma(1 - \gamma)z_{n-1} + \gamma(1 - \gamma)^2 z_{n-2} + \cdots \\
&+ \gamma(1 - \gamma)^{n-1} z_1 + (1 - \gamma)^n z_0. \quad (2.35)
\end{aligned}
```

The EWMA decision function in (2.35) can be expressed in compact form as

```
z_t = \gamma \sum_{t=1}^n (1-\gamma)^{n-t} z_t + (1-\gamma)^n z_0, \quad (2.36)
```

where \(\gamma(1-\gamma)^{n-t}\) denotes the weight for \(z_t\), which exponentially decreases for previous observations. The parameters \(L\) and \(\gamma\) play an important role in designing the EWMA scheme [56,54]. The value of \(L\) is frequently fixed in practice to be 3, which implies a false alarm rate of 0.27%. Generally, a choice of small values of \(\gamma\) (i.e., where less importance is placed on the newer observations) is used in order to extend the sensitivity to small deviations, while the use of large values of \(\gamma\) (i.e., EWMA with short memory) is suited for detecting larger changes in the process mean [56,62,56]. For the purpose of detecting small changes, in practice the value of \(\gamma\) is usually selected in the interval [0.1, 0.3] [62,56].

In the absence of anomalies, the distribution of the EWMA statistic is \(z_t \sim N(\mu_0, \sigma_{z_t}^2)\), where \(\sigma_{z_t} = \sigma_0 \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{(2-\gamma)}[1 - (1-\gamma)^{2t}]}\) and \(\sigma_0\) represents the standard deviation of the fault-free measurements. However, when a mean shift occurs at the time \(1 \le \tau \le n\), the distribution of the EWMA statistic is computed as \(z_t \sim N(\mu_0 + [1 - (1-\gamma)^{n-\tau+1}](\mu_1 - \mu_0), \sigma_{z_t}^2)\). Accordingly, when faults happen, the mean of the EWMA decision function, \(z_t\), is a weighted average of \(\mu_0\) and \(\mu_1\), and the weight related to \(\mu_1\) becomes large when \(n\) is large. Then, this clearly highlights that the statistic \(z_t\) provides pertinent information about the mean shift. The EWMA scheme flags faults when the monitoring statistic \(z_t\), as given in (2.34), exceeds the upper and lower control limits defined as

```
\(\begin{cases} UCL = \mu_0 + L\sigma_{z_t}, \\ LCL = \mu_0 - L\sigma_{z_t}, \end{cases} \quad (2.37)\)
```

where \(\mu_0\) is the targeted mean, \(L\) is the width of the control limit, and \(\sigma\) is the standard deviation of the fault-free or preliminary data set.
