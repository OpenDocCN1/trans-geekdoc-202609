
### BCP/DRP Plan Development Recommendations

Let us consider *BCP* and *DRP plan* development instructional guidelines of two well-known US Institutes: *SANS Institute*.

#### SANS Institute Approach

*SANS Institute* differentiates business continuity plan (`BCP`) and disaster recovery plan (`DRP`). `BCP` differs from `DRP` in that it plays the main role in *business continuity management program* (`BCM`) of organization (see [Figure 3.13](#Ch04.xhtml#cap_lbl0290) and [Figure 3.14](#Ch04.xhtml#cap_lbl0291)).

#### SANS Institute (www.sans.org)

##### Project Initiation

The support of the corporate management team is required for a successful start of the project.

**Step 1.1. Obtaining Management Support**

Obtaining management support ensures sufficient funding for the project, as well as assistance in coordinating and project management.

**Step 1.2. Studying Incident History**

Analysis and discussion of the factual account of incidents in *business continuity* (`BC`) allow timely identification of existing problems in that area and focus on solving the most pressing issues of business continuity management.

**Step 1.3. Business Requirements Analysis**

Before developing the `BCP/DRP` plan, it is recommended to determine the business requirements, for example:

*   Business continuity index minimization;
*   Minimization of time for resumption of mission-critical processes and operations;
*   Minimization of financial losses in case of emergency;
*   Maintenance of reputation and positive company image in case of emergency;
*   Fulfillment of international and national regulatory requirements, etc.

**Step 1.4. Designated Person Assignment**

It is recommended to assign a designated person responsible for the `BCP/DRP` plan development and implementation. Usually, a project manager of `BCP/DRP` plan development and implementation is assigned to this position. Their duties include:

*   Determination of purpose and aims of the project;
*   Project office organization;
*   Determination and control of project risks;
*   Determination and control of project's critical success factors;
*   Development of the project charter;
*   Project management;
*   Budgeting control;
*   Participation in project control board;
*   Required cooperation arrangements;
*   Project changes control;
*   Timely presentation of project results to management;
*   Project maintenance and support, etc.

**Step 1.5. Project Office Organization**

It is necessary to create a project office for successful project implementation. It is recommended to include representatives from all main divisions of the organization in the project office structure. For example, such representatives may be employees of the departments of financial management, accounting, production, sales, development, information technology, logistics, marketing, security, human resources, legal department, etc. Below is an approximate version of task allocation among all project participants:

*   **Project Manager** controls project progress and coordinates activities of different groups.
*   **Project Management Team** approves the project, budget apportionment, and sets requirements.
*   **Human Resources Department Team** manages hiring of temporary staff to support business operations, if necessary.
*   **Information Team** interacts with the media in case of emergency.
*   **Legal Team** deals with resolution of legal issues in case of emergency.
*   **IT Security Team** ensures confidentiality, integrity, and availability of machine data during the project.
*   **Physical Security Team** provides physical security of assets in case of emergency.
*   **Operational Safety Team** is responsible for technology operations during a crisis.
*   **Emergency Response Team** actuates business continuity and disaster recovery plans in case of emergencies.
*   **Damage Assessment Team** determines the type and character of damage caused by the incident.
*   **Backup Team** organizes backup, storage, and recovery of information assets.
*   **Backup Office Team** is responsible for organizing a working backup office to resume business in case of an emergency.
*   **Equipment Maintenance Team** is responsible for the repair of assets that failed as a result of the incident.

**Step 1.6. Financial Expenses Calculation for BCM**

It is necessary to calculate the direct and indirect costs of the `BCM program` to make an adequate decision in the field of business continuity management. Different techniques, such as `TCO` and `TVO Gartner`, can be used for this.
