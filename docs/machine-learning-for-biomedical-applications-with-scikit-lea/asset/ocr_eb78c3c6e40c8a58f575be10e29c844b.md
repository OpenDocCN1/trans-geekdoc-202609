# ## 10.4. Conclusions

We have now seen the design, training, and evaluation process for fully connected neural network. We have seen that one of the primary advantages of fully connected neural networks is their flexibility to adapt to complex patterns in the data, demonstrated in Sect.10.1. However, their disadvantage is a large number of parameters and resulting overfitting, as we have seen in Sect. 10.3. In fact, the fully connected networks are not very different from classical machine learning models presented in later chapters of this book and may perform any better. The power of neural networks can be seen when the convolutional layers are introduced and more complex tasks, such as image segmentation, are attempted. We will demonstrate this in Chapter 11.

Once we start working with large databases of images, rather than relatively compact feature matrices, we will also need to spend paying attention to computational resources, such as the training time and amount of memory. The problems with training time are addressed using a GPU to train networks and predict the outputs. The problems with memory can be addressed by training in smaller batches, rather than using the whole dataset, and loading these batches directly from the disk, rather than run-time memory. Training in batches also decreases the training time, as we explained in Sect. 3.1.2.

These advanced concepts and techniques are the topic of the final chapter of this book.
