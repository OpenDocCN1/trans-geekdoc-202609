
• Security zones are used to separate sensitive areas both in the perimeter of the facility, as well as internal sensitive areas

• Entry control points into a facility should be single points of entry or exit where personnel traffic can be controlled.

• Fencing is used to protect the perimeter of a facility and can be various heights and have features such as concertina wiring. Multiple fences may be used to create physical security zones.

• Barriers are used to block and route both vehicle traffic and pedestrian traffic and can be natural barriers or made of concrete, steel, and other materials placed in key areas around the facility.

• Both exterior and interior lighting should be bright enough to illuminate the area and prevent someone from being able to hide or remain undetected.

• External lighting should be spaced so that there are no dark spots between light poles.

• Surveillance can be human-based, in the form of security guards, or technology-based, in the form of video cameras.

• Human guards provide an added benefit in that they can react quickly and make split-second decisions when needed to protect lives, equipment, and facilities.

• Guard dogs can be used in limited situations, but often incur expense and liability.

In addition to controls mentioned for the perimeter, internal security controls that should be considered include the following:

• Personnel entry requirements usually are guided by security clearance, need-to-know based on job position, supervisory approval, and reason for access.

• Locks can be manual or electronic and provide an additional layer of protection for sensitive areas.

• Auditing facility access is necessary to ensure that only authorized personnel enter the facility and sensitive areas. Both success and failure of physical access events should be audited to determine both trends and to see significant events.

#### 7.14 ANSWERS

**[1](#ch07a.xhtml#r_ch7ques14-1).** C Installing a centralized single entry and exit point for the facility would prevent people from coming from all directions off the street into the main entrance. Although fencing would help achieve this goal, by itself fencing would not consolidate entry and exit of personnel into a single point. ### Address personnel safety and security concerns

In this objective we will discuss personnel safety and security. Many aspects of this topic have been covered in other objectives as well, but they bear repeating here because the preservation of human life and the safety and security of personnel are the most important priorities within the organization. We will discuss personnel safety and security within the contexts of travel, security awareness and training, emergency management, and duress.

#### Personnel Safety and Security

Giving precedence to the preservation of human life and the safety and security of personnel is required not only by numerous laws and regulations but also to imbue the organization with high moral and ethical standards. People are the most important resource the organization has; buildings, equipment, and even information systems and data can be replaced, but human lives cannot. We discussed personnel security in other objectives, so we will focus primarily on the safety aspect of this objective.

##### Travel

Before an employee travels on behalf of the organization, especially an executive or an employee carrying sensitive information of the organization, they need to be made aware of the broad range of threat actors who specifically target business travelers. They may not necessarily be a target due to where they work or what information they have, but anyone can fall victim to criminal activities. In addition to the precautions that any reasonable person should take in a potentially harmful situation, employees who have sensitive information or have critical responsibilities should be careful when traveling to prevent harm or compromise to themselves and to company assets, such as mobile devices.

Some of the specific precautions employees should take when traveling include

• Take a mobile device containing your organization’s sensitive information only if absolutely necessary; in that scenario, minimize the sensitive information to only that which is needed on the trip and ensure that it is encrypted.

• Take special precautions to maintain physical control of said mobile devices. During the times when maintaining physical control is impossible, such as when traveling through airport security, ensure the device is locked or powered down.

• Be aware of your surroundings at all times.

• Have a plan when traveling, particularly to high-risk international areas or areas with higher crime rates and share it with people who are traveling with you or at least people back at your home. Examples include specific times that you will call them or meet, as well as alternate meeting sites and what they should do if they don’t hear from you within a certain amount of time.

• Avoid free Wi-Fi hotspots, unless they are in a trusted location.

• Only use secure virtual private network (`VPN`) connections to access company network resources, including e-mail.

##### Security Training and Awareness

Although discussed previously, it’s important to emphasize the concept of security training and awareness, particularly when discussing personnel security and safety. We already discussed the differences between awareness, training, and education in Objective 1.13. Rather than review the types of training available or presentation techniques you should provide for your personnel, we will focus on the content of the training, and in this context, it should concern their own personal safety and security.

You should design and implement formal training programs that help employees increase their safety awareness and develop improved security habits. Cyber hygiene is a good topic to train on in terms of general personal security. It can help personnel not only understand the criticality of obeying security policies at work but also develop cyber hygiene habits that will protect their own personal information at home.

Security awareness and training focused on personnel security and safety generally includes the following topics:

• Personal and professional cyber hygiene (e.g., control of personal information, awareness of social engineering techniques, control of portable storage devices, etc.)

• Awareness of surroundings and environment
