### Software Assurance Maturity Model

The Open Web Application Security Project (OWASP) is a professional organization whose stated goal is to improve the security of software. Toward that goal, OWASP has developed its own Software Assurance Maturity Model (SAMM), which is specifically focused on secure software development. Any organization can use the model, regardless of the size of their development effort. SAMM is broken into five critical business functions. The idea behind the model is that an organization that produces software should perform these five functions. Each of these functions is further broken down into three security practices, which contain security-related activities within each function. The total of 15 security practices can be assessed for effectiveness and maturity, which in turn gives the organization an idea of the maturity level at which it currently resides, and which maturity level it needs to pursue.

### Operation and Maintenance

Regardless of the development methodology or life cycle model that is used, once the software has been developed, accepted by the user, and implemented, it goes into operation and maintenance (`O&M`). However, during `O&M`, development does not necessarily stop. Often patches or upgrades are produced, based upon new functional or performance requirements. Security mechanisms may be introduced into the software, and previously undiscovered flaws may be identified and scheduled for mitigation during another update or iteration of the software. Version control is of critical importance after implementation to ensure that only authorized production code is in the production environment.

In addition to new requirements, updates, flaw remediation, and other expected changes to software, the vulnerability management process can affect software during `O&M`. New vulnerabilities are discovered every day, either in applications, their code libraries, or even in components that are incidental to the software, such as network connectivity modules or authentication mechanisms. These vulnerabilities must be addressed through patching, secure configuration, and overall security management.

### Change Management

Change management is also a critical consideration in software development. No application ever survives its entire life cycle without changing to some degree, whether those changes are minor, such as a patch or an update, or even a major redesign of the software itself. Change management must be a formalized process with structure, rather than an ad hoc effort. Changes must always be carefully considered, tested, and documented.

While the formal change management process is necessarily an overarching management process, `change control` happens at a lower level, and is the process of controlling specific changes in a software application during its life cycle, and documenting those changes. Note that this can also be referred to as `software version control`. This is very similar to the configuration management process for a system in that it happens at the system or software level. The formal change and control management process essentially consists of requesting the change, testing the change, approving the change, implementing the change, and documenting the change; these formal processes may vary from organization to organization, depending on their software development maturity level.

### Integrated Product Team

An `integrated product team (IPT)` consists of team members with expertise in a wide variety of areas. This enables programmers to work with all manner of operations personnel, including IT, accounting, marketing, engineering, and other functional areas not traditionally involved with software development. This gives the developers insight into how these areas actually work, allowing them to produce better code that actually meets the needs of their customers. Note that an IPT is not necessarily a development methodology, but more of a management technique that contributes to software design and security by allowing the right people from a variety of functional areas to be involved in the software development process. However, IPTs often use Agile development methodologies.
