# 3.4. Prioritised experience replay

In order to efficiently reduce correlations between the consecutive samples, we store each time step's transition data (S_t, A_t, r_t, S_{t+1}) in a replay buffer and draw mini-batches of samples from there to update the Q network (Mnih, 2013). Instead of uniform sampling, we sample more important transitions from the buffer which is the essence of PER (Schaul et al., 2016) as is formulated in Eq. (6).

```
P(i) = p_i^α / Σ_k p_k^α, p_i = |l_i| + ε.  (6)
```

PER naturally generates a bias due to over-sampling of high priority items which may change the TD distribution and skew the expected value calculation. Importance sampling is used to control the sampling speed of high priority samples.

# 3.5. Discrete wavelet transformation

Lastly, recognising the intrinsically noisy nature of the minute stock data, we employ Discrete Wavelet Transformation (DWT) to mitigate the noise's bad influence on the real features of observed time series. Unlike the Fourier Transformation, which uses a series of sine waves with different frequencies, Wavelet Transformation uses a series of wavelets of different scales which makes the data localised in time (Karim et al., 2011)...

Working like a cascade of low-pass and high-pass filters, DWT effectively deconstructs time series data into its frequency sub-bands. We choose to use the Daubechies wavelets with a threshold λ of 0.7 determined through empirical analysis so as to filter away the high frequency part of the normalised version of stock time series whose frequency coefficients are higher than the threshold.
