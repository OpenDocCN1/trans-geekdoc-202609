Machine learning (ML), as a branch of artificial intelligence, has attracted mounting attention among materials scientists in recent years.16–18 This surge in attention is largely due to ML’s capacity to distill valuable insights from data sets and autonomously refine its computational models, thereby yielding reliable results that can significantly advance the field of materials science.19 For predicting material properties, ML methods utilize the material composition, fundamental element properties, or structural parameter as input (x) and the properties of interest as output (Y), which is equivalent to building a functional form of Y = f(x).20 Using a well-trained ML model can save the time and labor costs incurred by the trial-and-error approach, accelerate the discovery of new materials, and reveal the underlying relationships between properties, composition, and structure.21 Most published works involve combining ML methods with existing material databases22–24 and first-principles calculations to predict the intrinsic properties of materials.25–30 However, combining ML approaches with experiments provides a pathway for optimizing experimental processes, exploring physical mechanisms, and accelerating the research of novel materials more effectively. For instance, a framework combining ML and the finite element method (FEM) was proposed by Qiao et al. to optimize the injection molding process of complex ceramic parts.31 To identify the most important descriptors for ferroelectric materials, He et al. adopted regression and classification learning in different material systems including lead-free, lead-containing ceramics, and antiferroelectrics and then screened out three descriptors from 46 potential descriptors determining ferroelectricity.32 Yuan et al. proposed ML adaptive design strategies to find BaTiO3-based multicomponent solid solutions with enhanced energy storage density or electrostrain, significantly reducing the required experimental iterations for identifying compounds with higher Wrec or electrostrain.33,34 In this work, an ML approach combined with three strategies for feature construction is devised, utilizing data encompassing material composition, characterization properties, and sintering process parameters to build regression models to predict Wrec and η of BT-BMO ceramics. A systematic and comprehensive comparative analysis is undertaken to rigorously assess the performance of these models and

identify the most effective strategy and model for regression tasks. The key features are subjected to rigorous evaluation employing a multistep selection method, including the Pearson correlation matrix, importance analysis, and the best subset method. Through these methods, the dimensionality of the feature space is reduced without sacrificing accuracy, thus enhancing the efficiency of model training. Additionally, the transparency and comprehensibility of the model were augmented by employing the Shapley additive explanation (SHAP) method,35 revealing the relationships between model inputs and outputs, which is critical for understanding the model’s decision-making process and interpreting the results. Furthermore, an experimental synthesis is performed to validate the predictive capabilities of the models, confirming the reliability and practical applicability of the method. Our research integrates ML techniques with a practical application, ensuring robust, interpretable, and reliable estimations of Wrec and η in BT-BMO ceramics, providing valuable insights applicable to actual experimental scenarios.

**Data Collection and Processing.** When utilizing data-driven ML methods to predict specific properties, the creation of a high-quality data set is indispensable for building a robust ML model. The compilation of data involves a series of published literature studies spanning several decades on BaTiO3–BiMeO3 relaxor ferroelectric ceramics. To reduce the potential uncertainties arising from different experiment methods and characterization, the collected data in this study exclusively originated from bulk ceramic samples synthesized by a conventional solid-state reaction method.

Data of 28 kinds of BT-BMO material systems were collected from the published literature in which BMO contained single trivalent metallic ions (Me = Al3+, Y3+, Yb3+, etc.), binary trivalent metallic ion (Me = (Mg1/2Ti1/2)3+, (Ni2/Nb1)3/3+, etc.), and multiple solid solution systems.12,14,15,36–58 Each of the 28 kinds of materials has a different number of entries in the data set based on the content of BMO and applied electric fields.
