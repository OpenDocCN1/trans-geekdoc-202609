
## Machine Learning-Assisted Accelerated Research of Energy Storage Properties of BaTiO₃–BiMeO₃ Ceramics

Jian Liu, Peifeng Xiong, Changjiao Li, Hua Hao*, and Hanxing Liu*

### ABSTRACT
The exploration of dielectric materials with excellent energy storage properties has always been a research focus in the field of materials science. The development of a technical method that can accurately predict the energy storage characteristics of ceramics will significantly accelerate the pace of research into energy storage materials. In this research, a machine learning method was utilized with the aim of accurately predicting the energy storage density (Wrec) and energy storage efficiency (η) of BaTiO3–BiMeO3 (BT-BMO) ferroelectric ceramics. Initially, a data set was established on the energy storage properties of BT-BMO bulk ceramics by consulting relevant published literature. Three distinct feature vector spaces were constructed based on the physicochemical characteristics of constituent elements, related property information, and sintering process parameters. After that, multiple machine learning algorithm models were built to train and predict Wrec and η. For the prediction of energy storage density, the 10-fold cross-validation coefficient of determination (r²) and root-mean-squared error (RMSE) of the GBR model are 0.974 and 0.142, respectively. For the prediction of energy storage efficiency, the 10-fold cross-validation r² and RMSE of the LGBM model are 0.894 and 0.068, respectively. To enhance the interpretability of the models, the Shapley additive explanation method was introduced, revealing and briefly analyzing the important features that influence the target performance. Finally, the material system of xBaTiO3–(1 – x)Bi(Zn2/3Ta1/3)O3, which was not included in the data set, was synthesized experimentally and tested. The experimental results were found to be close to the model's predictions, thereby validating the effectiveness of the method. This study provides a new approach that could accelerate the development of dielectric materials with excellent energy storage properties.

### INTRODUCTION

As classical energy storage electronic devices, dielectric ceramic capacitors have been widely studied due to their high power density, fast charge/discharge rate, and high thermal stability.1–5 However, the relatively low energy storage density restricts their applications. Considering environmental concerns of lead-based ceramics, the development of lead-free ceramics with excellent energy storage properties is an issue of great significance. As known, the total energy density (W), recoverable energy storage density (Wrec), and efficiency (η) of dielectric materials can be calculated by polarization–electric field (P–E) hysteresis loops.6 At present, linear dielectrics (LDs), ferroelectrics (FEs), antiferroelectrics (AFEs), and relaxor ferroelectrics (RFEs) are actively researched for energy storage applications.7–11 Among these materials, RFEs are ideal for energy storage materials with high maximum polarization (Pmax), moderate breakdown strength (Eb), and low remnant polarization (Pr). BaTiO3-based RFE ceramics are one of the dominant branches of lead-free energy storage dielectrics. The BaTiO3–BiMeO3 (BT-BMO) [Me = Sc3+, (Mg1/2Ti1/2)3+, (Ni2/3Nb1/3)3+, etc.] ceramics recognized as a “weakly coupled relaxor”12 commonly exhibit slim P–E loops with negligible Pr and high η. It is reported that there are three advantages for BMO introduction: (1) it lowers the sintering temperature, inhibits grain growth, and improves density, thereby increasing Eb of materials; (2) it helps to form polar nanoregions, strengthening the relaxation behaviors of BT-based ceramics, consequently reducing their Pr; (3) the hybridization of Bi 6s and O 2p orbitals can increase Pmax of materials.13–15 It has been widely acknowledged that introducing an appropriate BMO can enhance the energy storage performance of BT-based ceramics. Nevertheless, there are numerous possible element combinations for BMO composition, and also, the doping content and processing parameters will certainly affect the final energy storage performance. It is quite a challenge to find an appropriate material system by the traditional trial-and-error method.

Machine learning (ML), as a branch of artificial intelligence, has attracted mounting attention among materials scientists in recent years.16–18 This surge in attention is largely due to ML’s capacity to distill valuable insights from data sets and autonomously refine its computational models, thereby yielding reliable results that can significantly advance the field of materials science.19 For predicting material properties, ML methods utilize the material composition, fundamental element properties, or structural parameter as input (x) and the properties of interest as output (Y), which is equivalent to building a functional form of Y = f(x).20 Using a well-trained ML model can save the time and labor costs incurred by the trial-and-error approach, accelerate the discovery of new materials, and reveal the underlying relationships between properties, composition, and structure.21 Most published works involve combining ML methods with existing material databases22–24 and first-principles calculations to predict the intrinsic properties of materials.25–30 However, combining ML approaches with experiments provides a pathway for optimizing experimental processes, exploring physical mechanisms, and accelerating the research of novel materials more effectively. For instance, a framework combining ML and the finite element method (FEM) was proposed by Qiao et al. to optimize the injection molding process of complex ceramic parts.31 To identify the most important descriptors for ferroelectric materials, He et al. adopted regression and classification learning in different material systems including lead-free, lead-containing ceramics, and antiferroelectrics and then screened out three descriptors from 46 potential descriptors determining ferroelectricity.32 Yuan et al. proposed ML adaptive design strategies to find BaTiO3-based multicomponent solid solutions with enhanced energy storage density or electrostrain, significantly reducing the required experimental iterations for identifying compounds with higher Wrec or electrostrain.33,34 In this work, an ML approach combined with three strategies for feature construction is devised, utilizing data encompassing material composition, characterization properties, and sintering process parameters to build regression models to predict Wrec and η of BT-BMO ceramics. A systematic and comprehensive comparative analysis is undertaken to rigorously assess the performance of these models and identify the most effective strategy and model for regression tasks. The key features are subjected to rigorous evaluation employing a multistep selection method, including the Pearson correlation matrix, importance analysis, and the best subset method. Through these methods, the dimensionality of the feature space is reduced without sacrificing accuracy, thus enhancing the efficiency of model training. Additionally, the transparency and comprehensibility of the model were augmented by employing the Shapley additive explanation (SHAP) method,35 revealing the relationships between model inputs and outputs, which is critical for understanding the model’s decision-making process and interpreting the results. Furthermore, an experimental synthesis is performed to validate the predictive capabilities of the models, confirming the reliability and practical applicability of the method. Our research integrates ML techniques with a practical application, ensuring robust, interpretable, and reliable estimations of Wrec and η in BT-BMO ceramics, providing valuable insights applicable to actual experimental scenarios.

### EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

In general, the relationship between material features (x_i) and the target attribute (y) can be established through the surrogate model (f) by using ML algorithms, which can be represented as y = f(x_i). **Data Collection and Processing.** When utilizing data-driven ML methods to predict specific properties, the creation of a high-quality data set is indispensable for building a robust ML model. The compilation of data involves a series of published literature studies spanning several decades on BaTiO3–BiMeO3 relaxor ferroelectric ceramics. To reduce the potential uncertainties arising from different experiment methods and characterization, the collected data in this study exclusively originated from bulk ceramic samples synthesized by a conventional solid-state reaction method.

Data of 28 kinds of BT-BMO material systems were collected from the published literature in which BMO contained single trivalent metallic ions (Me = Al3+, Y3+, Yb3+, etc.), binary trivalent metallic ion (Me = (Mg1/2Ti1/2)3+, (Ni2/3Nb1/3)3+, etc.), and multiple solid solution systems.12,14,15,36–58 Each of the 28 kinds of materials has a different number of entries in the data set based on the content of BMO and applied electric fields.
