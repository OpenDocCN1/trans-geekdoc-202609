## Feature Construction

To construct a high-precision ML model and to conduct a comprehensive exploration of how composition, physical properties, and the sintering process influence the energy storage performances of BT-BMO, we have collected data on material element information ($C_i$), characterization properties ($L_i$), and experimental parameters related to calcination and sintering processes ($P_i$).

The element information features are derived from easily accessible elemental information. Based on domain knowledge of relaxor ferroelectrics, 20 descriptors are selected to represent the properties of A-site and B-site elements, covering the atomic mass, atomic radius, electronegativity, and other descriptors (detailed information in the Supporting Information). In order to accurately describe each component, we represent the weighted ratio of A-site and B-site information as features and define the material descriptors as follows: [32]

```
$$X_{A/B} = \frac{X_A}{X_B} = \frac{\sum_{i \in A} f_i^X X_i}{\sum_{i \in B} f_i^X X_i}$$
```

where $f_i$ is the mole fraction of the element in the chemical composition and X is the property of an element. By calculating the ratio of A- and B-site information using eq 1, 16 material descriptors were obtained, as shown in the first 16 rows in the Supporting Information, Table S1. In addition, the electronegativity (EN), first ionization energy (IE), electron affinity (EA), and pseudopotential core radius (PR) are mainly used to compare the difference between cations and anions; we defined the material descriptors X by eq 2: [32]

```
$$X = ([X_A - X_O] + |X_B - X_O|)/2$$
```

where $X_O$ represents information on oxygen. In addition, the tolerance factor ($t$) and octahedral factor ($\mu$) reflecting the stability of perovskite structures are added. Since every BT-BMO compound is a solid solution, weighted fractions to proportions of BT and BMO in the solid solution are used to calculate the features representing the whole composite, defined by eq 3:

```
$$X_{solid solution} = (1 - x)X_{BT} + xX_{BMO}$$
```

Since the database contains data for the same component under different electric fields, the electric field ($E$) must also be an indispensable feature. Therefore, 22 features obtained through eqs 1–3, along with the $E$, constitute the feature pool for strategy I ($C_i$).

The manufacturing process of ceramics can significantly affect the microstructure of materials, particularly in the case of BT-BMO materials. Due to the relatively low melting point of Bi2O3 (825 °C), improper processing will result in abnormal grain growth and formation of voids at grain boundaries, which can adversely affect the energy storage performance. Thus, the processing parameters of each material are extracted from published papers to expand the feature pool ($P_i$), including calcination temperature (CT), calcination time (CT), sintering temperature (ST), and sintering time (ST). So, the feature pool of strategy III ($P_i + L_i + C_i$) encompasses 31 features. The detailed list of features is shown in Table S1 in the Supporting Information.

When the number of features in a data set is large, the complexity of the model will correspondingly increase, which will affect the efficiency of model training and may also lead to overfitting of the model. A combination of physical knowledge, the Pearson correlation coefficient, and feature importance analysis methods were employed to perform dimensionality reduction on the feature space. The Pearson correlation matrix is utilized to identify the highly correlated features, as shown in Figure 3. The red and blue colors represent negative and positive correlations between two features, with brighter shades denoting higher correlations. Generally, an absolute value of the Pearson correlation coefficient between features exceeding 0.8 is considered to indicate a strong correlation between them. For groups of strongly correlated features, only one feature was retained to avoid redundancy. During the process of feature selection and elimination, we relied on both physical knowledge and the feature importance feedback from the model. Considering that the calcination and sintering times (CT and ST) of BT-BMO ceramics reported in the literature are mostly in the range of 2–4 h with no significant variation, these two features were excluded from the final pool.
