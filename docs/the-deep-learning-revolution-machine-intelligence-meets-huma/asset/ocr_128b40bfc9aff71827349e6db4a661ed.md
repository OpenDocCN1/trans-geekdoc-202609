# The Case against Marvin Minsky

The early history of neural networks is a case study in how a small but influential group can derail the exploration of a competing research direction. Near the end of Perceptrons, Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert (figure 17.5) expressed the opinion that the perceptron learning algorithm could not be extended to multilayer perceptrons:

> The problem of extension is not merely technical. It is also strategic. The perceptron has shown itself worthy of study despite (and even because of!) its severe limitations. It has many features to attract attention: its linearity; its intriguing learning theorem; its clear paradigmatic simplicity as a kind of parallel computation.
