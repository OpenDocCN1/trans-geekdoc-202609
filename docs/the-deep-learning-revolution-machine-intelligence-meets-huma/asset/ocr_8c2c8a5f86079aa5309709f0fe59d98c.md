(Examples of synaptic learning algorithms that have been discovered in the brain will be discussed in later chapters.) Even more remarkable, new synapses are constantly being formed in the cortex and old ones removed, making them among the most dynamic organelles in the body. There are around 100 different types of synapses in the brain, with glutamic acid the most common excitatory neurotransmitter in the cortex and another amino acid, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the most common inhibitory transmitter. There is also a wide range of time courses for the electrochemical influences that these neurotransmitter molecules have on other neurons. For example, the bullfrog sympathetic ganglion cell discussed in chapter 4 has synapses with time scales ranging from milliseconds to minutes.
