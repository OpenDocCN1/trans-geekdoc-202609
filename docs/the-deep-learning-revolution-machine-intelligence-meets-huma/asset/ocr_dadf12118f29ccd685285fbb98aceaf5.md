When I first met Marr at Jackson Hole, he had already moved to MIT,
where he was working on vision and where, as a charismatic figure, he had
attracted talented students to work with him. Pursuing a bottom-up stra-
tegy, he started at the retina, where light is converted to electrical signals,
and asked how signals in the retina encoded the features of objects and
how the visual cortex represented the surfaces and boundaries of objects.
He and Tomaso Poggio (figure 4.2, left) developed an ingenious recurrent
neural network model for stereo vision with feedback connections to detect
the depth of an object from the slight lateral displacements of the images of
dots in the two eyes in random-dot stereograms.⁸ Binocular depth percep-
tion is the basis for how Magic Eye images pop out at you.⁹
