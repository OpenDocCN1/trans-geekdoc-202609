Figure 14.4
Dynamic Vision Sensor (DVS). (Top) Tobias Delbrück holding a DVS camera that he invented at the Institute for Neuroinformatics at the University of Zurich. The camera is a special-purpose chip that emits spikes asynchronously rather than frames like a digital camera. (Bottom) the camera has a lens that focuses images on an analog VLSI chip that detects incremental increases and decreases in light intensity at each pixel. Spikes are emitted along an “on” wire for positive increments and along an “off” wire for negative increments. The output spikes are processed by the circuit board, which displays the spike patterns seen in box 14.1. Your retina is a highly advanced DVS camera. The pattern of spikes from a retina is transformed in the brain but remains a pattern of spikes—there is no image anywhere in your brain even though you perceive the world that way.
