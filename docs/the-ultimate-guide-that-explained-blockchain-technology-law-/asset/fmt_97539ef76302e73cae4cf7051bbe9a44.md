## Introduction

In the digital world, the reality is that we live in a precarious situation and rely on third parties to secure and protect our digital data. These third-party sources, however, are vulnerable to being hacked, altered, or corrupted.

In today's world of instant communication, we're constantly exposed to new ideas and knowledge. Information is abundant, and so are the barriers to entry: You have a specialized background to understand everything. But with blockchain technology, cryptocurrency, and Web 3 (like decentralized apps or DApps) becoming more popular topics, which is often attributed to their simplicity, understanding how these concepts work has never been easier - unless you go with a well-informed GUIDE like the one in front of you.

## Blockchain, Cryptocurrencies, and Web 3 Explained

Blockchain technology is used to create and manage digital records called blocks. Cryptocurrencies are units of value that are created and managed using blockchain technology. Transactions on a blockchain are verified by network nodes through cryptography and recorded in a dispersed public ledger. Web3 is an API built on Ethereum that allows developers to build decentralized applications (DApps).

It could change how the digital world works by making distributed consensus possible. Any online transaction involving digital assets, past or present, can be checked at any time through this great technology. Moreover, it is done without compromising the privacy of the digital assets and the parties involved. 

Distributed consent and anonymity, transparency, and immutability are features of blockchain technology. This guide explains the fundamentals of blockchain technology, including what it is, how it works, its benefits, and how you may use it to improve society.

## Who invented Blockchain

An anonymous person known as Satoshi Nakamoto invented Blockchain who wanted to explore the idea of a "trustless" system that could help simplify online transactions. In 1991, two mathematicians outlined the idea of a blockchain, a digitally decentralized database. Then, in late 1995, Nick Szabo introduced the idea of bit gold, which wasn't implemented until 2008.

## The Brief History of Blockchain

During the 2008 financial crisis leading to the global recession, Satoshi Nakamoto, an unknown person or group, introduced the concept of blockchains to improve financial transactions. The design continued to evolve, with Nakamoto using a Hashcash-like method. Eventually, it became a primary component of Bitcoin, a popular cryptocurrency that serves as a public ledger for all network transactions. As of August 2014, Bitcoin blockchain file sizes had reached 20 gigabytes, then increased greatly and surpassed 200 gigabytes by early 2020.

## Why Blockchain is essential

If you are the recipient or sender of a payment while using online banking, there is a potential and very easy way to tamper with that transaction. Luckily, most people are aware of this and often choose not to use these types of transactions, with the rise of third-party applications in recent years. But this vulnerability is one of the major reasons for Blockchain technology.

Blockchain is a digital ledger that has recently drawn much attention and traction. But why has it become so popular? We'll dig a little deeper to gain insight into the whole concept. The process of creating, storing, and accessing data, as well as transactions, can be full of headaches for businesses. This information is often handled in-house or passed through third parties like brokers, bankers, or lawyers, increasing time and money on the business side. Fortunately, Blockchain makes it easy for the transaction to be done faster by cutting both time and cost for businesses.\*

When people hear the term "blockchain," they often assume it's synonymous with Bitcoin. However, it's not. Blockchain is a technology capable of multiple applications for many industries, this includes finance, supply chain, manufacturing, and more. In addition, Bitcoin is a currency that relies on the power of blockchain technology to maintain a secure system.
