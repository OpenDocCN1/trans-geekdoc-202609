
Banks would benefit the most from using `Blockchain` in their business processes. Financial establishments are only open during regular business hours, usually five days a week. If you deposit a check at 6 p.m. on Friday, you'll likely have to wait until Monday morning for the money to be in your account. Even if the deposit is made during business hours, it can take one to three days for the transaction to be verified due to the large number of transactions that banks must process. 

`Blockchain`, on the other hand, never sleeps. By banks adopting `Blockchain`, customers can process transactions in as little as 10 minutes, which is how long it takes to add a block to the `Blockchain`, whether it's a weekday, a holiday, or the middle of the night. 

`Blockchain` also allows banks to exchange money between institutions faster and more securely. It also improves upon previous methods of international money transfer due to its increased transparency and security

Medical records for patients may be securely stored by healthcare professionals using the `Blockchain`. #### Property Records

If you have ever been to the local land registry office, you know that the property registration process is cumbersome and inefficient. Today, the physical document is given to an official at the local land registry office, which must be entered by hand into the central database and the district public registry. In a dispute, property claims must be verified using public registers.

This process is not only costly and time-consuming, but it is also prone to human error, and any inaccuracies make title tracking less efficient. The `Blockchain` can eliminate the need to scan documents and search for physical records at the local land registry. Once the property is stored and verified on the `Blockchain`, owners can be confident that their titles are accurately and permanently recorded.

## Cryptocurrency

Defining cryptocurrencies is a challenging task. Like `Blockchain`, cryptocurrencies have become a "buzzword" for a wide range of technological developments that use a technique better known as cryptography. Simply put, cryptography is a way to protect information by changing it into a format that is not readable and that can only be deciphered (or decrypted) by someone who knows the secret key. This method uses a complex system of public and private digital keys to protect cryptocurrencies like `bitcoin`. Cryptocurrencies are generally decentralized digital currencies designed for use on the Internet. `Bitcoin` was the first cryptocurrency. It came out in 2008 and is still the biggest, most important. The best-known one in the decade since `bitcoin` and other cryptocurrencies, such as `Ethereum`, have emerged as digital alternatives to government-issued money.

### What is cryptocurrency?

A cryptocurrency is a way to send and receive money online that doesn't use banks to verify transactions. Instead, it is a peer-to-peer payment system that lets anybody, from anywhere, send and receive money. Instead of being physical money carried and exchanged in the real world, cryptocurrency payments are purely digital entries in an online database that describe specific transactions. A public ledger keeps track of every cryptocurrency transfer that takes place. Cryptocurrencies are stored in digital wallets.

### Types of cryptocurrencies

`Bitcoin` is the most popular and valuable cryptocurrency. An anonymous man named `Satoshi Nakamoto` invented and introduced it to the world through a white paper in 2008. There are presently hundreds of cryptocurrencies on the market. Each cryptocurrency claims to have a different function and different specifications. For example, `Ethereum`'s `Ether` goes through the gas of the underlying smart contract platform. Banks use `Ripple`'s `XRP` to facilitate transfers between different geographic areas. `Bitcoin`, made publicly available in 2009, remains the most traded and covered cryptocurrency. As of May 2022, there were more than 19 million `bitcoins` in circulation, with a total market capitalization of about $576 billion. After that, only 21 million `bitcoins` will exist.

After the success of `bitcoin`, many other cryptocurrencies, called "altcoins," were introduced. Some are clones or forks of `bitcoin`, while others are new currencies created from scratch. This list includes `Solana`, `Litecoin`, `Ethereum`, `Cardano`, and `EOS`. As of November 2021, the total value of all cryptocurrencies was more than $2.1 trillion, with `bitcoin` making up about 41% of this total.

### How do cryptocurrencies work?

Cryptocurrencies operate through a distributed public ledger called the `Blockchain`, a register of all transactions updated and managed by the currency holders. Cryptocurrencies are created through "mining," which uses the power of computers to solve complicated mathematical problems and create coins. Users can also buy coins from brokers and then store and spend them in electronic wallets. Those who own cryptocurrencies do not own anything tangible. Instead, one owns a key that allows a record or unit of measure to be transferred from one person to another without using a trusted third party.
