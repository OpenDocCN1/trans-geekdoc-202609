## What is Blockchain

The name "Blockchain" is derived from its structure, i.e., block and chain "-" individual records called blocks are strung together to form the chain. Blockchain is a new technology that will change how people record and share information. It is a global online database that anyone with an internet connection can use anytime,  anywhere.

Blockchain technology is a decentralized, distributed ledger system. It's made up of computers that interact with one another to maintain a continuously growing list of records called blocks. Each block contains a hash pointer as well as transaction information that is connected through cryptographic links. Because every block in the chain is linked to the previous block, it is extremely difficult to change any part without changing all the blocks around it. As a result, blockchain technology is incredibly secure and immune to corruption because it is not altered without everyone on the network noticing.

When I say that the Blockchain is immune, I mean that the Blockchain is a permanent and unalterable network. The distributed computational power across the computers ensures a better outcome!

Like traditional databases, they are not run by a single institution or authority, like a bank or the government. In addition, this technology makes hacking or changing the system almost impossible by making fake transactions, documents, or other information.

### The main features of Blockchain are

Decentralization: No central authority or centralized server is managing the database. Instead, the network comprises thousands of nodes spread across the globe, each holding a copy of the entire database.

#### Immutability

Once data has been written to a blockchain, it cannot be altered or deleted. It ensures that transaction history remains tamper-proof.

#### Transparency

The entire history of a particular asset can be traced back through the Blockchain from its current owner to its first owner. It allows for increased transparency and accountability.

#### Security

Cryptographic techniques ensure that data cannot be tampered with and that transactions are not reversed.

### Types of blockchain networks

#### Public blockchain network

A public blockchain network is a decentralized network that allows anyone to access and participate. A public blockchain is permissionless, meaning anyone can join the network and start validating transactions without needing approval from a central authority. The most popular public Blockchain is the Bitcoin network, launched in 2009. Other examples of public blockchains include Ethereum, Litecoin, and Monero.

"Anyone can read, write, and audit the ongoing activities on a public blockchain network, which helps achieve the self-governed, decentralized nature often touted when blockchain is discussed." The downsides are that it takes a lot of computing power, transaction data isn't always safe, and it's not very secure. These are important considerations for the use of Blockchain in business.

##### Advantages of public Blockchain

There are numerous advantages of public blockchains, such as the fact that anyone can access them and that they are transparent. As a result, it allows for trustless collaboration and auditing and increased security. Public blockchains are more decentralized than private ones, meaning there is no single point of failure. It makes them more resistant to attack and censorship.

#### Private blockchain networks

Private blockchain networks are permissioned, meaning approved members can join and help validate transactions. Businesses often use private blockchains to streamline internal processes and reduce costs. For example, a company might use a private blockchain to track inventory levels or manage employee records.

##### Advantages of private Blockchain

Private blockchains are more scalable than public blockchains. It is because private blockchains can be permissioned, meaning only approved users can access the network. It makes managing large-scale transactions and data on a private blockchain much easier.

Private blockchains are also more secure than public blockchains. Because they are not as decentralized as public blockchains, there are fewer attack points. Additionally, private blockchains can be permissioned so that only approved users can access the network. As a result, it makes it much harder for bad actors to infiltrate the network, steal data, or commit fraud.

It can also be customized to fit an organization's or application's specific needs. Again, it is because they are not as rigid as public blockchains, which means that they can be tailored to meet the specific requirements of a use case. For example, a private blockchain could be created that is only accessible to employees of a company to securely store sensitive data.

##### Private vs. Public Blockchains

The debate between private and public blockchains is among the most important in the blockchain community. On the one hand, private blockchains offer greater security and privacy, while on the other hand, public blockchains offer transparency and openness.

So, which is better? It depends on what you need. If you need a secure and private blockchain, then a private blockchain is the way to go. A public blockchain is a way to go if you need a transparent and open blockchain.

We'll take a closer look at the benefits and drawbacks of each below.

###### Pros:

Greater security: Private blockchains are permissioned, meaning only authorized users can access them. It makes them much more secure than public blockchains.

Greater privacy: Private blockchains are much more private than public ones. Only authorized users can see the data on a private blockchain.

###### Cons:

###### Less decentralized

Private blockchains are usually less decentralized than public blockchains. Again, it is because a single company or organization usually manages them.

###### Less open

Private blockchains are also less open than public blockchains. It means that it's harder for new users to join a private blockchain network.

Overall, private blockchain networks offer several advantages over public ones, including increased security, scalability, and flexibility.

### Permissioned blockchain networks

A permissioned blockchain network is a private network of nodes where a central authority approves each node. Permissioned networks are faster and more scalable than public networks like Bitcoin, but they sacrifice decentralization and security. A permissioned blockchain is either a consortium blockchain or a private blockchain. In a consortium blockchain, the consensus process is controlled by a pre-selected set of nodes; in a private blockchain, the consensus process is controlled by a single entity.

Permissioned blockchains are usually much faster than public blockchains like Bitcoin because they don't need to wait for all nodes in the network to reach consensus before adding new blocks to the chain. They can also be more scalable because there are fewer nodes in the network, and a central authority has vetted each node. However, permissioned blockchains sacrifice decentralization and security for speed and scalability. Because only certain nodes are allowed to add new blocks to the chain, these blockchains are less secure against 51% of attacks and other forms of fraud. And because there is no true decentralized consensus mechanism, these blockchains are also more vulnerable to censorship from their central authorities.

#### Advantages of Permissioned blockchain networks

There are a few advantages of permissioned blockchain networks over public ones. For one, they're more scalable since the number of nodes is capped and known. Secondly, they can be faster since consensus mechanisms don't have to be as decentralized. Finally, they can be more customized to fit the needs of the network participants. 

### Consortium blockchains

Several organizations can share the responsibility of running a blockchain. For example, who May submit transactions or access data determined by these pre-selected parties? "Consortium blockchains are networks in which several entities share the responsibility of managing, accessing, and authorization of ………." Therefore, a blockchain consortium is perfect for businesses where all participants must be authorized and share responsibility for the Blockchain.

#### Advantages of Consortium blockchains

A consortium blockchain is a private blockchain network where only certain pre-approved nodes can participate in the consensus process. It gives consortium blockchains many of the same advantages as private blockchains, such as improved security, privacy, and scalability. However, consortium blockchains also have some unique advantages.

For example, consortium blockchains can create shared databases between organizations. It can be useful for supply chain management or other areas where multiple parties need to share data. Consortium blockchains can also create shared services, such as identity management or payment processing. By pooling resources and sharing the cost of development and maintenance, consortium blockchains can provide these services more efficiently than each organization could.

Another advantage of consortium blockchains is that they can help manage regulatory compliance. For example, a group of financial institutions could use a consortium blockchain to share information about suspicious activity without violating anti-money laundering regulations. By keeping data on a private blockchain network, only authorized users would have access to it. And by using smart contracts, the data could be automatically encrypted and only shared with the relevant regulators when certain conditions are met.

Consortium blockchains also offer the benefit of flexibility. Because the same consensus rules do not bind them as public blockchain networks, they can be customized to meet the needs of their members. For example, a group of banks could agree to use a different consensus algorithm than a group of insurance companies.

A cryptocurrency is a way to send and receive money online that doesn't use banks to verify transactions. Instead, it is a peer-to-peer payment system that lets anybody, from anywhere, send and receive money. Instead of being physical money carried and exchanged in the real world, cryptocurrency payments are purely digital entries in an online database that describe specific transactions. A public ledger keeps track of every cryptocurrency transfer that takes place. Cryptocurrencies are stored in digital wallets.

### How do cryptocurrencies work?

## Web 3.0

The World Wide Web (WWW) has been used for about 20 years to improve communication, collaboration, and sharing of resources, encourage active learning and offer distance education. In recent years, many universities and educational institutions have offered online services such as admissions and virtual (online) learning environments to facilitate lifelong learning and make them compatible with other education management activities. ### What is Web 3.0?

Web 3.0 (Web3) is the third generation of web technologies that are constantly evolving. The Web, also known as the World Wide Web, is the fundamental layer for using the Internet and provides website services and applications. Since Web 3.0 is still in the development and definition phase, there is no canonical and universally accepted definition. Web 3.0 will, however, undoubtedly place a strong emphasis on decentralized apps and heavily utilize blockchain-based technology. Additionally, Web 3.0 will use AI and machine learning to allow smarter and more adaptable apps.

### Key ideas for Web 3.0

Although it is difficult to give a rigid definition of Web3, some basic principles guide its development.

Web3 is decentralized: Instead of large portions of the Internet being controlled and owned by centralized institutions, ownership is shared between creators and users.

Everyone has equal access to Web3 and is not barred because it is permissionless.

Web3 offers native payments, which do away with the antiquated infrastructure of banks and payment processors in favor of using cryptocurrencies for online purchases and payments.

Web3 is not trustworthy: it operates through economic incentives and mechanisms rather than relying on trusted third parties.

### How does Web 3.0 work?

In Web 1.0 and Web 2.0 technologies, Hypertext Markup Language (HTML) governs the design and presentation of Web pages. In Web 3.0, HTML will remain a fundamental layer, but how data sources are linked and where they are located may differ from previous Web generations. Many Web pages and almost all applications from the Web 2.0 era rely on some central database to provide data and enable functionality. Web 3.0 applications and services use a decentralized blockchain instead of a centralized database. In the Blockchain, the basic idea is that there is no arbitrary central authority but a form of distributed consensus.

An emerging governance ideal in the blockchain and Web 3.0 communities is the concept of a decentralized autonomous organization (DAO). Instead of a central authority governing the operation of a platform, Web 3.0 technologies and communities offer a form of self-governance through a decentralized approach. Web 3.0 also operates fundamentally with cryptocurrencies rather than fiat currencies. Finance and the ability to pay for goods and services with a decentralized form of payment are enabled in Web 3.0 by using cryptocurrencies based on blockchain technology. The IPv4 address space was the foundation for Web versions 1.0 and 2.0. Due to the massive growth of the Internet over the decades, Web 3.0 requires a larger number of Internet addresses, and IPv6 provides just that.

### Web3 concepts - the DAO

The Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) is a Web3 concept that describes a group, business, or collective bound by rules and standards encoded on a blockchain. In a DAO-based business, for example, the prices of all items and the details of who receives payments from the business would be stored on a blockchain. The DAO's shareholders could decide whether to change the prices or who gets the money.

Nobody could, however, alter the principles without authorization. And none of the owners of the physical infrastructure, such as the owners of the servers or facilities where the profits are stored, could intervene in any way, for example, by running away with the profits! In theory, DAOs eliminate the need for many of the "middlemen" required to run a business, such as bankers, lawyers, accountants, and owners.

Most believe that artificial intelligence will play an important role in Web 3 because of the machine-to-machine communication and decision-making required by many Web 3 applications.
