
The debate between `private` and `public` blockchains is among the most important in the blockchain community. On the one hand, `private` blockchains offer greater security and privacy, while on the other hand, `public` blockchains offer transparency and openness.

So, which is better? It depends on what you need. If you need a secure and private blockchain, then a `private` blockchain is the way to go. A `public` blockchain is a way to go if you need a transparent and open blockchain.

We'll take a closer look at the benefits and drawbacks of each below.

###### Pros:

Greater security: `private` blockchains are permissioned, meaning only authorized users can access them. It makes them much more secure than `public` blockchains.

Greater privacy: `private` blockchains are much more private than `public` ones. Only authorized users can see the data on a `private` blockchain.

###### Cons:

###### Less decentralized

`private` blockchains are usually less decentralized than `public` blockchains. Again, it is because a single company or organization usually manages them.

###### Less open

`private` blockchains are also less open than `public` blockchains. It means that it's harder for new users to join a `private` blockchain network.

Overall, `private` blockchain networks offer several advantages over `public` ones, including increased security, scalability, and flexibility.

### Permissioned blockchain networks

A `permissioned` blockchain network is a private network of nodes where a central authority approves each node. `Permissioned` networks are faster and more scalable than `public` networks like Bitcoin, but they sacrifice decentralization and security. A `permissioned` blockchain is either a `consortium` blockchain or a `private` blockchain. In a `consortium` blockchain, the consensus process is controlled by a pre-selected set of nodes; in a `private` blockchain, the consensus process is controlled by a single entity.

`Permissioned` blockchains are usually much faster than `public` blockchains like Bitcoin because they don't need to wait for all nodes in the network to reach consensus before adding new blocks to the chain. They can also be more scalable because there are fewer nodes in the network, and a central authority has vetted each node. However, `permissioned` blockchains sacrifice decentralization and security for speed and scalability. Because only certain nodes are allowed to add new blocks to the chain, these blockchains are less secure against 51% of attacks and other forms of fraud. And because there is no true decentralized consensus mechanism, these blockchains are also more vulnerable to censorship from their central authorities.

#### Advantages of Permissioned blockchain networks

There are a few advantages of `permissioned` blockchain networks over `public` ones. For one, they're more scalable since the number of nodes is capped and known. Secondly, they can be faster since consensus mechanisms don't have to be as decentralized. Finally, they can be more customized to fit the needs of the network participants.

### Consortium blockchains

Several organizations can share the responsibility of running a blockchain. For example, who May submit transactions or access data determined by these pre-selected parties? "Consortium blockchains are networks in which several entities share the responsibility of managing, accessing, and authorization of ………." Therefore, a blockchain consortium is perfect for businesses where all participants must be authorized and share responsibility for the Blockchain.

#### Advantages of Consortium blockchains

A `consortium` blockchain is a `private` blockchain network where only certain pre-approved nodes can participate in the consensus process. It gives `consortium` blockchains many of the same advantages as `private` blockchains, such as improved security, privacy, and scalability. However, `consortium` blockchains also have some unique advantages.

For example, `consortium` blockchains can create shared databases between organizations. It can be useful for supply chain management or other areas where multiple parties need to share data. `Consortium` blockchains can also create shared services, such as identity management or payment processing. By pooling resources and sharing the cost of development and maintenance, `consortium` blockchains can provide these services more efficiently than each organization could.

Another advantage of `consortium` blockchains is that they can help manage regulatory compliance. For example, a group of financial institutions could use a `consortium` blockchain to share information about suspicious activity without violating anti-money laundering regulations. By keeping data on a `private` blockchain network, only authorized users would have access to it. And by using smart contracts, the data could be automatically encrypted and only shared with the relevant regulators when certain conditions are met.

`Consortium` blockchains also offer the benefit of flexibility. Because the same consensus rules do not bind them as `public` blockchain networks, they can be customized to meet the needs of their members. For example, a group of banks could agree to use a different consensus algorithm than a group of insurance companies.

## How does blockchain technology work?

You may have observed that many businesses have recently adopted blockchain technology. But how exactly does blockchain technology work? Is it a major change or just an add-on? Blockchain, a young technology, is still undergoing lots of developments, and many new projects are launching on the network, so let's start by demystifying this technology.

Blockchain is the result of the convergence of three cutting-edge features:

*   `Cryptographic keys`

*   A `peer-to-peer` network that includes a shared ledger.

*   A computing device that stores transactions and records from the network.

#### "Cryptographic Keys"

`Cryptographic keys` consist of two keys, `private` and `public` "keys." These keys facilitate successful transactions between the two parties. Each person has these two keys, which allow them to create a secure digital identity.

#### "Digital Identification and Security"

`Digital identification` and security are becoming increasingly important in our digital world. Blockchain technology can help to provide a secure and decentralized way to store and manage digital identities.

Blockchain-based digital identity systems could solve many of the problems associated with current centralized systems, such as the need for a trusted third party and data centralization. With Blockchain, each user would have control over their data, and it is stored in a decentralized database that is secure and tamper-resistant. As a result, digital identity systems based on Blockchain verify the authenticity of documents, contracts, or other online transactions. In addition, it would add an extra layer of security and trust, as the data would be stored on the Blockchain and could not be altered without consensus from all parties involved.

In addition to digital identity management, blockchain technology can create tamper-proof digital signatures. As a result, it could have applications in areas like document verification, copyright protection, or even vote casting. With digital signatures based on Blockchain, it would be impossible to forge or change a signature without detection.

Blockchain technology offers a new way to think about digital identity and security. Using a decentralized database and cryptographic techniques makes it possible to create secure systems resistant to fraud and tampering. These systems could have wide applications in the public and private sectors.
