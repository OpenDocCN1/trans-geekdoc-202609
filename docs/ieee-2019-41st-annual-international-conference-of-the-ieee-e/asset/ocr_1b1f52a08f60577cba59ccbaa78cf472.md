# B. EFFECT OF DICTIONARY LEARNING

We analyze the results of the proposed deep dictionary learning and the effect of increasing the number of layers. The objective of this experiment is to show that the representations learned from a single level of dictionary and multi-level dictionaries different and yield better classification performance. For example, with just one level, we learn 50 atoms for MNIST; 625 atoms for the bg-newgroup and 148 for GTZAN; compared to the last level of deep learning, we performed experiments with up to three levels. For the MNIST database and its variants, the number of atoms in the multi-level dictionaries: 300-150.5. For the 20-newgroup and the GTZAN (music database), the number of atoms varies from the previous layer. The classifier is performed with a simple K-Nearest Neighbor (K=1). Column 2 shows the results for shallow dictionary and Column 3 shows the results with different levels of deep learning. The results show that for all the databases, deep learning offers improvements over shallow dictionary learning. The improvement in accuracy is possibly owing to more abstract representation learned from layers. Depending on the complexity of the dataset, the difference in performance varies from 0.4% to 9%. Table 2 indicates the first layer dictionary on MNIST database.

Generally, the atoms are initialized by random choices from the training set; this leads to variability. In this research, we propose a deterministic initialization based on the QR decomposition of the dictionary matrix. Orthogonal factors from Q (in order) are used to initialize the dictionary.

We show that multi-level dictionaries cannot be collapsed into a single one and should not be expected to yield the results.
