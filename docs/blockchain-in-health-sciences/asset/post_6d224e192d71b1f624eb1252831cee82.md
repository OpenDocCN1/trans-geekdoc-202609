## 15.3 Blockchain Networks

### 15.3.1 Blockchain Technology

Data produced by established criteria form a continuing succession of blocks in a blockchain. Separate and unconnected versions of the blockchain are usually stored on many computers [9, 10]. A Blockchain network consists of interconnected computing devices that employ a protocol to record transactions and add new blocks to the chain [11]. In this way, the Blockchain network acts as a decentralized database that documents every transaction that has ever taken place. Additionally, it operates under a predefined protocol that specifies the rules for conducting and validating transactions and the activities of individuals on the network [12]. Due the fact that every node in the network stores information about every transaction, this network is also called a distributed registry from time to time. The three main varieties of blockchain technology are:

- 1. Public Blockchain
- 2. Consortium Blockchain
- 3. Private Blockchain

Participation in the consensus-building process and visibility into all network transactions are both possible by the public blockchain. Instead of relying on a central administrator node to validate transactions, the public blockchain uses participant consensus to guarantee the authenticity of all transactions [13, 14]. Among the most famous instances of such networks are Bitcoin and Ethereum. With a permissionless and open public blockchain, anyone with an internet connection may sign up to a trusted node on the network. To verify transactions before adding them to the ledger, this user must undertake mining operations, which include complex computations. They have access to both historical and current data [15].

The consortium blockchain has an administrative node. In the event of a partnership, for example, the members of the network vote on it based on how it would help them reach their own business goals. Since the data stored in such networks might be either publicly or privately accessible, they can be seen as having a degree of decentralization.

Examples of such networks include the Hyperledger platform. Consortium blockchains, often called federated blockchains, provide an innovative alternative for companies who want a mix of public and private blockchain features. With a consortium blockchain, certain company information may be made public while others remains private [16].

A public blockchain differs from a private one in one respect. No information in such a registry is accessible to the general public. The administration node ensures that only members with authorization access blockchain data. Multichain and Hyperledger are two systems that may be used to construct such solutions. A blockchain network functions inside the confines of administrative control or in a limited environment, such as closed network, is known as a private blockchain. Although much smaller in scale, this blockchain type is functionally identical to a public network in terms of decentralization and peer-to-peer communication. Medical and drug regulatory bodies have also found low-quality and counterfeit products on the market. Following incidents of wrongdoing and supply chain breakdown, some pharmaceutical companies have sought to use blockchain technology to streamline operations, improve patient safety, and speed up medical transactions [1].

### 15.3.2 Blockchain Applications in the Medical Field

There are several applications for blockchain technology in the pharmaceutical industry. Blocks of transactional data are validated using cryptographic techniques, which solve major security problems [17, 18]. Drug tracking has also been enhanced to fight them. Ensuring constant quality from manufacturer to pharmacy is achieved via the use of data miners, blockchain chain codes, and digital signatures for quality control (Figure 15.2) [19]. It is common practice to seek multiple ingredients for medicine before finalizing its formulation. Once the perfect formula has been found, the medicine may be administered. The medicine will pass through many hands as it moves through the supply chain, but the two main ones are the manufacturer and the patient [8].

### 15.3.4 Product Distribution

There is a greater potential for dishonesty and a decrease in the supply chain’s efficacy when there are more dealers and intermediaries. One of the key advantages of blockchain is its potential to halt the spread of counterfeit drugs [22]. When it comes to distribution-related problems, which as the dissemination of illegal or fake pharmaceuticals, blockchain technology is an excellent match [23]. Serialization allows for the reconstruction of the product's history and packaging hierarchy [24]. In one model, four nodes were: The Food and Drug Administration, the producer, the wholesaler, and the store [25].

The responsibility for ensuring the manufacturer's pharmaceutical inventory is prepared for distribution to wholesalers lies with the manufacturer within the supply chain. Distributors and wholesalers keep the manufactured items when they get orders from manufacturers. Distributors will disclose inventory data to manufacturers to maintain transparency throughout the process. The role of wholesalers is to simplify and quick the acquisition of pharmaceutical prescriptions. Distributors connect and ship to thousands of pharmacies and dispensaries every. Instead of shipping pharmaceuticals to pharmacies one by one, manufacturers may distribute large batches of medications to a relatively small number of wholesalers. The wholesaler offers a range of services, including repackaging, computerized order processing, and medication delivery, after they have ownership of the goods [8].

### 15.3.5 Tracking and Tracing

Commodities in transportation should be monitored and traced from the point of dispatch to the destination. This makes perfect sense. Delivery delays are a headache for companies everywhere, but they can be particularly deadly in the healthcare and pharmaceutical industries. Blockchain technology is now being used by pharmaceutical supply systems [26]. Blockchain technology has the opportunity to revolutionize pharmaceutical traceability. This design not only promotes customer confidence but also allows for transparent medication monitoring, privacy for users, quality control, non-repudiation, and demand-supply management [27]. An extensive quantitative study was carried out in India to elucidate key aspects of blockchain technology, such as roles, credibility, and ability to monitor medicinal data [28]. Internet of Things (IoT) devices have also been used to assess the quality of pharmaceutical items and confirm the reliability of data sources, regardless of time or place. As an intermediary for data storage and sharing, blockchain guarantees transparency and traceability. Thanks to blockchain-based track and trace; important stakeholders in the supply chain may examine vital information on the date, location, and storage techniques of products.
