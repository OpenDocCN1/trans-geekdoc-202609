## 4.6 Challenges

Notwithstanding the possible advantages, several issues need to be resolved when combining blockchain technology and the Health Belief Model to encourage young people to take enough vitamin D. They involve challenges with scalability in technology, permission, and information ownership regarding ethics. Subsequent investigations should focus on obstacles within multidisciplinary partnerships comprising specialists in digital currencies, health behavior studies, and youth involvement. Furthermore, empirical research is required to assess the viability and efficacy of blockchain-enhanced therapies in practical contexts.

## 4.7 Discussion

Sufficient literature demonstrates the worldwide prevalence of VDD. In India, the prevalence is alarming. Recent studies point to a 70% prevalence after determining demographic and environmental variables. With youth comprising a large percentage of the Indian Population (the census year 2022), course correction becomes critical for India to lower its future public health burden.

As long-term behavioral patterning happens at a crucial young age, it is essential to understand the health problems of the youth population and identify interventions that protect their health. Understanding the belief systems of Youth regarding Vitamin D sufficiency can help formulate interventions to change their Vitamin D intake behavior. HBM became the most utilized conceptual health belief model of all the theories and models. The HBM, which applies to various health conditions, promises to understand the underlying health behaviors. It also promises to understand the most likely interventions the Population may or may not engage with. Interventions are crucial to turn the tide against a very manageable micronutrient deficiency.

Improving human health and wellbeing through health promotion is also the aim of Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 3), coordinated by the United Nations. An improved standard of living is possible, if theory—and evidence-based health interventions are employed for health promotion. For these interventions to have their desired outcomes (minus the unwanted negative consequences), it’s essential to understand the underlying human behaviors.

During the literature review, there was an apparent research gap when searching for the HBM and VDD in the Indian Youth. There were articles on women in pregnancy or specific populations where Vitamin D was also part of the questionnaire but not the focus. So, this topic has been studied fragmentedly but has yet to have a specific focus. The closest article regarding the impact of an HBM-based course of action on students’ use of dietary vitamin D supplementation female students in a city in Iran [38].

Most recent articles on HBM in India are in the context of COVID-19. In previous years, beginning in 1990, the HBM in India has been applied to many diverse health conditions for lifestyle modifications. However, this model has never been used for Indian lifestyle in VDD. India is a land of sunshine, and in the absence of knowledge about the consequences of VDD among Youth, a whole generation may be exposed to consequent disease conditions.
