In addition, blockchain technology has evolved in three stages: the first phase includes cryptocurrencies, the second phase is focused on smart contracts in various industries, and the third phase involves the use of blockchain in non-financial sectors such as health, government, and culture. This technology can potentially solve significant difficulties in the health sector, including public health management and automated claim authentication. This technology enables patients to keep their data and choose who can access it, resolving data sharing and ownership difficulties [24–26]. Furthermore, it enables authorities to integrate, alert, and retrieve data via consensus protocols. Also, blockchain technology can improve the data management process by reducing human error and the possibility of misusing or mishandling recorded information.
