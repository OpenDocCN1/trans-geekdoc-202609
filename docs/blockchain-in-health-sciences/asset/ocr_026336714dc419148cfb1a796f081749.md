The cognitive design science research cycle is depicted in Figure 10.5, highlighting the cognitive processes involved in the cycle's steps. Takeda et al. The triangle illustrates how these approaches generate new kinds of knowledge throughout the stages of design science research. In summary, this approach is an organized and iterative problem-solving method that demonstrates how creativity and knowledge can emerge across various fields.

We have assumed the design science research approach in this study, which focuses on performance improvement and problem-solving in the pharmaceutical supply chain business, or “Improvement Research” [33]. The main problem in this sector is the lack of transparency, which has contributed to the idea of creating a transparent, decentralized, and tamper-proof solution. We derived potential solutions through abductive reasoning from existing knowledge and theories relevant to the problem domain [34]. Figure 10.5 illustrates how deduction is the process of reasoning from premises to a certain conclusion, whereas abduction is the process of deducing a likely answer from observations. In the process of creating knowledge, reflection and abstraction are essential.
