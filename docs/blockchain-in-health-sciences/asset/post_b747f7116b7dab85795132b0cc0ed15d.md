## 21.3.1 Importance of Traceability in Pharmaceuticals

The way blockchain technology is applied in the healthcare industry is similar to how it does in other industries. The blockchain serves as a shared ledger that contains transaction records [74, 75]. These transactions are immutable, which means there is no way to change or modify them. Most importantly, no central authority is needed [76]. It is not necessary for members of the blockchain network to be acquainted with one another before refer [77]. For example, even if the insurer had never before interacted with the hospital, it could still access a patient's medical data that were kept in the EMR system with the patient's agreement [78]. The primary need is that the blockchain must first authenticate the hospital and the insurance [79, 80]. A company's existence might be jeopardized by a possible failure, but innovation also carries a risk that can provide significant benefits for developments and shape the pharmaceutical sector [81]. Pharmaceutical firms have to constantly adjust to new discoveries that have a significant impact on millions of lives as the company's bottom line [82]. Using innovation is crucial to accelerate growth and producing measurable results [83]. Companies in the pharmaceutical industry may increase their efforts in drug development and improve returns on investment through efficient innovation management [84]. The most important components in the pharmaceutical industry are traceability which refers to the capacity to track the origin, location and application of a drug or product through documented identification [85]. It comprises putting in place a system that makes it possible to accurately thoroughly monitor materials, processes and products [86].

In the pharmaceutical industry, traceability is crucial for guaranteeing the efficacy, safety and quality of goods [87]. It makes it easier to find and take potentially dangerous products off the market which helps to prevent recalls [88, 89]. The traceability is essential to effective supply chain management as it ensures accurate and on-time product delivery. Additionally, it helps in complying with industry norms and regulatory regulations [90, 91]. The pharmaceutical industry uses a range of traceability methods. The popular technique is barcode technology, which tracks materials and items using barcodes [92]. Radio-frequency identification (RFID) is another widely used solution that transfers data between a tag and a reader via waves [93]. A further approach that gives each product distinct identification serialization [94]. Also, goods and resources are tracked using blockchain technology which makes use of a distributed ledger [95, 96].

## 21.5 Intellectual Property Protection in Health Sector: Blockchain Implementation

With the integration of many stakeholders, the use of blockchain technology to intellectual property (IP) rights management provides a way to reduce the gap between open and closed innovation [111, 112]. In addition to offering a fresh technological approach, blockchain's distributed ledger system offers a chance to rebalance the power relationships between innovators and middlemen, fostering innovation in business [113]. The shift from information sharing to disintermediation and ultimately open innovation for the good of society has the potential to completely change how healthcare issues are approached [114]. It suggests a blockchain-based smart contract-supported architecture to carry out this goal [115]. This platform automatically recognizes contributions of IP sales, royalty fees, and licensing and sublicensing initiatives [116]. With retaining IP protection for multidisciplinary specialists and streamlining agreements between licensees and licensors without laborious negotiation procedures, this IP sharing mechanism encourages inventors to share their ideas while cutting down on transaction costs and time [117].

### 21.5.1 Importance of Intellectual Property in Pharma

The pharmaceutical sector is significantly impacted by intellectual property (IPR) in a number of areas, including drug research, discovery, pricing, distribution, competitive analysis and the availability and affordability of new drugs. Increased intellectual property rights (IPR) protection in developed nations has fueled the pharmaceutical industry's explosive growth [118, 119]. Developing countries frequently attack the patent system, pointing to worries about monopolies in the market and inflated medicine costs [120]. Blockchain technology provides a way to record and validate ownership of creative works in a decentralized, transparent, and tamper-proof platform, protecting intellectual property. Blockchain creates unquestionable proof of creation and ownership with features like smart contracts and immutable ledgers [120, 121]. With facilitating quick and global intellectual property registration, it reduces the possibility of conflicts or unapproved use [122].
