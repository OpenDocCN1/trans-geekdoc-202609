## 12.3.2 Data Collection and General Operational Phase of Each Component

Data in a SCM system can be collected in various ways. A huge volume of work has been carried out by using sensing technologies, such as IoT devices in the form of RFID, Near Field Communication (NFC) for collecting data from the physical world [13]. These data can be converted into digital information and added as a part of blockchain. In [25], the authors have provided an overall blockchain-based system structure as shown in Figure 12.5. In [14], the authors have shown a decentralized IoT architecture that integrates blockchain technology with IoT system. In [26], the authors have proposed a local blockchain network-based architecture where the implementation is based on smart contracts for building a secure, transparent, and traceable PSC management system.

In blockchain record keeping, unique identifiers, known assigned to assets or transaction flow valuables, as units of stock products, orders placements and execution, loan payment and disbursement, return credit, and bills of shipment [27]. Conceptually, these unique identifiers are similar to those associated with coins in cryptocurrency, such as bitcoin. In addition, participants in the blockchain, such as stakeholders, distributors or other agents in pharmaceutical SCM or general SCM generate unique identifiers or digital signatures that can be used to authenticate the blocks, added to the blockchain. When tokens are transferred between two users as a part of a transaction authentication proof, individual steps involved in the transaction process is recorded in the blockchain. The blockchain gains importance due to the chronological ordering of blocks, which combines all the three flows in the transactions and records details which is lacking in financial ledger systems.

In addition, each of these blocks are encrypted and shared with all participants, each of whom maintains a copy of the blockchain ledger. With all these features, a blockchain technology platform provides trustworthy, complete, and tamper-proof auditing trail for various activities in the supply chain. Any issues that exist in centralized system, such as execution, traceability, and coordination problems, are greatly reduced in blockchain-based system. The nodes possess individual updated version of the blockchain ledger, each user can review transaction status, detect errors,

## 12.3.3 Technical Challenges in Blockchain for PCM

Pharmaceutical supply chain raises various issues that are highly dependent on manufacturing environments, such as mix-ups, adulteration, contamination, cross-contamination, spurious, frequent failure, human error, intended fraud, and others [7, 13]. Apart from these factors, many other technical challenges may hinder in designing and implementing SCM systems using blockchain technologies. We analyze few technical challenges in the following sections.

### Scalability

Even in a small pharmaceutical supply chain system, a large number of stakeholders participate. If a large-scale PSC system involves crosscountry flow of pharmaceutical drugs and medical equipment, the involvement of stakeholders extends and cross the barrier of geographical localization. Each of these systems, a massive flow of newly created and time-sensitive data/information is generated. Time-sensitive data involves the pharmaceutical product manufacturing and expiry dates, composition, maintainability of such conditions such as temperature, humidity, demand specific stock availability, and removal of out-of-demand stock among others. The primary challenge to implement blockchain is that it works in decentralized mechanism, and therefore how the architecture can scale up and operate with the increasing stakeholder count, which is often associated with a large volume of generated transactional data.

Blockchain deals with network scalability by data immutability in the form of blocks that are appended to the blockchain. A new block is appended only after other participants verify and allow for adding that block following consensus model as we have discussed in Section 12.3.1. updating and performing changes in existing technology may be computationally difficult in blockchain due to many factors [24]. In permissionless blockchain networks that comprise many users, resolving this scalability issue is extremely hard due to its nature of distribution around the world, and governance by the consensus of the users. Modification of existing blockchain network's protocol, algorithms and data structures can be handled using forks. Forks in blockchain technology can be divided into the categories of hard forks and soft forks.

A soft fork is a modification to a blockchain implementation that is backward compatible with the nodes that have not been updated. If no or very few nodes follow the upgradation, updated rules are not followed in general. A hard fork is a modification to a blockchain implementation that is not backward compatible with the nodes that have not been updated [23, 24]. In general, all publishing nodes at a specific block number will require to be switched for the newly updated protocol. In addition, all nodes need to upgrade and modify to this new protocol, wherein finely formatted blocks with publishing requests are not getting rejected by them. Non-updated nodes can also allow transaction operation on blocks of non-updated node published blocks that follow old format. Similarly, non-updated nodes cannot allow doing transaction operation on newly formatted blocks from updated nodes.

### Integrating with IoT

Integrating decentralized IoT system structure with blockchain can impose various challenges as the real-world data may be collected in various forms. These data are converted into blockchain comparable digital data and included in blockchain. The conversion may take time and security breaches may occur during this end. Therefore, inconsistent time-lagged data are available in the blockchain. Blockchains involve computationally expensive operations and suffer from large bandwidth delay and resource overhead, which may not be suitable for IoT device-based system architectures [14]. Moreover, the end nodes of IoT architecture need a cryptographic functionality as it will be well-integrated with blockchain implementation.

### Quantum Computing

With the advancement of quantum cryptography, if a powerful commercial quantum computer turns into reality, the cryptographic primitives used in present day blockchain technologies for public key cryptography (PKC) operations will require replacement [24]. The PKC algorithms whose security are based on the computational hardness of integer factorization, such as RSA, or involved in solving discrete logarithm problems, such as Diffie-Hellman, the key exchange algorithm will be susceptible to attacks utilizing quantum computing.
