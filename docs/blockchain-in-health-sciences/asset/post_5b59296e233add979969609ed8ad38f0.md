## 9.4 Working With Distributed Ledger Technology and Blockchain

The five steps that make up the multistep process used by blockchain are as follows:

1. A transaction is entered by an authorized participant, and the technology needs to verify this.
2. A block representing that particular transaction or set of data is created as a result of that action.
3. Every networked computer node receives the block.
4. Transactions are verified by authorized nodes, which then append the block to the current blockchain.
5. The transaction is completed when the update is disseminated throughout the network.

These actions contain several components and happen almost instantly. In public blockchain networks, nodes are known as miners. They are usually compensated for their efforts in the form of cryptocurrency using procedures known as proof of work or proof of stake [3, 14]. Blocks and individual transactions are the two kinds of records that make up a blockchain ledger. ## 9.6 Blockchain Examples and Use Cases

Blockchain continues to evolve and gain acceptability as more businesses in many industries learn to use it. Blockchain’s use cases and industry applications have expanded far beyond its initial Bitcoin application to encompass smart contracts, cybersecurity, the Internet of things (IoT), and non-fungible tokens (NFTs). NFTs are digital assets that represent all or part of real-world artifacts like art or music. They are a popular way to purchase and sell digital artwork because they can be bought, sold and swapped online [15].

Here are some of the real-world applications of blockchain technology:

Supply chain management: Blockchain’s end-to-end visibility, transparency, and accountability make it useful for controlling supply chains. Stakeholders can record, track, and authenticate products, stop counterfeit goods from entering the supply chain, and improve logistics procedures.

![](img/fea41dfefcdce94be900e86661d80689_209_0.png)

(a) Healthcare: Blockchain, like artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things, has emerged as an advanced healthcare technology. In healthcare, blockchain is used to securely store and share patient data. The technology provides patients control over their medical records, allowing healthcare-providers to access them just as needed. This enables the seamless and secure sharing of medical information, leading to better treatment outcomes and lower administrative hassles.

(b)Identity management: Blockchain-based identity management systems improve security, privacy, and control over personal information. By putting identifying information on the blockchain, users can create a portable and verifiable digital identity. This removes the need for several identity documents, lowers identity theft, and streamlines the identity verification procedure [16].

(c)Voting systems: Blockchain technology can help traditional voting systems by creating safe and transparent voting platforms. Voting systems based on technology prevent voter fraud, preserve electoral integrity, and allow for remote voting while ensuring anonymity and privacy.

(d)Finance and banking: Blockchain technology is used in financial services to accelerate transactions and closing times. For example, PayPal, an online payment network, released a blockchain-based service in 2020 that allows customers to buy, hold, and sell cryptocurrencies. R3, a global coalition of financial institutions, created the Corda platform to record, manage, and synchronize financial data using blockchain application interfaces for specialized platforms [17].

## 9.7 Conclusion

This chapter aims to investigate blockchain technology. It is clear that its influence goes well beyond its beginnings in Bitcoin. With its ability to revolutionize voting systems, enable decentralized banking, and improve supply chain transparency, blockchain has proven its adaptability and potential.
