# Abstract

Human genomic data possess distinctive information about an individual and present unparalleled prospects for healthcare. Genomics research has made remarkable advancements, offering unparalleled understanding of human genetic variation, vulnerability to diseases, and the categorization of risk. By providing clinical interpretations, large genomic databases may greatly improve healthcare and pave the way for the creation of individualized medicines. Projections suggest a substantial rise in the production of genomic data in the next years. Together, with the rapid progress in science, will give rise to novel social, ethical, and legal dilemmas. Sharing genomic datasets presents significant obstacles to the unique nature of genomic data. This type of data might greatly indirectly disclose information about the owner's offspring and relations, and it can retain valid information long after the owner's death. Consequently, strict rules for data ownership must be set up and strictly enforced when dealing with genomic information. In this intricate and interdependent setting, the transfer of genomic data gives rise to questions of responsibility, authenticity, and openness: blockchain technology offers a novel and innovative way to address these concerns. Enhanced personal control over the use and disclosure of genomic data is possible with the use of distributed ledger technology in conjunction with a database. With this tool, people can choose who gets their data and how they are used, giving them more agency over their privacy. To overcome the many challenges that come with sharing genetic data, lawmakers and data owners must work together
