According to predictions from the World Health Organization (WHO), up to 2 billion people globally lack access to essential drugs, medical supplies, and vaccines, which often leads to the entry of inferior goods. Because of the rising demand for pharmaceuticals, the supply chain will include out-of-date and counterfeit drugs. The issue of subpar pharmaceuticals is compounded by the advent of e-commerce, which makes it easier to buy from unreliable sources, and the complexity of global supply chains, which allows products made in one nation to be packed and sold in another [17]. The production, delivery, and sale of medications to all stakeholders in the supply chain remain opaque. Because transactional procedures are verified using either localized or centralized methods, they can be manipulated [18].
