Permissionless blockchain networks, popularly known as decentralized ledger platforms, are open to all participating nodes for publishing blocks without needing permission from any authority. On the contrary, permitted blockchain networks which are either centralized or decentralized ledger platforms publishes blocks that must be authorized by certain authority depending on centralized or decentralized consensus algorithm.
The key aspect of determining which user will publish the next block within a blockchain system depends on various consensus models used for blockchain technology are proof-of-work (PoW) model, proof-of-state (PoS) model, such as chain-based proof-of-state, proof-of-authority/proof-of-identity consensus model, and proof-of-elapsed-time (PoET) consensus model. In the NIST 8202 document [24], the authors demonstrate a consensus matrix containing all the above-mentioned consensus models and analyze their goals, advantages and disadvantages, domains of the models, and various blockchain technologies evolved based on that configuration till October, 2018.
