Security in the supply chain has lately come to the forefront due to the passage of the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCA) in the US. One of the main reasons the Act was put into action was to address the problem of fake medications. Contraband medication poses a health risk to patients since it lacks the necessary active ingredients. Distributing, wholesaling, and manufacturing are all links in the intricate pharmaceutical supply chain that ultimately leads to the patient (Figure 15.1). Authenticity may be tracked by manufacturers with minimal visibility in the supply chain [7].

In the drugstore, retail, and manufacturing industries, manufacturers are responsible for ensuring their pharmaceutical inventory is prepared for delivery to wholesalers as part of the supply chain. After the wholesalers and distributors will provide their orders, manufacturers arrange the goods for safekeeping in their warehouses. To ensure complete transparency, including inventory data. The goal of the wholesalers is to make purchasing prescription drugs easier and faster. Manufacturers work with the wholesalers who supply thousands of pharmacies and dispensers. Instead of shipping pharmaceuticals to pharmacies one by one, manufacturers may send large quantities to a relatively small number of wholesalers. The wholesaler then offers a range of services, including packaging, computerized order processing, and medication delivery, after they have ownership of the goods. The final links in the supply chain are healthcare facilities and pharmaceutical companies.
