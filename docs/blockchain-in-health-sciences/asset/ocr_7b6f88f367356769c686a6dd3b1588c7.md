Various electronic product codes such as barcodes usually follow globally accepted rules, i.e., GS1 standards [22] for handling supply chain data. This standard eliminates execution errors in certain cases and improves traceability. However, the case study presented in [11] reveals that integrating various ERP systems and including electronic product code marking scheme results in increasing overall cost and time. Leading large enterprises may possess significant count of legacy ERP systems due to changes in organizational structures, mergers of different enterprises, and acquisitions over time. Such systems rarely communicate with one another in easier way due to several factors. For example, these systems may differ in basic structures, such as defining data fields. Additionally, a large company may possess multiple ledgers in different ERP systems a single activity, such as tracking. On the other hand, its product suppliers and distributors typically have their own stock, transaction or other activity based ledgers and ERP systems.

A blockchain may alleviate the issues involved in ERP-based SCM systems as its structure consists of a chronological string of blocks aiming to integrate all three types of transactional flows.
