# 1.3.3 Consensus Mechanism

In 1959, David Gale and Edmund Eisenberg delved into the intricacies of achieving a stable consensus probability distribution among individuals with subjective consciousness co-located in a specific setting. This led to certain conditions which were termed as consensus problem and outlined conditions under which consensus could be attained transacting parties. It was noted that their methodology was effective only within a confined and regulated node environment. It lacked suitability for open environments as it has various inherent limitations and drawbacks [24]. The Byzantine Generals dilemma also known as consistency dilemma was articulated by Satoshi Nakamoto. Central to mitigating such failures is the consensus algorithm, which ensures data uniformity across diverse nodes for proposed transactions or actions. Various consensus methods exhibit differing capacities to uphold equal accounting rights among nodes. An inadequately devised consensus algorithm risks the network abruptly in the face of an attack. Conversely, a meticulously crafted algorithm can sustain the blockchain This was applied to the internet because of the open architecture and large number of nodes found in systems like Bitcoin. It resolved the problems caused by Byzantine failures, which are essential to blockchain technology.

Consensus techniques can be classified into four types:

- 1. Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus
- 2. Hybrid consensus mechanism
- 3. DAG-based consensus
- 4. Non-Byzantine consensus

Blockchain is an unchanging, distributed, and decentralized network with security and privacy features. Even in the absence of centralized authority, transaction on the blockchains is approved and even considered safe. This is possible only because of a consensus mechanism, which is a crucial part of every Blockchain network. In the blockchain network, the peer node needs a consensus process to determine the present state of the ledger. Due to the absence of centralized authority, consensus algorithms help to manage trust in this distributed environment. This mechanism makes sure that every node has equal rights and must take part in the consensus process, so that a consensus is reached. The goal of a consensus algorithm is to reach an agreement that is advantageous to the blockchain network.

Different consensus techniques and their operation are discussed below:

- a. **Proof of Work (PoW):** Proof of work (PoW) is a consensus technique requiring a significant computational effort among a network of devices. The PoW consensus algorithm, attempts to effectively resolve a challenging conundrum. It is mainly used by Bitcoins. The node that uses the most processing power to solve this challenge fastest wins the privilege to mine the next block [25]. To use this decentralized consensus method, network participants exert effort to decipher an encrypted hexadecimal number.

- b. **Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT):** In the late 1990s, Miguel Castro and Barbara Liskov created Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance, a consensus technique. PBFT was designed to work well in asynchronous systems, in which there is no maximum duration for waiting for a response. Its optimisation of overhead time is low. Its objective to resolve the issues that have arisen in the past with Byzantine Fault Tolerance methods [26]. Blockchain and distributed computing are two areas of application. The ability of a distributed network to reach agreement when certain nodes in the network fail to react or reply with misleading information is known as Byzantine Fault Tolerance, or BFT. A BFT mechanism aims to reduce the impact of faulty nodes and guard against system failures by employing collaborative decision-making which includes both proper and faulty nodes.

- c. **Proof of Stake (PoS):** Proof of Stake has gained the most traction as a PoW replacement. Ethereum has moved from PoW consensus to PoS consensus. By investing some of their coins in the system through this type of consensus process, validators invest in the system's currencies as opposed to buying expensive equipment to get over a difficult obstacle. Each validator will then begin examining the blocks [27]. If a block appears to be able to be added to the chain, validators will take a chance to validate it. Rewards for each validator are given in accordance with their stakes, and these stakes are raised in response to genuine blocks that are added to the Blockchain. The validator chosen to create a new block is ultimately determined by their financial stake in the network. PoS, therefore, employs a set of incentives to encourage validators to reach a consensus.

- d. **Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS):** A development of the fundamental concepts behind Proof of Stake is represented by the consensus algorithm. Daniel Larimer, the man of Steamed, EOS, and Bit Shares, invented the DPoS consensus mechanism in 2014. Every staker in the Proof of Stake consensus mechanism is eligible to take part in the “minting” phase, which gives them the authority to choose two nodes that add new blocks to the blockchain and verifying existing ones [28]. To select nodes that verify blocks and uphold the DPoS system, an election method is employed These nodes are designated as “witnesses” or “block producers.”

The DPoS consensus procedure is as follows:

- **Voting:** Voting in the DPoS consensus gives users the choice of casting their votes themselves or delegating their power to a third party to do so. Selected witnesses who verify transactions generate blocks. They are paid if they verify and sign each transaction in a block; the payment is typically divided with the voters who selected the witnesses. If a witness fails to verify every transaction within the given timeframe, they miss a block, all transactions remain unverified, and they are not entitled to any reward. The prize is increased by the following witness's award, which attaches to that block. When these transactions are gathered by the next witness, the block in question is called stolen. The percentage of each voter's vote represents their stake.
