Blockchain technology also oversees pharmaceutical supply chains and medical data. In addition, bioinformatics can help identify and mitigate public health threats like infectious disease spread and pandemic prediction. This improves attempts to prevent and control disease. Overall, Blockchain and Bioinformatics together have enormous potential to transform healthcare by enhancing data exchange, security, tailored treatment, and medical research by offering strong data security, decentralized, effective consent management through smart contracts, improved data privacy, transparent audit trails, and creative access control models designed for healthcare data management, and also transformation of healthcare through precision medicine, data-driven methods, and the use of modern technologies to enhance patient outcomes and public health. Blockchain and bioinformatics tools promote health healthcare, privacy, decentralization of patient data, personalization, and more. However, healthcare facilities encounter numerous challenges when implementing these tools. Since bioinformatics and AI-based technologies may not be able to address liability, stability, and transparency for innovative medical devices, regulatory obstacles remain. This technology is new and sophisticated; thus, software developers are scarce. This raises blockchain application development and maintenance costs. The high implementation costs of blockchain technology for enterprises limit its widespread adoption and utilization. While the main function of blockchain technology is to secure and maintain the patient’s private information and secure the network via encryption, users risk losing all of their vulnerable information if they displace their private keys. This could be a huge problem since it is nearly impossible to recover the lost private key. Many fragmented and disparate data are collected from different health center departments, and it is a challenge to create a cohesive patient record on the blockchain. Medical records’ fragmentary, inaccurate, and inconsistently maintained nature might challenge the data quality used in developing bioinformatics algorithms. Bioinformatics algorithms may not be able to predict future results from historical data to medical data native. Blockchain technology is unable to scale to accommodate healthcare’s massive data volumes. Some learning algorithms in bioinformatics applications are “black boxes”, making their decision-making processes difficult to understand. Most importantly, since the information in the blocks cannot be altered, its immutability poses a serious issue in the event of an error. The blockchain is an inefficient and ecologically unfriendly technology since it requires significant energy to validate transactions.
