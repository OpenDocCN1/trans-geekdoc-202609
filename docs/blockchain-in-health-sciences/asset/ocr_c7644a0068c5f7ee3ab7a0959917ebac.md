In pharmaceutical supply chain, unavailability of raw materials due to supply shortage or resource-company shut down or local and global country-based policy and laws, can directly affect demand and human lives. On the other hand, excessive stock products and sporadic demand may cause expiry of drugs or pharmaceutical equipment in shelves and incur huge amount of losses to the companies.

The problem of designing the order of testing tasks wherein the presence of unlimited availability of resources is addressed in [16]. The difference of this model from conventional project scheduling is that each testing task comes with a failure probability. Therefore, guaranteed outcome from a single design of testing tasks may not be achievable. This problem is resolved as a continuous-time Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP) approach. Another critical issue lies in the risk-management at the development stage, where candidate drugs are selected and taken through trials and process development. Authors [17, 18], provided an approach towards product selection and planning test schedule while effectively handling the risks involved. The sources of uncertainty addressed in the scheduling problem comprise processing time resource requirements, probability of success, task cost, and market returns. To cope with the uncertainty problems in clinical trials, [19–21] addressed a deterministic version of the problem by distributing allocation of upcoming manufacturing load to the potential sites around the world.

In addition, auditing is one of the useful common approaches for verifying transactions to improve supply chain execution in every corporation. It helps to ensure compliance with contracts. However, it does not help much in decision making process to resolve operational deficiencies. For example, a food company or a pharmaceutical company faces a genuine problem when its products in stock reach the end-of-shelf life in retail stores. An inspection or audit of stock in a retail store may expose the count of items which have expired, but will not justify the reasons behind it. Product expiry in retail store can happen due to glitches in the supply chain process, which comprises inventory management upstream, sub-optimal product allocation policy to stores, inadequate advertising, declining demand, and inadequate rotation of shelf-products. Such instance of glitch comprises failure to put new manufactured products at the back of older ones on the shelf. An easy record maintenance of all those activities can help in reducing the expiration of the products at retail stores.
