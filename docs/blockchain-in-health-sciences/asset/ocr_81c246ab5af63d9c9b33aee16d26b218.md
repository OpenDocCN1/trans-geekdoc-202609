There are three main types of blockchains-

+   1. Public Blockchain: The information within this blockchain is accessible to everyone, and anyone is welcome to participate and support consensus and make modifications to the core software. Transactions taking place on this form of blockchain are viewable by each member of the network, although participants' identities are pseudonymous through the delegation of public-key cryptography; they are identified by their various public keys. Even if a participant's realworld identity does not have an associated correlation with their blockchain persona, through the use of linking related transactions and a subsequent public key, these particular transactions can be strung together to formulate a web. If an attacker is able to couple a participant's real-world identity with the public key, he can then access the entire transaction history on that blockchain. Public blockchains, like major cryptocurrencies, are best represented by Bitcoin and Ethereum, under which they fall under the term of public permissionless chains and find wide acceptance within the areas of cryptocurrencies [5, 7].
