
## 2.3.2 Electronic Health Records

Telemedicine uses Internet and communications infrastructure to provide healthcare remotely. This leads to a substantial decrease in the operating expenses accrued by healthcare professionals and enhances the efficiency of patient health. The smart healthcare monitoring system presented by Zouka and Hosni [18] utilizes neural networks and fuzzy systems to analyze data obtained from several sensors, including temperature, ECG, heartbeat, pressure and pulse oximeter. Azure IoT Hub and FBI are used to assess the data acquired from the sensors once it has been transmitted through the GSM module. By utilizing M2M monitoring system for patients and a remote health application, this technology empowers physicians to provide urgent medical care. According to [19] a healthcare monitoring system was presented for those residing in rural areas. Blood pressure, glucose levels, and abnormal cell development are some of the important indications that this system hopes to track. The patients are equipped with RFID tags to facilitate identification. In the event of any deviation from typical health parameters, notifications are transmitted to a distant healthcare facility, prompting the staff to undertake appropriate measures. The suggested system underwent simulation using NS2, resulting in a notable improvement in throughput.

## 2.3.3 Identifying and Preventing Chronic Diseases

Cancer, diabetes, asthma, obesity, and many more chronic diseases affect a large portion of the global population. Depression, a common side effect of long-term health problems, is linked to these conditions. A health monitoring system developed for people with autism was created by Kumar et al. [20]. Using information gathered from neurological sensors, system automatically deciphers EEG waves and notifies carers of any suspicious findings. Doctors who keep taps on their patients from a distance will get email alerts in case of an emergency. In order to help people with cancer healthcare solutions that are enabled by the Internet of Things (IoT), Onasana and Elshankagiri [21] proposed a number of architectural designs and frameworks. Cloud computing, which makes use of big data technologies to analyze data, and WSN, allows for the massive operation of sensors, are the main points of focus. Additionally, the article discusses the practical and security issues related to healthcare systems that utilize the IoT.

## 2.3.4 Residential and Geriatric Healthcare

The implementation of Internet of Things and its related technologies within residential environments facilitates the ongoing surveillance of elderly individuals who encounter restricted mobility and necessitate longer duration to arrive at hospitals for routine and emergency care [22] presented a health monitoring system specifically developed for those engaged in active and supported living. The technology integrates multiple sensors to collect data, which is then transmitted to the cloud for the purpose of analysis. The instance was created by the researchers to give an idea of system, which consists of 6 different types of sensors, an indoor location module that is supported by Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), sensor interface circuits, Raspberry Pi2 acting as the microcontroller, WiFi transceivers, and a cloud server. Yang et al. [23] developed a personal healthcare system for wheelchair users who are alone at home. V. S. Naresh et al. completed a study that employs IoT and WBSNS to provide efficient healthcare services to wheelchair users. The attainment of real-time health monitoring is facilitated by the utilization of sensors to detect various physiological parameters such as heart rate, electrocardiogram ECG, environmental sensing nodes, and actuators located in close proximity to and on the patient. The mobile phone with Zigbee and Bluetooth communication protocols being utilized to gather data from both the patient and the immediate surroundings. Furthermore, the present method takes into account the patient's motion in a wheelchair by employing sensors to observe the wheelchair's displacement across various positions.

## 2.4 Challenges

There is a noticeable increase in the implementation of IoT technology within the healthcare sector. Rapid technological innovation facilitated by a variety of government-integrated facilities is propelling market expansion. The healthcare industry has been profoundly impacted by the IoT, which has transformed patient experiences and the methods employed by professionals. Despite the indispensable benefits that the IoT provides, implementing it is not devoid of obstacles. Balancing the potential for ground-breaking advancements with the practicalities of deploying IoT in healthcare can present considerable obstacles. Data concerns, connectivity issues, and even device compatibility, which may seem trivial, can cause significant confusion. ### 2.4.1 Data Security

The advantage of using IoT in health care is that it enhances the experience of a patient monitoring system by efficiently analysing the data. As an illustration, a wearable device designed to monitor a patient’s pulse rate has the capability to promptly notify medical practitioners of any anomalous readings. Security and privacy concerns are however, inevitable wherever data is present, particularly sensitive data like medical information. It has never been more important to resolve security and privacy concerns with IoT in healthcare, given the prevalence of data breaches. Therefore, in order to ensure the dependability and safety of medical devices as well as the availability, confidentiality, and integrity of patient data, IoT in healthcare systems needs to be outfitted with strong security mechanisms. Because IoT in healthcare systems are resource resource-constrained, framing lightweight algorithms becomes laborious. Moreover, the primary challenge pertains to the preservation of energy efficiency. Implementing energy-efficient machine learning or deep learning algorithms that can process lightweight cryptography is therefore required in order to implement security threat mitigation features.

### 2.4.2 Scalability Challenges

To facilitate the installation of IoT systems on a large scale, scalability issues must be resolved. Moreover, the transmission of data within IoT systems is critical, and it is imperative to employ an appropriate mechanism to prevent packet loss. Aside from this, the dynamic character of the existing routing protocols in IoT systems hinders their performance. Additionally, there are significant concerns regarding coverage and localization. Processing the data traffic generated must not result in the imposition of supplementary expenses or delays in computation. As a result, challenges arise regarding the calibration of the network in the nodes that undergo constant change. Mobility concerns also appear to be obstacles for IoT-based systems. The utilization of sensors on a source, such as the human body, results in a decline in network performance whenever the user relocates. Therefore, dependable services must be implemented to maintain the network's accessibility while the user is in motion.

### 2.4.3 Inadequate Standardization

A major challenge associated with the deployment of IoT devices is that they establish connections with a considerable number of other devices via a network while although the number of devices may not reach hundreds of thousands, the integration of equipment tracking, drug delivery systems, and pulse monitors with routers, gateways, results in a significant number of devices. There is an extensive variety of languages, standards, and protocols that are utilized to link these devices. Ownership and regulation are fraught with ambiguity due to the absence of universally recognized standards. As a result, the integration of numerous IoT devices into the healthcare industry becomes difficult. Healthcare users encounter challenges in aggregating data due to the intricacies of the devices' network communication protocols.

### 2.4.4 Bandwidth and Connectivity Constraints

In the IoT system, connecting to different devices in limited connectivity constraints is the primary concern. Although the majority of medical IoT devices are linked via WiFi, a considerable number of them are equipped with cellular capabilities to compensate for potential Wi-Fi outages.
