+   (b) **Private Blockchain Networks:** It is also known as managed blockchains and is controlled by a single entity. The authority determines who is allowed to join the network and what privileges they have. Private blockchains are only partially decentralized due to the inclusion of access constraints. Ripple, a company-focused digital money exchange network, is one example of a private blockchain [7].

+   (c) **Hybrid Blockchain Network:** Blockchains that are hybrids incorporate components from public and private networks. Businesses can install permission-based private systems in addition to public ones. They maintain public access to the other data while controlling access to particular data contained in the blockchain. They allow members of the public verify if private transactions have been finished through the use of smart contracts. Hybrid blockchains, for instance, allow the public to access digital currency while maintaining the privacy of bank-owned currency [8].

+   (d) **Consortium Blockchain Networks:** Consortium blockchain networks are governed by a collection of organizations. Pre-selected organizations are equally accountable for data access rights determination and blockchain maintenance.
