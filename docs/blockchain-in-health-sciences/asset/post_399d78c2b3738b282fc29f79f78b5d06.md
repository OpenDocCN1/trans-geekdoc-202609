## 17.3.4.3 Handling Overfitting

An overfitted model brings us to square one wherein we do not have an efficient algorithm to predict the class of the image [22, 23]. To make sure that our efforts won't go in vain, we can implement one of the following methods:

- Data augmentation: Purposely feed noisy data to the model, while training. By doing so, the model can generalize the patterns in the images better [24].
- Reducing model complexity: Consider reducing the complexity of the model by decreasing the number of layers or neurons.
- Dropout of neurons: Add dropout layers to the network to randomly deactivate some neurons during training, preventing the model from relying too heavily on specific neurons.
- Early callback: Monitor the validation loss during training and stop training when the validation loss stops improving or starts to increase.

## 17.3.5 Applications of CNNs in Medical Diagnosis: A Powerful Tool for Improved Care

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have emerged as a transformative technology within the medical field, particularly in the realm of medical diagnosis. Their exceptional ability to analyze and extract patterns from medical images has opened doors to a plethora of applications, significantly impacting patient care. Here's a closer look at some key areas where CNNs are making a difference:

- Disease Detection: CNNs excel at identifying subtle patterns and anomalies within medical images. This allows for highly accurate detection of various diseases, including:

- Cancerous lesions in mammograms and other scans.
- Lung nodules in X-rays.
- Brain abnormalities in MRI and CT scans.

By analyzing vast datasets of labeled medical images, CNNs can learn to recognize these subtle signatures of illness that might escape the human eye. This translates to earlier diagnoses, improved patient outcomes, and potentially life-saving interventions.

- Risk Assessment: CNNs can be trained to evaluate a patient's risk of developing specific diseases based on medical imaging. For instance, a CNN could analyze a coronary angiogram to predict the likelihood of a future heart attack. This empowers physicians with the ability to implement preventive measures and personalize treatment plans for at-risk patients.

- Treatment Planning: Beyond diagnosis, CNNs can be instrumental in treatment planning. By analyzing medical images, CNNs can assist in:

- Tumor segmentation in scans, providing crucial information for surgeons and radiation oncologists.
- Identifying optimal treatment targets, leading to more precise and effective therapies.

This integration of CNNs into treatment planning can significantly improve the success rates of procedures and minimize side effects for patients.

- Drug Discovery: The power of CNNs extends beyond diagnosis and treatment. Their ability to analyze medical images can be harnessed to identify novel drug targets or biomarkers associated with specific diseases. This can accelerate the drug discovery process, leading to the development of more targeted and effective medications.

While CNNs hold immense potential, it's crucial to acknowledge that they are still under development. Their primary role in medicine is to serve as a decision-making aid for healthcare professionals. CNN complement human expertise, not replace it. The future of medical diagnosis lies in the collaborative power of advanced AI and the irreplaceable clinical judgment of medical professionals. There are several uses for machine learning-based image classification in the medical diagnostics and healthcare industries. The following are a few typical medical applications of CNNs and ML for image classification:

- The process of leveraging the power of machine learning on the outputs derived from CT, MRI and X-ray scanners is sickness diagnosis. One such case is identifying malignancies in brain images [25, 26] or symptoms of pneumonia in the chest X-rays [30].
- Skin Lesion Classification: Process involves using dermatological images to identify benign and malignant lesions to diagnose skin diseases [27].
- Retinal disease identification: A similar approach is used on fundus photographs to identify and classify retinal diseases [28].
- Mammography analysis: This clinical implementation of machine learning assists radiologists in identifying concerning regions and distinguishing between benign and malignant cancers, which in turn helps them detect breast cancer early [29].
- Histopathology Image Analysis: This method classifies and identifies tissue anomalies, such as malignant cells, using pictures from histology. This is really helpful for diagnosing cancer in pathology [31].

CNNs are frequently chosen in these applications due to their capacity to automatically extract important characteristics from images. This makes them perfect for jobs where patterns and spatial correlations are essential for diagnosis. Large datasets of labeled medical pictures are used to train CNNs, enabling the models to generalize and give healthcare workers important support in making prompt and accurate diagnoses.
