## 10.4.2 Cost Performance Analysis

This part focuses on measuring the price and gas amount consumed by each function to assess the performance of the dynamic smart contract. Furthermore, the smart contract has a minimum gas cost, which covers the transaction of deploying any of the commands onto the Ethereum network, and this must be met for the entire transaction to execute. The Remix IDE, which is capable of showing the purchaser gas cost for every transaction through the output console, is a very helpful tool. An illustration of the fees and costs associated with different smart contract features is provided in Figure 10.12. This chart serves as a good example of the patterns of smart resource consumption. Additionally, the red line in Figure 10.12 represents the computation costs incurred in all transactions executed by each user. The blue line, included on the Y-axis for medium and large transaction sizes, represents the transaction fees, which include the charges for sending the code of the dynamic smart contract to the Ethereum blockchain. The main analysis of resource utilization and the evaluation of dynamic smart contracts must be based on this kind of study.

## 10.5 Conclusions

This chapter postulates a new approach that builds upon the design science paradigm and merges dynamic smart contracts with the pharmaceutical supply chain business to gain a competitive advantage. The proposed framework dynamically integrates blockchain and IoT sensor technologies, along with smart IoCs, to manage the secure and verified distribution of drugs to customers. Our system, through smart contract coding and algorithms, is broad enough for direct implementation into the supply chain framework that distributes various perishable goods, although our main focus is on the pharmaceutical supply line. The capabilities of pocket contracts, based on the dynamics of the code written in the Remix IDE, were demonstrated using various scenarios, as those aimed restoring trust in drug delivery, identifying and fixing transport problems, and speeding up refund processes. We intend to implement these smart contracts using the Hyperledger platform and open decentralized applications, also known as DApps, with a wide variety of real-world entities like medicine producers, distributors, and patients. Thus, this business objective is truly monumental in building a trustworthy and robust supply chain ecosystem underpinned by dynamic smart contract technology. Future tasks will involve deploying these intelligent contracts on Hyperledger and creating decentralized apps (DApps) that include real entities such as manufacturers, suppliers, payers, and patients. The aim is to establish an open and robust supply chain ecology.
