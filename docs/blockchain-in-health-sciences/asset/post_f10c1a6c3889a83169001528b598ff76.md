- Tracking: Product end-to-end delivery is tracked in the process of supply-chain process using blockchain technology. The operations are transparent to the participants; they are aware of all the phases in the procurement of raw materials through manufacture, distribution and delivery across the customers [3].
- Product Counterfeiting: The legality of the product can be known by tracking the ownership and provenance of the data. Digital signatures are used to verify the originality of the product or goods. Anti-counterfeiting tactics are favored to protect consumers trust, confidence, quality and safety etc. [4].
- Auditing and Quality Assurance: Through the blockchain, one can track the origin, transfer of the products and services. And overall quality can be enhanced using this technology [14, 17].
- Inventory-Control: Set of guidelines and warnings are provided using smart contracts which can enhance the quality and regulatory procedures. By reordering the data into batches or proper analysis it can provide proper inventory control [15].
- Management of Vendors and Suppliers: By enabling real-time visibility over inventory levels, locations, and movements throughout the supply chain, blockchain improves the inventory management. By automating order, processing, inventory replenishment and invoicing, smart contracts can cut down on human error, lead-time, and expense associated with inventory holding. Demand forecasting and inventory optimization are made easier by the availability of a single, transparent view of inventory data to supply chain participants [17].
- Payments and Finance in the Supply Chain: Blockchain digitizes trade finance instruments including invoices, payment guarantees, and letters of credit to facilitate efficient supply chain financing and payments [16, 17]. Suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and financial institutions may enhance cash flow management, expedite trade finance procedures, and decrease payment processing times using blockchain-based systems. By automating payment settlements upon complete fulfillment, smart contracts can improve financial transaction transparency and confidence.
- Resilience and Risk Management: By reducing supply chain risks including delays, interruptions, political concerns, and supplier dependencies, blockchain improves resilience and risk management in the supply chain [12]. Risk management tools built on blockchain technology have the ability to evaluate risk factors, track events in the supply chain, send out notifications for impending disruptions, and assist with reaction and contingency planning. Proactive risk reduction methods are made possible by the transparent and decentralized nature of blockchain, which enhances supply chain visibility.
- Impact on the Environment and Society: Blockchain makes it possible to transparently track social and environmental impact across the supply chain, supporting sustainability activities [16]. Blockchain-enabled systems allow stakeholders to keep an eye on things like labor practices, ethical sourcing, trash creation, carbon emissions, and energy use. Sustainability reporting, stakeholder accountability, and ethical supply chain practices are all encouraged by this openness.

## 14.5 Aspects Related to Regulation

These are the following aspects related to Compliance and Regulation in pharmaceutical industry.

- Possibilities and regulatory obstacles for blockchain usage in pharmaceutical industry
The promise of blockchain technology to improve data integrity, validity of ambiguity, transparency, and security is entwined with possibilities and regulatory problems for the pharmaceutical business. Regulatory agencies must provide clarity and uniformity to address issues arising from regulatory ambiguity, particularly with regard to data privacy and validity of smart contracts. while encouraging innovation and cooperation in regulation, blockchain presents chances for regulators to improve patient data protection, supply chain traceability, and real-time monitoring [18]. Creating regulatory frameworks that strike a balance between innovation and compliance—by promoting trust, transparency, and efficiency in pharmaceutical operations—is crucial to overcoming these obstacles.

- Partnership between industry, technology providers, and regulators
The pharmaceutical industry can only fully utilize blockchain technology if regulators, industry stakeholders, and technology providers work together [17, 18]. To ensure compliance, protect consumer rights and promote innovation, regulators play a key role in establishing clear rules, standards, and regulations. The pharmaceutical industry, academic institutions, and healthcare providers are among those with the ability to influence regulatory frameworks and industry best practices. They also provide use cases, operational insights, and subject matter experience. Technology vendors provide innovative thinking, scalable solutions that meet industry and regulatory requirements, and technological know-how. Blockchain developers, solution providers, and platform designers are some examples of these vendors. Collaboration between multiple stakeholders includes ongoing discussions, information sharing, pilot projects, and regulatory sandboxes to test and evaluate blockchain technology in real-world environments while solving legal issues.

Regulators can partner with industry and technology providers to co-create regulatory frameworks that address data privacy, security and compliance concerns, promoting interoperability and standardization, while facilitating responsible implementation of blockchain technology [16, 17]. Regulators, industry insiders and technology providers can ensure regulatory compliance, unlock business value, improve patient outcomes and drive innovation in the evolving pharmaceutical market.
