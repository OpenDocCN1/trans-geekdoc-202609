# Exploring the Potential of Blockchain Technology in Healthcare and Data Security: Perceptions of General and Engineering College Students in West Bengal

Mazhar Shamsi Ansary¹*, Indrajit Mahato² and Santosh Kumar Behera³

¹Department of Education, Netaji Nagar College for Women, Kolkata, West Bengal, India

²Department of Education, Vinaya Bhavana, Visva-Bharat, West Bengal, India

## Abstract

The present work is an endeavor to explore the perceptions of general and engineering college students regarding the potentiality of blockchain technology in relation to their healthcare and data privacy in the state of West Bengal, India. A descriptive survey method was used. A total of 289 undergraduate (UG) students (159 from engineering colleges and 130 from general colleges) were selected as a representative sample of the population. A self-made perception scale was constructed by the researchers. Data were collected using a Google form. Mean, standard deviation (SD), t-tests, and F-tests were utilized to analyze the data. The results revealed that the level of perception of UG students toward blockchain technology is moderate. Additionally, there is no significant difference in the perception of students based on gender and category variables. However, differences in perception were observed based on location, institution, stream, and semester. Analyzing the perceptions of students from general-degree and engineering colleges separately reveals students from general-degree colleges have a moderate perception of the potential of blockchain technology in healthcare and data security. No differences were observed in the perceptions of general-degree college students based on gender and stream, although variations were found based on location, category, and semester. A moderate perception was also observed among engineering college students. Although differences were noted based on location, no difference was found in the perceptions of students in terms of gender, semester, and category variables.

## 5.1 Introduction

Time passes according to the river's flow. Keeping up with the times, we now live in a world where everything is centered on science and technology [1]. Unquestionably, science and technology have played a significant role in the remarkable advancements made in the three primary spheres of human existence—education, industry, and health. The result of this progress is Artificial Intelligence (AI) [2]. Blockchain technology is one of the achievements of AI. Since around 2016, several industrialized nations, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Japan, and others, have recognized its significance. As a result, there is currently strong demand for it from numerous businesses and organizations across these nations [3]. The late 20th century saw the emergence of the blockchain concept. We first got the idea of blockchain from Chaum's Ph.D. thesis published in 1982. The concept became clearer in 1991 from Haber and Stornetta’s “How to Time-Stamp a Digital Document [4],” Merkle Trees unveiled an improved version of the prior system in 1992, introducing blockchain technology to the general public. The new system featured a mechanism for securely storing all kinds of data. In 2000, Stefan Konst developed novel theory of secure chains and the implementation of cryptography. It is possible to refer to 2004 as a turning point in the development of blockchain technology, when Hal Finney offered “Reusable Proof of Work” as digital currency [5]. Nick Szabo introduced Bit Gold, a decentralized currency, in 1998, but it publicly described in 2005 [6]. In modern times, society believes that Satoshi Nakamoto first the idea of blockchain technology in his article “Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System” in November 2008 [7]. Since its introduction, currency, particularly Bitcoin, has become more widely accepted and is currently commonly seen in the domains of finance, health, and education [8].

Blockchain technology refers to the secure connection and verification of various transactions within a computer network through a decentralized and distributed ledger system [9]. In a blockchain, blocks are usually arranged in a chain. Information is enriched using the Internet in a decentralized process called a block, which comprises different kinds of information [10]. Each block has two main components: the block header and the block body. The block body evaluates and stores various types of data, whereas the block header links one to the next. Various data are kept in blocks, which are connected to create a chain [11]. This blockchain technology offers several unique features and benefits [12]:

- Ledger technology, where every link in a chain has access to the data and prevents third parties from altering this information;
- safety, where any transaction or piece of information is validated using a digital signature and cryptographic key for complete data security;
- privacy, in which crucial data are kept private;
- cryptocurrency, a consensus protocol, which enables the simple and secure exchange of information; where members in a chain or network validate transactions;
- smart contracts, where data has cryptographic evidence, making it unlikely that the data will be compromised if obtained from the outside.

Currently, there is a dearth of study on the advantages of adopting blockchain in higher education, despite numerous publications about its application in other domains highlighting its benefits [13]. West Bengal is one of the developing states in India; thus, technology has not fully taken over the higher education system. However, several education institutions now have the opportunity to deploy technology as a result of COVID-19. Students consequently have the chance to learn about online learning and benefit from it. Therefore, it is anticipated that students will perceive the application of blockchain in higher education favorably.

As per the guidelines of the National Education Policy 2020 in India, students can currently transfer their academic credits to different educational institutions in West Bengal and India for higher study [14]. The benefits of using blockchain technology to implement the new policy will be more fully realized by students, instructors, and other higher education staff members.

Just as blockchain technology has incredible importance in the field of education, it also plays an important role in healthcare and data security. However, healthcare has evolved since the time of Internet 2.0 [15]. If blockchain technology can be utilized in healthcare and data security, its potential for improvement may peak even higher. It will enable the easy storage of patient data, doctor data, medical history, and more [16]. An Electronic Health Record (EHR) is a robust way to digitally secure various health-related information.
