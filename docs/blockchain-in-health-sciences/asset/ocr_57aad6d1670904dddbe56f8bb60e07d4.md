# 19.3 An Overview of Genomics Research

Genomics is the scientific discipline that examines the entirety of an organism's genetic material. Within genomics, sequencing enables researchers to explore the connections between genetics and diseases, and can aid in the identification of genetic mutations that are responsible for diseases. This, in turn, can lead to personalized diagnoses and treatment strategies. Recent progress in DNA sequencing technologies has substantially reduced the cost of sequencing the human genome, making data more accessible to individuals at a price below $1000 USD [20]. Nevertheless, the augmented volume of genomic sequencing presents two concerns: firstly, the expanded quantity of genomic data necessitates significant computing capacity and data storage. Furthermore, the inclusion of genomic data raises privacy concerns due to its sensitive nature, and the expanding volume of genomic data exacerbates these concerns.

Present genomics research utilizes a powerful technology called high throughput sequencing that can simultaneously sequence hundreds of millions of DNA molecules [21]. Although useful, this technology produces an immense amount of data, requiring substantial computational power [20]. As a result, computational analysis is the bottleneck of genomics research [20]. A seemingly effective solution to address the increasing need for advanced computational power is to use cloud services [20, 22]. Cloud computing is a service that allows individuals to access and utilize additional computational processing power or store data by their hardware's capabilities from a shared supply of computational resources provided through the medium of the Internet [23].
