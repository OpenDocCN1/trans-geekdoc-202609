By improving data management security, efficiency, and precision, cloud computing, software-defined connectivity, blockchain, artificial intelligence (IoT), and large data sets have the potential to completely change the healthcare sector [11]. On the other hand, implementing these advances can be challenging and expensive, requiring specialized knowledge and staff [6, 7]. The integration of Internet of Things (IoT) devices, sensors, and wearables into current healthcare systems can present significant challenges due to their high cost. Additionally, ongoing maintenance expenses could be high, especially for healthcare providers that are smaller or have fewer resources [3, 8, 9]. Improved patient involvement, more accurate diagnoses, and better health outcomes are anticipated when AI and medicine are combined [10]. Healthcare organizations can benefit from highly dependable IoT systems that possess high fault tolerance, data security, constant QoS settings, low latency, good scalability with outstanding information integrity, seamless integration, ease of mobility, instant processing, and ease of deployment [9, 10].
