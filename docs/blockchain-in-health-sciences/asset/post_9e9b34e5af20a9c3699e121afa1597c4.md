## Abstract

The implementation of Internet Things (IoT) technology has resulted in a substantial and noteworthy transformation in healthcare systems. The use of IoT devices has enhanced the efficiency, convenience, and automation of healthcare services. Different IoT devices transmit vast quantities of captured data to the cloud, where it is processed to facilitate physicians' access to information. In the case of cloud-sensor architecture, where data transmission and reception incur significant latency, fog nodes are incorporated at the periphery of the sensor networks. However, due to the extremely sensitive nature of medical data, privacy, integrity, and access control are required. The aforementioned challenges may be resolved by employing blockchain technology, which can also foster trust between the healthcare provider and the patient. This chapter thoroughly examines the integration of IoT technologies into the health sciences. After, it discusses the significance and difficulties associated with cloud-based and fog-based data storage and computation. It also covers the security aspects and difficulties associated with IoT technology-based solutions, as well as the significance of blockchain in safeguarding medical data. Finally, it examines the future scope of this topic.

### 1.1 Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a pervasive network of physical devices composed of sensors and network connections. Each of these devices has a distinct identity and different processing capabilities. These devices sense data from the environment and share them with each other because they are interconnected. The domain of IoT covers a wide range of technologies, including computer science, engineering, electronics, and communications. It is also used to reduce human intervention and improve efficiency and accuracy. It facilitates real-time insights, data-driven decision making, and predictive analytics, allowing businesses to anticipate trends, mitigate risks, and capture new possibilities. Currently, Internet of Things (IoT) is being used in agriculture, industry, government, supply chain, smart cities, healthcare and many other sectors to transform their operations. IoT develops and matures, its revolutionary impact on society, economy and environment will continue to grow, leading to more effective, intelligent and sustainable solutions in the future.

The incorporation of IoT has brought a lot of change in the healthcare sector. The traditional healthcare system had inefficiencies and several shortcomings, such as late diagnosis of illness, inadequate health monitoring, lack of critical information, and inefficiencies in the medical supply chain. Amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, it became clear globally that the traditional healthcare system had to be improved to ensure that even people living in remote areas could access healthcare services. The use of telemedicine and remote patient monitoring has significantly changed the healthcare sector in recent years. They use IoT technology along with the Internet to improve health care access, patient outcomes, and cost-effectiveness. The use of telemedicine system has helped patients save time, money and effort by using online consultation and avoiding physical appointments with doctors. Patients who require hospitalization, also have the option of receiving constant hospital-like monitoring at home, called domiciliary hospitalization. This requires patients to have some wearable IoT gadgets, which capture vital signs and other health parameters and send to the physician on a regular basis through the internet, and the physician administers treatment according to the information received. This not only relieves the burden on hospitals by reducing the demand for beds, but it also allows the patient to feel more comfortable in a familiar environment with family members.
