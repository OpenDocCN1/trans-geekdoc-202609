# The Role of Blockchain in Genomics Sciences

Sunil Gautam*, Mansi Menon and Roshni Rana

Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Institute of Technology, Nirma University, Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India

# Abstract

Blockchain represents a digital, decentralized, and highly secure ledger that assures accessibility, tamper resistance, and transparency over any network of nodes. It ensures secure and transparent storage of Electronic Health Records (EHR) through cryptographic principles. Blockchain enhances interoperability by connecting disparate medical records. To enhance healthcare systems and decision medicine, blockchain provides elements such as immutability and decentralized trust, enhancing privacy and patient-centricity. Blockchain is increasingly applied to include supply chains, cryptocurrency, voting systems, smart contracts, and management in the field of health data.

Now, let us turn to genomics. It is the interdisciplinary branch of biology concerned with the structure of the genome, functioning, evolution processes for mapping, and editing. A genome is the complete set of genetic information of an organism, composed of genes, which are sequences of DNA that encode hereditary traits. There are two types of genomes: the DNA genome, found mostly in living organisms, and the nucleic acid (RNA) genome, found mostly in viruses. The contrast between DNA genomes in most organisms and RNA genomes in viruses reflects genetic diversity.

The applications of genomics include the production of personalized medicine, the prediction of diseases risks at an individual level, the study of drug interactions before the conduct of clinical trials, and the study of microorganisms in the environment as opposed to the laboratory.
