A study by Yibin Dong et al. [41], discusses the development of a blockchain-enabled sharing platform (BEST) prototype for patient-managed personal health records (PHRs) between healthcare providers with a strong emphasis on security, privacy, and granular consent management. Features of BEST include PHR security, account access capabilities, scalability, and interoperability. This is based on the Blockchain Access Control model for avoiding insider threats by providing access control policies in a secure way. In addition, this has system scalability in terms of millisecond performance for permission changes at access on hundreds of PHRs simultaneously. Blockchain technology is said to advance together with access control methods to improve security and consensus management.

Mohammed Alhazwi et al. [42], proposed DARC —Decentralized Anonymous Researcher Credentials- a decentralized, privacy-preserving approach to credentialing for access to federated genomic data. DARC uses smart contracts and 0-understanding proofs to augment consideration and privacy by allowing researchers to combine and change their credentials. This protocol enables researchers to show their credentials to computing services or realty files on a selective basis by disposing of the need for mediators. Using an evidence-of-concept implementation, it showcases the usefulness of DARC and highlights how nicely it handles decentralized credentials and scalability-disturbing scenarios.

The research by Gal Zarchi et al. [43], uses blockchain to improve incentives, privacy, and access to DNA databases. It highlights the importance of standardization and potential biases in genomic databases and distinguishes blockchains for big data management. The authors describe an encrypted GWAS experiment that uses multi-key homologous encryption to improve record storage. Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) are used to control access, utilize standards, integrate genetic information anonymously, and facilitate exchange. The method addresses moral, legal, and social issues, ensuring the secure storage of genetic records and the usage of private blockchains and NFTs. In addition, it provides a decentralized and profitable record-sharing system that is also transparent.
