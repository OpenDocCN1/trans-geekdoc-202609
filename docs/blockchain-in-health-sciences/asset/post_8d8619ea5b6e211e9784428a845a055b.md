
## 14.1 Introduction

Blockchain is the concept initiated for Bitcoin in 2008 by Satoshi Nakamoto. The idea was to develop a digital currency that works in a decentralized environment and not rely on third party user or eliminate the need for central control. Since then, blockchain has developed and was found useful in many industries other than cryptocurrency, such as banking, healthcare, supply chain management, pharmaceutical research etc.

Blockchain is a decentralized system which records the information in an immutable manner so that no one can do the modifications on the system. Its decentralized structure removes the needs for central authority or any third party [1]. It's used in various application areas like healthcare, pharmaceutical research, banking, supply-chain and many industries etc. Thus, Supply chain traceability solutions are being developed with the usage of blockchain technology. The authenticity and dependability of the data in the supply chain process are guaranteed by characteristics like immutability, integrity, and transparency which indicates blockchain technology helps supply chains process in pharmaceutical industries in a transparent and sustainable way [2]. Essentially blockchain technology is a distributed, decentralized ledger system that ensures the safety, secure, transparent and unchanging environment to the information.

Some important features of blockchain technology are:

- Decentralization: Unlike the traditional system, it provides decentralized environment for record-keeping. No need for central authority to verify the transactions in the network. Each device in decentralized network is called node. Its decentralized architecture improves resilience and security.
- Distributed System: This feature of blockchain improves security and transparency in the network. Blockchain network has a ledger system that is shared among all the nodes participants in the network. Each node in the network possesses a copy of those data which improve transparency in the network.
- Security: It makes the use of various cryptographic algorithms to ensure security of transaction. Every block is connected to each other using a cryptographic hash value. If there is any modification in the block, its hash value also changes.
- Transparency: It provides a transparent environment to every node in the network as the same copy of the ledger is available to every participating node in the network.
- Immutability: Once any transaction is made in the network, it is impossible to change or modify that transaction. Hence, it offers permanent records on the network.

Therefore, these salient features provide a safe, secure, immutable and transparent environment to the transactions in the network.

### 14.1.1 Blockchain in Pharmaceutical Industry

Pharmaceutical sector is a very important sector for providing essential services to the healthcare domains. From acquisition to creation, manufacturing, distributions among the users are some of the key functionalities of pharmaceutical sector. Pharmaceutical supply-chain is a kind of supplychain that usually includes the acquisition of raw materials for sourcing, production of unit, distribution, and delivery to the end user or patient. Supply-chain is a very crucial part of the healthcare system in pharmaceutical industries and there is a need to ensure that proper medication can reach the end user at the right time, at the right place with a minimal cost. So, it focuses on proper traceability, security, and availability of the resources. Blockchain can be used in healthcare and pharmaceutical sectors to improve patient outcomes, interoperability, and data security etc. It ensures the legitimacy of medications, monitoring pharmaceutical supply chains, and preserving electronic health records (EHRs) [2].

Some of the challenges are:

#### 1. Inefficiencies in Supply-Chain Process:

- Coordination: Inadequate co-ordination between the participants may result in delays, mistakes, and heightened susceptibility to fraud and theft.
- Traceability: Sometimes it may be difficult to trace items throughout the process, which can lead to problems like stock-outs, product recalls, and expired drugs etc.
- Complex: It includes number of parties, like manufacturers, retailers, distributors, and wholesalers, involved in this pharmaceutical supply chain process which may result in a complex system.

#### 2. Regulatory Compliance:

- Paperwork: It keeps thorough paperwork for regulatory compliance. Documentation errors or gaps may result in expensive penalties, delays, and reputational harm.
- Changing Regulations: It faces complexity due to sudden changes in regulations. This may result in more expenses for pharmaceutical industries.
- Strict Rules: This pharmaceutical industry follows the strictest regulations which have some standard guidelines for the whole procedures like production, testing, distribution among the end users.

#### 3. Data Security:

- Data Integrity: Data integrity is very important to ensure accurate research results and regulatory compliance, so maintaining the integrity of data throughout the process is very important.
- Threats: Cybercriminals are increasingly focusing on attempt to disrupt any important assets of users or the industries. It is very necessary to maintain strong cybersecurity defense system against threats.
- Private Information: Industries deal with lot of private or sensitive information, such as patient records, clinical trials, and information from exclusive research. So, it is very necessary to safeguard information from data breaches or any kind of illegal access.

#### 4. Counterfeit Drugs:

- Detection: Due to advanced counterfeiting tactics, it is difficult to differentiate real items from fake items. This makes it tough to identify counterfeit pharmaceuticals for healthcare professionals, customers, or other users also.
