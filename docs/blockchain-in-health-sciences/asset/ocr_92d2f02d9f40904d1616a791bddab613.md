Remotely tracking one's health through connected devices may save lives if there is a case of a medical emergency, such as diabetes, coronary artery disease, asthma attacks, etc. A smart medical gadget that is linked to a smartphone app enables real-time health condition monitoring. The connected medical equipment can gather medical data and other pertinent health information and send it to a doctor or the cloud via the smartphone's internet connection. According to a study by its Centre for Connected Health Policy, remote patient tracking for patients with heart failure lowered the rate of readmission after thirty days by 50%. The Internet of Things device gathers and sends data on ECGs, blood oxygen levels, insulin levels, weight, and blood pressure. These records are stored in the public cloud and may be accessed by anybody with the necessary authorization. This person might be a consultant from outside the company, your doctor, your insurance provider, an employee of the health organization, or entirely someone else. The individual in question has access to the information from any place, at any time, and using any device [18].

# 3.4.2 End-to-End Connectivity and Accessibility

With the help of new IoT innovations, such as healthcare mobility solutions, and also cutting-edge medical facilities, the Internet of Things (IoT) offers effective information exchange, interoperability, and AI collaboration between machines. With the use of connectivity standards like connectivity via Bluetooth LE, wireless internet, Z-wave, and ZigBee in addition to other contemporary protocols, healthcare experts may alter the way they detect patient illnesses and afflictions and variety of medical diseases. As a result, a technology-driven healthcare system reduces unnecessary visits, makes better use of better resources, and improves resource allocation and planning, all of which help to minimize healthcare costs [7].
