# 4.3 Literature Review

VDD has emerged as worldwide issue for health care across every age bracket [7]. Lacking vitamin D, 10% up to 15% of the calcium consumed through food gets absorbed, but calcium absorption is between 30% to 40% with Vitamin D deficiency. Ultraviolet B radiation from sunlight penetrates the skin and helps generate precursors to generate Vitamin D [8]. VDD has additionally been connected to cardiovascular diseases and other disorders [9].

VDD has become an important focus of research discussion in the last twenty years because of changes in the behavioral patterns of the Population. In Western society, increased use of sunscreen and reduced physical activity levels with increased time indoors [10] are are other contributors to VDD. In a study in the sunshine-rich Augusta, Georgia, USA, low vitamin D levels were typical among teens by decreased physical activity and fitness levels [11].

# 4.3.1 Vitamin D Insufficiency Definition

The serum 25(OH)D value is a marker in Vitamin D assessment. A person who is insufficient in vitamin D should have the value of Serum 25(OH) D within or below the 50 nmol/L-75 nmol/L range. A value 25 or 30 nmol/L is a severe vitamin D deficient state that markedly raises the probability of dietary risks [12].

# 4.3.2 The Incidence of Inadequate Vitamin D

In Western society, a significant lack of sunshine rates is 5.9% in the United States, 7.4% in Canada, and 13% in Europe. In comparison, inadequate levels of vitamin D rates are 24% (US), 37% (Canada), and 40% (Europe). In countries like India, Pakistan, and Afghanistan, more than 20% of the Population is severely vitamin D deficient. India is densely populated, and about 490 million individuals are estimated to have insufficient vitamin D [13].

An exhaustive analysis highlighted that only one-third of LMICs, including India, had good quality published studies on vitamin status, which too were limited to some geographical pockets.
