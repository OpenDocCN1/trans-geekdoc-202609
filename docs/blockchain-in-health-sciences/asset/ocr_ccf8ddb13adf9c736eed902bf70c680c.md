# 19.2 Overview of Blockchain Technology

Blockchains are a type of DLT that uses a distributed ledger to keep track of events in order. This is different from regular databases. Even though they work a lot, blockchain and DLT are two different platforms [9]. Instead of one person being in charge of all the data in a distributed ledger, a team of peers oversees the database all nodes in the network. One distinguishing feature of blockchains compared to other types of distributed ledger technology is the presence of data blocks, which serve as distinct data structures. Hashgraph and DAG are two additional contemporary applications of DLT that are readily apparent in the world [11].

The original open-source decentralized digital currency system, bitcoin, is based on blockchain technology, a groundbreaking innovation [12]. Blockchain is a system that allows nodes in a decentralized network to record and verify transactions on a common distributed ledger. The recorded transactions are secured by arranging them into blocks and using a combination of cryptographic algorithms. After that, a consensus technique is used to verify the blocks, and only those blocks that are verified are added to the growing chain of blocks. Having numerous parties, some of whom may not naturally trust one another, agree on the state of a shared record is no longer an issue; the first consensus technique to be implemented within blockchain technology was proof-of-work (PoW), which was used in bitcoin. Critics have pointed out that the Proof of Work (PoW) consensus mechanism uses too much energy and confirms blocks too slowly. Practical Byzantine Tolerance (PBFT) and Proof-of-Stake (PoS) are two protocols that were developed to overcome some of the problems of PoW [13].
