The administration of electronic health records offers a transparent method for securely sharing electronic medical records among healthcare practitioners, ensuring data accuracy, accessibility, and privacy. In healthcare, there are many aspects of a patient’s journey, from operation to billing, where blockchain can help with increased inspection and provide greater control over the information that flows from one to another along a predetermined path [10, 26, 29]. There are three forms of blockchain: public, private, and consortium, which are employed in various areas of healthcare. A public blockchain is a decentralized procedure that allows anyone to join the network without authorization and participate in the process using smart contract. This kind of blockchain can address inefficient operations for valid transactions. It validates the blocks by consensus proof of work or stake. However, public blockchains have slow action processing times and high energy consumption, which might affect scalability and environmental circumstances. On the other hand, private blockchains function in constrained environments such as closed networks or are controlled by one organization. They are smaller and a more regulated environment than their public versions, making them perfect for handling healthcare organizational procedures such as supply chain management, asset ownership monitoring, and voting mechanisms. Private blockchains provide more privacy, data control, and transaction speed but lack decentralized systems. Furthermore, consortium blockchains are partly decentralized and a combination of both private and public blockchains, with confident entity models and robust encryption methods required for transaction verification and confirmation [4, 30–32]. Medical data exchange is a difficult procedure; consortium blockchains are used for security, scalability, and efficiency [32]. Blockchain offers numerous uses and functionalities that make it easier to transfer patient records safely. Data privacy, data management, and other concerns are all included in technical aspects of blockchain. Blockchain empowers patients to initiate transparency by making medical data accessible [33, 34]. Furthermore, it can provide data storage facilities that keep health history, allowing doctors to easily retrieve information to make accurate diagnoses and recommendations.
