# 12.2 Salient Features of Pharmaceutical Supply Chain

The authors in [11] have studied seven major U.S based supply chain management (SCM) corporations that operate for manufacturing, technology, retailing, and financial services. The authors depicted the limitations of the present system of entries in financial ledger and ERP systems through a case study of a sample transaction. The sample transaction involves a retailer and a bank. The retailer collects a product from a supplier, and the bank needs working-capital for the supplier to fill the order. The state-of-the-art financial-ledger record-keeping systems, manual audits, and inspections are unable to smoothly connect information flows, inventory flows, and financial flows that are present in any transaction of an SCM system. Therefore, eliminating errors in execution process, improving data-driven decision-making, and resolving supply chain conflicts becomes difficult.

Real-time detection of operation errors such as missing shipments, errors in inventory records, and erroneous double or more repeated payments for the same become impossible in widely used ERP systems and financial ledger record-keeping systems. When a discrepancy is discovered, even then, it is costly and hard to pinpoint its origin or resolve the issue by tracking the flow of activities recorded documentation in ledger entries and other documents. Even if some ERP systems are able to capture various types of transactional flows, which are challenging to analyze and pinpoint which ledger entries such as payable accounts, exact amount of payments over repeated transactions, credit accounts for returns, and so correlated to which inventory transactions. This scenario is applicable for large-scale enterprises involved in huge volume of transactions each day throughout an extremely large network of stakeholders, actors and products within the supply chain.
