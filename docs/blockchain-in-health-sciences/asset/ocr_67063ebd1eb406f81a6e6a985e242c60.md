The challenges they pose are growing in tandem with the exponential growth of genomic data and genomic technology. Can you tell who has access to the data and what safeguards are in place to keep them safe? How can we use and share data responsibly so that researchers and patients in the future can reap the benefits of sharing? When dealing with genetic material, the importance of maintaining anonymity be considered alongside the necessity of sharing [3]. Some people believe that those who have benefited from genetic testing should be required by law to divulge this information to the public [4].

Genomic data possess distinct attributes that distinguish them from conventional health. Firstly, they have longevity as they retain valuable information even after the individual's demise. Secondly, they have an impact on the descendants and relatives of the data owner. Lastly, the size of genomic data is substantial, particularly when the entire genome is sequenced, with BAM files reaching a size of 138 GB [5]. Utilizing genomic data for patient diagnosis, screening, and treatment has significant benefits, despite the fact that improper use of genetic information might have far-reaching societal effects. Genetic data storage and dissemination [6] are the main technical issues highlighted in the literature. For genetic data acquisition, analysis, and exchange, there are no need for efficient methods, but most importantly, secure and privacy-preserving technological frameworks. Metadata describe the data and everything needed to access them, including their raw format and notations, but there are no universally accepted norms and conditions for this information. This creates another impediment. These issues hinder data exchange and reduce the efficacy of the process of discovering new information [7].

Blockchain is a decentralized technology that has the potential to revolutionize data management in the healthcare business, particularly in the field of genetic research [8]. The blockchain enables the storage and processing of extensive amounts of genetic data without relying on a single point of failure, thanks to the utilization of several nodes.

Quite few companies have seen the light and are using blockchain technology for this same reason. Integrating with a wide variety of analytical tools is made easy with blockchain-trustworthy and versatile platform. On the other hand, its peer-to-peer architecture has several limitations, as a lack of storage space for data and high consensus costs [8].
