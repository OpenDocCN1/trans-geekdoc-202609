# 19.2.2 Smart Contracts

As a consequence of Ethereum's popularity, smart contracts on the blockchain have been on the rise. But the concept of smart contracts was first proposed by Szabo in 1997 [17]. The idea has evolved since its start, but at its core it means letting a smart contract software run on a decentralized network and change the system's state independently, and verifiably without any third parties getting in the way. The notion has successfully implemented and utilized in various domains like finance, identity management, and healthcare; thanks to the advent of blockchain technology [18]. Multiple blockchain systems exist that facilitate the execution of smart contracts in various programming languages, with solidity being one of the most often used languages. The process of creating a smart contract consists of delineating a sequence of conditions and directives that, when fulfilled, initiate an automated response. Smart contracts possess numerous benefits in comparison to conventional contracts, including autonomous execution, public verification, and the elimination of intermediaries [18].

# 19.2.3 Security and Privacy of Blockchain Technology

Using several cryptographic techniques is crucial to blockchain's security and privacy features. While earlier iterations of the Bitcoin blockchain made use of specific methods, subsequent iterations of the blockchain made use of other strategies to enhance privacy and security. By utilizing its core security and privacy features, the blockchain ensures that the system meets the requirements for secure and private online transactions. By using a pre-established consensus procedure, blockchain ensures that ledger consistency is preserved across several nodes. Both validating miners and outside attackers trying to modify blockchain transactions cannot alter them. Digital signatures and cryptographic hashing make it possible to detect tampering with transaction data by checking the validity of the digital signature [17].

In addition, because the distributed ledger of transactions is maintained by all nodes within the network, altering blockchain transactions requires changing the data contained in every node. Due to its distributed ledger technology, the blockchain network is impervious to distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks. By utilizing public key infrastructure (PKI) pseudonymity, blockchain technology guarantees privacy. Instead of using real identities, the public addresses are used to identify the nodes or users.
