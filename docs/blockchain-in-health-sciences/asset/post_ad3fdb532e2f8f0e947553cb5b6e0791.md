# 7.3.5 Blockchain with AI Applications in Healthcare

There are numerous applications of blockchain and AI in healthcare, including IoT, supply chain management, telemedicine, data management, remote patient monitoring, and personalized healthcare.

# 7.3.5.1 Electronic Health Records

Patients' data is gathered and managed through a dynamic network provided by electronic health records. Then, it maintains the data management. This technology facilitates the hospital's easy access to data and facilitates patient data sharing [21]. These data can be utilized to enhance clinical trial performance metrics and analyze the disease. Additionally, it assists in the analysis of how the research and discoveries have improved results.

Since it contains patient personally identifiable information (PII), this data needs to be secured [15]. Here, blockchain technology offers features including interoperability, adding, viewing, and removing data in addition to securely updating data. Medshare is an example of a protected electronic health record (EHR); it describes a blockchain-based system that facilitates information sharing between data managers and data accessing parties. It incorporates activity tracking, auditing, monitoring of shared medical data, and data provenance. It keeps track of user activities via access control systems and smart contracts [16]. Similar to this, Vora et al. proposed Bheem, a blockchain-based EHR management system, in [26]. Since patient data may be dispersed around several organizations, the institutions must have easy access to the stored data. Blockchain's interoperability capability aids... the proposal for sharing medical data via blockchain and cloud computing technology is mentioned in [39].

When paired with AI technologies, EHRs are beneficial. With the use of EHR data, Early Warning Systems (EWS) seek to perform better, and accurately anticipate acute critical disease [27]. To get quantitative data from various tests in a visual summary, NLP and machine learning techniques have been applied to EHRs [17, 18]. Medical records can be analyzed by AI, which can then provide physicians with information. AI algorithms may utilize EHR to predict the possibility of a disease by considering the patient's previous medical records in addition to the medical history of their family. AI systems are trained on enormous amounts of data, and in the process, the algorithm correlates the outcomes to the last diagnosis by developing a set of rules. AI may perform a perspective on a patient based on past experiences when it receives new patient information in the future [28].

# 7.3.5.2 Supply Chain Management

Ensuring the provenance of medicinal supplies to determine their legality is a major concern in the healthcare industry. Thousands of deaths annually are caused by prescription pharmaceuticals in developing nations, making this a serious issue for the industry [7]. As more remote health monitoring is used, it is growing with time.

However, throughout the past ten years, there have been several breakthroughs in the delivery of medications. These days, medication distribution is managed by implantable microchips [41]. AI and ANN can be used to improve performance. AI-based platforms have the ability to search through a variety of pharmaceuticals and analyze biological data to discover prospective targets for drug delivery, which can lead to a more effective and better drug delivery procedure [29]. Drug delivery systems based on machine learning are excellent at the speed at which data is processed and analyzed, the potential for operating at the point of care through mobile embedded software, the capacity to learn from novel and unidentified microorganisms, the ability to learn features in a large and complex database; and the ability to create new rules and recognize unexpected patterns [19]. Moreover, effective medication distribution uses many of ML approaches. Pharmaceutical businesses use blockchain technology to monitor their supply chain and guarantee the security of their products.

Abbas et al. presents a system in [20] that employs a blockchain-based supply chain management system to track the delivery process and a machine learning system that uses LightGBM and N-gram models to recommend drugs to the consumer. In a similar vein, a framework for optimizing medicine distribution was offered to preserve supply and demand equilibrium [30]. # 7.3.5.3 Chronic Disease

At this time, managing chronic diseases comprises both active monitoring of the condition and passive treatment of the illness. Wearable technologies, AI, and blockchain all assist in the real-time monitoring of chronic illness [31, 43, 44]. The patient used to need advice, status evaluations, and patient monitoring in the past. These days, decentralized wearables, digital devices, and mobile apps have improved the collecting of personal health and physical data.

To provide extensive, constant, and proactive treatment for patients with persistent illnesses and to arrive at a scientific strategy for management that the development of a healthy condition, to prevent the spread of illness, and the lower rate of disability, AI can help enhance the efficacy of healthcare delivery. It may additionally collect the experience of nutritionists, chemists, and doctors [32]. Patients receive better care when wearable technology is combined with AI techniques. However, there is a problem with managing chronic illnesses and analyzing their various factors while maintaining patient's privacy [22]. The validation of transparency and testing is lacking in many deep learning algorithms.

Data sharing between different medical units may be restricted due to security concerns. The blockchain solution relies on smart contracts to regulate wearable device co-chain data, preserving patient privacy. Additionally, it depends upon the transfer of value and cross-chain communication of data from multiple blockchain systems utilizing a single module for consensus [25]. The incorporation of machine learning, wearable technology, and blockchain-based technologies could enhance automated processes, privacy, security of information, and the overall efficacy of handling persistent illnesses [33, 45].
