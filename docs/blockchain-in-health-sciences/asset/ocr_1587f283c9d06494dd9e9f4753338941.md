# 19.6 Current Blockchain Technological Applications in Genomics

Currently, multiple companies utilize blockchain technology for the storing of genetic data. One example of such a corporation is genomes which is a DNA and health data bank that enables users to have their genetic information sequenced and stored on their own platform. This is achieved through the use of homomorphic encryption, which ensures that only the data provider has access to their own data. Data providers have the option to grant anonymous permission to specific buyers to view their data in exchange for GENE tokens, which can be converted into currency. Luna DNA, another company, offers a similar platform but stands out by distributing shares of their data to contributors. This allows users to share in the profits from research conducted on Luna DNA's database. Nebula Genomics is yet another company that employs blockchain technology to give individuals control over their genetic data.

The founders of Nebula Genomics state that privacy and cost deter people from sequencing their genome [30]. With the blockchain and homomorphic encryption, Nebula Genomics, like genomes.io, allows owners to anonymously and securely share their data in exchange for financial rewards [30]. To address the cost of genomic sequencing, Nebula sequences one's genome as low as $99 [30]. More, Nebula Genomics offers some customers free sequencing services; thanks to partnerships with companies like EMD Serono [30]. EMD Serono is using the data on Nebula Genomic's blockchain to advance their lung cancer research this partnership can provide valuable information and help researchers accelerate medical developments [30]. DNATix also offers a blockchain that can anonymously and securely store and sell genomics data [31]. It is the first company to upload DNA sequences on a blockchain and uses a proprietary algorithm that can significantly compress data (99% compression rate) [31].
