Apart from the benefits for patients, IoT technology can also be used to streamline hospital operations and drug supply. Large hospitals face many problems in the administration of their departments, resources, and supplies. IoT can provide solutions for this by delivering timely and accurate information as well as performing predictive analysis. Efficient resource utilization and avoidance of inventory loss can be achieved by integrating sensors into hospital beds, wheelchairs, and other medical equipment. It can act similar to an asset tracking system, allowing real-time monitoring of the location, status, and use of hospital equipment. The use of IoT devices in hospitals can also improve patient mobility by providing real-time data on bed occupancy, staff distribution, and patient waiting times. Despite the many benefits of IoT technology in healthcare, it is very important to ensure the security of the overall system. All information, whether it is related to the patient or the hospital management, must be confidential and only authorized persons should have access to it. Patients' data is highly sensitive, and no one wants to get it in public domain, especially if they are suffering from leprosy, HIV, or other socially stigmatized diseases. In addition, hospital management information must be kept from unauthorized access and alteration; otherwise, they can cause the hospital's services to fail. Also, all the interconnected IoT devices must be properly authenticated because any of them starts behaving in a malicious manner, it may result in incorrect patient treatment or the lapse of the hospital's services. Therefore, confidentiality and integrity of data and information, authenticity of IoT devices and proper authorization of users are essential for IoT environments in healthcare.

## 18.2 Foundations of IoT Based Healthcare

The essential elements of IoT-based healthcare are: 1) interconnected sensors, 2) communication technologies, and 3) health data analytics. Interconnected medical sensors are a set of physical devices that communicate with one another via wireless network. They capture data from the human body, such as pulse rate, humidity, oxygen level, and other health traits, and send it to the cloud or a centralized server that physicians may also access. They come in wearable form and are easily attached to the patient's body. These devices require low computing power, which are typically wireless, long-lasting, energy-efficient, and affordable. They continually monitor human health without interruption. The sensed data is incorporated into an electronic health record and kept on a server for future decision-making.

All medical sensors perform local data processing to prevent the transmission of unnecessary information to the server. The processed data is subsequently transmitted to the IoT gateway via short-range communication protocols such as Bluetooth, Zigbee, or Near Field Communication (NFC). Subsequently, the IoT gateway connects to the internet and sends the received data to the server for storage and further processing. After accessing the information from the central server, the physician or doctor makes an informed decision. When transmitting data to the cloud or retrieving from the cloud, it is essential to incorporate security measures to ensure the confidentiality of communication and restrict access to authorized individuals only.

Although the medical sensors handle data processing locally, further processing or advanced analytics are still necessary to facilitate the physician's decision-making process. Due to the limited capacity and resource limitations of the devices, it is advisable to utilize AI tools and analytics software in alternative locations such as the cloud or other servers. The analysis process should be conducted with efficiency to enable prompt response or educated decision-making based on the available facts.

## 18.3 IoT Solutions in Health Monitoring

Researchers have thus presented numerous solutions in the literature for healthcare monitoring based on IoT technology. These solutions fall into three categories: care support systems, illness detection and prediction systems, and recommendation systems [1]. These solutions make use of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), deep learning, fuzzy logic, deep convolutional neural networks, image pattern analysis, machine learning, mathematical models, and other methods. A care support system monitors or tracks an individual's health and transmits the corresponding data to a centralized system. In this regard, Jeong and Shin [2] proposed a health monitoring system that can be installed in a vehicle, not limited to an ambulance. This system monitors the health of a patient during transportation and provides doctors detailed information about the patient's condition before they arrive at the hospital. This allows doctors to plan the treatment in advance, minimizing any potential delays. Jabbar et al. [3] and Ullah et al. [4] developed their own frameworks to achieve semantic interoperability across various heterogeneous devices utilized in patient monitoring. Ramirez López et al. [5] introduced a system to track the health of individuals experiencing respiratory problems. Vadaei et al. [6] developed a framework for monitoring an individual's health status in a COVID-19 environment and alerting them when necessary to maintain a safe distance. Ghashemi et al. [7] put a short home architecture that incorporates medication management and health monitoring capabilities for elderly patients. The patient monitoring system developed by Sahu et al. [8] transmits data to the cloud. In addition, the system provides an alert in the event of an emergency. An alternative monitoring system, as proposed by Bushang [9], acquires ECG data of distant patients in real-time and transmits it to the cloud. Rahman et al. [10] introduced a framework for the remote monitoring of patients with co-morbidities that have recovered from COVID. The devices acquire patient data and transmit it to a cloud. Ali et al. [11] developed a software that facilitated the transmission of patient-related data from various peripheral sensors to the cloud for the purpose of remote health monitoring. It conveys the patient's vital indicators to the physician as well.

Illness detection systems utilize IoT technology to identify health issues that may be present in patients.
