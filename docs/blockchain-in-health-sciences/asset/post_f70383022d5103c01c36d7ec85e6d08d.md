## 15.3.6 Safety and Inherent Security

Because of its inherent design flaws, traditional medication supply chain management makes secure and reliable transmission of critical information challenging. It is generally easy to change, remove, or otherwise tamper with data. Tracing and tracking capabilities meet regulatory criteria, while cryptography technology boosts medication security [31]. A higher level of security is maintained to prevent theft and the introduction of counterfeit pharmaceuticals. ## 15.8 Blockchain: Distributed Ledger Technology

A cryptographic one-way hash function generates the so-called hash, which links each block in the blockchain. It also ensures the anonymity, immutability, and tightness of the block [36, 37]. Nodes sign all the transactions before sending them out to the network for confirmation. The integrity and validity of a transaction may be guaranteed by using a digital signature that uses the private key. The first is that, signing the transaction requires a specific private key, and the second is that, decryption cannot proceed in the event of a data transmission failure. In networks that use proof-of-work or proof-of-stake as its consensus method, a group of nodes called miners collect all the transactions that have been distributed and accepted by the network into time-stamped blocks. After the blocks are broadcast to the network, the validation nodes check them. This is accomplished by referencing the previous block in the chain, and using the hash associated with the block to ensure that the blocks they have received legitimate transactions. Once both conditions are met, the nodes will add the block to the blockchain. If the conditions are not met, the obstacle is removed.

## 15.9 Conclusion

Many industries are looking for new technologies, such as blockchain, to brighten their future. There has been a recent shift in blockchain research towards its primary applications in healthcare, which includes health records, access control, and data interchange; applications like supply chain management and pharmaceutical administration are presently rare. Thus, blockchain offers a great deal of promise that has yet to be realized. Internet of Things (IoT)-enabled smart healthcare systems face significant security challenges and threats. To lessen these dangers one must understand the security of such systems. The security requirements of an IoT-enabled healthcare system cannot be met by conventional security measures due to inherent vulnerabilities, high initial investment, restricted scalability, and limited resources of IoT devices. Another is that blockchain is not perfect. Blockchain technology has an issue with scalability.
