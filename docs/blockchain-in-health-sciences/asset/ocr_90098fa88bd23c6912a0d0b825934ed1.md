# 15.3.6 Safety and inherent Security

Because of its inherent design flaws, traditional medication supply chain management makes secure and reliable transmission of critical information challenging. It is generally easy to change, remove, or otherwise tamper with data. Tracing and tracking capabilities meet regulatory criteria, while cryptography technology boosts medication security [31]. A higher level of security is maintained to prevent theft and the introduction of counterfeit pharmaceuticals. Decreased unauthorized drug modifications also deter pharmaceutical stakeholders from altering drugs in a way that lowers their quality [14].

# 15.4 Data Governance

The immutable ledger technology known as blockchain offers data-centric security for systems that handle sensitive transactions, needing fully auditable, immutable tracking methods. Blockchain and data governance (DG) are complementary in many respects, even if they maintain independent application domains and operational philosophies. Utilizing blockchain and IoT technologies has been advantageous for data governance and supply chains [32]. Regulatory compliance for medicinal items has also been ensured through the use of the IoT [33].

# 15.5 Data Quality

Utilizing sensor devices in every box, the Internet of Things (IoT) has been used to comply with GDP regulations and ensure quality while medical goods are being transported (e.g., temperature control) [17]. To get around these problems, blockchain technology ensures that all data is of high quality. Once all nodes verify that the data are authentic, the new block is accepted. However, the addition of blocks with poor quality ratings will not be allowed. For a blockchain to process a user's transaction, it must perform several specialized, controlled functions. The purpose of these is to verify the legitimacy of the transaction. Every node on the blockchain receives an immediate update when this transaction is published.
