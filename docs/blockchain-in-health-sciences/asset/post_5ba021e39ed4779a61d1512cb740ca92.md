## 20.2.1 Blockchain Technology in Financial Services

Probably one of the most extended applications of BCT used across the world is processing cross-border transactions. Indeed, transfer systems across borders have always been expensive and time-consuming in centralized systems. Contrary to this, in decentralized ledger systems, cross-border transaction processing and verification can happen instantaneously, even when they occur in time zones apart.

Another notable use is the smart bond technology. Now, with the power of smart contractual technology, it is possible to have intelligent bond contracts—popularly known as “smart bonds”—utilizing BCT for registration services. One advantage of these smart bonds is the ability to settle transactions instantly. Blockchain technology is being used by Point-of-Sale (POS) systems to allow customers and shops to accept Bitcoin payments. This adoption helps with cash management by reducing expensive payment processing solutions and excessive card costs.

## 20.2.2 Blockchain Technology in Banking

This blockchain technology is slowly reaching adoption in trade finance, replacing paper-based letters of credit with a distributed ledger. This means traditional paper-based letters of credit can be replaced by blockchain. Through this, exporters, importers, and banks can share information hassle-free among themselves in their network. This possibility of managing transactions independently of a third party can reduce processing times from a number of days to a few hours.

This, of course, is not without the risks of possible cyberattacks on international payment networks. Ripple blockchain technology empowers financial institutions to move money around the world more quickly, more securely, and more safely. The existing banking system in FX requires several records for different participants, which means reconciliation across multiple systems must be done continuously. By doing away with the requirement for separate trade records, BCT frees up resources and offers a shared perspective on trades. Because it interacts smoothly with all trading sources and venues, there are instant efficiency and cost-saving benefits. License fees, ticketing fees, staff costs, and IT overheads are all eliminated.

## 20.2.3 Blockchain Technology in the Insurance Sector

Blockchain technology has a lot of prospects for improving health insurance and healthcare provider services. Health insurance operations can be handled effectively, securely, and transparently with the use of distributed ledger technology (DLT) and robust data analytics. Both medical facilities and patients gain from this. For the automobile insurance market, blockchain technology can decrease paperwork, accelerate processes, streamline writing, and secure record information on auto repairs and damages.

As a result, reasonable insurance rates are obtained, and accident claim processing is expedited. Similar improvements can be made to the life insurance industry, such as the elimination of paperwork, increased productivity, and improved transparency in death claims and beneficiary money transfers. DLT can bridge numerous stakeholders, in the life insurance ecosystem. They include policyholders, beneficiaries, insurance companies, and government entities. Adanur Dedetur B et al. [9], reviewed blockchain applications in genetic studies and health services, covering various aspects as cost analysis, ownership, authorization, and security. Gamze Gürsey et al. [7], created SAMChain, a special blockchain network for on-chain nested database storing next-generation sequencing reads and personal genomic variants, using the MultiChain platform/API. SAMChain is the first publicly available proof-of-concept private blockchain network that makes simple to store and retrieve personal genetic variants (VCF files) and raw genomic data (SAM files). By using reference-based compression and indexing techniques, they minimized on-chain data and also produced tools for fast access and analysis.

Mohammed Alhazwi et al. [42], proposed DARC —Decentralized Anonymous Researcher Credentials- decentralized, privacy-preserving approach to credentialing for access to federated genomic data. DARC uses smart contracts and zero-knowledge proofs to augment consideration and privacy by allowing researchers to combine and change their credentials. This protocol enables researchers to show their credentials to computing services or realty files on a selective basis by disposing of the need for mediators. Using an evidence-of-concept implementation, it showcases the usefulness of DARC and highlights how nicely it handles decentralized credentials and scalability-disturb scenarios.

The research by Gal Zarchi et al. [43], uses blockchain to improve incentives, privacy, and access to DNA databases. It highlights the importance of standardization and potential biases in genomic databases and distinguishes blockchains for big data management. The authors describe an encrypted GWAS experiment that uses multi-key homologous encryption to improve record storage. Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) are used to control access, utilize standards, integrate genetic information anonymously, and facilitate exchange. The method addresses moral, legal, and social issues, ensuring the secure storage of genetic records and the usage of private blockchains and NFTs. In addition, it provides a decentralized and profitable record-sharing system that is also transparent.
