# 19.5 Incorporation of Blockchain Technology
into Genomics

Blockchain technology can efficiently handle the computational and stor-
age of large genomic databases. Currently, the computational power
used for mining and securing the bitcoin blockchain is extremely high,
surpassing even the computing power of the Berkeley Open Infrastructure
for Network Computing, which is the most powerful scientific comput-
tion network globally. This demonstrates that the vast network of com-
ers in a blockchain can be utilized for processing, exchanging, and
storing genomic data. Miners can be motivated by cryptocurrency rewards
to contribute their computational resources for this purpose. Within the
blockchain, encrypted copies of genomic data will be distributed across
multiple nodes, eliminating the risk of a single point of failure. Since
genomic data contains personal and sensitive information, individuals nat-
rally have privacy concerns regarding companies that collect it [2].

Creating a public blockchain to store this genomic data can effectively
tackle these problems. A public blockchain is a decentralized system
where the data is not owned or controlled by a third party. Instead, it gives
ownership of genomic data to the contributors themselves. During the
verification process, nodes in the network can access the data on public
blockchains, but the data can be encrypted using homomorphic encryp-
tion to protect the privacy of the contributors. Homomorphic encryption
