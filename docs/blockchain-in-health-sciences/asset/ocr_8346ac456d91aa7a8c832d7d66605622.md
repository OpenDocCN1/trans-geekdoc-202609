The emergence of blockchain-enabled health information exchanges, which would make it easier for medical personnel to handle electronic medical records, is another potential future trend. # 5.8 Conclusion

The current research is conducted to explore the perception of UG students (engineering and general-degree colleges) regarding blockchain technology and its impact on healthcare and data security in West Bengal, India. After analyzing the data, the results indicate that the overall perception of UG students shows that 73% have a moderate or average level of understanding, while very few students (10%) have a high level of perception, and the remaining students (17%) have a low level of perception regarding blockchain technology. When analyzing the overall UG student population (289 students), we can see that perceptions of blockchain technology significantly differ based on location and stream. In the analysis of general college students (130 students), perceptions significantly differ based on location, semester, and category (General, ST, SC, and OBC), but no significant differences are found regarding gender. Similarly, when the authors analyzed the data from engineering students (159), they also found significant differences in perception based on location. Several variables indicate that UG students from both Engineering and general-degree colleges have significantly different perceptions regarding blockchain technology as it relates to healthcare and data privacy.
