As crucial components in disease diagnosis, treatment, and prevention, counterfeit drugs have surged as an industry, allegedly resulting in between 100,000 and 1,000,000 deaths annually. Aside from impacting legitimate drug manufacturers' reputations and revenues, preventing counterfeit products from entering the market is crucial for patient safety. The distinctive attributes of blockchain technology, particularly its decentralized, transparent, trustworthy, anonymous, and stable nature, have marked it as a promising tool in combating the distribution of counterfeit drugs within the pharmaceutical industry. Blockchains offer the potential to track pharmaceutical origins, drug transportation, and raw material procurement. Additionally, by reducing the number of intermediaries involved, blockchain technology holds the promise of cost reduction and enhanced safety measures within the pharmaceutical process [11–14].

#  10.1.1 Problem Statement

The pharmaceutical industry confronts a pervasive challenge characterized by a lack of transparency and trust across the entire lifecycle of pharmaceutical products, spanning production, sales, and distribution. Complex and global supply chains are susceptible to unexpected disruptions. Even seemingly minor issues deep within this chain, such as the inadvertent use of incorrect components by a supplier’s supplier, can escalate into significant problems once the product reaches the final stage of production. Traditional supply chain processes involve reviewing invoices, reconciling shipments, initiating refunds for breaches in temperature or quality during transportation, and executing payments upon product arrival. However, this method is riddled with time-consuming procedures, high costs, delays, and errors. These inefficiencies not only hamper business processes but also directly impact consumers, potentially leading to the delivery of poor-quality medicines with harmful consequences. Moreover, consumers historically had minimal insight into the pharmaceutical supply chain due to a lack of awareness regarding its complexities and processes. When ordering pharmaceutical products, consumers frequently do not know the origin, the manufacturing circumstances, or the estimated delivery time. On one hand, workers in both developed and third-world countries are joining the fight to stop fraud with medical supplies by validating incomplete pharmaceuticals in hospitals [15, 16].
