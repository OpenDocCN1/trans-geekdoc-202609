In India, Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO) is responsible for the same. In 2018, CDSCO has published guidelines on good-distribution practices for restoring the good quality of pharmaceutical products throughout the entire distribution phase comprising procurement, purchasing, storing, distributing, transportation, record-keeping practices and further documentation [7]. The Department of Pharmaceuticals (DOP) of India, operating under the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, emphasizes its focus and momentum on the innovation and growth of pharmaceuticals sector in the country. Additionally, it puts stress on regulating critical aspects related to (i) pricing control such as availability of medicines at affordable prices, (ii) research and development of life-saving and generic medicine, (iii) protection of intellectual property rights of pharmaceutical products and (iv) complying with international pharmaceuticals sector obligation [8, 9]. The vision of DOP is to promote India as the global leader in medicine research and development, and to ensure accessibility, affordability, and cost control of pharmaceutical products, and medical devices within the country.

The quality of pharmaceutical products continues to be an implicitly high-valued point in any pharmaceutical industry due to its direct impact on human lives [1]. So, exporting pharmaceutical products by developing countries, as India goes through greater scrutiny that comes from global developed country-based regulatory agencies, as FDA. On the other hand, import bans on specific ingredients that helps in making pharmaceutical products, plant shutdowns, and critical monitoring by global regulatory agencies in the value chain for quality control have a light on export figures, which affects the credibility of the pharmaceutical companies, and hence their revenues as well. At national level, CDSCO of India has expanded the coverage of sites and frequency of inspections to mitigate the marketing and distribution of sub-standard pharmaceutical products. At present, India has nearly 500 FDA-approved sites. The global regulatory agencies often inspect these sites to address various issues such as lacking of data integrity and checking procedures to find data falsification, non-committal to standard operating procedures and data source traceability, significant cross-contamination, and documentation practices and insufficient calibration of equipment and instruments [5].
