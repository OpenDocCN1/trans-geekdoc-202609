## 5.5 Method of the Study

A descriptive survey method has been used in this study. Therefore, various techniques, strategies, and methods typical of descriptive survey research have been employed to collect, analyze, and interpret the data. To achieve this objective, a quantitative research approach was adopted.

### 5.5.1 Population

The population of any study refers to the total collection of elements or participants from which the researcher draws inferences. This study is conducted on UG students of Engineering and general-degree colleges in West Bengal, all UG students from these colleges in West Bengal are identified as the population of the study.

### 5.5.2 Sample and Sampling Procedure

The sample of the study refers to a set of representative individuals or participants from the population of the study. In this study, 289 UG students from both engineering colleges (159 students) and general-degree colleges (130 students) in West Bengal were taken as a representative sample for the whole population. Simple random sampling was used to select the sample.

### 5.5.3 Tools Used

To understand the perception of UG students on healthcare and data security related to blockchain technology, a perception scale was used (constructed by the researchers). The tool was finalized through a process of reliability and validity. For reliability, the test-reest method was used, and for validation, expert validation (by two experts) was employed.

### 5.5.4 Data Collection

Data were collected with the help of Google Forms, from various districts of engineering and general-degree colleges of West Bengal to know the perception of UG students on healthcare and data security of blockchain technology.

### 5.5.5 Statistical Techniques

The mean, SD, t-test, and ANOVA were used to analyze the collected data and verify the hypotheses.

## 5.6 Results and Discussion

Table 5.1 shows the evidence of based on the investigators verified H01(a): The cut-off point is M + 1σ. Mean = 65.26, N = 159, and σ = 5.98. This means that 65.26 + 1 × 5.98 = 71.24 is M + 1σ, and M - 1σ = 65.26 - 1 × 5.98 = 59.28. Most engineering college students (126, which is 79.24% of UG engineering students) scored between 59.28 and 71.24. It can therefore be concluded that the perception of engineering college students is moderate regarding the potentiality of blockchain technology in healthcare and data security.
