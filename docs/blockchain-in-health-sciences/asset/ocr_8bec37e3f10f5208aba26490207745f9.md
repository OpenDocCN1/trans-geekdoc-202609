We, however, will not burden the reader with technical details of blockchain technology in this chapter. Instead, we'll present some basic concepts, features, and terminologies related to blockchain that will help us understand how blockchain technology can be applied to solve challenges related to genetic data.

As shown in Figure 20.2, a blockchain is a kind of distributed ledger or database interlinked by blocks of data protected from manipulation due to cryptographic techniques. The working principle behind blockchain is that it is essentially decentralized and depends on the consent of the users who have a full copy of the blockchain, or lightweight nodes, which the blockchain network can be classified as full nodes, rely on full nodes for blockchain data. When new blocks are added to the blockchain, the data on it keep on growing and become immutable once they are included, only to be removed by consensus.

Here we will briefly describe how blockchain works. As shown in Figure 20.3, a block contains data, a hash of its own, and the previous block's previous hash is usually zero, as there is no block present before it. It is called the Genesis Block. Among the most important jobs of miners, also known as nodes on the blockchain network, are verifying transactions and updating the blockchain ledger of transactions.
