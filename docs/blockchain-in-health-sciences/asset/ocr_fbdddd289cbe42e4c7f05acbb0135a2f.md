# d) Interoperability and Regulatory Laws

The capacity of one blockchain to communicate with another is known as blockchain interoperability. Cross-chain communication messaging protocols, which allow blockchains read from as well as write to other blockchains, constitute the foundation of blockchain interoperability. Since patients may transfer to a different institution for extra medical attention, it is necessary for multiple institutions to exchange the patient’s medical history. Interoperability facilitates sharing and new data addition to medical records. Regulatory rules like ethical safety and standards, identity verification, data protection, financial regulations along with more are necessary for a peaceful communication between two parties. Therefore, they have been incorporated in all of these applications and implementations and are obligatory references. One of the main roadblocks to the adoption of Blockchain technology is the Ownership. As the data can be owned privately and publicly both in the Blockchain, therefore, there is absence of a feeling of authority; technological acts are absent, and several legal and regulatory law diff arise [5]. A lack of assurance between the many parties involved in the information flow across the various health organizations result in obstacles to the information flow. Furthermore, smaller healthcare organizations might not be able to afford the early setup costs associated with a blockchain-based system, even though they are more affordable in the long run.
