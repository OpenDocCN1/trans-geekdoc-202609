It is important to highlight that blockchain was advocated for all the publications included in this analysis, as opposed to distributed ledger technology (DLT). Despite the potential applications of DLT in the Internet of Things (IoT) and other domains, its incorporation appears to have been neglected in genomic investigations. To evaluate the potential of Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) in genomics, additional research is necessary. Advancements in off-chain computations utilizing zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) offer considerable potential for the expansion of blockchain-based applications. Off-chain computation is a state transition paradigm in which a node executes computations off-chain and verifies those computations with a Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP). The resulting state is subsequently chain-stored. An alternative approach, known as Arbitrum, verifies off-chain computations through incentive-based means [48].

Our contention is that further off-chain computation methods may prove advantageous in the field of genomics, given the substantial volume of genomic data and the computationally intensive nature of genomic research. Moreover, despite receiving insufficient attention, emerging technologies like Verifiable Credentials (VCs) [49] and Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) [49] have the potential to inspire confidence. In addition to safeguarding the confidentiality and integrity of information, constructing a decentralized trust system using blockchain technology can help overcome additional obstacles. The utilization of trust-over-IP principles in genomic applications [50] has the potential to enhance the dependability of data exchange in scientific inquiries. The application of blockchain technology in genomics is in its nascent stages, and there exist numerous unexplored potential applications. A fundamental transformation of the existing genomic ecosystem is anticipated to occur as a result of the implementation of blockchain-based solutions.
