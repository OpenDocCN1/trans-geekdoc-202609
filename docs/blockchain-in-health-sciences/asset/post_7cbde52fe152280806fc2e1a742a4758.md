## 2.2 IoT Health Sciences Framework

In today's world IoT has become very useful in healthcare applications as it provides innovative solutions for the treatment and detection of various health problems. During the spread of disease COVID, IoT has provided various services in the healthcare field by handling a large dataset along with providing various health services [6]. Consequently, to meet the requirements of patients for remote medical monitoring and long-term care while maintaining an acceptable standard of living, a practical, comprehensive, and computer-assisted technology is essential. The sanitary system has been transformed by IoT through the analysis of massive amounts of data and the integration of numerous IoT devices that gather real-time physiological data of patients into an intelligent and predictive system. Patient-centric medical services, including continuous monitoring of critical conditions and early disease detection, are the primary objective [7]. An IoT healthcare system establishes a network connecting all resources to facilitate healthcare operations, including remote interventions, monitoring, and diagnosis, via the Internet.

### 2.2.1 Perception Layer

Perception layer is used to sense the data using various sensors and medical devices such as infrared sensors, cameras, GPS, RFID, and smart device sensors. The motive of these sensors is to collect the data from various environments for further processing. IoT is predicated on technologies for identification and perception. Included among the various types of devices capable of detecting environmental changes are sensors and medical devices. The sensors are used for object recognition, geographic recognition, and location recognition, after that these sensors are capable of converting this information into digital signals [9].

The utilization of sensor technologies enables the real-time observation of trends and streamlines the collection of numerous physiological parameters about a patient, thereby expediting the process of diagnosing and administering high-quality treatment. There are numerous examples of IoT sensor devices with the potential to save lives [10-12].

### 2.2.2 Network Layer

IoT technologies operate at the network level, encompassing both wired and wireless networks that facilitate communication and the storage of processed (layer 1) data, which may be centralized or localized. Intercommunication can transpire via low, medium, or high frequencies, with the latter constituting the primary emphasis of the Internet of Things. The aforementioned technologies short-range communication systems, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, RFID, and Zigbee. The communication capabilities of high-frequency fourth-generation networks have been further enhanced. Moreover, the proliferation of developing 5G networks is anticipated to significantly propel the expansion of IoT applications in the healthcare sector, as they can simultaneously connect thousands of devices with dependable connectivity.

### 2.2.3 Middleware Layer

Middleware, serving as the foundational element of the IoT, mitigates numerous previous IoT challenges and improves application development by establishing a connection between applications and devices. Consequently, the primary obstacle is the development of appropriate middleware that satisfies the quality and functionality requirements for integrating heterogeneous hardware and software into an IoT system. In addition to storage, computation, and processing capabilities, the Middleware Layer also possesses action-taking capabilities. It stores the entire data set and gives the device with the appropriate data. It also has the ability to make decisions based on computations on a dataset obtained from sensors.

### 2.2.4 Application Layer

It is the responsibility of the application layer to interpret and implement data, also to give the user application-specific services. Artificial intelligence-based medical applications are among the most promising that the IoT offers. The scientific applications of AI have experienced significant growth. These applications include, but are not limited to drug activity design, gene mutation expression prediction, and image analysis [13]. Artificial intelligence (AI) possesses the capacity to analyze and interpret electronic medical data, encompassing physical, laboratory, treatment, and medication information, to generate treatment and diagnosis possibilities and/or decisions. As an illustration, IBM Watson employs artificial intelligence to analyze images by identifying primary and incidental findings [14].

It is the task of the application layer to give end-users with the user interface and applications interaction with the IoT system. This stratum comprises APIs, mobile and web applications, and dashboards that facilitate the interaction with access to IoT data.

Application layer technologies include web applications, mobile applications, and APIs utilized to interact with IoT data. In the Internet of Things, the application layer is vital because it grants end-users access to and interaction with data produced by IoT devices.

### 2.3.1 Creating Warnings and Monitoring in Real-Time

The ongoing surveillance of diverse health indicators such as temperature, heart rate, and blood oxygen levels plays a crucial role in delivering healthcare-services in real-time. The Internet of Things enables the deployment of sensors on the human body, facilitating the measurement of many parameters. The data can be subjected to analysis to provide recommendations for the appropriate treatment to patients during emergency situations. Electronic health records that continuously record vital signs (heart rate, temperature, and electrocardiogram) were developed by Shanin et al. [16]. This system is very adaptable and requires minimal power consumption. The patients’ RFID technology is employed to identify each individual patient. While GPS location is monitored to facilitate emergency healthcare, the Microcontroller Unit (MCU) employed in this study was the Arduino Uno, where the middleware utilized for data processing was Thingspeak. Swaroop et al. [17] developed a monitoring system that operates in real-time. The Raspberry Pi 3 is used in the gadget, which includes sensors such as the Sunrom blood pressure/heart rate sensor and the DS18B20 thermometer sensor. As health indicators, blood pressure and temperature were measured. Data was gathered and sent via a variety of communication channels, including WiFi, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and GSM.
