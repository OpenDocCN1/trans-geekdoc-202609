Their approach combines a framework for gene fragmentation with a privacy-preserving sharing protocol. Users can create data blocks with any genomic file type using this framework. Then, a decentralized-blockchain network made up of service providers stores these blocks. With a distributed method, there are no longer worries about centralized data storage, where one supplier may have undue control, and it is possible to share, analyze, and conduct collaborative research. To create decentralized-machine learning models, another line of inquiry focuses on fusing blockchain technology and machine learning. Continuing in this spirit, [17] suggests Swarm Learning (SL), a blockchain-based method for safe distributed peer-to-peer peering. SL makes safe participation without a central coordinator possible, which helps with the first phase of decentralized-machine learning. After that, the data are trained, and model parameters are combined to allow control and ownership of substantial amounts of genetic and medical datasets to stay near the data owners.

Secure Storage: A number of the publications that have evaluated examine the use of blockchain technology for the safe storage of genetic data. For example, the work reported in [18] shows that blockchain technology can be used to store and query pharmacogenomic data. The authors gives an example of how to store and retrieve genomic data that work using the Ethereum blockchain and smart contracts. When data items are stored in smart contracts, they are assigned unique identifiers that act as mapping keys when the objects are later retrieved.

This method uses genome-wide, multi-source data extraction to enable quick responses to data queries. It is noteworthy, therefore, that the amount of genetic data used in these investigations is small compared to the quantity of other kinds of genomic data. A follow-up study [7] looks into the problem of storing large data files, such as Sequence Alignment Maps (SAM) files, which can be larger than terabytes. The authors offer a novel remedy for this problem by fusing a private blockchain network with data compression.

Data Management and Access Control: Consent management systems are emphasized as a major motivator in a number of papers that emphasize the potential of blockchain technology for controlling and regulating access to genetic data. For example, [19], Dwarna suggests a web-based platform that uses blockchain technology to make it easier for researchers and research participants to gather and share dynamic consent.
