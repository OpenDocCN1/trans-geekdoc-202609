# 2.3.1 Creating Warnings and Monitoring in Real-Time

The ongoing surveillance of diverse health indicators such as temperature, heart rate, and blood oxygen levels plays a crucial role in delivering healthcare-services in real-time. The Internet of Things enables the deployment of sensors on the human body, facilitating the measurement of many parameters. The data can be subjected to analysis to provide recommendations for the appropriate treatment to patients during emergency situations. Electronic health records that continuously record vital signs (heart rate, temperature, and electrocardiogram) were developed by Shanin et al. [16]. This system is very adaptable and requires minimal power consumption. The patients’ RFID technology is employed to identify each individual patient. While GPS location is monitored to facilitate emergency healthcare, the Microcontroller Unit (MCU) employed in this study was the Arduino Uno, where the middleware utilized for data processing was Thingspeak. Swaroop et al. [17] developed a monitoring system that operates in real-time. The Raspberry Pi 3 is used in the gadget, which includes sensors such as the Sunrom blood pressure/heart rate sensor and the DS18B20 thermometer sensor. As health indicators, blood pressure and temperature were measured. Data was gathered and sent via a variety of communication channels, including WiFi, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and GSM. It was observed because WiFi requires multiple stages for data routing, it has a latency of about five minutes, whereas BLE has a shorter latency.
