## 20.6 Limitations

### Latency and Scalability:
These days, credit/debit card transactions, internet banking, and Unified Payment Interface (UPI) transactions all happen online in a matter of seconds. When it comes to Bitcoin transactions, there is a significant difference because of the way blockchain technology is built. Block-time, the term for the amount of time it takes for each transaction in the Bitcoin network to be added to the blockchain, is usually around 10 minutes. As such, latency presents a major problem to the blockchain design, making it difficult to achieve real-time transaction processing capabilities [36].

## 20.7 Future Scenarios

The creation of Health 5.0 signals the start of a new chapter in the advancement of healthcare. Blockchain offers a solution for the current EHR security and privacy bottlenecks; thanks to its decentralization, safe sharing, non-tampering, and high privacy [47]. Protecting medical records from hackers and maintaining patient privacy through authenticated access are two of the biggest issues the healthcare industry is currently facing. Blockchain security is the cornerstone of healthcare progress; going forward, blockchain security will mainly materialize through technology application, application deepening, and supervisory systems. Although it hasn't been used extensively in the healthcare sector, blockchain technology offers the potential to reduce data security issues [45].

Health 5.0, a new age in healthcare, requires large-scale, real-time health data collection from users remotely using a variety of sensors and smart wearables. These devices are then utilized for remote health monitoring. Large volumes of data are generated, and they need to be managed, transferred, and monitored securely. Other hospitals refuse to give insurance companies provide medicine information, or other consumers are afraid to give their private health information to a dispersed network. In addition to diagnosing and treating medical issues, blockchain technology can help with several security issues in the field [45].

The continued integration of genetics, IT, and healthcare may lead to advancements in precision medicine in the future. By leveraging technological advancements and overcoming associated challenges, the healthcare sector may be get closer to achieving the objective of truly personalized care. This will improve medicine's safety and efficacy and usher in a new era of tailored, preventive, predictive, and participatory healthcare [46]. The path to this future is challenging and full of obstacles. Nevertheless, considering the enormous benefits to human health and wellbeing, everyone participating in precision medicine and genomics see it as a worthwhile undertaking [46].
