# # Introduction

Good health is the most essential aspect of life. Various factors can influence people's health; healthcare refers to maintaining and improving a patient's physical and mental health by preventing, diagnosing, treating, and managing diseases, injuries, and disabilities. The impact of healthcare varies depending on the patient's capabilities and demands. Chemists, nurses, physicians, and other healthcare professionals contribute health through preventative care. To maintain patients' health, they conduct frequent testing, examinations, and health education, in addition to managing chronic illnesses and providing long-term care. Currently, healthcare systems consist of government, politics, outside organizations, and commercial authorities. Healthcare providers, emergency personnel, and patients comprise the three primary components of healthcare. Healthcare components include fundamental medical traits and access alternatives, such as health insurance. Storage, privacy, ownership, and data-related concerns are addressed by healthcare digitalization. Digital healthcare, which is frequently abbreviated as “digital health,” is a comprehensive multidisciplinary term that encompasses the integration of information technology and digital technologies within the healthcare sector. Telehealth, electronic health records, digital medications, and other technologies enhance healthcare accessibility, reduce expenses, and streamline clinical operations [1–3]. The pandemic has forced the healthcare industry to adapt to patient needs, revealing gaps and flaws. Population growth, medical services, and costs have raised health costs. Patient data administration is complicated and expensive for healthcare providers. Healthcare data is vast, while managing it while maintaining patient privacy and data security is difficult. Moreover, medical records are still predominantly handwritten, causing complexity in the scrutinizing data. Data modification and duplication are key challenges while keeping medical records since different people are involved in distinct locations, and inaccuracies such as mismatched records, lack of details, and data duplication are also visible. In digital healthcare, electronic health records demonstrate a viable solution where the difficulties of ample storage and data retrieval remain, but there is a risk of data loss due to system failure or server delay.
