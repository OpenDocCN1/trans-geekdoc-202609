Compared with public blockchains, which are accessed by users, private blockchains are restricted to a few nodes and usually operate under centralized administration. +   3. Consortium Blockchain: A consortium blockchain is partially centralized, allowing access to a restricted set of specific entities to participate in the consensus protocol. The main directions of such a blockchain will be directed by some powerful group or a single control entity that decides on which all entities have the right to read, append, and validate transactions on the blockchain. More than public blockchains, consortium blockchains still benefit from things like faster validation and throughput compared to private blockchains, while the majority of the network remains under the control of single entities. This will impart restrictions from the governing body onto users regarding anonymity and the public’s accessibility to read, append, and validate transactions. One example of a consortium blockchain is Hyperledger [5, 7].

# 20.2 General Applications of Blockchain

Cryptocurrencies are based on blockchain technology that allows peer-to-peer transactions without the interference of financial institutions like commercial banks [1]. Verification of transactions and creating an immutable record of the same allow anonymity by protecting the user’s personal identity information.

Blockchain runs a digital record of transactions by binding them via a consensus procedure, enhancing trust in a distributed network. Therefore, trust is built-in within the decentralized system itself; one does not need to trust middlemen or unknown people. It is in blockchain that one finds the very idea of a “Trust Machine”
