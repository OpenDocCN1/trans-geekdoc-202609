## 19.5 Incorporation of Blockchain Technology into Genomics

Blockchain technology can efficiently handle the computational and storage of large genomic databases. Currently, the computational power used for mining and securing the bitcoin blockchain is extremely high, surpassing even the computing power of the Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Network Computing, which is the most powerful scientific computation network globally. This demonstrates that the vast network of computers in a blockchain can be utilized for processing, exchanging, and storing genomic data. Miners can be motivated by cryptocurrency rewards to contribute their computational resources for this purpose. Within the blockchain, encrypted copies of genomic data will be distributed across multiple nodes, eliminating the risk of a single point of failure. Since genomic data contains personal and sensitive information, individuals naturally have privacy concerns regarding companies that collect it [2].

Creating a public blockchain to store this genomic data can effectively tackle these problems. A public blockchain is a decentralized system where the data is not owned or controlled by a third party. Instead, it gives ownership of genomic data to the contributors themselves. During the verification process, nodes in the network can access the data on public blockchains, but the data can be encrypted using homomorphic encryption to protect the privacy of the contributors. Homomorphic encryption is a cryptographic method that allows for the processing of encrypted data without the need for decryption. This is different from conventional encryption methods that require decryption before analysis. By using homomorphic encryption, miners can process genomic data using their computational resources while maintaining the privacy of the data contributors. Subsequently, the individuals who possess the data might get the deciphered information and opt to disseminate it to potential purchasers [29].

## 19.6 Current Blockchain Technological Applications in Genomics

Currently, multiple companies utilize blockchain technology for the storing of genetic data. One example of such a corporation is genomes.io which is a DNA and health data bank that enables users to have their genetic information sequenced and stored on their own platform. This is achieved through the use of homomorphic encryption, which ensures that only the data provider has access to their own data. Data providers have the option to grant anonymous permission to specific buyers to view their data in exchange for GENE tokens, which can be converted into currency. Luna DNA, another company, offers a similar platform but stands out by distributing shares of their data to contributors. This allows users to share in the profits from research conducted on Luna DNA's database. Nebula Genomics is yet another company that employs blockchain technology to give individuals control over their genetic data.

The founders of Nebula Genomics state that privacy and cost deter people from sequencing their genome [30]. With the blockchain and homomorphic encryption, Nebula Genomics, like genomes.io, allows owners to anonymously and securely share their data in exchange for financial rewards [30]. To address the cost of genomic sequencing, Nebula sequences one's genome as low as $99 [30]. More, Nebula Genomics offers some customers free sequencing services; thanks to partnerships with companies like EMD Serono [30]. EMD Serono is using the data on Nebula Genomic's blockchain to advance their lung cancer research this partnership can provide valuable information and help researchers accelerate medical developments [30]. DNATix also offers a blockchain that can anonymously and securely store and sell genomics data [31]. It is the first company to upload DNA sequences on a blockchain and uses a proprietary algorithm that can significantly compress data (99% compression rate) [31]. Such algorithms that can compress genomic data can effectively address the data storage concerns associated with a growing amount of genomic data. Authenticated Resources in a Hashed Block Registry (ARBoR) is a program that leverages the capabilities of a blockchain to tackle a different issue in genomics. Clinical sequencing is a valuable tool for the identification and management of several medical conditions [32].

However, the continuous progress in genetics research necessitates the ongoing reanalysis and updating of existing clinical genomic reports. This frequent revision raises several concerns. For example, it can be difficult to monitor the evolution of the field and guarantee that patients and doctors receive the most recent clinical genomic results. Furthermore, there is a chance that malicious changes to records will go unnoticed. Each report’s data is saved in its own data block in the ARBoR ledger to deal with these possible problems. A new block is made to fit and connect with the old block that has the data from the prior report version whenever the report is amended. At its conclusion, this procedure creates an very reliable but immutable record of record modifications [32].

## 19.7 Challenges of Implementing Blockchain in Genomic Research

Although blockchain technology shows potential in the field of genetic research, there are several barriers that prevent it from being widely used. A significant obstacle may arise from the correlation between cryptocurrencies and criminal activities. As an illustration, malvolent hackers insist on receiving ransom payments in the form of cryptocurrency, while illegal substances are bought online using cryptocurrency. These occurrences damage the standing of blockchain technology. An additional obstacle lies in the intricate and arduous nature of blockchain and cryptography, which may hinder individuals from embracing blockchain technology. Furthermore, the blockchain’s lack of environmental sustainability from its substantial electricity consumption. As an emerging technology, the blockchain holds potential for further advancement and enhancement. Researchers are actively exploring ways to create more energy-efficient blockchains, thereby reducing electricity consumption.
