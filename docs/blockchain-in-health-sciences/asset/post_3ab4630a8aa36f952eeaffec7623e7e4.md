## 3.5 IoT Based Healthcare Challenges

Because of the increased demand from numerous companies in the IoT healthcare system, many academians are working on developing and carrying out IoT-based medical applications [18]. However, there are numerous with IoT-based applications that are critical and require specific attention to develop IoT software and services, particularly in the healthcare sector. As a result, various databases, including IEEE, ACM, Google Scholar, Wiley, Willey, Elsevier, Springer, and Scopus, are searched for the latest challenges in IoT in healthcare [19]. To address these issues, IoT will bridge the technological gap between patients, medical professionals, carers, and the health care system [20].

### 3.5.1 The Establishment of Standards

Since the Internet of Things is still in its early stages in science and information technology, many suppliers do not adhere to particular standards for interface compatibility and protocols, resulting in device interoperability [19, 20]. Another challenge with standardization is the handling of added-value services such as management of access and healthcare professional registration [21]. It should be rectified as quickly as it's feasible by collaborating with mHealth, eHealth, and IoT organizations, as well as standardization authorities like the Computer Science and Innovations Foundation and the European Telecommunications Standard Institute, among others.

### 3.5.2 Foundation

The IoT platform differs from IoT-based healthcare hardware, requiring an operating system that operates in real-time and an appropriate computing platform [20, 21]. As a result, this platform strategy should follow the Service Orientated Approach (SOA) by utilizing a variety of Application package interfaces (APIs) and run-time libraries [20].

### 3.5.3 Application Development

App development is a highly technical procedure that does not include physicians, patients, caretakers, or healthcare professionals [20]. As a result, the engagement of relevant individuals and agencies should be necessary during app development to assure app quality [21]. Furthermore, the software should be maintained on an ongoing schedule if new changes have to be made or a new pandemic strikes the planet [21].

### 3.5.4 Conventional Technology

The healthcare system ought to replace obsolete equipment and sensors to be compatible with the present setup [20]. Nevertheless, this will increase the cost of the healthcare system and introduce compatibility issues between the Internet of Things (“IoT”) system and current devices [21].

### 3.5.5 Expansion of Bandwidth

In the current setup, networking equipment is only used to power handheld gadgets and desktop PCs that handle hospital administration activities and a few lightweight apps [19, 20]. However, with the development of IoT, hundreds of thousands of sensors, and other linked devices, patient data must now be set to servers or clouds [21]. As transmission of data and communication rises, the medical sector requires updates and sophisticated networking equipment, including routers, switches, upgraded cabling, and enormous databases [20]. As a result, a large bandwidth is required to ensure that communication and transmission between the individual, their physician, and the health care system work smoothly and consistently [21].

### 3.5.6 Deployment of Protocols

There are variety pf IoT healthcare devices, and communication layers can operate under several scenarios, including reception, delivery, deep slumber, and sleep [21]. However, each layer’s power needs differ from the previous layers. As a result, it is challenging to implement a device identification system that certifies feature availability at the MAC layer while consuming the smallest amount of energy [22]. As a result, it is necessary to create a power-efficient technique at each layer that will be adaptable in scenarios of pandemics [23].

### 3.5.7 Contingency Plan

In COVID-19, immediate time IoT processing of data between the patient, clinician, and healthcare system is a big difficulty [22, 23]. The health care system should have an IT director that saves patient data and facilitates communication between the patient, clinician, and the system. The IT department should have a backup plan for keeping data [21]. In the event of a hardware, software, or human failure, the system should continue to function normally. [22]. Backup plans are required for the health care system because the gap or interval between the patient’s condition and the hospital system is important in a life-saving event.

## 3.6 Patient Empowerment Through IoT in Healthcare

The concept of patient empowerment represents a paradigm shift in healthcare, where individuals actively participate in their own well-being and decision-making processes. In this era of advancing technology, the integration of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies emerges as a pivotal catalyst in fostering and enhancing patient empowerment throughout their healthcare journey [24]. IoT technologies, characterized by interconnected devices and seamless data exchange, redefining the traditional patient-provider relationship. By allowing patients to actively engage with their health, these technologies transform healthcare from a predominantly reactive system to a proactive and personalized approach. The potential benefits of empowering patients through the integration of IoT solutions are substantial and multifaceted. First, IoT facilitates the collection of Patient-Generated Health Data (PGHD). Through wearables, home monitoring devices, and other connected tools, individuals can contribute valuable health data in real time [25]. This influx of continuous, patient-generated information provides a comprehensive view of an individual's health status, enabling personalized and timely interventions. One of the notable impacts of IoT in healthcare is the improved management of chronic diseases. Continuous monitoring through IoT devices enables healthcare professionals to track vital parameters, detect anomalies, and tailor treatment plans accordingly.
