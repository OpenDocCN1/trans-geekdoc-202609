### 3.3 Key Features Internet of Things for Healthcare Management

The Internet of Things (IoT) has revolutionized healthcare by combining communication devices with advanced machine learning algorithms. IoT devices generate biomedical data, which can be used for diagnosis, treatment, and decision-making [3]. The Internet of Everything (IoE) is also gaining popularity due to the development of small devices to continuously track patient vital signs. The rising availability of wearable and linked devices enables healthcare professionals to collect huge amounts of information about patients, which can be analyzed to improve patient results and treatment quality [9]. Data processing in the Medical Internet of Things (H-IoT) entails gathering, storing, analyzing, and sharing data. This information may include vital signs, activity among patients' levels, sleep patterns, and medication adherence [3, 9]. This data can be used by healthcare providers to detect early diseases symptoms, remotely monitor patients, and personalize treatment programs [11]. The mind sponge theory proposes that healthcare personnel must digest huge amounts of data to make educated decisions to offer better treatment [12]. This can be accomplished using advanced machine learning and analytics methods [10]. Using IoT data, healthcare professionals can improve outcomes for patients and overall care quality. However, due to the rapid development and deployment of IoT devices, security concerns are projected to grow [10, 11].
