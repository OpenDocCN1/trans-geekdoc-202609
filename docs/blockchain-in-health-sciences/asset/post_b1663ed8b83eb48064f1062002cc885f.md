## Design and Test Interventions:

- Formulate or assess intervention programs for vitamin D based on the HBM theory for youths in India. Such interventions could be informational, such as public health campaigns, pharmacological, such as vitamins or mineral supplements, or even modifications in people's daily routines.

- The idea is to start testing these initiatives in various parts of India to identify the strategies' strengths and weaknesses.

## Cultural and Demographic Variations:

- Examine how the cultural, geographical, and socioeconomic characteristics of Indian youths affect their vitamin D deficiency and health practices. This may help make the interventions more specific and target-oriented for particular needs of the Population and unavoidable circumstances.

- The main task of this work will be to examine the data on the influence of urban and rural environments on crucial aspects such as vitamin D levels and the effectiveness of measures taken.

## Technology-Enabled Health Interventions:

- Leverage technology such as mobile apps, wearable devices, and blockchain-enabled health platforms to monitor and manage vitamin D levels in real time. These tools can provide personalized recommendations and reminders for individuals.

## 5. Integration with Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):

- Align future research and interventions with the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goal 3 to ensure healthy lives and promote wellbeing for all ages. Highlight the role of vitamin D sufficiency in achieving this goal.

- Collaborate with international organizations and stakeholders to share global best practices and resources for combating VDD.

## 4.9 Conclusion

VDD is alarming in India. Many studies have proven this, but very few focused research discussions have delve into interventions to tackle VDD as a public health emergency. In the West, dairy products and beverages are Vitamin D fortified and are important dietary sources of Vitamin D. Other recommendations for dietary sources of naturally occurring vitamin D are particular types of fish, fish oils, eggs, and mushrooms. In comparison, recommendations for Vitamin D-rich native foods in India are far and few.

Considering the high economic costs besides undeniable suffering due to faulty health behaviours, highly populous nations like India should initiate casual studies for public health concerns like VDD.

Without research on applying HBM in conjunction with VDD in Indian Youth, this study holds promise for health education practice in India. The HBM as a sociopsychological health behavior change model can be employed to identify, explain, and predict the Population's preventive health-related behaviors, especially in predicting the uptake of interventions that can stimulate the subjects to engage or disengage with a health-promoting behavior regarding VDD. Using HBM as a health behavior theory will lay the foundation for planning interventions regarding VDD nationally.

The Health Belief Model and blockchain technology provide a promising way to improve knowledge and encourage young people to take enough vitamin D. A lack of vitamin D can be effectively addressed by customizing solutions to each individual's requirements and tastes by utilizing Blockchain's organizing data, safety, ongoing along with incentive features. More study and cooperation are necessary to get around current obstacles and ultimately realize the promise of this novel strategy in enhancing young people's health outcomes.
