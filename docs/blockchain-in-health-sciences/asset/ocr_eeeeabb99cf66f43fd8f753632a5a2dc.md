# 12.1 Introduction

Pharmaceutical Supply Chain (PSC) is one of the socially important and complicated Supply Chain Management (SCM) primarily due to two factors. The first factor comprises the well-being of a community, which could be a nation as well as world economy. It depends on the healthcare system and supply chain process carried on regional, national, inter-continental and world level. The healthcare domain and authentic pharmaceutical products directly impact the quality of public life overcoming problems of citizens [1]. For example, [2] mentioned that the low-quality, below-standard or counterfeit antibiotics may cause significant number of child-deaths worldwide from pneumonia. In the second factor, PSC involves multiple vendors for supplying the ingredients and equipment for drug production. The produced drug in a company is regulated through multiple distributors and stakeholders for delivery to biotech companies, health regulatory agencies, hospitals, patients, and pharmacy shops. The involvement of multiple stakeholders in PSC requires authenticity of stockholders, their product data transparency, flow of minimum time-updated data within the supply chain, and immutability of data once it is generated by authentic stakeholder for maintaining the trust in the system [3, 4]. Therefore, the implementation of such PSC systems is difficult due to its inherent complex structure. A major focus of present pharmaceutical research has been on the location of manufacturing facility and its design, production of pharmaceutical products with standard quality, inventory storage and product distribution while maintaining environment constraints such as temperature or humidity and process scheduling. Moreover, there has been an increased requirement to balance capacity with upcoming market demand, which is partially driven by clinical trials and performance metrics of competitor companies.
