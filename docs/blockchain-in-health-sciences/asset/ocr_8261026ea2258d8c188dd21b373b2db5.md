The Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DQSA), initially signed in 2013 by United States president Barack Obama, urged pharmaceutical supply chain participants and stakeholders to collaborate and form an electronic and inter-operable system to identify and trace drugs due to their transportation in the United States. Despite stringent drug distribution and quality control policies by national and general regulatory bodies other rising counterfeit production and sale are continuing to push millions of lives to risk, and increase the cost to billions in healthcare fraud.

Due to its decentralized nature, immutability, traceability, privacy, distributed properties, and smart contract features, blockchain technology poses as a promising solution for alleviating these problems that are inherently associated with the SCM as the PSC system [1–15]. Blockchain technology supports in maintaining logs and auditing of the data; this property along with various stakeholder authentication schemes and product identification schemes within the supply chain help in tracking the authenticity of pharmaceutical products as well as drugs. Therefore, research attempts have been made to use blockchain technology for generic chain management (SCM) systems. However, each technology has its own advantages and disadvantages. In addition, the PSC has its own inherent features, and therefore, it is different from other SCM systems. Due to its complex structure, successful implementation of blockchain technology in PSC is yet to be achieved. In this chapter, we analyze features of PSC for domestic and international market, various existing architectures for SCM and PSC, and the scope of blockchain technology for PSC management. The organization of this chapter begins with the detail analytics of various aspects and issues involved in pharmaceutical supply chain according to both domestic and international market. Subsequently, we discuss generic Indian PSC architecture. In Section 12.4, blockchain technology features and existing architectures are discussed with respect to generic supply chain management (SCM). Next, we analyze how blockchain technology can alleviate issues inherently applicable to PSC management in Section 12.5.
