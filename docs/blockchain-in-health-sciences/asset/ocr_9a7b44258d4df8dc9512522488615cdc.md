# 20.4 Application of Blockchain Technology in Genomics

In 2014, Genecoin was introduced as the first application [10] of blockchain technology in the genetics industry as a for-profit project [10]. Outside of Bitcoin, blockchain technology is turning out to be increasingly useful, especially in fields like genetics. This has been taken up by researchers examining the effect it has on scalability, security, and privacy, with contributions to the growing body of literature over the subject.

The two classes in which the applications are split: i) commercial, and ii) non-commercial. To further enhance our classification, we segment various categorizations depending on whether blockchain is used as a dual tool for monetary transactions and a sharing. Commercial genomic marketplaces earn profit due to direct sales of genetic information for profit.

# 20.4.1 Commercial Genomic Marketplaces

Recent technological advancements boosted the participation of customers in direct-to-consumer DNA sequencing. These alterations accelerated to make the genome sequencing process quicker, more affordable, and more accessible. The sale of accumulated DNA data is one of these DTC companies' primary revenue streams, and pharmaceutical companies are among their core clientele [20]. However, concerns have been voiced regarding the fairness of this strategy, particularly in relation to the distribution of the sales proceeds. Some argue that people, not intermediary companies, should be the real beneficiaries of these gains. Consequently, a new generation of companies offering markets for genetic data exchange has surfaced.

By utilizing blockchain technology, these markets enable consumers to maintain control over their data and do away with the need for middlemen. Participants in these marketplaces are encouraged to sell or rent their genetic data in a variety of ways. The popularity of cryptocurrency rewards has increased as a result.
