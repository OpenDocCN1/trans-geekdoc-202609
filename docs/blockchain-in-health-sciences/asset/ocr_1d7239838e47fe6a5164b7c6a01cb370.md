+   - leaky ReLU: A variant of ReLU that addresses a potential drawback of the standard ReLU. Leaky ReLU allows a small, non-zero gradient for negative inputs, preventing some neurons from becoming permanently inactive. f(x) = max (alpha * x, x) where alpha is a small constant like 0.01.
- Sigmoid: Outputs a value between 0 and 1 (f(x) = 1 / (1 + e^(-x))). While it can introduce non-linearity, Sigmoid suffers from vanishing gradients in deep networks and is not as commonly used in CNNs compared to ReLU variants.
- TanH (Hyperbolic Tangent): Outputs a value between -1 and 1 (f(x) = (e^x - e^(-x)) / (e^x + e^(-x))). Similar to Sigmoid, TanH can have vanishing gradient issues and is not as popular as ReLU in CNNs.

The choice of activation function can impact the performance of your CNN. Some important considerations generally considered as follows - ReLU and Leaky ReLU are strong defaults for most CNN tasks due to their computational efficiency and ability to avoid vanishing gradients, whereas Sigmoid and TanH might be preferable in output layers for tasks requiring a probability-like output (between 0 and 1) Figure 17.3 [12–15]. In determining the optimal activation function for your CNN, it is crucial to consider the unique nature of the problem you are addressing and the specific attributes of your data. Experimentation is valuable for discovering the best option.
