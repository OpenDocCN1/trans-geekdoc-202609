### 13.4.2 Increase R&D

Data mining and analysis of information in published and newly created research could be enhanced by combining blockchain technology with other developing technologies like artificial intelligence and machine learning in the pharmaceutical industry. Protecting patients' personal information and data during clinical studies is another significant use of the technology. ### 13.4.3 System for Verifying Drug Origin

An excellent use case for blockchain technology in the pharmaceutical industry is the elimination of fraud and the verification of medication validity. And people in the business world have been taking note. According to a 2017 survey conducted by the industry non-profit Pistoia Alliance, just over 70% of pharmaceutical and life science executives saw blockchain technology as having the most significant influence on their sector. It should come as no surprise that solutions utilizing this technology are currently under development. The goal of one project, MediLedger, is to make pharmaceutical supply chains more secure, private, and efficient. One of MediLedger's initial offerings is a blockchain-based authentication system that aims to streamline the process of confirming the legitimacy of resalable medications.

### 13.4.4 Serialization

The pharmaceutical sector is subject to strict regulations and oversight because of the vital role it plays in public health. For industrialized economies, this is particularly the case. Numerous serialization requirements exist on a global scale, and business participants are obligated to adhere to them. According to these rules, all pharmaceuticals and their packaging must have a distinct identifier. In addition, the drug's origin and logistic path, among other facts, must be shared by all supply chain partners that help get the drug to the patient. Utilizing blockchain technology can enhance the industry's capacity to adhere to these rules. In addition, regulatory audits can easily access the data on the blockchain due to the immutability of the distributed ledger.

### 13.4.5 Administration of Data from Clinical Trials

Clinical trials, which are an essential component of the pharmaceutical industry, provide assistance to the process of drug discovery as well as other medical research and development operations. The industry faces a huge challenging problem in finding the most efficient way to handle and interpret the data from this research. Equally important, businesses frequently fail to guarantee that patients participating in clinical trials are afforded enough protection of their privacy. Now, businesses are attempting to find solutions to these problems with the assistance of blockchain technology. The company Nebula Genomics, which has its headquarters in Boston, Massachusetts, is endeavoring to use the technology to enhance the process of genome sequencing. The organization is in the process of constructing a genetic database with the objective of removing superfluous intermediaries and assisting patients in the secure of their encrypted genetic data. Encryggen is yet another company that is actually operating in this sector. A platform that is based on blockchain technology has been established by the company that is situated in Florida. This platform makes possible to share, store, sell, and buy genetic data.

## 13.5 Challenges

There is a noticeable increase in the implementation of blockchain within the healthcare sector. Rapid technological innovation facilitated by a variety of government-integrated facilities is propelling market expansion. The pharmaceutical industry has been profoundly impacted by Blockchain technology. Blockchain may retain transaction records immutably and function as a public ledger. This characteristic may be used to create transparent permission management systems that encourage the exchange of data. There are various challenges [15] in using blockchain for pharmaceutical research, some of them are addressed here.

### 13.5.1 Throughput

More checks will need to be made as the amount of transactions in the network increases, potentially creating congestion in the system. High value of throughput is a challenge in healthcare systems because delays in access could hurt a life-saving diagnosis [16]. There are several elements that might have an impact on the scalability and performance of a framework. One of these factors is the requirements for the system receiving continual updates [17], keyword set size, network configuration, and disk space required depending on the kind of blockchain utilized by the framework. In a similar vein, [18] proposes that increased performance-related costs may be mitigated by adding certain capabilities to already-existing systems, such as global smart transactions.

### 13.5.2 Latency

The time it takes to validate a block is around ten minutes; this could potentially be damaging to the effectiveness of the system security services, since it could be a period during which successful attacks occur. In the healthcare industry, networks are complex and should constantly be accessed since any delay might potentially hinder the analysis of an examination.

### 13.5.3 Standardization

Globally safe financial transactions can be facilitated by blockchain technology, but interoperability concerns pose a serious problem. Blockchain activities can be standardized by using distributed ledger technology (DLT). While the ITU is striving to find and standardize distributed ledger technology (DLT) applications and related services, additional research is required to determine the most effective ways to put these applications into action.

### 13.5.4 Privacy and Data Security

According to research by Yang et al. [19], protecting patient privacy and data security is another major concern. Blockchain promises immutability and transparency, but it also needs to make sure that critical health information is private and only available to authorized users. It is a popular belief that the Bitcoin structure permits the blockchain to ensure node anonymity [20]. Blockchain-based solutions must abide by the General Data Protection Regulation by privacy laws and regulations.

### 13.5.5 Complexity of Big Data

Data availability has been greatly enhanced by the adoption of edge computing, cloud computing, and IOT applications. The implementation of blockchain encounters various challenges due to its extensive growth, encompassing issues related to data management, accuracy redundancy, and privacy. Blockchain technology has endeavored to leverage operational efficiency and enhance the efficiency of transactions, the safety of patients, and tracking processes. Heterogeneous multicore processors [21] have become increasingly important in many applications recently because of their improved flexibility and performance in adjusting power restrictions.

### 13.5.6 Resource Consumption

Due to the high energy requirement of the mining process the resource requirements are very high. However, there may be substantial computational and energy expenses associated with the deployment of blockchain technology. The time complexity of the sequential executed algorithm can be reduced to a particular extent by using the parallel approaches [22].

## 13.6 Summary

The use of blockchain technology in drug development, distribution, and discovery is revolutionizing several facets of pharmaceutical research. By offering an unchangeable transaction record, blockchain enhances the transparency and accountability of pharmaceutical research while safeguarding the dependability and integrity of clinical trial data. Blockchain has been utilized by pharmaceutical companies to enhance supply chain governance and compliance. Smart contracts are created by blockchain technology to automate processes and keep track of each transaction. Even in the event that the compliance requirements are not met, smart contracts generate a particular transaction record and carry out alerts. In this chapter, the uses of blockchain, its layered approach as well as various challenges in pharmaceutical research have been discussed. The chapter showcased various characteristics of blockchain technology such as reliability, traceability, authorization, and security. The chapter also outlined the main domains in the pharmaceutical sector where blockchain technology is being used: product distribution, security, tracking, and prevention of drugs. Promising developments in clinical trials, tailored treatment, and expedited regulatory procedures are all part of the future of blockchain. To fully realize blockchain’s promise, though, stated obstacles must be overcome, including technological complexity, regulatory adaptation, and privacy issues.
