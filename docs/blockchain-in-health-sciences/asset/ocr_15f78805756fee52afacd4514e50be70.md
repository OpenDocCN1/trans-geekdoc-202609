# 2.5 Internet of Things (IoT) Health Security Concerns

More than 10 billion IoT devices are currently wirelessly joined, according to the "ABI (Allied Business Intelligence, Inc) Research report" [24]. Plus, this number will have soared 30 billion devices by the year 2026. Deploying IoT devices will result in a multitude of applications that greatly enhance our everyday life. With time, e-health will likely become one of the prominent worries [25]. Many problems, however, have emerged as a result of IoT incorporation into health sciences sector. Data management and storage, unified and ubiquitous access, privacy and security, and data transfer between devices are just a few of the many areas that fall under this umbrella of worries [26]. Data breaches can damage the credibility of companies and users alike. On top of that, hackers can spy on consumers' personal life if they manage to acquire access to their Internet of Things devices. Security remains a concern with IoT, even if it is growing at a rapid pace. Several new applications have emerged as a result of the expansion of the Internet of Things, with a focus on the health sciences. Nonetheless, sensitive information about individuals, such as medical records and patient identities, is often linked with the health sciences. Because of this, concerns about the safety of such systems are well-founded. Therefore, secured connections are essential for overseeing an IoT rollout of this magnitude. There is also the possibility of data theft or profiling due to the database's security measures, which store all of the information. Many obstacles exist in the realm of security for healthcare IoT devices. Privacy, secrecy, integrity, and authentication of devices are among these, along with information flow traces and persistence, and data transfer flows. There is a high prevalence of data theft, data privacy, and confidentiality at sensitive, active, and gateway environmental nodes, among others.
