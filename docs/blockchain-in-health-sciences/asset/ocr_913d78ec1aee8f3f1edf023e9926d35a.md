# BLOCKCHAIN APPLICATIONS IN PHARMACEUTICAL RESEARCH

+   * Management of Vendors and Suppliers: By enabling real-time visibility over inventory levels, locations, and movements throughout the supply chain, blockchain improves the inventory management. By automating order, processing, inventory replenishment and invoicing, smart contracts can cut down on human error, hot-time, and expense associated with inventory holding. Demand forecasting and inventory optimization are made easier by the availability of a single, transparent view of inventory data to supply chain participants [17].

+   * Payments and Finance in the Supply Chain: Blockchain digitizes trade finance instruments including invoices, payment guarantees, and letters of credit to facilitate efficient supply chain financing and payments [16, 17]. Suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and financial institutions may enhance cash flow management, expedite trade finance procedures, and decrease payment processing times using blockchain-based systems. By automating payment settlements upon complete fulfillment, smart contracts can improve financial transaction transparency and confidence.

+   * Resilience and Risk Management: By recognizing reducing supply chain risks including delays, interruptions, political concerns, and supplier dependencies, blockchain improves resilience and risk management in the supply chain [12]. Risk management tools built on blockchain technology have the ability to evaluate risk factors, track events in the supply chain, send out notifications for impending disruptions, and assist with reaction and contingency planning. Proactive risk reduction methods are made possible by the transparent and decentralized nature of blockchain, which enhances supply chain visibility.

+   * Impact on the Environment and Society: Blockchain makes it possible to transparently track social and environmental impact across the supply chain, supporting sustainability activities [16]. Blockchain-enabled systems allow stakeholders to keep an eye on things like labor practices, ethical sourcing, trash creation, carbon emissions, and energy use. Sustainability reporting, stakeholder accountability, and ethical supply chain practices are all encouraged by this openness.
