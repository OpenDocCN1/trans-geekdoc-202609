## 7.2 Related Works

The Internet of Things or IoT, wearable technology, blockchain, AI, big data, and other emerging technologies have made significant contributions to the healthcare industry. It has reduced the time required for diagnosis while additionally enhancing disease identification [1]. Data administration, data analysis, data storage, and data collection were among the numerous aspects mentioned in the analysis. Electronic storage of medical prescriptions and other records have been utilized by healthcare professionals, patients, and other stakeholders. Every person has multiple prescriptions from physicians, all of which need to be kept in their records. Records are utilized in a situation of emergency. The researchers of [54] have looked at significant blockchain research within the medical industry. Artificial Intelligence has the potential to identify and rank specific patients for dosage monitoring. Additionally, suggested combining blockchain technology with artificial intelligence for healthcare management.

The authors of [3] examined fault tolerance, automation by frameworks, upcoming technologies, and other intelligent functions for patients. The paper's authors have proposed a secure architecture built on blockchain and AI. Then, through analyzing the malware's many attributes, including Dynamic Link Library (DLL), file size, and more, dynamic examination of malware is utilized to eliminate the data to connected. Additionally, a common dataset has been utilized to train AI models for network-related assaults in smart healthcare systems. Furthermore, the blockchain layer receives harmless data (categorized by AI models) for safe data storage against risks to data integrity.

The potential for integrating blockchain technology to develop trustworthy AI models for e-health is discussed in [7]. AI makes use of a wide range of recommended algorithms, judgement, and vast quantities of data.

Thus, the medical system would have improved efficiency, reduced expenses, and democratized healthcare by incorporating some of the most recent advancements in these technologies. Blockchain, however, makes it possible to store the cryptographic records that AI requires.

A number of patient monitoring methods were examined in [8]. Additionally, there is also mention of difficulties in the daily and clinical care of persistent illnesses. More sources of knowledge regarding the environment and human posture, which includes real-time location, video materials, built-in sensors, and more, must be incorporated to make it easier to administer suitable emergency care in the case of an acute exacerbation [8].

Data manipulation, the possibility of malevolent cyberattacks, decentralized authority, single point failure, problems with data management, and many other concerns are the main dangers and problems in a typical healthcare system. The Blockchain-based client-server architecture [13] addresses privacy and security problems. There is a possibility of a one-point failure with this solution. The authors of [25] established a realtime, intelligent contract-based patient surveillance system that stores wearable device data events, thereby removing the need for third parties and addressing vulnerabilities associated with remote monitoring.

Patient electronic healthcare records are stored in a decentralized, patients-centric framework in the healthcare industry using a decentralized data management architecture, as described in [35]. Patients are given the ability to take authority over their information through the use of rule-based smart contracts. According to [14], the exponential growth in parameters, intricate topologies, and large amounts of training data necessitate higher processing power for AI-based healthcare systems.

Researchers can gain useful insights from patient data using the federated learning approach, without disclosing private information such as patient names, text messages, or medical histories. Instead of focusing on raw data, researchers are usually more interested in statistical outcomes, where in the absence of access to the data. A conceptual framework for healthcare alliances based on blockchain-orchestrated federated learning was presented by Jonathan et al. [36]. Their design records network activities and preserves privacy by creating an audit trail that obscures user identities.
