These problems are taken to a whole new level by genomes, which are the comprehensive compilation of an organism's DNA. They act as the complex storehouse of instructions that control an organism's course through development, physiological processes, and inherited traits. Genomes provide priceless information on genetic variety, the likelihood of developing a disease, and the need for safe and efficient data-sharing methods, which are problematic in many domains, such as evolutionary science, health, and agriculture. A genome's structure is shown in Figure 20.1 below.

As mentioned above, genomic research faces numerous challenges, including data ownership and integrity, particularly given the sensitive nature of individual genomic data. In this situation, blockchain technology is useful since it provides immutable records and allows data owners to contribute to scientific discoveries while maintaining the security of their data. The large-scale data storage problem in genomic research is blocked by blockchain's capacity to store genetic variants and reads on-chain using nested database indexing. This ensures efficient access and analysis. Blockchain gives assurance of secure, encrypted data storage and access to combat potential privacy leakages in personal genetic information.
