Advancements in off-chain computations utilizing zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) offer considerable potential for the expansion of blockchain-based applications. Off-chain computation is a state transition paradigm in which a node executes computations off-chain and verifies those computations with a Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP). The resulting state is subsequently chain-stored. An alternative approach, known as Arbitrum, verifies off-chain computations through incentive-based means [48].

Our contention is that further off-chain computation methods may prove advantageous in the field of genomics, given the substantial volume of genomic data and the computationally intensive nature of genomic research. Moreover, despite receiving insufficient attention, emerging technologies like Verifiable Credentials (VCs) [49] and Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) [49] have the potential to inspire confidence. In addition to safeguarding the confidentiality and integrity of information, constructing a decentralized trust system using blockchain technology can help overcome additional obstacles. The utilization of trust-over-IP principles in genomic applications [50] has the potential to enhance the dependability of data exchange in scientific inquiries. # 19.9 Conclusion

Genetic data which require substantial processing resources and reliable privacy features, blockchain technology provides a unique solution. The ability of blockchain systems to solve multiple consensus and security problems, like safe computation over a publicly available network and data sharing, has a domino effect. One of the main goals of blockchain platforms is to solve the problems associated with the regulation of genetic data exchange. Organizations and participants will be able to transmit data using algorithms that protect and encourage compliance with ethical and legal standards; this is the main goal. The majority of these platforms are in their infancy, yet they have the potential to solve genetic data sharing governance problems in novel ways.

In addition to being a technological framework, blockchain offers a fresh approach to managing decentralized networks. It entails satisfying needs and customer preferences while capitalizing on the power of decentralized networks. Here, the technological advancement is not only a cause but also an enabler of the crucial breakthrough. Blockchain networks aim to handle more data in less time, introduce new ownership models, and let people have a say in how data is shared and managed. Solutions built on the blockchain might make genetic data access more transparent and equitable while also streamlining access control processes. By utilizing smart contracts, the enforcement of access agreements will make each easier. Although this is a massive undertaking, it will ensure that researchers and data custodians are certain that any future uses of the data will comply with the data usage agreement. Furthermore, upon successful implementation, the Blockchain-based solution has the potential to revolutionize the practice of data exchange. Specifically, there is potential to enhance the involvement of patients and persons in the data-sharing system, while reducing the dominance of public and commercial test providers in managing the sharing of genomic data.

Blockchain possesses the capacity to generate shared resources that exist in a realm between the marketplace and public goods. To facilitate this trend, considerable efforts in education, design, and incentivization are necessary to empower patients and citizens to manage and distribute their data via Blockchain-based platforms. However, it is essential for Blockchain-based solutions to have legal frameworks supporting them. Monetizing individual genomic databases and establishing ownership rights are prime examples of situations where self-regulation may be inadequate. Furthermore, it is imperative to thoroughly examine the possible ramifications of these regulations within the wider scope of biomedical research, and ensure that the ultimate policies align with the optimal outcomes for science, society, and innovation.
