## 6.2 Related Work

Microsoft is well known for adopting cloud computing in the healthcare industry. Azure is a prominent platform that integrates AI and IoT with cloud computing for patient monitoring. HoloLens 2, developed by Microsoft, also utilizes the cloud to store holographic images. Since 2016, Pfizer, a biotechnology and pharmaceutical corporation, has used cloud services for its initiatives. The company recently started collaborating with Amazon Web Services to develop cloud-based solutions aimed at expediting the clinical trial testing, development, manufacturing, and distribution processes. Oracle has a long and expanding history in the global healthcare industry, serving payers, providers, university medical institutions, and specialty care groups. Oracle Cloud redefines how healthcare can innovate and evolve in the digital age, offering the most potent, unified cloud solution package on the market today.

TigerConnect is a cloud-based software that provides healthcare professionals with a messaging platform to exchange documents, records, patient data, and communications. Physician-on-call scheduling is one application of the program, enabled by a scheduling system that can generate a full year's worth of schedules almost instantaneously.

The products from Consensus Solutions are designed to address issues in the healthcare industry and boost efficiency by using cloud computing to ensure that providers can access patient data and other vital information stored on paper documents.

Medsphere offers healthcare organizations a range of cloud-based IT solutions through its electronic record platform, which performs functions such as scheduling, registration, billing, claims, medical records, and more, utilizing Microsoft's secure Azure cloud platform. NetApp is a provider of hybrid cloud data services, with its platform delivering real-time clinical data. The Medable platform supports clinical applications and stores patient health data for researchers and providers to facilitate healthcare collaboration. Healthcare practitioners can manage patient data analytics, documentation, and services through Athenahealth's cloud-based platform. This application enables access to patient health records, revenue cycle management, telemedicine support, personal marketing programs, and other features. The open platform from CareCloud enhances the efficiency and transparency of healthcare providers, including applications for practice management, electronic health records, patient experience, revenue cycle management, and support for mobile apps and healthcare analytics.

Gautam et al. [12] proposed a light and secure healthcare solution using blockchain and IoT for medical devices. They presented various obstacles in remote patient monitoring using IoT devices. Cryptographic techniques, including the RSA encryption scheme, are adopted for deployment in IoT. Ring signatures provide privacy properties such as anonymity and signature correctness, while a double encryption scheme is used to secure the symmetric key. The Diffie-Hellman exchange is utilized for securing public keys.

Farouk et al. [13] analyzed medical data and its necessity in industrial healthcare. The impact of blockchain and IoT on industrial healthcare is reviewed in terms of efficiency by the authors. With blockchain technology, the healthcare sector may better manage information security; patient data can be shared and analyzed while maintaining its security and privacy. The deployment of blockchain technology offers both benefits and rewards for the medical field and research.

Healthcare executives should also consider two supplementary technologies that can expedite adoption and time when planning blockchain and IoHT installations. First, artificial intelligence (AI) can be used to identify patterns and correlations in massive amounts of data to extract insights. The second essential component of a solid blockchain and IoHT foundation is hybrid clouds. These new hybrid cloud-at-customer models maintain clinical data behind business firewalls while offering highly scalable public-cloud applications and, ensuring compliance with organizational and regulatory requirements.

IoBHealth will facilitate the seamless integration of IoT and blockchain for the provisioning of services related to e-healthcare. The three primary subsystems of this framework include the IoT-based e-health provider node, the IoT-blockchain platform, and the IoT-based e-healthcare patient node. The EHR engine, back-end library, gatekeeper, and cryptocurrency client are used in the construction of both nodes. Both transactional and service-specific data may be stored by the nodes [14–16].

An Internet of Things (IoT)-based blockchain-aided dyslexia mobile health management system was created in [17–19], allowing dyslexic individuals and their caregivers to utilize, save, and retrieve dyslexic patterns within a framework for multimodal information sharing. Agility was tested against a software-based blockchain architecture built on the Internet of Things to provide e-healthcare applications with greater flexibility, ability, and privacy protection [20]. Using blockchain architecture, an Internet of Things-based patient-centric agent (PCA) was developed to enable end-to-end service for ongoing patient monitoring.

## 6.3 Cloud Services in the Healthcare Industry

The healthcare industry maintains electronic medical records that hold information about patients and their clinical data. These records support doctors and patients during examination and recovery procedures. Next, the Healthcare Information System (HIS) evolved, providing a graphical user interface to support EMR in storing and retrieving information as needed. The integration of EMR with results in Electronic Health Records (EHR), which incorporate the therapeutic records of patients and make them accessible to healthcare professionals. Implanted devices, such as sensors, have been developed for further advancement.

The data collected and initially stored in local storage are sent to cloud storage for further analysis. Continuous advancements have paved the way for Personal Health Records (PHR), in which patients actively participate in gathering their own data, monitoring their health status, and more, using their wearables or sophisticated mobile phones. Such systems present several challenges, which can be addressed through advancements in cloud technology. The cloud framework supports the sharing and processing of real-time data as well as big data.

Patient data are initially stored as health records, which are subsequently stored in the cloud. The data stored in the cloud take the form of electronic medical records. Meanwhile, hospitals can also store data collectively as electronic medical records. Both patients and doctors can access this data from anywhere at any time. However, there is a greater possibility of vulnerabilities and attacks on the data stored and retrieved in the cloud.
