# Abstract

Lifestyle diseases have seen an upsurge worldwide. Micronutrient deficiencies like Vitamin D (VDD) have been pointed out as the underlying cause of many disease burdens in urban and rural populations. Young adults in urban settings are especially vulnerable to such deficiencies because of their lifestyle choices. This relates to their beliefs and perceptions, shaping health behaviors. Among the many behavior theories, the Health Belief Model (HBM), shaping the theoretical foundation, is the most widely used for various disease conditions. This paper explores the existing research on VDD in India, particularly Youth.

Further, an attempt has been made to explore whether the HBM can help understand behaviors that may stimulate the Youth to engage with inadequate vitamin D intake. When addressing challenges regarding vitamin D deficiency, especially within the youth population in urban settings, this paper proposes the integration of Blockchain-Enabled Health Platforms as a transformative solution. By leveraging decentralized health data management, blockchain technology can enhance the effectiveness of health interventions and encourage youth participation in Vitamin D-deficiency behaviors. The transparency and security offered by Blockchain can facilitate real-time tracking of sunlight exposure, nutritional intake, and health outcomes, creating a trustful and immutable record. This integration aligns with the Health Belief Model and introduces a novel approach to promoting and sustaining health behaviors through innovative and secure digital platforms.

## ## 4.1 Introduction

Vitamin D is a crucial micronutrient vital in maintaining bone mineral homeostasis. Synthesized by the body, it functions as a hormone essential for normal development. A deficiency in vitamin D can lead to rickets in children and osteoporosis in adults [1]. Additionally, micronutrients, including vitamin D, are essential for human function, cartilage health, and overall organ function [2]. Beyond bone health, vitamin D, known for its antioxidant properties, is linked to other health outcomes, including neoplasms, autoimmune disorders, and neurological conditions, which results are mixed and inconclusive [3].

Vitamin D deficiency (VDD) significantly increases the susceptibility to numerous health issues, and its worldwide prevalence is alarming. In India, over 20% of the population is severely deficient in vitamin D and nearly one-third is lacking [4]. The health of the Youth, the future leaders, is essential. This critical phase of life involves significant physical, physiological, psychological, and behavioral changes, influencing their current health and their likelihood of developing long-term illnesses as they age [5]. As young individuals undergo identity shifts during emerging adulthood, healthy lifestyle interventions can have a long-lasting impact on their health behaviors [6].

Various behavioral models have been proposed to understand health behaviors, with the Health Belief Model (HBM) developed by Rosenstock being widely applied as a theoretical foundation for predicting health behaviors and interventions across various conditions, including VDD. Several published papers focus on VDD in the Youth of India. In contrast, scant attention is given to the practical implementation of the HBM in this context.

## 4.3 Literature Review

VDD has emerged as worldwide issue for health care across every age bracket [7]. ### 4.3.1 Vitamin D Insufficiency Definition

The serum 25(OH)D value is a marker in Vitamin D assessment. A person who is insufficient in vitamin D should have the value of Serum 25(OH) D within or below the 50 nmol/L-75 nmol/L range. A value 25 or 30 nmol/L is a severe vitamin D deficient state that markedly raises the probability of dietary risks [12].

### 4.3.2 The Incidence of Inadequate Vitamin D

In Western society, a significant lack of sunshine rates is 5.9% in the United States, 7.4% in Canada, and 13% in Europe. In comparison, inadequate levels of vitamin D rates are 24% (US), 37% (Canada), and 40% (Europe). In countries like India, Pakistan, and Afghanistan, more than 20% of the Population is severely vitamin D deficient. India is densely populated, and about 490 million individuals are estimated to have insufficient vitamin D [13].
