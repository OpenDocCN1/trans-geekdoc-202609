
## Internet of Cloud: Secure and Privacy Preserving Cloud Model with IoT Enabled Service

Indrani Inapakolla, SVB Revanth, Siliveru Akhil Durga, Sirisha Potluri, Sachi Nandan Mohanty

**Abstract:** Security and safety are among the critical difficulties of Internet of Things (IoT). Unpatched and inappropriate gadgets, the lack of robust and effective security mechanisms, customer ignorance, and using a well-known powerful gadget are some of the problems IoT faces. In this work, we explore the basis of IoT frameworks and security efforts, and we see other safety and security matters, methods used to verify IoT-based components and structures, security arrangements in place, and the best protection models are important and suitable for the various layers of IoT-operated applications. In this work, we have proposed an enhanced IoT model: standard and expanded with security and safety components and layers of physical devices. The proposed cloud/edge IoT framework is developed and tested. The bottom layer comprised IoT devices created via Amazon Web Service (AWS), such as virtual machines. The centre layer (edge) has been developed as a resource unit for Raspberry Pi 4 with the help of Greengrass Edge Environment in AWS. We used cloud-enabled IoT core in AWS to create the top layer (cloud). Security mechanisms and basic management mechanisms were among all these areas to ensure the security of customer data. We have verified security mechanisms to allow data to flow between the layers of the proposed cloud-enabled model. Not only does the proposed framework model eliminate the perceived security vulnerabilities, but it can also be used alongside the best security measures to combat network security risks facing all the latter layers: cloud, edge, and IoT.

**Keywords:** Internet of Things, Cloud Computing, Cloud Privacy, Cloud Security, Edge Computing

## 1 Introduction

Internet of Things (IoT) points to the concept of articles related to gadgets of many kinds on the Internet or wireless Internet. The popularity of IoT or Internet of Things has grown rapidly, as these new devices are used for a variety of purposes, including literature, travel, education and business improvement. IoT introduced the concept of connectivity, which means that organizations and individuals can communicate from far and wide easily. Kevin Ashton coined the term ‘IoT’ in 1999 to promote the concept of Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), which combines sensors and actuators. Nevertheless, the first idea was introduced in the 1960s. At the time, the idea was inevitable to register or install the Internet. Ashton presented the IoT’s idea of promoting good exercise with chains. Be that as it may, the versatility of IoT has helped to boost strong availability during 2010. The Chinese government gave greater demand to the IoT by introducing a five-year plan. Approximately 26.66 billion IoT devices reside on the earth. The big bang started in 2011 with the introduction of home appliances, wearable gadgets, and smart energy meters. The rapid explosion of IoT has helped organizations and in various ways improved statistical and business research methods. In addition, IoT has improved the way people live by introducing computer management. However, uncontrolled explosions have exacerbated security and safety challenges [1].

The unrecognized use, weak password management, and the absence of updates to gadgets have increased the risks of online security and the acceptance of malicious applications on sensitive IoT frameworks. Such improper practices increase the likelihood of information leaks and accidents. The vast majority of security experts view the IoT as a vulnerable area for digital attacks due to weak security mechanisms and configurations. Although a few security items are designed to protect IoT gadgets from digital attacks, security rules are not properly documented. In line with these lines, end clients have not been able to use defensive tactics to deter information attacks. Attackers have created a variety of malware to compromise IoT gadgets since before 2008. They organize various forms of crime to steal sensitive information in order to coerce public representatives to share sensitive information. Similarly, workplaces and certain companies are constantly facing security breaches due to prominent attacks. If gadget manufacturers and security experts assess digital risks accurately, they can create a protective component to prevent or mitigate digital accidents [2].

IoT-enabled gadgets are used for automated applications and for various business purposes. Applications help these organizations achieve their goals. However, due to the unnecessary selection of various smart gadgets that share information and connectivity, access to security and information becomes a major concern for many organizations, as it interferes with workflow, operations, and organizational management. It is important for professionals to overcome these dangerous concerns and make comprehensive safety efforts and strategies to protect the resources of their business and ensure the continued integrity and security of management. For example, powerful IoT kitchen appliances associated with a local organization can be a source of entry for attackers to gain business access and steal relevant information, or to control and disrupt the business process.

New developments are emerging, or changes are being made in existing ones. Consider the latest developments in the 5G networks, for example. 5G is expected to take a major role in IoT frameworks and applications. It is significant enough to be recognized and carries safety and security risks, with its high frequency and data transfer. However, the shorter wavelength necessitates adjustment at the base, as a result of which the need for additional channels to cover the same region is met by new and different remote techniques [3].

In this work, we plan to provide a framework for IoT applications, benefits, and expected risks. In addition, building a structure that needs to be considered and further enhances the best practice by doing or researching existing programs or developing new ones. Based on the findings, we offer suggestions for avoiding such dangers and addressing potential safety concerns. This work will direct management organizations to continue to implement strategies, instruct stakeholders, clients, and IoT-related partners to create and implement appropriate security and protection measures.

We developed our model using Amazon Web Service (AWS) as a proof of concept, which later referred to real sensor frameworks that mimic the overall IoT structure. By creating a prototype, we can test and consider different security measures by creating real deployments and benchmarks.

We have adopted a case-study research philosophy to explore a set of experiments and the basics of IoT frameworks, their security and privacy issues, and countermeasures. We have expressed our views about the inclusive and expanded IoT model and its privacy and security. We designed and tested the cloud/edge IoT model with the physical devices (sensors), the hub edge (Raspberry Pi), and cloud management (AWS). This prototype was intended to test the model we have proposed in the sections accompanying this paper. Our work does not delve into different IoT applications (healthcare, smart cities, supply chain, transportation, etc.); their features, their preferences, and their challenges, or security risks that can be considered within these applications. The literature is rich in such works. In this work, we wanted to have a complete review of the literature and to lay the groundwork for further testing and evaluation.
