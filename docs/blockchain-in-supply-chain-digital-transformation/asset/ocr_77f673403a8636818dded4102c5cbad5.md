# 1. Introduction

Food safety is a major area of concern for all countries and its regulation is beneficial to international trade [37]. With the globalization of trade, food supply chains have become more diverse and complex, necessitating tighter food safety regulatory frameworks [5]. Countries’ regulators are often brought into action when food safety incidents occur which then leads to stricter regulations [8,45]. The Chinese government has gradually strengthened its legal provision with respect to food import over the past seventy years (see Appendix A). In recent years, the implementation of a more stringent regulatory framework was influenced by both domestic incidents [35], and the internationalisation of regulation [9]. Moving from a food hygiene to food safety regulation environment, the central government in China has developed stricter measures over the past two decades for both national production and imported products.
