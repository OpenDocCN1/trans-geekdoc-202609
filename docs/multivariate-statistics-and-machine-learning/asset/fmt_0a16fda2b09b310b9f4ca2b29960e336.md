
The assumption under the null is that population covariance matrices are equal. For these data, we reject the null (`p` = 2.2e-16) and infer the alternative hypothesis that at least two covariance matrices differ. When covariance matrices differ, it behooves the researcher to have a look at such matrices and to consider substantive reasons as to why they may differ. *Never obtain a statistical result without carefully considering what it might imply about your data on a scientific, not only statistical, level*. For additional theory on the test, see [Johnson and Wichern (2007)](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000173).

## 4.7 Linear Discriminant Analysis (as the “Reverse” of MANOVA)

The linear discriminant analysis model (that we have just briefly seen in our `MANOVA` example) features a binary or polytomous categorical response variable in which a linear combination of predictors is sought that maximizes group differences on the response. Discriminant analysis is sometimes referred to as a *filter model* and a *feature selection method* in the data mining literature ([Aggarwal, 2015](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000005)). While at first glance, this may be challenging for the reader to grasp or “see” the big picture, the `LDA` model can actually be regarded as a “hub” model, that is, a model from which many other models can be considered *scaled-up* or *scaled-down* versions of the `LDA` model structure.

For one, it can be operationalized in a few different ways, including being the “opposite” of the `MANOVA` model. This is the interpretation that most interests us here to supplement our discussion of `MANOVA` and also understand the above output provided in R by the `MANOVA` follow-up analysis or, crudely, “*post-hoc”* analysis (though it is not strictly a *post-hoc* *test* as we are not attempting to control for any error rates, we are using the word “*post-hoc*” here merely *descriptively*). `MANOVA` and `LDA` are often conceived as *opposites* of one another, and many times, a discriminant analysis will be performed as a follow-up to `MANOVA` to help learn more about the underlying function(s) (called the *discriminant function(s)*) responsible for separating groups so efficiently.
