## 6.4 Elastic Net

A third option for shrinking parameters is that of *elastic net*, with penalty equal to

$$\\left. {\\sum\\limits\_{j = 1}^{p}\\left( \\alpha \\middle| \\beta\_{j} \\middle| + \\left( 1 - \\alpha \\right) \\right.}\\beta\_{j}^{2} \\right)$$  

(6.10)

Elastic net is considered a compromise of sorts between the ridge and lasso, a kind of “hybrid” of the aforementioned estimators ([Hastie et al., 2009](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000145)). It can be shown that elastic net actually employs a linear combination of the ridge and lasso penalty functions (see [Izenman, 2008](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000167), p. 153 for details).

Note that elastic net features two coefficients, `β`[*j*]² and |`β`[*j*]|. To understand the role of `α`, the tuning parameter in this context, note that it is being applied to both `β`[*j*]² and |`β`[*j*]|, both the losses that are used in the ridge and lasso, respectively (i.e., quadratic and absolute function losses). Hence, we can see that the role of `α` is to differentially weight `β`[*j*]² and |`β`[*j*]|. This is why elastic net is interpreted as a “compromise” between ridge and lasso, and such compromise depends on the chosen value for `α` in differentially weighting the ridge and lasso L2 and L1 losses, respectively.

## 6.5 Which Regularization Penalty Is Better?

To conclude our discussion of these regularization approaches, it should be noted that neither ridge nor lasso nor elastic net will universally dominate each other. *There is no free lunch!* Lasso might perform better when a few coefficients are close to 0 and the rest quite substantial, where ridge often performs best when all coefficients are approximately equal, or at minimum, of equal importance to the investigator. However, there are no guarantees. The use of *cross-validation* in some capacity is recommended to determine which approach is better for a given data set, with tuning parameters selected via *grid search* on different values and cross-validation employed to observe which gives the lowest *test error*. To reiterate, we want to minimize the *test error*, not necessarily *train error*. This is why cross-validation is so important to choosing an optimal tuning parameter. See [Frank and Friedman (1993)](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000108) for a comparison of ordinary least-squares to not only ridge regression, but also principal components regression and partial least-squares.
