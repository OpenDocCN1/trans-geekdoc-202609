
With regard to the OceanGate submersible, perhaps to a large extent its prior success was based on capitalizing on random chance. Without theoretical justification, the experiences or instances of what you have obtained could simply be *lucky instances*. The fact that the submersible successfully visited the *Titanic* `n` times does not guarantee a successful visit on the (`n` + 1)th time. Regardless of how many successful trips there were prior to the accident, those successful trips were nonetheless a *sample*, and in this case not a random sample of all potential trips since most likely the probability of failure of the submersible structure increased the more times it traveled down to see the *Titanic* due to increasing structural fatigue of the vessel. A sample of successful voyages was observed, but making any kind of inference to the population of potential voyages requires some guesswork. *Statistical inference is guesswork. It is the art of imposing rigorous quantification on such guesswork.*

## 1.8 Level of Analysis Problem

Some physicists will claim that their discipline is the “fundamental” one, meaning that subjects such as biology, psychology, etc., are merely special cases of this fundamental discipline. Let’s briefly examine what this might mean, and how it introduces the *level of analysis problem*.

Suppose for a moment the physicist is correct, that all science reduces down to near infinitesimal particles, the presumed true building blocks of the universe of which all else is constructed. For the moment, we will call this the *deepest* level of analysis. Even if this is the case, the psychology experiment that finds a group difference in psychological health between those who practice mindfulness vs. those who do not could be considered a higher-level analysis (in the sense of a *top-down* view only) of these infinitesimally small particles. Whether the particles also conform to this group difference structure is simply a different *perspective*. The point is that the psychology experiment has revealed at a higher “bird’s eye” level a group difference and that group difference is just as meaningful as analyzing the atoms at a lower level. *The representation of the data is at different levels, but the phenomenon under study may be essentially the same*.

Hence, aspiring scientists should be aware that there are different *levels of analysis* and that they all contribute to understanding. Debating over which is the most “fundamental” unit of analysis is an interesting debate, but is not one that should detract from the triangulation of many different approaches to studying a given phenomenon.32

## 1.9 Base Rates, Common Denominators, and Degrees

So much of everything in research is about *base rates*. As a data analyst or researcher, you should always be aware of the base rate phenomenon whenever you either conduct or perform research. It is literally impossible to contextualize research correctly without such an awareness. Regarding the *Titan*, many experts in the area warned that the submersible may have problems due to its structural integrity or lack thereof. Upon first hearing there were concerns about the sub, it may have been tempting to interpret this information as *unique* to that particular submersible. The news reports brought to mind of a particular sub with significant weaknesses and dangers. Upon hearing these reports, however, it behooves the critical listener to consider *whether such dangers were unique to all submersibles of which the dangers with this one were simply a special case.*

What we are getting at with the above question is the concept of a *base rate*. Surface knowledge can be very misleading and is too often the only knowledge we “see.” There is usually always a base rate to any knowledge, but too often it’s the surface knowledge that will be incorrectly emphasized at the expense of the background or context layers. Now, it turns out that the issues with the submersible were *uniquely* significant and likely led to the demise of the sub and all who were aboard, but the point is that when you learn of abnormality or presumably “unique” problems with anything, always ask yourself whether those things are truly unique to the object you are working with or whether there is a deeper underlying *base rate* such that the unique characteristic is actually much more common than initially believed. Simply because an event is observed does not imply that such an event is unique *to the particular object you are working with*, even if on the surface the mind can quickly treat the characteristic as if it is unique.

[Gigerenzer (2003)](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000129) calls this awareness of base rates a *mind tool* that all researchers or anyone consuming research should practice and nurture in their thinking. The (first) attempted assassination on President Trump in 2024 was presumably carried out by a “loner” type. However, many human beings would fit that description and many more human beings likely have psychologies exponentially darker than that of the gunman. Simply because we observed a characteristic of the gunman does not explain, by itself, the behavior. Awareness of base rates here is essential. When the “warning signs” are characteristic of say, 20–30% of the population, they are not warning signs anymore. *They are features of human nature*.

Relativity also plays a role in the consideration of base rates. One begins to think less in terms of *categories* and more in terms of *degrees along a continuum*. Once more referring to the *Titan* submersible, it was said in the aftermath of the implosion that passengers should not be allowed into experimental submersibles or indeed any experimental transport due to the risks involved. But what is *not* experimental? Few would regard airline travel as experimental. Due to its safety record it has been put into the “category” of non-experimental travel. However, from a philosophy of science perspective, *airline travel is indeed still experimental;* *it is simply less so than venturing on the OceanGate submersible*. Airline travel is still a relatively recent invention. Relative to the entire span of humanity, people have been flying airplanes for a very short time. Contextualizing events along a continuum instead of assigning such events “black and white” labels is epistemologically fruitful. For many travelers, their Delta flight tomorrow may be “experimental” enough of a risk!

### 1.9.1 Base Rates and Splenic Masses

A failure to consider base rates can have drastic consequences on decision-making. German Shepherd Dogs are well known to be very intelligent and for the most part well-tempered and trainable dogs, which is one reason why they are often employed as officers in police departments and beloved by owners as family. However, they are unfortunately known to have health problems, especially as they age. One typical health problem is that of tumors of the spleen, which may be benign or malignant (cancerous). Though cancer is of course detrimental, the worst part of a splenic mass is the chance that it ruptures into the abdomen and the dog bleeds internally.33
