
Hence, in mimicking biological neuronal processes (or at minimum, *hypotheses* about such processes), self-organizing maps start with cells that “organize themselves” via updating themselves during training, a version of Hebbian learning ([Yin, 2008](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000398)). Such training concludes with the approximation of a smooth manifold ([Bishop, 2006](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000032)). Since there is no objective function to optimize, initial parameter estimates are crucial, perhaps “too important” since they could, in the end, essentially determine whether structure exists in the ensuing map ([Bishop, 2006](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000032)). The `SOM` algorithm can also be applied in a variety of contexts and customized to such applications. For example, various similarity measures can be adopted that suit the task at hand. That is, it need not be a distance function of the kind `x[i] − m[k]` discussed earlier, but instead can be an inner product ([Yin, 2008](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000398), p. 726). `SOMs` have also been extended to incorporate mixture distributions. Work has also been done on kernelized versions of `SOMs`.

Self-organizing maps can be fit in Python using `sklearn`’s `SOM` and in R using the package `aweSOM` (Boelaert et al., [2022](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000034)).441

In Figure 11.17, what is generally referred to as a *manifold*, sometimes called a *topological manifold*, since it satisfies certain topological conditions, such as being able to define continuous functions on that manifold. A manifold, first and foremost, is a topological space. A topological space is, roughly, a geometrical space where, for our purposes, measuring distances between points may prove challenging. A manifold is a topological space that is further *locally Euclidean* (Murray, 2022), which essentially generalizes the concept of a generalized surface in space. “Euclidean” here implies a differentiable mapping ([Hubbard & Hubbard, 2002](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000161)). By “locally Euclidean” we mean simply that at each point the space appears or resembles to be Euclidean, or, more crudely, *flat*. The space or neighborhood around each point should also consist of an *open set*, where by open set we may consider to be a higher-space generalization of an open interval on the one-dimensional real line in mathematics (Meila & Zhang, [2023](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000247)).

A classic and practical example of such a manifold is the earth, a curved two-dimensional surface, but everywhere on the earth it appears locally flat (Murray, 2022). The local two-dimensional space is sometimes referred to as the *tangent space*. Hence, when you walk down the street on what appears to be a relatively flat surface on the earth, you are exploring the *tangent space* of a locally sectioned part of the earth. The earth is round, but the tangent you are on appears flat. Indeed, a two-dimensional map of a country is not a perfect representation since the actual surface is not actually flat but instead spherical. `ℜ^(n)` is a manifold since everywhere you look you can be in `ℜ^(n)` space. Any finite-dimensional vector space can be regarded as a manifold as well.

A one-dimensional manifold is a smooth curve, for example that of a wire or garden hose ([Hubbard & Hubbard, 2002](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000161)). An ordinary line is a special case of a curve and thus a one-dimensional manifold as well. A *smooth* manifold is generally one that is many times differentiable or even infinitely differentiable in some cases. The absolute value function `f(x)=|x|` is an example 442of a simple manifold (curve) that is not smooth, as it is not differentiable at zero. The existence of a manifold is also independent of coordinates and can be easily translated.

Manifold learning and topology are largely about how to calculate on *local areas* of these more complex generalized surfaces. Differentiable manifolds are preferred as they allow one to perform calculus operations on the manifold. Topology essentially generalizes concepts of metric spaces (which are used to specify a distance function on that space) to the concept of *neighboring points* without having to specify a distance function explicitly.

Another way of saying this is that in the dimension reduction, the *essential elements of the geometry are preserved*. Of course, we can immediately deduce that classical principal components analysis, for example, is a special case of manifold learning, since a high-dimensional space is reduced to a lower-dimensional topological space via linear transformation. The previously discussed multidimensional scaling, Sammon mapping, and self-organizing maps also can be included under the more general theme of manifold learning.

### 11.12.1 Manifold Hypothesis

The *manifold hypothesis* holds that even though data may *appear* in higher dimensions, it is possible to place it in a lower-dimensional manifold (Murray, 2022). That is, the *learning* of a manifold is an attempt to take high-dimensional multivariate data and reduce it down to a lower dimensionality. One can think of a manifold as being an *embedding* from a higher- to lower-dimensional `R^(n)` space. Most embedding algorithms have the priority of identifying the set of eigenvectors associated with the most important (largest) eigenvalues. These methods are often referred to as *spectral embedding methods* ([Izenman, 2008](#ref.xhtml#re_CIT000167)), however some reserve the classification of an embedding as distinct from other methods that necessarily preserve geometric and topological data properties.

Manifold learning is generally equated with *nonlinear* dimensionality reduction since in most cases the actual true manifold underlying the data will be nonlinear and the linear hypothesis is merely a special case of the more general nonlinear framework. It is not an exaggeration to say that most linear manifold methods seek out *global* *structure* whereas most nonlinear manifold methods seek out more *local nonlinear structure*. This parallels the idea of a line capturing global behavior of a scatterplot whereas a curve (or spline, for example) would capture potential nonlinear and more local behavior.
