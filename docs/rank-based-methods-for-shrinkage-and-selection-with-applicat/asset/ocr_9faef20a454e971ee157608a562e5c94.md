\(\mathbf{y} = X\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}.\) (2.3.3)

Our goal is to study the estimation of \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) in a variety of different ways depending on the requirements of the problem. In our first approach, the quadratic or L\(_2\) loss function is as follows:

\(J(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = (\mathbf{y} - X\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{y} - X\boldsymbol{\beta}).\) (2.3.4)

To obtain the least squares estimate, \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\mathrm{LS}}\), we take the derivative of \(J(\boldsymbol{\beta})\) w.r.t. \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) and set it to 0, i.e.

\(\frac{\partial J(\boldsymbol{\beta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\beta}} = -2X^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{y} - X\boldsymbol{\beta}) = 0.\) (2.3.5)

Then, it follows that

\(-X^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y} + X^{\mathsf{T}}X\boldsymbol{\beta} = 0,\) (2.3.6)

which can be solved to obtain

\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\mathrm{LS}} = (X^{\mathsf{T}}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}\) (2.3.7)

provided \(X^{\mathsf{T}}X\) is invertible. This is the direct matrix solution for least squares estimation (LSE) and it provides the best linear unbiased estimate. It is also referred to in the literature as the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator.

The variance-covariance matrix of the estimates is given by

\(\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\mathrm{LS}}) = \sigma^2(X^{\mathsf{T}}X)^{-1} = \sigma^2\mathbf{C}^{-1},\) (2.3.8)

where \(\mathbf{C} = X^{\mathsf{T}}X\). Then, the variance of the \(j\)th parameter is

\(\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{LS}}) = \sigma^2 C^{jj}, \quad j = 1, \dots, p,\) (2.3.9)

where \(C^{jj}\) is the \(j\)th diagonal entry of \(\mathbf{C}^{-1}\). Hence, the standard error (s.e.) is

\(\mathrm{s.e.}(\hat{\beta}_j^{\mathrm{LS}}) = \hat{\sigma}\sqrt{C^{jj}}, \quad j = 1, \dots, p,\) (2.3.10)

where \(\hat{\sigma} = \sqrt{\mathrm{SSE}/(n - p)}\) is an unbiased estimator of \(\sigma\), and SSE is the sum of squares of the errors defined as

\(\mathrm{SSE} = (\mathbf{y} - X\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\mathrm{LS}})^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{y} - X\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\mathrm{LS}}).\) (2.3.11)

This can be summarized as \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\mathrm{LS}} \sim \mathcal{N}_p(\boldsymbol{\beta}, \sigma^2\mathbf{C}^{-1}).\)
