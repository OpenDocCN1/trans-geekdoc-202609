
We consider the expressions for the asymptotic distributional bias (ADB), L$_2$-risk (ADL$_2$-risk) and the weighted ADL$_2$-risk of various R-estimators. The ADB and ADL$_2$-risk expressions are defined by

\(\text{ADB}(\hat{\theta}_n^*) = \left( \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E} \left[ \sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{1n}^* - \theta_1)^\top \right], \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E} \left[ \sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{2n}^* - \theta_2)^\top \right] \right)^\top,\)
(4.4.5)

and

\(\text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_n^* : \mathbf{W}_1, \mathbf{W}_2) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E} \left[ n(\hat{\theta}_{1n}^* - \theta)^\top \mathbf{W}_1(\hat{\theta}_{1n}^* - \theta_1) \right]\)
\(+ \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E} \left[ n(\hat{\theta}_{n2}^* - \theta)^\top \mathbf{W}_2(\hat{\theta}_{2n}^* - \theta_2) \right]\)
\(= \text{tr}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{M}_1) + \text{tr}(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{M}_2) \quad (4.4.6)\)

where \(\mathbf{W}_1\) and \(\mathbf{W}_2\) are the weight matrices of \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) dimensions, respectively.

If \(\mathbf{W}_1 = \mathbf{I}_{p_1}\) and \(\mathbf{W}_2 = \mathbf{I}_{p_2}\), then we obtain the trace of the MSE matrices of \(\mathbf{M}_1\) and \(\mathbf{M}_2\) as \(\text{tr}(\mathbf{M}_1) = \sum_{i=1}^{p_1} \chi_i\) and \(\text{tr}(\mathbf{M}_2) = \sum_{i=p_1+1}^p \chi_i\), respectively, where

\(\mathbf{M}_i = \lim_{n \to \infty} n \mathbb{E} \left[ (\hat{\theta}_{in}^* - \theta_i)(\hat{\theta}_{in}^* - \theta_i)^\top \right], \quad \forall i = 1, 2.\)

Thus,

\(\text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_n^* : \mathbf{W}_1, \mathbf{W}_2) = \text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_{1n}^* : \mathbf{W}_{p_1})\)
\(+ \text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_{2n}^* : \mathbf{W}_{p_2}).\)

Briefly, the unrestricted R-estimator of \(\theta\) may be written as \((\hat{\theta}_{1n}^{\text{UR}\top}, \hat{\theta}_{2n}^{\text{UR}\top})^\top\) with the marginal asymptotic distribution of \(\hat{\theta}_{in}^{\text{UR}}\) given by \(\mathcal{N}_{p_i}(\theta_i, \eta^2 \mathcal{K}_i)\), where \(\mathcal{K} = \text{blockdiagonal}(\mathcal{K}_1, \mathcal{K}_2)\).

### Theorem 4.1 Under the local alternatives, \(K_{(n)}\), the asymptotic distributional bias, and ADL$_2$-risk of the unrestricted R-estimator, \(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}}\), are respectively given by

\(\text{ADB}(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}}) = (\mathbf{0}_{p_1}^\top, \mathbf{0}_{p_2}^\top)^\top,\)
\(\text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}}) = \eta^2 \left( \sum_{i=1}^{p_1} \chi_i + \sum_{i=p_1+1}^{p} \chi_i \right).\)

## 4.5 Test of Significance

From Eq. (4.4.2), the statistic

\(T_{n_j}(0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_j}} \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} \text{sgn}(y_{ij}) \alpha_{n_i}^+ (R_{n_i}^+(0))\)

may be used to test the null hypothesis \(\mathcal{H}_o : \theta_j = 0\) vs. \(\mathcal{H}_A : \theta_j \neq 0\). Note that \(\mathbb{E}[T_{n_j}(0)] = 0\) and

\(\text{Var}(T_{n_j}(0)) = \frac{1}{n_j} \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} [\alpha_{n_i}^+(i)]^2 = A_{n_j}^2.\)

It may be shown that, as \(n \to \infty\),

\(T_{n_j}(0) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}} \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, A_\phi^2 \kappa_j^{-1})\) (4.5.1)

and we can use \(\sqrt{\kappa_j}T_{n_j}(0)/A_{n_j}\) as the test statistic for \(\mathcal{H}_o : \theta_j = 0\) vs. \(\mathcal{H}_A : \theta_j \neq 0\). Using Hajek and Sidák (1967), as \(n \to \infty\) and \(\theta_j = 0\), we have that

\(\left| \sqrt{n}\hat{\theta}_{n_j}^{\text{UR}} - \gamma^{-1}(\phi, \psi)\kappa_j T_{n_j}(0) \right| \xrightarrow{P} 0.\) (4.5.2)

Then, after some rearrangement and substitutions, we obtain the statistic

\(\mathcal{L}_{n_j} = \frac{\kappa_j T_{n_j}^2(0)}{A_{n_j}^2}, \quad j=1, ..., p.\) (4.5.3)

Alternatively, we may define the statistic as

\(\mathcal{L}_{n_j} = \frac{n (\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}})^2}{\hat{\gamma}^2 \kappa_j}, \quad j=1, ..., p\) (4.5.4)

where \(\hat{\gamma} = \text{MAD}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{B}\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}})\) for the ANOVA model of Eq. (4.2.1).

For the whole vector, we have

\(\left| \sqrt{n}\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}} - \gamma^{-1}(\phi, \psi)\mathcal{K}\mathbf{T}_n(\mathbf{0}) \right| \xrightarrow{P} 0.\) (4.5.5)

Next, we consider the test of any sub-hypothesis

\(\mathcal{H}_o : \theta_2 = (\theta_{p_1+1}, ..., \theta_p)^\top = \mathbf{0} \text{ vs. } \mathcal{H}_A : \theta_2 \neq \mathbf{0}.\)

In this case, one may use the statistic, \(\mathcal{L}_{n(2)}\), given by

\(\mathcal{L}_{n(2)} = \mathbf{A}_{n(2)}^{-2} \mathbf{T}^\top_{n(2)}(\mathbf{0})\mathcal{K}_2 \mathbf{T}_{n(2)}(\mathbf{0}),\)

where

$T_n(\mathbf{0}) = (T_{n(1)}^\top(\mathbf{0}), T_{n(2)}^\top(\mathbf{0}))^\top, \quad A_n = \text{Diag}(\mathbf{A}_{n(1)}, \mathbf{A}_{n(2)}), \quad \mathcal{K} = \text{Diag}(\mathcal{K}_1, \mathcal{K}_2),$

and $T_{n(2)}(0)$ is $p_2$-vector. We also use the definition

$A^2_{n(2)} = \sum_{j=p_1+1}^p (n_j - 1) A_{nj}^2.$ (4.5.6)

Under the local alternatives,

$K_{(n)} : \theta_{\eta_j} = n^{-\frac{1}{2}} \delta_j, \quad \text{for } j = 1, ..., p.$

From chapter 5, section 5.2 of Hájek and Sidák (1967), we have

\(\forall j = 1, ..., p, \quad Z_j = \frac{\sqrt{n} \hat{\theta}_{nj}^{\text{UR}}}{\eta \sqrt{\kappa_j}} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}} \mathcal{N}(\Delta_j, 1), \quad \frac{\delta_j}{\eta \sqrt{\kappa_j}} = \Delta_j.\)

Under the assumed regularity conditions, as \(n \to \infty\), \(\mathcal{L}_{n(2)}\) is asymptotically distributed as a non-central \(\chi^2\)-distribution with \(p_2\) degrees of freedom (d.f.) and non-centrality parameter

\(\Delta^2 = \frac{1}{\eta^2} \delta_2^\top \mathcal{K}_2^{-1} \delta_2, \quad \delta_2 = (\delta_{p_1+1}, ..., \delta_p)^\top\)

using the local alternatives \(\theta_{2(n)} = n^{-\frac{1}{2}} \delta_2\).

## 4.6 Restricted R-estimator

The restricted R-estimator (RRE) of \((\theta_1^\top, \mathbf{0}^\top)^\top\) is \((\hat{\theta}_{1n}^{\text{UR}^\top}, \mathbf{0}^\top)^\top\).

### Theorem 4.2 Under the local alternatives, \(K_{(n)}\), the asymptotic distributional bias, and \(\text{ADL}_2\)-risk of the restricted R-estimator, \(\hat{\delta}_n^{\text{RR}}\), are respectively given by

\(\text{ADB}(\hat{\delta}_n^{\text{RR}}) = (\mathbf{0}_{p_1}^\top, -\delta_2^\top)^\top \quad \text{and}\)

\(\text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\delta}_n^{\text{RR}}) = \eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^{p_1} \kappa_i + \delta_2^\top \delta_2.\)

The lower bound of \(\text{ADL}_2\)-risk is given by

\(\text{ADL}_2^*\text{-risk}(\hat{\delta}_n^{\text{RR}}) = \eta^2 \{ \text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_1) + \Delta^2 \text{Ch}_{\min}(\mathcal{K}_2) \}, \quad \Delta^2 = \frac{1}{\eta^2} \delta_2^\top \mathcal{K}_2^{-1} \delta_2.\)

Hence,

ADRE(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{RR}} : \hat{\theta}_n^{\text{UR}}) = \left( 1 + \frac{\text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_2)}{\text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_1)} \right) \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta^2 \text{Ch}_{\min}(\mathcal{K}_2)}{\text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_1)} \right)^{-1}.

## 4.7 Shrinkage Estimators

When the problem is sparse, that is, we suspect that some estimates may be 0, we can partition the problem into two subsets, i = 1,2, and retain subset 1 while applying shrinkage estimators of various forms to the potentially null subset 2.

### 4.7.1 Preliminary Test R-estimator

We partition the parameters as \(\theta = (\theta_1^\top, \theta_2^\top)^\top\), where \(\theta_i\) is a \(p_i\)-vector then \(p_1 + p_2 = p\). Now, we may define the preliminary test R-estimator of \(\theta\) as follows

\(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{PTR}}(\lambda) = \left( \hat{\theta}_{1n}^{\text{UR}\top}, \hat{\theta}_{2n}^{\text{UR}\top} I(\mathcal{L}_n > \lambda^2) \right)^\top\)

where \(I(A)\) is the indicator function of the set \(A\) and \(\mathcal{L}_n\) is given by

\(\mathcal{L}_n = A_{n(2)}^{-2} T_{n(2)}(\mathbf{0}) \mathcal{K}_2^{-1} T_{n(2)}(\mathbf{0})\)

and \(\chi_1^2(\lambda^2)\) is the \(\lambda\)-level upper critical value of a central \(\chi^2\) distribution with \(p_2\) d.f.

### Theorem 4.3 Under the local alternatives, \(K_{(n)}\), the asymptotic distributional bias, and ADL\(_2\)-risk of the preliminary test R-estimator, \(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{PTR}}(\lambda)\), are respectively given by

\(\text{ADB}(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{PTR}}(\lambda)) = (\mathbf{0}_{p_1} - \delta_2^\top \mathcal{H}_{p_2+2}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda^2), \Delta^2))^\top\)

and

\(\text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{PTR}}(\lambda)) = \eta^2(\text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_1) + \text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_2)) - \eta^2 \text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_2) \mathcal{H}_{p_2+2}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda), \Delta^2) + \theta_2^\top \mathcal{H}_2 \{ 2 \mathcal{H}_{p_2+2}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda^2), \Delta^2) - \mathcal{H}_{p_2+4}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda^2), \Delta^2) \}.\)

The lower bound of the ADL\(_2\)-risk is given by

\(\text{ADL}_2^*\text{-risk}(\hat{\theta}_n^{\text{PTR}}(\lambda)) = \eta^2 \left[ \text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_1) + \text{tr}(\mathcal{K}_2)(1 - \mathcal{H}_{p_2+2}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda^2), \Delta^2)) + \Delta^2 \text{Ch}_{\min}(\mathcal{K}_2) \{ 2 \mathcal{H}_{p_2+2}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda^2), \Delta^2) - \mathcal{H}_{p_2+4}(\chi_{p_2}^2(\lambda^2), \Delta^2) \} \right].\)
