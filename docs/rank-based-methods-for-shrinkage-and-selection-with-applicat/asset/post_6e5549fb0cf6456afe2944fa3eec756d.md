
$$X_t = \rho_1 X_{t-1} + ... + \rho_{p_1} X_{t-p_1} + \varepsilon_t, \quad 1 \le t \le n, p_1 \ge 1. \quad (9.1.4)$$

Our primary interest in this chapter is to develop the theory of R-estimation of various types, beginning with selection of subsets and ending with shrinkage R-estimation.

## 9.2 R-estimation of $\rho$ for the AR($p$)-Model

In this section, we introduce a class of R-estimators of $\rho$ for the AR($p$) model of Eq. (9.1.1) and discuss their large sample properties. Let $Y_t = (X_t, ..., X_{t-p+1})^\top, 1 \le t \le n$, and $R_t(b)$ be the rank of $X_t - b^\top Y_{t-1}$ among $\{X_j - b^\top Y_{j-1}, 1 \le j \le n\}$. Set $R_i(b) = 0$ for $i \le 0$. Let $\phi$ be a non-decreasing function from $[0, 1]$ to the real line and define $L_n(b) = (L_1(b), ..., L_p(b))^\top$, where

$$L_s(b) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{t=s+1}^n X_{t-s} \phi \left( \frac{R_t(b)}{n+1} \right), \quad 1 \le s \le p, \ b \in \mathbb{R}^p. \quad (9.2.1)$$

The class of rank statistics, $L_n(b)$, one for each $\phi$, is analogous to a class of similar rank statistics in linear regression models where one replaces the weights $\{X_{t-j}\}$ by appropriate design points.

Thus, for the R-estimation of $\rho$, we apply Jaeckel (1972) to the AR($p$)-model. Accordingly, we set

$$a_n(t) = \phi \left( \frac{t}{n+1} \right), \quad 1 \le t \le n,$$

and $Z_{(t)}(b)$ is the $t$th largest residual among $\{X_k - b^\top Y_{k-1}, 1 \le k \le n\}$, and

$$D_n(b) = \sum_{t=1}^n a_n(t) Z_{(t)}(b), \quad b \in \mathbb{R}^p. \quad (9.2.2)$$

If $\sum_{t=1}^n a_n(t) = 0$, then $D_n(b)$ may be shown to be convex on $\mathbb{R}^p$ with its derivative \(-n^{-\frac{1}{2}} L_n(b)\). Thus, a minimum of $D_n(b)$ exists.

Let us now define the R-estimator $\hat{\rho}_n^{UR}$ of $\rho$ by the relation

$$\inf_{b \in \mathbb{R}^p} \| L_n(b) \| = \left\| L_n(\hat{\rho}_n^{UR}) \right\| . \quad (9.2.3)$$

The following theorem gives the asymptotic uniform linearity (AUL) result similar to Jurečková (1971) for ordinary regression models.

### **Theorem 9.1** (Koul and Saleh, 1993) (AUL of Statistics). Assume Eqs. (9.1.1) and (9.1.2) hold. In addition, assume the following:

- (a) (i) \(\mathsf{E}[\varepsilon] = 0, 0 < \mathsf{E}[\varepsilon^4] < \infty\),
- (ii) \(F\) has uniformly continuous density \(f, f > 0\) a.e.
- (b) \(\phi\) is non-decreasing and differentiable with its derivative, \(\phi'\), being uniformly continuous on \([0, 1]\).

Then, for every \(0 < M < \infty\),

\(\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|\le M} \left\| \mathbf{L}_n(\boldsymbol{\rho} + n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}) - \hat{\mathbf{L}}_n + \mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\gamma \right\| = o_p(1)\) (9.2.4)

where \(\hat{\mathbf{L}}_n = (\hat{L}_1, ..., \hat{L}_p)^{\top}\) with

\(\hat{L}_j = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=j+1}^n (X_{i-j} - \bar{X}_j) \left[ \phi(F(\varepsilon_i)) - \bar{\phi} \right], \quad 1 \le j \le p\) (9.2.5)

and

\(\bar{\phi} = \int_0^1 \phi(\omega)\mathrm{d}\omega\)
\(\bar{X}_j = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=j+1}^n X_{t-j}\)
\(\gamma = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} f(t)\mathrm{d}\phi(F(t))\)
\(\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = (\sigma(i - j)), i = 1, ..., p; j = 1, ..., p\)

where

\(\sigma(k) = \text{Cov}(X_0, X_k), \quad 1 \le k \le p.\)

The above theorem covers Wilcoxon-type scores but not the normal score. Using the AUL result of Eq. (9.2.4), one may obtain

\(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\rho}_n^{\text{UR}} - \boldsymbol{\rho}) = \gamma^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{L}}_n + o_p(1), \quad \gamma = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} f\mathrm{d}\phi(F).\) (9.2.6)

Observe that \(\hat{\mathbf{L}}_n\) is a vector of square integrable mean zero martingale with

\(\mathbb{E}\left[ \hat{\mathbf{L}}_n \hat{\mathbf{L}}_n^{\top} \right] = A_{\phi}^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}\) where \( A_\phi^2 = \text{Var}(\phi(\boldsymbol{u})) \). Thus, by a routine Cramér–Wold device and Corollary 3.1 of Hall and Heyde (1981), one obtains as \( n \to \infty \),

\(\hat{\boldsymbol{L}}_n \to^{\mathcal{D}} \mathcal{N}_p(\mathbf{0}, A_\phi^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}), \quad A_\phi^2 = \int_0^1 \phi^2(u)du \tag{9.2.7}\)

and hence,

\(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\rho}_n^{\text{UR}} - \rho) \to^{\mathcal{D}} \mathcal{N}_p(\mathbf{0}, \eta^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}), \quad \eta^2 = \frac{A_\phi^2}{\gamma^2}. \tag{9.2.8}\)

## 9.3 LASSO, Ridge, Preliminary Test and Stein–Saleh-type R-estimators

In this section, we present various R-estimators of \( \rho \). First, we consider the usual penalty R-estimators beginning with the hard threshold R-estimator (HTE). We shall refer to this estimator as the preliminary test subset selector (PTSS). It is defined by

\(\hat{\rho}_n^{\text{PTSS}}(\lambda) = \left( \hat{\rho}_{jn}^{\text{UR}} I(|\hat{\rho}_{jn}^{\text{UR}}| > \lambda \eta \sqrt{\sigma_n^{jj}} ) \mid j = 1, \dots, p \right)^{\top}, \tag{9.3.1}\)

where \( \sigma_n^{ii} \) is the (ii)-element of \( \Sigma_n^{-1} \). A continuous version of the PTSS is the Saleh-type selector

\(\hat{\rho}_n^{\text{SS}}(\lambda) = \left( \hat{\rho}_{jn}^{\text{UR}} \left( 1 - \frac{\lambda \eta}{|\hat{\rho}_{jn}^{\text{UR}}|} \sqrt{\sigma_n^{jj}} \right) \mid j = 1, \dots, p \right)^{\top}. \tag{9.3.2}\)

One of the most important penalty R-estimators is the LASSO due to Tibshirani (1996) (alternatively referred to as soft-threshold or positive-rule Saleh-type) R-estimator which is given by

\(\hat{\rho}_n^{\text{LassoR}}(\lambda) = \left( \text{sgn}(\hat{\rho}_{jn}^{\text{UR}})(|\hat{\rho}_{jn}^{\text{UR}}| - \lambda \eta \sqrt{\sigma_n^{jj}})^+ \mid j = 1, \dots, p \right)^{\top}, \tag{9.3.3}\)

where \( \hat{\rho}_n^{\text{UR}} = (\hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{UR}}, \dots, \hat{\rho}_{pn}^{\text{UR}})^{\top} \) and \( \sigma_n^{jj} \) is the \( j \)th diagonal element of the estimator of the matrix, \( \Sigma_n^{-1} \) defined by

\(\Sigma_n = (\sigma_{nij}) \quad \text{with} \quad \sigma_{nij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n-\max\{i,j\}} (X_{k-i} - \bar{X}_i)(X_{k-j} - \bar{X}_j), \quad 1 \le i, j \le p \tag{9.3.4}\) which is the estimator of \(\Sigma\) defined in Eq. (9.2.2). We partition \(\Sigma\) as

\[
\Sigma = \left( \begin{array}{cc} \Sigma_{11} & \Sigma_{12} \\ \Sigma_{21} & \Sigma_{22} \end{array} \right) \text{ and } \Sigma_n = \left( \begin{array}{cc} \Sigma_{n11} & \Sigma_{n12} \\ \Sigma_{n21} & \Sigma_{n22} \end{array} \right) \quad (9.3.5)
\]

where \(\Sigma_{11}\), \(\Sigma_{12}\), and \(\Sigma_{22}\) are \(p_1 \times p_1, p_1 \times p_2, p_2 \times p_2\) matrices, respectively, with \(\Sigma_{12} = \Sigma_{21}\) and similarly for \(\Sigma_{n11}, \Sigma_{n12}, \Sigma_{n22}\) with \(\Sigma_{n12} = \Sigma_{n21}\), respectively. We let

\[
\Sigma_{nrr.s} = \Sigma_{nrr} - \Sigma_{nrs}\Sigma_{ss}^{-1}\Sigma_{nsr}, r \neq s = 1, 2.
\]

Define \(\Sigma_{rr.s}\) analogously \(r \neq s = 1, 2\) using the above partition of \(\Sigma\) (or \(\Sigma_n\)). Note that \(E[\epsilon_i^2] < \infty\) and the ergodic theorem implies \(n^{-1}\Sigma_n \to \Sigma\) and \(n^{-1}\Sigma_{nrr.s} \to \Sigma_{rr.s}\) as \(n \to \infty\). Since \(\Sigma^{-1}\) exists, \(\Sigma_n^{-1}\) and \(\Sigma_{nrr.s}^{-1}\) also exist for a sufficiently large \(n\).

Using subset selectors, namely, PTSS or LASSO, we may partition \(\rho\) into two subsets \(\rho^\top = (\rho_1^\top, \rho_2^\top)\) when \(\rho_i\) is a \(p_i, i = 1, 2\) vector. We are primarily interested in the R-estimation of \(\rho\) when we suspect that \(\rho_2\) may be sparse. For this case, we partition \(L_n(\boldsymbol{u}) = \left( \boldsymbol{L}_{n(1)}^\top(\boldsymbol{u}), \boldsymbol{L}_{n(2)}^\top(\boldsymbol{u}) \right)^\top\) and \(\boldsymbol{Y}_{i-1}^\top = (\boldsymbol{Y}_{i,1}^\top, \boldsymbol{Y}_{i,2}^\top)\) where

\[
\boldsymbol{L}_{n(1)} = \left( L_1(u), \dots, L_{p_1}(u) \right)^\top, \quad \boldsymbol{L}_{n(2)} = \left( L_{p_1+1}(u), \dots, L_p(u) \right)^\top
\]

\[
\boldsymbol{Y}_{i,1} = \left( X_{i-1}, \dots, X_{i-p_1} \right)^\top, \quad \boldsymbol{Y}_{i,2} = \left( X_{i-p_1+1}, \dots, X_{i-p} \right)^\top, \quad 1 \le i \le n.
\]

Further, consider the estimator \(\Sigma_n\) of \(\Sigma\), when

\[
\Sigma_n = \left( \sigma_{nij} \right) \text{ with } \sigma_{nij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n-\max(i,j)} (X_{k-i} - \bar{X})(X_{k-j} - \bar{X}), \quad 1 \le i, j \le n
\]

as in Eq. (9.3.4).

Next, partition the R-estimation of \(\rho\) as \(\hat{\rho}_n^{\text{UR}} = \left( \hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{UR}\top}, \hat{\rho}_{2n}^{\text{UR}\top} \right)^\top\) and call \(\hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{UR}}\) the unrestricted rank estimator (URE) of \(\rho_1\). The restricted R-estimator of \(\rho_1\) is given by

\[
\inf_{\boldsymbol{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_1}} \| L_{n(1)}(\boldsymbol{u}, \boldsymbol{0}) \| = \| L_{n(1)}(\hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{RR}}, \boldsymbol{0}) \|.
\]

For the test of \(\mathcal{H}_o : \rho_2 = \boldsymbol{0}\), we consider the test-statistic as

\[
\mathcal{L}_n = n\sigma^{-2} \hat{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{n(2)}^\top \Sigma_{n22.1}^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{n(2)}. \quad (9.3.6)
\]

It can be shown that under \(\mathcal{H}_o\), \(\mathcal{L}_n\) asymptotically has the central chi-square distribution with \(p_2\) d.f. Then, the preliminary test R-estimator (PTRE) is defined by

\[
\hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{PTR}}(\lambda) = \hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{RR}} + (\hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{UR}} - \hat{\rho}_{1n}^{\text{RR}}) I(\mathcal{L}_n > \lambda^2). \quad (9.3.7)
