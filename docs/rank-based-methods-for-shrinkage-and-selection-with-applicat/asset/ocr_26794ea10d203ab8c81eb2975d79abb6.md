where Ch$_{max(min)}(A)$ is the largest (smallest) eigenvalue of $A$. We obtain

\(\eta^2\Delta^2 Ch_{min}(C_{22:1}^{-1}) \le \delta_2^\top \delta_2 \le \eta^2\Delta^2 Ch_{max}(C_{22:1}^{-1}).\) (6.3.10)

Thus, the lower-bound of ADL$_2$-risk($\hat{\beta}_n^{RR}$), denoted using ‘*’, is given by

\(ADL_2^{*risk}(\hat{\beta}_n^{RR}) = \eta^2 tr(C_{11}^{-1}) + \Delta^2 Ch_{min}(C_{22:1}^{-1}).\)

The ADRE of $\hat{\beta}_n^{RR}$ is given by

\(ADRE(\hat{\beta}_n^{RR} : \hat{\beta}_n^{UR}) = \frac{tr(C_{11:2}^{-1}) + tr(C_{22:1}^{-1})}{tr(C_{11}^{-1}) + \Delta^2 Ch_{min}(C_{22:1}^{-1})},\)
\(= \left( 1 + \frac{tr(C_{22:1}^{-1})}{tr(C_{11:2}^{-1})} \right) \left( \frac{tr(C_{11:2}^{-1})}{tr(C_{11}^{-1})} \right)\)
\(\times \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta^2 Ch_{min}(C_{22:1}^{-1})}{tr(C_{11}^{-1})} \right)^{-1}\) (6.3.11)

where Ch$_{min}(A)$ is the minimum eigenvalue of matrix $A$.

If we use weights $C_{11:2}$ and $C_{22:1}$, then we obtain

\(ADRE(\hat{\beta}_n^{RR} : \hat{\beta}_n^{UR}) = \frac{p_1 + p_2}{tr(C_{11}^{-1}C_{11:2}) + \Delta^2}, \text{ where } \Delta^2 = \frac{\delta_2^\top C_{11:2} \delta_2}{\eta^2}.\) (6.3.12)

# ## 6.4 General Shrinkage R-estimator of \(\beta\)

In this section, we assume sparsity of \(\beta = (\beta_1^\top, \beta_2^\top)^\top\), i.e. \(\beta_2\) may be at or near **0**. In general, the shrinkage R-estimator of \(\beta\) is defined as

\(\hat{\beta}_n^{ShrinkageR}_{(c)} = (\hat{c}\beta_{1n}^{UR\top}, c\hat{\beta}_{2n}^{UR\top})^\top, \quad 0 < c < 1\)

where \(c\) may be constant or random. Thus, under local alternatives, \(\beta_{2(n)} : n^{-\frac{1}{2}} \delta_2\), we have

\(ADB(\hat{\beta}_n^{ShrinkageR}_{(c)}(c)) = (\mathbf{0}, -(1-c)\delta_2)^\top,\)

and

\(ADL_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\beta}_n^{ShrinkageR}_{(c)}(c)) = \eta^2 tr(C_{11:2}^{-1}) + c^2\eta^2 tr(C_{22:1}^{-1}) + (1-c)^2 \delta_2^\top \delta_2.\) (6.4.1)
