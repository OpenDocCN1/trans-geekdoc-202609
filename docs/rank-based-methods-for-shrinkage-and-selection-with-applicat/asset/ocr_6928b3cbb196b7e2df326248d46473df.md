Following Turkmen and Ozturk (2014) one may define a rank-based Liu estimator as

```
\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{LiuR}} = \text{argmin}_{\beta \in \mathbb{R}^p} \{ D_n(\beta) + \| d_n \hat{\beta}_n^{\text{UR}} - \beta \|^2 \},
```

where \(d_n \in (0, 1)\) is the regularization parameter.

In order to find a closed form ridge-type rank estimator based on \(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{UR}}\), Saleh et al. (2019) suggested to minimize the distance between \(\beta\) and the R-estimator \(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{UR}}\) to combat multicollinearity in robust regression. Using the marginal theory, they derived a closed form estimator given by

```
\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{ridgeR}}_{(k)} = \left( \hat{\beta}_{1n}^{\text{UR}^\top}, \frac{1}{1+k} \hat{\beta}_{2n}^{\text{UR}^\top} \right)^\top .
```

Indeed, the form \(\frac{1}{1+k} \hat{\beta}_{2n}^{\text{UR}^\top}\) is reminiscent of the contraction estimator defined by Mayer and Wilke (1973) in classical theory.

The marginal theory due to Saleh et al. (2019) can also be used to define a closed form Liu-type R-estimator. In this case, one may consider it as

```
\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{LiuR}}_{(d)} = \text{argmin}_{(\beta_1, \beta_2) \in \mathbb{R}^p} \{ D_n(\beta_1, \beta_2) + \| d \hat{\beta}_{2n}^{\text{UR}^\top} - \beta_2 \|^2 \} = \left( \hat{\beta}_{1n}^{\text{UR}^\top}, \frac{1+d}{2} \hat{\beta}_{2n}^{\text{UR}^\top} \right)^\top
```

where one suspects that \(\beta_2 = \mathbf{0}\) may hold. Hence, the performance analysis of \(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{LiuR}}(d)\) is similar to \(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{ridgeR}}(k)\). In order to add some fresh ideas, we follow the construction methodology that was initially used by Akdeniz and Kaciranlar (2001), i.e. we pre-multiply the R-estimator by a factor.
