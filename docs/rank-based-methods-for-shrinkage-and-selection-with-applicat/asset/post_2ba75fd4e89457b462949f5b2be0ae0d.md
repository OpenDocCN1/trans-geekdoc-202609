

Case 1: Suppose \(\eta^T F_d^T(C + I_p)^{-1} \beta\) is positive, then the R.H.S. of Eq. (8.5.4) is negative, since \(\eta^T F_d^T(C + I_p)^{-1} \beta\) is the expectation of a positive random variable is positive. Thus, for all \(\Delta^2\) and \(d\)

\[ ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur+}(d)) \le ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur}(d)). \]

Therefore, under this condition, the PRLRE not only confirms the inadmissibility of SSLRE but also provides a simple superior estimator for the ill-conditioned data.

Case 2: Suppose \(\eta^T F_d^T(C + I_p)^{-1} \beta\) is negative, then the difference in Eq. (8.5.4) will be positive when

\[ \eta^T F_d^T F_d \eta \ge \frac{f_5(\Delta^2, d)}{g_4(\Delta^2)}, \tag{8.5.5} \]

where

\[ f_5(\Delta^2, d) = 2(1 - d)\eta^T F_d^T(C + I_p)^{-1} \beta \, a_3 - \eta^2 tr(F_d^T A F_d) \, a_1 \]

and

\[ g_4(\Delta^2) = -(a_2 + 2a_3) \].

Since \(\Delta^2 > 0\), assume that both the numerator and the denominator of Eq. (8.5.6) are positive or negative, respectively. Then \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur+}(d)\) dominates \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur}(d)\), when

\[ \Delta^2 \le \Delta_5^2(\Delta^2, d) = \frac{f_6(\Delta^2, d)}{Ch_{\max}[(F_d^T F_d)C^{-1}] \times g_7(\Delta^2)} \]

and \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur}(d)\) dominates \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur+}(d)\), when

\[ \Delta^2 > \Delta_6^2(\Delta^2, d) = \frac{f_9(\Delta^2, d)}{Ch_{\min}[(F_d^T F_d)C^{-1}] \times g_7(\Delta^2)}. \]

## 8.5.4 Comparison of Liu-Type Rank Estimators With Counterparts

Here, we only consider the case when the null hypothesis does not hold, which is more general. Further we only compare SSLRE with SSRE. For more details and similar comparisons, we refer the reader to Arashi et al. (2014).

The risk difference between SSRE and SSLRE is

\[
\begin{aligned}
ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSR}) - ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiur}(d)) = \\
\eta^2 tr(C^{-1} - F_d^T C^{-1} F_d) - \eta^2(q - 2)tr(A - F_d^T A F_d)X(\Delta^2) \\
+(q - 2)\eta^T(I - F_d^T F_d)\eta Y(\Delta^2) \\
+2(q - 2)(1 - d)\eta^T F_d^T[C + I_p]^{-1} \beta \mathbb{E} \left[ \chi_{q+2}^{-2}(\Delta^2) \right] \\
+(1 - d)^2 \beta^T [C + I_p]^{-2} \beta.
\end{aligned}
\]

The above difference will be greater than or equal to 0, when

\[ \eta^T(I - F_d^T F_d)\eta \ge \frac{h_1(\Delta^2, d)}{Y(\Delta^2)} \]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_1(\Delta^2, d) = -\eta^2 \text{tr}(C^{-1} - F_d^T C^{-1} F_d) &+ \eta^2(q - 2) \text{tr}(A - F_d^T A F_d)X(\Delta^2) \\
&- 2(q - 2)(1 - d)\eta^T F_d^T [C + I_p]^{-1} \beta E \left[ \chi_{q+2}^{-2}(\Delta^2) \right] \\
&-(1 - d)^2 \beta^T [C + I_p]^{-2} \beta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(\Delta^2 > 0\), we assume that the numerator of Eq. (8.5.6) is positive. Then, the SSLRE will dominate SSRE whenever \(\Delta^2 \le \Delta_2^2(\Delta^2, d)\), where

\[ \Delta_2^2(\Delta^2, d) = \frac{h_1(\Delta^2, d)}{(q - 2)Ch_{\max}[(I - F_d^T F_d)C^{-1}]Y(\Delta^2)}. \]

However, SSRE dominates SSLRE when

\[ \Delta^2 < \Delta_g^2(\Delta^2, d) = \frac{h_1(\Delta^2, d)}{(q - 2)Ch_{\min}[(I - F_d^T F_d)C^{-1}]Y(\Delta^2)}. \]

Then, we compare these two estimators based on the biasing parameter, d. For our purposes, we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiUR}(d)) = \eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(\lambda_i + d)^2}{\lambda_i(\lambda_i + 1)^2}
- \eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(q - 2)q_i(\lambda_i + d)^2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2}X(\Delta^2) \\
+ 2(q - 2)(1 - d) \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{\theta_i \eta_i^*(\lambda_i + d)}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2} E \left[ \chi_{q+2}^{-2}(\Delta^2) \right] \\
+ \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(q - 2)\eta_i^{*2}(\lambda_i + d)^2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2}Y(\Delta^2)
+ (1 - d)^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{\theta_i^2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now differentiating Eq. (8.5.7) with respect to the biasing parameter d, we get

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiUR}(d))}{\partial d} = \eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{2(q - 2)}{\lambda_i(\lambda_i + 1)^2} \\
\times \{(\lambda_i + d) - \lambda_i q_{ii}(\lambda_i + d)X(\Delta^2) + \eta^2 \lambda_i \eta_i^{\star 2} (\lambda_i + d)Y(\Delta^2) \\
+ \eta^2 \lambda_i (\theta_i \eta_i^\star - \theta_i \eta_i^\star \lambda_i - 2\theta_i \eta_i^\star d) \mathbb{E} [ \chi_{q+2}^{-2}(\Delta^2) ] - (q-2)^{-1} \eta^2 \lambda_i (1-d) \theta_i^2 \} \\
= \eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2} (h_2(\Delta^2, \alpha)d - h_3(\Delta^2, \alpha))
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[ h_2(\Delta^2, \alpha) = \min_i \{ 1 - \lambda_i q_{ii} X(\Delta^2) + \eta^{-2} \lambda_i \eta_i^{\star 2} Y(\Delta^2) - 2\eta^{-2} \lambda_i \theta_i \eta_i^\star \mathbb{E} [ \chi_{q+2}^{-2}(\Delta^2) ] + (q-2)^{-1} \eta^{-2} \lambda_i \theta_i^2 \} \]

and

\[ h_3(\Delta^2, \alpha) = \max_i \{ \lambda_i ( \lambda_i q_{ii} X(\Delta^2) - 1 - \eta^{-2} \lambda_i \eta_i^{\star 2} Y(\Delta^2) - \tau_\psi^{-2} \theta_i \eta_i^\star (1 - \lambda_i) + (q-2)^{-1} \eta^2 \theta_i^2 ) \} \]

Now we define

\[ d_3(\Delta^2, \alpha) = \frac{h_2(\Delta^2, \alpha)}{h_3(\Delta^2, \alpha)} \]

Suppose d > 0, then we have the following:

- 1. If \(h_i(\Delta^2, \alpha) > 0, i = 2, 3\), it follows that for each positive d with \(d > d_3(\Delta^2, \alpha)\), \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiUR}(d)\) has risk value less than that of \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSR}\).
- 2. If \(h_i(\Delta^2, \alpha) < 0, i = 2, 3\), it follows that for each positive d with \(d < d_3(\Delta^2, \alpha)\), \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSLiUR}(d)\) has risk value less than that of \(\hat{\beta}_n^{SSR}\).

## 8.6 Estimation of d

Consider the ADL_2-risk of the ULRE is given by

\[
\begin{aligned}
f(d) = ADL_2-risk(\hat{\beta}_n^{ULiUR}(d)) &= \eta^2 tr(F_d^\top C^{-1} F_d) + (1-d)^2 \beta^\top (C + I_p)^{-2} \beta, \\
&= \eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(\lambda_i + d)^2}{\lambda_i (\lambda_i + 1)^2} + (1-d)^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{\theta_i^2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2} \\
&= \delta_1(d) + \delta_2(d). \tag{8.6.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

For the Liu estimator, one would like to find a value of d such that the decrease in variance (\(\delta_1(d)\)) is greater than the increase in squared bias (\(\delta_2(d)\)). In order to show that such a value less than 1 exists so that

\[ \text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{ULiuR}}(d)) < \text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{UR}}), \]

we will take derivative of Eq. (8.6.1) with respect to d as follows,

\[ \frac{\partial f(d)}{\partial d} = 2\eta^2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(\lambda_i + d)}{\lambda_i(\lambda_i + 1)^2} - 2(1 - d) \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{\theta_i^2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2}. \tag{8.6.2} \]

For d = 1 in Eq. (8.6.2), we obtain

\[ \frac{\partial f(d)}{\partial d} = 2 \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{1}{\lambda_i(\lambda_i + 1)} > 0, \quad \text{since } \lambda_i \ge 0. \tag{8.6.3} \]

Therefore, there exists a value of d (0 < d < 1), such that

\[ \text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{ULiuR}}(d)) < \text{ADL}_2\text{-risk}(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{UR}}). \]

Now from Eq. (8.6.2), the risk of \(\hat{\beta}_n^{\text{ULiuR}}(d)\) is minimized at

\[ d = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^p \frac{\theta_i^2 - \tau^2}{(\lambda_i + 1)^2}}{\sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(\eta^2 + \lambda_i \theta_i^2)}{\lambda_i(\lambda_i + 1)^2}}, \]

where \(\theta_i\) is the ith component of the vector, \(\theta = \Gamma^\text{T} \beta\). Replacing \(\theta_i^2\) and \(\eta^2\) by their corresponding unbiased estimate \(\left(\hat{\theta}_i^{\text{UR}}\right)^2 - \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2}{\lambda_i}\) and \(\hat{\eta}^2\), we get the optimum value of d as

\[ d_{\text{opt}} = 1 - \eta^2 \left\{ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^p \frac{1}{\lambda_i(\lambda_i+1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^p \frac{(\hat{\theta}_i^{\text{UR}})^2}{(\lambda_i+1)^2}} \right\}. \]

Refer to Akdeniz and Ozturk (2005) and Arashi et al. (2017b) for further information on the estimation of d, among others.

### 8.7.1 Penalty Estimators

For the purpose of comparison and adaptivity with other parts of the book, we give the penalty estimators again according to the model setup of Eq. (8.2.1). The key idea in penalized regression methods is minimizing an objective function \(J_{\rho, \lambda}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\) in the form of

\[ J_{\rho, \lambda}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = (\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\top}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}) + \lambda P_{\rho}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) \tag{8.7.1} \]

to obtain the estimates of the parameter. The first term in the objective function is the sum of the squared error loss, the second term of Eq. (8.7.1) is a penalty function, and \(\lambda\) is a tuning parameter which controls the trade-off between two components of \(J_{\rho, \lambda}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\).
