The estimator of Yang and Chang (2010) is

\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{YC}}(k, d) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + d\mathbf{I}_p)(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y},\)

\(k > 0, \quad d \in (0, 1).\)

The estimator of Kurnaz and Akay (2015) is given by:

\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{KA}}(k) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y} + g(k)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^*), \quad k > 0,\)

where \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^*\) can be any estimator and \(\text{g}(\cdot)\) is a continuous function.

Regarding simultaneous optimization of both tuning parameters \(k\) and \(d\) in Eq. (8.2.2) using cross validation, we refer to Roozbeh et al. (2020). Knowing the fact that \(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X}\) is invertible in the full rank model of Eq. (8.2.1), we may write

\(\begin{aligned} \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ridge}}(k) &= (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_n)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y} \\ &= \mathbf{R}_n(k)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}} \end{aligned}\)

where

\(\mathbf{R}_n(k) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_n)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} = (\mathbf{I}_n + k(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X})^{-1})^{-1}.\)

Hence, one way of constructing methodology for ridge-type estimators is to pre-multiply the expression \(\mathbf{R}_n(k)\) to the OLS estimator or unrestricted estimator.

In a similar manner, we may represent

\(\begin{aligned} \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{Liu}}(d) &= (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + d\mathbf{I}_p)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}} \\ &= \mathbf{F}_n(d)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}}, \end{aligned}\)

where

\(\mathbf{F}_n(d) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + d\mathbf{I}_p).\)

We must point out that, in the high-dimensional case where \(p > n\), the Liu estimator and some of its variants are not applicable due to the fact that the OLS estimator does not exist. This is a substantial shortcoming of the Liu estimator dealing with high-dimensional data. As a remedy, borrowing the structure of \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{Liu}}\), one may replace \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}}\) with a selector estimator such as LASSO, aLASSO, Enet.
