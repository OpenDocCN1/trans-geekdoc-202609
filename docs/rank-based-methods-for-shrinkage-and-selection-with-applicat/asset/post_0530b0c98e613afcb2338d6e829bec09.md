
The estimator of Yang and Chang (2010) is

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{YC}}(k, d) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + d\mathbf{I}_p)(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y},
\]

\(k > 0, \quad d \in (0, 1).\)

The estimator of Kurnaz and Akay (2015) is given by:

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{KA}}(k) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y} + g(k)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^*), \quad k > 0,
\]

where \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^*\) can be any estimator and \(g(\cdot)\) is a continuous function.

Regarding simultaneous optimization of both tuning parameters \(k\) and \(d\) in Eq. (8.2.2) using cross validation, we refer to Roozbeh et al. (2020). Knowing the fact that \(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X}\) is invertible in the full rank model of Eq. (8.2.1), we may write

\[
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ridge}}(k) &= (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y} \\
&= \mathbf{R}_n(k)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}}
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\mathbf{R}_n(k) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + k\mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} = (\mathbf{I}_p + k(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X})^{-1})^{-1}.
\]

Hence, one way of constructing methodology for ridge-type estimators is to pre-multiply the expression \(\mathbf{R}_n(k)\) to the OLS estimator or unrestricted estimator.

In a similar manner, we may represent

\[
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{Liu}}(d) &= (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + d\mathbf{I}_p)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}} \\
&= \mathbf{F}_n(d)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}},
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\mathbf{F}_n(d) = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} + d\mathbf{I}_p).
\]

We must point out that, in the high-dimensional case where \(p > n\), the Liu estimator and some of its variants are not applicable due to the fact that the OLS estimator does not exist. This is a substantial shortcoming of the Liu estimator dealing with high-dimensional data. As a remedy, borrowing the structure of \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{Liu}}\), one may replace \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LS}}\) with a selector estimator such as LASSO, aLASSO, Enet. However, we kill the variable selection property of the selectors. To fulfil both the selection and multicollinearity resistance, Arashi et al. (2017a) proposed the following optimization problem

\[
\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \|\boldsymbol{y} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|^2 \quad \text{such that} \quad \|d\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\text{LS}} - \boldsymbol{\beta}\|^2 < \lambda_1, \|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1 < \lambda_2, \quad (8.2.3)
\]

where, in this case, \(\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j|\). The authors argue that we can use

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LassoLiu}} \xrightarrow{d} \boldsymbol{F}_n(d)\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{\text{LassoLS}} \quad (8.2.4)
\]

as the solution to Eq. (8.2.3). Recently, Genc and Ozkale (2020) solved the optimization problem of Eq. (8.2.3) using gradient descent. Although they did not obtain a closed-form estimator, they proved the grouping effect of the solution of Eq. (8.2.3) following the approach in Zou and Hastie (2005). Very recently, Arashi et al. (2021) discussed the low-dimensional theoretical properties and derived the asymptotic distribution of Eq. (8.2.4).

## 8.2.1 Liu-type R-estimator

Recall in the multiple regression model of Eq. (8.2.1), under the named regularity conditions, following the rank-based objective function of Jaeckel (1972) defined by

\[
D_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \boldsymbol{x}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta})a_n(R_{n,i}(\boldsymbol{\beta})),
\]

the unrestricted R-estimator (URE) is obtained by \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{UR}} = \underset{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p}{\text{argmin}}\{D_n(\boldsymbol{\beta})\}\).

In 2014, Turkmen and Ozturk considered the penalized objective function and defined the rank-based ridge estimator as

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ridgeR}} = \underset{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p}{\text{argmin}}\{D_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}) + n\lambda_n\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|^2\},
\]

where \(\lambda_n \ge 0\) is the regularization parameter. Due to the form of the objective function, the solution does not have a closed form. In this regard, we have the following important result due to Turkmen and Ozturk (2014).

**Theorem 8.1** Under the model (8.2.1), in addition to the named regularity conditions, assume \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \sqrt{n}\lambda_n = \lambda_0 < \infty\) and \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \lambda_n = 0\). Then, we have

\[
\sqrt{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ridgeR}} - \boldsymbol{\beta}_0) \xrightarrow{D} \mathcal{N}(-2\lambda_0\eta\boldsymbol{\beta}_0, \eta^2\boldsymbol{I}_p)
\]

where \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0\) is the true regression parameter and \(\eta^2 = \frac{A_\phi^2}{\gamma^2(\phi, f)}\) where \(A_\phi^2 = \int_0^1 \phi^2(u)du - \left(\int_0^1 \phi(u)du\right)^2\), and \(\gamma(\phi, f) = \int_0^1 \phi(u)\psi(u, f)du\) where \(\psi(u, f) = \frac{f'(F^{-1}(u))}{f(F^{-1}(u))}, 0 < u < 1\).

Following Turkmen and Ozturk (2014) one may define a rank-based Liu estimator as

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LiuR}} = \underset{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p}{\text{argmin}}\{ D_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}) + \| d_n \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{UR}} - \boldsymbol{\beta} \|^2 \},
\]

where \(d_n \in (0, 1)\) is the regularization parameter.

In order to find a closed form ridge-type rank estimator based on \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{UR}}\), Saleh et al. (2019) suggested to minimize the distance between \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) and the R-estimator \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{UR}}\) to combat multicollinearity in robust regression. Using the marginal theory, they derived a closed form estimator given by

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ridgeR}}_{(k)} = \left( (\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1n}^{\text{UR}})^\top, \frac{1}{1+k} (\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{2n}^{\text{UR}})^\top \right)^\top .
\]

Indeed, the form \(\frac{1}{1+k} (\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{2n}^{\text{UR}})^\top\) is reminiscent of the contraction estimator defined by Mayer and Wilke (1973) in classical theory.

The marginal theory due to Saleh et al. (2019) can also be used to define a closed form Liu-type R-estimator. In this case, one may consider it as

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LiuR}}_{(d)} = \underset{(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1, \boldsymbol{\beta}_2) \in \mathbb{R}^p}{\text{argmin}} \{ D_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1, \boldsymbol{\beta}_2) + \| d (\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{2n}^{\text{UR}})^\top - \boldsymbol{\beta}_2 \|^2 \} = \left( (\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{1n}^{\text{UR}})^\top, \frac{1+d}{2} (\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{2n}^{\text{UR}})^\top \right)^\top
\]

where one suspects that \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2 = \mathbf{0}\) may hold. Hence, the performance analysis of \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{LiuR}}(d)\) is similar to \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ridgeR}}(k)\). In order to add some fresh ideas, we follow the construction methodology that was initially used by Akdeniz and Kaciranlar (2001), i.e. we pre-multiply the R-estimator by a factor. For this and the rest of our study regarding shrinkage Liu-type R-estimators, we shall assume in the regression model (8.2.1).

- (1) Errors \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = (\varepsilon_1, ..., \varepsilon_n)^\top\) are i.i.d. random variables with (unknown) c.d.f. \(F\) having an absolutely continuous p.d.f. \(f\) with finite and non-zero Fisher information,

  \[
  0 < I(f) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left( - \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} \right)^2 f(x) dx < \infty.
  \]

- (2) For the definition of linear rank statistics, we consider the score generating function \(\varphi : (0, 1) \mapsto \mathbb{R}\) which is assumed to be non-constant, non-decreasing and square integrable on \((0, 1)\) so that

  \[
  A_\varphi^2 = \int_0^1 \varphi^2(u) du - \left( \int_0^1 \varphi(u) du \right)^2 . \tag{8.2.6}
  \]

  The scores are defined in either of the following ways:

  \[
  a_n(i) = \mathbb{E} \left[ \varphi(U_{i:n}) \right], \quad \text{or} \quad a_n(i) = \varphi \left( \frac{i}{n+1} \right), \quad i = 1, ..., n,
  \]

  where \(U_{1:n} \le ... \le U_{n:n}\) are order statistics from a sample of size \(n\) from the uniform distribution \(\mathcal{U}(0, 1)\).

- (3) Let us define

  \[
  \mathbf{C}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \boldsymbol{x}_i \boldsymbol{x}_i^{\top}, \tag{8.2.7}
  \]

  where \(\boldsymbol{x}_i\) is the \(i\)'th row of \(\boldsymbol{X}\). We assume that

  - (a) \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbf{C}_n = \mathbf{C}\)
  - (b) \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \max_{1 \le i \le n} \boldsymbol{x}_i^{\top} \mathbf{C}_n^{-1} \boldsymbol{x}_i = 0\) \tag{8.2.8}

  where \(\mathbf{C}\) is a non-negative definite matrix.

- (4)

  \[
  \lim_{n \to \infty} \boldsymbol{F}_n(d) = \boldsymbol{F}_d, \quad \boldsymbol{F}_d = (\mathbf{C} + \boldsymbol{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{C} + d\boldsymbol{I}_p),
  \]

  where

  \[
  \boldsymbol{F}_n(d) = (\mathbf{C}_n + \boldsymbol{I}_p)^{-1}(\mathbf{C}_n + d\boldsymbol{I}_p).
  \]

  Then, we define the unrestricted Liu-type R-estimator (ULRE) as

  \[
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{ULiR}}(d) = \boldsymbol{F}_n(d) \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_n^{\text{UR}}, \quad 0 < d < 1. \tag{8.2.9}
  \]
