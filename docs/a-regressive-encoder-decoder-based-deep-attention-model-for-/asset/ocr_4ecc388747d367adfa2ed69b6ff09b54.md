Mean Square Error(MSE) = \(\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (Q_i - T_i)^2\) (5)

The final hybrid regression equation of the proposed model is thus given as the weighted summation of the cross entropy and MSE in Eq. (6)(6):

Segmented\(_{Loss}\) = W * (WCE + MSE) (6)

where W magnify the loss on fetus head boundary during training with emphases on edges given by the Eq. (7):

W(x) = 1 + w0.exp \(\frac{D_x}{2 * \sigma^2}\) (7)

where, Dx gives the distance between the pixel x and ground truth (Target), \(\sigma\) is Gaussian kernel variance and w0 is a constant.

# 3.4. Eclipse fitting and dimension assessment

The last phase of the proposed methodology is the post-processing of the segmented output map, called eclipse fitting. As the shape of the fetus is in ellipse and also the available ground truth is in form of the ellipse, the fitting of the eclipse is an important part of the methodology. The fitment of the eclipse on the ultrasound image can help to make the assessment of the parameters easily and predicts the growth of the fetus.

An ellipse is defined as the locus of all those points in a two-dimensional plane where the distance addition from two fixed points, also known as foci, in the plane remains constant. This constant ratio is called the eccentricity of an ellipse which demonstrates its elongation. The description of the ellipse is shown in Fig. 8.

It is to be noted that the shape of the eclipse is in form of an oval, where the longest bisection in middle along the x-axis is called the major axis and the smallest bisection along the y-axis is called the minor axis. The distance between the center (intersection of major and minor axis) to surface points is called the semi-major axis and semi-minor axis. The area enclosed by the eclipse is given by the Eq. (8):
