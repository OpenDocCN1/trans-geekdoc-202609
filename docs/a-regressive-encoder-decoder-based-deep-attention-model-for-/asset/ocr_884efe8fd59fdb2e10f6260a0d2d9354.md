# ABSTRACT

Ultrasound imaging is the most commonly used imaging during pregnancy for tracking the fetus's growth and monitoring other biological parameters. The assessment of the baby's growth requires imaging-based analysis in every trimester. The automatic computerized software and systems provide the platform for radiologists to more accurately access the fetus's head circumference as compared to manual estimation. The improvement of such computerized algorithms is always the key demand to improve accuracy and precision. This paper proposes an improved encoder-decoder model for the segmentation of the fetal head segmentation in 2D-ultrasound images. The proposed model uses regression in combination with attention to the encoder-decoder model to determine the fetus's head circumference. The model is further extended with the post-processing ellipse fitting to superimpose the segmentation region on ultrasound images for clear visualization of the fetus's head. Further, the proposed model performance is evaluated by using various statistical measures using segmented regions and available ground truth. The experimental results demonstrate a similarity score of 94.56%. The comparative result suggests that the proposed model is providing a more accurate fetus head segmentation region on 2D-ultrasound images as compared to other existing approaches.

# 1. Introduction

Computer-aided systems, attributed to the power of artificial intelligence, can help radiologists in various aspects. These systems are commonly used in medical imaging for various tasks such as segmentation, localization, prediction, and classification. The backbone of such systems is the sophisticated and advanced algorithms that make the task easier and automated. This automation assists radiologists in accurately predicting abnormalities and making a better diagnosis.

The ultrasound-based diagnosis in radiology is considered to be complex compared to other imaging like CT, MRI, and X-ray due to the unclear visualization of the inner anatomy of the human organs as seen in other imaging modalities. Radiologists use ultrasounds to evaluate the brain, spine, and hips. Moreover, it also helps guide biopsies and heart disease conditions. But, the most commonly used application of ultrasound is seen for problems related to abdominal. One such application is during pregnancy when females need to undergo an ultrasound-based assessment by gynecologists.

For pregnant females, the most important recommended diagnosis by the gynecologist is an assessment of the fetus using Ultrasound Imaging. In prenatal care, ultrasound imaging is a widely used mechanism and must require in every trimester. Gynecologists are particularly interested in monitoring fetal development and examining various biological parameters during each trimester [1]. In clinical routine checkups, the assessment of the fetus's head circumference is the essential measurement for fetus's growth monitoring, estimating gestational age and estimating the expected delivery date [2].
