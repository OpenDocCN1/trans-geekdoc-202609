## ABSTRACT

Ultrasound imaging is the most commonly used imaging during pregnancy for tracking the fetus's growth and monitoring other biological parameters. The assessment of the baby's growth requires imaging-based analysis in every trimester. The automatic computerized software and systems provide the platform for radiologists to more accurately assess the fetus's head circumference as compared to manual estimation. The improvement of such computerized algorithms is always the key demand to improve accuracy and precision. This paper proposes an improved encoder-decoder model for the segmentation of the fetal head in 2D-ultrasound images. The proposed model uses regression in combination with attention to the encoder-decoder model to determine the fetus's head circumference. The model is further extended with the post-processing ellipse fitting to superimpose the segmentation region on ultrasound images for clear visualization of the fetus's head. Further, the proposed model performance is evaluated by using various statistical measures using segmented regions and available ground truth. The experimental results demonstrate a similarity score of 94.56%. The comparative result suggests that the proposed model is providing a more accurate fetus head segmentation region on 2D-ultrasound images as compared to other existing approaches.

## 1. Introduction

Computer-aided systems, attributed to the power of artificial intelligence, can help radiologists in various aspects. These systems are commonly used in medical imaging for various tasks such as segmentation, localization, prediction, and classification. The backbone of such systems is the sophisticated and advanced algorithms that make the task easier and automated. This automation assists radiologists in accurately predicting abnormalities and making a better diagnosis.

The ultrasound-based diagnosis in radiology is considered to be complex compared to other imaging like CT, MRI, and X-ray due to the unclear visualization of the inner anatomy of the human organs as seen in other imaging modalities. Radiologists use ultrasounds to evaluate the brain, spine, and hips. Moreover, it also helps guide biopsies and heart disease conditions. But, the most commonly used application of ultrasound is seen for problems related to abdominal. One such application is during pregnancy when females need to undergo an ultrasound-based assessment by gynecologists.

For pregnant females, the most important recommended diagnosis by the gynecologist is an assessment of the fetus using Ultrasound Imaging. In prenatal care, ultrasound imaging is a widely used mechanism and must require in every trimester. Gynecologists are particularly interested in monitoring fetal development and examining various biological parameters during each trimester [1]. In clinical routine checkups, the assessment of the fetus's head circumference is the essential measurement for fetus's growth monitoring, estimating gestational age and estimating the expected delivery date [2]. Also, trimester-wise ultrasound imaging can help to monitor the organs of the newborn in the fetus itself. The trimester-based sample ultrasound images of a random patient are shown in Fig. 1. Such analysis of the ultrasound image not only requires the latest machines but also the expertise of the radiologist for manual interpretation.

The literature presents several methods based on fetus' head segmentation using an ellipse-based bounding box, considering that the fetus' shape is elliptical. However, machine learning-based algorithms lead to the loss of visual anatomy providing only segments based on user-defined criteria.

This paper introduces a novel model for fetus' head segmentation using a deep learning approach and thus minimizes the dependency on user-defined criteria. The proposed model is distinct as compared with traditional models like UNet in many aspects including network architecture incorporating regression layer with attention. The key difference between the two models is given as:

- Output Representation: The UNet model is primarily used for pixel-wise semantic segmentation, where the goal is to classify each pixel in the input image into different predefined classes or labels. It produces a segmentation mask where each pixel is assigned a specific class label. Whereas, the Encoder-Decoder-based Deep Attention Model with a regression layer is designed for localization and instance segmentation tasks. Instead of predicting class labels for each pixel, this model aims to directly regress the bounding box coordinates or segmentation masks for specific objects or regions of interest. The regression layer outputs the coordinates or pixel-wise values that define the extent or boundary of the object.

- Task Focus: The UNet model is commonly used for tasks such as segmenting organs or structures in medical images, where the emphasis is on identifying and classifying different regions of interest within the image. It is suitable for scenarios where the boundaries between different classes are distinct and well-defined. On the other hand, the Encoder-Decoder-based Deep Attention Model with a regression layer is more suitable for tasks that require precise localization or instance segmentation, such as detecting and segmenting specific objects or regions within an image. By directly regressing the bounding box coordinates or segmentation masks, this model can provide more accurate localization and fine-grained segmentation results, even in scenarios where objects may have complex shapes or overlapping boundaries.

- Network Architecture: The UNet model follows a symmetric encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections to capture both local and global information. It is commonly composed of convolutional layers, pooling layers for down-sampling, and transposed convolutional layers for up-sampling. In contrast, the Encoder-Decoder-based Deep Attention Model with a regression layer may also utilize an encoder-decoder architecture with attention mechanisms for capturing informative regions. However, it incorporates an additional regression layer at the end of the network, which directly predicts the coordinates or pixel-wise values defining the object of interest. This regression layer enables the model to perform precise localization and regression-based instance segmentation.

The main contributions of the manuscript are described as follows:

- 1. The paper proposes a novel architecture that combines a regressive encoder-decoder network with an attention mechanism. This architecture enables the model to learn both global and local features of the ultrasound images, while the attention mechanism helps to focus on informative regions for more accurate segmentation.

- 2. The proposed model directly predicts the bounding box coordinates of the fetal head. This regression-based approach allows for more precise localization and segmentation of the fetal head.

- 3. The paper introduces a deep attention mechanism within the model that allows the model to focus on relevant regions of the ultrasound images, enhancing the accuracy of fetal head segmentation. The attention mechanism assigns different weights to different spatial locations, emphasizing informative regions while suppressing irrelevant areas.

- 4. The paper describes the training process of the proposed model, including the use of a large dataset of annotated 2D ultrasound images. Additionally, it discusses the utilization of various data augmentation techniques to increase the diversity of the training data.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: The description of the existing works on Fetus analysis that was used as a theoretical foundation is presented in Section 2. Section 3, gives insight into the proposed model design using the regression-based attention deep UNet model. Section 4, demonstrates the evaluation results of the proposed model when experimented with the state-of-the-art benchmark dataset of the fetus' head. The comparative analysis of the proposed model with the existing literature and benchmark models are presented in Section 5. Finally, Section 6 presents the overall conclusion of the proposed methodology with the future work directions that guides the extension of the work in the near future.
