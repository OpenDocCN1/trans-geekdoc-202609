## Ubiquitous Computing and Augmented Reality

### 9.1 Introduction

Human-computer interaction (HCI) is a branch of computer science that studies how people interact with computers. The most fundamental and essential aspects of HCI are augmented reality (AR) and ubiquitous computing (UC).

### 9.2 Ubiquitous Computing (UC)

UC is also known as pervasive computing, everywhere, and ambient intelligence. UC refers to the emerging trend of embedding computing capabilities, particularly microprocessors, into everyday objects.

### 9.2.1 UC's History

Mark Weiser, the Chief Technologist at Xerox Palo Alto Research Center (PARC), coined the term “ubiquitous computing” in 1988. Weiser published some of the initial papers on the subject, both alone and with PARC Director and Chief Scientist Seely Brown, primarily defining it and sketching out its obstacles. The Active Badge, a “clip-on computer” the size of an employee ID card, was designed at Olivretti Research Laboratory in Cambridge, England, allowing businesses to track the exact locations of people as well as the objects to which they were attached. At PARC, Weiser was inspired by social scientists, anthropologists, and philosophers, who explored a new approach to computers. He believes that people live by following their routines and knowledge bases, so that the most powerful things are effectively invisible. This is a problem that affects the entire field of computer science. First and foremost, it makes the world a better place by activating computers. Hundreds of wireless devices of different sizes, per person and per office, are offered. This necessitated new work in areas such as operating systems, user interfaces, the internet, wireless, and a variety of other domains.
