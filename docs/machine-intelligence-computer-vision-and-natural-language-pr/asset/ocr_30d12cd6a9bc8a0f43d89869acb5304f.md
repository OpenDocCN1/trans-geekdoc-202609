# ## 11.1 Introduction

The geographical incidence of gastric cancer has changed dramatically since the 1940s. Gastric cancer was the most common reason behind cancer death in men. Recent studies reported that gastric cancer is 2.2 times more likely to be diagnosed in males compared with females (Rawla and Barsouk, 2019). Mortality rates of gastric cancer in males are high in several countries, such as Central and East Asia and Latin America. Gastric cancer peaks within the seventh decade of life. Often, a delay in diagnosing can account for the poor prognosis. Luckily, dedicated analysis into its pathological process and identification of recent risk factors, treatment and advanced endoscopic techniques offer to earlier detection of gastric cancer. Recognition of Helicobacter pylori infection causes most stomach ulcers has revolutionized the approach to gastric cancer these days.
