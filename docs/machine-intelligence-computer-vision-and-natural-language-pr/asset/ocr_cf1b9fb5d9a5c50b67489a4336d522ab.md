# ## 9.1 Introduction

Human-computer interaction (HCI) is a branch of computer science that studies how people and computers interact. HCI researchers study how people interact with computers and create technologies that enable people to connect with computers in unique ways. HCI is at the crossroads of computer science, cognitive science, design, mass communication, and a number of other specialties. The most fundamental and essential aspects of HCI are augmented reality (AR) and ubiquitous computing (UC) [1–7].

# ## 9.2 Ubiquitous Computing (UC)

UC is a new computing model characterized by the seamless integration of hundreds of thousands of self-communicating small-scale computers and intelligent devices found in the user's environment and daily activities. UC is also known as pervasive computing, everywhere, and ambient intelligence. UC refers to the emerging trend of embedding computing capabilities, particularly in the form of microprocessors, into everyday objects in order to enable them to efficiently transmit information.
