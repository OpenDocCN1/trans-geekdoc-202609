The Indian health care sector can be a valuable asset as it includes pharma stocks too. India has remarkably well in the field of generic medicines in the pharmaceutical industry and is the largest supplier of generic medicines globally. The Indian pharmaceutical sector industry accounts for over 50 percent of global demand for various vaccines, nearly 40 percent of demand from the USA and 25 percent from the United Kingdom are fulfilled by the Indian pharmaceutical manufacturers and suppliers. India supports the second largest share of pharmaceutical biotech workforce on the global platform. The valuation of the pharmaceutical industry in India was calculated at $33 billion in 2017 [2] and $38 billion in 2019 [3]. The turnover of India’s domestic pharmaceutical market in 2018 was measured as Rs 1,29,015 ($18.12 billion), which grew by 9.4 percent year-on-year from Rs 11,369 crore ($17.87 billion) in 2017 [1]. India has earned a very good reputation in the pharmaceutical industry at a global level by developing a large pool of engineers and scientists who have been instrumental in winning appreciation for their work, and they have the potential to direct the industry forward to achieve more accolades at global level,. Around 80 percent of the antiretroviral drugs which are used to fight AIDS (Acquired Immunity Deficiency Syndrome), a disease which has killed millions of people across the globe, are supplied by Indian pharmaceutical manufacturers and suppliers. The pharmaceutical and allied sectors like health care and insurance have contributed in a major way to India’s economic growth and played a very important role in the comparatively lower mortality rate in India in recent years. Estimates suggest that this industry has become a major source of employment in India with over 2.7 million people directly or indirectly on highly skilled areas like manufacturing and research & development activities. The industry is among the five best performing sectors that generate a surplus of over $11 billion and it has helped the Indian economy to reduce the burden of deficit [4]. Foreign direct investors have shown interest in the Indian pharmaceutical industry and have invested more than $2 billion over the past three financial years, making it one of the top 10 priority sectors attracting foreign direct investment (FDI).
