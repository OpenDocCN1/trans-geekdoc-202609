## Chapter 15: Economic Sustainability, Mindfulness, and Diversity in the Age of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

### 15.1 Use of AI and ML in Traditional Industry

In India, under the Company Act, corporate social responsibility (CSR) funding is mandatory, providing a boost for non-governmental organizations (NGOs) to receive such funding from various organizations. Often, social work is motivated by emotion, and many NGOs seek to save traditional industries. They fail to recognize that time has passed and new technology is introduced. Today, most NGOs in India are working to save traditional industries. They believe that they will keep tradition alive; in the same spirit, those involved in manufacturing have strong attachments to traditional skills, practices, and machines that have been in use for a long time. Stories have been created over the ages; it is not easy to leave the past. All, the capitalist can use automation to continue the business in an automated manner and make profit. One such industry is the handloom-based industry. However, it is widely assumed that automation cannot replace manual labour. If we consider fabric's durability, hand-made cloth is much more durable and can last a lifetime, but today, consumers are looking for low prices and constant changes. Creating hand-made fabric takes a lot of labour, and the customer wants attention to detail. Automated technology has replaced traditional weaving, and similar fabrics can be made instead of a week. This is a problem because machine-made cloth will not last as long as hand-made cloth; and customers may not understand the difference between machine-made and hand-made. The vicious cycle weavers suffer does not stop because of too much implication in the whole process. In geographical areas, the weaver faces difficulty finding a suitable groom because the sustainability of that career is in doubt. Today's social climate has deteriorated. The solution is for them to know copy rights, patents, and other legal aspects of the entire process. Filing of trademarks and patents in India is a complicated process that needs simplification for weavers. Obtaining digital signature for patent drafting and copyright filing may require legal professionals, but all of this can be automated using a PAN card or Aadhaar card number, thereby reducing the cost. In most cases, NGOs, government bodies, or institutions provide the skills and real-time training in patent drafting and copyright filing. In the meantime, a local language database on agriculture must be built, which is a costly endeavour, given that India has 1,652 or more languages.

### 15.2 Use of AI to Create an Agricultural Database

One of the pressing challenges in India pertains to the absence of a comprehensive national database encompassing essential information on land use, soil health, crop patterns, disease prevalence, and agricultural extension services. The landowners have difficulty finding agricultural labour and water for the soil. Some landowners converted their properties into multi-story buildings, schools, and colleges. In most cases, the land's viability for cultivation was finished, and farmers are familiar with one type of cultivation. That main issue is tobacco cultivation. At present, tobacco cultivation in India is declining not as a result of any livelihood training but as a result of decreased land vitality for the tobacco product. A farmer who knows rice cultivation is generally familiar with the entire process. However, they are concerned shifting cultivation to other crops because they are unfamiliar with the whole process and the markets. For Indian agriculture to be sustainable, there is an urgent need for scientific intervention. There are agricultural offices in every district in India, and the government employs qualified professionals in each of these offices. Much of the work is not being done as it should. The potential solution is an interactive app that understands the local language and answers all of the farmer's questions in the local language. The challenge is obtaining the information from the local language database, which must exist. It would take some time for such an app to learn most aspects of agriculture. Ideally, the learning algorithm of such technology can be automated, which has its own risk of providing incorrect information. The best answer is a human teacher, but it takes time and money, and it is the best way to go. In the meantime, a local language database on agriculture must be built, which is a costly endeavour, given that India has 1,652 or more languages.

In most cases, NGOs and government agencies wash their hands of the matter simply by raising awareness and submitting a report. This doesn't help, and most of the success stories submitted by various agencies are fabricated. The process of copyright filing and patent filing can be automated in a simplified manner so that any citizen should be able to file a copyright or patent.
