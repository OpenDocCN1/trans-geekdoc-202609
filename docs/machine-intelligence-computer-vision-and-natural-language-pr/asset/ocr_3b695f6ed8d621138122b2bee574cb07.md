In the 1960s, the early computer artists, especially for visual art, one of the significant limitations was the output. Plotters, connected with a brush or pen, were used in early experiments. An example of a complex algorithmic work from that period was a screen-print of a drawing artiste, titled “Hommage à Paul Klee’s oil painting entitled "13/9/65 Nr. 2," created by Frieder N in 1965. The work was inspired by Paul Klee’s painting entitled “Highroads and Byroads” (1929) [3].

One of the pioneers of computer art or what is now called generative art, was Harold Cohen. He was an accomplished painter and an outstanding engineer. developed a system called AARON that became increasingly sophisticated (and large in size), and the output was exhibited at prestigious museums and galleries [6]. When asked who was the artist, Cohen or Aaron, Harold gave the relationship between Renaissance painters and their assistants. This question of authorship, which appears quite academic and abstract, has some serious commercial and legal implications.
