The landscape of developing smart cities has undergone a radical change
towards a new and more understandable architecture that combines IoT with WSN.
The IoT concept is centered on connecting devices so that they may easily
share data. To collect and share environmental data, a WSN is built up of
sensor nodes that are wirelessly connected to one another [7]. These technologies work well to provide a powerful and efficient way to manage municipal
bodies and the ecosystems of interconnected, smart cities.

The architecture of an IoT-WSN framework depends on a number of crucial components that perform well. Its core is made up of sensors, which are necessary for acquiring vital data about the environment. These sensors come in several sizes and shapes, and they may be used to monitor many different parameters, such as temperature, humidity, air quality, noise levels, and much more. By gathering data in real-time, these sensors provide invaluable insights into the performance and operation of municipal services.
