# The Role of Sustainable IoT in Present-Day Life

Parul Saini, Lipika Gupta, Shivani Malhotra, and Imali Dutta



The Internet of Things (IoT) has caused an important paradigm shift. IoT has made it possible for linked devices to simultaneously communicate with one another and the operator about their current state. The deployment of IoT across various sectors and domains is driven by the improved analytical functionalities, fast response times, and real-time monitoring. COVID-19 has fuelled the adoption of IoT in every sector, especially healthcare [1]. This chapter focuses on what IoT is, why it is important, applications of IoT in different fields for sustainable development of society, how IoT devices are being made sustainable, challenges in the development of IoT, and what the future of IoT looks like. This chapter contains data from research papers, articles, and reports from authenticated sources.

The most basic definition of IoT is as follows: It describes the interconnection of various physical devices communicating through networks without any human mediation. These objects can range from simple sensors to complex machines, and can be found in a wide variety of settings. IoT makes devices such as TVs, refrigerators, washing machines, doorbells, etc. smaller by allowing them to communicate with people and other IoT-enabled things. IoT as a commercial field is thriving with an estimate of 30 billion IoT connections by the end of 2025 [2].

A smart home is the simplest example of IoT. It involves integrating the household appliances, such as a smoke detector, water temperature sensor, own, and AC, to share data with the end user over any smartphone or voice assistant. For example, a smart home may have a thermostat that can control the room temperature according to the weather conditions and occupancy of the room, smart lighting system, and smart security cameras that give real-time monitoring and alert the user in case of any risk. In this, the Internet serves as a medium to connect people but the same Internet has made possible for the objects and things to communicate with each other. For example, in the morning, when the alarm goes off, an IoT system can effortlessly do the daily tasks like opening the window blinds, starting the coffee machine, turning on the water, powering on the music system, etc. While all of this is a great deal of behind-the-scenes activity that takes place to ensure the smooth operation of these tasks.
