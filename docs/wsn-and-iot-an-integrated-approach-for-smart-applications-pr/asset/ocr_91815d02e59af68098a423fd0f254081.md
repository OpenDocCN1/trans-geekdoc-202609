Selecting the appropriate wireless protocol: When selecting wireless protocols for IoT, it is essential to consider factors like energy efficiency, range, and reliability to ensure that the chosen protocol meets sustainability goals [15]. These protocols must be selected by keeping in mind the application is being used; for example, the BLE protocol should be selected for short-medium range transmission, which in turn will save power. Low wide area network (LPWAN) can be used for a range up to 15 km, which can make battery-powered IoT devices last. Cellular and satellite-based wireless protocols can offer long-range connectivity, making them a good option for IoT devices deployed in remote locations.

+   e. Wake-up radio (WUR) technology: This technology from IEEE 802.1ba will significantly extend the battery life of devices and sensors in IoT. This technology will place a small-power radio in IoT devices that will tell the radio to wake up use power when needed. This will significantly extend the battery life of the devices.

+   f. Use of energy harvesting: Energy harvesting techniques such as solar cells, piezoelectric, or thermoelectric can help generate power for IoT devices, reducing the dependence on batteries [16].

+   g. Optimizing code: The code can be optimized by using the right types, using a smaller number of delays, writing simple code, and using a smaller number of instructions. All these features can optimize power usage.

+   h. Flexibility and durability: The devices must be manufactured, installed, and shipped only once. After that, these devices should be able to adapt to requirements or be used flexibly for longer periods of time, which will automatically reduce the power consumption.

2. Reducing the carbon footprint: IoT devices have a significant impact on carbon emissions, both during their manufacturing process and their use. The carbon footprint of IoT devices is primarily determined by their energy consumption and the materials used in their production. Manufacturers can focus on using more sustainable materials, improving production processes to minimize waste and energy use, and designing devices that are more energy-efficient. Major contributors to the carbon footprint are combustion, water pollution, and emissions of harmful gases [17].
