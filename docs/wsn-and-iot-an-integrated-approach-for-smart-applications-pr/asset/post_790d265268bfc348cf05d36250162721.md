## 9.5 DEEP LEARNING FRAMEWORK

A general-purpose artificial intelligence (AI) technique called machine learning (ML) can infer associations from data without defining them [16]. The key selling point is the capacity to generate models of prediction without making firm beliefs about the underlying methods, which are frequently unidentified or inadequately characterized. Data harmonization, representation learning, model fitting, and assessment make up the usual ML pipeline. Conventional procedures are constrained in their ability to deal with information in their raw form since they only involve one, frequency linear, alteration of the input space [17].

In contrast to conventional ML, DL learns and deals with the raw information. In reality, DL makes it possible for computational models with several neural-based processing phases to execute specifications of information with various degrees of abstraction [18]. The amount of human learning (HL), its connections, as well as DL's capacity to educate important aspects of the inputs are the main distinctions between DL and conventional artificial neural networks (ANNs).

However, ANNs are often limited to three stages and are trained to provide supervised models that are tailored to a certain task and are not transferable to other contexts. Each stage of DL uses the information to obtain signals from the stage to generate a representation of the observed trends by optimizing unsupervised criteria [19]. The main feature of DL is, rather than being designed through human engineers, it features stages that are learned automatically from data. The architecture of DL is made up of multiple layers of neural networks, as opposed to ANNs, which typically consist of three stages and one conversion toward the final outcomes. Layer-wise unsupervised pre-training makes it possible to effectively tune deep networks and extract complex structures from inputs to utilize as higher-level functions to get suitable estimations [16].

## 9.5.2 APPLICATIONS OF DL IN BIOINFORMATICS

- 1. Translational bioinformatics (BI); The discipline of BI has as its goal the molecular understanding of biological processes. The Human Genome Project (HGP) offers a vast amount of unmapped information and permits fresh theories about what genes communicate with the environment to produce proteins. Other advancements in biotechnology reduced the cost of genome sequencing or allowed researchers to concentrate on using genes and proteins to assess disease prognosis as well as therapy [28]. Additional research on bioinformatics is divided into three categories: identifying causes of disease, preventing disease, and treating an individual. Genetics investigates the structure and operations of the data contained in the DNA of living cells, or in other words, the analysis of alleles expressing genotypes and phenotypes. The goal of genomics is to categorize the genes or environmental alleles that cause disease like cancer. The creation of tailored medicines is made possible by the identification of these types of genes.
