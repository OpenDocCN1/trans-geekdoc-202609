+   - *Predictive analytics*: IoT systems may learn from past data to produce forecasts and predictions by utilizing ML and AI approaches. This can be helpful in several areas, including chain optimization, healthcare surveillance, and predictive maintenance. AI systems, for instance, can analyze data from smart meters to forecast patterns of electricity usage and improve distribution of energy [13].

+   - *Automation*: Based on data analysis and specified rules, AI-powered IoT systems may streamline decision-making and control procedures. Increased effectiveness and cost savings may result from this. Algorithms, for instance, can analyze traffic patterns in smart cities, improve traffic lights, and dynamically change routes to ease congestion [12].

+   - *Personalization*: IoT devices with AI capabilities can learn from user preferences and provide individualized experiences. AI algorithms, for instance, can examine data from wearables to comprehend a user’s fitness objectives and offer personalized recommendations [20].

+   - *Computing*: Instead of depending on cloud-based solutions, AI-powered IoT frequently uses edge computing, where data processing and analysis take place locally on the IoT devices or edge servers. Edge computing allows for real-time decision-making in sparsely connected locations while lowering latency and enhancing privacy.
