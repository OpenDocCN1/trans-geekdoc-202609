In terms of privacy and security, intelligent systems can extract extensive user-related data with the help of seemingly unrelated data, allowing for the identification of individual behavioral patterns and even personality traits. The traditional methods for security, related to anonymization of data, prove inefficient against AI’s capacity to learn and infer data repeatedly, rendering personal privacy more vulnerable to public exposure.



The current implementation of AI in communication networks introduces greater complexity to the system. This complexity arises from the use of case-specific ML techniques, prioritize achieving a single objective while disregarding other objectives and overall goals. Consequently, a significant challenge that remains is the focus of optimizing one objective, while neglecting factors such as latency, quality, storage limitations, and processing overhead in the network as a whole. For instance, a study in [21] proposes increasing efficiency through reinforcement learning but fails to address the potential costs associated with information sharing, storage, and processing when applied to real-world scenarios or large-scale networks. The open challenges are as:

+   * Interoperability and standardization: IoT platforms and devices frequently work in contexts with a variety of protocols and standards. Effective integration and cooperation between AI and IoT technologies depend on achieving interoper interoper and standardization across devices, communication protocols, and data formats [5].
* Scalability and performance: Scalability becomes a key difficulty as the number of linked devices increases. Advanced infrastructure, edge computing, and effective AI are needed to handle the enormous volume of data created by IoT devices and to process it in realtime while preserving performance and low latency [2].
* Efficiency: Numerous IoT devices rely on batteries or have constrained power sources. The task of creating AI algorithms that are energy efficient and optimize power usage in IoT without sacrificing performance. It is necessary to investigate methods like model compression, energy-conscious scheduling, and adaptive power management.
* Ethical and social implications: The deployment of AI in IoT poses ethical questions about responsibility, fairness, bias, and privacy.
