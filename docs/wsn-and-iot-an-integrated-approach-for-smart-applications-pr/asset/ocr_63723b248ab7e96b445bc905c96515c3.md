# # The Role of IoMT Technologies in Revolutionizing Healthcare
A Comprehensive Overview

Sushmita Sunil Jain, Saurabh Manoj Kothari, and Satyam Kumar Agrawal



The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) is an application of the Internet of Things (IoT) in healthcare [1, 2]. It involves the use of medical devices attached to patients to monitor their medical parameters and communicate the information to healthcare professionals [3, 4]. However, ensuring patient privacy is critical for IoMT security [5]. Creating a robust authentication method for IoMT is challenging due to limited capacity, diverse platforms and protocols used by different providers, and the inherent vulnerability of IoMT systems to security breaches [6].

Despite these challenges, the IoMT environment, consisting of clinical sensors, frameworks, and systems, has significantly improved healthcare services by collecting physiological data and providing access to authorized healthcare professionals. It holds the key to closing the gaps in diagnosis, care, and patient well-being while reducing costs and improving clinical efficacy. However, data leaks and breaches remain a significant concern in the IoMT environment. Personal information transmitted from clinical sensors over open Internet routes may be remapped by malicious actors, leading to data breaches [7].

Remarkable developments in the healthcare sector are driven by notable breakthroughs in the miniaturization of devices and technology, wireless technology, processing power, and computing capacity. With the ability to collect, analyze, and transfer data, connected medical devices have now become a reality as a result of this advancement. It is an interconnected ecosystem made up of software applications, medical devices, healthcare services, and computing systems. These devices and the data they collect comprise its core. The IoMT can assist in monitoring, alerting, and informing not carers but also healthcare professionals by providing real-time data to solve problems before they become life-threatening or by enabling early intervention [8].
