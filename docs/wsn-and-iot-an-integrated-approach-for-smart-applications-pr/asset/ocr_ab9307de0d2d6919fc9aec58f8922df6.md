One example of how Industry 5.0 is being applied through the use of cobots,
or collaborative robots. Cobots are simple to use and do not require training,
where workers can quickly incorporate them into their workflows. These are
robots that work alongside humans, assisting with tasks such as amate-
rial handling, which helps to improve overall efficiency and reduce the chances of
mistakes. Another example of Industry 5.0 in action is the use of augmented reality
(AR) and virtual reality (VR) technologies that help workers with real-time data and
instructions. AR and VR can be used to train workers, providing a more immer-
siving engaging learning experience.

The main difference between Industry 4.0 and Industry 5.0 is how focus
on automation. Fully automating the manufacturing process is the goal of Industry
4.0, while Industry 5.0 intends to balance automation and human involvement.
Therefore, they represent two different approaches to manufacturing. Industry 5.0
recognizes that some tasks are better suited to machines, while others require
ununique capabilities of humans. It encourages collaboration between humans
and machines to achieve better outcomes than either could achieve. With the
entire of the Industry 5.0 paradigm, the primary research shifted from sus-
tainability to human-centricity [32].
