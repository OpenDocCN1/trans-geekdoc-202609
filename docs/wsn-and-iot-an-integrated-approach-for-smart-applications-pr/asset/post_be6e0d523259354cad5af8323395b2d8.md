## 15.4.4 RECURRENT NEURAL NETWORKS (RNNs)

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are well-suited for hyperspectral classification tasks that involve sequential data, as neighboring pixels in a hyperspectral image. RNNs can model the temporal dependencies between adjacent pixels and capture the contextual information within hyperspectral data.

The key feature of RNNs is the presence of recurrent connections, allowing information to propagate not only forward but also backward in the network. This enables RNNs to learn and exploit the spectral dependencies in hyperspectral data. Long short-term memory (LSTM) and gated recurrent unit (GRU) are popular variants of RNNs that address the vanishing gradient problem and improve the ability to capture long-term dependencies [19].

In hyperspectral image classification, RNNs can effectively model the spectral variations across neighboring pixels, incorporating the spatial-spectral information. By considering the context of neighboring pixels, RNNs can improve the classification accuracy and handle the challenges posed by spectral variability and spatial-coupling (Figure 15.4).

## 15.4.5 DEEP BELIEF NETWORKS (DBNs)

DBNs learn hierarchical representations and extract latent variables from the hyperspectral data. Each layer of the DBN learns increasingly abstract features, capturing different levels of abstraction within the data. The unsupervised pretraining of DBNs allows them to learn effective representations without the need for labeled data. Once pretrained, the DBN can be fine-tuned for supervised classification. A systematic illustration is shown in Figure 15.5. DBNs have been applied to hyperspectral image classification by treating the spectral bands as visible variables and learning hidden representations that capture the underlying structure of the data.

To guarantee high-quality data and algorithm compatibility, hyperspectral classification with CNNs on the Pavia University dataset has to be preprocessed. The following preprocessing methods can be applied if:

- **Data format**: Datasets from Pavia University are frequently available in raw or other uncommon file types. The data must be transformed into a recognized format before employing a DL framework or library. The data may be converted into numpy arrays or tensors in TensorFlow.
- **Spectral band selection**: The nature of the classification task at hand, as well as the availability of computational resources, necessitate the selection of a subset of spectral bands for processing. The spectrum’s band selection preserves critical information while reducing load and the risk of noise from irrelevant bands.
- **Spatial resampling**: The spatial resolution of hyperspectral imaging depends on the spectral band being studied. It is possible to align the spatial resolution of all bands by resampling methods like k-nearest neighbors or bilinear interpolation. These methods can be applied to guarantee uniformity.
- **Data normalization**: It is normal to observe significant fluctuation in the intensity levels of the various bands while analyzing hyperspectral data. By bringing all the bands to a similar scale, data normalization approaches like min-max scaling and z-score normalization make it easier to train the model.
- **Data augmentation**: Increasing the variety of training samples and improving the model’s generalization performance are two goals of data augmentation. The images are cropped, given a noisy, grainy look, then randomly rotated or flipped.
