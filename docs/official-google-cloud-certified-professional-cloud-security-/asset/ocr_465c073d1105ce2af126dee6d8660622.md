# Applying constraints using the Organization Policy Service

The Organization Policy Service provides you with a centralized and programmatic method to apply constraints across your organization and the respective cloud resources. We often dictate requirements that need to be applied organization-wide; these constraints are non-negotiable and every resource in your cloud environment has to adhere. This is where Organization Policy Service comes in, giving you the ability to centrally apply these policies to avoid or mitigate misconfigurations downstream in your projects and cloud resources. This helps you create guardrails that you can define, which can be based on regulatory requirements or internal compliance. The development teams who are now consuming Google Cloud services not to worry about applying these policies and duplicating the effort. This further reduces the number of errors that may happen as you are now centrally in control of applying organization-wide constraints.

It is important to know the difference between IAM and Organization Policy Service constraints. While IAM focuses on who can execute an authorized action on a particular resource, policy constraints focus on the what, making what are enforced on the resources dictating how they can be configured. An example would be giving access to a user called Bob to perform administrative functions on Compute Engine using an IAM policy, but by defining a constraint of not allowing external IP addresses to be assigned to the compute, you can restrict Bob from configuring an external IP address for any instance under the organization.
