## 2.5. Definition of Applicability Domain (AD) of Models

Due to the limitation of the chemical space of the data sets, any built models in principle need to predefine AD,30 which is used to reasonably define the applied scope of the classification models. The setting of AD can prevent excessive prediction deviation resulting from the large characteristic difference between the tested chemicals and the training set. It means that if the query chemical is distributed in the AD, the model prediction can be considered to be relatively reliable; otherwise, it is less credible. In this study, the Euclidean distance-based method31 was used to obtain the AD, which is defined by the structural similarity between the training and the test sets. The distance of each molecule in the test set with its k-nearest neighbors in the training set was calculated. If one of these k distances exceeds the threshold of AD, the query compound is regarded as outside the AD;32 otherwise, it has fallen in the domain. The AD calculation is implemented according to the following formula

$$
D_x = \bar{y} + Z\sigma \quad (5)
$$

where $\bar{y}$ and $\sigma$ represent the average value and standard deviation of the calculated Euclidean distance, respectively, and $Z$ is an arbitrary parameter, which determines the size of the AD.

## 2.6. Identification of Privileged Substructures

In this study, privileged substructures refer to molecular functional groups that frequently appear in the CYP2C8 inhibitors. These substructures in inhibitors tend to react with the CYP2C8 metabolic enzyme to exert biological activity. Therefore, identification of these privileged substructures has an essential role for the determination of potential CYP2C8 inhibitors.33 Here, high-frequency substructures were evaluated by information gain (IG)34,35 and substructure frequency analysis.36 IG calculates the information entropy of each pattern in a molecule to assess its probability in the validation system. The greater the values of IG are, the more credible the substructures are. The substructure frequency can be calculated by the following functional formula

$$
\text{frequency of a fragment} = \frac{N_{\text{fragment}} \times N_{\text{total}}}{N_{\text{fragment, total}} \times N} \quad (6)
$$

where $N_{\text{fragment}}$ is the number of compounds containing the fragments in CYP2C8 inhibitors or noninhibitors, $N_{\text{total}}$ is the total number of compounds in the training set, $N_{\text{fragment, total}}$ is the total number of compounds containing this fragment, and $N$ is the number of CYP2C8 inhibitors or noninhibitors.

The MoSS node embedded in the KNIME 3.037 and the SARpy software38 was employed to identify the substructures in the training set. MoSS is a graph-based approach that generates substructures by embedding fragments parallel to all compounds throughout the growth process.39 SARpy uses “string mining” to extract substructures from the SMILES notation of the compounds, which does not split the ring structures.40 After many trials, in MoSS, “Minimum fold support in %” was set to 10, “Maximum complement support in %” and “Minimum fragment size” were set to 5, and “Maximum fragment size” was set to 16. The rest adopted the default settings. In SARpy, the numbers of atoms were set to range from 5 to 16, accuracy was set to “OPTIMAL”, and CYP2C8 inhibitors were chosen as the study object.

## 3. RESULTS

### 3.1. Data Set Analysis

We initially collected 303 inhibitors and 211 noninhibitors from PubChem and the literature. During the data collection process, we found that three compounds in PubChem were stereoismers of each other, one compound is the R configuration, one is S configuration, and the remaining one is a racemate. However, these three compounds are all labeled as “active”. We did not consider the chirality of the compound in the data processing so we treated two of the three compounds as duplicates and removed them. Three compounds extracted from the literature are incompatible with those extracted from PubChem. In the literature, these compounds are considered as noninhibitors, but they are labeled as “active” in PubChem. Thus, we removed these compounds for model construction. In addition, among these chemicals, 11 are duplicates and 1 is an inorganic compound, which were also removed. As such, we obtained 290 inhibitors and 204 noninhibitors in total. Since the inhibitors retrieved from the PubChem are very similar to each other, we clustered these inhibitors by the molecular fingerprint similarity method to obtain 250 inhibitors. For the validation data set, we collected 81 compounds from three sources. Among them, one is duplicated in the training and test sets and one is an inorganic compound containing boron. We removed these 2 compounds and got 79 compounds as the external validation set. Finally, we obtained 533 organic compounds from four different sources, including the literature, Web site, and two publicly available databases. Among them, 312 chemicals are positive (inhibitors) and 221 are negative (noninhibitors).

After data splitting, the training, test, and external validation sets contain 363, 91, and 79 compounds, respectively.
