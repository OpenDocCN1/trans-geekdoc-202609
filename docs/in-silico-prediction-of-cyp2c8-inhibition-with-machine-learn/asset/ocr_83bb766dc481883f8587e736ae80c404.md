# In Silico Prediction of CYP2C8 Inhibition with Machine-Learning Methods

Xiaoxiao Zhang, Piaopiao Zhao, Zhiyuan Wang, Xuan Xu, Guixia Liu, Yun Tang, and Weihua Li*

Cite This: Chem. Res. Toxicol. 2021, 34, 1850−1859

ABSTRACT: Cytochrome P450 2C8 (CYP2C8) is a major drug-metabolizing enzyme in humans and is responsible for the metabolism of ~5% drugs in clinical use. Thus, inhibition of CYP2C8, which causes potential adverse drug events, cannot be neglected. The in vitro drug interaction studies guidelines for industry issued by the FDA also point out that it needs to be determined whether investigated drugs are CYP2C8 inhibitors before clinical trials. However, current studies mainly focus on predicting the inhibitors of other major P450 enzymes, and the importance of CYP2C8 inhibition has been overlooked. Therefore, there is a need to develop models for identifying potentiaI CYP2C8 inhibition. In this study, in silico classification models for predicting CYP2C8 inhibition were built by five machine-learning methods combined with nine molecular fingerprints. The performance of the models built was evaluated by test and external validation sets. The best model had AUC values of 0.85 and 0.90 for the test and external validation sets, respectively. The applicability domain was analyzed based on the molecular similarity and exhibited an impact on the improvement of prediction accuracy. Furthermore, several representative privileged substructures such as 1H-benzo[d]imidazole, 1-phenyl-1H-pyrazole, and quinoline were identified by information gain and substructure frequency analysis. Overall, our results would be helpful for the prediction of CYP2C8 inhibition.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Inhibition of cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYPs) is one of the most important reasons for adverse drug reactions and drug delisting.1 Therefore, it needs to be determined whether drug candidates are inhibitors of major drug-metabolizing P450s before clinical trials. The in vitro drug interaction studies guidance for industry issued by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) stipulates that drug developers need to evaluate whether drugs inhibit the major P450 metabolic enzymes, including CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4.2

Experimental identification of P450 inhibition is generally time consuming, costly, and labor intensive. Thus, many in silico models have been developed to identify P450 inhibition.3−8 At present, the in silico prediction for P450 inhibition reported in the literature is mainly based on machine-learning methods, which are increasingly used in data analysis, classification problems, and other fields.9−11 Shao et al. used the rule-based C5.0 decision tree algorithm to build classification models for the inhibitors of five major P450s. Rydberg et al. exploited a support vector machine (SVM) to classify inhibitors and noninhibitors for the five main P450 enzymes and developed the WhichCyp Web server for predicting the P450 inhibitors. Our group12 employed an artificial neural network to develop a combined classifier, which is used to predict the inhibitors of five major P450

metabolic enzymes. Recently, Li et al.13 developed a multitask model based on an autoencoder deep neural network to discriminate inhibitors and noninhibitors of the five major P450 isoforms. Wu et al.14 used two ensemble-learning and two deep-learning methods to build classification models to predict inhibitors for the five major P450 enzymes. Although the reported models demonstrated relatively high accuracy, these studies mainly focused on the five major P450 isoforms including CYP3A4, 2D6, 2C9, 2C19, and 1A2. Other major drug-metabolizing P450s have not been involved in the public reports.
