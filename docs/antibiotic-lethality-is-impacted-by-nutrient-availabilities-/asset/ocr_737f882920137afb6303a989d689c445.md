In this Cell Year, Wright et al. describe a machine learning approach that can provide mechanistic insight from chemical agents. They use this to uncover the nutritional availability for *Escherichia coli* impacts lethality through widely used antibiotics.

With the rise of antibiotic resistance, there is a need for more antibiotics, but more important is the need for efficient methods screening the mechanisms governing antibiotic lethality. Chemical and genetic screens are used for drug discovery, but they suffer from low throughput. Furthermore, new drugs are difficult to identify the mode of action. For infectious diseases, it is often complicated by complex interactions between the host and microbe; for example, the specific mechanism that supports survival even in the presence of antibiotics.

In this Cell Year, Wright et al combine flux analysis with machine learning to get new insight into the epistatic factors underlying the lethality of three different antibiotics (Yang et al., 2019). *Escherichia coli* was grown in a wide range of different media, including

many different carbon and nitrogen sources, with the objective of measuring the effect of metabolic supplementation on the lethality of antibiotics (AMP, ciprofloxacin (CIP), and GENT). For each case, the IC₅₀, the concentration of antibiotic to inhibit growth, was measured in percentage of carbon sources that the bacteria improved tolerance towards when the antibiotic availability changed. To identify the cause of this effect, we used flux balance analysis (FBA) (O’Brien et al., 2015) for the different media. By FBA, it is possible to calculate the flux of the metabolic network in each condition and predict the phenotype at a detailed level (Niessen, 2003) (Figure 1). For each condition, the calculated fluxes were used as inputs for machine learning to identify which

changes alter lethality of three antibiotics, leading to the identification of 477 reactions that affected the antibiotic lethality. Next, we computed pathway scores for each pathway and antibiotic by performing least-squares regression on the changes in IC₅₀ of the central metabolism pathways (e.g., glycolysis and TCA cycles and amino acid synthesis). This is surprising as flux through these pathways is highly correlated with growth. More importantly, we found that decreased flux through a cluster of pathways in pyruvate metabolism resulted in decreased lethality of AMP and CIP but not of GENT.
