## Introduction

Integrating artificial intelligence (AI) in the workplace has created many challenges and opportunities for human work. Increased human–automation collaboration is expected on physical or cognitive tasks. Several disciplines have echoed the fact that these new technologies automate part of the work steps in collaboration with human operators rather than replacing entire professions, such as Information Technologies ([Seeber et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_37)), economics ([Frey & Osborne, 2017](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_11)), work psychology ([Parker & Grote, 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_30)) and human factors & ergonomics ([Mueller et al., 2021](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_27)). The area of Industry 4.0 (I4.0) is at the forefront of the digitalization of human work wherein AI plays a central role. I4.0 intends to increase production system capabilities in terms of productivity, repeatability, flexibility, real-time monitoring, and process standardization ([Zheng et al., 2021](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_47)). This is done by integrating a set of digital, robotic, and automated technologies into production ([Kadir et al., 2019](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_18)) and combining different digital solutions together ([Zheng et al., 2021](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_47)). The latest technological advances in I4.0 have increased the capabilities of machines in performing complex, cognitive tasks ([Xiong et al., 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_46)). However, the development of I4.0 technologies follows a technocentric approach ([Sony & Naik, 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_23)). Focusing on technology development first ([Carayannis et al., 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_4)). Bibliometric analyses quantified the technocentric directions of I4.0. A recent literature review noted that out of a sample of 4885 studies with a search strategy that included the terms *Industry 4.0* and *Human Factors*, 4849 studies focused on technical factors and 36 on human factors ([Passalacqua et al., 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_31)). This top-down approach often neglects the contextual factors that govern work systems and their potential integration into situated operational practices ([Loup-Escande, 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_21)).

Consequently, increased attention and principles for more effective and safer human–AI collaboration have been proposed by academics, governments, and industrial groups ([Kadir et al., 2019](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_18); [Maddikunta et al., 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_22); [Neumann et al., 2021](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_28)). The European Commission launched the Industry 5.0 (I5.0) stream to operationalize human-centricity, sustainability, and resiliency principles to align new technological development toward social values. New subfields emerged in response to the potential harm created by adopting a techno-centered design approach considering only the technical dimension of AI. First, several studies underlined the potential benefits that I5.0 could bring by relying on potential application cases and by formalizing avenues for future research ([Jiao et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_14); [Maddikunta et al., 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_22); [Seeber et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_37)); Second a series of frameworks or design principles were proposed to integrate humans and technologies in collaborative work settings ([Dubey et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_8); [Mueller et al., 2021](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_27)) or to consider human and organizational factors in digital transformation ([Liao et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_20); [Peeters et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_32)). However, while these efforts have provided initial coarse-grained guidance, these general principles do not consistently translate into everyday design practices or final AI-based products. Design teams require systematic methods that provide fine-grained guidance to design new solutions that promote long-term human well-being, engagement, and system performance within complex socio-technical environments ([Roth et al., 2018](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_35)).

This chapter presents HUMan-centered AI for Industry 5.0 (HUMAI5.0), an innovative six-step framework that supports design teams in adopting a human-centered approach for designing and implementing AI-fused systems in the workplace. We apply this framework to two use cases in the I5.0 context. This chapter adopts a human factors’ perspective wherein human-centered AI focuses on the collaboration between humans and AI systems in the workplace. The rest of the chapter is organized as follows: [Section 2](#pt2.xhtml) delves into the literature from which the framework is derived; [Section 3](#pt3.xhtml) presents the framework; and Section 4 applies it to two use cases.

## Related Literature

### Existing Design Methods and Frameworks

#### Technology-Centered Design for AI and Limits

AI-infused solutions often follow a technocentric roadmap where figures of merit estimate how mature the hardware and software components are ([Sony & Naik, 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_23)). When the solution reaches a satisfying level of technological readiness, development teams seek areas and problems where the device could be applied. While this top-down approach narrows the problem space by focusing only on technical details, it neglects the contextual and human factors that govern work systems ([Spies et al., 2020](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_39)). Moreover, techno-centrism often accompanies a desire to automate as many functions as possible to reduce human intervention in industrial processes, a source of uncertainty and failure in this philosophy ([Millot et al., 2015](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_25)). However, AI cannot, at present, fully acquire and reproduce tacit knowledge, socio-cognitive processes, and “a*dvanced know-how rooted in decades of institutional experience”* ([Miller & Feigh, 2019](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_24); p.2). This technology could however support or automate a specific portion of a task in human–technology collaboration scenarios ([Xiong et al., 2022](#ch23.xhtml#ref23_46)). Complementary design methods that pay equal attention to human, organizational, and technical factors in design processes have emerged, which will be discussed in the following subsection.
