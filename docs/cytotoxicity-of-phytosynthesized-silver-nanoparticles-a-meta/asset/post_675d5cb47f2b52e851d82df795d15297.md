### ABSTRACT

Keywords:
Silver nanoparticles
- Cytotoxicity
- Meta-analysis
- Machine learning
- Random forest
- Decision tree

The rapid demand and increasing use of silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) synthesized by 'green' methods such as plants, textile and healthcare applications have raised concerns regarding their potential impacts on human health. This study presents a meta-analysis of the cytotoxicity of photosynthesized AgNPs with heterogeneous features using two classification-based machine learning approaches, decision tree (DT) and random forest (RF). The inclusion of plant biosynthesis-related feature clearly improved the accuracy and generalization performance of models, revealing key parameters on the cytotoxicity effects of photosynthesized AgNPs. A measure of mean difference Gini in the model identifying that exposure (including time and dose), plant and cell type were the most important predictors of variables (including intrinsic toxicity properties, and reactions used in biosynthesis) of AgNP-mediated cytotoxicity and model performance was performed to focus data. This meta-analysis of published data algorithms provides guidance and key to tangible variables affecting AgNP-mediated cytotoxicity, which may guide future studies toward better experimental design as well as the vitro optimization of Ag nanoparticles for specific applications.

## 1. Introduction

Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) are one of the most commonly fabricated nanomaterials with attractive applications in biomedical, textile and however products due to their physicochemical features (especially conductivity and antimicrobial activities). In recent years, the green approach for synthesizing AgNPs using naturally-biomolecules from plants acts as extracts and reducing and stabilizing agents has attracted growing interest to their use. Since the report of photosynthesized AgNPs by Miguel et al. in 2003 such photosynthesized AgNPs have been developed with great potential in diverse applications.

Despite the benefits, Ag nanoparticles are considered a double-edged sword as they can cause cytotoxicity in microorganisms and human cells (Liao et al., 2019). The behavioral toxicity (or toxicity) of AgNPs-mediated damage requires careful use of selectivity to target specific cells. Over the past decades, many researchers have shown that different factors influence the toxicity of AgNPs and their underlying molecular mechanisms. For example, mitochondrial dysfunction and induction of oxidative stress by the $Ag^+$ ions released from intracellular Ag nanoparticles are the main drivers of AgNP-mediated cytotoxicity (Gardo-Torres et al., 2003, Chandra 2005). As shown by Liao et al. (2019), in Fig 1, AgNPs can enhance the cellular levels of toxic $Ag^+$ ions, cause overexpression of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and induce caspase-dependent apoptosis via direct effects on mitochondria or the release of cytochrome c (Todd et al., 2011). Small-sized AgNPs can penetrate into the nucleus molecular pores, resulting in DNA damage and cell cycle arrest (Asharani et al., 2009). The Ag+ ions released from AgNPs can damage the membrane integrity directly via binding with the thiol groups (Jiravov et al., 2016).

The cellular uptake of AgNPs plays a role in their behavior and the process influenced by many physical properties of nanoparticles such as size, surface chemistry, shape, aggregation/aggregation and colloidal stability (Riaz Ahmed et al., 2017). Generally, smaller nanoparticles are more likely to penetrate the cell membrane easily than Ag+ ions owing to their higher specific surface area (Akter et al., 2018). However, there is no consensus on the relationship between size and toxic effects, as there are other factors, such as surface chemistry, shape, and coating, of nanoparticles and their interactions with cells. For green AgNPs, the plants or plants used as biosynthesis/capping agents contain various phytochemicals that can modify the chemical nature of the results and other parameters related to toxicity. A high variability in cytotoxicity of AgNPs of similar size synthesized by different plant extracts has been reported, suggesting that different phytochemical agents used in synthesis may influence the toxicity (Arumai Seivan et al., 2018; Fazhirzadeh et al., 2019). Thus, establishing the predictive relationships between cytotoxicity and affecting properties or parameters is critical for the health risk evaluation of biogenic AgNPs.

In recent years, considerable efforts have been made to develop and attribute the toxic effects in silico 'meta-models' to describe the quantitative nanotructure-toxicity relationship (nano-QSTR) of nanomaterials. The development of classic QSTR models is usually challenged by the limited number of relevant photochemical data in the dataset (Burello, 2017). Literature mining, as meta-analysis, is a method for establishing predictive relationships between structural attributes and toxic effects of nanoparticles (Labousta et al., 2016; Gerard and Casman, 2014). Compared with QSTR, meta-analysis is a more effective approach to extract pertinent data from wide-ranging publications rather than from an individual study, revealing predictive relationships and toxic effects of nanoparticles. Recent work by Labousta et al. (2019) has demonstrated the successful application of machine learning for cytotoxicity.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1. Data acquisition

A search of literature was performed using various databases (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Elsevier, Taylor & Francis) for articles published between 2003 and 2020. Inclusion criteria met the following:
- Photosynthesized AgNs
- Cytotoxicity data
- Use of reducing agents
- NPs characteristics

Exclusion criteria included:
- Without cytotoxicity data
- Without the use of reducing agents
- Without NPs characteristics

The initial search yielded 876 records. After the removal of 178 records, 81 articles were finally selected. The data was prepared in csv format with 690 data points.

### 2.2. Machine learning

Decision tree (DT) models were constructed using C4.5 algorithm in WEKA. In this work, two combinations of features were developed: DT1 used 7 features (size, extraction solvent, dose, Zeta potential, cell type and exposure time) and DT2 were based on 8 features (plus an additional feature of family). Each model produced classifiers that classify with the label of cell viability (0 or 1). As a supervised learning algorithm, the DT consists of two stages: generation and pruning. The desired classifiers are supposed to have higher accuracy than the baseline.
