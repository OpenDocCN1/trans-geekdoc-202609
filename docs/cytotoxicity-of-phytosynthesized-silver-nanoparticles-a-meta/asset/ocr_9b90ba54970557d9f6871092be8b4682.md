The cellular uptake of AgNPs plays a role in their behavior and the process influenced by many physical properties of nanoparticles such as size, surface chemistry, shape, aggregation/aggregation and colloidal stability (Riaz Ahmed et al., 2017). Generally, smaller nanoparticles are more likely to penetrate the cell membrane easily than Ag+ ions owing to their higher specific surface area (Akter et al., 2018). However, there is no consensus on the relationship between size and toxic effects, as there are other factors, such as surface chemistry, shape, and coating, of nanoparticles and their interactions with cells. For green AgNPs, the plants or plants used as biosynthesis/capping agents contain various phytochemicals that can modify the chemical nature of the results and other parameters related to toxicity. A high variability in cytotoxicity of AgNPs of similar size synthesized by different plant extracts has been reported, suggesting that different phytochemical agents used in synthesis may influence the toxicity (Arumai Seivan et al., 2018; Fazhirzadeh et al., 2019). Thus, establishing the predictive relationships between cytotoxicity and affecting properties or parameters is critical for the health risk evaluation of biogenic AgNPs.

In recent years, considerable efforts have been made to develop and attribute the toxic effects in silico 'meta-models' to describe the quantitative nanotructure-toxicity relationship (nano-QSTR) of

nanomaterials. The development of classic QSTR models is usually challenged by the limited number of relevant photochemical data in the dataset (Burello, 2017). Literature mining, as meta-analysis, is a method for establishing predictive relationships between structural attributes and toxic effects of nanoparticles (Labousta et al., 2016; Gerard and Casman, 2014). Compared with QSTR, meta-analysis is a more effective approach to extract pertinent data from wide-ranging publications rather than from an individual study, revealing predictive relationships and toxic effects of nanoparticles that are not evident in the literature (Labousta et al., 2019; Oh et al., 2016; Gerard and Casman, 2014). Recent work by Labousta has demonstrated the successful application of machine learning for the assembly and generalization of heterogeneous cytotoxicity to provide simple, accurate and predictive classifiers for a spectrum of nanoparticles (Labousta et al., 2019). However, the intrinsic physical properties of nanoparticles are often insufficient to represent the complex interactions between AgNPs, biosynthesis agents and cytotoxicity.

Here, we report a more rigorous and comprehensive meta-analysis of cytotoxicity of green AgNPs. We hypothesize that the cell viability outcomes can be determined by a set of features including physical properties of nanoparticles (size, Zeta potential), cell types (human/animal/normal), exposure regime (dose and time), and the biosynthesis-related variables (plant factors, extraction method).
