
## 2 Related Works

Depression is a common mood illness that afflicts millions of people around the globe, essentially altering emotional states, cognitive functions, and daily routines. Successful treatment and prevention of long-term consequences of the disease need timely and correct diagnosis. The recent progress in ML and AI has allowed us to create new diagnostic approaches based on ample sources of heterogeneously diverse data such as psychological questionnaires, physiological readings, and social media feeds.

### 2.1 Hybrid Ensemble Model for Depression

Depression has recorded a remarkable increase in incidence in the last few decades, remaining undiagnosed for long periods and having serious impacts on mental as well as physical well-being. To tackle such a situation, computer-based detection systems with powerful ML algorithms are being more commonly researched. DeprMVM, a hybrid ensemble-based model presented here, is proposed for detecting depression through combining psychological and sociodemographic characteristics. The combination takes SVMs and multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) as the base (level 0) learners, whereas DeprMVM acts as the meta-learner (level 1). The results from SVM and MLP, together with other concerned variables, are utilized to train the meta-classifier to achieve better predictive accuracy. To handle class imbalance in the database, a blend of oversampling technique and cluster sampling was used. This approach not only enhanced accuracy of detection but also minimized overfitting risk. The performance of the model was strictly tested with various metrics and compared to the current methods on the same data. The results proved that the ensemble model presented in this work outperformed the existing work with a detection accuracy of 99.39% and an F1-score of 99.51%. These results accentuate the promise of hybrid ensemble learning in creating efficient, automatic systems for detecting depression [13].

### 2.2 Predicting Depression and Anxiety

After the COVID-19 pandemic, depression and anxiety levels have increased hugely as individuals adjusted to a new normal. Early detection, treatment with precision, and community care are the key factors for counteracting the influence of mental illness. Consistent with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which promote mental well-being through fair means and reduced inequalities, this study targets efficient, data-driven methods of detecting mental health disease. While depression detection is entering usage based on social media, its complete potential is limited because it lacks comprehensive annotated data. As an antidote, we propose a novel strategy of harnessing psychological questionnaire data to create population-level predictive models.

Enhanced hybrid models DT + kNN and RF + NN are presented in this paper and evaluated on three independent datasets for depression and anxiety prediction. DA-TabSVM, our multiclass classification model intended to handle complex patterns across datasets, is also presented. DT + kNN achieved 95% and 97% for depression and anxiety, respectively, while RF + NN achieved 98% for both. DA-TabSVM achieved 97%, 99%, and 99% accuracy for the three datasets for depression and up to 100% accuracy for anxiety detection. The hybrid approaches outperform traditional models by leveraging complementary strengths of algorithms and have a large potential for maximizing mental health diagnostics with ML [14].

### 2.3 AI-Driven Mental Health Assessment Platform

Mental disorders are a major source of emotional distress and it affects the perception, feelings, and behavior of people toward any situation. Physical and mental health is important to lead a productive life and achieve one's full potential. Mental well-being must be developed from childhood to adulthood. The usual causes of mental illness are stress of a long duration, social phobia, depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, drug addiction, work-related stress, and personality disorders. In this current research, publicly available web data sets were utilized for experimentation. The data was labeled to enhance predictive accuracy. LR, Naive Bayes (NB), decision trees (DTs), neural networks, and SVM were some of the ML algorithms employed. Among these, DTs, SVMs, and neural networks were most reliable in predicting stress, depression, and anxiety. These models were applied to the dataset to yield labeled results. Based on these labels, a predictive system was developed to identify the mental status of an individual. The primary target audience for this model would be working professionals who are above the age of 18, and they would use the system via a web-based interface [15].

### 2.4 Attention-Based Deep Depression Detection

An increasing number of people are suffering from mental illness because timely diagnosis and treatment are unavailable, especially for depression. The delay causes serious disorders such as anxiety, bipolar disorder, and sleep disorders, and in worst-case scenarios, self-inflicted injury or suicide. Early detection and treatment are among the biggest issues in mental health services. To counter this, we suggest a new hybrid DL model for predicting depression at its early stages. The model combines a CNN and an LSTM network with an added two-state LSTM architecture that uses a feature attention mechanism. The attention mechanism helps the model better identify predominant indicators and contextual patterns for depression. The model is tested on a big, publicly available corpus of text-based queries posed by children using an online platform. Features were extracted via one-hot encoding from symptom indicators established by psychology and medical professionals. Comparative assessments indicate that the novel model performs better compared to classic approaches [16].

### 2.5 Optimized Depression Classification

Depression is a common and disabling psychiatric illness that requires early and proper diagnosis in an effort to provide helpful intervention and treatment. Electroencephalography (EEG) has also been indicated as an effective noninvasive method to investigate neural signatures of depression and can provide useful assistance in clinical diagnosis. In this study, a methodology for diagnosing depressive disorders through EEG on publicly available multimodal open dataset for mental-disorder analysis is presented. EEG signals were recorded from 55 subjects with merely three electrodes in rest condition. Twelve temporal features were extracted from the gathered data by dividing the EEG signals into nonoverlapping segments of 10 s. A new FS technique was utilized to select the most discriminative features to distinguish depressing patients from healthy controls. The parameters that were detected were tested with three classifiers: Best-First (BF) Tree, kNN, and AdaBoost. BF-Tree classifier provided the best accuracy of 96.36% using a 12-feature vector that surpasses the current depression detection techniques. Notably, the system reported here has good classification performance with minimal EEG setup and hence is realistic and cost-effective. The model promises to be useful for application in psychiatric practice, offering clinicians a useful tool for assisting the diagnosis of depressive disorders [17].

### 2.6 Data-Driven Detection of Mental Health Disorders

Millions of people in the United States and other countries suffer from two of the most prevalent mental illnesses: fear and depression. These diseases not only affect individuals, they also have important social and economic consequences. The main focus of this study was the development of sophisticated ML models that could predict and track the risk of anxiety and depression. The advancement of AI and ML in the past few years has the potential to completely transform the diagnosis and treatment of disease. ML algorithms can be used to create predictive models that can go through enormous volumes of data and detect minute details that human clinicians might miss. For this research, a publicly available dataset from Kaggle and Harvard Dataverse was used. It contained behavioral, psychophysiological, and demographic information of 593 participants in the range of 18–35 years. Three ML models LR, XGBoost Regressor, and RF were used to predict probabilities of anxiety and depression.
