## A Hierarchical Cross-Fusion Feature Extraction Network for Accurate Cervical Cancer Classification Using Cytology Images

Abstract: Globally, cervical cancer is a cause of serious health concern, especially in less developed countries with a lack of frequent screening and specialized diagnosis. Although there has been advancement in the automated detection of cervical cancer due to the developments in deep learning over the recent past, many of the existing models suffer from poor feature representation, poor interpretability, and poor performance on class imbalance datasets. Particularly, these models tend to overlook inter-class similarity and high-resolution cellular differences and end up with low diagnostic reliability and generalizability. We introduce an innovative architecture named hierarchical cross-fusion feature extraction network (HCFEN) to address these issues. HCFEN is designed to improve contextual and morphological features in images of cervical cytology. HCFEN achieves powerful, multi-level features by combining cross-attention blocks, hierarchical fusion blocks, and a dual-branch convolutional neural network backbone. For minimizing dataset imbalance and improved minority class identification, a class-aware loss function is also utilized. Herlev dataset was used to evaluate the model proposed. For Herlev, HCFEN recorded F1-score: 98.5%, sensitivity: 98.2%, specificity: 99.0%, and accuracy: 98.7%. These facts establish that HCFEN provides an extremely accurate, robust, and interpretable model for computer-aided cervical cancer detection.

Keywords: cervical cancer, deep learning, feature extraction, cytology images, cross-attention, medical image classification

## 1 Introduction

Cervical cancer has long been an important public health issue, especially among low-resource communities where routine screening and early detection are not available. The early part of the twentieth century saw the development of cytological devices and historic milestones that initiated the path toward successful cervical cancer screening. The Pap smear test, devised by Dr Georgios Papanicolaou as early as 1928 for the detection of precancerous and cancerous cervical cells, was the revolutionary method employed for screening cervical cancer. Through the possibility of early detection that immensely lowered the mortality rates of the disease, the method revolutionized the screening of cervical cancer. Following Papanicolaou's publication with Dr Herbert Traut, proving the value of the test in the diagnosis of cancer of the uterus, Pap smear test became widely applied in the 1940s.

Though effective, the Pap smear test is not without limitations such as subjectivity in interpretation, variation in sample quality, and the need for trained cytotechnologists. Various approaches have been developed in order to improve presentation of contextual and morphological cues in cervical cytology images in an attempt to solve these issues. The use of high-tech staining methods, including the Papanicolaou stain, which helps pathologists diagnose through the capability to identify cells in smear preparations, is a technique that is used. Furthermore, the Bethesda system for reporting cervical cytology, which was first launched in 1988 and has since been updated numerous times, provides standardized language and criteria for reporting and interpreting Pap smear results. This promotes more effective communication and consistency among healthcare professionals.

Cervical cytology images are now more clearly depicted through developments in imaging technology. Cellular detail and pathology can be more precisely visualized as a result of computer-aided analysis and high-resolution digital photography. Computerized assessment is used by automated screening systems like AutoPap and AutoCyte Screen to examine Pap smear slides, eliminating errors and gaining efficiency. Accurate acquisition and understanding of intricate morphological and contextual information of cervical cytology images continue to prove difficult using these advancements. Proper identification is blocked by superimposed cells, inconsistency of staining quality, and slight variance between normal and abnormal cells. Moreover, noise, artifacts, low-resolution images, and heterogeneous sample preparation may pose a problem for current automatic systems.

Deep learning techniques being applied to cervical cancer screening have been promising in addressing some of these concerns. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are specific deep learning algorithms that have been performing extremely well in image classification and segmentation. Through the learning of intricate patterns and features from large datasets, these models hold the potential to improve the accuracy and efficacy of cervical cytology examination.

Deep learning approaches also have some drawbacks. They tend to need tremendous, tagged datasets to train them, which are not always easily available. Clinicians would find it difficult to comprehend the justification for those models' predictions because of their sensitivity to image quality fluctuations and potential uninterpretability. Furthermore, imbalance datasets may provide biased predictions to deep learning models when certain classes (e.g., rare precancerous lesions) are underrepresented.

Therefore, innovative approaches are required that can beat the shortfalls of current approaches and accurately extract contextual and morphological information from cervical cytology images. This is the motivation to create the hierarchical cross-fusion feature extraction network (HCFEN). Through its fusion of multi-scale information and cross-fusion processes, HCFEN can improve feature representation and identify compound patterns in cervical cytology images. HCFEN can enhance the precision and validity of automated cervical cancer screening by surmounting the shortcomings of existing techniques, thus ending up benefiting patients.

The main two objectives of the study based on the proposed HCFEN for cervical cancer classification are:

- 1. To develop an advanced deep learning framework (HCFEN) that effectively captures both morphological and contextual features from cervical cytology images using hierarchical and cross-fusion mechanisms.
- 2. To enhance classification performance across both binary and multi-class cervical cancer detection tasks, particularly on imbalanced and noisy datasets, by incorporating attention-guided learning and class-aware loss functions.

## 2 Related Study

In the last decade, the application of deep learning and artificial intelligence (AI) strategies has revolutionized cervical cancer classification based on cytology images. While Pap smear examinations and other traditional screening methods have been effective, they are prone to subjectivity and human mistake. Deep learning, specifically CNNs, has improved the automation and accuracy of cervical cancer diagnosis. In order to enhance the performance of classification, scientists have recently suggested different models that try to extract and utilize both the morphological features of cells as well as contextual knowledge regarding their spatial organization.

CNNs were first used in a seminal work to classify cervical cancer from cytology images automatically, proving that deep learning can work better than manual or semiautomatic methods [1]. However, management of intricate image transformations and class imbalances was difficult for this early use.
