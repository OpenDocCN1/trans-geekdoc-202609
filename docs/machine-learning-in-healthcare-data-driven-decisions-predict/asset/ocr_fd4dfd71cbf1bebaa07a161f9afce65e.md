# ## 2.2 FAIR Federated Health Data Prediction

A federated ML framework has been designed as privacy-friendly and uses “Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable” (FAIR) health data to facilitate cooperative model development among various healthcare organizations without divulging confidential patient data. An agent-based system, the framework applies classification models in a decentralized way, building an overall predictive model from local models learned in each organization’s secure environment. The design was tested by five healthcare organizations, which each converted their datasets to FAIR-compliant formats through a standardized FAIRification process. Federated ML agents at each location interacted with a central federated ML manager to jointly train a model for 30-day readmission risk prediction for COPD patients, which had an accuracy of 87%. This study illustrates the viability of deploying federated ML on FAIR health data and the capacity for balancing data interoperability, predictive performance, and privacy to enable real-world clinical decision-making under data protection regulations [12].

# ## 2.3 Federated Learning for Rare Diseases

A FL model is suggested to improve privacy-preserving EHR analysis. The model makes it possible for numerous healthcare organizations to work together securely by training ML models without explicitly sharing patient data. A number of ML methods, including random forests (RF), support vector classifiers, decision tree classifiers, logistic regression, and stacking classifiers, were employed and evaluated to predict the treatment requirements of patients. With an _F_<sub>1</sub>-score of 80% and an accuracy of 90%, the RF classifier outperformed the others and was capable of handling intricate and class-imbalanced data. The FL-based technique helps to address the major issues of sharing health information and is also complaint with the majority of privacy requirements including GDPR and HIPAA. As a result, it is especially useful in rare disease research, when concerns about confidentiality and data scarcity are more prominent. The system significantly improves the ability to investigate rare illnesses and advances therapy development by facilitating safe data aggregation at institutions. In order to further enhance performance, future research will concentrate on expanding the framework’s application to other health domains and incorporating increasingly complex ML approaches. A new benchmark for the safe, open, and moral examination of health data is set by this work [13].
