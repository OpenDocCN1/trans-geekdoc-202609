As scored with the ROC-AUC metric analysis in Figure 4, a measure of how well a model can discriminate between positive and negative classes across all probability thresholds, the TNPE achieves a greater value of 0.963. That failing is particularly unfortunate in medical diagnosis applications, when very high specificity and sensitivity are crucial to make sure clinical judgments are trustworthy, while preventing pernicious mistakes of diagnosis.

## 5 Discussion

Enhanced Accuracy and Performance: The TNPE technique consistently outperformed the standard ML models such as kNN, DT, and NB across all the performance measures, that is, accuracy, F1-score, ROC-AUC, and MCC. This confirms that TNPE is very capable of handling the complexities of mental health data and provides sound predictions for detecting depression and anxiety.

Hybridization of Heterogeneous-Based Learners: By making use of three base learners, ELM, PNN, and CatBoost, the model combines the best of each. ELM provides speed and generalization on small- and medium-sized datasets, PNN has strong probabilistic decision-making capabilities, and CatBoost works well with categorical variables and mixed-type data. This heterogeneity allows TNPE to learn a very wide range of patterns in mental health data, which positions it as a general-purpose model for real-world use.

Interpretability: TNPE's highest value may be its interpretability. LIME and SHAP techniques have been applied in the model, with explainable predictions being given, allowing clinicians to have rational reasons for why they made particular decisions. This feature of explaining predictions is invaluable in clinical application, where one cannot ill afford to lose trust in an AI system.

FS and Balancing Data: Use of ADASYN to counter class imbalance and Boruta to perform FS is strengthening the model and reducing overfitting, which enhances generalizability. This preprocessing is ensuring that the model is learning the most significant information within data and therefore leading to better performance in general.

Scalability and Flexibility: The TNPE method can be used for the majority of mental health data like clinical reports, questionnaires, and tweets. Since it supports a high number of data sources, it is a general-purpose solution for depression and anxiety prediction in most varied environments.

Even though TNPE is better than traditional models, its size which includes a variety of basic learners and is improved by PSO may result in longer training durations, particularly when dealing with larger datasets. Such computational costs can render it inapplicable in real-time or low-resource environments such as mobile health. Model performance is highly reliant on input data quality and preprocessing. Poor-quality clinical records, missing data, or improperly labeled social media data can decrease model performance. In addition, preprocessing steps like text embedding by the USE might be sparse in representing the complexity of text compared to mental health and, in doing so, potentially impacting the model's understanding of more complex linguistic patterns. The data employed here was sampled from clinical histories, mental health surveys, and text data from social media, which would correspond to only some demographic groups or world populations. Therefore, although TNPE is good on this data, how it will perform on other populations or regions with distinct cultural, demographic, or linguistic compositions is not well established. It would perhaps have to be tested again and tuned more generally for broader applicability.

Although satisfactory performance was presented by the model through 10-fold cross-validation, the actual model itself hasn't been tested in real clinical settings. While practicing in real life, real-life predictions output by the model can face missing or inconsistent information, which could impact its dependability. Its real-world utilization and constant inspection must be executed so as to demonstrate its use in real-world clinical settings. While Boruta algorithm FS tries to constrain overfitting, even the combination of an enormous number of models and their interactions in the forms of pushing each other in the ensemble framework are still susceptible to overfitting, especially with cases of enormous features and relatively small amounts of data. Although TNPE performed best under the existing configuration, it must be tested against a huge volume of datasets so that its stability and strong performance under different mental health datasets are tested.

## 6 Conclusion

This chapter presents TNPE as novel ensemble architecture to enhance the detection of depression and anxiety by synergistically integrating the advantages of ELM, PNN, and CatBoost. With a soft voting ensemble augmented with PSO, TNPE outperforms certain ML models in terms of predictive accuracy. Among the six benchmarking techniques, TNPE performed better than kNN, DT, NB, MLP, GBM, and AdaBoost on all the evaluation metrics. In particular, TNPE scored an impressive ROC-AUC of 0.963 and F1-score of 0.927, reflecting its stronger capability to distinguish depression/anxiety cases from non-cases as well as maintain both recall and precision in balance. Further, accuracy and MCC were much better in TNPE, which depicts its high accuracy on advanced mental health data.
