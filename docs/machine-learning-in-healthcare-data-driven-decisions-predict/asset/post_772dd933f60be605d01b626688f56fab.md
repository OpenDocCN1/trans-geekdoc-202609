On the other hand, there are many research works [7–10, 12, 13], where the researchers collected various sensor data, such as accelerometer, gyroscope, GPS, screen status, SMS, phone call logs, and typing patterns, to predict emotions through smartphones. FFT and other frequency-domain methods have become vital tools in biomedicine due to their ability to extract meaningful patterns from noisy, nonstationary data. In recent years, researchers have significantly used the Fourier transformation to enhance signal clarity, reveal latent periodic features, and improve classification accuracy. In the domain of EEG-based emotion recognition, many studies demonstrate the utility of FFT. Wang et al. [21] proposed a Fourier adjacency transformer to separate periodic and aperiodic EEG components and enhance inter-channel correlation by using adjacency-based attention mechanisms. This model yields a 6.5% increase in emotion recognition accuracy on the SEED and DEAP datasets. Similarly, Cruz-Vazquez et al. [22] integrated the Fourier neural network with a quantum rotation layer to enhance emotion classification. This research highlighted how frequency-domain preprocessing can improve the performance of convolutional models, particularly in detecting stable emotional states such as sadness. Further, Yang et al. [23] advanced this line of work by developing a DFrt-ES model using the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT), which aids FFT by allowing analysis at varying angles between the time and frequency domains. By extracting features like power spectral density (PSD) and differential entropy (DE), they achieved a classification accuracy of 95.63% on the DEAP dataset. Similarly, several studies demonstrated the effectiveness of FFT in extracting spectral features for emotion recognition using EEG signals. In [24], spectral centroid and entropy features were extracted from EEG using FFT and filtered with Butterworth and surface Laplacian filters. They achieved 91.33% accuracy in classification using k-nearest neighbor.

The previous studies have demonstrated impressive results using FFT and machine learning models for EEG-based emotion recognition or biomedical signal analysis. Previous studies primarily depend on controlled, lab-oriented datasets such as DEAP and SEED. These datasets are collected under structured environments with minimal noise and with standard protocols, within limits, for their real-world applicability. In contrast, our approach stands out by experimenting on an in-the-wild dataset collected from real-world environments using a commercial smartwatch, Fitbit Sense, which introduces natural variability, noise, and diverse user behaviors. Applying FFT in such unstructured datasets not only tests the robustness of frequency domain feature extraction but also the generalizability of classical machine learning models in wild conditions. This makes our work more representative of practical applications and demonstrates a crucial step toward deploying emotion recognition systems in real-world scenarios, something most prior studies have not addressed. Thus, our strategy offers a novel contribution by bridging the gap between a controlled research environment and in-the-wild data contexts.

### 3.2 Feature Extraction and Preprocessing

In this section, we first describe the details of feature extraction in two domains: (I) the time domain and (II) the signal domain. Then, we discuss the preprocessing of the data we consider in our experiment.

- **(I) Feature Extraction in Time Domain:** We merge Fitbit, SEMA3, and survey data to analyze time domain features. We only consider the PANAS and STAI surveys when creating the customized dataset. The PANAS survey measures a person's enthusiastic, alert, and active behavior based on positive and negative affects over a specific period. Higher positive affect indicates a person is highly excited, concentrated, and engaged in pleasurable activities, while lower positive affect reflects sadness and lethargy. Negative affect captures a person's dimensions of subjective distress and unpleasant engagement. Lower negative affect signifies calmness and serenity, while higher negative affect encompasses feelings of anger, fear, guilt, and anxiety. Conversely, the STAI provides anxiety scores. This is why we only include these two surveys, despite the availability of many psychological surveys. After successfully merging the datasets, we obtain 7,435 rows and 82 columns.

- **(II) Feature Extraction in Signal Domain:** To extract additional features in the signal domain and find hidden patterns in physiological data related to emotions, we use statistical analysis, FFT, peak detection, and power spectral density on heart rate variability. The details of the feature extraction are written below step-by-step:

- 1. **Time Domain**: To extract time domain features, we apply statistical analysis and generate new features: mean heart rate (HRmean), standard deviation (HRstd), minimum and maximum HR, interquartile range (HRIQR), skewness, and kurtosis.
- 2. **Frequency Domain**: To derive the spectral characteristics, we use FFT to analyze frequency components and also use PSD estimated via Welch's method.
- 3. **Linear Features**: This category consists of time and frequency domain features, as well as statistical and signal-based measures, assuming some linear relation within the HRV signal. For example, the power spectrum features, autocorrelation metrics, and several slope- and energy-based measures fall under this feature set. While there is some redundancy with the time- and signal-domain features, these features are grouped based on their use of linear analysis models.
- 4. **Nonlinear Features**: These features capture the intricate, messy, and fractal-like behaviors of the HRV signal, which cannot be reframed using linear models. Popular non-linear measures include sample entropy and approximate entropy, Poincaré plot descriptors (SD1 and SD2), detrended fluctuation analysis (DFA), wavelet entropy, and recurrence quantification metrics. Nonlinear metrics and entropy-based metrics stand out from other linear techniques due to their efficacy in revealing minute shifts in autonomic control and physiological complexity.
