# 1 Introduction

The recent years have seen increasing significance of real-time public health surveillance systems in disease outbreak prediction and control. Global health emergencies like the COVID-19 pandemic [1], Ebola outbreaks [2], and seasonal flu [3] outbreaks emphasize the need for more robust and precise prediction techniques to inform public health responses. Early detection and immediate response to the new epidemic outbreak can limit the spread to a large extent, reduce the effect on society, and preserve lives. ARIMA and long short-term memory (LSTM) [4] are good old-fashioned forecasting methods but tend to ignore the dynamic and spatial distribution of epidemic outbreaks. This is especially achieved when performing outbreak predictions in real-time because such models might not necessarily be developed to handle spatio-temporal data or to learn how to adapt to evolving patterns of disease over time.

EpiCastNet overcomes these constraints by proposing a new framework integrating spatiotemporal modeling, GNNs [5], and temporal convolutional networks (TCNs) [6] with adaptive online learning. EpiCastNet combines heterogeneous data sources, such as health records, social media behavior, weather, and mobility patterns, to generate real-time disease predictions [7]. The combined architecture allows the system to accommodate not only the temporal dimension of disease dynamics but also the spatial dimension including how transmission during outbreaks works between regions or populations. This integrated strategy provides early warning and early detection with considerably longer lead times than conventional forecasting models.

The architecture is also flexible-capable of adapting to new streams of data and evolving disease patterns over time. The flexibility helps ensure that EpiCastNet still provides correct predictions as disease patterns evolve. Apart from its predictive function, EpiCastNet also seeks to enhance spatial accuracy, that is, predicting the geographical regions that will be most impacted by a outbreak. Through graph neural networks (GNNs) in modeling the relationships between various regions, the system can identify the regions where interventions (e.g., medical supplies and lockdowns) will be most effective.

Whereas more conventional outbreak forecasting instruments will typically apply old data to their predictions, EpiCastNet is equipped with the state-of-the-art technology of real-time data streams. This produces more contemporary forecasts but, critically, ones which more accurately represent the latest patterns in outbreaks as well as permitting a window period of prevention measures for governments and agencies prior to the outbreak peak.
