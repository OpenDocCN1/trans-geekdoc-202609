The eICU dataset supplemented 31,762 additional records in 200 hospitals. Preprocessing both datasets included using consistent temporal granularity (hourly bins), SNOMED/ICD-10 standard coding through mapping, and note segmentation, with a pretrained BioClinicalBERT tokenizer.

We developed several strong baselines in order to provide comparable performance: For tabular EHR features, employed XGBoost (gradient boosted decision trees); feedforward neural networks (FNNs) with dropout regularization and ReLU activations; models that only employ transformers, e.g., standard encoder stacks, which are learned from concatenated multimodal embeddings; GAT-only variant, excluding the policy and VAE layers; variation that only employs VAE, with no reinforcement layers or graph structure; policy learner based on REINFORCE, as a non-deep RL baseline. To provide balanced representation for both positive and negative outcome classes (e.g., ICU readmission, death, and sepsis onset), each model was trained and validated in a fivefold stratified cross-validation environment. Early stopping was used based on validation AUC with a 10-epoch patience duration.

Three primary aspects of the assessment were accuracy of early risk prediction, precision of recommendations for treatment, and clinical utility outcomes. We utilized AUROC, F1-score, accuracy, precision, and recall for classification tasks (e.g., 24-h sepsis prediction). We utilized NDCG (normalized discounted cumulative gain) and top-K precision (K = 1, 3, 5) for recommendation tasks. We quantified readmission reduction, mortality prediction accuracy, and length-of-stay minimization by replicating care pathway optimizations under simulation using retrospective data to estimate real-world effectiveness.

From Figure 2, PATHTRACK-RL surpassed the top baseline (Transformer-only, 88.2% accuracy) by attaining 94.1% accuracy, 92.6% recall, and 93.3% F1-score on early ICU risk prediction (Figure 3). In Figure 4, compared to 84.7% and 0.891 for GAT-only, treatment recommendation tasks had a Top-3 precision of 91.8% and NDCG@5 of 0.944. Longitudinal simulations also recorded a 7.4% improvement in discharge outcome scores based on SAPS-II risk scale and an average 11.2% reduction in the ICU readmission rate. Omitting reinforcement-based treatment optimization reduced the quality of recommendations by over 7.1% in Top-3 precision, whereas removing the GAT layer reduced accuracy by 4.9% based on ablation experiments. Including the stacked VAE was also essential, as it enhanced multimodal encoding efficiency by 3.4% according to reconstruction loss and latent clustering purity.

Table 1 clearly shows how much better PATHTRACK-RL performs in early risk prediction compared to deep learning and standard baselines. Though FNNs and XGBoost models perform reasonably well, their prediction is undermined by their incapacity to detect intricate relations within relational data. PATHTRACK-RL possesses the best balance between accuracy and recall, attaining the highest accuracy (94.1%) and F1-score (93.3%). Its AUROC of 0.963 is remarkable in that it is found to have the extraordinary power of discriminating between critical and noncritical cases.
