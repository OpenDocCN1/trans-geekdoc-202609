# 2.7 Ensemble-LR-RF and SVM-Based Stroke Risk Prediction

The deployment of a stroke prediction system based on ML can be an exciting development in the identification of individuals at high risk of having a stroke. With the use of large data sets at their command, ML models can identify subtle, nonlinear patterns, and weak predictors that are not even apparent to clinicians. Such ability has the potential to improve early detection substantially, facilitate timely intervention, and inform preventive action to lower stroke prevalence and better serve patients. Although previous research has applied individual ML models to predicting strokes, this research investigates a combination of the LR, RF, and SVM models. Each of the algorithms has its own strengths and weaknesses. RF provided the highest predictive accuracy due to its ensemble and ability to process nonlinear relations and FI. Conversely, LR and SVM provided better computational efficiency and training complexity, rendering them appropriate in situations needing fast or limited deployments. The hybrid modeling method leveraged the predictive power of the three classifiers compared on aspects such as accuracy, training time, and stability. Among the three, RF performed best for this dataset and was appropriate for application within stroke risk assessment procedures. LR and SVM are also suitable depending on application requirements. Further, ML-based stroke prediction models provide medical science with a breakthrough tool that makes it possible to identify risk patients early. The research confirms the capability of ML in medical diagnosis and highlights the need to conduct more research on developing more advanced algorithm selection, feature extraction, and model training for easier clinical applicability [19].

The reviewed works collectively emphasize the revolutionizing potential of ensemble and hybrid ML models in stroke prediction model development.
