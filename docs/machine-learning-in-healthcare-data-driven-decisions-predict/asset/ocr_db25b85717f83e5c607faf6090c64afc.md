However, with the increasing availability of various healthcare data like imaging data, sensor output data, genomic sequences, and clinical notes, it has made it possible for advanced computational methods that try to extract valuable information pertaining to treatment for patients. That means, because there is this unprecedented influx of multimodal data, with intelligent systems that can do real-time analytics and decision support, it offers a chance to transform patient monitoring, diagnosis, and treatment planning.

Healthcare optimization has emerged as a key facilitator for efficiently utilizing this data. By determining the optimum course of action within specified limitations, such as resource availability, patient safety, and clinical effectiveness, optimization techniques seek to enhance the outcomes of healthcare delivery. Many strategies have been designed and innovated over the years to optimize many aspects of healthcare systems, ranging from artificial intelligence and machine learning to operations research. In general, the methods included swarm intelligence, evolutionary computation, heuristic algorithms, linear and nonlinear programming, and more recently, reinforcement learning (RL). Since healthcare decision-making is complex, dynamic, and multi-objective, each method has its unique merits and also its natural drawbacks.

For resource allocation issues like operating room scheduling, staff rostering, and supply chain management, traditional optimization approaches like linear programming and mixed-integer programming have been widely applied. Although these approaches offer mathematically sound answers, they frequently call for oversimplified issue formulations that overlook the complexities of clinical procedures and patient diversity. In an effort to circumvent computational complexity, heuristic and metaheuristic algorithms such as particle swarm optimization, simulated annealing, and genetic algorithms have been devised to yield approximations of answers within reasonable quantities of time. Notwithstanding their flexibility for treating nonlinear and multi-objective problems, these algorithms often require careful tuning of parameters and could be led to converge to less-than-desirable solutions due to premature convergence or local optima.

Machine learning-based optimization frameworks that adaptively learn from data to enhance therapeutic outcomes have been introduced by more recent developments in artificial intelligence. Predictive modeling challenges in diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment recommendation have been addressed by methods like supervised learning, unsupervised clustering, and deep learning. These methods are typically implemented in static or batch learning modes, which limits their ability to respond to patient states or clinical situations that dynamically change. RL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for dynamic optimization across the healthcare sector. RL describes decision-making as a sequential process with an environmental feedback loop. Personalized treatment plans, medication dose schedules, and patient monitoring policies have all been optimized through the application of reinforcement learning techniques such as Q-learning, deep Q networks, and proximal policy optimization (PPO). However, pure RL approaches face challenges related to sample inefficiency, reward design, and the difficulty of balancing multiple clinical objectives simultaneously.
