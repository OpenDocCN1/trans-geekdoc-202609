# 7 Open Datasets and Research Resources

Open access repositories are instrumental to the development of stress detection studies through the provision of varied, labeled physiological datasets. In Table 12, the repositories constitute important resources for training, benchmarking, and reproducibility of research results.

| Repository/platform | Description |
|---|---|
| IEEE DataPort | A full fledged platform with a vast collection of physiological and biomedical signal datasets, such as ECG, EEG, and stress data. |
| Open Science Framework (OSF) | An open collaborative platform for research data, tools, and protocols management and sharing across disciplines. |
| Zenodo | An open access general purpose repository under development within the European OpenAIRE program and housing datasets, software, and publications. |
| Figshare | An online repository for sharing scholarly outputs like datasets, figures, and code with DOI support. |
| NIH data sharing repositories | Federated sources of biomedical and clinical data, curated under U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH) guidelines. |

Open access data repositories are the foundation to the development of stress detection systems using ML. The platforms not only offer well-labeled, high-quality physiological data but also enable global collaboration, reproducibility, and transparency in the research. Repositories like IEEE DataPort offer extensive datasets of real-world biomedical signals such as ECG, EEG, and skin conductance, which are widely utilized to train supervised and unsupervised learning algorithms for stress detection.

The Open Science Framework (OSF) is a flexible framework in which multidisciplinary researchers can share datasets, protocols, and code together – facilitating reproducibility and reducing redundant effort. Zenodo, within the European OpenAIRE project, offers persistent preservation and citability of publications and data sets, most suitable for scholarly use and peer review [19,20].

Figshare and others permit sharing of research output by assigning digital object identifiers (DOIs) to figures, datasets, and supplementary data, which can be retrieved and cited. NIH data sharing repositories are also critical to access biomedical and clinical datasets that have been curated, especially for mental illness and chronic stress research, under stringent U.S. federal regulations.
