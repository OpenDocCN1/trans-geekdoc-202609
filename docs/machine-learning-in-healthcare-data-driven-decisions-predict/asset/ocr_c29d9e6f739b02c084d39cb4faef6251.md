This asymmetry often results in universal therapy suggestions without consideration for each patient's individual characteristics. In addition, most models are restricted in their ability to generalize and transfer to diverse populations and settings of care as they are trained on retrospective data from specific hospital systems or cohorts.

Data integration and interoperability are also limitations. EHRs, imaging systems, wearable technology, and genomic databases are only some of the varied data sources that comprise the current healthcare ecosystem and often do not effectively communicate with each other. Beyond hindering the comprehensiveness of patient profiles, these data silos also constrain the capability of AI models that rely on large, longitudinal datasets. In addition, the incorporation of unstructured data remains a key challenge. While radiological reports, clinical narratives, and patient comments provide valuable information, their effective use requires the application of sophisticated natural language processing (NLP) techniques.

Interpretability concerns and reliability issues are also longstanding in healthcare AI. Even with their power, deep learning models are often called “black boxes” due to their impenetrable decision-making processes. The inability to offer an explanation for a treatment that has been prescribed could erode professional confidence and hinder uptake in a healthcare sector where reliability and transparency are paramount. The risk of bias in AI models, especially when they are trained on unbalanced datasets that underrepresented specific groups, worsens this and may lead to inequities in care.

Many existing systems cannot keep up with new medical data and treatment protocols. New evidence often results in revisions to clinical guidelines, but frozen models cannot learn to automatically accommodate new changes without being retrained. Their application in real-time clinical scenarios is constrained by this inflexibility. In addition, relatively few systems incorporate feedback and continuous learning processes from treatment results, which are essential for improving clinical effectiveness and long-term model precision.

A genuinely patient-centric, smart therapy recommendation platform is more needed than ever against these challenges. Artificial intelligence (AI) is regarded as a fundamental facilitator of the increasing phenomenon of personalized medicine, which tailors medical treatment to each patient’s distinct characteristics. The need to overcome the limitations of current models through the use of all available health data, ensuring model transparency, encouraging interoperability, and continually learning from actual outcomes drives the creation of a new system.

In addition, the COVID-19 pandemic has underscored the deficit of the health system as it exists now and the importance of scalable, resilient, and flexible digital health solutions. Patients have greater expectations for active disease control, telemedicine, and personalized care experience than those offered by conventional systems. Conversely, clinicians require robust decision support systems that can reduce cognitive burden, reduce medical errors, and enhance treatment precision.
