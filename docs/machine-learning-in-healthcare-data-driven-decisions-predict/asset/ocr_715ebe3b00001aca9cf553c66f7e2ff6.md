Pap smear image variability hinders classification. Contextual information, including background artifacts or clustering patterns, is as relevant as morphological characteristics such as cell size, shape, texture, and nucleus-to-cytoplasm ratio. Fractional coefficients and machine learning classifiers were used in a research study to screen for cervical cancer; these tools produced more accurate results with balanced datasets, but they failed to generalize to larger and more heterogeneous samples [4]. The use of whole slide images (WSIs) and liquid-based cytology was a significant innovation that supported mass learning and noise elimination, as illustrated by models that prioritize AI-aided screening across entire specimens [5].

Ensemble learning techniques are now more commonly used as a solution for dealing with variability in features at varying magnifications and imaging conditions. Through the utilization of numerous deep models, one ensemble technique showed higher robustness and classification performance on cytology images [6]. Similarly, CervixFuzzyFusion improved cervical cell image classification sensitivity and explainability through the combination of CNNs and fuzzy logic [7]. The study demonstrated the increased ability of AI for routine screening using application of precision AI-based models to classify cervical cytology grades with high accuracy [8].

Feature extraction is still a fundamental aspect. By employing a pyramid structure to preserve high-resolution details as well as learning representations at various levels of abstraction, exemplar pyramid deep feature extraction models have been promising [9]. In the meanwhile, graph convolutional networks are suggested to better encode the spatial interaction of cells [10]. These methodologies highlight the significance of not just being aware of the properties of a single cell but also cell contact and topological relationship between cells.

Multimodal systems are utilized to boost classification. There are multimodal models that incorporate imaging, pathology, and clinical data. An example is the enhanced multimodal fusion deep learning neural network used to diagnose lung cancer and provide results that can be used in the detection of cervical cancer [11]. In order to improve feature extraction and avoid background noise, some studies have focused on cell nuclei and cytoplasm separation and consequently improved the use of segmentation-based methods [12].

For the sake of learning from unbalanced and noisy data, there have been some techniques, such as CervixNet and its variants, which have applied attention mechanisms and hierarchical architectures [13]. Reviews also spoke on the significance of trust and explainability in computer-aided diagnosis, particularly in high-stakes applications like cancer detection [14]. More contemporary approaches like single-cell segmentation have also been fruitful in increasing the accuracy of classification, particularly when used in liquid-based cytology [15].
