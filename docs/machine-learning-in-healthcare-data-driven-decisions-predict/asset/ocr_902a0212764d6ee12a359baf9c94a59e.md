Without supplemental resources, patients and professionals working in the sys-
tem generally lack requisite digital competence and digital platform for realizing ben-
efits of advanced medical solutions. Validity and reliability of AI models have been
questionable for a long time, particularly when based on low or biased data. Clinical
resistance to the adoption of new technology is also hindered by a desire to continue
being consistent with previous practice or distrust of machine-based decision-making.
Furthermore, most current infrastructure was designed with technology in mind be-
fore patients. Patient empowerment and patient outcomes have thus been harmed by
irrational solutions. Even in most instances, excessive dependence on electronic tech-
nology has added to the administrative burden of healthcare professionals, leading to
burnout and reduced direct time with patients.

A stronger, patient-centered digital healthcare system that is not just technically
sound but also adaptive, secure, and scalable is required in the light of such challenges.
The abovementioned principal shortcomings and weaknesses in prevalent digital health
solutions are the driving force behind the given methodology. Systems capable of inte-
grating heterogeneous systems, facilitating data interoperability, improving the preci-
sion of diagnosis, and offering ongoing, personalized care through real-time monitoring
are more in demand. The need for digital transformation in the healthcare sector was
also augmented by the COVID-19 pandemic.

Digital prescriptions, home monitoring devices, and remote consultations were a
necessity due to lockdowns and social distancing. In addition to reaffirming the im-
portance of digital technology in enabling continuity of care during crises, this has-
hastened adoption of telehealth. It did reveal, though, structural vulnerabilities as well,
including absence of infrastructure in some areas and incapacity of the current digital
infrastructure to handle excess traffic. The system proposed is designed to close the
gap between historic care and future-proof digital health. The system must simulate a
real healthcare setting where AI, IoMT, and cloud technologies collaborate to enhance
outcomes through the use of publicly available facts.

The objective is to render the system usable, accessible, and flexible across a vari-
ety of therapeutic settings rather than focusing on technical complexity.

The main objectives are:

+   1. To implement a simulation-based digital healthcare framework that integrates
+   EHR, AI, IoMT, and telemedicine to improve healthcare accessibility and quality.

+   2. To design an AI-driven diagnostic model using the MIMIC-III dataset for accurate
+   prediction of critical health conditions and patient risk stratification.
