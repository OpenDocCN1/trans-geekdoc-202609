To facilitate better feature differentiation, contextual awareness, and localization accuracy, researchers have introduced hybrid models that combine multi-scale representations with attention modules.

By combining data of various resolutions, multi-scale fusion gives the network a comprehensive vision of high-level semantics and fine textures. Attention mechanisms guide the network to pay attention to the most informative parts of the image and highlight clinically significant structures and suppress background noise that is unnecessary. Current studies have shown remarkable improvement in the segmentation of intricate anatomical shapes, lesions, and pathological regions in various imaging modalities like MRI, CT, ultrasound, and mammography by combining these two approaches together. Medical image segmentation techniques founded on multi-scale and attention-based methods are reviewed in this section. The ability of computerized segmentation systems to withstand variability, be accurate, and be useful in the clinical setting is increased through the novel developments in architecture design, feature fusion, and performance optimization that each paper makes.

In an effort to enhance contextual understanding by integrating local and global features, segmentation architecture was introduced in [1], which utilizes a multi-scale self-guided attention mechanism. Through the emphasis of most relevant spatial and semantic cues in input images, the method significantly enhanced segmentation accuracy. For solving issues like overlapping regions and boundary vagueness of medical images, a collaborative multi-scale feature fusion model with attention modules was proposed in [2]. To obtain more compact segmentation of intricate anatomical regions, the model collaborated features at disparate resolution levels.

Two-branch architecture with a guidance mechanism in the form of an attention mechanism that works at two scales in parallel was proposed in [3]. With the high dice similarity coefficients achieving consistent performance, the network worked very well for prostate and tumor segmentation, detecting organ boundaries and tiny lesion regions correctly. Multi-scale attentional feature integration was incorporated into a deep learning model in [4] to allow the network to learn rich contextual features while selectively paying attention to salient spatial locations. In the identification of diffuse and subtle areas of medical images, the model performed with excellent accuracy.

Attention-augmented deep network was shown for vascular segmentation of the liver [5]. It enhanced the connectivity and continuity of the vessel maps segmented by combining multi-scale information and detection of thin vessel structures with attention gates. Multi-scale attention modules were used in a mammography classification model for the capture of both local differential textures and global differential forms [6]. Benign and malignant tumors were thus distinguished more effectively with improved discrimination of features. For prostate segmentation, a transformer-based multi-scale fusion framework was proposed in [7]. The attention approach delivered precise and robust segmentation results over volumetric medical images and enhanced modeling of long-range dependencies.

Multi-scale residual feature fusion and attention gates were used to propose a dual-U-Net model [8]. For all organ and lesion segmentation tests, the architecture outperformed the standard method by successfully combining semantic information from different layers. To achieve the global contextual information at different scales, a hybrid model for the U-Net constructed of transformer layers and attention modules was developed in [9]. In case the segmentation conditions are difficult, like low contrast or irregularly shaped lesions, the technique proved to be more robust. For boosting the receptive field without compromising spatial resolution, a dilation-based attention network was utilized [10]. The network’s generalizability was enhanced through multi-scale dilation modules and attention, so that a wider range of lesion sizes could be identified.

For combining multi-scale feature extraction and attention fine-tuning, a medical image segmentation technique was suggested in [11]. This led to improving small feature segmentation and more accurate localization of anatomical boundaries. A two-path network combined parallel multi-scale attention modules to get deep semantic information and high-resolution edge data efficiently [12]. High-accuracy segmentation was achieved by this method, particularly for small or diffuse lesions. Attention-based multi-scale feature aggregation was utilized in building a brain tumor segmentation model. It offered enhanced border delineation across various tumor subregions and was competent in handling the heterogeneity in tumor appearances [13].

Asymmetric multi-scale fusion and attention mechanisms were employed in a polyp detection segmentation model [14]. Improved segmentation performance on colonoscopy images was obtained through the system’s ability to accommodate the changing size and shape of polyps. In order to pool features from various dimensions and direct attention towards clinically significant locations, a network was employed in [15]. In an attempt to point the model’s attention towards lesion boundaries and repress unnecessary background clutter, gated aggregation was utilized.

For image segmentation, a gated axial attention transformer with multi-scale attention layers was constructed in [16]. This allowed it to represent spatial hierarchies and long-range interactions well, which is particularly useful for organ segmentation with complex structures. To fuse low-level texture with high-level semantic features, an ultrasound image segmentation model utilized multi-scale feature extraction and fusion blocks [17]. The method boosted segmentation confidence in imaging conditions with low contrast or noise. Attention mechanisms sharpened multi-scale contextual features in a context-aware segmentation model [18]. The model could handle variability in anatomical presentations across individuals through dynamic contextual relevance evaluation.

Transformer-based channel attention modules and attention-enhanced multi-scale features were employed within a coronary artery segmentation network [19]. Thin vascular structures were then properly detected, and prediction consistency improved. A multi-scale feature extraction network was used for carotid artery segmentation with a depthwise attention approach [20]. The technique was appropriate for real-time implementation since it reduced computer complexity without compromising fine anatomical structures. For lesion segmentation in brain stroke, an attention network with multi-scale fusion blocks was implemented in [21]. Lesion-specific channels were highlighted in the network, boosting the contrast of stroke-affected regions and healthy tissue and producing sensitive and precise segmentation results.

The review adequately demonstrates that one of the most significant works in medical image segmentation in the current era is multi-scale feature fusion with attention. These methods successfully strike a balance between local structural information and global context information extraction to permit accurate organ and lesion delineation, despite adverse conditions like low contrast, noise, or structural variation. Whereas multi-scale architectures help improve generalization across patient populations and image modalities, the introduction of attention modules has been observed to greatly improve the model’s attention to diagnostically useful regions.

These developments represent a revolutionary step towards better and more interpretable medical image understanding, whether using transformer-based attention, dual-path architectures, or hierarchical feature fusion. All these developments highlight the significance of intelligent feature extraction and spatial attention in developing robust deep learning models for medical applications. The findings obtained through these researches provide a robust foundation for continued research and development in the suggested pursuit, which aims to introduce novel improvements into this fast-evolving domain.

## 3 System Methodology

### 3.1 Dataset Used

With the publicly available NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset, the proposed MSAG-DFE architecture was empirically tested. Comprising 112,120 frontal-view chest X-ray images acquired from 30,805 unique patients, this dataset is one of the largest and most comprehensive medical imaging datasets. Of the 14 thoracic disease classes, atelectasis, cardiomegaly, consolidation, edema, effusion, emphysema, fibrosis, hernia, infiltration, mass, nodule, pleural thickening, pneumonia, and pneumothorax, each image is labeled with one or more of these. Figure 1 shows the data distribution. The labels were produced by extracting radiography data through NLP and each image has a resolution of 1,024 × 1,024 pixels. With such uncommon diseases as hernias appearing in fewer than 200 images and conditions such as infiltration appearing in more than 42,000 samples, the dataset is very imbalanced. For avoiding data leaking, the dataset is partitioned into three sets: training (70%), validation (10%), and testing (20%). Patient-wise separation is ensured.
