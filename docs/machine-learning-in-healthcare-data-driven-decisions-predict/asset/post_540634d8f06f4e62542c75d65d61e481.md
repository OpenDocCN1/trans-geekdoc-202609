These new possibilities bring new opportunities for constructing intelligent systems that are capable of directly learning from raw multimodal patient data without being strongly dependent on hand-engineered features or strict scoring systems.

However, clinical use of AI-based models for stroke prognosis is still limited. One of the key reasons is the use of single-modality methods in the majority of current studies. Models based on imaging data alone tend to neglect important clinical context, whereas models based on tabular clinical data alone cannot capture important anatomical features visible on brain scans. Additionally, conventional ML models are not sufficiently flexible and expressive to deal with extensive, nonlinear, and interdependent relationships that are typically found in patient data. There is a strong need to transcend unimodal systems and toward hybrid systems that can integrate evidence from multiple sources of data in a coherent, interpretable, and clinically relevant manner.

This study presents a unified, multimodal, and explainable DL architecture for predicting stroke outcomes. By integrating spatial, relational, and domain-specific information, the model captures the multifaceted nature of stroke pathology and individual variability in recovery. The hybrid approach is adaptable to a variety of clinical tasks beyond stroke, including outcome prediction in other neurological or cardiovascular conditions. As healthcare increasingly transitions toward precision medicine, such integrative AI-driven tools hold the promise to revolutionize prognosis, personalize treatment planning, and ultimately improve patient outcomes. The study aims to achieve the following objectives:

- To construct a multimodal hybrid model that merges multimodal data sources, such as imaging data, structured clinical data, and handcrafted features, to forecast stroke patient outcomes in an effective manner.
- To integrate a CNN for image, a GNN for clinical information, and an ML model that is traditional (LightGBM) for handcrafted features together in one single model.
- To utilize a multitask learning approach to both perform risk stratification and functional outcome prediction (on the basis of the mRS).
- To assess the efficiency of the hybrid model in relation to standard models (logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), unimodal CNN, and an ensemble late fusion model) based on metrics such as AUC-ROC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) for risk classification and quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) for mRS prediction.

## 2 Related Works

Current developments in ML have transformed medical diagnosis, providing robust means for processing complex clinical data and detecting individuals at risk. This review discusses hybrid and ensemble ML methods for stroke prediction, combining algorithms including LR, SVM, RFs, DL architectures, and metaheuristic optimization algorithms. These models, augmented with feature selection (FS) and class balancing methods, exhibit remarkable accuracy and clinical utility enhancements and are scalable, interpretable, and robust solutions for early stroke detection and prevention.

### 2.1 Hybrid ML Model for Stroke Prediction

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) is a serious global health problem due to its high rates of mortality, morbidity, and disability, with survivors living lifelong physical, cognitive, and emotional impairments. For the provision of prompt clinical decision and individualized treatment, a successful hybrid ML model has been proposed for predicting three-month poor outcomes in AIS patients. Based on a 731-case dataset and internally and externally validated, the study compared 25 DL and ML models on 14 performance measures. On the basis of clustering and correlation analysis of measures, 12 high-performing models were shortlisted out of which XGBoost and CatBoost performed better than others both in terms of accuracy as well as reliability. Ensemble modeling and cost curve analysis threshold tuning resulted in the final model with respectable performance on both sets. Decision analysis and fairness checks were done to measure clinical utility. SHAP-based interpretability utilized albumin, fasting blood glucose, BMI, serum creatinine (Scr), white blood cell count, and age as important predictors, importantly interacting with gender. Notably, the model uses only 30 easily available clinical features, enhancing its utility. The resulting prognostic platform provides precise, interpretable, and clinically actionable predictions, justifying its implementation in electronic health records or telemedicine systems. This research demonstrates how hybrid, interpretable ML models can inform early AIS management and rehabilitation planning safely and reliably and is an exciting step toward precision medicine for stroke therapy [13].

### 2.2 EEG-Based Hybrid Stroke Diagnosis

In recent decades, longer average life spans have increased the number of stroke cases among the elderly population, which has made stroke diagnosis and treatment an important topic of research, particularly in the field of ML. Early diagnosis is crucial for stroke treatment, but current imaging technologies such as CT and MRI are not very good. While MRI is costly and difficult to obtain, CT can take up to 8 h to provide a reliable diagnosis. This has prompted the search for alternative, faster electroencephalogram (EEG)-based diagnostic techniques. This work presents a novel hybrid model for classifying strokes from EEG data, which is a time-series with frequency content, by combining DL with meta-heuristic optimization. A CNN and a bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU) are used in the model to effectively collect time and space information. The CNN-BiGRU model outperformed the harmony search (HS) algorithm, which is excellent at handling frequency data, when it was applied to feature extraction. The multivariate optimization (MVO) approach outperformed the other algorithm when hyperparameter tuning was used. In comparison to previous studies employing portable EEG, the CNN-BiGRU-HS-MVO model achieved a remarkable prediction accuracy of 99.991% when verified using EEG data acquired from Al Bashir Hospital using the MUSE-2 portable equipment. Faster, simpler patient care is now feasible anywhere, at any time, thanks to a cloud-based decision support system that was further developed to enable remote real-time stroke diagnosis and provide results via SMS [14].

### 2.3 Hybrid Classifier with Feature Selection

Brain stroke is a serious medical condition that needs to be predicted on time and in a suitable manner for appropriate treatment and prevention. A hybrid approach that involves FS methods with ML classifier algorithms has been proposed for improved prediction of brain stroke. The Stroke Prediction Dataset provided in Kaggle has been used, wherein imbalance in the data was handled through the synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE). Five classifiers, naïve Bayes, SVM, RF, adaptive boosting (Adaboost), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), were compared with three FS methods: mutual information, Pearson correlation, and feature importance (FI). The performance was compared in terms of accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and F-measure using k-fold cross-validation. The best performance was obtained by the hybrid model with FI as the FS method and RF as the classifier. It decreased the features by 36.3% and had an accuracy of 97.17%. The high-dimensional reduction along with a strong classification method allowed the model to make accurate predictions for brain stroke. The results highlight the benefit of this hybrid system to enable early detection of stroke and prevention, with healthcare specialists having a useful tool with which to make timely and targeted interventions toward better patient outcomes and the reduction of complications from stroke [15].
