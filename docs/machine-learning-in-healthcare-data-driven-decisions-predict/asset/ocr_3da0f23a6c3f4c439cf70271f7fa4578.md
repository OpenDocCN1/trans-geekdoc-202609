# 2 Related Works

Depression is a common mood illness that afflicts millions of people around the globe, essentially altering emotional states, cognitive functions, and daily routines. Successful treatment and prevention of long-term consequences of the disease need timely and correct diagnosis. The recent progress in ML and AI has allowed us to create new diagnostic approaches based on ample sources of heterogeneously diverse data such as psychological questionnaires, physiological readings, and social media feeds.

# 2.1 Hybrid Ensemble Model for Depression

Depression has recorded a remarkable increase in incidence in the last few decades, remaining undiagnosed for long periods and having serious impacts on mental as well as physical well-being. To tackle such a situation, computer-based detection systems with powerful ML algorithms are being more commonly researched. DeprMVM, a hybrid ensemble-based model presented here, is proposed for detecting depression through combining psychological and sociodemographic characteristics. The combination takes SVMs and multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) as the base (level 0) learners, whereas DeprMVM acts as the meta-learner (level 1). The results from SVM and MLP, together with other concerned variables, are utilized to train the meta-classifier to achieve better predictive accuracy. To handle class imbalance in the database, a blend of oversampling technique and cluster sampling was used. This approach not only enhanced accuracy of detection but also minimized overfitting risk. The performance of the model was strictly tested with various metrics and compared to the current methods on the same data. The results proved that the ensemble model presented in this work outperformed the existing work with a detection accuracy of 99.39% and an F1-score of 99.51%. These results accentuate the promise of hybrid ensemble learning in creating efficient, automatic systems for detecting depression [13].

# 2.2 Predicting Depression and Anxiety

After the COVID-19 pandemic, depression and anxiety levels have increased hugely as individuals adjusted to a new normal. Early detection, treatment with precision, and community care are the key factors for counteracting the influence of mental illness. Consistent with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which promote mental well-being through fair means and reduced inequalities, this study targets efficient, data-driven methods of detecting mental health disease. While depression detection is entering usage based on social media, its complete potential is limited because it lacks comprehensive annotated data. As an antidote, we propose a novel strategy of harnessing psychological questionnaire data to create population-level predictive models.
