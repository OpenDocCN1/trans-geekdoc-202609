Transparency, or the ability to recognize and provide a reason for model decisions, is equally important. Black-box models, such as deep neural networks, can deliver high accuracy but are often inscrutable to clinicians. XAI techniques, including SHAP values and LIME, help demystify predictions by highlighting key contributing factors. These tools support clinician trust and aid in regulatory compliance [26].

On the criminal front, privacy laws like the GDPR in Europe and the HIPAA in the USA establish strict guidelines for the collection, storage, and use of private health information. ML models need to be designed with data minimization, consent management, and auditability in mind. Federated learning, which trains models on distributed data without centralized sharing, is gaining traction as a privacy-preserving alternative.

Liability in ML-driven decisions is another emerging issue. Questions remain about who is responsible – developers, hospitals, or clinicians – when an AI system makes an incorrect or harmful recommendation. Legal frameworks are still evolving, and institutions are encouraged to maintain human oversight and clear documentation to mitigate risks.
