Despite having strong data and evidence, a trained doctor must look at each patient cautiously to come to a conclusion. To use it well, a healthcare professional should be able to apply evidence to suit the specific requirements of each patient. Decisions should take into account what is important to the patient. It is understood that letting patients help choose their care and treatment takes into account their goals and experiences. People need to receive these data-driven insights in a way that helps them understand and decide for themselves.

The use of evidence-based recommendations has improved with the design of CDSS. They analyze patient information with clinical guidelines and models to support quick choices by doctors. CDSS need to help rather than replace what doctors decide, and their recommendations have to be constantly updated with recent evidence [14]. The basis of evidence-based data use is constant re-evaluation and improvements. With the rapidly changing nature of healthcare, continuous data acquisition and analysis are required for monitoring, noticing issues, and optimizing ways of working. This idea is present in healthcare settings where diagnosis and treatment methods are constantly changing due to feedback loops created by learning health systems [15]. Evidence-based data use in healthcare is based on a well-defined framework focused on the importance of good data, solid analysis, and uniting evidence with medical knowledge and patient concerns.
