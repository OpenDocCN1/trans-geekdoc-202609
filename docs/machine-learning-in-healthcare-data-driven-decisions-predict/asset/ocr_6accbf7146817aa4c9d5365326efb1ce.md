# 1 Introduction

The rapid digitization of healthcare systems has resulted in an unprecedented accumulation of electronic health records (EHRs) [1], medical imaging [2], genomics [3], and wearable sensor data [4]. These data have immense potential for catalyzing novel breakthroughs in disease prediction, treatment personalization, and optimization of healthcare delivery through machine learning (ML). Nonetheless, the collaboration of these data assets across institutions is frequently frustrated by strict privacy laws worldwide [5]. These legal and ethical limitations to centralized sharing of data hinder shared model training and restrict the scalability of conventional ML methods in medicine.

Federated learning (FL) is a potential approach to address such issues. In contrast to the conventional ML where raw data are collected at a central server, FL supports decentralized training of the model where data stay at their original location and model updates are shared. The framework allows for collaboration across multiple institutions without any violation of patient confidentiality [6]. Conventional FL is not free of challenges, however. It continues to be exposed to inference attacks, has no formal privacy guarantees, and faces non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) client data. It also provides only partial support for personalized healthcare analysis.

To deal with these problems, the study presents a privacy-enhanced FL (PEFL) [7] framework designed for clinical research environments. The suggested method leverages DP [8] to add calibrated noise at the model updates such that knowledge about each data point remains limited from leakage. The study further utilizes secure multi-party computation (SMPC) [9] or homomorphic encryption (HE) for secure aggregation such that the central server cannot learn private client information from the updates. This two-layer privacy protocol significantly improves the system’s resilience against reconstruction and membership inference attacks.

Finally, actual clinical datasets from real-world environments are inherently heterogeneous in nature. The patients from various regions, demographic classes, or facilities show varying patterns of disease, responsiveness to drugs, and healthcare exposure. Standard FL is not so effective in these environments as it is based on a single global model. To mitigate this, the approach involves personalized FL (PFL) [10] via local fine-tuning and clustering-based aggregation, which enables models to be tailored according to client data distributions. This enhances predictive accuracy as well as the suitability of models in local clinical decision-making environments.
