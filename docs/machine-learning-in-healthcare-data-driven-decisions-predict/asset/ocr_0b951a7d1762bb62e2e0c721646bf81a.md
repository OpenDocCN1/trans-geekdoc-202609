# 5 Conclusion

A significant contribution to the predictive modeling of CKD, particularly in clinical
environments with small, unbalanced datasets, is the proposed HRAB algorithm.
HRAB effectively addresses significant issues such as overfitting, class imbalance, and
high feature correlation, issues commonly encountered in existing ML implementa-
tions in healthcare by integrating various regularization methods into the boosting
paradigm. HRAB promises model simplicity with no sacrifice of predictive power
through the incorporation of a wide range of lightweight base learners, for example,
regularized decision trees, penalized logistic regression, and smoothed naive Bayes
classifiers. Additionally, the architecture has the capability of penalizing intricate or
high-variance models through the incorporation of regularization penalties on every
boosting cycle, thereby inducing the use of learners that generalize better. Conse-
quently, the ensemble is more robust and can maintain high accuracy and reliability
on different patient profiles. HRAB was very good when applied to a CKD dataset with
400 cases and 24 features and achieved 96.3% accuracy, 95.1% precision, 94.7% recall,
94.9% F1-score, and 0.97 AUC-ROC. As shown by detailed confusion matrices and per-
formance plots, these results surpass more traditional methods such as RF, logistic re-
gression, and even baseline AdaBoost.
