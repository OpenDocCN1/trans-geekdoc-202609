## 1 Introduction

The recent years have seen increasing significance of real-time public health surveillance systems in disease outbreak prediction and control. Global health emergencies like the COVID-19 pandemic [1], Ebola outbreaks [2], and seasonal flu [3] outbreaks emphasize the need for more robust and precise prediction techniques to inform public health responses. Early detection and immediate response to the new epidemic outbreak can limit the spread to a large extent, reduce the effect on society, and preserve lives. ARIMA and long short-term memory (LSTM) [4] are good old-fashioned forecasting methods but tend to ignore the dynamic and spatial distribution of epidemic outbreaks. This is especially achieved when performing outbreak predictions in real-time because such models might not necessarily be developed to handle spatio-temporal data or to learn how to adapt to evolving patterns of disease over time.

EpiCastNet overcomes these constraints by proposing a new framework integrating spatiotemporal modeling, GNNs [5], and temporal convolutional networks (TCNs) [6] with adaptive online learning. EpiCastNet combines heterogeneous data sources, such as health records, social media behavior, weather, and mobility patterns, to generate real-time disease predictions [7]. The combined architecture allows the system to accommodate not only the temporal dimension of disease dynamics but also the spatial dimension including how transmission during outbreaks works between regions or populations. This integrated strategy provides early warning and early detection with considerably longer lead times than conventional forecasting models.

The architecture is also flexible-capable of adapting to new streams of data and evolving disease patterns over time. The flexibility helps ensure that EpiCastNet still provides correct predictions as disease patterns evolve. Apart from its predictive function, EpiCastNet also seeks to enhance spatial accuracy, that is, predicting the geographical regions that will be most impacted by an outbreak. Through graph neural networks (GNNs) in modeling the relationships between various regions, the system can identify the regions where interventions (e.g., medical supplies and lockdowns) will be most effective.

Whereas more conventional outbreak forecasting instruments will typically apply old data to their predictions, EpiCastNet is equipped with the state-of-the-art technology of real-time data streams. This produces more contemporary forecasts but, critically, ones which more accurately represent the latest patterns in outbreaks as well as permitting a window period of prevention measures for governments and agencies prior to the outbreak peak. With its increased ability in prediction as well as explainability, EpiCastNet is a revolution in disease forecasting that presents a scalable, accurate, and explainable solution for real-time epidemic surveillance and management.

The main objectives of the present study are as follows:

- To develop a hybrid spatiotemporal model, EpiCastNet architecture, the integration of GNNs and TCNs, to model spatiotemporal dynamics of disease epidemic structures.
- To enhance the early warning and prediction and to improve the predictability of lead times through early outbreak prediction in comparison to present methods, in order to provide public health administrators with timely opportunities for intervention.
- Compare the performance of the model on predictive accuracy, spatial accuracy (IoU), and alert quality index (AQI) against baseline models such as ARIMA, LSTM, and Prophet.

## 2 Related Works

New advances in artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and deep learning (DL) have transformed epidemic modeling into a discipline that can develop more sophisticated, data-based approaches capable of capturing the complex spatiotemporal dynamics of disease transmission. This section presents an exhaustive review of state-of-the-art epidemic forecasting models that cover from classical time-series models to hybrid and ensemble learning frameworks, GNNs, and mechanistic deep learning (DL) models. These models draw on a range of methods ranging from statistical extrapolation and multimodel ensembles to deep transfer learning and GNNs to improve prediction quality, interpretability, and resilience. Together, they form an important step toward creating solid, smart systems for early warning, intervention planning, and epidemic preparedness.

### 2.1 Epidemic Outbreaks with Time-Series Analytics

In spite of monumental advancements in medical science, the risk of local epidemics turning into mass epidemics remains a significant issue in world health. Pandemics like COVID-19, monkeypox, influenza, and HIV have been infecting populations and overburdening public health systems worldwide. For example, COVID-19 has already affected more than 500 million people in more than 100 nations and claimed more than 6 million lives, whereas HIV is an established global health emergency with a record of 85.6 million reported deaths up to the year 2023. Statistical epidemic trend forecasting is of vital significance for the development of early warning systems and minimizing national and international health threats. For this purpose, in this chapter, an intelligent model of forecasting is introduced and a detailed comparative analysis of some of the most widely used time-series models is conducted. Time-series models to be analyzed in this chapter are ARIMA, polynomial regression, SARIMA, Holt's exponential smoothing, and Facebook Prophet (Fb-Prophet). This chapter contributes to SDG-3 as this precise prediction of epidemic patterns alleviates healthcare systems. The top-performing models gave mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) values of 0.0129 for Monkeypox, 0.0035 for HIV, 0.0011 for COVID-19, and 0.024 for influenza, exhibiting exceptional predictive performance on several disease datasets [8].

### 2.2 EpiForecaster: Deep Ensemble Epidemic Prediction

EpiForecaster is a novel DL ensemble architecture that has been developed to enhance epidemic forecasting accuracy during rapidly evolving outbreaks. This study evaluates its performance using weekly case data from Brazil, the USA, Mexico, the UK, and France for the 2022 Mpox outbreak between July 10 and October 9. The approach integrates four state-of-the-art DL models CNNs, LSTM networks, bi-directional LSTM (Bi-LSTM), and hybrid CNN-LSTM models. After rigorous hyperparameter tuning, every model produces independent stacked forecasts, which are then aggregated by using multiple meta-model methods. It uses the limited-memory Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno with bounds (L-BFGS-B) optimization algorithm for dynamically distributing the weights across each model depending on its forecasting accuracy. EpiForecaster yields enhanced forecasting accuracy compared to standard ensemble methods such as gradient boosting and genetic algorithm-based ensembles, with an equal-weight ensemble as a control baseline. Metrics such as mean-squared error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) show that EpiForecaster consistently offers improved forecasting precision. Contrary to the assumption that larger ensembles are better by default, this contribution stresses the significance of the role played by intelligent weight optimization. EpiForecaster is both an efficient and scalable method for modeling nonstationary time series and can be a resourceful tool in a wide range of predictive tasks other than epidemic prediction [9].
