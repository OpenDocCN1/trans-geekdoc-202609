
## Analyzing the Impact of Social Network on Epidemiological Spread in the Healthcare Sector

Abstract: The emergence of complex network-based frameworks has significantly evidenced the development in health monitoring in the most complicated terrain of disease tracking. This shift in paradigm brings together sophisticated network theory, live data feeds, and social network analytics to develop a flexible system that is comprehensive. By exploring the complex network of connections between individuals, healthcare facilities, and locations within a geographical area, this framework utilizes graph theoretic concepts to depict relations as well as identify central individual elements (nodes/vertices) in disease spread. Predictive modeling gets enhanced by complex network concepts and statistical techniques that allows early outbreak detection and targeted intervention. The ultimate aim of this study is toward tracking of diseases through a complex network framework by analyzing the disease spread patterns in an egocentric network. This provides insightful findings for future healthcare surveillance and enables healthcare providers to contain the disease spread by exploiting social network analytics.

Keywords: disease spread detection, complex network, healthcare, statistical analysis, social network analytics

### 1 Introduction

Over the years, monitoring and managing diseases in healthcare has been greatly influenced by the development of networks [1]. Network theory targets the interconnectedness and this has opened up opportunities in using it among healthcare entities such as hospitals, clinics, and research institutions [2]. This paradigm change has enabled healthcare professionals to understand the systemic patterns, movements, and reactions in the ecosystem. The analysis of networks provides valuable insights into the spread among communities and has turned into a tool for tracking diseases. The network theory is useful in some cases of modeling the relationships between individuals, healthcare facilities, and geographic locations [3, 4]. It helps to locate hotspots of disease outbreaks, while determining resource allocation rationally for better results. Collecting real-time data within these networks ensures that the healthcare providers have access to information for proactive decision-making.

Nowadays, disease tracking implementation is technologically more advanced, as it incorporates various sources such as electronic health records (EHRs), social media data, and geographical information [5]. Such multidimensional approach allows understanding disease dynamics, transmission patterns, and the ways in which social factors affect health outcomes. Sophisticated network models can capture the links that are actually present and give healthcare experts a way to make data-driven decisions when it comes to disease monitoring, interventions, and so on [6].

The field of network theory in healthcare has grown even greater with the advent of devices and Internet of Things (IoT) [7]. These devices produce large volumes of data that can be used to create networks that change, depending on real-time health parameters. These networks allow healthcare givers to track patients, identify indications of diseases, and personalize treatment interventions for precision healthcare [8].

To address the dynamic environment of disease monitoring, we introduce an emerging network-based framework for disease monitoring [9]. This framework uses statistical techniques, real-time data streams, and social network analysis to build an interactive system that can track diseases. Findings from the framework enable understanding of how diseases are transmitted by individuals, healthcare facilities, as well as geographic locations. This proposed framework relies on the use of graph theory to represent relationships within healthcare systems, enabling identification of nodes and patterns for disease transmission. Statistical models presented in this chapter improve the modeling functions of being able to detect possible outbreaks and allowing for directed interventions [10, 11]. More so, this framework focuses on the privacy and security of data to ensure compliance with healthcare regulations as well as ensuring that sensitive patient information is protected. This complicated network-based approach is representing a change in disease tracking by providing a data-driven strategy, for healthcare surveillance. It has the potential to transform how healthcare professionals understand, monitor, and react to diseases in various populations by leveraging power of networks.

### 2 Related Works

Social networks are complex in nature and have high levels of heterogeneity in it. In social networks, some nodes have large numbers of edges (high degree), while some nodes have only a countable number of edges (low degree). Using social network, we can analyze and study complex systems like Covid-19 epidemics, airline transportation networks, railway transportation networks, computer viruses spreading network, Ebola epidemics, HIV epidemics, vehicular network, and spread of diseases under immunization [12].

Recently we faced the Coronavirus outbreak, which was an infectious disease that threatened mankind. According to the up-to-date available data till July 2022, World Health Organization (WHO) has confirmed that 562 million people were infected and 6.3 million people lost their lives due to the disease. It just spread from human to human in contact or in air (small distance); for tracking, we had to backtrack the persons' past contact history with others. All these studies are just like studying a complex network. These things follow a pattern that can be analyzed in deep to tackle the problem.

There are several other virus outbreaks that happened in the world, for example, influenza A(H1N1) pandemic from 2009 to 2010, Ebola virus disease epidemic in West Africa from 2013 to 2016, and the Zika virus epidemic from 2015 to 2016; all these diseases spread human to human through spreaders in numerous countries across different continents. The investigation of the occurrence of these rising disease epidemics has been driven by an increasing research curiosity. Concurrently, there has been a growing need for trustworthy open data from authoritative sources. The available databases are restricted in terms of the subject matter, since they only cover a few infectious illnesses or are focused on a particular demographic, nation, or area. In addition, there are additional datasets that rely on unofficial data, which may include inaccurate facts or deliberate misinformation from fraudulent reports, and are not accessible to the public, thus impeding their reuse and usage.

Leung et al. conducted a study in Cambodia in 2012 and they found that social interactions inside families tend to occur between individuals of different age groups, highlighting intergenerational relationships [13]. The research targeted places with high levels of interaction, such as homes and schools, and emphasized differences in mixing patterns between rural and urban inhabitants. These findings provide useful information for predicting the spread of infectious diseases.

In an investigation of viral contact relationships, using network analysis is a valuable method for uncovering the specific structures of individual infectious contact networks. During the investigation in Korea of the 2015 Middle East Respiratory Syndrome (MERS) epidemic, Yang and Jung [14] examined how illnesses spread among people via contact networks. They focused on identifying the unique characteristics and structural advantages of a small number of super-spreaders, who had a significant role in aiding the continuous transmission of the disease.

The WHO has compiled a comprehensive dataset on infectious disease outbreaks. This dataset includes data on 70 different infectious illnesses and 2,227 public health events. It covers data from January 1996 to March 2022 and spans across 233 countries and territories. The data was sourced from the Illness Epidemic News and the Coronavirus Dashboard. Munguía et al. examined the dataset's potential usefulness for the research community [15]. They also exemplified by analyzing the spatial distribution of disease outbreaks, which uncovers concentrated areas or “hot spots” in Asia, Africa, and America. This analysis offers policymakers valuable insights for identifying regions with a greater probability of experiencing disease outbreaks.
