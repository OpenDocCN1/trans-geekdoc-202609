## 5 Conclusion

This work presents a novel, data-driven ML approach toward oncological disease prediction and early stage diagnosis of precursors. Through the integration of heterogeneous, multimodal datasets and application of a hybrid ensemble of classical and deep ML models, the method is able to capture the complex interrelationships in cancer-related data. The results indicate that the combined strategy has higher predictive accuracy, sensitivity, and robustness compared to conventional single-modality or individual ML models. The ability to detect precursor and early stage disease biomarkers of high reliability responds to the potential of the framework to increase clinical decision-making and oncology patient outcomes. While the model developed here works well on most metrics, there are some limitations. The model's direct generalizability is somewhat constrained by the utilization of big and high-quality datasets, especially in locations or clinics with inadequate data infrastructure. Even if FS and model awareness help to some extent with model interpretability, future iterations with more inclusive transparency features tailored for clinical usage would be helpful.

## 6 Future Directions

Future studies will try to adapt the model to bigger and more diverse patient scenarios in an effort to increase its therapeutic use and applicability. Using survival analysis models like the Cox proportional hazards model and deeper survival networks forecast not only illness progression and early diagnosis but also patient prognosis and late outcomes is one of the work's useful extensions. The models will render the framework applicable for reporting clinician's risk-adjusted survival probabilities and individualized prognostic information, hence more relevant for clinical decision-making. To address data privacy and noncentralized large-sized databases, future activities will also investigate using federated learning approaches. Through this method, cooperative model training among different healthcare organizations will be enabled without the explicit sharing of data, thereby preserving patient confidentiality and use of larger, distributed datasets to enhance model performance. In addition, significant work will be devoted to implementing an open, real-time clinical decision support system on the contemplated platform. The system will strive to provide actionable information and risk estimates at the point of care in a format such that timely and informed decisions regarding treatment can be facilitated. The implementation of predictive analytics in daily clinical oncology practice, the study seeks to close the research-based innovation to everyday adoption gap, hence achieving the ultimate goal of precision oncology and improved patient care.
