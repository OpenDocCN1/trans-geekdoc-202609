# # Exploring Machine Learning Approaches for Maximizing the Likelihood of Diabetes Classification

**Abstract:** Advancements in machine learning research have enabled the development of powerful models capable of predicting diseases such as diabetes with greater accuracy than human diagnoses. This chapter explores a range of machine learning techniques to enhance predictive performance and examines the limitations of using a single model, particularly in terms of generalization and robustness. To address these challenges, the use of ensemble methods, that is, combining multiple models, has become increasingly common. Numerous studies have focused on creating effective frameworks for model integration. This chapter presents and evaluates various ensemble methods, with findings indicating that the XGBoost model achieved the highest accuracy of 81%.

**Keywords:** ensemble learning, bagging, boosting, voting, stacking

# # 1 Introduction

Diabetes occurs when blood sugar levels remain elevated over an extended period. It is one of the most common diseases worldwide, with a higher prevalence in developing countries compared to developed countries. Lifestyle changes are among the major contributing factors to the rise in diabetes cases [1]. However, diabetes can often be prevented or managed effectively if diagnosed in its early stages. Early detection, along with proper dietary recommendations and other preventive measures, can significantly reduce its impact on a patient’s health. There are three types of diabetes: Type 1, Type 2, and gestational diabetes. Type 1 diabetes is very rare, and it occurs when the human body fails to produce sufficient insulin. Type 2 diabetes is very common in the world; around 90% of diabetes patients suffer from type 2 diabetes alone. This type of diabetes occurs when the human body cannot use insulin properly.
