### 2.5 Hybrid Sampling-Optimized Stroke Risk Prediction

A new supervised ML algorithm has been proposed to predict stroke risk on a comprehensive list of causative factors. As a clinical practitioner's assistant, the model aims at enhancing diagnostic accuracy, preventing fatalities, and facilitating early intervention procedures. The model was trained and tested on an unbalanced dataset consisting of diverse features related to stroke. For improving performance, hybrid sampling strategies were applied for balancing classes and grid search was used to tune hyperparameters in five baseline models. Integrated classification method had the best performance with precision, accuracy, F1-score, and recall rates ranging from 94.5% to 95.5%. The performance of the model suggests its suitability in facilitating clinical decision-making in stroke risk prediction [17].

### 2.6 Hybrid Ensemble Machine Learning for Stroke Prediction

Stroke is a chronic condition due to poor blood supply to some brain cells, resulting in deprivation of oxygen and nutrients, followed by cell death. Precise prediction and early detection of stroke are ongoing crucial challenges in the field of healthcare. In recent times, ML approaches have proven very capable in the prediction of disease with possibility of better clinical outcomes. The study introduces a hybrid ensemble ML model that has three heterogeneous base classifiers: SVM, LR, and RF. The ensemble, which is named SVM-LR-RF, utilizes the unique capabilities of each individual model to improve stroke prediction accuracy using a healthcare dataset available in the Kaggle repository. The models were designed and experimented in the Python programming environment. The base learners LR, SVM, and RF were first separately trained and tested through confusion matrices to determine performance in classification. Next, the base model outputs (prediction probabilities) were combined through a soft voting methodology to construct the hybrid ensemble model. This enables the model to combine each learner's predictions, enhancing overall robustness and mitigating individual drawbacks. Experimental findings showed that the hybrid model of SVM-LR-RF considerably outshone the standalone classifiers, with the prediction accuracy of 95%. This success illustrates the potential of merging different ML algorithms using ensemble learning for sophisticated medical prediction tasks such as stroke. Based on these encouraging findings, it is suggested that the hybrid SVM-LR-RF model be applied as a functional and effective tool for improved stroke risk prediction, which could support clinicians with early intervention and improved patient care [18].

### 2.7 Ensemble-LR-RF and SVM-Based Stroke Risk Prediction

The deployment of a stroke prediction system based on ML can be an exciting development in the identification of individuals at high risk of having a stroke. With the use of large data sets at their command, ML models can identify subtle, nonlinear patterns, and weak predictors that are not even apparent to clinicians. Such ability has the potential to improve early detection substantially, facilitate timely intervention, and inform preventive action to lower stroke prevalence and better serve patients. Although previous research has applied individual ML models to predicting strokes, this research investigates a combination of the LR, RF, and SVM models. Each of the algorithms has its own strengths and weaknesses. RF provided the highest predictive accuracy due to its ensemble and ability to process nonlinear relations and FI. Conversely, LR and SVM provided better computational efficiency and training complexity, rendering them appropriate in situations needing fast or limited deployments. The hybrid modeling method leveraged the predictive power of the three classifiers compared on aspects such as accuracy, training time, and stability. Among the three, RF performed best for this dataset and was appropriate for application within stroke risk assessment procedures. LR and SVM are also suitable depending on application requirements. Further, ML-based stroke prediction models provide medical science with a breakthrough tool that makes it possible to identify risk patients early. The research confirms the capability of ML in medical diagnosis and highlights the need to conduct more research on developing more advanced algorithm selection, feature extraction, and model training for easier clinical applicability [19].

The reviewed works collectively emphasize the revolutionizing potential of ensemble and hybrid ML models in stroke prediction model development. Through an ensemble of multiple classifiers, that is, RF, LR, SVM, XGBoost, CatBoost, and deep networks including CNN-BiGRU with optimization techniques and feature selectors, all the aforementioned approaches have achieved high predictive accuracy, typically more than 95% accuracy. The use of oversampling techniques (e.g., SMOTE, ADASYN, and ROSE) sufficiently addressed class imbalance and contributed to the reliability of the model. Besides, the application of interpretable models (like SHAP analysis) and real-time clinical decision support system deployment (like cloud-based or SMS-based systems) demonstrated pragmatic usefulness in real-world healthcare settings. Not only do these models gain from early and accurate stroke diagnosis, but they also offer the scalability and flexibility required to embed into electronic health records and telemedicine systems. Despite the effectiveness of such initiatives, the study calls for further efforts in external validation, real-time deployment, and integration of multimodal data sources (e.g., EEG, imaging, and clinical notes). Ethical considerations, fairness evaluation, and explainability are also significant hurdles to bridge to implement accountable AI in clinical environments. Finally, hybrid ML algorithms for stroke prediction constitute a promising area of precision medicine with the capability to support clinicians via early intervention tools and optimize patient outcomes in various healthcare infrastructures.

## 3 Methodology

Stroke continues to be a major cause of morbidity and mortality globally, and early risk stratification and the prediction of functional outcome are essential in enhancing patient care and long-term rehabilitation. Standard prognostic approaches are usually inadequate in describing the intricate relationship between imaging biomarkers, clinical markers, and comorbidities that determine stroke outcomes. To solve this challenge, we introduce a new multimodal ML system that combines high-resolution neuroimaging, structured clinical information, and domain-informed handcrafted features for accurate prediction of poststroke risk and functional recovery. Taking advantage of the dense ISLES dataset, our method integrates a 3D CNN to learn spatial lesion features from MRI images, a GNN to represent clinical similarity between patients, and a LightGBM-based conventional model to retain interpretable clinical knowledge. These diverse data representations are combined into a single latent space and optimized through a multitask learning approach to predict stroke severity risk levels and 90-day functional outcomes on the mRS simultaneously. This DL model hybrid provides an optimistic direction for personalized stroke management with data-driven, explainable, and precise clinical decision support.
