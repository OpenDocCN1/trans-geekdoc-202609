Preprocessing is equally essential and includes data cleaning (e.g., removing duplicates and correcting errors), normalization (e.g., scaling and encoding), imputation of missing values, and anonymization to comply with privacy regulations such as Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Feature engineering, which includes temporal aggregation or creating composite scores (e.g., Charlson Comorbidity Index), often enhances model performance. Additionally, understanding the clinical context of the data – how and why it was recorded – is critical to avoid spurious correlations and biases. Through meticulous preparation and contextual understanding, ML engineers can ensure that the models they build are both technically robust and clinically relevant.
