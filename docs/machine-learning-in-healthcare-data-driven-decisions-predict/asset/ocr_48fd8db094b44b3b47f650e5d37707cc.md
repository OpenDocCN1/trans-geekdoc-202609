On the other hand, there are many research works [7–10, 12, 13], where the researchers collected various sensor data, such as accelerometer, gyroscope, GPS, screen status, SMS, phone call logs, and typing patterns, to predict emotions through smartphones. However, they did not consider physiological data from participants in their emotion prediction work. To detect stress, Toshnirazarov et al. [14] proposed a technique combining physiological and contextual data using smart wearable devices, ECG-based heart rates, and smartphones. This experiment was conducted in both controlled and real-life environments with 26 and 18 participants, respectively. The research in [15] illustrated emotional transitions and states through data collected from smartwatches and smartphones. The researchers employed machine learning techniques to predict the relationships between emotional transitions, states, and contextual information, using the ExtraSensory [20] dataset. Wang et al. [16] utilized the Empatica E4 to design an emotion recognition system. Similarly, in EmoWear [17], a voice messaging app was developed for a smartwatch, featuring 30 animated treasures to express emotions. Shu et al. [18] used heart rate data from a smart bracelet to create an emotion recognition system, collecting data from 25 participants shown emotional video clips. Several studies [14–17] focused on emotions using smartwatches. However, no studies were conducted with a diverse dataset involving many participants over a long period of time using commercially available smartwatches.

FFT and other frequency-domain methods have become vital tools in biomedicine due to their ability to extract meaningful patterns from noisy, nonstationary data. In recent years, researchers have significantly used the Fourier transformation to enhance signal clarity, reveal latent periodic features, and improve classification accuracy. In the domain of EEG-based emotion recognition, many studies demonstrate the utility of FFT. Wang et al. [21] proposed a Fourier adjacency transformer to separate periodic and aperiodic EEG components and enhance inter-channel correlation by using adjacency-based attention mechanisms. This model yields a 6.5% increase in emotion recognition accuracy on the SEED and DEAP datasets. Similarly, Cruz-Vazquez et al. [22] integrated the Fourier neural network with a quantum rotation layer to enhance emotion classification. This research highlighted how frequency-domain preprocessing can improve the performance of convolutional models, particularly in detecting stable emotional states such as sadness. Further, Yang et al. [23] advanced this line of work by developing a DFrt-ES model using the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT), which aids FFT by allowing analysis at varying angles between the time and frequency domains. By extracting features like power spectral density (PSD) and differential entropy (DE), they achieved a classification accuracy of 95.63% on the DEAP dataset. Similarly, several studies demonstrated the effectiveness of FFT in extracting spectral features for emotion recognition using EEG signals. In [24], spectral centroid and entropy features were extracted from EEG using FFT and filtered with Butterworth and surface Laplacian filters. They achieved 91.33% accuracy in classification using k-nearest neighbor.
