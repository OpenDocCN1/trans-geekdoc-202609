Privacy Loss (ε): Traditional FL does not provide any explicit privacy guarantee, with its privacy loss parameter essentially at ∞. This implies that while raw data is not exposed, model update data possibly may expose information regarding individual patient data. The PEFL approach uses DP, adding mathematically bounded noise to model updates before sharing. This limits loss of privacy to an empirically measurable fraction (e.g., ε = 2.1), with confidentiality that is provable and guaranteed so that adding or removing any single data point will not make a noticeable impact on the global model.

Secure Aggregation: Legacy FL is vulnerable from the aggregation side, leaving model updates susceptible to transmission and aggregation operations. The lack of security boosts the risk of unauthorized access or unwanted information revelation. In contrast, the novel approach applies SMPC or HE to execute encrypted model parameter aggregation. As a result, not even the central server can view individual updates, which greatly enhances security and privacy regulations such as HIPAA or GDPR compliance.

Resistance to Inference Attacks: Without other defense mechanisms, traditional FL is susceptible to inference attacks like model inversion or membership inference where attackers can use model updates to extract or learn sensitive patient information. The Proposed PEFL solution is robust against such attacks by both differential privacy and secure aggregation. These techniques restrict the amount of information that can be learned from an individual update, hence making attacks challenging.

Personalization Facilitation: Classic FL trains one global model on all clients regardless of local data idiosyncrasies, which typically results in suboptimal performance particularly in healthcare where patient cohorts and disease incidence are location-specific. The answer here employs PFL methods like fine-tuning and clustering-based aggregation to enable each client to personalize the global model to its own data. Local personalization significantly improves relevance, accuracy, and clinical utility.

Processes Non-IID Data: Clinical data are never IID, yet conventional FL is not robust to such heterogeneity and hence suffers from biased models or generalization failure. The method has avoided this by partitioning clients with similar data distributions into groups and using localized training protocols. In regard to data imbalance and diversity, the model is more robust and generalizable, achieving better performance on diverse institutions with diverse patient populations.

## 5 Discussion

Our PEFL framework results provide a significant contribution in healthcare machine learning with privacy preservation. In comparison to conventional FL, the PEFL method shows higher accuracy, precision, and recall, which are largely due to the addition of differential privacy, secure aggregation, and personalization mechanisms. Application of differential privacy (\(\epsilon\) = 2.1) effectively reduces information leakage, addressing especially significant issues in clinical AI, especially in multi-institutional data sharing. Moreover, SMPC ensures that no individual participant, including the coordinating server, possesses full knowledge of individual updates, thereby increasing resistance to inference attacks.

Yet another important improvement is in model generalization. Standard FL suffers from the problem of non-IID data, where client datasets are rather dissimilar in distribution. This tends to result in a one-size-fits-all model's failure. Through the use of PFL techniques and clustering-based aggregation, our approach enables clients to fine-tune global models locally and exchange updates with peer groups of similar distributions. This enhances model generalizability and applicability to multi-population contexts a major concern in clinical release. Despite such developments, problems persist. Privacy versus model utility scales, specifically in noise calibration for DP, need to be further optimized. Secure aggregation protocol communication overhead can also cause latency, which can restrict real-time use areas such as medicine. Nevertheless, overall development reiterates PEFL efficacy in critical application areas such as medicine. The method opens doors to more participative, privacy-aware AI systems in regulated fields.
