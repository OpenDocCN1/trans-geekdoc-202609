These new possibilities bring new opportunities for constructing intelligent systems that are capable of directly learning from raw multimodal patient data without being strongly dependent on hand-engineered features or strict scoring systems.

However, clinical use of AI-based models for stroke prognosis is still limited. One of the key reasons is the use of single-modality methods in the majority of current studies. Models based on imaging data alone tend to neglect important clinical context, whereas models based on tabular clinical data alone cannot capture important anatomical features visible on brain scans. Additionally, conventional ML models are not sufficiently flexible and expressive to deal with extensive, nonlinear, and interdependent relationships that are typically found in patient data. There is a strong need to transcend unimodal systems and toward hybrid systems that can integrate evidence from multiple sources of data in a coherent, interpretable, and clinically relevant manner.

This study presents a unified, multimodal, and explainable DL architecture for predicting stroke outcomes. By integrating spatial, relational, and domain-specific information, the model captures the multifaceted nature of stroke pathology and individual variability in recovery. The hybrid approach is adaptable to a variety of clinical tasks beyond stroke, including outcome prediction in other neurological or cardiovascular conditions. As healthcare increasingly transitions toward precision medicine, such integrative AI-driven tools hold the promise to revolutionize prognosis, personalize treatment planning, and ultimately improve patient outcomes. The study aims to achieve the following objectives:

+   - To construct a multimodal hybrid model that merges multimodal data sources, such as imaging data, structured clinical data, and handcrafted features, to forecast stroke patient outcomes in an effective manner.

+   - To integrate a CNN for image, a GNN for clinical information, and an ML model that is traditional (LightGBM) for handcrafted features together in one single model.

+   - To utilize a multitask learning approach to both perform risk stratification and functional outcome prediction (on the basis of the mRS).

+   - To assess the efficiency of the hybrid model in relation to standard models (logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), unimodal CNN, and an ensemble late fusion model) based on metrics such as AUC-ROC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) for risk classification and quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) for mRS prediction.
