## 2.7 Anxiety and Depression Forecasting

Although various algorithms and approaches have been developed for prediction of depression, none of the automated systems studies have been recommended for early assessment of mood changes. This study focused on improving early diagnostic accuracy in the event of persistent depression disorder (PDD) development with an improved ML approach via the stack-SVM-ensemble method. To enable faster initial screening of students’ dysthymia, quantitative behavioral data analysis was used with the help of ML. The study collected behavioral information from 137 university students and used the data to create models and perform experiments. Results showed that PDD is most frequently seen in middle-class students who have completed non-technical courses. Interestingly, the frequency of PDD among rural students was higher from the high and low income groups. The proposed stacked SVM model worked with better performance and achieved an accuracy of 89.4%. Early detection of PDD for students was important for mental health professionals, and the stacked SVM approach was effective in this regard [19].

## 2.8 BERT-RF Hybrid Model for Depression Detection from Tweets

Depression is a severe mental illness impacting feelings, thoughts, and everyday functioning. Aside from personal suffering, depression impacts society due to decreased productivity and higher healthcare expenditures. Social media websites tend to mirror users' emotional lives in the form of posts, providing useful information for early detection of depression. This study applies ML to predict depression from social media posts based on a benchmark set of 20,000 tweets labeled as depressed or not with an RF classifier. A novel BERT-RF feature engineering approach has been introduced that uses contextual representations from the BERT model and probabilistic values obtained through an RF classifier. This informative feature improves depression detection. For classification, five models such as RF, MLP, kNN, LR, and LSTM networks were compared. Experiments show that LR using BERT-RF features works better with 99% accuracy. The approach offers an efficient means of early detection of depression on social media, encouraging computational linguistics and mental health analysis. It enables timely intervention and better mental health outcomes through the utilization of rich information contained in online user-generated data [20].

## 2.9 High-Accuracy Depression Prediction from Twitter Data

Depression is a prevalent and disabling mental illness that still causes challenges to the global healthcare systems. Successful therapy relies on timely detection and treatment, but the challenges of detecting depression often lead to under diagnosis and under treatment. A comprehensive review of employing ML models in diagnosing depression accurately is conducted in this research. The goal of this study is to improve early diagnosis, risk assessment, and patient care in the field of depression detection utilizing predictive models. For this purpose, the Depression Prediction dataset available on Kaggle was utilized, and 10 different ML models were employed. The results showed that RF outperformed the rest of the models by achieving 98.22% accuracy while ensemble method obtained 0.98 F1-score [21].

ML has also been set forth as a strong tool to detect depression and anxiety, with real-time accuracy and scalable diagnostic tools. Hybrid and ensemble models such as DeprMVN, DA-TabSVM, and BERT-RF recent research show can obtain more than 95% accuracy using heterogeneous data like questionnaires, EEG measures, and social media data. Deep learning techniques that use CNN, LSTM, and the attention mechanism increase efficiency further. Such models are better than conventional approaches, yielding robust, early identification in multi-population samples. As mental health disorders increase globally, AI-based systems offer cost-effective, efficient mental health screening to enable early intervention, avoid under diagnosis, and enable global objectives for equitable delivery of care.

## 3 Methodology

The mental health data were extracted from various open-source and publicly available data repositories to provide data diversity and comprehensiveness. Organized patient and clinical data, like demographic data, mental health observations, and level of diagnosis, were collected from existing databases such as the UCI ML Repository dataset for Mental Health in Tech Survey and the Kaggle Mental Health in the Workplace dataset [22]. In addition, self-report survey information that measures depression and anxiety indicators was obtained from the public dataset, which offers anonymized mental health and behavioral risk factors of a general population sample. To complement this study, a clinical-based data, unstructured social media textual data on depression and anxiety were gathered from publicly accessible tweets on Twitter (via Twitter's Academic API) and Reddit communities of r/depression and r/anxiety, where posts were manually labeled or automatically annotated by keyword-based and sentiment analysis methods, as elaborated in prior research studies. Ethical procedures were strictly adhered to, where all the information was gathered either from open-access sources or data sets after necessary ethical approvals and anonymization steps had been completed. ### 3.1 Data Preprocessing

The mental health data were preprocessed using a series of preprocessing operations to clean data for processing and consumption by models. The missing values in the numeric and categorical structured columns were imputed using kNN imputation [23], wherein missing values are estimated by similarity of nearest points. In case of the unstructured text information pulled from social media posts and clinical notes, general natural language processing [24] functions like lemmatization and tokenization were utilized to transform text content to normal form and truncate it into root form. Next, sentence embeddings were obtained using the Universal Sentence Encoder (USE), converting text inputs into high-dimensional numerical vectors with semantic meaning and interpretable by ML algorithms. Numerical features in the dataset were normalized by MinMaxScaler, scaling values onto a typical range of [0,1] so that there was uniformity among features and the model was not biased by varying scales. To handle the common class imbalance encountered in mental health data, in particular where cases of depression and anxiety were underrepresented than those not affected, the adaptive synthetic (ADASYN) sampling technique was used [25]. ADASYN generates minority class synthetic samples from the data distribution so that the model becomes more sensitive and class imbalance is minimized in the training data.

### 3.2 Feature Selection

The predictive performance is enhanced and computational complexity is reduced by a rigorous FS conducted prior to model training. A computationally effective wrapper-based FS method using the Boruta algorithm [26] was used to choose and retain only the most predictive predictors from both structured clinical information as well as unstructured text-derived embeddings. Boruta works by repeatedly comparing base feature importance against randomly generated shadow features with a base estimator, in this example a simple neural network classifier, to establish the importance of every feature at each iteration. Features that repeatedly score higher than their shadow counterparts are considered highly important and are stored for model construction, while poorer or redundant features are discarded.

The decision to use Boruta with a light neural network was motivated by the algorithm's ability to capture faint, nonlinear relationships in high-dimensional and heterogeneous data, as in datasets containing both clinical measurements and dense vector embeddings of text embeddings. Pairing ensured not only that FS was statistically valid but also contextually aligned with the downstream modeling architecture. By retaining only strongly relevant features, this step effectively reduced dimensionality, avoided overfitting, and improved model interpretability by focusing on predictors with clear and measurable contributions to mental health classification. This filtering process also optimized computational efficiency, particularly important when working with computationally costly ensemble models and high-dimensional text data represented by the USE. Mathematically, the original dataset be represented as

$$D = \{(x_i, y_i)|i=1, 2, \dots, n\}$$ (1)

where \(x_i \in R^d\) is the feature label vector for the ith instance and \(y_i \in \{0, 1\}\) is the corresponding binary class label indicating the presence or absence of depression/anxiety. The Boruta algorithm enriches the feature set by incorporating shuffled copies of the original features, labeled as shadow features:

$$X_{shadow} = shuffle(X)$$ (2)

A neural network classifier \(f(.)\) is then trained on the combined dataset:

$$X_{combined} = [X, X_{shadow}]$$

Feature importance for both original and shadow features is computed using a relevance score derived from the neural network's learned weights or feature contribution to the output. The importance score for feature j can be expressed as

$$I_j = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \left| \frac{\partial f(x_i)}{\partial x_{ij}} \right|$$ (3)

Boruta then compares each original feature's importance score \(I_j\) with the maximum importance among shadow features \(I_{shadow.max}\). The decision rule is

- if \(I_j > I_{shadow.max}\), the feature is considered important.
- if \(I_j < I_{shadow.max}\), the feature is marked as important.
- if the decision is ambiguous the feature remains tentative and is evaluated in subsequent iterations.
