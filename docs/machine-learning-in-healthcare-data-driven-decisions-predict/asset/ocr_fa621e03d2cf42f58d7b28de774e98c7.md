# # 6 Medical Imaging and Computer Vision

# ## 6.1 Deep Learning in Radiology and Pathology

Medical imaging plays a crucial role in disease diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment planning. Radiology (e.g., X-rays, CT scans, and MRI) and pathology (e.g., histopathological slides) generate vast amounts of image data daily, which require expert interpretation. Figure 6, represents due to increasing workload and shortage of specialists, ML – particularly deep learning – has become an essential tool to assist clinicians in interpreting these complex visuals efficiently and accurately.

CNNs have become the backbone of scientific image analysis due to their ability to learn hierarchical features directly from pixel data. CNN-based models have demonstrated performance on par with, and sometimes surpassing, human experts in tasks such as detecting pneumonia on chest X-rays, identifying brain tumors in MRI scans, and grading diabetic retinopathy (DR) from retinal images. Transfer learning, where pre-trained models like ResNet, VGG, or DenseNet are fine-tuned on clinical datasets, has significantly reduced the amount of labeled data required, enabling faster deployment in clinical settings [19].

In pathology, whole-slide imaging has enabled the digitization of biopsy samples, opening new avenues for ML applications. Deep learning models can detect cancerous cells, quantify tumor proliferation, and even predict molecular subtypes directly from histological slides. This capability facilitates early diagnosis, reduces inter-observer variability, and improves treatment decisions.

Explainability is an important aspect of deep learning in clinical imaging. Techniques like Grad-CAM, saliency maps, and Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) help highlight regions of interest in a photograph that influenced the model's prediction, increasing trust and transparency. Despite the impressive accuracy metrics, models should be tested across diverse populations and imaging devices to ensure generalizability. Integration of deep learning into radiology and pathology workflows enhances productivity, reduces diagnostic errors, and provides decision support in time-sensitive scenarios – bringing AI-assisted diagnostics toward to routine clinical practice [20].
