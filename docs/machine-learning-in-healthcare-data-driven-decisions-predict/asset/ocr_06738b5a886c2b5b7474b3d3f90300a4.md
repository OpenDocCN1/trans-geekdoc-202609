As of now, there is no cure for diabetes, but healthcare professionals recommend maintaining a good diet and performing proper exercise; a patient can keep diabetes in control. Diabetes can be detected by a few symptoms, such as frequent urination, increased thirst, and hunger. Diabetes should be detected by as early as possible to avoid complex health issues and prevent medical costs. In extreme cases, insulin can be injected into the human body to lower the sugar level [3].

In traditional times, only healthcare experts were involved in handling patients with diabetes. Later, some computer applications were developed to help these experts manage patients more easily. In recent times, the use of machine learning models has begun to replace older computer applications. These models can detect the presence of diabetes without the need for a healthcare expert and are significantly more accurate. Machine learning-based classification models require training data that allows the model to understand and remember patterns between the variables. When new data is provided, the model uses its memory to classify the data into one of the categories (diabetic positive or negative) [4]. Machine learning models can also replace healthcare experts and can give accurate results much faster. This also eliminates lots of human errors and can give recommendations without any bias. As the need for good medical experts around the world is a challenging problem, machine learning models can fill the gap [5].

To get better classification accuracy, multiple models are applied, and ensemble learning is done. In ensemble learning, multiple models are trained, and the best combination is found. There are two methods by which ensemble learning is done: one method is to use different models, and the other is to use multiple models. Some of the common methods [8] in the ensemble method are bagging, boosting, stacking, and voting.
