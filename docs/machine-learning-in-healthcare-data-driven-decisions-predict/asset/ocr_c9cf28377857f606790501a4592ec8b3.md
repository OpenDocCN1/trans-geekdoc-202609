All the features explored in the said study were in time domain. In this chapter, we would like to explore if the addition of other features could improve the performance. We primarily focus on frequency domain analysis and utilize fast Fourier transformation (FFT) to derive additional features to verify if it improves the machine learning classification performance. We propose two feature selection approaches: (1) selective feature-based approach and (2) holistic feature-based approach to select the best set of features from a mixed pool of features (existing time domain features from the LifeSnaps and derived frequency domain features), which can enhance emotion classification performance with limited feature set. Using these two approaches, we observed that, among nonneural machine learning methods, SVM improves its accuracy by 7.24% and the highest accuracy achieved by XGBoost, which is 81.01%. However, neural network models surpass the nonneural ML models significantly in both cases.

# 2 Literature Survey

Detecting human emotions through various physiological and contextual data has been one of the most fascinating fields in which several researchers have engaged over the last few decades. In recent years, extensive research has focused on detecting human emotions using sensor-based customized devices [4-6], smartphones [7-13], and smartwatches [14-19].

Yu et al. [4] proposed a wearable system to recognize the emotions and motions of individuals while they are wearing it. The wearable device comprises three parts: (a) a hardware system, (b) a software system, and (c) a 3D display platform. The authors mapped the physical, emotional, and environmental contexts of the user in virtual space, utilizing twin technology. They conducted their experimental study using 10 volunteers with an average age of 22.3 years. Similarly, some research works detected emotion from keystrokes. Qi et al. [5] used touch panels to identify emotions. In their experiment, they employed 24 volunteers (12 males and 12 females) for evaluation. Kwon et al. [6] developed a glass-type wearable device that detects emotions using local facial images, EDA (electrodermal activity), and PPG (photoplethysmography). They classified four emotions (fear, happiness, depression, and calm) with an accuracy of 76.09%. The researchers in [10] detected emotions using a smartphone.
