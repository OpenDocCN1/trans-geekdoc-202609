## 2.4 Epidemic-Guided Deep Learning for Tuberculosis Forecasting

Tuberculosis (TB) remains a major global public health issue because it has complex spatiotemporal patterns of transmission influenced by human movement and behavioral changes. To tackle this problem, we propose an epidemic-guided DL (EGDL) framework that harmoniously combines mechanistic epidemiological modeling with sophisticated DL methods. At the center of our strategy is a networked susceptible-infectious-recovered model supplemented with a saturated incidence function and graph Laplacian diffusion to reflect both long-term diseases spread and local mobility patterns. Model parameters are estimated strictly with Bayesian methods through Markov chain Monte Carlo, and theoretical analysis guarantees the global stability of both disease-free and endemic equilibria. Building on these observations, we introduce two hybrid forecasting structures EGDL-parallel and EGDL-series that integrate mechanistic predictions into deep neural models, alleviating overfitting and noise suppression in surveillance data. When applied to TB incidence data across 47 Japanese prefectures, our approach produces high accuracy in forecasting across differing time horizons. Importantly, the addition of conformal prediction allows for quantification of uncertainty, thus increasing the robustness and usefulness of the model as an aid to inform data-driven public health measures [11].

## 2.5 AI Applications for Epidemic Forecasting

Infectious diseases present with varied, sometimes volatile risks to individual and public health. AI and associated technologies, already augmenting decision-making in disciplines, such as economics, medicine, and social sciences, have tremendous potential to change the face of infectious disease epidemiology. This chapter delves into the use of AI systems combining ML, computational statistics, information retrieval, and data science to infectious disease modeling. The recent advances in AI have the ability to speed up progress in meeting urgent epidemiological challenges, with emphasis placed on AI methods appropriate for regular infectious disease surveillance data. We then cover the wider social context for the application of AI in epidemiology, with major considerations including explainability, safety, accountability, and ethical considerations. Lastly, we discuss present limitations in using AI for this field and provide suggestions for capitalizing on present and future advancements in AI to achieve maximum impact in infectious disease epidemiology [12].

## 2.6 Hybrid Ensemble Model for COVID-19 Epidemic Prediction

Precise epidemic trend prediction is crucial for successful intervention and control of the COVID-19 pandemic. Nevertheless, it is hard to forecast the path of COVID-19 epidemics because the data are highly volatile. Most studies have used a single prediction approach without considering the advantages of consolidating various predictive methods, causing suboptimal results. To achieve this, we introduce a new ensemble architecture integrating three ML models, namely kernel ridge regression (KRidge), deep random vector functional link (drVFL), and ridge regression, into a linear fusion framework (L-KRidge-drVFL-Ridge). Optimization of the framework is performed by adopting an efficient hybrid algorithm based on adaptive differential evolution and particle swarm optimization (A-DEPSO). Moreover, input features are decomposed through the time-varying filter (TVF) empirical mode decomposition method to improve feature representation. We do feature selection using the light gradient boosting machine to select the most significant variables. We apply our method to daily COVID-19 case data from Italy and Poland, predicting at two future time points: 10 and 14 days ahead ($t + 10$ and $t + 14$). The model presented delivers high correlation coefficients for both nations Italy ($t + 10 = 0.965, t + 14 = 0.961$) and Poland ($t + 10 = 0.952, t + 14 = 0.940$) outperforming the capabilities of alternative models. These findings affirm that our ensemble system provides strong and reliable predictions even in complicated epidemic contexts, showing its potential as a strong instrument for epidemic forecasting and decision-making [13].

## 2.7 COVID-19 Forecasting with ML

ML-based forecasting framework has been proposed to improve COVID-19 number of case prediction and compare the performances of various ML models. Some of the most popular models MLP, LR, KNN, and SVM are employed in this work with an ensemble-based approach optimized for delivering the highest effectiveness in predictions. Applying a sequential model deployment process, data gathering, and framework construction, the study validates the performance of all models according to standard measures like MAE, MSE, and RMSE. Graphical plots illustrate trends in COVID-19 case reports and respective forecasts by every algorithm, that is, the proposed model. Results indicate that the method obtained using linear regression performs better than individual models such as MLP, KNN, and SVM. Ensemble modeling reduces errors in predictions by taking the average of the forecasts offered by different algorithms and improves overall accuracy. Explainable and accurate modeling techniques are vital in COVID-19 risk modeling and pandemic readiness, as research indicates. Besides, the chapter also covers potential applications of accurate projections in the direction of timely interventions in response campaigns, resource allocation, and policy development. The results reflect the significant role of reliable and reactive forecast systems in public health response campaigns. The chapter also covers potential directions of future studies such as application of these novel ML techniques and development of real-time adaptive models. Inter-coordination of policymakers, clinicians, and researchers is necessary to establish a bridge between research evidence and highly effective public health interventions. Overall, the study provides increased epidemiological prediction as well as increased evidence for COVID-19 and planning for future epidemics [14].

## 2.8 Epidemic Spreading Processes Using Deep Transfer Learning

Traditional data-driven approaches to epidemic propagation modeling and forecasting typically depend on the independent and identically distributed (IID) data assumption. However, epidemic spreading over networks is extremely heterogeneous with regard to the various phases, regions, and virus types, suggesting that the epidemic time series are subject to temporal and spatial heterogeneities and therefore probably do not adhere to the IID assumption. A novel deep transfer learning model by fusing CNNs and Bi-LSTM networks has been proposed to effectively model and forecast epidemics based on heterogeneous data. The method employs a CNN layer for extracting local spatial features, a Bi-LSTM layer for capturing temporal relationships, and a fully connected layer for prediction. In order to enhance the generalization ability of the model, transfer learning techniques are applied. In addition, hyperparameter optimization is performed with particle swarm optimization while training for further improvement in prediction accuracy. The paradigm is applied to study the spatio-temporal dynamics of COVID-19 and explore the pathologic differences between SARS, influenza A (H1N1), and COVID-19 epidemics. The experimental findings in detail demonstrate that the approach is effective to keep up with spatiotemporal variations in epidemic dissemination and distinguish significant epidemiological characteristics among different viruses. Moreover, the approach tremendously reduces the prediction error in epidemic modeling with more accurate predictions of disease dissemination [15].
