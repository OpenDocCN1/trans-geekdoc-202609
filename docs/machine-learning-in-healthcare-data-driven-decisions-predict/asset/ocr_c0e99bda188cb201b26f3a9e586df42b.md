The previous studies have demonstrated impressive results using FFT and machine learning models for EEG-based emotion recognition or biomedical signal analysis. Previous studies primarily depend on controlled, lab-oriented datasets such as DEAP and SEED. These datasets are collected under structured environments with minimal noise and with standard protocols, within limits, for their real-world applicability. In contrast, our approach stands out by experimenting on an in-the-wild dataset collected from real-world environments using a commercial smartwatch, Fitbit Sense, which introduces natural variability, noise, and diverse user behaviors. Applying FFT in such unstructured datasets not only tests the robustness of frequency domain feature extraction but also the generalizability of classical machine learning models in wild conditions. This makes our work more representative of practical applications and demonstrates a crucial step toward deploying emotion recognition systems in real-world scenarios, something most prior studies have not addressed. Thus, our strategy offers a novel contribution by bridging the gap between a controlled research environment and in-the-wild data contexts.
