Statistical ML techniques such as decision trees, logistic regression, and naive Bayes classifiers came to be popular in medical informatics in the 1990s. These models were utilized for disease prognosis, patient stratification, and risk prediction as they were reasonably comprehensible. However, they could not handle high-dimensional data or nonlinear relationships. The subsequent significant turning point came with the mass use of electronic health records (EHRs) and digitization of medical imaging, leading to huge volumes of structured as well as unstructured data. More advanced ML models, including support vector machines (SVMs), k-nearest neighbors (k-NN), and random forests (RFs), were made possible by this data explosion. These models could handle nuances of correlation between variables and make more advanced predictions.

Image processing and sequence modeling in the time domain were transformed by subsequent research on deep learning in the form of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), respectively. Diagnostic imaging applications like tumor identification, diabetic retinopathy grading, and radiological abnormality categorization have been greatly enhanced by such models. Also, natural language processing (NLP) models like transformer-based models BERT and GPT have helped facilitate the retrieval of clinical data from unstructured medical publications and notes.

The requirement for optimization methods to enhance these sophisticated ML architectures' learning, generalization, and efficiency grew as the healthcare industry started implementing them. Optimization in ML is the process of adjusting model parameters or configurations to enhance prediction performance or minimize loss functions. Training neural networks has been based heavily on traditional optimization algorithms like stochastic gradient descent and gradient descent.

Techniques for modifying learning rates during training and speeding up convergence were introduced by variants like momentum-based techniques, AdaGrad, RMSProp, and Adam. Despite their widespread usage, these techniques do not perform very well when facing problems like saddle points, vanishing gradients, and hyperparameter sensitivity. Metaheuristic optimization techniques were developed by researchers to overcome these drawbacks. Supported by natural selection, genetic algorithms (GAs) have been used to optimize hyperparameters, network structure, and feature subset selection.

Ant colony optimization, particle swarm optimization (PSO), and simulated annealing have been used to optimize ML models for healthcare data. Such population-based techniques are especially efficient in searching an expanded area and avoiding local minima. Their only drawback is that they require many iterations to converge and are computationally costly, particularly in high-dimensional optimization. Also, the effectiveness of such algorithms in automation can be offset by the fact that such algorithms often require a great deal of manual tweaking of their parameters. Bayesian optimization is another group of optimization algorithms that have become popular.
