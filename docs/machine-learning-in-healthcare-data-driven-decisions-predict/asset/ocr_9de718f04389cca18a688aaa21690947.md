+   5. Blood Oxygen Levels (bo): Stress-induced breathing abnormalities, apnea, or hyperventilation, result in variations in blood oxygen saturation levels. Chronic low oxygen levels can impair brain function and raise concerns of susceptibility to cognitive and physical stress reactions.

+   6. Eye Movement Rate (rem): REM activity is deeply associated with brain function as well as processing speed of emotions. Abnormalities in the rate of eye movement, i.e., too frequent REMs or irregular REMs, indicate unresolved emotional conflicts, nightmares, or sleep stress disorders.

+   7. Sleeping Rate in Hours (srh): This is one of the most immediate and critical indicators. Chronic sleep loss or inconsistent sleep timing hampers the body's capacity to recuperate, which makes it more prone to stress and lower psychological resilience.

+   8. Heart Rate (hr): Heart rate is arguably the most widely accepted stress biomarker. A rise in heart rate, particularly unlinked with physical exertion, generally reflects increased alertness or tension. Coupled with HRV (when accessible), it provides deep insights into the balance of autonomic function.

+   9. Stress Level (sl) – Target Variable: This is the target class for supervised learning models. It is classified on the basis of either expert labeling or self-assessment validated by physiological responses as follows:
+   0 – No stress
+   1 – Mild stress
+   2 – Moderate stress
+   3 – High stress
+   4 – Severe stress

# 4.6 Application in Machine Learning Models

This data set offers a labeled and structured collection of points of data well-suited for training classification or regression models. Every entry within the data set is a snapshot of the subject's physiological status over some interval of time. Features may be normalized, engineered (for example, by including interaction terms or statistical aggregates), and subsequently passed into any of the following algorithms:

+   – Decision trees
+   – Random forests
+   – Support vector machines (SVM)
+   – k-nearest neighbors (KNN)
+   – Deep neural networks (DNN)

Classification metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrix can be used to measure models against one another in order to compare their performance in separating the five levels of stress.
