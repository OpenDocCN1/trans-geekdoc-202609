# 9.1 Advancements in Real-Time and Predictive Analytics

Healthcare data will be used more effectively if real-time and predictive analytics can be employed. Most healthcare analytics have concentrated on using past data to draw conclusions. Yet, using newly developed sensors, devices worn by patients, and automatic monitoring tools allows for the quick and continuous collection of vital information from patients in all kinds of hospitals. With all the data, healthcare professionals can easily monitor patients and quickly take actions if anything potentially dangerous is detected. Tools for real-time analytics take streaming information from many sources and produce suggestions that assure patient safety and increase the hospital's effectiveness. In case, experts quickly find worsening vital signs, it could lead to quick action that lowers the rate of adults being sent to the hospital and experiencing further difficulties. Furthermore, using analytics in real time allows hospitals to manage available resources by guessing both the number of patients and how many beds will be taken during different parts of the day. Using this technology, real-time data processing can be boosted by applying certain techniques to forecast what will happen in a patient's future, how an illness might develop, and what resources are necessary. By using these models, it becomes possible to find people who might face issues after leaving the hospital, when taking drugs or with specific diseases. With predictive analytics in EHRs, doctors get support in making decisions about treating and managing patient diseases [34]. They may enable healthcare workers to focus on stopping health issues early by being more active in caring for patients. Managing health issues and handling any public health emergency will depend greatly on the flexibility of real-time and predictive analytics [33].

# 9.2 The Role of Artificial Intelligence and Deep Learning

AI and deep learning are now leading transformations in the medical field due to their use of data. Using different computer methods, AI allows machines to perform acts that require thinking, recognizing information, and interpreting speech. A part of AI, deep learning trains with neural networks to identify complex details found in large amounts of data [50].

There is rapid growth in using AI and deep learning for diagnostics, planning treatments, viewing images, working with DNA data, and supervising patients' health. Thanks to these technologies, it is now easier to find useful details from data types such as images, pathology slides, and medical notes. Both in finding unusual symptoms, and identifying many kinds of diseases and predicting outcomes, deep learning algorithms have performed better than other methods. AI is now helping to design personal treatment plans using information from genetics, lifestyle habits, and a patient's clinical history.
