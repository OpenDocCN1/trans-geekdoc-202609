The creation of solutions is made more difficult by the fact that healthcare optimization is multi-objective. Clinical judgments frequently have to balance operational costs, patient safety, therapeutic effectiveness, and diagnostic accuracy all at once. These trade-offs are difficult for traditional single-objective optimization techniques to capture, thus methods that can identify Pareto-optimal fronts – solutions, where achieving an improvement in one objective cannot be accomplished without compromising another – are required. To achieve this, multi-objective evolutionary algorithms such as MOEA/D and NSGA-II have been used, providing a variety of solution sets that reflect various trade-offs. However, these evolutionary approaches are usually offline and do not support real-time feedback, which is essential for adjusting to context-dependent and patient-specific aspects.

In order to enable dynamic, adaptive, and patient-centric decision-making in the healthcare industry, integrated optimization frameworks that combine the advantages of multi-objective evolutionary algorithms and reinforcement learning are desperately needed. This gap serves as the impetus for the suggested system. The system seeks to provide customized diagnostic and treatment pathways that adapt to patient responses and clinical contexts by utilizing the synergy between multi-objective optimization's ability to balance conflicting clinical goals and reinforcement learning's sequential decision-making capability.

PPO, the reinforcement learning agent used in the proposed reinforced multi-objective optimization framework for healthcare decision intelligence (RMO-HDI), effectively and steadily learns policies for successive decisions. At the same time, NSGA-II is incorporated to direct the policy search in the direction of Pareto-efficient options that strike a compromise between operational efficiency, treatment safety, and diagnostic accuracy. By allowing for continuous learning from real-time data, explicitly taking into account numerous objectives and giving physicians a selection of optimal, interpretable treatment pathways, this hybrid approach overcomes the main shortcomings of current approaches.

The increasing need for precision medicine and flexible treatment paths that adapt to the unique characteristics of each patient and changing disease dynamics serves as further support for this drive. In addition to improving patient outcomes, the capacity to dynamically optimize healthcare delivery lowers operational pressures and healthcare expenditures. Furthermore, the suggested approach is intended to assist the growing emphasis by regulatory bodies on the value of data-driven, evidence-based decision-making, backed by transparent and understandable AI systems.
