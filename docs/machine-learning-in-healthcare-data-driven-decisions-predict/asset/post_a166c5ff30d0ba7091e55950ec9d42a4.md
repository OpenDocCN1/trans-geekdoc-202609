
However, the proposed model also has numerous flaws despite its improved performance. The Bi-LSTM module, which is involved in sequential data processing and involves high memory usage and computational requirements, is primarily responsible for its higher computational complexity and training duration. In addition, random forest models are noninterpretable, especially when combined with deep learning modules. This can be an issue in medical diagnostic-type applications where explainability takes precedence. Another disadvantage is overfitting to training data, especially with small datasets, unless strict regularization and validation processes are put in place.

Subsequent work can contemplate making the model more explainable with explainable AI (XAI) methods such as LIME or SHAP values that can introduce transparency into decisions. In addition, the model would be more suitable for edge or real-time application if its training performance can be optimized through techniques such as parameter sharing, pruning, or quantization. To further promote the learning of effective temporal features, the model can also be supplemented with attention mechanisms. Finally, extensive external validation across a range of large-scale datasets would improve its generalizability across different applications and demographic partitions.

## 5 Conclusion

To address the shortcomings of conventional healthcare systems, particularly in resource-constrained environments, this study suggested DigiCure, a smart, simulation-based digital healthcare platform. DigiCure provides an end-to-end, patient-centric environment to perform real-time monitoring, predictive diagnosis, and improved patient engagement through the integration of telemedicine, IoMT, EHR, and AI algorithms. The central model of the system, a hybrid deep learning model based on random forest and Bi-LSTM architectures, performs better than both deep learning baselines like CNN-LSTM and more conventional models like SVM and LR by a considerable margin. The results of the experiments validate the superiority of the model presented, which obtained the highest scores in all the evaluation measures: F1-score (91.5%), accuracy (92.5%), precision (91.8%), recall (91.2%), and AUC-ROC (94.2%). These are improvements in terms of how accurate it is in labeling the positive cases correctly and in reducing the false positives and false negatives. Practical performance advantages of system simulation also involved decreased simulated readmission (7.6%), increased levels of participation (74.5%), and reduced virtual patient waiting times (19.8–12.4).
