As privacy regulation compliance has already been taken into consideration for data being secured, these architectures are proposed to emphasize increased levels of interaction between patients and systems. Arguing for its case based on evidence pointing toward the inclusive UX approach being highly influential to adoption, compliance, and health outcomes, UX research has become part of digital health design [21]. Digital services must be synthesized in an attempt to address various patient populations in the context of global diversity increases in healthcare systems. Patient-centered standards have been brought forward in a new review as one means to ensure safe and fair access to digital health services in different socio-technical settings [22].

In order to pinpoint vulnerable populations, monitor patterns of health prior to the fact, and enhance means of care provision based on information in real time, predictive analytics and AI-enabled models are currently being used [23]. Patient empowerment also came under increased scrutiny in the COVID-19 pandemic, and systems to support shared decision-making, personalized messaging, and emotional support were further enhanced [24]. Notably where face-to-face interaction is restricted, experience served to further highlight the role that digital technology has in-patient trust and continuity of care.
