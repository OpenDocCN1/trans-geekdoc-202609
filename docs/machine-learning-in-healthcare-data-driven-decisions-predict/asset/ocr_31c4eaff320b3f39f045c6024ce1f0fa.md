# 2.1 Continuous Stress Detection Using a Wrist Device: In Laboratory and Real Life

Stress is a widespread health issue that has important physical and mental health consequences. Conventional stress measurement techniques – self-reports and clinical interviews – are subjective and not well-suited for real-time or long-term monitoring. Advances in wearable technology and the miniaturization of physiological sensors have made objective, continuous, and noninvasive stress monitoring possible, which has opened the door for ML-based techniques. The quality and diversity of input data directly affect the performance of stress detection models.

Physiological Signals: Heart rate (HR), heart rate variability (HRV), electrodermal activity (EDA/GSR), body temperature, blood oxygen saturation (SpO2), and respiration rate are some of the most frequently used.

Behavioral and Contextual Information: Sleep, limb and eye movement, and activity rate supply more context to stress prediction. Wearable devices: The inclusion of wearable sensors (e.g., Empatica E4, Fitbit, and Oura Ring) has facilitated continuous real-time data capture both in controlled and free-living settings. Feature engineering, such as time-domain and frequency-domain feature extraction, is crucial in enhancing model performance. Sophisticated models increasingly make use of both domains to have a complete overview of the stress response.
