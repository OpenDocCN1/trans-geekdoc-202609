Novel multi-objective learning algorithms have been developed for disease identification and classification within electronic healthcare systems. These algorithms combine feature selection and classification tasks to optimize diagnostic accuracy and computational efficiency simultaneously. The study underscores the importance of balancing performance metrics in automated diagnosis tools, though real-world deployment requires extensive validation on heterogeneous clinical datasets [8].

Comprehensive reviews on reinforcement learning applications in personalized medicine highlight strategies for treatment optimization, including dosage adjustment and therapy scheduling. The surveyed approaches emphasize MORL for balancing treatment effectiveness and side effects. The review calls for greater emphasis on explainability and clinical interpretability to enhance practitioner trust and adoption [9].

Reviews of reinforcement learning in intelligent healthcare systems provide critical insights into the challenges, applications, and open research issues. These works discuss issues such as the scarcity of labeled clinical data, ethical considerations, and the need for scalable algorithms capable of handling multi-objective optimization in real-time clinical settings. They advocate hybrid frameworks, combining reinforcement learning with evolutionary or multi-objective optimization techniques, to address these challenges effectively [10].

Feature selection improvements via multi-objective optimization have been investigated to enhance predictive model performance in healthcare. By simultaneously optimizing for relevance, redundancy, and computational cost, these methods improve diagnostic accuracy and speed, essential for real-time clinical decision support. However, the complexity of healthcare data and varying clinical needs call for adaptable feature selection frameworks [11].

Adaptive scheduling algorithms for multi-objective resource allocation in wireless body area networks represent another application area where healthcare meets optimization and reinforcement learning. These algorithms employ multi-criteria decision-making, combined with deep Q-networks, to balance energy consumption, latency, and reliability. While this technology supports wearable health monitoring systems, its integration with broader healthcare optimization frameworks remains a subject for future research [12].

Surveys of reinforcement learning for intelligent healthcare systems also highlight ongoing efforts to integrate multi-agent reinforcement learning and edge computing for distributed healthcare management. These approaches aim to optimize local and global objectives such as patient monitoring accuracy and network resource utilization, though ensuring privacy and security remains a key concern [13].

A multi-objective model-based reinforcement learning framework developed for infectious disease control explores balancing health outcomes with societal costs. The research underlines the need for models capable of adapting to rapidly evolving epidemiological data, with reinforcement learning enabling flexible policy adjustments. Scalability and real-time applicability are ongoing challenges [14].
