# # 8.2 Explainable AI and Trust in ML Models

XAI is essential to fostering trust in ML models within healthcare, where decisions can have life-changing consequences. Clinicians must be able to understand the intent behind AI-driven predictions to validate and integrate them into their decision-making processes. Unlike black-box models that provide little to no insight into their workings, Table 3. represents the XAI tools such as SHAP and local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME) offer interpretable outputs that reveal which variables most influenced a prediction and why.

This interpretability is not just about transparency; it also builds clinician confidence, aids in model validation, and enhances regulatory compliance. When a ML model recommends a diagnosis or treatment, healthcare professionals are more likely to trust and act on it if they understand the underlying logic. Moreover, transparent models enable organizations to identify potential biases, correct misalignments, and ensure fairness across diverse patient populations [27].

**Table 3:** Techniques for explainable AI in healthcare.

| Technique | Description | Strengths | Limitations | Examples of use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LIME | Locally approximates complex models using simpler ones | Model-agnostic, interpretable | Unstable explanations | Cancer risk prediction from EHR |
| SHAP | Computes feature contributions using Shapley values | Consistent, theoretically sound | Computationally expensive | Drug response modeling |
| Saliency Maps | Visualizes areas influencing neural network decisions | Intuitive, image-specific | Limited to image data | Chest X-ray abnormality detection |
| Counterfactuals | Shows how minimal changes can alter the model's decision | Enhances user understanding | May lack feasibility in healthcare data analysis | Treatment plan revision support |
| Decision Trees | Inherently interpretable rule-based model | Transparent, logical | Limited scalability | Diabetes risk prediction |

Building trust also involves collaboration. Co-designing AI systems with clinicians, incorporating their domain expertise, and iteratively refining models based on medical feedback results in tools that are not only more accurate but also more usable and relevant in real-world settings. This inclusive approach fosters a sense of ownership and acceptance among end-users.
