
Social network analysis (SNA) describes the interaction patterns between people that impact the transmission of infectious illnesses in communities. Marquetoux et al. examines the network features that influence the creation of disease control programs in their study [16]. It uses SNA techniques to analyze livestock movement over a network consisting of 180 farms in New Zealand from 2006 to 2010. The analysis reveals that the overall number of links remains stable, but the trading partners vary. It highlights the significant role that a few central farms play in spreading infections.

This study investigates the influence of the behavior of highly connected nodes on the transmission of infection within or across communities in a network. Feldman and Janssen used two stochastic process spatial preferential attachment (SPA) networks and susceptible-infected-recovered (SIR) processes to model the spread of illness [17]. Within a given epidemic situation, the transmissibility of highly connected vertices is reduced, indicating that the spread of illness occurs inside communities rather than between them. This deduction is substantiated by theoretical findings and simulations.

Contact tracing is a method used in epidemiology to manage the transmission of infectious illnesses and identify people who have had prior contact with infected patients. Chen et al. demonstrates that adding geographical contacts in SNA for diseases that spread beyond personal contacts improves connectivity [18]. They used Taiwan SARS data and found that including geographical contacts resulted in higher connectivity compared to personal contacts. ### 3.1 Overview of Complex Network

A complex network is a network that consists of nodes and edges, which links and exhibits sophisticated patterns of connections or interactions among these nodes [19]. The complex network structure also displays nontrivial topological characteristics, which are often defined by elaborate patterns of connections and interactions among its components [20]. These networks are often used in many real-world systems such as social networks, biological systems, transportation systems, and the Internet. In recent times, the study of complex networks has emerged as a multidisciplinary domain, including ideas from mathematics, physics, computer science, biology, and sociology.

A complex network exhibits some characteristics:

- 1. Nodes and Edges:
  These are the simple things that every network consists of. These two things are called as the basic building blocks of a network. A node or vertex is an individual entity that can connect with other individual entities by using edges, which can be called as links or arcs.

- 2. Scale-Free Network:
  In a scale-free network, a small number of nodes, called as Hubs, have a larger number of connections than the bulk of nodes, which have less numbers of connections. This distribution exhibits a power-law pattern, by which a limited number of nodes have a crucial impact on the overall structure and operation of the network.

- 3. Small-World Phenomenon:
  Complex networks often display the small-world phenomenon, by which the maximum number of nodes may be accessible from any other node within a few numbers of steps. Although the network is large, the average distance between nodes is relatively small.

- 4. Clustering:
  Clustering refers to the inclination of nodes to create groups (classes/clusters) that have a greater concentration (tightly coupled) of connections inside the group as compared to connections (loosely coupled) with nodes outside the group. This indicates the existence of localized structures inside the network.

- 5. Hierarchy:
  Some complex networks exhibit hierarchical structure, which means that nodes are arranged into distinct layers or tiers, each fulfilling a unique purpose or duty. This hierarchical arrangement is widely used in biological systems.

- 6. Randomness and Preferential Attachment:
  The development and progression of complex networks frequently include processes like preferential attachment, which means new nodes have a greater likelihood of connecting to existing nodes with higher degrees, resulting in the establishment of hubs.
