Any approach to data-driven innovation should ensure that the costs and rewards are distributed justly among all people. In addition, we ought to reduce health gaps for marginalized people by actively taking steps and ensuring resources are distributed evenly. It is important to get the community involved in this kind of work. Learning from patients and stakeholders through each step of a data project helps the systems address the needs important to the community [4]. Working cooperatively with people improves the integrity and survival of health programs that rely on data. To create responsible guidelines for new healthcare technologies, all these professionals should cooperate [5]. It requires promoting openness, responsibility, and involvement when creating and implementing data technologies. Initiatives should be used to make clinicians, data scientists, and policy makers aware of data equity [52]. Overall, healthcare data should be handled ethically using justice, respect, and care for others as the main guide. Since digital transformation is changing health services, it is essential to pay attention and take action to observe if the new technology helps improve healthcare for all (Figure 5).
