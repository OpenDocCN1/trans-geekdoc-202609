The greatest impact of deep learning has been in analyzing medical images and natural language processing for understanding clinical text.

- CNNs: These types of neural networks are made for medical image analysis, X-ray, MRI, CT scan, etc.

- Recurrent Neural Networks: These are mainly made for temporal data like health records from electronic or wearable devices, which track data over a period of time [10].

- Transformers: They are a relatively new class of neural network that has shown much promise in natural language processing and is increasingly being used in other types of medical data.

## 2.4 Personalized Treatment Design

Recently, health professionals and medical researchers started to appreciate a patient's uniqueness, not only with respect to clinical presentations of symptoms, but also with individual genetic background, lifestyle, and biological response to treatments. Hence, standard treatment protocols that follow a “one-size-fits-all” paradigm, which, while gaining acceptance from many patients, do not guarantee the maximum expected result for every patient in every indicated situation. Thanks to numerous advances in ML, this paradigm is now being contradicted in favor of one that views treatment as a necessity and very much individualized in terms of need and biological constitution of each patient. The application of ML is truly important in precision medicine. Thus, ML models can link the patients’ genetic profile to specific recommendations for response to certain therapies. For example, certain cancer treatments may be favored in their response by genetic markers, whilst others may warn against a higher risk of adverse effects. In conjunction with clinical history and environmental exposure, ML is able to weigh and filter vast populations, drawn from genomic data, to home in on treatments that are effective and tolerated by that one single individual. This kind of understanding of the prescription will not only improve therapeutic efficacy but will also ensure that more patients can be safely treated, thereby improving patient safety. Another major application is optimizing dosage regimens. Dosing, as a general rule, is very complicated for any patient. It involves interacting factors such as metabolism, age, weight, genetics, and concomitant diseases that influence the indicated dose. Concerning the methods of determining dosage, in general, mainstream approaches depend on highly generic guidance and clinical experience. However, this is the area where ML methods stand to offer a precise advantage in modeling these complex interplays. By combining real-time patient data with historical treatment outcome information, the ML system has the ability to predict the most effective dosage that would maximize treatment efficacy and reduce toxicity or ineffective treatment. All in all, the personalized healthcare philosophy delivered through ML is one major achievement in medicine. Such tailoring allows the physician to use an individual's profile in the treatment design, thereby maximizing reasonable health outcomes and better patient satisfaction with more judicious use of medical resources [14]. At the same time, customized care will also really work toward the bigger picture of saving costs and making healthcare sustainable by steering clear from the generalized treatment that may well be altogether ineffective or overly aggressive for certain patients.

## 2.5 Drug Discovery and Development

The traditional process of drug development would usually take a lot of time, at least a decade, and cost a lot of money, often billions of dollars – just to have a drug approved for the market. A big portion of the timeline and cost is related to laboratory investigations, testing, clinical trials, and regulatory workings. Thus, another pathway has been opened up, thanks to ML, which has brought powerful tools to accelerate drug discovery and development, improve efficiency, and fulfill the need to mitigate risks in pharmaceutical innovation. One of the major ways in which ML is reshaping drug discovery is through the prediction of molecular behavior [15]. ML models, particularly deep learning architectures, can be trained on huge datasets, with information about chemical compounds, biological pathways, and mechanisms of diseases. These models can predict how different chemical compounds would behave when interacting with biological targets such as proteins and enzymes. If such interactions are simulated computationally, researchers can identify promising drug candidates in a more rapid manner, doing away with labor-intensive and costly laboratory work [16]. Such an alternative would not only save time but also allow scientists to explore a far larger chemical space than would be feasible using conventional techniques, thereby increasing the chances of finding effective treatments for complex diseases. Apart from early-phase discovery, ML is instrumental in optimizing clinical trials. Clinical trials are the actual proof for these drugs, probably the last and most important step in confirming their safety and efficacy. Several challenges are faced in conducting such clinical trials, including recruitment challenges, high dropout rates, and unpredictable outcomes [17]. ML predictive analytics can bring forth the design of efficient clinical trials by identifying the most suitable participants from genetic profiles, medical history, and demographic factors. In addition, ML models can help identify possible trial outcomes, optimize dosage regimens, and flag early signs of any toxicity issues that would warrant intervention. By increasing the targeting and efficiency of clinical trials, ML reduces the time to market new therapies and raises the chances of success. Overall, the incorporation of ML into the field of drug discovery and development marks a paradigm shift, providing the capability through acceleration, smartness, and frugality [18]. Furthermore, developers of ML promise faster delivery of life-saving treatments at a cost much lower than the traditional model.
