# ### 4.1.1 Correlation Between Emotional States and Physiological Parameters

This section examines the relationship between self-reported emotional states (e.g., happy, sad, neutral, and relaxed) and various physiological parameters (such as bpm, calories burned, and resting heart rate). In our customized dataset, there are a total of 80 features, where ID and date are removed in the correlation. When we find the correlation between physiological data and emotions, it looks very messy and does not provide any good insight, so we only show the most significant features correlated with the emotions in Table 2. We take the correlation range from –0.4 to 0.6 for better and clearer visibility of patterns among the data. From Table 2, we observe that filtereddemographicVO2max and resting heart rate show the highest correlation. Similarly, steps, distance, and very_active_minutes show very low correlation with emotions; on the other hand, bpm and sedentary_minutes show a positive correlation with emotions, and lightly_active_minutes show a negative correlation.
