## 7 Future Directions

Even though the proposed TNPE has registered promising results in detecting depression and anxiety, there are several areas where this research can be developed further and expanded. The future studies must explore multimodal sources of data, such as clinical and text information but also physiological signals like EEG, heart rate variability, and speech patterns to increase the feature space of the model and detect subtle behavioral features that are correlated with mental illness disorders. Another step forward includes validating the TNPE framework on larger, more diverse medical datasets, and actual clinical data to test its scalability and generalizability to different population groups and cultures. Application of federated learning approaches may also enable models to be trained collaboratively across institutions without violating patient confidentiality, addressing problems with data sharing common in healthcare. Furthermore, extending the ensemble by cross-testing other sophisticated neural architectures, including graph neural networks for relational data or temporal convolutional networks for longitudinal monitoring of mental health, could further improve predictive performance and temporal sensitivity. Integrating an application of real-time mental health monitoring with TNPE in combination with wearable sensors or social media monitoring software provides a viable pathway for the translation of this research into a clinically useful decision support system. Lastly, working out the explainability framework from counterfactual explanations or causal inference-based interpretability will add more transparency to models, which clinicians can then leverage to create actionable evidence-based findings. Improving this way in the future will make the framework more clinically useful and translate into more tailored, preventive mental healthcare practices.
