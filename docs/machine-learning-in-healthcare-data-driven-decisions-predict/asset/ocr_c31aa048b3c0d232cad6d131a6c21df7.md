The modeling pipeline is a deep-classical hybrid ensemble. Classical ML models like logistic regression (LR) [14], RF [15], and XGBoost are employed because they can process tabular and structured clinical data. Such models provide excellent interpretability, deployment simplicity, and resistance to overfitting, which are adequate for decision support systems in clinical environments. Meanwhile, DL models process unstructured data: hierarchical spatial contexts in imaging data like CT or MRI scans by convolutional neural networks (CNNs) [16] and temporal contexts in longitudinal patient data and treatment histories by recurrent neural networks, for example, long short-term memory (LSTM) [17] units. This labor division between model types ensures that each modality is handled by the optimum algorithm, best performance.

For further improvement in predictive stability and reliability, predictions from individual models are averaged out in an ensemble methodology. Methods like model stacking and weighted voting are used to aggregate the predictions together in such a way that the resulting conclusion is enriched with the advantages of each individual contributing model. Additionally, a meta-learner is also learned over the output of the said base models, enhancing the final prediction and minimizing variance of each individual model. This ensemble strategy enhances the system to make more stable and confident predictions as well as identify fine patterns on diversified data types compared to single models.

The hybrid ensemble architecture is thoroughly tested for performance to make it generalizable and non-overfitting. Common evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score are employed to measure the performance of the models. The proposed scheme outperforms the traditional ML techniques and deep neural networks when they are utilized separately. Specifically, it has higher sensitivity and specificity in cancer disease and precursor diagnosis at more advanced intelligence levels, and it is therefore an applicable tool in early diagnosis and preventive clinical therapy.

This study proposes a novel and comprehensive ML approach that sufficiently tackles the problem of multimodal early cancer diagnosis with robust data fusion and hybrid modeling. Under a single predictive framework, which integrates conventional and DL approaches, the system is very efficient, adaptable, and clinically relevant. The findings highlight the possibilities of change with AI-driven predictive analytics in oncology as a feasible route to the fulfillment of early diagnosis, individualized treatment, and enhanced patient outcomes. The study objectives are as follows:

+   – To develop an integrated multimodal data model by integrating various types of patient-specific and disease-specific data.

+   – To develop a stable end-to-end data preprocessing pipeline for heterogeneous, high-dimensional oncological data.

+   – To develop a hybrid ensemble ML architecture by integrating common classifiers (e.g., LR, RF, and XGBoost) with DL algorithms (with CNN for imaging data and LSTM for longitudinal data) to leverage the particular strengths of each algorithm for addressing different data modalities.
