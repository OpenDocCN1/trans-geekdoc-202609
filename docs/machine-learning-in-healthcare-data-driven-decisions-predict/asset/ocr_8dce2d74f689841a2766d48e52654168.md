AI, through NLP, is able to find insights in statements written by doctors, discharge reports, or pathology records. Adverse drug events, assessing who is eligible for clinical trials, and improving EHRs can all be achieved with NLP models. Reinforcement learning uses AI algorithms to find the best actions by being part of an environment. This field is studied in healthcare to support personal evolution of therapies, adjust settings in intensive care, and maintain chronic conditions [40]. Still, when ML and AI are used, it is important to be cautious, as they may display bias, behave in unpredictable ways and their results may only apply to specific situations. It is still difficult to explain, verify fairness in AI, and test clinical AI for use in a variety of populations.

# 5.3 Risk Stratification and Outcome Prediction Models

Risk stratification involves grouping patients who have a higher risk of experiencing problems related to their health. Practicing medicine in this way ensures that health needs of those who are at highest risk are met as efficiently as possible. Many times logistic regression, survival analysis, and ML-based scores are used for risk estimates. The CHADS2 model is used to check for stroke risk in individuals living with atrial fibrillation. Another use is to estimate the chances of hospital readmission within 30 days, find patients who could experience a heart failure episode, and guess the probability of surgical complications. Predicting the outcome of a condition depends on information such as clinical markers, societal factors, lifestyle habits, comorbid illnesses, and demographics. Clinicians can administer proactive care by using integration of models into their dashboard tools. A real-time algorithm for predicting sepsis may be used in a hospital to send a message to the care team once early symptoms are spotted. Accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC-ROC are a few methods used to assess how well a prediction model performs. Probability estimations must be in agreement with what happens in the real world. Because overfitting, data drift, and testing on a small set often happen, people should always test and retrain the machine learning models [41].
