# ## 2.2 Evolution of Data Utilization in Healthcare Practice

Improvements in healthcare data use are based on new technologies, better approaches, and cultural changes. In the beginning, healthcare information was managed by hand-written charts, making it tough to obtain and examine complete history. When standards were not the same and information was spread out, doctors had trouble providing the same level of care each time. At the end of the twentieth century, the introduction of electronic health records (EHRs) made it much easier to handle, store, and share medical records. In the early days, EHRs were mainly for recording data, which left most information unused because it was not analyzed. With the help of protocols such as ICD and lab data standards, secondary uses of EHR data, such as quality measurement and research, became more practical over time [11]. Improved capabilities for HIE networks allowed patient data to be more widely shared, so doctors and other health professionals had access to better patient records. Integrating HL7 and FHIR made it easier to share data, and thus prepared the foundation for integrated analytics in care programs. Healthcare data now benefit greatly from new processing and utilization technologies, such as big data, AI, and machine learning. Through artificial intelligence, algorithms can look at both organized and unorganized data from several sources and spot complex relationships that are too hard for people to notice. For example, we now use predictive models to identify patients at risk of deterioration, allocate resources efficiently, and adapt the way we treat each patient [12]. Furthermore, data coming from wearable devices, mobile health apps, and home monitoring systems makes it possible to obtain data all the time. This increases the scope of health records and supplies a more complete view of patient well-being at home. The adoption of data from daily life is influencing healthcare, so doctors focus more on preventing diseases and managing chronic cases. Beginning with the digitization of data and continuing to data integration and standardization, advanced analytics, and ultimately using real-time and patient-centered data in decision-making, there is a phased track followed in data utilization. # ## 2.3 Principles of Evidence-Based Medicine and Decision-Making

EBM is the process of combining proven medical evidence, what healthcare professionals know, and what patients want to create treatment decisions. Evidence-based data utilization is built on EBM principles, which determine the best ways to interpret and use data in healthcare [13]. Firstly, we should generate valid evidence and then examine it closely. We should ensure that data is taken, examined, and interpreted using reliable methods to help avoid bias. This means that patients should first know that systematic reviews and randomized controlled trials are the most trustworthy, then observational studies, and finally expert opinion.
