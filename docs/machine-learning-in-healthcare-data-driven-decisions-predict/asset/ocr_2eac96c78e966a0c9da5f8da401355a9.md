Jayaprabha et al. [10] gave a comprehensive overview of some ML methods and their application to CKD prediction in the early days. While precision statistics were not reported, the survey highlighted the importance of feature engineering and data preprocessing to model performance. Sawhney et al. [11] compared performances of a number of artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms in early CKD prediction. GBM performed better than the other models tried, with the maximum accuracy of 99.0%. James et al. [12] discussed the needs of CKD early detection and prevention. The research explained how early detection would prevent disease progression and further improve patient outcomes, although none was directed toward a special single ML algorithm.

Habiba et al. [13] investigated the combination of feature selection techniques and ML models employed to forecast CKD. With a 98.3% accuracy rate using the RF classifier, the research concluded that adding feature selection enhanced model performance. Tangri et al. [14] used clinical features to construct a model to predict CKD progression to renal failure. Rather than level comparison with ML-based models, the model, constructed with classical statistical methods, had a c-statistic of 0.91 and exhibited excellent predictive ability. Schena et al. [15] evaluated the application of AI algorithms for predicting the development and course of CKD. Without firm performance metrics, the study demonstrated the ability of AI to detect high-risk patients.

George et al. [16] looked at the need for screening and early detection of CKD, especially in low- and middle-income countries with populations at risk for diabetes. The article did not address specific ML methods or results but did emphasize predictive models in poor-resource areas. Debal et al. [17] highlighted CKD prediction using multiple ML algorithms such as decision trees and SVM. The SVM classifier's ability to correctly classify CKD detection tasks came in at a rate of 98.3%. Shih et al. [18] utilized health screening data to develop a risk prediction model for the early detection of CKD. Based on logistic regression, the model was reasonably good with an AUC of 0.88.

Chien et al. [19] employed a cohort analysis to develop a prediction model of the probability of incident CKD. The c-statistic value of 0.85 from traditional statistical analysis reflects a good degree of predicted accuracy. Islam et al. [20] addressed prediction of CKD with ML methods and the comparison of performance of a number of classifiers. The reliability of the RF classifier for the prediction issues in CKD was confirmed through the accuracy of 98.0% of the study. Xiao et al. [21] compared and developed ML algorithms to predict the trajectory of CKD. Following algorithm testing, the study concluded that the XGBoost classifier performed best in modeling the trajectory of CKD with the best accuracy of 99.2%.
