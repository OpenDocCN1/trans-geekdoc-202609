# 2 Related Works

Current developments in ML have transformed medical diagnosis, providing robust means for processing complex clinical data and detecting individuals at risk. This review discusses hybrid and ensemble ML methods for stroke prediction, combining algorithms including LR, SVM, RFs, DL architectures, and metaheuristic optimization algorithms. These models, augmented with feature selection (FS) and class balancing methods, exhibit remarkable accuracy and clinical utility enhancements and are scalable, interpretable, and robust solutions for early stroke detection and prevention.

# 2.1 Hybrid ML Model for Stroke Prediction

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) is a serious global health problem due to its high rates of mortality, morbidity, and disability, with survivors hearing lifelong physical, cognitive, and emotional impairments. For the provision of prompt clinical decision and individualized treatment, a successful hybrid ML model has been proposed for predicting three-month poor outcomes in AIS patients. Based on a 731-case dataset and internally and externally validated, the study compared 25 DL and ML models on 14 performance measures. On the basis of clustering and correlation analysis of measures, 12 high-performing models were shortlisted out of which XGBoost and CatBoost performed better than others both in terms of accuracy as well as reliability. Ensemble modeling and cost curve analysis threshold tuning resulted in the final model with respectable performance on both sets. Decision analysis and fairness checks were done to measure clinical utility. SHAP-based interpretability utilized albumin, fasting blood glucose, BMI, serum creatinine (Scr), white blood cell count, and age as important predictors, importantly interacting with gender. Notably, the model uses only 30 easily available clinical features, enhancing its utility. The resulting prognostic platform provides precise, interpretable, and clinically actionable predictions, justifying its implementation in electronic health records or telemedicine systems. This research demonstrates how hybrid, interpretable ML models can inform early AIS management and rehabilitation planning safely and reliably and is an exciting step toward precision medicine for stroke therapy [13].
