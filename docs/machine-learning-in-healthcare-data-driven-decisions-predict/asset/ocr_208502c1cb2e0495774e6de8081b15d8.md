# 2.5 Hybrid Approach to Mammogram-Based Cancer Classification

BC, the leading cancer in the world involving more than 2.3 million women, is still a major public health problem. In spite of the fact that survival rates have been increasing because of enhanced screening, diagnosis, and treatment, early detection continues to be paramount in effective management of the condition. In order to improve the identification and classification rate of BC lesions on mammograms, a unique hybrid model utilizing DL and sophisticated image-preprocessing techniques is proposed in this study. The dataset of 20,000 mammograms was used to validate the model. To improve image quality and enable improved feature extraction, it was first preprocessed utilizing image-enhancement operators such as Gaussian Blur and Sharpening, YOLOv5 and MedSAM segmentation models were used in the DL architecture to facilitate lesion extraction, categorization, and appropriate display. Following a clinical evaluation on 800 patients, the blended model produced extremely high improvements in diagnostic speed and accuracy. The model only took 0.75 s to process each image, achieving a 99.7% global accuracy. In terms of clinical accuracy, it was 98.6% for malignant cases and 97.2% for benign ones. According to these findings, the hybrid model is a reliable and efficient diagnostic tool for early BC recognition with promising opportunities for clinical practice improvements in terms of diagnostic accuracy [22].
