# 2.8 Epidemic Spreading Processes Using Deep Transfer Learning

Traditional data-driven approaches to epidemic propagation modeling and forecasting typically depend on the independent and identically distributed (IID) data assumption. However, epidemic spreading over networks is extremely heterogeneous with regard to the various phases, regions, and virus types, suggesting that the epidemic time series are subject to temporal and spatial heterogeneities and therefore probably do not adhere to the IID assumption. A novel deep transfer learning model by fusing CNNs and Bi-LSTM networks has been proposed to effectively model and forecast epidemics based on heterogeneous data. The method employs a CNN layer for extracting local spatial features, a Bi-LSTM layer for capturing temporal relationships, and a fully connected layer for prediction. In order to enhance the generalization ability of the model, transfer learning techniques are applied. In addition, hyperparameter optimization is performed with particle swarm optimization while training for further improvement in prediction accuracy. The paradigm is applied to study the spatio-temporal dynamics of COVID-19 and explore the pathologic differences between SARS, influenza A (H1N1), and COVID-19 epidemics. The experimental findings in detail demonstrate that the approach is effective to keep up with spatiotemporal variations in epidemic dissemination and distinguish significant epidemiological characteristics among different viruses. Moreover, the approach tremendously reduces the prediction error in epidemic modeling with more accurate predictions of disease dissemination [15].
