The recent COVID-19 pandemic has served as an eye-opener, revealing the scalability of analytics platforms and vulnerabilities of the available healthcare infrastructure. Models based on ML were quickly created amid the pandemic to predict infection trends, filter patients, allocate resources as efficiently as possible, and track vaccination side effects. These attempts demonstrated the potential of AI in crisis management and made apparent the constraints of not fully tested systems made ready for market too quickly. Clear from this contrast of potential and constraint is that methodological rigor, interpretability, and domain adaptability are the specifications of the next generation of healthcare analytics.

By introducing a new ML architecture designed especially for predictive healthcare analytics, the current work strives to overcome these limitations. For the improvement of accuracy, reliability, and clinical significance of AI-driven decision support, the system uses multimodal data fusion, adaptive learning framework, and explainability techniques.

The main objectives are

+   1. To develop a robust, interpretable, and generalizable ML framework that effectively integrates multimodal healthcare data, including EHRs, medical imaging, and physiological signals, for accurate and early prediction of critical clinical outcomes.

+   2. To address key limitations of existing healthcare analytics systems such as data heterogeneity, lack of interpretability, and poor cross-institutional generalizability by incorporating explainable AI techniques and adaptive learning mechanisms that enhance clinical trust and real-world applicability.
