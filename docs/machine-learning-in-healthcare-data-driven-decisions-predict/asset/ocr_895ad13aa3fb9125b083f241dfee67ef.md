# ## 1.1 Background and Rationale

The expansion of digital data and developments in computing have caused a major transformation in healthcare delivery today. Globally, healthcare organizations are swapping instinct-based decisions with those supported by carefully collected data. Not only is this an advancement in technology, but it is also a major change in the way organizations meet the needs of their patients. Dependence on evidence-based data is now a main strategy to ensure healthcare is safe, effective, and works efficiently.

Throughout history, clinicians made medical decisions mainly informed by their own understanding, patient evidence, and the known medical conventions of the time. While encouraging responsible care, this way of work led to variable, biased, and sometimes inconsistent treatment of patients. With the digitization of health and administrative files, healthcare professionals can use structured and unstructured data in real time. Thanks to this transformation, making precise decisions in healthcare is possible, along with building policies and managing both resources and care for patients. EHRs, for instance, have made it much easier for different departments to share patient information, thus leading to fewer mistakes and reduced recurrence of data collection [1]. At the same time, various data analytics tools are now available that support users in harnessing data to their advantage. These findings are essential when it comes to early diagnosis, estimating outcomes, organizing patients, and assessing treatment success. In addition, using real-world data from wearable devices and other tools helps strengthen the evaluation of patient behaviors and their surroundings [2]. There is a strong reason to focus on data because it may help resolve persistent issues and gaps in healthcare. Results from studies show that relying on data leads to fewer patients being readmitted, fewer deaths, improved patient satisfaction, and managing costs [3]. In addition, given the current health problems around the world, including pandemics, aging populations, and chronic diseases, it is even more necessary to use data to guide our actions. With healthcare providers aiming to prioritize the health of entire populations, applying evidence-based wisdom becomes very important [4, 5].
