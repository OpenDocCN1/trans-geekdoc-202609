Data were gathered from Harvard Dataverse Repository including clinical, physiological, behavioral, demographic, and medical history variables. Three oversampling methods SMOTE, adaptive synthetic sampling (ADASYN), and random oversampling technique (ROSE) were used to overcome the prevalent problem of class imbalance of stroke databases to spread the minority classes more uniformly and enhance model performance. Introducing a new hybrid method, this chapter integrates ADASYN oversampling with RF classifier (ADASYN_RF) to accurately predict strokes from highly imbalanced physiological data. The comparison experiments for different oversampling techniques and ML algorithms reveal that the ADASYN_RF hybrid outperforms others with a whopping 99% detection rate. The hybrid approach presents a cost-effective yet reliable answer to the stroke prediction, and it is a valuable tool for timely clinical diagnosis and treatment [16].

# 2.5 Hybrid Sampling-Optimized Stroke Risk Prediction

A new supervised ML algorithm has been proposed to predict stroke risk on a comprehensive list of causative factors. As a clinical practitioner's assistant, the model aims at enhancing diagnostic accuracy, preventing fatalities, and facilitating early intervention procedures. The model was trained and tested on an unbalanced dataset consisting of diverse features related to stroke. For improving performance, hybrid sampling strategies were applied for balancing classes and grid search was used to tune hyperparameters in five baseline models. Integrated classification method had the best performance with precision, accuracy, F1-score, and recall rates ranging from 94.5% to 95.5%. The performance of the model suggests its suitability in facilitating clinical decision-making in stroke risk prediction [17].

Precise prediction and early detection of stroke are ongoing crucial challenges in the field of healthcare. In recent times, ML approaches have proven very capable in the prediction of disease with possibility of better clinical outcomes. The study introduces a hybrid ensemble ML model that has three heterogeneous base classifiers: SVM, LR, and RF.
