The requirement for a more holistic, interpretable, and universally applicable method of healthcare analytics is the motivation behind the development of the HAD-Net architecture. The complementariness between structured (such as EHR) and unstructured (such as imaging) data is often underleveraged in current models, resulting in disjointed clinical insights. HAD-Net addresses this by employing multimodal learning mechanisms that involve self-attention in order to model high-level dependencies between modalities, GNNs for relational learning between patients, and BiLSTM for EHR dynamics over time. HAD-Net is robust and trustworthy, as the Bayesian-tuned adaptive learning scheduler enhances optimization efficiency, and SHAP ensures predictions are interpretable to medical professionals.

Real-world deployment is complicated by data heterogeneity, missing values, and inconsistent timestamps between imaging and EHR timestamps. Having such a deep and multimodal network entails powerful infrastructure and efficient training methods because of its high computational cost. In addition, although SHAP promotes transparency, clinical experience is still required to confirm the interpretation of feature relevance. Strong domain adaptation methods are required, since model generalizability across institutions with varying data distributions remains problematic.

Upcoming work will focus on scaling the model’s ability to handle increasingly complex data sources such as wearable sensor streams, genomics, and clinical notes through natural language processing. Federated learning can enhance data privacy without centralizing sensitive patient information, and enable cross-institutional model generalization. Moreover, deployment of the system in clinical workflows and integration of real-time inference functionality will be explored, emphasizing clinician feedback loops and regulatory compliance. Following this trajectory, HAD-Net will become a deployable and scalable decision support system from a research prototype.

## 5 Conclusion

The primary limitations of current and traditional ML paradigms in the healthcare sector, poor generalizability, lack of interpretability, and poor multimodal fusion are addressed well by the proposed HAD-Net (hybrid attention-driven network) model. HAD-Net offers a scalable and robust solution to the tasks of risk stratification and early diagnosis by fusing structured EHR information with unstructured chest X-ray images using an integrative ensemble of graph neural networks, BiLSTM layers, and self-attention mechanisms. Dynamic convergence was ensured by the inclusion of a Bayesian-tuned adaptive learning scheduler, whereas interpretability and transparency were encouraged through the use of SHAP, which provided clinically useful explanations. Outcome of experimental validation on the combined NIH ChestX-ray14 and MIMIC-III datasets verified that HAD-Net outperforms state-of-the-art models on accuracy, F1-score, and AUROC. In addition, the utility of the model in real, evidence-based healthcare is exemplified by its ability to identify clinically significant biomarkers. This study fills the gap between state-of-the-art AI and robust clinical application by demonstrating the transformative ability of smart multimodal systems to enable active and personalized patient care.
