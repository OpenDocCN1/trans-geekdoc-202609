## 3.1 Datasets Used

We employed three publicly available benchmark healthcare datasets: the Wisconsin Breast Cancer Diagnostic Dataset, the UCI Heart Disease Dataset, and the MIMIC-III Critical Care Database to evaluate and verify the proposed AGBO-CP framework. The unique features each dataset displays regarding clinical factors, diversity of patients, and prediction tasks make it possible to perform an in-depth analysis of the system's adaptability and performance.

### 3.1.1 MIMIC-III Clinical Database

Over 60,000 ICU admissions from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center during 2001–2012 are contained within the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care (MIMIC-III) database, which was developed at MIT and comprises extensive de-identified health information. Individuals 16 years of age and older are represented in the dataset, and the composition of male and female patients is roughly equal. We pulled a vetted cohort of 32,763 intensive care unit admissions from MIMIC-III, targeting patients who were admitted to cardiac, surgical, and medical intensive care units. There are over 40 clinical factors in each patient record, including demographics such as age, sex, and ethnicity. Table 1 shows the features of MIMIC-III dataset.

Table 1: MIMIC-III dataset features.

| Feature name | Description | Type | Value range / Categories |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | Age of the patient | Numeric | 16 to 90+ years (de-identified) |
| Gender | Sex of the patient | Binary | 0 = Female, 1 = Male |
| Admission type | Type of hospital admission | Categorical | Emergency, elective, urgent |
| ICU length of stay | Duration of ICU stay (days) | Numeric | 0 to >50 days |
| Heart rate (avg.) | Average heart rate during ICU stay | Numeric | 40 to 200 bpm |
| Systolic blood pressure (avg.) | Mean systolic BP | Numeric | 70 to 220 mmHg |
| Respiratory rate (avg.) | Average respiratory rate | Numeric | 10 to 40 breaths/min |
| Temperature (avg.) | Mean body temperature | Numeric | 35.0 to 41.0 °C |

Due to its severe imbalance, the dataset is treated with caution when training the model. After preprocessing and data cleaning (excluding patients with missing core vitals or extremely short ICU stays of less than 4 h), we had 28,457 records remaining, and distributed them into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) sets.

### 3.1.2 UCI Heart Disease Dataset

The UCI Cleveland Heart Disease Dataset, a widely referenced benchmarking dataset in clinical prediction work, is the second dataset utilized in this study. To predict whether or not cardiac disease exists, it contains data from 303 unique patients, all of whom had features obtained through clinical assessment and diagnostic examinations. There are thirteen mandatory input factors, both numeric and categorical, that constitute the dataset. Gender is provided as 1 if male or 0 if female, and patient ages vary from 29 to 77 years. Resting blood pressure, serum cholesterol, fasting blood sugar, and electrocardiogram values are other significant features.

Features such as maximal heart rate achieved, angina on exercise, and ST depression are utilized to quantify cardiovascular function. Criteria such as the number of principal vessels stained by fluoroscopy, the steepness of the ST segment, and the type of thalassemia (normal, fixed defect, or reversible defect) are valuable diagnostic indicators. Whether or not a patient has been diagnosed with heart disease (1 or 0) is represented by the binary target variable. With 54% of cases having cardiac disease and 46% non-diseased, the class distribution is somewhat unbalanced.

Three cases of missing values were imputed with median replacement to prepare the dataset for modeling. Z-score normalization was subsequently employed to normalize all numerical fields to ensure consistent scaling and improve model convergence. The dataset was randomly split into training (60%), validation (20%), and testing (20%) sets in order to preserve the class distribution during splits. This well-structured dataset provides a good basis for gauging how well the AGBO-CP optimization technique predicts cardiovascular disease. Table 2 depicts the UCI dataset features.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### 3.1.3 Breast Cancer Wisconsin Diagnostic Dataset (WBCD)

The third dataset provided was the Wisconsin Breast Cancer Diagnostic (WBCD) dataset, consisting of digital images of fine needle aspirates (FNAs) of breast tumors and 569 breast cancer biopsy cases. A binary classification problem is formed by the tagging of each sample as benign (357 cases) or malignant (212 cases). These groups are the thirty numeric attributes calculated from an image of a breast mass: Mean values are radius, texture, area, perimeter, smoothness, compactness, concavity, symmetry, and fractal dimension; standard error for all the above aspects; worst values: maximum value across the entire FNA image for each feature. After normalization, each feature has a real value ranging from 0 to 1. Since there were no missing values in the raw dataset, preparation proceeded smoothly. For class balancing, the dataset was split into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% test sets by stratifying it. Table 3 shows the WBCD dataset features.

Table 3: WBCD dataset features.

|---|---|---|---|
| Radius (mean) | Mean of distances from center to points on perimeter | Numeric | ~6 to ~30 |
| Texture (mean) | Standard deviation of gray-scale values | Numeric | ~10 to ~40 |
| Perimeter (mean) | Mean size of tumor perimeter | Numeric | ~40 to ~200 |
| Area (mean) | Mean size of tumor area | Numeric | ~150 to ~2,500 |
| Smoothness (mean) | Local variation in radius lengths | Numeric | ~0.05 to ~0.2 |
| Compactness (mean) | Measure of compactness of cell | Numeric | ~0.02 to ~0.35 |
