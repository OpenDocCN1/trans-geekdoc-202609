## 5 Experimental Results

To validate high-performance model training and reproducibility, the CareRecNet framework was experimentally tested in an entire computing environment. TensorFlow 2.8 and PyTorch 1.10 libraries were used in the implementation that used Python 3.9 for neural network building and training. A workstation-class computer with an Intel Xeon Gold 6226R processor, 64 GB of system RAM and an NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU with 32 GB of video RAM were used for experiments. This configuration gave CareRecNet the computational muscle that it needed to cope with the extensive multi-modal data sets and intricate model architectures.

A selection of benchmark models from various algorithmic frameworks was considered for testing; an SVM classifier as a classic machine learning baseline; a decision tree-based recommendation model that estimates rule-based performance; and a classic LSTM network that processes sequential health data. To be able to make a fair evaluation, the models were optimized and trained with the same train-test splits. Table 2 shows the process iterations.

The evaluation metrics selected were accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score; these measures indicated the appropriateness of treatment suggestions as well as the consistency between detecting true positives and rejecting false negatives and false positives. Recall estimated how well the model was able to recall all relevant treatments, accuracy calculated the overall correct classification rate, precision measured the proportion of relevant treatment suggestions among all positive predictions, and the F1-score gave the harmonic mean of precision and recall, offering an overall measure of performance.

From Figure 2, with a staggering 91.6% accuracy, much higher than the baseline models, the accuracy plot clearly illustrates CareRecNet’s superiority in treatment suggestion tasks. At 84.2%, the conventional LSTM model was better than the decision tree-based model, which was at 79.4%. At 83.6%, the SVM model's accuracy was moderate. This shows how effective CareRecNet's multimodal data fusion and hybrid framework is in performing better compared to other traditional deep learning and machine learning techniques by 7.4% to 12.2%, which indicates it is better at predicting the correct treatments.

Precision is the metric of how many of the positive predictions by all models are accurate. From Figure 3, the proposed system had the highest precision metric of 93.1%. CareRecNet demonstrated that the majority of its suggested therapies are applicable and suitable in the clinical setting. This is against the 85.7% accuracy of the baseline LSTM, 78.5% accuracy of the decision tree, and 82.9% accuracy of the SVM. Compared to other models, improvements of CareRecNet are at 7.4% to 14.6%, indicating that it is effective in minimizing false positive suggestions, a critical function of healthcare, which avoids damaging or unnecessary therapy.

The recall of the model captures its capacity to recognize all of the relevant therapy courses. From Figure 4, with a recall of 90.4%, CareRecNet was highly sensitive to accurately identifying patient therapies. SVM ranked slightly above at 84.1% and decision tree at 80.2%, while traditional LSTM ranked at 83.0%. By better identifying a significantly higher percentage of accurate therapies, the memory of CareRecNet surpasses the others by a ratio of about 6.3% to 10.2%, lowering the chances of missed or forgotten therapeutic avenues.

F1-score provides a general measure of model performance through attainment of balance between precision and recollection. CareRecNet leads with 91.7% F1-score, which indicates its well-rebalanced ability to correctly identify treatments and prevent false recommendations. The F1 scores of SVM, decision tree, and traditional LSTM were 83.5%, 79.3%, and 84.3%, respectively. As a result, CareRecNet outperforms these models by 7.4% to 12.4% in F1-score, which justifies its general robustness and dependability in clinical decision support.

The envisioned CareRecNet framework's complete integration of clinical knowledge representation and temporal modeling is a paradigm shift from conventional treatment recommendation systems. CareRecNet utilizes a BiLSTM-based architecture to capture complex temporal dynamics along patient health trajectories, as opposed to static classifiers or conventional sequence models. This enables the model to capture how variations in vitals, diagnostics, and treatments change over time.

Also, the system can be embedded with medical domain knowledge like drug-disease mappings, comorbidity correlations, and contraindications, thanks to the presence of a clinical knowledge graph, enhancing clinical relevance and safety. Collectively, these advances produce quantifiable performance gains across metrics like accuracy (91.6%), precision (93.1%), recall (90.4%), and F1-score (91.7%), far surpassing state-of-the-art baselines. But not without some challenges along the way.

To cope with noise, missing values, and time misalignment, integrating disparate data sources (e.g., lab tests, wearable sensor data, and EHRs) needed significant preprocessing and synchronization. Furthermore, building and updating a precise clinical knowledge graph is also an arduous task involving expert curatorial effort and ongoing updates to mirror evolving treatment procedures. Subsequent versions can further increase CareRecNet's flexibility and reliability by adding real-time streaming health data, supporting multilingual and inter-hospital compatibility, and integrating doctor review-based feedback loops. Besides, research on federated learning frameworks would enable training to be carried out across institutions without the sharing of patient privacy, allowing scalability. Finally, CareRecNet provides a good basis for smart, explainable, and context-aware treatment recommendation systems, guiding digital health towards evidence-based and personalized clinical decision support.

## 6 Conclusion

To enhance treatment suggestions in today's digital health systems, this study introduced CareRecNet, an advanced, patient-focused AI model. The proposed approach effectively encapsulates structured medical knowledge such as drug-disease interactions and comorbidities as well as sequential relationships in patient health trajectories by integrating BiLSTM-based temporal modeling with a clinical knowledge graph. CareRecNet draws from multimodal sources of data, including wearable sensor data, lab reports, and electronic health records, to offer highly personalized and clinically interpretable treatment suggestions compared to conventional models that rely solely on static EHR components or handcrafted rules.
