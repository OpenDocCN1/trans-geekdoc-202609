An effective, multistage ML algorithm named Adaptive Gradient Boosted Optimization for Clinical Prediction (AGBO-CP) was developed, as shown in Figure 1 and Figure 2, to enhance clinical prediction accuracy in healthcare settings. It integrates dynamic adaptive techniques like learning rate modulation, feature selection, and data balance into the principles of gradient boosting. The model preprocesses clinical data first by applying SMOTE to address class imbalance, dealing with missing values, and feature normalization. Once recursive elimination and mutual information are blended in a multilayered feature selection approach, PCA is employed for dimensionality reduction.

AGBO-CP is a multi round boosting method where residual-based updates and an adaptive learning rate, dependent on model convergence behavior, are employed to recursively refine weak learners (decision trees). With the inclusion of adaptive weight update and gradient correction based on momentum, the optimization module is enhanced over regular gradient boosting to attain better convergence and to avoid overfitting. To realize good generalization on unseen data, the algorithm fine-tunes its parameters through rigorous validation and early stopping. With its high precision, recall, and overall accuracy, AGBO-CP is particularly suited for difficult clinical prediction problems due to its thorough and ingenious methodology.

# 4 Experimental Results

To ensure repeatability and performance integrity, the proposed Adaptive Gradient Boosted Optimization for Clinical Prediction (AGBO-CP) approach was experimentally analyzed in a well-managed and carefully tested computing environment. Utilizing modules such as Scikit-learn, XGBoost, Optuna, and PyCaret, the implementation was carried out using Python 3.10 on a computer with an Intel Core i9-12900 K processor, 64GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3,090 GPU. MIMIC-III (Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care) and UCI Heart Disease datasets, which involve high-dimensional and heterogeneous patient information across several attributes, e.g., demographics, lab values, vitals, and diagnoses, were the actual clinical datasets for which the AGBO-CP model was designed to process and analyze.

A 5-fold stratified cross-validation method was employed to ensure validation of the model's efficacy with balanced representation of target classes across folds. Clinically interpretable performance measures like Accuracy, Precision, Recall (Sensitivity), Specificity, F1-score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) were utilized. These measures provide a comprehensive evaluation of the ability of the model to identify patients that are sick and those that are not with accuracy, a very critical consideration in medical diagnosis. A number of baseline ML models such as RF, SVM, and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) were compared against AGBO-CP. To ensure fair benchmarking, grid search or Bayesian hyperparameter tuning were applied to optimize individual models.
