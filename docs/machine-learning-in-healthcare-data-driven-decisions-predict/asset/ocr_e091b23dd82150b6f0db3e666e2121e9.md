The hybrid model captures complex, nonlinear interactions across data modalities, offering a holistic understanding of stroke recovery trajectories.

The LR model, serving as the baseline, yields the lowest scores across all metrics, with an AUC-ROC of 0.76, F1-score of 0.68, MAE of 0.61, and QWK of 0.62. Its linear nature limits its ability to model complex, nonlinear relationships common in stroke data, particularly when interactions between clinical and imaging features are critical. Despite being interpretable and computationally efficient, LR lacks the capacity to process high-dimensional or multimodal inputs effectively, resulting in poor predictive performance for both risk and functional outcomes.

RF performs moderately well, with an AUC-ROC of 0.81 and F1-score of 0.73 for risk stratification, along with a MAE of 0.55 and QWK of 0.70 for mRS prediction. It improves upon LR by handling nonlinearities and feature interactions more effectively. However, as a purely tabular model, it lacks the ability to leverage unstructured data like imaging, which may limit its accuracy for clinical problems where visual patterns (e.g., lesion location or size) significantly influence outcomes. It also offers only moderate interpretability through FI measures.

The deep CNN model, trained solely on imaging data, achieves relatively good performance in outcome prediction (MAE: 0.50 and QWK: 0.68), suggesting that spatial and visual features in neuroimaging are informative for functional recovery. However, its AUC-ROC (0.78) and F1-score (0.71) for risk stratification are lower, likely due to the absence of contextual clinical data such as age, comorbidities, or stroke severity scores. Without integrating clinical parameters, the CNN's view of the patient condition remains incomplete, and its performance suffers in tasks requiring broader context.

Multimodal ensemble model, a fusion of individual models’ output trained on various data modalities, achieves an AUC-ROC of 0.88 and F1-score of 0.80. It also achieves good functional prediction (MAE: 0.45 and QWK: 0.78). Although it capitalizes on the use of multiple sources of data, having a late fusion approach of aggregating predictions as opposed to features constrains rich cross-modal interaction. This model can possibly neglect detailed cross-modal patterns that the hybrid model would learn. Nevertheless, it performs better than single-modality models and shows the potential utility of multimodal methods.
