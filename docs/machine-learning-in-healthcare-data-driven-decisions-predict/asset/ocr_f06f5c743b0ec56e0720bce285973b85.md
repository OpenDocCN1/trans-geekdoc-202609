These findings raise an interesting research question: Is it possible for blockchain and FL to be combined into a model of safe, intelligent, and privacy-conscious exchange of medical information that does not suffer from the flaws of traditional models? This is the main driving force behind the current study. A few recent experiments tried to bridge FL and blockchain. Blockchain has been utilized to guarantee fair contribution in FL environments or to align the aggregation of model updates. Nevertheless, the majority of these practices either disregard the processing cost on the blockchain or do not have sufficient identity authentication processes in place to securely manage members.

Moreover, although being central elements within healthcare systems, user consent and ownership of data are usually neglected. Identity and access control systems are one of the limitations. In decentralized settings, traditional centralized identity systems are not sufficient, particularly where permission management and empowerment of patients are high. Thus, self-sovereign identity (SSI) systems are the next step in decentralized identity management. With the support of verifiable credentials (VCs) and decentralized identifiers (DIDs), SSI enables users (patients or institutions) to manage their own identification credentials without central control.

All actors can be safely authenticated and each access event tracked without revealing private credentials when SSI is implemented as part of a federated blockchain-based healthcare ecosystem. SSI, FL, and blockchain form a powerful trinity that provides secure identity and access management, privacy, and distributed trust. By enabling AI-based research and diagnosis without undermining institutional data sovereignty or patient confidentiality, this integral approach has the potential to completely revolutionize healthcare data exchange.

The central driving force behind this research is the need to develop an architecture for federated healthcare analytics that is scalable, secure, privacy-preserving, and rule-compliant. Through the combination of permissioned blockchain and FL with SSI-based identity management, the proposed MediChain-FL architecture fills this gap and provides a solid foundation for smart healthcare data exchange.

The two main objectives of the proposed research work are:

+   1. To develop a secure and privacy-preserving healthcare data exchange framework by integrating FL with a permissioned blockchain infrastructure, ensuring that patient data remains localized while enabling real-time, decentralized, and tamper-proof collaboration across multiple healthcare institutions.

+   2. To implement a decentralized identity management system using SSI and smart contracts, providing fine-grained access control, verifiable authentication, and regulatory-compliant audit trails for all participants involved in collaborative medical data analytics.
