# 2 Related Study

Recent studies on privacy-guaranteeing healthcare systems have more thoroughly examined how blockchain technology and FL can possibly work together towards overcoming scalability, privacy, and data-sharing issues. A basic architecture that combines FL and blockchain technology to secure private IoT health data was put forth in [1]. While secure aggregation and distributed ownership are highlighted here, effective consensus mechanisms and dynamic identification verification are not enabled. Sai et al. [2] presents an innovative architecture that combines FL with NFTs to improve diagnosis, reliability and traceability of data within intelligent healthcare environments. While the method promises privacy guarantees, the technique incurs extra blockchain overhead and does not handle model poisoning attacks.

A comprehensive review of privacy-preserving techniques with FL in smart healthcare is brought forth by the survey in [3]. It refers to glaring gaps within latency, interoperability, and trust management, opening the door for proposals of solutions that combine identity frameworks with blockchain. An intelligent hospital-specific federated blockchain-based learning framework is also suggested by Ngan Van et al. [4]. It identifies the advantage of decentralized orchestration and device-side learning but does not analyze scalability and model drift across different healthcare scenarios. In the meantime, Abou El Houda et al. [5] offer a blockchain-based distributed cooperation mechanism to eradicate trust problems among medical professionals during federated learning. Though their consensus protocol achieves highest fairness, it is more latency-sensitive and less flexible in low-resource scenarios.

For the protection of healthcare data exchanges between institutions, an exchange infrastructure for patient information is formed by applying blockchain in [6]. Although the system guarantees access control and auditability, the system is inflexible in the sense that it does not accommodate changing patient consent models but is based on static role-based permissions instead. A privacy-preserving cloud-integrated care framework is introduced in [7] considering secure blockchain-based exchange of health information among cloud infrastructures. However, collaborative analytics and data localization in real time are not taken into consideration in this framework. Sezer et al. [8] provides PFPChain, i.e., a blockchain-facilitated privacy-preserving FL model for sensor networks with model integrity checking and privacy-preserving updating. Though appropriate for IoT-based systems, its practical clinical use has yet to be investigated.

The study in [9] investigates edge intelligence for privacy protection in dispersed healthcare systems with FL. Energy efficiency and low-latency inference are its key features but it lacks strong, tamper-proof audit trails. Alzubi et al. [10] achieved with regards to privacy-protecting EHR sharing over Cloud- IIoT infrastructure through combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with FL and blockchain.
