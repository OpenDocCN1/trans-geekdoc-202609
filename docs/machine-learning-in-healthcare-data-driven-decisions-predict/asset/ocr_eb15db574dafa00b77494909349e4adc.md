A privacy-protected high-performance blockchain-supported e-healthcare data-sharing system is realized in [12]. The paradigm has poor anti-collusion protection among the owners but synchronizes multiparty data access with low communication cost. To compensate for transaction latency and provide immutability of data in the context of collaborative training, a newer approach [13] emphasizes scalability and incorporates efficient block structures. User control is reduced unless patient-oriented models of identity exist. FL's principal benefit of data localization is undermined by an AI and blockchain-based hybrid approach to secure IoT medical data transfer investigated in [14]. The model gives decentralized storage and top-level encryption but has a centralized learning aspect.

The collaborative service model presented in [15] offers trustworthy medical cooperation services by integrating blockchain technology with FL. It lacks adaptive control over changing healthcare legislation, but it offers multiparty analytics that have inherent trust mechanisms. Focusing on real-time threat prevention, the anomaly detection-based protection system in [16] suggests an access control layer for cloud-based medical systems. But it still relies heavily on the cloud and is not as decentralized as blockchain. Meanwhile, Lakhani et al. [17] develop a blockchain and FL hybrid model for IoMT fraud detection. Though it does not utilize SSI protocols for anti-credential spoofing, it does propose fraud detection with historical transaction modeling.

Decentralized diagnosis and record-sharing are conducted with FL in [18] within a privacy-preserving smart healthcare system. Though it prioritizes data security via encrypted aggregation and local model training, it does not have end-to-end audit trails and multi-jurisdictional compliance requirements. Blockchain-based smart contracts are used in the smart healthcare exchange paradigm in [19] to implement EMR exchange regulation ensuring privacy. The consensus mechanism of the system is computationally costly and not suitable for medical environments where time is of the essence, although it provides traceability and integrity of data. Alsamhi et al. [20] discusses decentralized sharing of information using blockchain technology in combination with FL to enable hospitals to cooperate and train models without disclosing data. It utilizes static identity management, though.

Singh et al. [21] suggest a less power-hungry blockchain-facilitated FL in smart healthcare. It offers lightweight cryptographic computations and trust-based participation scoring with notable communication cost savings. It lacks fine-grained access control and possesses a plain identification mechanism. Taken collectively, what they prove is that there is increasing sentiment that federated intelligence must be brought into decentralized, impenetrable infrastructures for the sharing of medical information.
