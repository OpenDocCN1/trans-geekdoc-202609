# 4 Experimental Results

The proposed Reinforced Multi-Objective Optimization Framework for Healthcare Decision Intelligence (RMO-HDI) was experimentally evaluated utilizing Python 3.10 with TensorFlow 2.13, Stable-Baselines3, and NSGA-II (via DEAP) frameworks in a controlled simulation environment. A 16-core Intel Xeon CPU, 128 GB of RAM, and two NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs running Ubuntu 22.04 LTS were used to run the simulations, allowing for parallelized training and real-time inference evaluation. PhysioNet’s eICU Collaborative Research Database and MIMIC-IV (version 2.2) were included in the dataset, which comprised more than 35,000 patient records, more than 200 clinical variables, and multi-temporal modalities such as lab results, vital signs, and medication history.

Four significant evaluation metrics are false positive rate (FPR), diagnostic accuracy (DA), treatment recommendation accuracy (TRA), and convergence time (CT) for optimal policy stabilization that were utilized to thoroughly assess the efficacy of the proposed RMO-HDI framework. They are selected to test whether such a system can generate recommendations fast, accurately, and specifically to a patient. Four cutting-edge comparison techniques were applied in order to provide a solid baseline: (i) Standard expert systems are represented by the rule-based clinical pathway engine (RB-CPE); (ii) ensemble-based learning is reflected by the static multi-class XGBoost (MC-XGB); (iii) sequence-aware deep learning, without adaptive learning, is represented by the attention-based LSTM with fixed weights (ALSTM-FW); and (iv) a deep multimodal neural architecture that integrates temporal and spatial patterns is the multi-head CNN-RNN hybrid (MHCNN-RNN). A clear performance landscape was presented by this multi-model comparison, emphasizing the significant gains in RMO-HDI's training efficiency, diagnostic reliability, recommendation accuracy, and error minimization.

From Figure 2, in every parameter, the suggested RMO-HDI performed better than the baselines. In particular, its accuracy in making treatment recommendations was 92.3%, while those of RB-CPE, MC-XGB, ALSTM-FW, and MHCNN-RNN were 70.4%, 75.6%, and 80.1%, respectively.
