These societal dimensions will need to be addressed together by the technologists, clinicians, legislators, and the public, thereby ensuring that ML would improve the health of everyone. Ultimately, a multidisciplinary approach to the responsible design and use of ML in healthcare is warranted, one that acknowledges the technical, clinical, ethical, regulatory, and societal ramifications in concert to ensure that AI is designed and used to the benefit and fairness of all in society.

While enhancing human-AI collaboration is key, ML is not seen as a replacement for human clinicians, and ML models examine data for patterns and make predictions without the relevant clinical judgment, compassion, and contextual understanding that are critical to patient care of quality. For instance, an AI algorithm may indicate an area of suspicious concern on a medical image, while the radiologist with their medical training and knowledge of the patient's clinical history shall render a formal diagnosis.
