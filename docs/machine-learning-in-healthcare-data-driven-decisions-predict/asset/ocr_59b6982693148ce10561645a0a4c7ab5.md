# 6 Future Directions

While the hybrid ML model envisioned in this work shows promising results, a number of future directions of research and development are present. First, broadening the data to cover additional heterogeneous patient populations across various hospitals and geographic areas would enhance generalizability and robustness. Multicenter validation trials need to be conducted to test the model under conditions of real-world clinical practice such as imaging protocol variability, electronic health records, and stroke practice. Second, temporal modeling must be incorporated to better reflect the dynamic progression of stroke recovery. The incorporation of recurrent or transformer modules can potentially predict recovery courses over time, and the model can be made increasingly useful for long-term care planning on a clinical basis. Other modalities of data like genetic biomarkers, blood proteomics, or speech/language testing could provide more relevant patient profiles and enhance predictive accuracy. Third, whereas existing approaches such as SHAP and Grad-CAM offer insight, there could be emphasis on creating domain-specific interpretability modules that follow clinical reasoning steps, to enable trust establishment and clinical adoption. Clinical workflow integration is also a top priority. Developing an intuitive, real-time software interface or decision support tool that can be directly integrated into hospital information systems would enable clinicians to use model predictions at high-stakes decision points, for example, during discharge planning or selecting rehabilitation strategy. Lastly, fairness and model bias ethics need to be stringently tested, especially in underrepresented groups. Ensuring transparency through open auditing procedures and fairness-aware training procedures will be needed as these systems proceed toward application in medicine.
