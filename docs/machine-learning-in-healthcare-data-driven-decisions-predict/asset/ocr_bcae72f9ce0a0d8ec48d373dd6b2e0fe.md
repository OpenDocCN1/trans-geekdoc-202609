These results are clear enough to demonstrate how predictive models using ML can learn to recognize risk markers and underlying patterns within structured clinical data.

There are concerns over whether the models generalize to broader, more varied populations since the majority of them were trained using very small or imbalanced datasets [6, 13]. Although boosting methods and ensemble classifiers such as RF have the tendency to overcome this issue, they are still highly dependent on pretreatment of data and tuning, which varies significantly across implementations. It is challenging to evaluate the diagnostic reliability of these studies [8, 11, 17] with high accuracy but without exhaustive analysis with clinically relevant metrics like precision, recall, F1-score, or ROC-area under the curve (AUC).

Apart from this, although precise, some models were black-box and noninterpretable in nature, a prerequisite for their clinical uptake and acceptance [15]. Apart from this, to prevent redundancy and enhance model explainability, not many studies employed domain knowledge or feature selection techniques explicitly [13, 20]. The non-availability of standard benchmarking datasets and evaluation protocols, preventing direct model comparison, was another common shortfall in some reports. While published research to date shows the possibility of ML to diagnose CKD in its early stages, shortcomings caused by the biased dataset, lack of interpretability, and over-reliance on hyperparameter tuning indicate the urgent need for a more credible, generalizable, and interpretable method. Emboldened by these limitations, this work introduces a novel ML model named HRAB, and the model is designed to balance clinical usability, interpretability, and performance. The emphasis lies on incorporating strict feature selection and regularization techniques to prevent overfitting and to render the model interpretable by physicians. Overall, though previous studies have set a good foundation, the above shortcomings in previous studies justify the construction of a better and context-aware ML framework for predicting early CKD.
