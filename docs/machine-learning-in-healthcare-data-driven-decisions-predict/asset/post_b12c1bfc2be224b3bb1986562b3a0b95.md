### 2.3 Expert System for Diabetics Prediction

An expert system for diagnosing type II diabetes, a deadly condition prevalent in most nations, was created using ensemble ML techniques. The purpose is to investigate and compare different classification approaches to decide correctly if a person is diabetic or not. Fifteen classifiers were considered, with five primary approaches being: ANN, SVM, KNN, NB, and ensemble methods. EL takes advantage of the fact that many different models have strengths that complement each other, increasing accuracy and minimizing majority voting misclassifications. There were 400 participants across various regions and 10 physiological features, 5 being numerical and 5 nominal. The ensemble approaches performed better than the individual classifiers with a very high accuracy of 98.60%. This clearly points toward the strength of model combination to deliver enhanced predictions. A simple graphical interface for prediction of diabetes in real time using user input of the 10 features is used. The system can be implemented as a clinical DSS to aid healthcare professionals to diagnose diabetes early and accurately. With timely observation, it facilitates improved patient outcomes and treatment plans and it demonstrates the effectiveness of EL in medical diagnosis [15].

### 2.4 Super Learner Model for Early Diabetes Detection

A super EL model has been designed for early DM diagnosis, a rapidly increasing world-wide health problem. Conventional diagnosis is time-consuming and expensive but ensemble ML method provides quicker and more affordable solutions. The new model, termed a super learner, is based on cross-validation and combines the predictive results of an ensemble of ML algorithms in order to enhance accuracy. The super learner model combines four foundation learners – LR, DT, RF, and gradient boosting – and SVM as the meta-learner for combining. The performances of the model were tested on three datasets. The super learner model obtained good measures of accuracy: 99.6% for early-stage diabetes data, 92% for PIMA data, and 98% for 130 US hospitals data. The outcomes outperformed one base learner, proving the capability and practicability of the super learner model in real applications. The study as a whole demonstrates the capability of super learner algorithms in supporting early, correct, and extensive detection of DM [16].

### 2.5 Hybrid Deep Ensemble Diabetes Prediction

Diabetes is a critical world health problem and as such the demand for advanced predictive techniques in the sense of providing early accurate detection. In this study, the predictive accuracy of a range of ML and DL algorithms is compared for predicting the development of diabetes from a comprehensive clinical and demographic data set. The traditional ML classifiers such as DTs, RF, KNN, and XGBoost were attempted initially, and ensemble-based methods were found to be high performers in terms of predictive accuracy. For better prediction performance, DL-based models such as simple DNN, CNN, and RNN were evaluated. While these models were found useful particularly in identifying correct instances, these models’ ability to surpass ensemble methods in overall classification accuracy was not realized. The most closely related result of the work was the development of a DL Meta Learner, combining RF and gradient boosting model predictions. The approach achieved close to perfect accuracy, showing the power of combining DL’s feature extraction ability with high pattern identification from ensemble methods. The results confirm hybrid model incorporation in diabetes prediction and present an inspiring alternative for cutting-edge medical informatics and supporting medical professionals to deliver timely and customized care [17].

### 2.6 Advanced Ensemble Modeling for Early Diabetes Prediction

With the growing incidence of diabetes, there is a need to create standard and punctual prediction models, particularly for women who may have specific risk factors. A strong ensemble of deep ML models was proposed to identify diabetes in its initial phase, providing doctors efficient tools to enable early diagnosis and enhance patient results. Some of the powerful classifiers such as RF with boosting algorithms were chosen as base learners for stacking ensemble models because they had high prediction precision. Before training the model, the dataset was heavily preprocessed in order to have optimal performance and robustness. The suggested ensemble model achieved an accuracy of 92.91%, which is very similar to, and in certain cases superior to, current procedures for predicting diabetes. The study also applies SHAP to make the models interpretable, and meaningful information is obtained. Such interpretation will be important in deriving clinical confidence and knowing the predictors of risk of diabetes. In general, the outcome of this study will be likely to be useful to the healthcare providers, researchers, students, and stakeholders with an interpretable and effective prediction model. All these advances will be able to enable early intervention planning, enhance personalized patient care, and ultimately lead to improved diabetes control in clinic [18].

### 2.7 CatBoost-Enhanced Ensemble Model for Accurate Diabetes Prediction

Diabetes is a serious worldwide health issue that is being addressed with a very sophisticated forecasting model based on multiple ML algorithms. The model uses the general algorithms such as LR, SVM, Naïve Bayes, and RF and the aggressive ensemble methods including boosting and bagging methods. These ensemble methods pool different base learners to increase the precision and stability of the forecasts. Based on a real Kaggle dataset and coded in Python, model performance was evaluated using accuracy, sensitivity, and confusion matrix. Among all the ensemble techniques attempted, CatBoost scored the highest accuracy of 95.4%. The results reflect the efficiency of EL, especially CatBoost, in providing steady and accurate diabetes predictions to aid early diagnosis and better patient care [19].

### 2.8 EGG-Based Type-II Diabetes Prediction

DM, especially Type-II, has emerged as a serious world-wide health issue with highly prevalent rates in migrant Indians. An increase in Type-II diabetes is more or less due to lifestyle, senility, and changed eating habits. To address the critical demand for early and reliable diagnosis, this study proposes a novel, noninvasive method on the basis of electrogastogram (EGG) signals. The study employs recording of EGG signals from diabetic patients and healthy patients between 50 and 65 years of age with a three-electrode EGG recording system. The obtained data is processed with sophisticated AI methods to obtain important features that distinguish between diabetic and nondiabetic EGG traces. The study involves the use of explainable AI, that is, SHAP to provide model prediction explainability, and uses meta-heuristic algorithms in FS optimization. The proposed classifier is critically contrasted and compared with conventional models such as RF and conventional XGBoost. Experimental results exhibit the proposed model with a maximum classification accuracy of 95.8%, maximum sensitivity of 100%, and specificity of 92.3%. The study confirms that the suggested classifier, coupled with explainable AI and robust feature selection, is a valid, understandable, and practical method of early Type-II diabetes diagnosis using EGG signals. It is highly promising for real-time noninvasive medical screening, especially in risk-prone populations [20].
