### 3.2 AGBO-CP Algorithm

- Step 1: Let \( D = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n \) denote the clinical dataset where \( x_i \in R^d \) is a \( d \)-dimensional feature vector and \( y_i \in \{0, 1\} \) is the target label indicating disease presence.

- Step 2: For each feature \( x_j \) in \( x_i \), impute missing values using feature-wise \( k \)-nearest neighbor (KNN) imputation or mean strategy

  \( x_{i,j} = \begin{cases} x_{i,j}, \text{if } x_{i,j} \text{ is observed} \\ \frac{1}{k} \sum_{l=1}^k x_{l,j}, \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \) (1)

- Step 3: Apply z-score standardization

  \( x'_{i,j} = \frac{x_{i,j} - \mu_j}{\sigma_j} \) (2)

- Step 4: Split into training \( D_{train} \), validation \( D_{val} \), and testing \( D_{test} \) sets in 60:20:20 ratio.

- Step 5: Set number of estimators \( N \), learning rate \( \eta \), initial prediction \( \gamma^{(0)}_i = \text{logit}^{-1}(y^-) \), and initialize decision trees \( T_k \).

- Step 6: For each instance

  \( r^{(0)}_i = y_i - \gamma^{(0)}_i \) (3)

- Step 7: Iterative boosting loop For \( t = 1 \) to \( N \):

- Step 8: Fit base learner \( T_t \) on residuals

  \( T_t = \arg \min_T \sum_{i=1}^n L \left( r^{(t-1)}_i, T(x_i) \right) \) (4)

![](img/106ef0dba620d314f9842503e8ef40c8_385_0.png)

- Step 9: Update predictions

  \( y^{\hat{t}_i} = y^{\hat{t}_{(t-1)i}} + \eta \cdot T_t(x_i) \) (5)

- Step 10: Apply sigmoid for classification output

  \( p^{\hat{t}_i} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-y^{\hat{t}_i}}} \) (6)

![](img/106ef0dba620d314f9842503e8ef40c8_386_0.png)

Figure 2: Proposed AGBO-CP process – II.

- Step 11: Recalculate residuals

  $$r_i^{(t)} = y_i - p^i$$ (7)

- Step 12: Adaptive learning rate adjustment Update \(\eta_t\) based on loss curvature

  $$\eta_t = \frac{1}{1 + \alpha \cdot \frac{\partial^2 L}{\partial y^2}}$$ (8)

- Step 13: Feature importance regularization

  Introduce a feature weight matrix $W \in R^d$, update via

  $$W_j = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^N |G_j^t|}{\sum_{j=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^N |G_j^t|}$$ (9)

  where $G_j^t$ is the gradient contribution of feature $j$ in iteration $t$.

- Step 14: Perform SHAP-based interpretability tracking at each iteration to monitor clinical relevance.

- Step 15: Apply Genetic Optimization (GO) to tune hyperparameters

  $$\max_{(\eta, \max\_depth, \lambda)} \text{AUC}(D_{\text{val}})$$ (10)

- Step 16: Crossover and mutation on hyperparameter vectors

  $$\theta^{\text{new}} = \theta^a \oplus \theta^b \text{ (mutation with probability } p_m\text{)}$$ (11)

- Step 17: Evaluate fitness for each candidate \(\theta_i\) on the validation set.

- Step 18: Select top-$k$ candidates using tournament selection.

- Step 19: Update model with optimized parameters from GO.

- Step 20: Incorporate early stopping; terminate if:

  $$L^{(t)} - L^{(t-1)} < \delta \text{ for } \tau \text{ consecutive steps}$$ (12)

- Step 21: Introduce cost-sensitive reweighting for imbalanced classes

  $$w_i = \begin{cases} \frac{n}{2 \cdot n_1}, & y_i = 1 \\ \frac{n}{2 \cdot n_0}, & y_i = 0 \end{cases}$$ (13)

- Step 22: Ensemble the final top-5 best performing trees using weighted averaging

  $$\hat{y}^i = \sum_{j=1}^5 \omega_j \cdot T_j(x_i), \sum \omega_j = 1$$ (14)

- Step 23: Apply confidence calibration (e.g., Platt scaling or isotonic regression) for probability outputs

  $$\hat{p}^{\text{cal}}_i = \text{Platt}(\hat{p}^i)$$ (15)

- Step 24: Output the final trained AGBO-CP model and deploy with an explainable risk score dashboard, integrated using SHAP explanations for clinical interpretability.

An effective, multistage ML algorithm named Adaptive Gradient Boosted Optimization for Clinical Prediction (AGBO-CP) was developed, as shown in Figure 1 and Figure 2, to enhance clinical prediction accuracy in healthcare settings. It integrates dynamic adaptive techniques like learning rate modulation, feature selection, and data balance into the principles of gradient boosting. The model preprocesses clinical data first by applying SMOTE to address class imbalance, dealing with missing values, and feature normalization. Once recursive elimination and mutual information are blended in a multilayered feature selection approach, PCA is employed for dimensionality reduction.

AGBO-CP is a multi round boosting method where residual-based updates and an adaptive learning rate, dependent on model convergence behavior, are employed to recursively refine weak learners (decision trees). With the inclusion of adaptive weight update and gradient correction based on momentum, the optimization module is enhanced over regular gradient boosting to attain better convergence and to avoid overfitting. To realize good generalization on unseen data, the algorithm fine-tunes its parameters through rigorous validation and early stopping. With its high precision, recall, and overall accuracy, AGBO-CP is particularly suited for difficult clinical prediction problems due to its thorough and ingenious methodology.

## 4 Experimental Results

To ensure repeatability and performance integrity, the proposed Adaptive Gradient Boosted Optimization for Clinical Prediction (AGBO-CP) approach was experimentally analyzed in a well-managed and carefully tested computing environment. Utilizing modules such as Scikit-learn, XGBoost, Optuna, and PyCaret, the implementation was carried out using Python 3.10 on a computer with an Intel Core i9-12900K processor, 64GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. MIMIC-III (Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care) and UCI Heart Disease datasets, which involve high-dimensional and heterogeneous patient information across several attributes, e.g., demographics, lab values, vitals, and diagnoses, were the actual clinical datasets for which the AGBO-CP model was designed to process and analyze.

A 5-fold stratified cross-validation method was employed to ensure validation of the model's efficacy with balanced representation of target classes across folds. Clinically interpretable performance measures like Accuracy, Precision, Recall (Sensitivity), Specificity, F1-score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) were utilized. These measures provide a comprehensive evaluation of the ability of the model to identify patients that are sick and those that are not with accuracy, a very critical consideration in medical diagnosis. A number of baseline ML models such as RF, SVM, and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) were compared against AGBO-CP. To ensure fair benchmarking, grid search or Bayesian hyperparameter tuning were applied to optimize individual models.
