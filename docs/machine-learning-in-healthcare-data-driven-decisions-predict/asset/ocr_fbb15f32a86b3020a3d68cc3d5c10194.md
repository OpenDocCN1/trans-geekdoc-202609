One-hot encoding of categorical fields like diagnosis codes (ICD-9) and drugs yielded a sparse but organized matrix that could be fed into deep learning models.

A bespoke NLP pipeline was utilized to preprocess the unstructured textual data, i.e., discharge summaries and physicians’ notes. Lemmatization, removing stop-words, tokenization, and domain-specific entity recognition with cTAKES and SciSpacy were all part of this process. Semantic integration with the structured attributes was supported through mapping the identified medical entities, symptoms, illnesses, and procedures to standard ontologies such as UMLS (Unified Medical Language System). In order to transform irregularly spaced events into fixed-size time series, temporal feature alignment was also carried out. A 24-h time frame was allocated to each patient episode, and the values were aggregated based on statistical functions (mean, max, and last seen) in order to take a single representation to model.

# 3.3 Feature Extraction

A new feature set was formed for model training following preprocessing with a focus on features known to affect treatment decisions. Feature extraction generated an enriched multimodal input vector for each patient through the aggregation of structured, derived metrics, and NLP-based concept identification.

+   The following feature categories were utilized in aggregating the structured data:
+   - Age, gender, ethnicity, insurance type, and admittance type are some of the demographics.
+   - Vital signs are temperature, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, respiration rate, and SpO₂.
+   - Laboratory results are creatinine, sodium, potassium, hemoglobin, glucose, and WBC count.
+   - Medications are administration of insulin, antihypertensives, anticoagulants, and antibiotics.
+   - Comorbidities are renal failure, COPD, diabetes, and hypertension (binary markers).
+   - Temporal trends are abnormal lab count, 24-hour change in vitals rate.

The NLP pipeline generated concept attributes for unstructured information based on clinical named entities that were derived from doctor notes. They consisted of diagnoses (“acute MI,” “sepsis”), symptoms (“chest pain,” “shortness of breath”), and treatment words (“dialysis,” “angioplasty”).
