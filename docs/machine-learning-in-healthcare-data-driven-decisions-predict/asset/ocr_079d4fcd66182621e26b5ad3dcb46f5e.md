These models can assist clinicians in decision-making, enhance early detection methods, and provide scalable techniques for population-level mental health screening.

Furthermore, confidentiality concerns patients and the sensitive character of mental health data limit access to large, diverse data sets, contributing to the challenge of having to analyze large samples and make generalization. Despite these challenges, greater access to electronic health records, mobile health applications, and online behavioral data have provided avenues for computational models to facilitate assessment and prediction of mental health. The last few years have provided new windows of opportunity to improve mental health healthcare, courtesy of the arrival of AI and ML approaches. Random forest (RF) [8], support vector machine (SVM) [9], logistic regression (LR) [10], and deep boosters like XGBoost [11] and LightGBM [12] are few of the most commonly applied ML algorithms for predicting mental health. These models have exhibited decent performance in diagnosing mental health disorders when trained on both clinical and non-clinical data.

However, several challenges exist in the proper utilization of these models in mental health informatics. Numerous current models fail to address class imbalance, a typical problem in medical datasets where positive instances of mental disorder may be fewer than healthy controls. In addition, interpretability of sophisticated ML models continues to be an issue of major concern, especially in medicine, where it is essential to have transparent and interpretable decision-support tools in order to make their responsible usage possible and gain confidence in AI-driven recommendations. More importantly, most current research in this area have been dedicated to applying single classifiers or standard ensemble methods like RFs.

Although these approaches have some benefits in predictive performance and stability, they might not be as flexible as required to deal with the heterogeneous nature of mental health data, which tends to have a mix of categorical, numerical, and textual features. More scalable, flexible, and interpretable ML architecture is yet to be developed. The objectives of this study are as follows:

To create, deploy, and test a novel ensemble-based prediction model framework to detect depression and anxiety by integrating heterogeneous ML algorithms with experience in dealing with complex and heterogeneous mental health data.

To analyze the proposed ensemble framework against existing benchmark models using various prediction performance metrics so that accuracy, robustness, and reliability could be ensured during mental health classification tasks.
