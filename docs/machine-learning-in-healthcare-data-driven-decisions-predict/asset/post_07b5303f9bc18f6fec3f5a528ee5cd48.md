
In Figure 4, with 95.9%, AGBO-CP again tops the list when it comes to sensitivity, which measures the actual positive rate or the ability to correctly classify patients with disease. This is 5.6 percentage points higher than RF’s 90.3% sensitivity and 8.1 percentage points higher than SVM’s 87.8%. Over this model, AGBO-CP enhances sensitivity by 6.3 percentage points, as XGBoost reflects a sensitivity of 89.6%. This heightened sensitivity demonstrates the capability of AGBO-CP to identify true cases of illness, which is highly important in clinical practice, as missing a positive case can lead to treatment delay.

AGBO-CP is also good in specificity, which is the true negative rate or the ability to correctly classify healthy individuals, with a score of 94.7%. From Figure 5, AGBO-CP is better by 2.9 percentage points in specificity compared to RF’s 91.8%. AGBO-CP’s improvement is 6.2 percentage points higher than SVM, whose specificity of 88.5% is far below. AGBO-CP has a margin of 3.8 percentage points, while XGBoost achieves 90.9% specificity. To avoid unnecessary anxiety and therapy in healthy patients, this gain in specificity reduces the likelihood of false positives.

The overall measure, called the AUC, indicates the ability of the classification model to perform as a whole at various threshold values. From Figure 6, with 97.3% AUC, AGBO-CP is seen to have outstanding discrimination capability between instances of disease and non-disease. This is an improvement of 4.1 percentage points and is better than RF’s AUC of 93.2%. With AUC of 90.5%, AGBO-CP is 6.8 percentage points higher than SVM. AGBO-CP is 4.6 percentage points higher than XGBoost, with AUC of 92.7%. AGBO-CP seems to make more reliable predictions at various classification thresholds, as seen from the higher AUC value.

The extensive comparison indicates that AGBO-CP consistently performs better than state-of-the-art ML models by margins of about 2.9% to 8.1% on all significant performance metrics. These improvements identify how effectively AGBO-CP’s adaptive gradient boosting and optimization techniques perform to enhance clinical prediction accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and diagnostic performance overall. These enhancements are necessary for real-world healthcare uses since clinical judgment and patient care directly rely on accuracy and reliability. The results support the development of AGBO-CP and further clinical verification by proving it to be a better algorithm for predictive modeling in the healthcare sector.

## 5 Conclusion

Through a sophisticated combination of ensemble learning and adaptive hyperparameter optimization methods, the AGBO-CP system effectively addresses the major limitations of traditional ML models in healthcare settings, including overfitting and poor parameter tuning. The clinical utility and robustness of AGBO-CP are attested to by the high performance measures achieved on popular healthcare datasets such as MIMIC-III and UCI Heart Disease, with 96.8% diagnostic accuracy, 95.9% sensitivity, 94.7% specificity, and AUC of 97.3%. The results demonstrate the potential for the model to enhance decision support and predictive analytics in real-world clinical environments, resulting in more accurate, reliable, and personalized patient care.
