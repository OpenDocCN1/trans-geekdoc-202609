# 3.1.2 UCI Heart Disease Dataset

The UCI Cleveland Heart Disease Dataset, a widely referenced benchmarking dataset in clinical prediction work, is the second dataset utilized in this study. To predict whether or not cardiac disease exists, it contains data from 303 unique patients, all of whom had features obtained through clinical assessment and diagnostic examinations. There are thirteen mandatory input factors, both numeric and categorical, that constitute the dataset. Gender is provided as 1 if male or 0 if female, and patient ages vary from 29 to 77 years. Resting blood pressure, serum cholesterol, fasting blood sugar, and electrocardiogram values are other significant features.

Features such as maximal heart rate achieved, angina on exercise, and ST depression are utilized to quantify cardiovascular function. Criteria such as the number of principal vessels stained by fluoroscopy, the steepness of the ST segment, and the type of thalassemia (normal, fixed defect, or reversible defect) are valuable diagnostic indicators. Whether or not a patient has been diagnosed with heart disease (1 or 0) is represented by the binary target variable. With 54% of cases having cardiac disease and 46% non-diseased, the class distribution is somewhat unbalanced.
