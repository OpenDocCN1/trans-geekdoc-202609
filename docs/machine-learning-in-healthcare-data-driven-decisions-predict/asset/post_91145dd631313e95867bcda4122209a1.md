## 2.2 FAIR Federated Health Data Prediction

A federated ML framework has been designed as privacy-friendly and uses “Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable” (FAIR) health data to facilitate cooperative model development among various healthcare organizations without divulging confidential patient data. An agent-based system, the framework applies classification models in a decentralized way, building an overall predictive model from local models learned in each organization’s secure environment. The design was tested by five healthcare organizations, which each converted their datasets to FAIR-compliant formats through a standardized FAIRification process. Federated ML agents at each location interacted with a central federated ML manager to jointly train a model for 30-day readmission risk prediction for COPD patients, which had an accuracy of 87%. This study illustrates the viability of deploying federated ML on FAIR health data and the capacity for balancing data interoperability, predictive performance, and privacy to enable real-world clinical decision-making under data protection regulations [12].

## 2.3 Federated Learning for Rare Diseases

A FL model is suggested to improve privacy-preserving EHR analysis. The model makes it possible for numerous healthcare organizations to work together securely by training ML models without explicitly sharing patient data. A number of ML methods, including random forests (RF), support vector classifiers, decision tree classifiers, logistic regression, and stacking classifiers, were employed and evaluated to predict the treatment requirements of patients. With an F1-score of 80% and an accuracy of 90%, the RF classifier outperformed the others and was capable of handling intricate and class-imbalanced data. The FL-based technique helps to address the major issues of sharing health information and is also compliant with the majority of privacy requirements including GDPR and HIPAA. As a result, it is especially useful in rare disease research, when concerns about confidentiality and data scarcity are more prominent. The system significantly improves the ability to investigate rare illnesses and advances therapy development by facilitating safe data aggregation at institutions. In order to further enhance performance, future research will concentrate on expanding the framework’s application to other health domains and incorporating increasingly complex ML approaches. A new benchmark for the safe, open, and moral examination of health data is set by this work [13].

## 2.4 Federated Deep Learning for Glaucoma

A privacy-preserving DL model to detect glaucoma based on a FL strategy is suggested for patient confidentiality and data security issues and the study is conducted in a multicenter study in seven eye care centers in Hong Kong, USA, and Singapore. The framework enabled every center to locally train models from their optical coherence tomography (OCT) optic disc volumetric datasets. Rather than exchanging data, parameters of the model alone were sent to a central server, which was aggregated using the FedProx algorithm and then redistributed for local optimization. Three various three-dimensional neural networks were experimented in order to determine the robustness of the FL methodology. With 9,326 OCT scans from 2,785 patients, the FL model performed well and consistently at all centers, with accuracy ranging from 78.3% to 98.5%, and maintained high accuracy (81.3–87.8%) on two unseen datasets. The federated model was outperformed by single models and performed similarly to that of regular centralized models, without the transfer of data. This study proves the feasibility and efficacy of FL for developing generalizable, privacy-preserving AI models for glaucoma diagnosis, an essential step toward clinical implementation in ophthalmology in practice [14].

## 2.5 Privacy Challenges and Mitigations in Healthcare Federated Learning

Collaborative effort across multiple healthcare facilities could significantly facilitate clinical AI uses by accessing larger and more diverse data sets. FL is an approach to training AI systems in which each facility provides only model updates on its local private data rather than the real patient data. The approach has been demonstrated to improve the performance of various clinical AI tasks. But establishing open and ongoing federations creates tremendous trust challenges, considering model updates can lead to information leakage by their nature. Previous work has explored privacy attacks on FL and constructed techniques to mitigate these challenges. This review discusses the privacy concerns specific to FL in healthcare, the trade-offs, costs, and limitations of previous mitigation techniques. It is meant to be a practitioner-oriented manual for medical researchers willing to apply FL as a privacy-aware and secure approach to collaborative AI development [15].

## 2.6 Adaptive Federated Transfer Learning for Secure Medical Imaging

With the rise of AI-based diagnosis, data privacy in medical picture classification is becoming a more significant health concern. Over 30% of health institutions worldwide experienced data breaches in the past year, making secure and privacy-preserving solutions imperative. This project aims to deliver appropriate privacy-preserving medical image classification by fusing FL and transfer learning. In order to fine-tune on domain-specific medical images, such as TB chest X-ray images, brain MRI tumor images, and diabetic retinopathy images, network architectures employing GoogLeNet and VGG16 as baseline models with ImageNet pretrained weights are used. Various aggregation techniques were shown to yield good classification accuracy. This study presented a new aggregation technique that demonstrated rapid communication and use of local datasets. Federated averaging (FedAvg) and federated stochastic gradient descent (FedSGD) were adapted as needed and based on data divergence in the communication rounds. In order to assess scalability and resilience, the study also verified contemporary topologies like ResNet-RS and EfficientNetV2. The findings showed that combining the advantages of FL and transfer learning to create a scalable, secure, and reliable paradigm for medical diagnostics gives healthcare professionals a way to collaborate on training precise medical image classification models without disclosing private patient information [16].

## 2.7 Decentralized Privacy-Preserving ML for Collaborative Healthcare

DeCaPH is a collaborative, decentralized, and privacy-preserving ML system designed to enable many healthcare institutions to work together to train models without exchanging raw data or utilizing a centralized server. DeCaPH limits privacy leaks during the training process while enabling safe collaboration in order to meet strict privacy and regulatory requirements. Three distinct medical tasks were used to validate the framework's effectiveness: predicting patient death from EHRs, classifying cell types from single-cell genomic data, and identifying pathology from chest radiography images utilizing distributed datasets from the real world. The results demonstrated that models trained on DeCaPH limited vulnerability to privacy assaults by up to 16% while seeing less than a 3.2% decrease in performance compared to non-privacy-preserving techniques. Additionally, DeCaPH models achieved up to 70% and 18.2% better results than previously used privacy-preserving techniques and significantly segregated private trainee models, respectively. All of these findings highlight DeCaPH's advantages in terms of maintaining strong privacy protection while simultaneously enhancing model generalizability and accuracy as well as its appropriateness for widespread application in collaborative healthcare AI [17].
