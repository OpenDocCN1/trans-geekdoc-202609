# 8 Barriers and Challenges to Effective Data Utilization

Deciding how to use data is often seen as a vital step toward helping patients, increasing “smart spending” by healthcare providers and aiding doctors with evidence-based decisions. Meanwhile, many healthcare organizations find it difficult to use data in the best way because of different challenges [23]. They exist at the level of the organization, technology, information, humans, and interpretation. To deal with these problems, one must recognize their backgrounds, impacts, and how they interact. The authors here discuss the challenges that prevent data from being used effectively: policies in the company, technology, problems with data quality and standards, difficulties in getting used to new things, and interpreting data that involves risks [21].

# 8.1 Organizational and Technological Barriers

The usage of health data often suffers due to technical and organizational issues that centers often run into. Primarily, organizational barriers result from goals being misaligned, information being kept in different systems, and too little attention from top leaders. In several healthcare areas, management of data is considered a technical role, so there is not enough focus on providing the right tools and staff for analyzing data [53]. Even though many organizations fail to develop a clear plan for data, support from their leaders is necessary to implement new approaches and sustain changes. Additional problems are created by the latest technologies. Legacy information systems used by various healthcare institutions are not meant for senior support nor do they allow data to be shared easily among other systems. Since the systems used in healthcare are not integrated, data from various areas is scattered and cannot be analyzed quickly [54]. Additionally, obsolete software is not flexible enough to allow use of new analytical systems like artificial intelligence or machine learning. Getting data out of a wide variety of sources and processing and loading it increases the workload needed to operate a company. Because health information technology is moving quickly, organizations are worried about the challenges linked to trying out new approaches, so they are reluctant to change. Dealing with cybersecurity is also a significant problem for technology. Because healthcare data is often attacked by cybercriminals, extra security rules are put in place that can prevent access to specific information within the organization [53]. Providing access to data in healthcare with security and privacy is always a problem. When there is little motivation to change and leaders are not engaged enough, systems built long ago are in use, data systems cannot communicate, and poor cyberdefenses exist, resulting in using data effectively becoming challenging.
