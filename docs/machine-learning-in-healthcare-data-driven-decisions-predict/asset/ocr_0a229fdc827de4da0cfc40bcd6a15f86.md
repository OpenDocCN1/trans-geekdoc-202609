Besides, ML, a subset of artificial intelligence, has witnessed enviable strides in the science of pattern recognition, prediction, and complex decision-making, long before being considered science fiction. The coalescence of the two – massive healthcare data and much sophisticated ML algorithms – brings a never-before opportunity to radically transform the delivery of health from disease prevention and diagnosis to personalized treatment and operational efficiency. Traditional analytical methods have once proved useful, but will hardly reveal the complex nonlinear relationships within high-dimensional and heterogeneous healthcare datasets [1]. ML learns from data without explicit programming, thus providing potent means of yielding actionable insights and good predictive capabilities, and subsequently enhancing the clinician's decision-making process through evidence-based approaches. This chapter discusses essential ML techniques: those used to date or expected to see widespread usage in the healthcare domain: how they work, where the potential applications might lie, what obstacles exist, and future developments. This text will describe the transition of the field of ML from theory to an operative and, therefore, integral tool aimed toward better patient care and a sustainable system for healthcare. Researchers, practitioners, and policymakers must understand the techniques used to cope with the complex ethical, regulatory, and technical questions concerning the deployment of artificial intelligence in such a sensitive domain. The successful implementation of ML will require technical ability and a strong appreciation of the clinical context, data characteristics, and implications for patients and healthcare providers. Given the volume and complexity of today's healthcare data, a shift from simple numerical maintenance toward automation and intelligent analytics is necessary to realize its full value through predictive or prescriptive avenues capable of changing care pathways and public health programs.

# ## 2 Core Machine Learning Paradigms and Their Relevance to Healthcare Data

ML techniques can broadly be categorized into several paradigms, each suited to different problems and data structures commonly encountered in healthcare [2]. Supervised learning, arguably the most prevalent paradigm in current healthcare applications, involves training models on labeled datasets, where the desired output is known for each input example. This is particularly useful for tasks like diagnosis (classifying a patient's condition based on symptoms and test results, where the correct diagnosis is the label), prognosis (predicting the likelihood of a future event like disease progression or treatment response, with the outcome being the label), and image analysis (identifying abnormalities in medical images, with expert annotations as labels).
