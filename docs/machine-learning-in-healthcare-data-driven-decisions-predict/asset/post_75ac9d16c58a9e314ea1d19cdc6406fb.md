## 2.3 AI-Driven Precision Diagnosis in Breast Cancer

Advancements in AI and ML have significantly impacted precision medicine, particularly for BC diagnosis and therapy. From vast genomic, imaging, and clinical databases, AI algorithms make precise tumor detection, identification, and predictive value of outcomes compared to traditional diagnostic processes. ML methods, specifically DL systems, reveal sophisticated patterns in multimodal data and enable the generation of personalized and directed treatment protocols. Applicability of AI to BC care is discussed by this review with examples of cutting-edge solutions, overcoming significant challenges to deployment in the clinic and achieving a perspective of future directions in research. Through comparison of data-driven approaches, it recognizes their contribution toward enabling early diagnosis, supporting clinical decisions, and ultimately benefiting patients [20].

## 2.4 Colorectal Chemotherapy Toxicity Prediction Model

ML algorithms reveal patterns within data through the identification of feature correlations and predictive insight into intricate clinical situations. The research pursued building a predictive model for recognizing colorectal cancer patients who are more likely to develop chemotherapy-induced toxicity and distinguishing the characteristics responsible for inducing the risk of side effects of treatment. Using a dataset of 95 pre-treatment health features from 74 patients, we have trained RF models with appropriate preprocessing to achieve a compromise between interpretability and predictive performance. The model successfully detected the relative contribution of numerical and categorical variables toward predicting toxicity outcome. The findings highlight the promise of integrating AI into colorectal cancer management, offering a personalized approach to anticipate and prevent treatment toxicities, advancing the boundary of precision medicine [21].

## 2.5 Hybrid Approach to Mammogram-Based Cancer Classification

BC, the leading cancer in the world involving more than 2.3 million women, is still a major public health problem. In spite of the fact that survival rates have been increasing because of enhanced screening, diagnosis, and treatment, early detection continues to be paramount in effective management of the condition. In order to improve the identification and classification rate of BC lesions on mammograms, a unique hybrid model utilizing DL and sophisticated image-preprocessing techniques is proposed in this study. The dataset of 20,000 mammograms was used to validate the model. To improve image quality and enable improved feature extraction, it was first preprocessed utilizing image-enhancement operators such as Gaussian Blur and Sharpening, YOLOv5 and MedSAM segmentation models were used in the DL architecture to facilitate lesion extraction, categorization, and appropriate display. Following a clinical evaluation on 800 patients, the blended model produced extremely high improvements in diagnostic speed and accuracy. The model only took 0.75 s to process each image, achieving a 99.7% global accuracy. In terms of clinical accuracy, it was 98.6% for malignant cases and 97.2% for benign ones. According to these findings, the hybrid model is a reliable and efficient diagnostic tool for early BC recognition with promising opportunities for clinical practice improvements in terms of diagnostic accuracy [22].

## 2.6 Stacked Ensemble for Breast Cancer

BC is among the most common cancers globally, only surpassed by lung cancer, and occurs in both women and men. Being largely asymptomatic during its initial stages, its detection becomes a challenge at an early stage, which usually ends up in late-stage diagnosis that is more difficult and expensive to treat, hence contributing to increased mortality. In spite of an increasing use of ML in cancer studies, there have been comparatively fewer studies on ensemble learning methods for the prediction of BC versus single-classifier systems. The study suggests a heterogeneous ensemble ML architecture aiming to assist early detection of BC. The proposed model uses a stacking ensemble with KNN, SVM, and DT as foundation learners in accordance with the CRISP-DM methodology. A homogeneous ensemble model called RF as well as various standalone classifiers like LR, ANN, NB, stochastic gradient descent, and its separate rate base models (KNN, SVM, and DT) are used to evaluate the effectiveness of this meta-classifier. The five most important features were selected for prediction using the chi-square approach. Accuracy, F1-score, precision, recall, specificity, log loss, AUC, and ROC curve are the metrics used to assess the model's performance using K-fold cross-validation. The null hypothesis was rejected by the proposed ensemble model at K = 20, which had a minimum log loss of 0.56 and a maximum accuracy of 78%. The p-value of 0.014, obtained from a one-tailed t-test, was statistically significant at the α = 0.05 level. All of these results suggest that the ensemble approach may be effective in predicting BC early on using only biochemical and anthropometric characteristics [23].

## 2.7 Deep Ensembles for Cancer Diagnosis

A novel deep hybrid heterogeneous ensemble model has been suggested for histological BC image classification with the aim of supporting pathologists in detecting malignancies and guiding appropriate treatment measures. The method combines seven strong pretrained DL models for strong feature extraction with four classical classifiers (multilayer perceptron, SVM, KNN, and decision tree) using hard and weighted voting techniques. Twenty-four ensemble hybrid models were tested and compared with deep stacked ensemble baselines to find the best ensemble method. Empirical verification was performed on the public BreakHis dataset at four magnification levels (40×, 100×, 200×, and 400×) with fivefold cross-validation. The introduced hybrid ensembles were found to perform better than individual and stacked models with a maximum accuracy of up to 96.3% at several magnifications. These results highlight the clinical value of deep ensemble models in enhancing histological BC diagnosis and minimizing diagnostic errors [24].

## 2.8 Hybrid CNN-GRU Model for Breast Cancer Detection

BC is an adenocarcinoma that occurs in the tissue of the breast and remains one of the most prevalent cancers in women, after lung cancer in terms of mortality rates. Early detection and correct classification of BC are crucial for improving the treatment. Manual examination of histopathological slides, however, is usually time-consuming, cumbersome, and subject to operator errors, thus potentially resulting in misclassification. To alleviate these challenges, this study suggests a hybrid DL model that integrates CNNs into computer-aided detection of BC invasive ductal cancer (IDC) based on the detection of a computer-assisted recovery unit (GRU) of BC based on the entire foil image (WSI) from PCAM-Kaggle data. The proposed CNN GRU model uses the CNN characteristic extraction ability and GRU time learning ability to isolate IDC negative samples from IDC positives. This model was tested using common measures. It leads to accuracy of 86.21%, accuracy of 85.50%, sensitive rate of 85.60%, accuracy of 84.71%, F1-score of 88%, AUC accuracy of 0.89, minimizing diagnostic errors and increasing classification acid crisis. The strength and efficiency of developed hybrid models are evaluated using BC identification compared to other DL architectures such as CNN-BiLSTM, CNN-LSTM, and current ML methods [25].
