
## 2 Related Study

The demand for patient-focused services has been fueled by evolving medical needs and international health issues, further accelerating the digitization of healthcare in recent years. There has been much literature generated that addresses the transformation from the traditional provider-based systems to those focused on individual patient needs and facilitated by digital technologies. According to the latest studies, digital innovation can make a great contribution to patient-centeredness via data-driven decision-making and tailored services [11]. Technical innovation in the form of blockchain, cloud computing, IoT, and AI is considered here as the main driver of such change. Some frameworks have been made available to enable such change. Value-driven digital transformation is the emphasis on re-designing workflows and systems around patient outcomes instead of organizational processes [12].

In order to combat the COVID-19 pandemic, patient-centered applications with blockchain and AI were created for contact tracing, secure data management, and real-time diagnosis [13]. These kinds of systems offer a glimpse into how decentralized data exchange protocols can evolve to deliver openness, trustworthiness, and timely crisis management. Another significant innovation is redesigning health ecosystems toward enhancing the quality of care of patients. Blockchain-based platforms have been proposed by research studies, enabling the patient along with ensuring auditability, access controls, and immunity to data [14]. Thinking in systems perspective whose thinking occurs in the background of complete sociological, technical, and behavioral environments greatly favors patient-based models [15]. These models illustrate how static repositories of information have been replaced by smart, interactive systems that are able to track the trajectory of health for each individual.

Interoperability also emerged as a recurring problem and point of controversy. For easy integration of data between systems, evidence favors semantic interoperability solutions through the employment of blockchain and semantic web technologies [16]. In the same manner, long-term HIT in the United States has demonstrated that patient accessibility, integration of care, and interaction between the patient and the physician are supported with the ease of electronic interfaces through organizing the digital platforms around the end-users’ method [17]. The creation of a culture of working together designed to support concord with this revolution of HIT functions to enforce concordance.

Systemic changes also need to be accompanied by changes on the cultural level. To ensure that digital technology is employed empathetically, inclusively, and user-centered, research indicates the need for the establishment of patient-centered values within medical matters as well as in the governance of institutions [18]. Patient privacy and control are also highlighted by individual health records through the use of blockchain technology [19]. These frameworks demonstrate that permission management, ownership of personal data, and flow of trackable data can be leveraged within digital health ecosystems. To conceptualize end-to-end, intelligent, and scalable healthcare ecosystems, new architectures based on blockchain, cloud, and IoT have also been proposed [20].

As privacy regulation compliance has already been taken into consideration for data being secured, these architectures are proposed to emphasize increased levels of interaction between patients and systems. Arguing for its case based on evidence pointing toward the inclusive UX approach being highly influential to adoption, compliance, and health outcomes, UX research has become part of digital health design [21]. Digital services must be synthesized in an attempt to address various patient populations in the context of global diversity increases in healthcare systems. Patient-centered standards have been brought forward in a new review as one means to ensure safe and fair access to digital health services in different socio-technical settings [22].

In order to pinpoint vulnerable populations, monitor patterns of health prior to the fact, and enhance means of care provision based on information in real time, predictive analytics and AI-enabled models are currently being used [23]. Patient empowerment also came under increased scrutiny in the COVID-19 pandemic, and systems to support shared decision-making, personalized messaging, and emotional support were further enhanced [24]. Notably where face-to-face interaction is restricted, experience served to further highlight the role that digital technology has in patient trust and continuity of care.

## 3 System Methodology: DigiCure Framework

In resource-limited environments, conventional health systems tend to be hampered by inefficiencies like faulty data, delayed diagnosis, and lack of patient participation. These inefficiencies lead to suboptimal care, delayed treatment, and wasteful healthcare expenditure. To address these inefficiencies, we present DigiCure, a simulation-driven digitalization platform that takes advantage of emerging technologies like telemedicine, IoMT, AI, and EHRs. The aim is to create an optimal, patient-focused health system with real-time monitoring, enhanced decision-making, and provider integration without a hitch.

### 3.1 Data Acquisition and Integration

The DigiCure system constructs an integrated digital health environment using a diverse set of data sources. The primary source of simulation is the MIMIC-III database, which contains over 53,000 patient records with diagnosis information, clinical measurements, and demographics. This set contains a wide range of measurements such as blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, test results, and medication history. IoMT streams are simulated for real-time monitoring of health and the MIMIC-III dataset. The IoMT sensors track vital parameters such as

- Heart rate: HR(t)
- Blood pressure: BP(t) = (Systolic(t), Diastolic(t))
- Temperature: T(t)

These IoMT devices stream continuous time-series data that is integrated with the AI models to monitor patient conditions dynamically. The AI model relies on both structured (e.g., lab tests and medications) and unstructured data (e.g., clinical notes) from the MIMIC-III database.
