
## 2 Related Study

Recent studies on privacy-guaranteeing healthcare systems have more thoroughly examined how blockchain technology and FL can possibly work together towards overcoming scalability, privacy, and data-sharing issues. A basic architecture that combines FL and blockchain technology to secure private IoT health data was put forth in [1]. While secure aggregation and distributed ownership are highlighted here, effective consensus mechanisms and dynamic identification verification are not enabled. Sai et al. [2] presents an innovative architecture that combines FL with NFTs to improve diagnosis, reliability and traceability of data within intelligent healthcare environments. While the method promises privacy guarantees, the technique incurs extra blockchain overhead and does not handle model poisoning attacks.

A comprehensive review of privacy-preserving techniques with FL in smart healthcare is brought forth by the survey in [3]. It refers to glaring gaps within latency, interoperability, and trust management, opening the door for proposals of solutions that combine identity frameworks with blockchain. An intelligent hospital-specific federated blockchain-based learning framework is also suggested by Ngan Van et al. [4]. It identifies the advantage of decentralized orchestration and device-side learning but does not analyze scalability and model drift across different healthcare scenarios. In the meantime, Abou El Houda et al. [5] offer a blockchain-based distributed cooperation mechanism to eradicate trust problems among medical professionals during federated learning. Though their consensus protocol achieves highest fairness, it is more latency-sensitive and less flexible in low-resource scenarios.

For the protection of healthcare data exchanges between institutions, an exchange infrastructure for patient information is formed by applying blockchain in [6]. Although the system guarantees access control and auditability, the system is inflexible in the sense that it does not accommodate changing patient consent models but is based on static role-based permissions instead. A privacy-preserving cloud-integrated care framework is introduced in [7] considering secure blockchain-based exchange of health information among cloud infrastructures. However, collaborative analytics and data localization in real time are not taken into consideration in this framework. Sezer et al. [8] provides PFPChain, i.e., a blockchain-facilitated privacy-preserving FL model for sensor networks with model integrity checking and privacy-preserving updating. Though appropriate for IoT-based systems, its practical clinical use has yet to be investigated.

The study in [9] investigates edge intelligence for privacy protection in dispersed healthcare systems with FL. Energy efficiency and low-latency inference are its key features but it lacks strong, tamper-proof audit trails. Alzubi et al. [10] achieved with regards to privacy-protecting EHR sharing over Cloud-IIoT infrastructure through combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with FL and blockchain. The architecture does not involve decentralized identity or permission mechanisms, although it is very diagnostic. Although it has intensive processing requirements for block validation, an FL-based blockchain medical data-sharing solution with fine-grained access is introduced in [11].

A privacy-protected high-performance blockchain-supported e-healthcare data-sharing system is realized in [12]. The paradigm has poor anti-collusion protection among the owners but synchronizes multiparty data access with low communication cost. To compensate for transaction latency and provide immutability of data in the context of collaborative training, a newer approach [13] emphasizes scalability and incorporates efficient block structures. User control is reduced unless patient-oriented models of identity exist. FL's principal benefit of data localization is undermined by an AI and blockchain-based hybrid approach to secure IoT medical data transfer investigated in [14]. The model gives decentralized storage and top-level encryption but has a centralized learning aspect.

The collaborative service model presented in [15] offers trustworthy medical cooperation services by integrating blockchain technology with FL. It lacks adaptive control over changing healthcare legislation, but it offers multiparty analytics that have inherent trust mechanisms. Focusing on real-time threat prevention, the anomaly detection-based protection system in [16] suggests an access control layer for cloud-based medical systems. But it still relies heavily on the cloud and is not as decentralized as blockchain. Meanwhile, Lakhani et al. [17] develop a blockchain and FL hybrid model for IoMT fraud detection. Though it does not utilize SSI protocols for anti-credential spoofing, it does propose fraud detection with historical transaction modeling.

Decentralized diagnosis and record-sharing are conducted with FL in [18] within a privacy-preserving smart healthcare system. Though it prioritizes data security via encrypted aggregation and local model training, it does not have end-to-end audit trails and multi-jurisdictional compliance requirements. Blockchain-based smart contracts are used in the smart healthcare exchange paradigm in [19] to implement EMR exchange regulation ensuring privacy. The consensus mechanism of the system is computationally costly and not suitable for medical environments where time is of the essence, although it provides traceability and integrity of data. Alsamhi et al. [20] discusses decentralized sharing of information using blockchain technology in combination with FL to enable hospitals to cooperate and train models without disclosing data. It utilizes static identity management, though.

Singh et al. [21] suggest a less power-hungry blockchain-facilitated FL in smart healthcare. It offers lightweight cryptographic computations and trust-based participation scoring with notable communication cost savings. It lacks fine-grained access control and possesses a plain identification mechanism. Taken collectively, what they prove is that there is increasing sentiment that federated intelligence must be brought into decentralized, impenetrable infrastructures for the sharing of medical information. They mention a number of shortcomings, chief among them being the absence of patient-controlled data sharing protocols, excessive transaction latency in consensus structures, weak support for cross-institutional regulatory compliance, and the lack of dynamic identity verification.

Although federated learning and blockchain offer a revolutionary potential for intelligent healthcare, current solutions lack the ability to properly merge efficiency, privacy, and trust into one architecture. The majority ignore the full set of criteria, such as identity sovereignty, compliance automation, and distributed system interoperability, to concentrate on one or two – e.g., privacy or auditability. These restrictions strongly highlight the importance of having a top-down model that combines blockchain, FL, and decentralized identity management like SSI to provide secure, open, and legally compliant exchange of healthcare data at scale.

## 3 System Methodology

The proposed algorithm integrates FL with permissioned blockchain technology to facilitate secure, private, multi-institution collaborative healthcare data analysis. The approach reduces privacy concerns and direct data sharing by allowing each institution to train models locally on their sensitive patient information. While blockchain ensures transparent, unbreachable coordination using consensus and access control via smart contracts, homomorphic encryption and differential privacy methods secure model updates as they are in transit. This dual-mode approach is best suited to today's healthcare environments because it addresses the significant concerns of data confidentiality, integrity, and regulatory alignment.
