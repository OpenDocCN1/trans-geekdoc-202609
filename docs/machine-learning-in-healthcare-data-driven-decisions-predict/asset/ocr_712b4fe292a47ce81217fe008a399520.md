# 8.4 Managing Uncertainty in Data Interpretation

Data interpretation in healthcare is inherently complex due to the probabilistic nature of medical knowledge, variability in patient populations, and limitations in data completeness. Managing uncertainty is a fundamental challenge when leveraging healthcare data for decision-making. Uncertainty arises from multiple sources, including measurement errors, incomplete data capture, biases in data collection, and limitations in analytic models [55].

Healthcare data often contains noise and outliers that can skew results, if not appropriately handled. For example, vital signs recorded during emergency situations may be atypical and misrepresent the patient's baseline status. Similarly, missing data due to skipped tests or lost records introduces uncertainty that complicates analysis.
