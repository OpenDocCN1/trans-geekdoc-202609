By combining data of various resolutions, multi-scale fusion gives the network a comprehensive vision of high-level semantics and fine textures. Attention mechanisms guide the network to pay attention to the most informative parts of the image and highlight clinically significant structures and suppress background noise that is unnecessary. Current studies have shown remarkable improvement in the segmentation of intricate anatomical shapes, lesions, and pathological regions in various imaging modalities like MRI, CT, ultrasound, and mammography by combining these two approaches together. Medical image segmentation techniques founded on multi-scale and attention-based methods are reviewed in this section. The ability of computerized segmentation systems to withstand variability, be accurate, and be useful in the clinical setting is increased through the novel developments in architecture design, feature fusion, and performance optimization that each paper makes.

In an effort to enhance contextual understanding by integrating local and global features, segmentation architecture was introduced in [1], which utilizes a multi-scale self-guided attention mechanism. Through the emphasis of most relevant spatial and semantic cues in input images, the method significantly enhanced segmentation accuracy. For solving issues like overlapping regions and boundary vagueness of medical images, a collaborative multi-scale feature fusion model with attention modules was proposed in [2]. To obtain more compact segmentation of intricate anatomical regions, the model collaborated features at disparate resolution levels.

Two-branch architecture with a guidance mechanism in the form of an attention mechanism that works at two scales in parallel was proposed in [3]. With the high dice similarity coefficients achieving consistent performance, the network worked very well for prostate and tumor segmentation, detecting organ boundaries and tiny lesion regions correctly. Multi-scale attentional feature integration was incorporated into a deep learning model in [4] to allow the network to learn rich contextual features while selectively paying attention to salient spatial locations. In the identification of diffuse and subtle areas of medical images, the model performed with excellent accuracy.

Attention-augmented deep network was shown for vascular segmentation of the liver [5]. It enhanced the connectivity and continuity of the vessel maps segmented by combining multi-scale information and detection of thin vessel structures with attention gates. Multi-scale attention modules were used in a mammography classification model for the capture of both local differential textures and global differential forms [6]. Benign and malignant tumors were thus distinguished more effectively with improved discrimination of features.
