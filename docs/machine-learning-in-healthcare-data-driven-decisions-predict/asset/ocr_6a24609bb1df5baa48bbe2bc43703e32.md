ML algorithms learn patterns from past data and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed for such tasks. This has opened up several opportunities in healthcare, ranging from early disease diagnosis and predictive diagnosis to personalization of treatment and drug discovery. For instance, thousands of medical images analyzed through ML models can oftentimes detect fine subtlety of signs for diseases like cancer or neurological disorders, at times equal to or even better than that of healthcare experts. ML-based systems also allow the healthcare practitioner to predict patient outcomes, improve hospital operations, and streamline administrative work by providing assistance in real-time decision support during critical care. Personalized medicine is about administering treatment based on an individual’s specific genetic makeup for optimal efficacy and minimal side effects; ML has made this possible. Wearable devices and remote monitoring technologies that work on ML algorithms are rapidly changing patient engagement and chronic disease management by allowing FLT (for life tracking) [1]. In public health, ML is also transforming disease outbreak prediction, health insurance modeling, and biomedical data-expedited research. Meanwhile, it promises to reduce costs, increase accessibility, and improve quality in healthcare delivered to diverse populations. The promise notwithstanding, the application of ML in healthcare raises challenges, such as privacy concerns, ethical considerations, and the need for thorough validation and regulatory approval. Nevertheless, the collaboration among data scientists, clinicians, and policy-makers to date will shape the responsible and effective integration of ML technologies into healthcare. In this chapter, we will discuss the multi-angled applications of ML in healthcare. We will see how, from the patients’ point of view to healthcare practitioners to systems, ML benefits them all. By understanding how ML can change the way medication approaches its applications and also some pitfalls of its journey, we will further appreciate its role in shaping the future of medicine.

# ### 2.1 Disease Diagnosis and Risk Prediction

Nowhere else has the application of ML fulfilled such a great promise as in the diagnosis of diseases and the prediction of risks. Traditional diagnostic tests possess a degree of reliance solely on an individual’s experience, gut feel, and subjective interpretation. Medical professionals interpret a portion of the patient-reported symptoms in tandem with laboratory tests and through various modalities of different imaging technologies like X-rays and MRIs.
