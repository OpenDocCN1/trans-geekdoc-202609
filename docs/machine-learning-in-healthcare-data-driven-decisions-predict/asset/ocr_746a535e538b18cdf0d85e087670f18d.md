One of them is incorporating multimodal data combining structured features and unstructured clinical notes, lifestyle details, or wearable sensor data readouts in order to enrich a patient profile. Merging time-series data and longitudinal health histories can also be able to facilitate temporal modeling and progression prediction, particularly helpful in monitoring prediabetic conditions. The researcher also has the option to use DL models such as LSTMs or transformers, especially when processing sequential or EHR. Model interpretability is also an important area; techniques such as SHAP or LIME can further be exploited to offer interpretable predictions to clinicians and patients. Also, creating models for deployment on mobile or edge devices may enable low-resource real-time screening tools for rural or underserved populations. From the fairness and ethics perspective, additional focus in future research should be placed on minimizing bias to allow equiP performance with regards to age, gender, ethnicity, and socioeconomic status. Privacy-preserving ML mechanisms such as differential privacy and federated learning can also be explored to develop more secure patient-centered systems. Lastly, large-scale clinical trials and validations in wide-based populations and geographies need to be conducted to establish the generalizability and strength of the models in real-world environments.
