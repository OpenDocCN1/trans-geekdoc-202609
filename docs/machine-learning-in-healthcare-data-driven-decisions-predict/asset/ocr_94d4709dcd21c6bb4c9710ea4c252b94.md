# 1 Introduction

In the twenty-first century, causes like social rat race, fast-paced lifestyle, increased work pressure in corporate sectors, digital overexposure, etc. are affecting mental health significantly, resulting in rising cases of anxiety, stress, and depression [1]. Conventional therapy and medical treatments remain essential for addressing mental health problems no doubt, but timely detection of such conditions is still a challenge. However, with advancements in wearable smart devices, a new method emerges to monitor health and offers hope for early detection of mental health conditions and symptoms. Wearable smart devices, such as smartwatches, smart bands, and smart rings, all come equipped with a variety of sensors. Core features of these devices include activity tracking, heart rate monitoring, blood pressure monitoring, oxygen level measurement, and GPS tracking.

These wearable devices collect physiological parameters closely related to mental health, as it is already an established fact that emotional and physiological states influence bodily functions. Various body signals, such as heart rate variability, skin conductance, sleep patterns, and brain activity, provide valuable insights into an individual's mental well-being. Understanding these physiological parameters enables the detection of stress, anxiety, depression, and other mental health issues. A crucial aspect of understanding an individual's well-being is detecting their emotional state. A continuously negative emotional state over an extended period indicates early signs of mental health issues [2]. Previous researchers [4–11, 33, 34] used various nonneural and neural network machine learning models to classify emotions. We will discuss this briefly in the literature survey part.

Several professional roles, such as doctors, pilots, and loco pilots, require optimal mental stability during work hours because a single poor decision can lead to life-threatening consequences. Therefore, early detection of an individual's unstable emotional state becomes vital. Numerous research studies focus on this domain, with researchers using various smart devices to predict emotional states. A detailed discussion of existing research in this category appears in Section 2. However, many limitations exist in earlier research. Researchers often develop custom devices, such as headbands, helmets, or heavy medical equipment, to collect physiological data. However, these custom devices tend to be expensive and are typically tested in controlled lab environments rather than real-life conditions.

Hence, we investigate whether it is feasible to detect human emotions using commercially available smartwatches. To study this, we explore the LifeSnaps [3] dataset. LifeSnaps data were collected over four months in real-life situations across various geographical locations. The device used in the LifeSnaps dataset is a Fitbit smartwatch, which is a commercially available product. The LifeSnaps dataset contains various physiological features such as heart rate, oxygen level, calories burned, filtered demographic VO2max, resting heart rate, and step count, among others.
