
### 2.8 Federated Transfer Learning for Breast Cancer

BC continues to be a top cause of death among women globally, and so early and precise diagnosis is crucial in maximizing patient outcomes and survival rates. Although machine learning, and more so deep learning, has been immensely successful in image recognition applications, its deployment in classifying BC is not without challenges given the shortage of labeled data and medical domain privacy issues. The study presents a new approach that integrates transfer learning in the context of an FL framework, allowing researchers to mitigate these challenges in multi-institutional healthcare settings. Mammography and MRI images were obtained from three medical centers, which allowed for a joint global model for training, without any raw data transfer, preserving the privacy of patients. The method utilizes a pretrained ResNet model as a feature extractor and fine-tuned the upper layers on BC data from multiple institutions to learn additional specialized, domain-specific data representations while inheriting the general image representations learned from ImageNet, which was trained on large datasets. Furthermore, the method also implemented domain adversarial training to mitigate domain shifts due to shifts in data distributions across centers, allowing the model to learn domain-invariant features by minimizing the differences in domains and maximizing the accuracy of the classification. Overall, experiments show that their proposed method improved on conventional standalone training and FL without domain adaptation with accuracy on classification of 98.8% and computation time of 12.22 s. These results show significant increases in accuracy and generalizability and validate the use of privacy-preserving FL for improving BC classification, especially in real-world, multi-institutional healthcare settings [18].

### 2.9 Privacy-Preserving Collaborative Medical Diagnosis

A privacy-preserving collaborative medical diagnosis (CMD) approach based on FL has been introduced to enhance real-time patient monitoring and diagnosis in multiple hospitals while ensuring protection of sensitive information. The establishment of FL preserves patient privacy and data localization through sharing model parameters instead of sharing raw data, which allows the decentralized model training on distributed data. Using a partially homomorphic cryptosystem (PHC) with a residual learning-based deep belief network (RDBN) made it possible to provide secure and accurate classification of physiological data. Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed CMD provides high diagnostic accuracy at various healthcare institutions while providing appropriate protection for privacy. Specifically, PHC and RDBN computations require around 150 and 1,000 ms for encryption and classification, respectively, with 4 and 5 MB of data transmitted in the user-to-server and reverse directions. Additionally, the technique reduces communication overhead by 30% and improves classification accuracy by 10% on average across a range of datasets, proving its effectiveness and impact in safe collaborative healthcare diagnosis [19].

This survey reports on the latest advances in privacy-preserving FL solutions in several medical disciplines including innovative techniques such as DP, transfer learning, HE, and decentralized systems. These developments indicate encouraging improvement in model performance, robustness, and scalability with confidentiality of patients maintained in multi-institutional data sharing. FL is a novel breakthrough in health AI that enables jointly trained models without breaching patient privacy. Interaction with state-of-the-art privacy-protection technologies such as DP, HE, and adaptive aggregation safeguards the data with robust diagnostic performance and solid models. These technologies effectively tackle key issues of data heterogeneity, regulation, and domain change to provide scalable, reliable, and viable AI solutions for clinical applications. Additional collaboration and exploration of FL platforms can have a huge impact on precision medicine, facilitate better patient care, and promote responsible collaboration in health centers in multiple jurisdictions around the world.

## 3 Methodology

To address the issues of privacy, data heterogeneity, and compliance with data governance issues in clinical machine learning, we present a unique FL framework to co-create machine learning models in a multi-institutional healthcare setting. Our framework proposes that ML models can be co-created by multiple healthcare institutions without the need for patient data to be centralized by any institution, achieving data security, patient privacy, and data governance using secure protocols and model personalization, thereby increasing privacy and model performance.

### 3.1 System Architecture

System architecture is built to promote secure and collaborative model cocreation from a number of decentralized sources of clinical data. It has multiple local clients which can be any healthcare or research center, such as hospitals and clinics, that are independent data silos, that fully control their private datasets, like EHRs or medical imaging. Clients will cocreate ML models through FL by training them locally using their private datasets. A central coordinating server facilitates the learning process by initializing and distributing a global model to all clients. It then collects privacy-preserving model updates from each institution, aggregates them securely, and redistributes the updated global model. This decentralized structure ensures that sensitive patient data remains within the originating institutions, maintaining compliance with privacy regulations while enabling collaborative intelligence across the healthcare network. A modified version of the federated averaging (FedAvg) algorithm [20] is adopted to enhance secure aggregation and privacy-preserving mechanisms. The overall goal is to optimize a global model collaboratively across multiple decentralized clients without exposing their raw data. Let K be the number of clients, w_t denote the global model parameters at round t, w_t^k denote the local model parameters for client k at round t, η be the local learning rate, D_k be the local dataset at client k with size n_k, and n = \sum_{k=1}^K n_k is the total number of data points across all clients.

### Initialization

The first step in the training process is for the central server to initialize a global ML model with an architecture, model, and set of parameters. This model is then sent to all participating client institutions. All clients obtain a copy of this model so that they are all initialized in the same way at the beginning of the training process. The choice of the model architecture will generally depend on the specific clinical task which the FL process is addressing, such as classifying diseases or predicting risk. The central server initializes the global model parameters and then sends this model to all the participating clients:

```
w_0 \leftarrow \{ w_0^1, w_0^2 ... w_0^K \} (1)
```

### Local Training

When each client receives the global model, it then conducts local training on its private dataset. The training must consist of data preprocessing tasks, initially; standardization or normalization, to ensure consistency in input features. The clients must address other issues such as class imbalance by choosing between techniques such as data augmentation or class-weighted loss functions. The models will be optimized using optimization algorithms such as SGD or Adam, and training can occur for a predetermined amount of local epochs, in order to avoid overfitting, and to save communication costs. Each client k will perform local updates on its dataset D_k. The client minimizes a local objective function:

```
$F_k(w) = \frac{1}{n_k} \sum_{i=1}^{n_k} l(w; x_i^k, y_i^k)$ \tag{2}
```

Equation (2) is the loss function and x_i^k, y_i^k \in D_k. The local model is updated using gradient descent for E epochs:

```
$w_t^k \leftarrow w_t^k - \eta \nabla F_k(w_t^k)$ \tag{3}
```

### Model Update Sharing

After completing local training, clients do not share any raw patient data. Instead, they extract and encrypt only the updated model parameters such as weight matrices or gradient values. These encrypted updates are securely transmitted to the central server. This ensures that sensitive health information never leaves the local site, thus preserving patient privacy and complying with data protection regulations. The client k sends its updated model w_t^k or the difference \Delta w_t^k = w_t^k - w_t to the server. To preserve privacy, the update is encrypted:

```
$\hat{w}_t^k = \text{Encrypt}(w_t^k)$ \tag{4}
```

### Secure Aggregation

The central server receives encrypted updates from multiple clients and performs secure aggregation. Techniques such as HE or SMPC are used to combine the updates without decrypting them, preventing any single party including the server itself from accessing individual client data. This layer of security ensures that even the model updates cannot be reverse-engineered to infer sensitive information. The server performs secure aggregation of encrypted updates using protocols such as HE and SMPC [21].

```
$\hat{w}_{t+1} = \text{Aggregate}(\{ \hat{w}_t^1, \dots, \hat{w}_t^K \})$ \tag{5}
```

After decryption, the server computes the new global models as a weighted average:

```
$w_{t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{n_k}{n} w_t^k$ \tag{6}
```

### Global Model Update

Once aggregation is complete, the server computes a new version of the global model by averaging or otherwise combining the encrypted client updates. This updated model reflects knowledge learned from the collective datasets of all clients, enhancing its generalizability.
