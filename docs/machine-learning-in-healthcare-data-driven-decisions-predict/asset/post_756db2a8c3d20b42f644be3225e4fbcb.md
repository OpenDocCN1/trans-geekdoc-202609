## A Hybrid Machine Learning Model for Risk Stratification and Functional Outcome Prediction in Stroke Survivors

**Abstract:** Stroke continues to be a major cause of long-term disability globally, and precise and timely prediction of patient outcomes is needed to guide focused rehabilitation and resource allocation. This chapter introduces a new hybrid machine learning (ML) model that combines multimodal data sources for the concurrent task of risk stratification and functional outcome prediction in stroke survivors. The hybrid model combines a convolutional neural network (CNN) for imaging data, a graph neural network for structured clinical data, and a conventional machine learning model (LightGBM) for some handcrafted features. These heterogeneous elements are integrated in a common latent representation space and optimized end-to-end using a multitask learning strategy. The model was trained and validated against a robust dataset of demographic data, clinical rating, neuroimaging (CT/MRI), and follow-up functional outcomes as per the modified Rankin scale (mRS). Comparative evaluation against four traditional models: logistic regression, random forest, unimodal CNN, and an ensemble late fusion model illustrates better performance by the proposed hybrid approach with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.92 for risk classification and a quadratic-weighted kappa of 0.84 for mRS prediction. The findings indicate that the combination of varied learning paradigms and data types in one model improves predictive power and interpretability and opens the door to more patient-centered stroke treatment.

**Keywords:** stroke prediction, hybrid machine learning, multimodal data integration, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), graph neural networks (GNNs), LightGBM

## 1 Introduction

Stroke continues to be a major global cause of mortality and disability, placing a strain on society and healthcare [1]. As virtually all strokes but very small percentages are ischemic, rapid and precise patient outcome prediction is important to clinical decision-making as well as planning for resource consumption. Traditionally, prediction of stroke outcome has relied on clinical grading scales such as the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and the mRS [2]. While useful, such scales are generally handicapped by their reliance on subjective assessment, fixed variables, and failure to capture the complex, multimodal nature of stroke pathology. There is hence growing interest in integrating machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) methods to maximize prediction performance as well as guide individualized treatment choices [3].

Advanced technological advances in clinical informatics and medical imaging have enabled the acquisition of high-dimensional, heterogeneous data consisting of neuroimaging, physiological measurements, laboratory test values, and extensive patient histories [4]. However, these heterogeneous data sources often remain untapped in conventional predictive models. Single-modality approaches, such as image-only or clinical-only models, may be inadequate in harnessing the synergy advantage of multimodal data. Thus, there is a need for an exhaustive, evidence-based strategy to optimize the precision and reliability of outcome prediction in patients with stroke [5].

To close this gap, this work presents a novel hybrid model that integrates imaging data, structured clinical features, and relational clinical graphs. The proposed architecture combines three high-performance components: (1) a 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) [6] for extracting spatial features from volumetric CT or MRI scans; (2) a graph neural network (GNN) [7] for clinical relationship modeling between patients; and (3) a traditional ML pipeline based on LightGBM [8] for leveraging hand-engineered tabular clinical features.

Multimodal data fusion in which various data types are combined and analyzed together has been receiving growing interest as a solution to this challenge. The argument is simple: various data modalities capture complementary information about the patient’s status [9]. As an example, imaging data will disclose anatomical and pathological alterations in the brain, whereas clinical data including blood pressure, glucose level, or atrial fibrillation status can provide contextual information on risk and recovery. When properly combined, these modalities have the potential to provide a richer and more informative image of a patient’s health status. Nevertheless, combining such heterogeneous information is still a great challenge because of the differences in data structure, dimension, and semantics [10].

The increase in the availability of data is the innovation in artificial intelligence (AI) and ML methods, specifically DL. These methods have proven to hold great promise for modeling complex high-dimensional relationships and for learning hierarchical representations from pure input data [11].
