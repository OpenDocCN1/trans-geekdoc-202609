# 2.2 EpiForecaster: Deep Ensemble Epidemic Prediction

EpiForecaster is a novel DL ensemble architecture that has been developed to enhance epidemic forecasting accuracy during rapidly evolving outbreaks. This study evaluates its performance using weekly case data from Brazil, the USA, Mexico, the UK, and France for the 2022 Mpox outbreak between July 10 and October 9. The approach integrates four state-of-the-art DL models CNNs, LSTM networks, bi-directional LSTM (Bi-LSTM), and hybrid CNN-LSTM models. After rigorous hyperparameter tuning, every model produces independent stacked forecasts, which are then aggregated by using multiple meta-model methods. It uses the limited-memory Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno with bounds (L-BFGS-B) optimization algorithm for dynamically distributing the weights across each model depending on its forecasting accuracy. EpiForecaster yields enhanced forecasting accuracy compared to standard ensemble methods such as gradient boosting and genetic algorithm-based ensembles, with an equal-weight ensemble as a control baseline. Metrics such as mean-squared error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) show that EpiForecaster consistently offers improved forecasting precision. Contrary to the assumption that larger ensembles are better by default, this contribution stresses the significance of the role played by intelligent weight optimization. EpiForecaster is both an efficient and scalable method for modeling nonstationary time series and can be a resourceful tool in a wide range of predictive tasks other than epidemic prediction [9].
