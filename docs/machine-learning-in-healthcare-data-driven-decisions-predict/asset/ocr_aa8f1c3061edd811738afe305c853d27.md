+   **Model Generalizability:** Models trained on particular populations (e.g., age group, sex, culture) tend to fail when tested on alternate demographics. Reduces real world applicability and may cause biases.

+   **Real-World Deployment:** Stress detection models work excellently in lab settings but lose efficacy substantially in realistic dynamic environments as a result of noise, motion artifacts, and context shifts. Erodes the reliability and trust in deployed systems.

+   **Ethical and Privacy Issues:** The collection of sensitive physiological and emotional information is controversial on issues of consent, data ownership, and misuse. Compromises user trust and may result in regulatory non compliance (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA).

+   **Scalability and Resource Limitations:** Processing, storing, and interpreting continuous streams of physiological information from large populations is resource demanding.

Compromises cost efficiency and timely intervention at scale.

# ## 7.3 Directions for Future Research

+   **Multimodal Fusion:** Integrate physiological, behavioral, and environmental information for more precise and personalized stress estimation.

+   **Edge AI Integration:** Allow real time, low latency inference on wearable or mobile devices to decrease reliance on cloud computing.

+   **Personalized AI Models:** Employ user calibration and continuous learning to personalize models to individual baselines and temporal changes.

+   **Digital Twin Integration:** Create AI based digital copies of users for ongoing stress monitoring and simulation.

+   **Policy and Governance Frameworks:** Create ethical and legal frameworks to govern responsible utilization of stress detection technologies in healthcare, workplaces, and personal applications.
