# UN Flags 150% Spike in Tech Giants' Emissions Linked to AI

![](bbox=[0.4, 0.03, 0.9, 0.26])

The United Nations has raised alarms over a dramatic 150% increase in carbon emissions from major tech companies, a surge largely attributed to the explosive growth of artificial intelligence infrastructure. This warning underscores the environmental cost of training and running massive AI models, which require enormous computational resources and energy consumption.

According to recent UN reports, data centers powering advanced AI models consume vast amounts of electricity, often sourced from fossil fuels. Training a single large language model can emit as much carbon as five gasoline-powered cars over their entire lifetimes. With companies like Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon racing to deploy AI at scale, the environmental impact is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

The UN is urging governments and corporations to adopt more sustainable practices, including powering data centers with renewable energy, improving model efficiency, and enforcing environmental transparency. Without such measures, the climate impact of AI could undermine global efforts to reduce carbon emissions in line with the Paris Agreement.

This warning puts the tech industry's sustainability promises to the test. While AI innovation continues at breakneck speed, the question now is whether this progress can be achieved without accelerating environmental damage.

# Reuters Launches AI Suite to Supercharge Video Production

![](bbox=[0.39, 0.46, 0.9, 0.69])

Reuters' launch of its AI Suite for video production illustrates how traditional media organizations are adapting to technological change rather than simply being disrupted by it. The suite offers integrated tools for transcription, translation, metadata generation, and content organization that address specific challenges in news production workflows.

Unlike generic AI tools, Reuters has developed solutions tailored to the unique requirements of news organizations. The system can quickly transcribe interviews in multiple languages, generate searchable metadata for video archives, and help journalists identify key moments in lengthy recordings. These capabilities are particularly valuable for breaking news situations where speed and accuracy are paramount.

The difference between generic and specialized AI tools becomes clear when you consider the specific challenges news organizations face. A general-purpose transcription service might struggle with technical terminology, proper nouns, or accented speech that's common in international news coverage. Reuters' specialized system has been trained to handle these challenges, producing more accurate results that require less manual correction.
