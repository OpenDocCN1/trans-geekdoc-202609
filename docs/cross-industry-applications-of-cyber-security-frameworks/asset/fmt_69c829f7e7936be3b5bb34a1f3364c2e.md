
# AI in Healthcare: Applications, Cybersecurity Challenges, and IoT Integration

## AI Applications in Healthcare Domains

AI has been employed in applications across a number of healthcare domains such as cancer research, cardiology, diabetes, mental health, identification of Alzheimer’s disease, stroke-related studies, and identification of cardiovascular disease. Rather than robotics, AI in healthcare typically refers to physicians and hospitals having access to extensive datasets of potentially life-saving information. The healthcare sector must protect patients' personal information since hackers can access it and exploit it to commit clinical fraud and other crimes for monetary gains. Cybersecurity helps keep data safe, and for criminal purposes, the affected person's information is kept private. Additionally, cybercrime prevention is a priority (Thyagarajan, 2017).

Recent advancements in computing power can analyze distinct elements from multisensory information for predictive analytics to discover conceivable fitness consequences via machine learning techniques. Artificial intelligence and machine learning methods use statistical methods to analyze incoming sensory and network statistics to identify patterns and security hazards and make decisions with minimal human interaction.

## Internet of Things (IoT) and Cyber-Physical System (CPS) in the Generation of AI

Healthcare systems in hospitals/clinics are one of the key targets of attackers for carrying out Internet-of-Things (IoT) and Cyber-Physical System (CPS)-focused cyberattacks. The most critical endpoints from the medical institution security standpoint are patient health monitoring, ventilation, anesthesia, and infusion pumps.

A Cyber-Physical System (CPS) is a collection of physical world systems or devices that interact and communicate with one another to perform their functions. For operations and control, CPS combines the principles of computing, communication, and control. Physical systems are monitored, and cyber-physical systems may be dynamic and interactive, consisting of dispersed subsystems with the capacity to auto-connect and self-operate (TR, R., 2021).

There is growing use of IoT in healthcare settings, including mobile devices, wearables, robots, drones, and contactless devices. IoT is enabling the control of coronavirus. Early detection of Covid-19, isolation of contaminated people, and tracing possible contacts are necessary to stopping the spread of the virus. IoT and CPS protocols, GPS, and Wi-Fi are presenting solutions to the challenges that distance and accessibility would have posed.

Using IoT to combat virus outbreaks has been advantageous during Covid-19. Interconnected tech devices, such as smart thermometers to check a patient’s temperature, are used to build unique datasets for more accurate analysis and diagnosis. Quarantine compliance is additionally substantially assisted by the use of IoT. By using a patient’s existing smartphone or wearable devices, it is easier to ensure compliance with quarantine policies and establish patterns via track-and-trace methods.

Cybersecurity is the principal problem of the nation’s standard cyber-physical protection and economic interests. Safety analysts in every organization are facing many challenges associated with cybersecurity, including securing federal and state exclusive data. One must distinguish between immediate goals and long-term desires when developing long-term analysis, development, and application of AI in cybersecurity.

There are various methods AI can be directly applied in cybersecurity. Currently, there are immediate cybersecurity issues that require intelligent solutions. In the future, users will see promising prospects for the application of entirely new concepts of information handling. A key application area of AI is information administration for cyber threats. AI-based systems are already used in several applications, like protection measures hidden inside software. However, AI will gain wider application as massive databases for healthcare systems are developed. Many technologies are normally referred to as most healthcare databases are incorporating AI for cybersecurity. However, there are many distinct technologies that, if they reach a high degree of sophistication, would bring about the introduction of smarter-than-human intelligence.

## How to Enhance Cybersecurity for AI

The improvement of AI and machine learning technologies will impact cybersecurity in numerous ways. The security industry has successfully adopted some AI-based techniques (Bertino et al., 2021). Cyber attackers can assault network systems from anywhere in the world at any time. It is observed that cybersecurity applications have acquired large technological development over the last few years.

There are many approaches to enhance cybersecurity for AI:
- Improving cyber threat detection with machine learning
- AI and machine learning play a necessary role in mitigating phishing attacks
- Automated network security
- Strong behavioral analytics

AI and machine learning make smarter cybersecurity feasible, and these rising technologies have great potential applications in healthcare, finance, retail, etc. There are several comparable issues regarding how AI systems remain invulnerable when used to augment the safety of accumulated healthcare data and computer networks. The application of AI security solutions to respond to rapidly evolving threats makes the need to secure AI itself even more pressing. It is all the more necessary that those algorithms be protected from interference, compromise, or misuse if we depend on machine learning algorithms to detect and shield from cyberattacks. Increasing dependence on AI for critical functions and services will not only create greater incentives for attackers to target these algorithms but also increase the potential for each successful assault to have more severe consequences.

The enhancement of cybersecurity and security for AI is one of the key challenges. AI has become a more popular and widely used technology in many different sectors, including the healthcare industry. Policymakers find it increasingly indispensable to consider the intersection of cybersecurity with AI. Recently, several researchers have been working to decrease the opportunity for adversaries to access private AI training data or models in healthcare systems during the Covid-19 era. As noted above, one of the key safety threats to AI systems is the opportunity for adversaries to compromise the integrity of their decision-making processes. This occurs when adversaries take direct control of an AI device to determine the outputs the system generates and the decisions it makes. An attacker may try to affect these selections directly by delivering malicious inputs or training data to an AI model.

## Improving the Adoption of Digital Health Care with Internet of Things and Big Data Technologies

### Evidence-Based Care

The exponential increase in the volume of health-care data generated by IoT devices makes information processing very challenging. Big data can provide evidence-based care by aggregating datasets from diverse sources. Analysis of data can grant useful insights into detecting anomalies and offering appropriate redress to patients. Intelligent analysis using new strategies can supply significant financial savings on the order of several hundred billion dollars, which amounts to about eight percent of national health expenses.
