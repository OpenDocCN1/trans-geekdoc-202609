
$$J_{\ast}^{(i)} - J(\mathbf{\theta}_{\ast}) \leq \frac{\left\| \mathbf{\theta}^{(0)} - \mathbf{\theta}_{\ast} \right\|^{2}}{2\sum_{k = 1}^{i}\mu_{k}} + \frac{\sum_{k = 1}^{i}\mu_{k}^{2}}{2\sum_{k = 1}^{i}\mu_{k}}G^{2}.$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000147/si325.png) (8.54)

Leaving *i* to grow to infinity and taking into account the assumptions, the claim is proved. □

There are a number of variants of this proof. Other choices for the diminishing sequence can also guarantee convergence, such as $\mu_{i} = 1/\sqrt{i}$.

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000147/si326.png)

Moreover, in certain cases, some of the assumptions may be relaxed. Note that the assumption of the subgradient being bounded is guaranteed if *J* is *γ*-Lipschitz continuous (Problem [8.26](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml#o0160)), that is, there is $\gamma > 0$ such that

$$|J(\mathbf{y}) - J(\mathbf{x})| \leq \gamma \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x}\|, \quad \forall \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{(l)}.$$
