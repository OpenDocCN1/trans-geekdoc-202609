
$P(\mathbf{x}_{n}|\mathbf{x}_{n - 1}) = \prod\limits_{i = 1}^{K}\prod\limits_{j = 1}^{K}P_{ij}^{x_{n - 1,j}x_{n,i}}.$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000226/si225.png)(16.33)

Indeed, if the jump is from a specific state *j* at time *n* − 1 to a specific state *i* at time *n*, then the only term that survives in the previous product is the corresponding factor, *P*[*i**j*]. The joint probability distribution of the complete set, as a direct consequence of the Bayesian network model of [Fig. 16.15](#Chp016_805-853_B9780443292385000226.xhtml#f0080), is written as

$p(Y,X) = P(\mathbf{x}_{1})p(\mathbf{y}_{1}|\mathbf{x}_{1})\prod\limits_{n = 2}^{N}P(\mathbf{x}_{n}|\mathbf{x}_{n - 1})p(\mathbf{y}_{n}|\mathbf{x}_{n}),$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000226/si227.png)(16.34)

where

$p(\mathbf{y}_{n}|\mathbf{x}_{n}) = \prod\limits_{k = 1}^{K}\left( p(\mathbf{y}_{n}|k;\mathbf{\theta}_{k}) \right)^{x_{n,k}}.$
