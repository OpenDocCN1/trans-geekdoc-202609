In Section [13.5](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#s0045), we commented on the inadequacy of a Gaussian prior to provide a reasonable statistical description of a sparse random vector. A heavy-tailed distribution that enjoys popularity for sparse modeling is the Laplacian one. After all, adopting a Laplacian prior to **θ**, that is,

```
p(θ) = ∏_{k=1}^K p(θ_k) = ∏_{k=1}^K (λ/2) exp(-λ|θ_k|)
```

and a Gaussian conditional PDF, `p(y|θ)`, for the observations **y** as in Eq. [(13.22)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq29), makes the MAP estimation identical to our familiar LASSO task, discussed in Chapter [9](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml). In this vein, we will build this section around the Laplacian PDF. [Fig. 13.13](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#f0075)A shows the Laplacian for different values of `λ`. In [13.13](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#f0075)B the two-dimensional plot is provided, from which the respect that this PDF shows to sparse solutions is readily observed, by its slower decay along the two axes.

The problem with the Laplacian PDF is that its presence in Eq. [(12.14)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml) makes the computation of the integral computationally intractable. Also, recall from Section [12.8](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml) that the Laplacian PDF does not belong to the computationally attractive exponential family. To facilitate its treatment, we will employ the variational bound approximation method in order to approximate the Laplacian by a Gaussian, following Eqs. [(13.90)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq135) and [(13.92)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq138). The variational parameters will be determined by maximizing the respective evidence via the EM algorithm. This method in recovering sparse solutions was first introduced in [21] in the context of dictionary learning.
