
## 6.2

Show that under (a) the independence assumption of successive observation vectors and (b) the presence of white noise independent of the input, the LS estimator is asymptotically distributed according to the normal distribution, i.e.,

$$
\sqrt{N}\left( \mathbf{\theta} - \mathbf{\theta}_{0} \right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\left( 0,\sigma_{\eta}^{2}\Sigma_{x}^{- 1} \right),
$$

where $\sigma_{\eta}^{2}$ is the noise variance and $\Sigma_{x}$ the covariance matrix of the input observation vectors, assuming that it is invertible.

## 6.3

Let $X \in \mathbb{C}^{(m \times l)}$. Then show that the two matrices

$X X^{(H)}$ and $X^{(H)} X$

have the same nonzero eigenvalues.

## 6.4

Show that if $X \in \mathbb{C}^{(m \times l)}$, then the eigenvalues of $X X^{(H)}$ ($X^{(H)} X$) are real and nonnegative. Moreover, show that if $\lambda_{i} \neq \lambda_{j}$, $\mathbf{v}_{i} \perp \mathbf{v}_{j}$.

## 6.5

Let $X \in \mathbb{C}^{(m \times l)}$. Show that if $\mathbf{v}_{i}$ is the normalized eigenvector of $X^{(H)} X$, corresponding to $\lambda_{i} \neq 0$, then the corresponding normalized eigenvector $\mathbf{u}_{i}$ of $X X^{(H)}$ is given by

$$
\mathbf{u}_{i} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}X\mathbf{v}_{i}.
$$
