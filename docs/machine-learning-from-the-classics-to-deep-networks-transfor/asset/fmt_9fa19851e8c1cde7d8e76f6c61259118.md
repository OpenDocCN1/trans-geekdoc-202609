## Text-to-image generation: a brief discussion

The previously discussed state-of-the-art text-to-image generators turn out to be very powerful to combine unrelated concepts such as objects, textures, color, to generate sampling examples with unprecedented degree of photorealism. This provides them with significant potential for their deployment and use to various applications. However, due to their powerful capabilities, a number of ethical issues are raised.

For example, such models can become powerful tools for malicious actors to generate harmful content and deepfakes^([^(153)](#fn153))

Spreading of disinformation is not only harmful to individuals, e.g., bullying or sexual abuse. It goes much beyond and poses threats that undermine basic core functions and norms related to our social and political structure as human beings. As social beings, we need to participate in collective actions based on well-informed decision-making. The potential of large-scale disinformation that spreads fast can seriously harm this social activity that is unique to human beings, and it comprises one of the basic forces that drives progress and civilization, e.g., [143].

Furthermore, the related platforms pose a big threat for artists and graphic designers. To this direction, some of the latest versions of such platforms are designed to decline requests to generate images in the style of living artists.

## Remarks 19.13

*   **Performance measures**: We have already discussed in Section [19.7.5](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#s0270) that a major goal in designing a generative network is the generated samples to be of good quality and exhibit wide coverage. In other words, the generated samples should be "faithful" to the real examples used for training and, also, to be as diverse as possible and their variation should match that in the training set. At the end of Section [19.2.5](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#s0040), the inception score (IS) and the Fréchet inception distance (FID) were discussed as related measures. These are also used for evaluating performance in diffusion models. However, these metrics, from their definition, attempt to measure the previous concepts via a single index.

*   More recently, e.g., in [162], [106], it is proposed that quality and diversity could be measured via two separate metrics. The *precision* measures the average sample quality and the *recall* quantifies the coverage of the sample distribution. In a more "technical" jargon, let `p[r]` and `p[g]` be the distributions for the real and generated samples, respectively. *Precision* is the probability that a randomly generated sample from `p[g]` falls within the support of `p[r]`. *Recall* is the probability that a randomly drawn sample from `p[r]` falls within the support of `p[g]`.

*   **Super-resolution**: In the applications section, the emphasis was on text-to-image generation. However, many of the concepts that were discussed there can be "borrowed" and used in many other applications. Take, as an example, a related area in computer vision, that of super-resolution (SR). The task in SR is to generate a high-resolution image, `y ∈ ℝ^(h*w)` (vectorized form of an image array `Y ∈ ℝ^(h×w)`), given a low-resolution version of it, `x ∈ ℝ^(h̄*w̄)` (vectorized form of an image `X ∈ ℝ^(h̄×w̄)`) where, `h > h̄` and `w > w̄`.
