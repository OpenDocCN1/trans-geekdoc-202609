
$$
\mathcal{L}\left( y,f(\mathbf{x}) \right) = \begin{cases} 
\epsilon|y - f(\mathbf{x})| - \frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}, & \quad\text{if}\quad|y - f(\mathbf{x})| > \epsilon, \\ 
\frac{1}{2}|y - f(\mathbf{x})|^{2}, & \quad\text{if}\quad|y - f(\mathbf{x})| \leqslant \epsilon, 
\end{cases}
$$

for some parameter $\epsilon$. This is known as the Huber loss function, and it is shown in [Fig. 11.10](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#f0055). A loss function that can approximate the Huber one and, as we will see, turns out to have some nice computational properties, is the so-called *linear $\epsilon$*-insensitive loss function, defined as (see also Chapter [8](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml))

$$
\mathcal{L}\left( y,f(\mathbf{x}) \right) = \begin{cases} 
|y - f(\mathbf{x})| - \epsilon, & \quad\text{if}\quad|y - f(\mathbf{x})| > \epsilon, \\ 
0, & \quad\text{if}\quad|y - f(\mathbf{x})| \leqslant \epsilon, 
\end{cases}
$$
