
$$
\alpha_{nt} = \frac{\exp(e_{nt})}{\sum_{k = 1}^{N}\exp(e_{nk})},\ \text{with}\ e_{nt} = \phi(\mathbf{h}_{t}^{T}\mathbf{h}_{n - 1}^{\prime}),
$$

where $\phi$ is a nonlinearity that is implemented via a neural network, whose parameters are "learned" during training. The output state vector at the $t$th stage of the encoder, $\mathbf{h}[t]$, is formed as the concatenation of the respective forward ($\overset{\rightarrow}{\mathbf{h}}_{t}$) and backward ($\overset{\leftarrow}{\mathbf{h}}_{t}$) state vectors. The variable $e[n t]$ is known as the *alignment* model and accounts on how well the state vectors of the encoder, at time $t$, and the decoder, at time $n - 1$, match, i.e., how similar they are. Note that similarity is measured through the inner product of the respective vectors.

The context vectors are passed over to a decoder RNN that outputs the sequence of predictions, i.e., $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_{n}$, $n = 1, 2, \dots, N^{\prime}$. Note that, in general, the size of the output sequence at the decoder, $N^{\prime}$, is different from that of the input sequence at the encoder, $N$. The encoder-decoder network is trained with the goal the decoder to act as a *predictor*. In other words, the decoder is trained to predict at stage $n$ ($\widehat{\mathbf{y}}_{n}$), the corresponding target word ($\mathbf{y}[n]$), given the input encodings, i.e., $\mathbf{c}[n]$, as well as the previous target words, i.e., $\mathbf{y}[1], \mathbf{y}[2], \dots, \mathbf{y}[n - 1]$. A detailed description of the RNN network in the context of the translation task can be downloaded from the book's website under the Additional Material part that is associated with the current chapter.

Using an RNN encoder, the context-related information is unveiled by two mechanisms. One is through the hidden states. However, recall that the states are processed sequentially. Thus, disambiguating the word, e.g., "bank," has to be delayed until the last word "river" (or "road") has been processed. The second mechanism is by applying attention with the aim to extract more information by assigning weights to the sequentially formed states. These weights are trained to quantify *similarity* between the state vectors of the decoder and those of the encoder (see the Additional Material from the book's website for more details). Furthermore, this information has to be *aligned* with the sequentially formed states in the decoder. As long as the phrases are short, RNNs can work well.
