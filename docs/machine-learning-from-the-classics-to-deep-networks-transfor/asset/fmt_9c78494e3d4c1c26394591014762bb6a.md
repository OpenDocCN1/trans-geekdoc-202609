
### Diffusion Gradient Descent

$$
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = {\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{(i)} := \sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}_{k \setminus k}} b_{mk} \boldsymbol{\psi}_{m}^{(i)}
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si497.png)

where

$$
\sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}_{k \setminus k}} b_{mk} = 1, \quad b_{mk} \geqslant 0,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si498.png)

and $\mathcal{N}_{k \setminus k}$ denotes the elements in $\mathcal{N}_{k}$ excluding $k$. In other words, at each iteration, we update $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{k}$ so as to move it toward the descent direction of the local cost and at the same time we constrain it to stay close to the convex combination of the rest of the updates, which are obtained during the computations in Step 1 from *all* the nodes in its neighborhood. Thus, we end up with the following recursions.

#### Diffusion Gradient Descent

![](images/B9780443292385000111/fx018.jpg)

where we set

$$
a_{kk} = 1 - \mu_{k}\lambda \quad \text{and} \quad a_{mk} = \mu_{k}\lambda b_{mk},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si502.png) (5.88)

which leads to

$$
\sum_{m \in \mathcal{N}_{k}} a_{mk} = 1, \quad a_{mk} \geqslant 0,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si503.png) (5.89)

which are nonnegative for small enough values of $\mu_{k}\lambda$. Note that by setting $a_{mk} = 0$, $m \notin \mathcal{N}_{k}$ and defining $A$ to be the matrix with entries $[A]_{mk} = a_{mk}$, we can write

$$
\sum_{m = 1}^{K} a_{mk} = 1 \ \Rightarrow \ A^{T}\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si508.png) (5.90)

that is, $A$ is a left stochastic matrix. It is important to stress here that, irrespective of our derivation before, *any* left stochastic matrix $A$ can be used in Eq. (5.87).
