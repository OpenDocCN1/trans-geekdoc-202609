The precision `β` is also treated as an unknown deterministic parameter. Our goal is (a) to obtain estimates for `β` and `α[k]`, `k = 0, 1, …, K − 1`, and (b) to compute the predictive distribution, `p(y|x, y)`, where `y` is the vector of observations.^[92]

In this section, we will adopt a different path, exploiting the Gaussian nature of the involved PDFs. A Type II maximum likelihood method will be employed, which was introduced in [Remarks 12.2](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml). Type II likelihood is defined as the marginal one, after integrating out the parameters `θ`. Following the discussion in Section [12.2](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml) and for our current needs, Eq. [(12.15)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml) is written as

`p(y; α, β) = N(y|0, β^(−1)I + ΦA^(−1)Φ^(T))`.

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si225.png) (13.57)

`p(θ|y; α, β) = N(θ|μ, Σ; α, β)`,
