
### 数学推导

$$
p(\mathbf{y};\beta) = \int\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y}|\Phi\mathbf{\theta},\beta^{-1}I)p(\mathbf{\theta})\ d\mathbf{\theta} \geqslant \left( \int\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y}|\Phi\mathbf{\theta},\beta^{-1}I)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{\theta}|0,\Xi)\ d\mathbf{\theta} \right)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}\phi(\xi_{k}).
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si349.png)

We know by now that the integral results in a new Gaussian function (recall Eq. [(12.15)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml)), and hence

$$
p(\mathbf{y};\beta) \geqslant \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y}|0,\beta^{-1}I + \Phi\Xi\Phi^{T})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}\phi(\xi_{k}) := \widehat{p}(\mathbf{y};\beta,\Xi).
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si350.png)(13.93)

The unknown values of `β` and `Ξ` could be obtained by direct maximization of the previous bound. An alternative EM algorithm approach, similar to that of Section [12.5](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml), is provided in the chapter's Additional Material part that can be downloaded from the website of the book.

### Remarks 13.4

*   As we have already commented in Chapter [9](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml), sparsity-aware learning has been a field of intense research. Undoubtedly this is also the case for the Bayesian approach to sparsity promoting models. So far, we presented a hierarchical approach in Section [13.5](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#s0045), where sparsity was indirectly imposed by associating a gamma PDF prior on each one of the precision variables individually; this led to an equivalent high-tail Student's `t` PDF description of the involved parameters `θ`. Sparse Bayesian learning was described in Section [13.6](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#s0050). In the current section, a Laplacian prior was imposed on `θ` in order to promote sparseness. These are not the only possibilities. We focused on them in order to demonstrate some possible paths to treat the evidence maximization whenever the resulting integral is computationally "awkward."

*   In [18], sparsity in the Bayesian framework is attacked by imposing a Gaussian prior on the parameters, treating variance as latent variables with an exponential prior. Such modeling is equivalent to a Laplacian PDF, once variances are integrated out. The EM procedure is then used to compute the required estimates. Also in this paper, the use of Jeffreys' prior `p(x) ≈ 1/x` is proposed as an alternative to the Laplacian one.

*   In [5], sparsity is imposed in a similar way as before, but in the hierarchical model, the parameter controlling the exponential prior of the precisions is also treated as a latent variable with a Jeffreys' prior.
