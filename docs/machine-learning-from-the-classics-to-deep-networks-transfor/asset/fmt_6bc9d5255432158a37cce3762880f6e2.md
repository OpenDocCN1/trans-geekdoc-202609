
Consider a stick of unit length ([Fig. 13.16](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#f0095)). The stick is divided into a sequence of infinitely many segments of length `P[i]`, `i = 1, 2, …`, according to the following algorithm. First, make a draw of a beta (Chapter [2](#Chp002_19-64_B9780443292385000081.xhtml)) distributed random variable, `β[1] ∼ Beta(β|1, α)`, and break off a segment of the stick of length equal to `β[1]`; we set `P[1] = β[1]`. The length of the remaining segment is `1 − β[1]`. Then make another draw, `β[2] ∼ Beta(β|1, α)`, and break off another segment in proportion to `β[2]`. Thus, the length of the discarded segment is equal to `β[2](1 − β[1])` and we set `P[2]` equal to its length. The length of the remaining segment is, obviously, `(1 − β[1]) − β[2](1 − β[1]) = (1 − β[1])(1 − β[2])`. Following this rationale recursively, by breaking off pieces from the remaining segments, and starting at `P[1] = β[1] ∼ Beta(β|1, α)`, the `i`th step of the algorithm is

```
P[i] = β[i] ∏_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 − β[j]),  β[i] ∼ Beta(β|1, α)
```

Using the resulting sequence of probability values, `P[i]`, a random distribution is formed according to Eq. [(13.113)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq169), with `θ[i]` drawn i.i.d. from `G[0]`. It can be shown that the resulting distribution is a DP, i.e., `G ∼ DP(α, G[0])`.

![Figure 13.16 The stick breaking construction of a DP. At each iteration, we sample from a beta distribution Beta(β|1,α) and we break off a piece from the remaining segment in proportion to the value of the sample.](images/B9780443292385000196/gr016.jpg)

[Figure 13.16](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#cf1420) The stick breaking construction of a DP. At each iteration, we sample from a beta distribution `Beta(β|1,α)` and we break off a piece from the remaining segment in proportion to the value of the sample.

Note that as `i` increases, the probability values decrease, because smaller and smaller fractions of the stick remain. Thus, out of the infinite possible terms in Eq. [(13.113)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq169) only a relatively small number survive that have significant contribution. Moreover, the probability values associated with these surviving terms are the first ones to be generated, which, in practice, is very important when one implements a DP to generate a prior (see, e.g., [60]).

A concise tutorial concerning DPs can also be found in [19]. There, a number of sites with publicly available software tools are also provided.
