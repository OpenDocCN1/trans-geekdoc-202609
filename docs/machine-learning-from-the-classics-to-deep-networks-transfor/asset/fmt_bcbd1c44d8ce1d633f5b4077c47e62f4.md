
$$
\mathcal{W}_{\theta} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{|\theta_{1}|} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{|\theta_{2}|} & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \frac{1}{|\theta_{l}|} \\ \end{bmatrix},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000160/si197.png)

and where in the case of $\theta[i] = 0$, for some $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, l\}$, the respective coefficient of $\mathcal{W}[\theta]$ is defined to be 1. If $\mathcal{W}[\theta]$ were a constant weighting matrix, that is, $\mathcal{W}[\theta] := \mathcal{W}[\tilde{\theta}]$, for some fixed $\widetilde{\mathbf{\theta}}$, then obtaining the minimum

$$
\widehat{\mathbf{\theta}} = {\arg\min}_{\mathbf{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{l}}\ \left\| \mathbf{y} - X\mathbf{\theta} \right\|_{2}^{2} + \lambda\mathbf{\theta}^{T}\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\theta}}\mathbf{\theta},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000160/si202.png)

would be straightforward and similar to the ridge regression. In the iterative reweighted scheme, $\mathcal{W}[\tilde{\theta}]$ is replaced by $\mathcal{W}[\theta^{(i)}]$, formed by using the respective estimates of the parameters, which have been obtained from the previous iteration, that is, $\widetilde{\mathbf{\theta}} := \mathbf{\theta}^{(i)}$, as we did before. In the sequel, each iteration solves a weighted ridge regression task.
