
$$ \mathbf{y} = \frac{\mathbf{\theta}^{T}\mathbf{x}}{||\mathbf{\theta}||}\frac{\mathbf{\theta}}{||\mathbf{\theta}||} $$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/si229.png)

where $\frac{\mathbf{\theta}}{||\mathbf{\theta}||}$ is the unit norm vector in the direction of `θ`. From now on, we will focus on the scalar value of the projection, $y := \mathbf{\theta}^{(T)}\mathbf{x}$, and ignore the scaling factor in the denominator, because scaling all features by the same value has no effect on our discussion. The goal, now, is to select that direction `θ` so that after projecting along this direction, (a) the data in the two classes are as far away as possible from each other, and (b) the respective variances of the points around their means, in each one of the classes, are as small as possible. A criterion that quantifies the aforementioned goal is *Fisher's discriminant ratio* (FDR), defined as

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/fx009.jpg) (7.62)

where $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are the (scalar) mean values of the two classes after the projection along `θ`, meaning

$$ \mu_k = \mathbf{\theta}^{T}\mathbf{\mu}_k, \quad k = 1, 2. $$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/si234.png)

However, we have

$$ (\mu_1 - \mu_2)^2 = \mathbf{\theta}^{T}(\mathbf{\mu}_1 - \mathbf{\mu}_2)(\mathbf{\mu}_1 - \mathbf{\mu}_2)^{T}\mathbf{\theta} = \mathbf{\theta}^{T}S_b\mathbf{\theta}, \quad S_b := (\mathbf{\mu}_1 - \mathbf{\mu}_2)(\mathbf{\mu}_1 - \mathbf{\mu}_2)^{T}. $$
