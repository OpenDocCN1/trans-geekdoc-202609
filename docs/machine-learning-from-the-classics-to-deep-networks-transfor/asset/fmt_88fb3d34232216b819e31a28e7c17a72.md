
Let us now consider two different input samples, e.g., images represented as vectors, `x` and `x'`. Each one of them generates a corresponding pair of transformed versions, i.e., `(x̃_i, x̃_j)` and `(x̃'_i, x̃'_j)`. According to the previously given definitions, `(x̃_i, x̃_j)` and `(x̃'_i, x̃'_j)` form positive pairs/samples, while pairs such as `(x̃_i, x̃'_j)` or `(x̃'_i, x̃_j)` correspond to negative pairs/samples, since they originate from different images.

For the current notational needs, let us reserve `N_t` to denote the number of the available training samples. After the data augmentation step, there will be `2 * N_t` samples. Also, for every positive pair of samples, there will be `2 * (N_t - 1)` negative ones.

The goal of training is to obtain estimates of the involved parameters, `θ`, `ϕ`, so that the outputs that correspond to positive pairs to be "pulled" together and those associated with negative ones to be "pushed" apart.
