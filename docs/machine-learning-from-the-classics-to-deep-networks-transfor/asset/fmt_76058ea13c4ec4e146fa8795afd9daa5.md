
Let us now consider the following identity:

$$p(x) \equiv \int_0^{p(x)} 1 \, dx = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \chi_{[0, p(x)]}(u) \, du$$

where $\chi_{[0, p(x)]}(\cdot)$ is our familiar characteristic function in the interval $[0, p(x)]$, that is,

$$
\chi_{[0, p(x)]}(u) = \begin{cases}
1, & \quad 0 \leqslant u \leqslant p(x), \\
0, & \quad \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Comparing Eqs. [(14.21)](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml#deq35) and [(14.22)](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml#deq36), it turns out that $\chi_{[0, p(x)]}(u)$ can be interpreted as the joint PDF of the pair $(x, u)$ defined over the set

$$\mathcal{A} = \{(x, u) : x \in \mathbb{R}, 0 \le u \le p(x)\}.$$
