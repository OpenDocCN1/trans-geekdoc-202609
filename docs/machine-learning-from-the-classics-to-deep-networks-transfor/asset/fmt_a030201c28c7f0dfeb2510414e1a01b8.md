The reverse process follows a `learned` backward Markov chain, `pθ(xt−1|xt)`, that tries to “match” the diffusion steps of the forward path.

One could also look at the reverse path as a denoising process, whose aim is to remove the noise that was gradually added during the forward process. From this point of view, the underlying action resembles that of the denoising autoencoders (Section [19.2.4](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#s0035)).

### Training

The goal of training is to obtain estimates of the parameters `θ`. Note that these parameters implicitly define the mean values and the variances of the involved conditionals in the reverse path. The loss function to be optimized is the usual “suspect,” i.e., the likelihood function, `pθ(x0)`. In other words, the objective is to select `θ` such that the probability `pθ(x0)`, assigned to `x0`, is as large as possible. To this end, the variational approximation method, which has been introduced and discussed in Chapters [12](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml) and [13](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml), will be mobilized.
