
$$
\ln p(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n};\mathbf{\theta}) = \sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\ln p(\mathbf{x}_{n};\mathbf{\theta}) \geq \sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{\theta},\mathbf{\phi};\mathbf{x}_{n}),
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si135.png)

where

$$
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{\theta},\mathbf{\phi};\mathbf{x}[n]) := \mathbb{E}_{q}\left[ \ln p(\mathbf{x}[n],\mathbf{z};\mathbf{\theta}) - \ln q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}[n];\mathbf{\phi}) \right].
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si136.png)(19.21)

Note that in the previous equation, we explicitly introduce the notation for the posterior $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}[n];\mathbf{\phi})$![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si137.png), in contrast to the definition of the lower bound defined in Eq. [(12.63)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml), where the definition of the bound is given in terms of any distribution of the latent functions. After applying the Bayes theorem on the joint distribution term and recalling the definition of the KL divergence (see Eq. [(2.161)](#Chp002_19-64_B9780443292385000081.xhtml)), Eq. [(19.21)](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#deq46) can be rewritten as,

$$
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{\theta},\mathbf{\phi};\mathbf{x}_{n}) = \underbrace{\underset{\text{reconstruction term}}{\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[ \ln p(\mathbf{x}_{n}|\mathbf{z};\mathbf{\theta}) \right]}} - \underbrace{\underset{\text{prior matching term}}{\text{KL}\left( q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_{n};\mathbf{\phi})||p(\mathbf{z}) \right)}},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si138.png)(19.22)
