
### 10.4.4 Sparse adaptive projection subgradient method (SpAPSM)

The APSM family of algorithms was introduced in Chapter [8](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml), as one among the most popular techniques for online/adaptive learning. As pointed out there, a major advantage of this algorithmic family is that one can readily incorporate convex constraints. In Chapter [8](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml), APSM was used as an alternative to methods that build around the sum of squared errors cost function, such as the LMS and the RLS. The rationale behind APSM is that because the data are assumed to be generated by a regression model, the unknown vector could be estimated by finding a point in the intersection of a sequence of hyperslabs that are defined by the data points, that is, `S_n[ε] := {θ ∈ ℝ^l : |y[n] − x[n]^T θ| ≤ ε}`. Also, it was pointed out that such a model is very natural when the noise is bounded. When dealing with sparse vectors, there is an additional constraint that we want our solution to satisfy, that is, `‖θ‖_1 ≤ ρ` (see also the LASSO formulation [(9.7)](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml)).
