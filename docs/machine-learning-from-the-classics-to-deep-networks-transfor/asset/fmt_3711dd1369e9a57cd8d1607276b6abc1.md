
*p*(**y**) = 𝒩(**y**|0, 𝒦 + *σ*[*η*]²*I*).

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si661.png) (13.139)

This is also obvious from the fact that the sum of two independent Gaussian variables is also Gaussian and the mean and covariance matrix can directly be obtained from Eq. [(13.138)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq225).

To obtain *p*(*y*|**x**, **y**) we can use Eq. [(13.139)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq229) and apply it recursively. It will also be useful here to bring into the notation the number of available observations, *N*, explicitly and write

```
\mathbf{y}_{N + 1}^{\ } = \begin{bmatrix} y \\ \mathbf{y}_{N} \\ \end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{y}_{N}: = {\lbrack y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\rbrack}^{T}.
```

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si662.png)

From Eq. [(13.139)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq229), **y**[*N* + 1] follows a Gaussian distribution

*p*(**y**[*N* + 1] | 0, *Σ*[*N* + 1]),

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si664.png)

with

*Σ*[*N* + 1] := 𝒦[*N* + 1] + *σ*[*η*]²*I*[*N* + 1].

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si665.png)

Then, from the product rule, we have

```
p(y|\mathbf{y}_{N}) = \frac{p(\mathbf{y}_{N + 1})}{p(\mathbf{y}_{N})}.
```

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000196/si666.png) (13.140)

However, because the joint PDF is Gaussian, the conditional in Eq. [(13.140)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq233) is also Gaussian. The respective mean and variance are computed by partitioning the matrix *Σ*[*N* + 1] (see Appendix of Chapter [12](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml), Eqs. (12.134) and (12.133)).
