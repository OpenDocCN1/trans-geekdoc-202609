
$R_{\phi} := \mathbb{E}\left[ \mathbf{\phi}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{\phi}^{T}(\mathbf{x}) \right] \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\mathbf{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{n})\mathbf{\phi}^{T}(\mathbf{x}_{n}) = \frac{1}{N}\Phi^{T}\Phi$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000184/si77.png)

or

$\Phi^{(T)}\Phi \approx N R[\phi]$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000184/si78.png)(12.24)

where $R[\phi]$ is the autocorrelation matrix of the random vector $\mathbf{\phi}(\mathbf{x})$.

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000184/si79.png)![Equation](images/B9780443292385000184/si80.png)

Employing Eq. [(12.24)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq29) into Eq. [(12.23)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq27) leads to

$\sigma_{y}^{2} \approx \sigma_{\eta}^{2}\left( 1 + \sigma_{\theta}^{2}\mathbf{\phi}^{T}(\mathbf{x})(\sigma_{\eta}^{2}I + N\sigma_{\theta}^{2}R[\phi])^{-1}\mathbf{\phi}(\mathbf{x}) \right)$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000184/si81.png)(12.25)

which for large enough $N$ becomes

$\sigma_{y}^{2} \approx \sigma_{\eta}^{2}\left( 1 + \frac{1}{N}\mathbf{\phi}^{T}(\mathbf{x})R_{\phi}^{-1}\mathbf{\phi}(\mathbf{x}) \right).$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000184/si82.png)

Thus, for a large number of observations, $\sigma_{y}^{2} \to \sigma_{\eta}^{2}$, and our uncertainty is contributed by the noise source, which cannot be reduced anymore. For smaller values of $N$, there is extra uncertainty associated with the parameter $\mathbf{\theta}$, measured by $\sigma_{\theta}^{2}$ in Eq. [(12.25)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq30).
