Besides the CLIP-based guidance, GLIDE also used the classifier-free guidance concept of Section [19.84](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#deq166), where the label *y* is replaced by the associated captions **t** and ∅ corresponds to an empty text sequence. ### unCLIP

In \[152\], a three-step approach is used in the text-to-image generation task.

*   A CLIP network is first trained to learn a joint representation of text and images in a latent space, via the two encoders' output embeddings.
*   A so-called *prior* diffusion model is trained to generate *CLIP image embeddings*, which are *conditioned* on the respective encoded text captions and, optionally, on the corresponding CLIP text embeddings. To improve sample quality, the classifier-free guidance approach was also employed by randomly dropping text conditioning information 10% of the time during training.
*   A diffusion model, called the decoder, is employed which is trained to generate images *conditioned* on *CLIP image embeddings* that are generated from the prior diffusion model. Optionally, conditioning on encoded text captions may be employed. The classifier-free guidance was also used in the decoder, by randomly setting the CLIP embeddings to zero 10% of the time, and randomly dropping the text caption encodings 50% of the time during training. The decoder also involves two super-resolution diffusion upsampler models.

While the decoder could work with direct CLIP image embeddings, the prior can *generate* image embeddings conditioned on the caption inputs. The name unCLIP indicates that images are generated by inverting the CLIP image encoder. Furthermore, this setup facilitates image manipulations in the latent (CLIP embedding) space, such as interpolation, editing, etc.
