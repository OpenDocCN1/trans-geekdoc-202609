
## Subdifferential of Absolute Value Function

The subdifferential of $f(x) = |x|$ is given by:

$$
\partial f(x) = \begin{cases}
\{ \text{sgn}(x) \}, & \text{if } x \neq 0, \\
\{ g : g \in [-1, 1] \}, & \text{if } x = 0,
\end{cases}
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000147/si289.png)

where `sgn(·)` is the sign function, being equal to 1 if its argument is positive and −1 if the argument is negative.

Indeed, if $x > 0$, then

$$
g = \frac{dx}{dx} = 1,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000147/si292.png)

and similarly $g = -1$, if $x < 0$. For $x = 0$, any $g \in [-1, 1]$ satisfies

$$
g(y - 0) + 0 = gy \le |y|,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000147/si297.png)

and it is a subgradient. This is illustrated in [Fig. 8.29](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml#f0155).

At the point x1, there is a unique subgradient that coincides with the gradient and defines the unique tangent hyperplane at the respective point of the graph.](images/B9780443292385000147/gr028.jpg)

## Lemma 8.2

**Given a convex function** $f: \mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^l \mapsto \mathbb{R}$, a point `x[*] ∈ X` **is a minimizer of f if and only if the zero vector belongs to its subdifferential set, that is,**

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000147/fx023.jpg) (8.47)

### Proof

The proof is straightforward from the definition of a subgradient. Indeed, assume that `0 ∈ ∂f(x[*])`. Then the following is valid:

$$
f(\mathbf{y}) \ge f(\mathbf{x}^*) + \mathbf{0}^T(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x}^*), \quad \forall \mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{X},
$$
