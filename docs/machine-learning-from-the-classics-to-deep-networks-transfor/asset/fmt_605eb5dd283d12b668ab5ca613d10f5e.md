
In words, both the regressors `x[n]` and the outputs `y[n]` are corrected in order for the points `(y[n] - e[n], x[n] - e[n])`, `n = 1, 2, ..., N`, to lie on a hyperplane in `ℝ^(l+1)`. Also, once such a hyperplane is computed and it is unique, it has an interesting interpretation. It is the hyperplane that *minimizes the total squared distance* of all the training points `(y[n], x[n])` from it. Moreover, the corrected points `(y[n] - e[n], x[n] - e[n])`, `n = 1, 2, ..., N`, are the *orthogonal projections* of the respective `(y[n], x[n])` training examples onto this hyperplane (see Additional Material part). This is illustrated in [Fig. 6.12](#Chp006_249-291_B9780443292385000123.xhtml#f0075).

The Total Least-Squares method has been used in a number of applications, such as computer vision [47], system identification [59], speech and image processing [25, 51], and spectral analysis [63]. A distributed algorithm for solving the TLS task in ad hoc sensor networks has been proposed in [4]. A recursive scheme for the efficient solution of the TLS task has appeared in [12]. For further reading, the interested reader can look at [37, 64] and the references therein.
