
### 19.3.13 Decoder: inference

$$
\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{QK^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}} \right)V = \begin{bmatrix} s_{00} & \ldots & s_{0,N + 1} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ s_{N^{\prime} + 1,0} & \ldots & s_{N^{\prime} + 1,N + 1} \end{bmatrix}V \in \mathbb{R}^{(N^{\prime} + 2) \times d_{v}},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si348.png)

where now the value matrix $V \in \mathbb{R}^{(N + 2) \times d_{v}}$ corresponds to the $N + 2$ outputs of the encoder. The vector of the $n$th row of the attention matrix is

$$
\mathbf{z}_{n}^{\prime} = \sum\limits_{j = 0}^{N + 1}s_{nj}\mathbf{v}_{j},\ n = 0,1,\ldots,N^{\prime} + 1.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si350.png)

In other words, in this layer, the updated embeddings of the decoder are expressed as linear combinations of the value vectors associated with the outputs of the encoder. This makes a lot of sense. The final outputs of the decoder should be the response to the input sequence of the encoder. However, the input to the encoder is optimally represented by the embedding vectors at its output, which encode the rich contextual information that resides in the input sequence.

**Step 6**: In this final step, the corresponding Head matrix is computed, i.e.,

$$
\text{Head} = \text{Attention}(Q, K, V)W^{0} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N^{\prime} + 2) \times d_{m}}.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si351.png)

The generalization to the multi-head case is straightforward, and it follows the same philosophy as in Section [19.3.6](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#s0100).

The rest of the layers of the decoder block in [Fig. 19.13](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#f0070), i.e., the two "Add & Normalize" layers and the fully connected network layer have the same structure as their counterparts in the encoder blocks.
