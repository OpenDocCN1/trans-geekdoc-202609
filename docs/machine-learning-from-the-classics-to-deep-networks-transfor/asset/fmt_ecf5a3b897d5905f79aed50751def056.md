
### 15.7.2 Exact inference in trees

$$ \mu_b(x_{k-1}) = \sum_{x_k} \psi_{k-1,k}(x_{k-1}, x_k) \mu_b(x_k), \quad \mu_f(x_{k+1}) = \sum_{x_k} \psi_{k,k+1}(x_k, x_{k+1}) \mu_f(x_k) $$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000214/si259.png)

are replaced by

$$ \mu_b(x_{k-1}) = \psi_{k-1,k}(x_{k-1}, \hat{x}_k) \mu_b(\hat{x}_k), \quad \mu_f(x_{k+1}) = \psi_{k,k+1}(\hat{x}_k, x_{k+1}) \mu_f(\hat{x}_k) $$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000214/si260.png)

In other words, $x_k$ is considered a delta function at the *instantiated* value. Once $P(x_j, x_k = \hat{x}_k)$ has been obtained, normalization is straightforward and is locally performed at the $j$th node. The procedure can be generalized when more than one variable is observed.
