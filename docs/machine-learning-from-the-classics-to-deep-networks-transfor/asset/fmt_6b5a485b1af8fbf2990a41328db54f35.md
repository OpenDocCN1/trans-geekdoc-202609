
Let us consider the chain graph of [Fig. 15.21](#Chp015_755-803_B9780443292385000214.xhtml#f0115) and focus our interest on computing marginals. The naive approach, which overlooks factorization and independencies, would be to work directly on the joint distribution. Let us concentrate on discrete variables and assume that each one of them, `l` in total, has `K` states. Then, in order to compute the marginal of, say, `x[j]`, we have to obtain the sum

```
P(x_j) := ∑_{x_1}⋯∑_{x_{j-1}}∑_{x_{j+1}}⋯∑_{x_l} P(x_1,…,x_l) = ∑_{x_i:i≠j} P(x_1,…,x_l).
```

Each summation is over `K` values; hence, the number of the required computations amounts to `O(K^l)`. Let us now bring factorization into the game and concentrate on computing `P(x[1])`. Assume that the joint probability factorizes over the graph. Hence, we can write
