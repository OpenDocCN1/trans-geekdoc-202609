
$$
\Sigma_{\overline{x}} = \Lambda^{- 1/2}\Sigma_{\widehat{x}}\Lambda^{- 1/2} = \Lambda^{- 1/2}\Lambda\Lambda^{- 1/2} = I.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si358.png)

That is, the condition number of $\Sigma_{\overline{x}}$ is equal to 1. In practice, this technique is difficult to apply, due to the complexity associated with the eigendecomposition task; more importantly, $\Sigma[x]$ must be known, which in online implementations is not the case. In practice, we resort to unitary transforms, $T$, that approximately whiten the input, such as the DFT and DCT. Then Eq. [(5.67)](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#deq142) is replaced by

$$
{\widehat{\mathbf{\theta}}}_{n} = {\widehat{\mathbf{\theta}}}_{n - 1} + \mu D^{- 1}{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}_{n}e_{n}^{\ast},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si360.png)

where $D$ is the diagonal matrix with entries the variances of the respective components of ${\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}_{n}$, or

$$
[D]_{ii} = \mathbb{E} \left[ (\hat{x}_{n}(i))^{2} \right] = \sigma_{i}^{2}, \quad i = 1, 2, \dots, l,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000111/si362.png)

where $\hat{x}_{n}(i)$ is the $i$th entry of ${\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}_{n}$, which has been assumed to be a zero mean vector; this is justified from the fact that if $\Sigma[\hat{x}]$ is truly diagonal and equal to $\Lambda$, the eigenvalues correspond to the variances of the respective elements. The entries $\sigma_{i}^{2}$ are estimated in a time-recursive fashion, and the scheme given in [Algorithm 5.6](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#enun0065) results.
