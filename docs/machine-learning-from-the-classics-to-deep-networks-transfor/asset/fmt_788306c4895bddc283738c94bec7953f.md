### III. Excess mean-square error

We know that the minimum MSE is achieved at **θ**[*]. Any other weight vector results in higher values of the MSE. We have already said that in the steady state, the estimates obtained via the LMS fluctuate randomly around **θ**[*]; thus, the MSE will be larger than the minimum *J*[min]. This “extra” error power, denoted as *J*[exc], is known as the *excess* MSE. Also, the ratio

```
\mathcal{M}: = \frac{J_{\text{exc}}}{J_{\min}}
```

is known as the *misadjustment*. No doubt, we should seek for the relationship of ℳ with *μ* and in practice we would like to adjust *μ* accordingly, in order to get a value of ℳ as small as possible. Unfortunately, we will soon see that there is a tradeoff in achieving that. Making ℳ small, convergence becomes slower and vice versa; there is no free lunch!
