We emphasize that the input set, $\mathcal{X} = \{\boldsymbol{x}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{x}_N\}$, is considered fixed, so all the randomness associated with the output random variables, $\boldsymbol{y}$, is due to the noise source. Assuming both the prior and the conditional PDFs to be Gaussians,^([^(85)](#fn85))

$$
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta}|\boldsymbol{\theta}_0, \Sigma_\theta) \tag{12.8}
$$

and

$$
p(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{y}|\Phi\boldsymbol{\theta}, \Sigma_\eta), \tag{12.9}
$$

where Eqs. [(12.2)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq4) and [(12.3)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq5) have been used, the posterior $p(\boldsymbol{\theta}|\boldsymbol{y})$ turns out also to be Gaussian with mean vector

$$
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\theta|y} := \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\theta}|\boldsymbol{y}] = \boldsymbol{\theta}_0 + (\Sigma_\theta^{-1} + \Phi^T \Sigma_\eta^{-1} \Phi)^{-1} \Phi^T \Sigma_\eta^{-1} (\boldsymbol{y} - \Phi\boldsymbol{\theta}_0). \tag{12.10}
$$

Because the maximum of a Gaussian coincides with its mean, we have

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{MAP}} = \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\theta}|\boldsymbol{y}]. \tag{12.11}
$$

\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{MAP}} = (\lambda I + \Phi^T \Phi)^{-1} \Phi^T \boldsymbol{y}, \tag{12.12}
