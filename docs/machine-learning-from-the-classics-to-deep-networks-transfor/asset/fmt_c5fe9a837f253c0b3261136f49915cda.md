
### 20.8.3 Mixture of factor analyzers: a Bayesian view to compressed sensing

Let us go back to our original model in Eq. [(20.79)](#Chp020_1103-1179_B9780443292385000263.xhtml#deq113) and rephrase it into a more “trendy” fashion. Matrix `A` had dimensions `l × m` with `m < l`, and `z ∈ ℝ^(m)`. Let us now make `m > l`. For example, the columns of `A` may comprise vectors of an overcomplete dictionary. Thus, this section can be considered as the probabilistic counterpart of Section [20.6](#Chp020_1103-1179_B9780443292385000263.xhtml#s0105). The required low dimensionality of the modeling is expressed by imposing sparsity on `z`; we can rewrite the model in terms of the respective observations as [47]

```
x[n] = A(z[n] ∘ b) + ϵ[n],  n = 1, 2, …, N,
```

where `N` is the number of our training points and the vector `b ∈ ℝ^(m)` has elements `b[i] ∈ {0, 1}, i = 1, 2, …, m`. The product `z[n] ∘ b` is the point-wise vector product, that is,

```
z[n] ∘ b = [z[n](1)b[1], z[n](2)b[2], …, z[n](m)b[m]]^(T).
```
(20.93)
