
Although Wirtinger's calculus has been known since 1927 [51], its use in applications has a rather recent history [7]. Its revival was ignited by the widely linear filtering concept [27]. The interested reader may obtain more on this issue from [2,25,30]. Extensions of Wirtinger's derivative to general Hilbert (infinite-dimensional) spaces was done more recently in [6] and to the subgradient notion in [46].

### Application in linear estimation

The cost function in this case is

$$J(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}) = \mathbb{E} \left[ |y-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{H}\boldsymbol{x}|^{2} \right] = \mathbb{E} \left[ (y-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{H}\boldsymbol{x})(y^{*}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{T}\boldsymbol{x}^{*}) \right].$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si126.png)

Thus, treating $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ as a constant, the optimal occurs at

$$\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}}J = \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{x}e^{*}] = 0,$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si127.png)

which is the orthogonality condition leading to the normal equations [(4.16)](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#deq25)–[(4.18)](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#deq27).

### Application in widely linear estimation

The cost function is now (see notation in Eq. [(4.35)](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#deq47))

$$J(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}) = \mathbb{E} \left[ \left( y - \boldsymbol{\varphi}^{H}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}} \right)\left( y^{*} - \boldsymbol{\varphi}^{T}{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{*} \right) \right],$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si128.png)

and treating $\boldsymbol{\varphi}$ as a constant,

$$\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}}J = \mathbb{E}\left[ \widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}e^{*} \right] = \mathbb{E} \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{x}e^{*} \\ \boldsymbol{x}^{*}e^{*} \end{bmatrix} = 0,$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si129.png)

which leads to Eq. [(4.36)](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#deq51).
