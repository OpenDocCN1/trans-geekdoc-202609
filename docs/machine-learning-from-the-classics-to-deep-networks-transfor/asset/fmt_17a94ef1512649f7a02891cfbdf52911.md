
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N} \lambda_n y_n = 0 \tag{11.76}
$$

For the case where the original task has been mapped to an RKHS, the cost function becomes

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N} \lambda_n - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \sum_{m=1}^{N} \lambda_n \lambda_m y_n y_m \kappa(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{x}_m) \tag{11.77}
$$

*   According to Eq. [(11.72)](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#deq162), if $\lambda_n \neq 0$, then necessarily
    $$
    y_n(\boldsymbol{\theta}^T \mathbf{x}_n + \theta_0) = 1
    $$

*   That is, the respective points are the closest points, from each class, to the classifier (distance $\frac{1}{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|}$). They lie on either of the two hyperplanes forming the border of the margin. These points are the support vectors and the respective constraints are known as the *active constraints*. The rest of the points, associated with
    $$
    y_n(\boldsymbol{\theta}^T \mathbf{x}_n + \theta_0) > 1 \tag{11.78}
    $$
    which lie outside the margin, correspond to $\lambda_n = 0$ (*inactive constraints*).

*   The cost function in [(11.64)](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#deq148) is strictly convex and, hence, the solution of the optimization task is *unique* (Appendix C).

### 11.10.2 Nonseparable classes

We now turn our attention to the more realistic case of overlapping classes and the corresponding geometric interpretation of the task in [(11.61)](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#deq144)–[(11.63)](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#deq146). In this case, there is no (linear) classifier that can classify correctly all the points, and some errors are bound to occur. [Fig. 11.17](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#f0090) shows the respective geometry for a linear classifier. Note that although the classes are not separable, we still define the margin as the area between the two hyperplanes $f(\mathbf{x}) = \pm 1$. There are three types of points.

*   Points that lie on the border or outside the margin and in the correct side of the classifier, that is,
    $$
    y_n f(\mathbf{x}_n) \geq 1
    $$
    These points commit no (margin) error, and hence the inequality in [(11.62)](#Chp011_519-582_B9780443292385000172.xhtml#deq145) is satisfied for
    $$
    \xi_n = 0
    $$
