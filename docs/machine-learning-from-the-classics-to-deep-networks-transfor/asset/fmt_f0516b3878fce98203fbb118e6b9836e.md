
The previous approach is also generalized to random vectors in ℝ^(*l*). One can first draw samples from **x** ∼ 𝒩(**x**|0, *I*) by stacking together *l* i.i.d. samples drawn from the standard Gaussian, 𝒩(*x*|0, 1), and then apply the transformation

**y** = *L***x** + **μ**,

which is equivalent to drawing samples from

**y** ∼ 𝒩(**y**|**μ**, *Σ*), (14.20)

where *Σ* = *LL*^(*T*) (Cholesky factorization, Problem [14.4](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml#o0025)).

## 14.4 Rejection sampling

Applying the previously reported techniques relies on having the involved transformation functions available in a convenient (analytic) form, which in general is the exception instead of the rule. From now on, we turn our attention to alternative methods.

*Rejection sampling* (e.g., \[7,37\]) is conceptually a simple technique; in order to generate independent samples from a desired PDF, *p*(**x**), one draws samples from another one, say, *q*(**x**), that is easier to handle, and then, instead of applying a transformation, some of the points are *rejected* according to an appropriate criterion.
