
Once all the available training samples have been used, training carries on using the estimates `ŝ[n − L + 1]`. For example, for a binary information sequence `s[n] ∈ {1, −1}`, the decision concerning the estimate at time `n` is obtained by passing `d̂[n]` through a threshold device and `ŝ[n − L + 1]` is obtained. Note that the DFE is one of the early examples of *semisupervised* learning, where training data are not enough, and the estimates are used for training [94]. In this way, assuming that at the end of the training phase `ŝ[n − L + 1] ≈ s[n − L + 1]`, and also that time variations are slow, so as to guarantee that `d̂[n] ≈ d[n]`, we expect, with good enough probability, that `ŝ[n − L + 1]` will remain close to `s[n − L + 1]`, so that the equalizer can track the changes. More on DFEs and their error performance can be found in [77].
