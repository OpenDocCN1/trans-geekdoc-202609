
$Z_n = \int p(\mathbf{y}_n|\mathbf{x}_n)p(\mathbf{x}_n|\mathbf{y}_{1:n-1}) d\mathbf{x}_n = p(\mathbf{y}_n|\mathbf{y}_{1:n-1})$

and we have used the fact that $p(\mathbf{y}_n|\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{y}_{1:n-1}) = p(\mathbf{y}_n|\mathbf{x}_n)$, which is a consequence of Eq. [(17.15)](#Chp017_855-884_B9780443292385000238.xhtml#deq21). For those who have already read Chapter [15](#Chp015_755-803_B9780443292385000214.xhtml), recall that Kalman filtering is a special case of a Bayesian network and corresponds to the graphical model given in [Fig. 17.2](#Chp017_855-884_B9780443292385000238.xhtml#f0020). Hence, due to the Markov property, $\mathbf{y}_n$ is independent of the past given the values in $\mathbf{x}_n$. Moreover, note that

$$
p(\mathbf{x}_n|\mathbf{y}_{1:n-1}) = \int p(\mathbf{x}_n|\mathbf{x}_{n-1}, \mathbf{y}_{1:n-1})p(\mathbf{x}_{n-1}|\mathbf{y}_{1:n-1}) d\mathbf{x}_{n-1} = \int p(\mathbf{x}_n|\mathbf{x}_{n-1})p(\mathbf{x}_{n-1}|\mathbf{y}_{1:n-1}) d\mathbf{x}_{n-1}
$$
