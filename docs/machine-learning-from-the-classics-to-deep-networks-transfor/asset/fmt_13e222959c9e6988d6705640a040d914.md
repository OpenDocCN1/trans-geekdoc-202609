
### 19.7.5 Beyond the basic models: a discussion

At the time the current edition of the book is being prepared, there is a “happening” in the research and applications front of diffusion models. Our aim is not to exhaustively review the related literature, but to present some basic directions, where research and applications are currently focusing on. For a more extensive literature review, one may consult, e.g., [41], [200], [115].

A major drawback associated with the previous three basic diffusion models is their slow sampling, which, in practice, requires many steps. In [198], it is pointed out that generative models struggle to simultaneously meet three requirements: a) sample quality, b) mode coverage, c) sampling speed.

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) (Section 19.2.5) generate high-quality samples fast, but exhibit a rather low mode coverage. This could be due to the fact that their adversarial learning procedure does not easily scale to modeling complex, multimodal distributions. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) (Section 19.2.6) exhibit a rather broad mode coverage and fast sampling, but often suffer from low sample quality. Diffusion models, being likelihood-based models, do not exhibit mode-collapse and can generate surprisingly high quality samples and they have a broad mode coverage. Yet, their sampling speed is low, requiring thousands of network evaluations. For example, it takes 20 hours to sample 50K images of size 32 × 32 from DDPM, but less than a minute to do the same task using a GAN on Nvidia 2080 Ti GPU. When images of size 256 × 256 are involved, 1000 hours are needed on the same GPU, when diffusion models are employed; see [174]. A further discussion related to the comparative performance of different families of generative models can be found in [44].
