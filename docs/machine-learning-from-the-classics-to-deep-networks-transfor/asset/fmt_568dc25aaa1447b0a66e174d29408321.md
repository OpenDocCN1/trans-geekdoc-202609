
$$
\text{KL}\left( q(\mathbf{x}_{t - 1}|\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{0})||p_{\mathbf{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t - 1}|\mathbf{x}_{t}) \right) = \frac{1}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}||{\widetilde{\mathbf{\mu}}}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{0}) - \mathbf{\mu}_{\mathbf{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)||^{2} + C,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000251/si555.png)(19.66)

*   where `C` is a constant independent of `θ`, and `|| ⋅ ||` denotes the Euclidean norm. Thus, the goal of training now becomes to estimate `θ` to “push” the mean of the model conditional, `μ_θ`, as close as possible to `μ̃_t` in the Euclidean norm sense.

*   In practice, one employs a neural network, parameterized in terms of `θ`, e.g., `f_θ(⋅)`, whose input comprises samples of `x_t`, and it is trained so that its output, `μ_θ(x_t, t) := f_θ(x_t)`, to be close, in the Euclidean norm sense, to the target `μ̃_t(x_t, x_0)`. In other words, the task of training becomes equivalent to a nonlinear regression one. Similar arguments hold if the covariance matrix is not fixed but parameterized, [169].
