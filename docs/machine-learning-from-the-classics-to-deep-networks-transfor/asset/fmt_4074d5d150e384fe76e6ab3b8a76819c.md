
A variant of the celebrated Nesterov algorithm [143] was suggested in [197]. The update rule for the correction term becomes

$$
\Delta\mathbf{\theta}^{(i)} = a\Delta\mathbf{\theta}^{(i - 1)} - \mu\frac{\partial J\left( \mathbf{\theta} + a\Delta\mathbf{\theta}^{(i - 1)} \right)}{\partial\mathbf{\theta}}|_{\mathbf{\theta}^{(i - 1)}}.
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si202.png)

The difference with the previous momentum update rule lies in that the gradient of the cost function is computed after pushing `θ` in the direction of the current correction term, `Δθ^((i−1))`.

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si203.png)

In words, the gradient is not computed at the currently available estimates of the parameters but at their approximate future values. As has been reported in [197], this can lead to substantial gains in convergence speed. The values for `a` are in the same range as in the case of the momentum algorithm.

### The AdaGrad algorithm

The AdaGrad algorithm was introduced in its online form in the convex cost functions setting in Chapter [8](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml), Eq. [(8.71)](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml). Borrowing the main rationale behind the algorithm, in our current framework it can be rephrased as

$$
\mathbf{\theta}^{(i)} = \mathbf{\theta}^{(i-1)} - \mu\mathbf{G}[i-1]^{-1/2}\mathbf{g}^{(i-1)},
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si204.png)

where by definition

$$
\mathbf{g}^{(i)} := \frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{\theta}}|_{\mathbf{\theta}^{(i)}}
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si205.png)

is the gradient computed at the respective estimate, `θ^((i))`.

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si172.png)

and the matrix `G[i]`

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si207.png)

is the sum of the outer products of all the gradient vectors up to and including iteration `i`, that is,

$$
G_{i} := \sum\limits_{t = 0}^{i}\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\mathbf{g}^{{(t)}^{T}}.
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500024X/si208.png)
