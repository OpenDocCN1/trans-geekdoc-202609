**M-Step 2:** We have

$$P_{k}^{(j + 1)} = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\rho_{n_{k}}.$$

The previous steps have concluded the algorithm. Observe that the iterations retain the functional form of the PDFs that were adopted for the respective priors; this is a consequence of their exponential family origin. In [13], it is suggested that this procedure can also be used to determine the number of mixtures, instead of adopting a cross-validation technique, as pointed out in the remarks of Section [12.6](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml). By adopting a large enough value for $K$, the probabilities $P[k]$ associated with the irrelevant components will be driven to zero during the M-step. Note that such a modeling is possible in the Bayesian framework, because it automatically achieves a tradeoff between model complexity and data fitting. In [4], the probabilities $P[k], k = 1, 2, \dots, K$ were considered as random variables and a Dirichlet prior was also imposed on them (Problem [13.7](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#o0135)). However, such priors need to be selected with some care; otherwise it may affect the sparsification potential of the algorithm (e.g., [9]).
