Observe that the first term on the right-hand side has the form of a KL divergence, where the hidden variables, `θ`, have been averaged out. Note, however, that by its definition, `p̃` is not necessarily a distribution. As we will soon see, in order to become a distribution, a normalizing constant is required.

We have by now all the ingredients to go ahead with the maximization of `ℱ(q[𝒳^(l)], q[θ], ξ)`. The optimization will take place by maximizing first with respect to `q[𝒳^(l)]`, in the sequel with respect to `q[θ]`, and finally with respect to `ξ`. The algorithm is initialized to some arbitrary values for `ξ^((0))` as well as for `q[θ]^((0))`. The latter is achieved by initializing parameters (statistics) related to `q[θ]^((0))` (this will become more clear while dealing with the examples).
