
$$
\mathbb{E}\left[{\widehat{\theta}}^{(L)}\right] = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{i = 1}^{L}\mathbb{E}\left[{\widehat{\theta}}_{i}\right] = \theta_{o}.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si131.png)

Moreover, assuming that the involved estimators are mutually uncorrelated, i.e.,

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[({\widehat{\theta}}_{i}-\theta_{o})({\widehat{\theta}}_{j}-\theta_{o})\right] = 0,
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si132.png)

and of the same variance, $\sigma^{2}$.

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si133.png)

The variance of the new estimator is now much smaller (Problem [3.2](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#o0015)), i.e.,

$$
\sigma_{{\widehat{\theta}}^{(L)}}^{2} = \mathbb{E} \left[ \left( {\widehat{\theta}}^{(L)} - \theta_{o} \right)^{2} \right] = \frac{\sigma^{2}}{L}.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si134.png)

Hence, by averaging a large number of such unbiased estimators, we expect to get an estimate close to the true value. However, in practice, data are a commodity that is not always abundant. As a matter of fact, very often the opposite is true and one has to be very careful about how to exploit them. In such cases, where one cannot afford to obtain and average a large number of estimators, an unbiased estimator may not necessarily be the best choice. Going back to Eq. [(3.22)](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#deq37), there is no reason to suggest that by making the second term equal to zero, the MSE (which, after all, is the quantity of interest to us) becomes minimal. Indeed, let us look at Eq. [(3.22)](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#deq37) from a slightly different point of view. Instead of computing the MSE for a given estimator, let us replace $\widehat{\theta}$ with $\theta$ in Eq. [(3.22)](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#deq37) and compute an estimator that will minimize the MSE with respect to $\theta$ directly. In this case, focusing on unbiased estimators, or $\mathbb{E}[\theta] = \theta_{o}$,

introduces a constraint to the task of minimizing the MSE, and it is well known that an unconstrained minimization problem always results in loss function values that are less than or equal to any value generated by a constrained counterpart.
