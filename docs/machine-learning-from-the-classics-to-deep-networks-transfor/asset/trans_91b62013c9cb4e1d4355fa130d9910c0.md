
Each time a sample is drawn from $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{x}_{n-1}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$, the new sample will fall with high probability inside a circle of radius $\sigma$ centered on $\mathbf{x}_{n-1}$. For the new sample to have a high probability of falling inside the high-probability ellipsoidal region, $\sigma$ must be of order $\sigma_{\text{min}}$ or smaller. If a larger value of $\sigma$ is chosen, the sample will most likely fall outside the ellipsoid and be rejected. Thus, once the sampling has started inside the ellipsoid, a small value of $\sigma$ will ensure, with high probability, that the samples stay inside the ellipsoid and are therefore accepted. On the other hand, if $\sigma$ is small, a large number of iterations will be required to cover the interior of the ellipsoid adequately with points. If the sampling process is viewed as an approximate random walk with step size $\sigma$, the number of iterations required to cover a scale of order $\sigma_{\text{max}}$ will be $\left( \frac{\sigma_{\text{max}}}{\sigma} \right)^{2}$; if $\sigma \approx \sigma_{\text{min}}$, this becomes $\left( \frac{\sigma_{\text{max}}}{\sigma_{\text{min}}} \right)^{2}$. In high-dimensional spaces, where some dimension is likely to have a scale that is relatively small compared to the maximum scale, this square dependence rule of thumb will significantly slow down convergence.
