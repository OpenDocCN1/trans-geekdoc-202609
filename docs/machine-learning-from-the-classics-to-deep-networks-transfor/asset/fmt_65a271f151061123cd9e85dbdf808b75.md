
## 数学推导

$p(\mathbf{y};\theta) = \prod\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}}\exp\left( - \frac{{(y_{n} - \theta)}^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}} \right),$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si172.png)(3.34)

or

$\ln p(\mathbf{y};\theta) = - \frac{N}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma_{\eta}^{2}) - \frac{1}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}{(y_{n} - \theta)}^{2}.$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si173.png)(3.35)

We will derive the corresponding Cramér–Rao bound. Taking the derivative of the logarithm with respect to $\theta$ we have

$\frac{\partial\ln p(\mathbf{y};\theta)}{\partial\theta} = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}(y_{n} - \theta) = \frac{N}{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}(\overline{y} - \theta),$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si174.png)(3.36)

where

$\overline{y}: = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}y_{n},$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000093/si175.png)

that is, the sample mean of the observations. The second derivative, as required by the theorem, is given by

$\frac{\partial^{2}\ln p(\mathbf{y};\theta)}{\partial\theta^{2}} = - \frac{N}{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}},$
