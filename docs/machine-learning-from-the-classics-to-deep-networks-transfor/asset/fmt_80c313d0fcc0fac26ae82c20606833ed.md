
Assign $\mathbf{x}$ to class $\omega_{i}$: $i = \arg\min\limits_{j}{(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{\mu}_{j})}^{T}\Sigma^{- 1}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{\mu}_{j}),\quad j = 1,2,\ldots M.$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/si107.png)(7.25)

Thus, instead of looking for the minimum Euclidean distance, one searches for the minimum Mahalanobis distance; the latter is a weighted form of the Euclidean distance, in order to account for the shape of the underlying Gaussian distributions [38]. ## Remarks 7.2

*   In statistics, adopting the Gaussian assumption for the data distribution is sometimes called *linear discriminant analysis* (LDA) or *quadratic discriminant analysis* (QDA), depending on the adopted assumptions with respect to the underlying covariance matrices, which will lead to linear or quadratic discriminant functions, respectively. In practice, the ML method is usually employed in order to obtain estimates of the unknown parameters, namely, the mean values and the covariance matrices. Recall from [Example 3.7](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml) of Chapter [3](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml) that the ML estimate of the mean value of a Gaussian PDF, obtained via $N$ observations, $\mathbf{x}[n],\ n=1,2,\ldots,N$, is equal to

$$
{\widehat{\mathbf{\mu}}}_{ML} = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{n}.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/si109.png)

*   Moreover, the ML estimate of the covariance matrix of a Gaussian distribution, using $N$ observations, is given by (Problem [7.4](#Chp007_293-342_B9780443292385000135.xhtml#o0025))

$$
{\widehat{\Sigma}}_{ML} = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{n} - {\widehat{\mathbf{\mu}}}_{ML}){(\mathbf{x}_{n} - {\widehat{\mathbf{\mu}}}_{ML})}^{T}.
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/si110.png)(7.26)
