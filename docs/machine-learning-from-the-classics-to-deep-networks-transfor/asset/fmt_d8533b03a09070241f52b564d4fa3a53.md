
# 19.1 Introduction

This is the second chapter related to neural networks and deep learning. The chapter deals with some of the more recent advances in machine learning, namely deep generative models, transformers and large language models (LLMs), and self-supervised learning. Generative networks have revolutionized the way we model and generate data. In an era of data-driven science, technology, industry and services, these techniques have escaped the terrain of research and have been diffused and used in a wide range of applications. In another front and after many years of reign of supervised and unsupervised learning, self-supervised techniques have risen to be key players in machine learning offering new vast potential in using and exploiting the abundance of unlabeled data that are available via the internet. Transformers and the large language models comprise a major breakthrough in language understanding, processing and text generation. Within only a couple of years from their appearance, these models have found their way in many areas beyond language processing tasks and they are becoming an indispensable tool for new scientific discoveries, new technological achievements and are transforming the way humans interact, entertain, and work. Although still early to forecast, it seems that all these machine learning advances will have a significant impact in all aspects of our lives, as persons and as social beings.

The chapter begins with refreshing the memory with some very basic notions from graphical models and then it presents deep belief networks. Besides their significant educational value, these networks have played a key role for the revival of the interest on neural networks, following the visionary work of G. Hinton and his co-workers in the early years of this century. In the sequel, generative adversarial networks (GANs) are discussed, followed by autoencoders and variational autoencoders (VAEs). Normalizing flows are briefly considered. A major focus of the chapter lies on the concept of attention and the transformer networks. The GPT and the BERT families of models are presented. The baton is then passed over to self-supervised learning, where its main categories are highlighted, such as masked prediction methods and contrastive techniques. The chapter concludes with an introduction to diffusion models, discussing the key concepts, the main modeling paths, algorithms and applications. Some hints on ethical issues related to the use of these technologies are, also, briefly touched.
