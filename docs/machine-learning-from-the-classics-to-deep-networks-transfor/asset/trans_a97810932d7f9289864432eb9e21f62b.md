
To address the current problem, we will constrain `q(𝒳^(l), θ)` within a specific family of functions. Note that in this case, if the unknown `p(𝒳^(l), θ|𝒳; ξ)` does not belong to the chosen family of functions, the KL divergence *cannot* become zero, and the lower bound of the marginal log-likelihood `ℱ(q, ξ)` *cannot* become *tight*. This is why the method is called variational approximation.

### Mean-Field Approximation

This approximation arises by constraining `q(𝒳^(l), θ)` to be of a factorized form, i.e.:

```
q(𝒳^(l), θ) = q[𝒳^(l)](𝒳^(l)) q[θ](θ)
```
(13.3)

Furthermore, the latent variables can be further factorized, i.e., `q_θ(θ) = ∏_i^K q_θ_i(θ_i)`. This factorized functional approximation, inspired by the field of statistical physics, is called the *mean-field approximation* (e.g., [17,47,62]). Undoubtedly, various combinations of grouping different variables can also be used. To simplify notation without loss of generality, we will use Eq. [(13.3)](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml#deq4), which involves only two factors.
