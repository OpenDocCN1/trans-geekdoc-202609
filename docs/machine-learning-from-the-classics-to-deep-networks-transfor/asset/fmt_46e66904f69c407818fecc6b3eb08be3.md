### Remarks 5.2

*   **The analysis method presented in this section**, concerning the transient and steady-state performance of the LMS, can be considered as the most primitive one and goes back to the early work of Widrow and Hoff [102]. Another popular path for analyzing stochastic algorithms in an averaging sense is the so-called *averaging method*, which operates under the assumption of small values of the step size $\mu$. Another approach that builds around the assumption of small step sizes, $\mu \approx 0$, is the so-called ordinary-differential-equation approach (ODE) [57]. The difference update equation is "transformed" to a differential equation, which paves the way for using arguments from the Lyapunov stability theory. An alternative elegant theoretical tool, which can be used as a vehicle for analyzing the transient, the steady state, and the tracking performance of adaptive schemes is the so-called *energy conservation* method, developed in [82] and later on extended in [3,105]. More on the performance analysis of the LMS as well as of other online schemes can be found in more specialized books and papers [4,13,47,62,83,91,92,96,103].

*   **$H^{\infty}$ optimality of the LMS**. It may come as a surprise that the LMS algorithm, a very simple structure, has survived the time and is one of the most popular and widely used schemes in practical applications. The reason is that, besides its low complexity, it enjoys the luxury of robustness. An alternative optimization flavor of the LMS algorithm has been given via the theory of $H^{\infty}$ optimization for estimation.
