
## Lemma 9.2

$\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0 \ge \text{spark}(X), \quad \forall \boldsymbol{\theta} \in \text{null}(X), \boldsymbol{\theta} \neq 0.$

**Proof**

To derive a contradiction, assume that there exists a $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \text{null}(X), \boldsymbol{\theta} \neq 0$, such that $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0 < \text{spark}(X)$. Because by definition $X\boldsymbol{\theta} = 0$, there exists a number of $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0$ columns of $X$ that are linearly dependent. However, this contradicts the minimality of $\text{spark}(X)$, and the claim of Lemma 9.2 is established.

## Lemma 9.3

*If a linear system of equations, $X\boldsymbol{\theta} = \boldsymbol{y}$, has a solution that satisfies*

$$\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0 < \frac{1}{2}\text{spark}(X)$$

*then this is the sparsest possible solution. In other words, this is necessarily the unique solution of the $\ell_0$ minimizer.*

**Proof**

Consider any other solution $\boldsymbol{h} \neq \boldsymbol{\theta}$. Then $\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{h} \in \text{null}(X)$, that is,

$$X(\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{h}) = 0.$$

Thus, according to Lemma 9.2,

$$\text{spark}(X) \le \|\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{h}\|_0 \le \|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0 + \|\boldsymbol{h}\|_0. \tag{9.24}$$

Observe that although the $\ell_0$ "norm" is not a true norm, it can be readily verified by simple inspection and reasoning that the triangular property is satisfied. Indeed, by adding two vectors together, the resulting number of nonzero elements will always be at most equal to the total number of nonzero elements of the two vectors. Therefore, if $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0 < \frac{1}{2}\text{spark}(X)$, then (9.24) suggests that

$$\|\boldsymbol{h}\|_0 > \frac{1}{2}\text{spark}(X) > \|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_0.$$
