
The encoders' outputs **h**[*i*] and **h**[*j*] feed the modules, *g*[**ϕ**](·). Since the goal in this type of learning is to enforce similarity (or dissimilarity), one may wonder why this extra module is needed. One could train only with respect to the parameters **θ** of the encoder, based on **h**[*i*] and **h**[*j*]. However, it turns out that introducing *g*[**ϕ**](·) further improves the learned representations. This extra network is also known as the *projector* head that maps, non-linearly, the set **h**[*i*] and **h**[*j*] to a new set of vectors, i.e., **z**[*i*] and **z**[*j*] that, in general, lie in a different subspace from that of **h**[*i*], **h**[*j*].

In [35], the `ResNet-50` network ([71]) was used in place of the encoder network, and a 2-layer MLP projection head to project the representations to a 128-dimensional latent space. Referring to the generic scheme in Section 19.6.2, note that in this case the input and output sets, 𝒳 and ℋ, respectively, are singletons, i.e., *N* = 1. The inputs to the encoder consist of images (not patches) and the corresponding outputs at the encoder of 2048-dimensional vectors.
