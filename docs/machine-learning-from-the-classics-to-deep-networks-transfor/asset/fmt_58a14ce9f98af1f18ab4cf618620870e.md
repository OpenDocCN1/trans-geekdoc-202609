
**θ**[*n*] = (*β*^(−1)*P*[*n*−1] − *β*^(−1)**k**[*n*]**x**[*n*]^(*T*)*P*[*n*−1])*β***p**[*n*−1] + *P*[*n*]**x**[*n*]*y*[*n*] = **θ**[*n*−1] − **k**[*n*]**x**[*n*]^(*T*)**θ**[*n*−1] + **k**[*n*]*y*[*n*],

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si187.png)

and finally,

**θ**[*n*] = **θ**[*n*−1] + **k**[*n*]*e*[*n*],

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si188.png)(6.47)

where

*e*[*n*] := *y*[*n*] − **θ**[*n*−1]^(*T*)**x**[*n*].

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si189.png)(6.48)

The derived algorithm is summarized in [Algorithm 6.1](#Chp006_249-291_B9780443292385000123.xhtml#enun0020).

Note that the basic recursive update of the vector of the parameters follows the same rationale as the LMS and the gradient descent schemes that were discussed in Chapter [5](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml). The updated estimate of the parameters **θ**[*n*] at time *n* equals that of the previous time instant, *n*−1, plus a correction term that is proportional to the error *e*[*n*]. As a matter of fact, this is the *generic scheme* that we are going to meet for all the time-recursive algorithms in this book, including those used for the very “trendy” case of neural networks. The main difference from algorithm to algorithm lies in how one computes the multiplicative factor for the error. In the case of the LMS, this factor was equal to *μ***x**[*n*]. For the case of the RLS, this factor is **k**[*n*]. As we shall soon see, the RLS algorithm is closely related to an alternative to the gradient descent family of optimization algorithms, known as Newton's iterative optimization of cost functions.
