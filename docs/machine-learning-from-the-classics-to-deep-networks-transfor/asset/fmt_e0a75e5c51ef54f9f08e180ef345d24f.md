
$w(\mathbf{x}) := \frac{\phi(\mathbf{x})}{q(\mathbf{x})}$.

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000202/si133.png) (14.26)

The normalizing constant can readily be obtained as

$$
Z = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left( \frac{\phi(\mathbf{x})}{q(\mathbf{x})} \right) q(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x} \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} w(\mathbf{x}_i).
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000202/si134.png) (14.27)

Combining Eqs. [(14.25)](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml#deq47) and [(14.27)](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml#deq49), we finally obtain

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[ f(\mathbf{x}) \right] \approx \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w(\mathbf{x}_i) f(\mathbf{x}_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w(\mathbf{x}_i)},
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000202/si135.png) (14.28)

or

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000202/fx001.jpg)

where $W(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{w(\mathbf{x}_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w(\mathbf{x}_i)}$ are the normalized weights. It is not difficult to show (Problem [14.6](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml#o0035)) that the estimate

$$
\widehat{Z} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} w(\mathbf{x}_i)
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000202/si137.png) (14.29)

corresponds to an unbiased estimator of the normalizing constant. This is very interesting, because computing the normalizing constant is particularly useful information in a number of tasks. Recall that the evidence function, discussed in Chapter [12](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml), is a normalizing constant; see also [26] for related comments.
