
An advantage of the exponential family is that one can find conjugate priors for `θ`; that is, priors that lead to posteriors, `p(θ|X)`, of the same functional form as `p(θ)` (Section [3.11.1](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml), [Remarks 3.4](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml)).

Given Eq. [(12.72)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq96) its conjugate prior is given by

```
p(θ; λ, v) = h(λ, v)(g(θ))^(λ)exp(ϕ^T(θ)v)
```

where `λ > 0` and `v` are known as *hyperparameters*; that is, parameters that control other parameters. The factor `h(λ, v)` is an appropriate normalizing constant. It is easy to see that defining the prior PDF as in Eq. [(12.74)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq98) and the conditional PDF as in Eq. [(12.72)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq96), the posterior `p(θ|x)` is of the same form as that in Eq. [(12.74)](#Chp012_583-634_B9780443292385000184.xhtml#deq98).
