### Example 5.5: LMS versus transform-domain LMS

In this example, the stage of the experimental setup is exactly the same as that considered in [Example 5.3](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#enun0070) for the AR(1) case. The goal is to compare the LMS and the transform-domain LMS. [Fig. 5.18](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#f0095) shows the obtained averaged error curves. The step size was the same as the one used in [Example 5.3](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#enun0070), `μ = 0.01`; hence the curve for the LMS is the same as the corresponding one appearing in [Fig. 5.16](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#f0085). Observe the significantly faster convergence achieved by the transform-domain LMS, due to its (approximate) whitening effect on the input.

### Example 5.6

The experimental setup is similar to that of [Example 5.4](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#enun0075), with the only exception that the unknown parameter vector was of higher dimension, `θ[o] ∈ ℝ^(60)`, so that the differences in the performance of the algorithms are more clear. The goal is to compare the LMS, the NLMS, and the APA. The step size of the LMS was chosen equal to `μ = 0.025` and for the NLMS `μ = 0.35` and `δ = 0.001`, so that both algorithms had similar convergence rates. The step size for the APA was chosen equal to `μ = 0.1`, so that `q = 10` will settle at the same error floor as that of the NLMS. For the APA, we also chose `δ = 0.001`. The results are shown in [Fig. 5.19](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#f0100).

![Figure 5.19 The NLMS converges at the same rate to a lower error floor compared to the LMS. For the APA, increasing q improves the convergence, at the expense of higher error floors (MSE in dBs).](images/B9780443292385000111/gr019.jpg)

[Figure 5.19](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#cf0930) The NLMS converges at the same rate to a lower error floor compared to the LMS. For the APA, increasing `q` improves the convergence, at the expense of higher error floors (MSE in dBs).

Observe the lower error floor, for the same convergence rate, obtained by the NLMS compared to the LMS and the improved performance obtained by the APA for `q = 10`. Increasing the number of past data samples (re)used in APA to `q = 30`, we can see the improved convergence rate that is obtained, although at the expense of higher error floor, as predicted by the theoretical results reported in Section [5.6](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml#s0065).
