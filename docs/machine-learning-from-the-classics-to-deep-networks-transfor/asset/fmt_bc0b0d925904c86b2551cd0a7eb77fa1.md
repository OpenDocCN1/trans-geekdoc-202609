
**z**[*n*]^(*T*) = **z**[*n*]^(′^(*T*)) *W*⁰, *W*⁰ ∈ ℝ^(*d*[*v*] × *d*[*m*]), **z**[*n*] ∈ ℝ^(*d*[*m*]),

where the elements of the matrix *W*⁰ are learned during training. Thus, referring to [Fig. 19.10](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#f0055), a) the output sequence of the updated embeddings of the self-attention layer is of the same size as the input sequence embeddings, i.e., *N* + 2 vectors, and b) each one of the output vectors is of the same dimensionality, *d*[*m*].

### Matrix Notation

In practice, it is more convenient to employ matrices that not only lead to more compact notation but also facilitate vectorized operations for parallel computations. To this end, let us stack all query, key, and value vectors in three matrices, namely, *Q*, *K*, and *V*, respectively, to obtain

```
Q = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{q}_{0}^{T} \\ \mathbf{q}_{1}^{T} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{q}_{N + 1}^{T} \\ \end{bmatrix},\ 
K = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{k}_{0}^{T} \\ \mathbf{k}_{1}^{T} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{k}_{N + 1}^{T} \\ \end{bmatrix},\ 
V = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_{0}^{T} \\ \mathbf{v}_{1}^{T} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{v}_{N + 1}^{T} \\ \end{bmatrix},
```
