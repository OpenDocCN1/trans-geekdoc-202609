$\sigma_{\widehat{\theta}}^{2} \geq \frac{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}{N}$.

We can easily verify that the corresponding estimator $\overline{y}$ is indeed an unbiased one under the adopted model of Eq. [(3.33)](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#deq54),

$\mathbb{E}[\overline{y}] = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[y_{n}] = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[\theta + \eta_{n}] = \theta$.

Moreover, the previous formula, combined with Eq. [(3.36)](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#deq57), also establishes the regularity condition, as required by the Cramér–Rao theorem.

The bound in Eq. [(3.38)](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml#deq61) is a very natural result. The Cramér–Rao lower bound depends on the variance of the noise source. The higher this is, and therefore the higher the uncertainty of each observation with respect to the value of the true parameter is, the higher the minimum variance of an estimator is expected to be. On the other hand, as the number of observations increases and more "information" is disclosed to us, the uncertainty decreases and we expect the variance of our estimator to decrease.
