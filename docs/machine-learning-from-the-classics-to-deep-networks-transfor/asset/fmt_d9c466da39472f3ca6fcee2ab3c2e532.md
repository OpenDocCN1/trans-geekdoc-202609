
$\widehat{\mathbf{y}} := X\mathbf{\theta} = \sum\limits_{i = 1}^{l}\theta_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{c},$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si16.png)

and

$\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{y} - \widehat{\mathbf{y}}.$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si17.png)

Note the `e` can be viewed as the *error vector* between the vector of the output observations, `y`, and $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}$![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si18.png); the latter is the prediction of `y` based on the input observations, stacked in `X`, and given a value for `θ`. Obviously, the *N*-dimensional vector $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}$![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si18.png), being a linear combination of the columns of `X`, lies in the span{**x**[1]^(*c*), …, **x**[*l*]^(*c*)}![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si19.png). By definition, the latter is the subspace of ℝ^(*l*)![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si20.png) that is generated by all possible linear combinations of the *l* columns of `X` (see Appendix A). Thus, naturally, our task now becomes that of selecting `θ` so that the error vector between `y` and $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}$![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si18.png) has minimum norm. In general, the observations vector, `y`, does not lie in the subspace spanned by the columns of `X`, due to the existence of the noise.

According to the Pythagorean theorem of orthogonality for Euclidean spaces, the minimum norm error is obtained if $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}$![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si18.png) is chosen as the *orthogonal projection* of `y` onto the span {**x**[1]^(*c*), …, **x**[*l*]^(*c*)}![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si21.png). Recalling the concept of orthogonal projections (Appendix A and Section [5.6](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml), Eq. [(5.63)](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml)), the orthogonal projection of `y` onto the subspace spanned by the columns of `X` is given by

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/fx001.jpg)(6.3)

assuming that `X`^(*T*)`X`![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si25.png) is invertible. Recalling the definition of $\widehat{\mathbf{y}}$![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si18.png), the above corresponds to the LS estimate for the unknown set of parameters, as we know it from Chapter [3](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml), i.e.,

$\widehat{\mathbf{\theta}} = {(X^{T}X)}^{- 1}X^{T}\mathbf{y}.$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000123/si26.png)
