
$Z_{i} := \sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}w_{n}^{(i)}\exp\left( - y_{n}a_{i}\phi(\mathbf{x}_{n};\mathbf{\theta}_{i}) \right).$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000135/si363.png)(7.98)

Looking at the way the weights are formed, one can grasp one of the major secrets underlying the AdaBoost algorithm: The weight associated with a training sample **x**[n] is increased (decreased) with respect to its value at the previous iteration, depending on whether the pattern has failed (succeeded) in being classified correctly. Moreover, the percentage of the decrease (increase) depends on the value of *a*[i], which controls the relative importance in the buildup of the final classifier. Hard samples, which keep failing over successive iterations, gain importance in their participation in the weighted empirical error value. For the case of the AdaBoost, it can be shown that the training error tends to zero exponentially fast (Problem [7.18](#Chp007_293-342_B9780443292385000135.xhtml#o0095)). The scheme is summarized in [Algorithm 7.1](#Chp007_293-342_B9780443292385000135.xhtml#enun0060).

## Algorithm 7.1

### The AdaBoost algorithm

*   **Initialize:** $w_{n}^{(1)} = \frac{1}{N},\ i = 1,2,\ldots,N$
*   **Initialize:** $i = 1$
*   **Repeat**
    1.  Compute the optimum **θ**[i] in $\phi( \cdot ;\textit{\textbf{θ}}_{i})$ by minimizing *P*[i]; [(7.91)](#Chp007_293-342_B9780443292385000135.xhtml#deq139)
    2.  Compute the optimum *P*[i]; [(7.92)](#Chp007_293-342_B9780443292385000135.xhtml#deq140)
    3.  $a_{i} = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1 - P_{i}}{P_{i}}$
    4.  $Z[i] = 0$
    5.  **For** $n = 1$ to *N* **Do**
        *   $w_{n}^{(i + 1)} = w_{n}^{(i)}\exp\left( - y_{n}a_{i}\phi(\textit{\textbf{x}}_{n};\textit{\textbf{θ}}_{i}) \right)$
