
*ŷ* = (**θ**[*r*]^(*T*) − *j***θ**[*i*]^(*T*))(**x**[*r*] + *j***x**[*i*]) = (**θ**[*r*]^(*T*)**x**[*r*] + **θ**[*i*]^(*T*)**x**[*i*]) + *j*(**θ**[*r*]^(*T*)**x**[*i*] − **θ**[*i*]^(*T*)**x**[*r*]).

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si76.png)(4.22)

Eq. [(4.22)](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#deq31) reveals the true flavor behind the complex notation; that is, its *multichannel* nature. In multichannel estimation, we are given more than one set of input variables, namely, **x**[*r*] and **x**[*i*], and we want to generate, jointly, more than one output variable, namely, *ŷ*[*r*] and *ŷ*[*i*]. Eq. [(4.22)](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#deq31) can equivalently be written as

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \widehat{y}_{r} \\ \widehat{y}_{i} \end{bmatrix} = \Theta \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}_{r} \\ \mathbf{x}_{i} \end{bmatrix},
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si80.png)(4.23)

where

$$
\Theta := \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{\theta}_{r}^{T} & \mathbf{\theta}_{i}^{T} \\ -\mathbf{\theta}_{i}^{T} & \mathbf{\theta}_{r}^{T} \end{bmatrix}.
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si81.png)(4.24)

Multichannel estimation can be generalized to more than two outputs and to more than two input sets of variables. We will come back to the more general multichannel estimation task toward the end of this chapter.
