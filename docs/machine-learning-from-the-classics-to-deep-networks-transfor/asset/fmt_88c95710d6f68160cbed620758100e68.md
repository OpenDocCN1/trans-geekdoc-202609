\widehat{\mathbf{\theta}}: \min_{\mathbf{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{l}} \left\| \mathbf{\theta} \right\|_{1}, \text{s.t.} \left( \mathbf{y} - X\mathbf{\theta} \right)^{T}\left( \mathbf{y} - X\mathbf{\theta} \right) \leq \epsilon,

given the user-defined parameters $\rho, \epsilon \geq 0$. The formulation in [(9.7)](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml#deq9) is known as the LASSO and the one in [(9.8)](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml#deq10) as the *basis pursuit denoising* (BPDN) (e.g., [15]). All three formulations are equivalent for specific choices of $\lambda, \epsilon$, and $\rho$ (see, e.g., [14]). Observe that the minimized cost function in Eq. [(9.6)](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml#deq8) corresponds to the Lagrangian of the formulation in [(9.7)](#Chp009_417-462_B9780443292385000159.xhtml#deq9). However, this functional dependence among $\lambda, \epsilon$, and $\rho$ is hard to compute, unless the columns of $X$ are mutually orthogonal. Moreover, this equivalence does not necessarily imply that all three formulations are equally easy or difficult to solve. As we will see later in this chapter, algorithms have been developed along each one of the previous formulations. From now on, we will refer to all three formulations as the LASSO task, in a slight abuse of the standard terminology, and the specific formulation will be apparent from the context, if not stated explicitly.

We know that ridge regression admits a closed form solution, that is,

$$
\widehat{\mathbf{\theta}}_{R} = \left( X^{T}X + \lambda I \right)^{-1}X^{T}\mathbf{y}.
$$

In contrast, this is not the case for LASSO, and its solution requires iterative techniques.
