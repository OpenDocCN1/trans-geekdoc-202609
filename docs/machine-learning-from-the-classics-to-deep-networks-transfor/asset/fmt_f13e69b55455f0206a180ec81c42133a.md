
## Gauss–Markov Theorem and BLUE

The optimal linear unbiased estimator is given by

$$
H^* = (X^T \Sigma_\eta^{-1} X)^{-1} X^T \Sigma_\eta^{-1}
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si404.png) (4.100)

and the associated minimum MSE is

$$
J(H^*) := \text{MSE}(H^*) = \text{trace}\{(X^T \Sigma_\eta^{-1} X)^{-1}\}.
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si405.png) (4.101)

The reader can verify that

$$
J(H) \ge J(H^*)
$$

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si406.png)

for any other linear unbiased estimator (Problem [4.19](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#o0100)).

The previous result is known as the **Gauss–Markov theorem**. The optimal MSE linear unbiased estimator is given by

![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/fx020.jpg) (4.102)

It is also known as the **best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE)**, or the **minimum variance unbiased linear estimator**. For complex-valued variables, the transposition is simply replaced by the Hermitian one.

### Remarks 4.6

*   For the BLUE to exist, $X^T \Sigma_\eta^{-1} X$ ![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si407.png) must be invertible. This is guaranteed if $\Sigma_\eta$ ![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si397.png) is positive definite and the $N \times l$ ![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si365.png) matrix $X$, $N \ge l$ ![Equation](images/B978044329238500010X/si408.png), is full rank (Problem [4.20](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml#o0105)).
*   Observe that the BLUE coincides with the maximum likelihood estimator (Chapter [3](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml)) if **$\eta$** follows a multivariate Gaussian distribution; recall that under this assumption, the Cramér–Rao bound is achieved. If this is not the case, there may be another unbiased estimator (nonlinear), which results in lower MSE. Recall also from Chapter [3](#Chp003_65-117_B9780443292385000093.xhtml) that there may be a biased estimator that results in lower MSE; see [13,38] and the references therein for a related discussion.
