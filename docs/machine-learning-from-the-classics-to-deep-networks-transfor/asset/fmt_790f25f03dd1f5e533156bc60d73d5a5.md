
Diffusion models are `probabilistic` models that are closely related to the notion of Markov chains, which have been introduced in Chapter [14](#Chp014_717-754_B9780443292385000202.xhtml). The basic idea that underpins these networks was proposed in [169] and it is inspired from the field of statistical physics. The main rationale is that in order to learn a generative model, a two-stage procedure is followed. One starts by systematically and slowly destroying structure in a data distribution via an iterative process. This is known as the `forward or diffusion process`. In the sequel, during the second stage, one `learns` a `reverse process` which `restores` structure in the data. In practice, in the forward process the input data, e.g. an image array, is gradually perturbed by adding Gaussian noise in a step-wise fashion. In the reverse path, the task is to recover the original input data from the generated noisy (diffused) versions, by reversing the process in a corresponding step-wise rationale.

There are three main directions in implementing diffusion models, namely: a) the denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs), b) the noise conditional score networks (NCSNs), and c) modeling the forward and reverse processes via stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Our emphasis will be on the first path that was historically the first to be proposed in [169]. Furthermore, the underlying theoretical notions are easier to be grasped by the newcomer, and the related mathematical tools have already been treated in previous chapters in the book.
