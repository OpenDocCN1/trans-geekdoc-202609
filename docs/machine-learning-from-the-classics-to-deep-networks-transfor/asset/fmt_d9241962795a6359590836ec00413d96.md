
Besides the previously treated DDPMs, other approaches for building diffusion models have also been developed. The `noise conditional score network` (NCSN) proposed in [172] has its roots in [93]. In a general setting, given a probability distribution, `q(x)`, its Stein `score function` is defined as `∇_x ln q(x)`. Note that the gradient is a vector that points to the direction along which the pdf, `q(x)`, has its largest growth (see, e.g., Section [5.2](#Chp005_177-248_B9780443292385000111.xhtml)). Thus, if the goal is to sample from this distribution, it is reasonable to exploit this information so that to move from a randomly drawn sample, `x^(0)`, toward samples in regions of high density. This process can take place iteratively, via what is known in physics as the `Langevin dynamics`, described as,

```
x^(i) = x^(i - 1) + (γ/2) ∇_x ln q(x^(i - 1)) + √γ ε_i, i = 1,2,…,N,
```

where `ε_i ~ N(0, I)`. The starting point `x^((0))` is sampled from a prior distribution and `γ > 0` is a control variable that adjusts the relative magnitude of the involved terms. It can be shown that as `N → ∞` and `γ → 0`, the distribution of `x^((N))` equals that of `q(x)`. Thus, `x^((N))` becomes an exact sample from `q(x)` under some general regularity conditions, [172].
