
## Factorial HMMs

The observation model is given by:

$$
p(\mathbf{y}_{n}|\mathbb{X}_{n}) = \mathcal{N}\left( \mathbf{y}_{n} \middle| \sum\limits_{m = 1}^{M}\mathcal{M}^{(m)}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(m)},\Sigma \right),
$$

where

$$
\mathcal{M}^{(m)} = [\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{K}^{(m)}], \quad m = 1, 2, \dots, M,
$$

are the matrices comprising the mean vectors associated with each state, and the covariance matrix is assumed to be known and the same for all. The joint probability distribution is given by:

$$
p(\mathbb{X}_{1}\dots\mathbb{X}_{N},Y) = \prod\limits_{m = 1}^{M}\left( P^{(m)}\left( \mathbf{x}_{1}^{(m)} \right)\prod\limits_{n = 2}^{N}P^{(m)}\left( \mathbf{x}_{n}^{(m)}|\mathbf{x}_{n - 1}^{(m)} \right) \right) \times \prod\limits_{n = 1}^{N}p(\mathbf{y}_{n}|\mathbb{X}_{n}).
$$

The challenging task in factorial HMMs is complexity. This is illustrated in [Fig. 16.21](#Chp016_805-853_B9780443292385000226.xhtml#f0115), where the explosion in the size of cliques after performing the moralization and triangulation steps is readily deduced.
