
## Properties of Autocorrelation

### Property I

$$r(k) = \mathbb{E}[u[n]u[n-k]^*] = \mathbb{E}[u[n+k]u[n]^*] = r^*(-k)$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000081/si282.png)

### Property II

$$r(0) = \mathbb{E}[|u[n]|^2]$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000081/si283.png) (2.114)

That is, the value of the autocorrelation at $k=0$ is equal to the mean-square of the magnitude of the respective random variables. Interpreting the square of the magnitude of a variable as its energy, $r(0)$ can be interpreted as the corresponding (average) power.

### Property III

$$r(0) \ge |r(k)|, \quad \forall k \ne 0$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000081/si286.png) (2.115)

The proof is provided in Problem [2.9](#Chp002_19-64_B9780443292385000081.xhtml#o0050). In other words, the correlation of the variables, corresponding to two different time instants, cannot be larger (in magnitude) than $r(0)$. As we will see in Chapter [4](#Chp004_119-175_B978044329238500010X.xhtml), interpreting the expectation as an inner product, this property is essentially the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the inner products (see also Appendix of Chapter [8](#Chp008_343-416_B9780443292385000147.xhtml)).

### Property IV

The autocorrelation sequence of a stochastic process is *positive definite*. That is,

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000081/fx021.jpg) (2.116)

#### Proof

The proof is easily obtained by the definition of the autocorrelation,

$$
0 \le \mathbb{E}\left[ \middle| \sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n u_n|^2 \right] = \sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}a_n a_m^* \mathbb{E}\left[ u_n u_m^* \right]
$$

![Equation](images/B9780443292385000081/si287.png) (2.117)

which proves the claim. Note that strictly speaking, we should say that it is positive semidefinite. However, the "positive definite" name is the one that has survived in the literature. This property will be useful when introducing *Gaussian processes* in Chapter [13](#Chp013_635-716_B9780443292385000196.xhtml). □
