
### 2.2.5 Transformation of random variables

Let **x** and **y** be two random vectors, which are related via the transformation,

**y** = **f**(**x**), (2.43)

where **x** and **y** are random vectors related by the transformation function **f**.

For the rest of the chapter, we are going to deal mainly with real random variables. Whenever needed, differences with the case of complex variables will be stated.

Similarly, for complex random vectors, **z** = **x** + *j***y** ∈ ℂ^(*l*), we have

*p*(**z**) := *p*(*x*[1], …, *x*[*l*], *y*[1], …, *y*[*l*]), (2.41)

where *x*[*i*], *y*[*i*], *i* = 1, 2, …, *l* are the components of the involved real vectors, respectively.

The covariance and correlation matrices are similarly defined as

Cov(**z**) := 𝔼 [(**z** − 𝔼[**z**])(**z** − 𝔼[**z**])^(*H*)], (2.42)

where “*H*” denotes the Hermitian (transposition and conjugation) operation.

The variance of a complex random variable *z* is defined as:

*σ*[*z*]² = 𝔼 [|*z* − 𝔼[*z*]|²] = 𝔼 [|*z*|²] − |𝔼 [*z*]|². (2.40)
