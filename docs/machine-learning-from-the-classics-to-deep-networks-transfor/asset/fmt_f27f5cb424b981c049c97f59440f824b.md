
## Remarks 19.4

$B_{n} := - \text{KL}\left( q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{\phi}) \| p(\mathbf{z}) \right) = \frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}\left( 1 + \ln(\sigma_{ni}^{2}) - \mu_{ni}^{2} - \sigma_{ni}^{2} \right).$

The term $B_{n}$ is a function of $\boldsymbol{\phi}$, through the involved mean values and variances. The cost function is formed by summing over all the input samples, i.e.,

$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}(A_{n} + B_{n}),$

which is minimized w.r. to $\boldsymbol{\phi}$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ via the backpropagation algorithm. Usually, mini-batch algorithmic variants are employed.

Once training has been completed, sampling for generating samples can be carried out in various ways. One can sample from the standard Gaussian prior, $p(\mathbf{z})$, and then pass the generated latent variable vector through the encoder to generate a sample by sampling the conditional. Sometimes, instead of sampling the conditional one can set the generated sample equal to the corresponding mean of the conditional distribution, as this is the predicted output of the neural network that implements the decoder (Eq. [(19.19)](#Chp019_997-1102_B9780443292385000251.xhtml#deq43)). However, sampling from the prior, $p(\mathbf{z})$, usually results in rather low quality samples. Another alternative is to sample the latent variables from the distribution that results by averaging out the learned posteriors, $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{\phi})$ over all input samples, $\mathbf{x}_{n}$. This is no more Gaussian, it is a mixture of Gaussians and can be more representative of the true distribution in the latent space. If, on the other hand, one wants to generate samples from a single class, e.g., the digit "3" from the MNIST data set, sampling in the latent space can be performed by drawing samples from the corresponding learned posterior, $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{\phi})$.

Although VAEs are easier to train compared to GANs, experimental evidence indicates that when VAEs are used for generating images, the obtained images tend to be more blurred compared to the ones that are produced via GANs. In [185], the Wasserstein distance is used to replace the KL divergence in the cost function for training VAEs, and it is reported that this has a beneficial effect on the obtained performance. Another line of research that has been pursued is along the convergence of GANs and VAEs, in an effort to combine the benefits of both; see, e.g., [126], [129]. Another path that has been followed is to construct VAEs with a hierarchy of latent variables. In this vein, latent variables are interpreted as generated from other higher-level ones, see, e.g., [104]. Conditional VAEs have been proposed in [170].
