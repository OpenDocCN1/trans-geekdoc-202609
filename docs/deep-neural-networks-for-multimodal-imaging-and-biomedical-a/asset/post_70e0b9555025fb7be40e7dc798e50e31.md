## Multimodal Image Analyzing

Multimodal image analysis refers to the combined investigation of multiple images of the same instance obtained from various imaging modalities, generally employed to acquire more data or knowledge about the selected instance than is possible from an individual modality.

To identify infected plants (due to flower bud rot, powdery mildew, inflorescence blight, damping off, leaf spots, and blight) and to overcome the effects of predictable marigold plant diseases, employing a multimodal image analysis system is essential. Most marigold crop diseases occur due to fungal agents. To prevent such scenarios, an efficient multimodal image analysis system should be adopted for optimal irrigation to avoid or prevent crops from fungal agents. ### Evaluation of NSCT Approach

Figure 11 shows that NSCT formally recovers the maximum edge content of captured images. The improvement of the technique can be studied from Figure 11(a), where marigold leaves are mostly prone to leaf spot, which must be monitored for proper countermeasures. Thus, collections of different images are denoised to enhance image quality. These enhancements provide detailed information essential for feature extraction and future analysis. Similarly, from Figure 12, collections of marigold flower images affected by flower spot are denoised and then enhanced during reconstruction (especially around the petals). For reconstruction, the noise standard deviations and noise variance of the collected images are estimated. Eventually, the maximum magnitude and mean of the respective coefficients in all sub-band directions are computed and classified into strong or weak edges. Thus, local adaptive shrinkage is performed in each sub-band, including thresholding (L. da Cunha et al., 2006).
