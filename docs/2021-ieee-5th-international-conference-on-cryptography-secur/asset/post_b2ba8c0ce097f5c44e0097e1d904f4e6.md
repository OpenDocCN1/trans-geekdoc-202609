
3) Recommendation algorithm based on TOP-N: The value of \( N \) is critical in TOP-N based recommendation algorithm. In our experiments, its value is decided dynamically, i.e., \( N = \frac{1}{3} Q \), where \( Q \) is the number of ratings with value 5.

We choose the algorithm where PCMS is not performed as the baseline. The bucket number b is set to 1536 and 24576 for ML\_100K and ML\_20M. In this case, the TOP-N recommendation results of two different algorithms are shown in Fig. 3.

We can observe that when the \( \varepsilon \) is larger, the Precision and \( F_1 \) score is correspondingly higher, but when \( \varepsilon \) is greater than 0.4, the improvement is limited, which indicates a trade-off between privacy guarantee and recommendation accuracy. Then combining with Fig.2, we can choose the proper \( \varepsilon \) to well balance the accuracy and the privacy. When compared with the baseline, proposed mechanism does not entail much performance loss especially for the ML\_20M with more data samples, which means the proposed mechanism is effective enough.

## V. DISCUSSION

As shown in Table II and Table III, the optimal bucket number b can be chosen for the efficiency purpose. However, this paper only evaluates the effectiveness of Min-Hash based clustering, whose binary form can tell whether users have watched a movie or not. The inherent simplicity would bring accuracy loss to some extent. Therefore, we argue that it could yield a more accurate clustering result if users' preference features are taken into consideration in the clustering algorithm, or other well-designed algorithms are applied.

Fig.2 shows the practicability of the local differential privacy based on PCMS to preserve one's privacy. Actually, various perturbation mechanisms are able to achieve the similar goal. In the future work, a comparison between these methods will be conducted to pick up one with lower performance loss for our recommendation mechanism.

Fig.3 suggests an acceptable accuracy when suitable \( \varepsilon \) is chosen, which demonstrates a balance between utility and privacy. However, the \( F_1 \) score is lower than expected. One possible explanation is that the inappropriate value of \( N \) in TOP-N recommendation system. In this paper, we only propose a simple rule empirically to set the value dynamically. Thus, how to decide the proper value of \( N \) remains to be studied.

## VI. CONCLUSION

In this paper, we propose a privacy-preserving recommendation mechanism based on blockchain technology. The distributed nature of blockchain enables users to get recommendations while keeping the raw data locally. The feature of the proposed mechanism lies in the combination of IPFS and local differential privacy mechanism, which greatly reduces the computation and communication cost and provides a privacy guarantee during the recommendation process. The experiments conducted on two real world datasets suggest that by choosing the proper \( \varepsilon \), a desirable balance can be maintained between the recommendation accuracy and users' privacy. The acceptable performance of proposed mechanism inspires us to further refine the mechanism and apply it into real world recommendation systems mainly targeting on books or mobile apps in the future work.
