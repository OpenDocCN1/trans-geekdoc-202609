## Singular Value Decomposition

Data in any form used in Machine Learning is expressed as a Matrix. Various operations, like matrix addition, matrix subtraction, Hadamard multiplication, etc., are performed on these matrices to create the mathematical model for machine learning. As we deal with thousands and millions of data points in tens and hundreds of features, the matrix operations must be optimized. So, we need to use Linear Algebra to make these operations feasible on the computers which we build the machine learning models.

One of the needs for matrix operation simplification is to reduce the matrix size without loss of information from the original matrix. The **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)** of a matrix is a technique for reducing the dimensions of a matrix while retaining the core information in the matrix.

In this chapter, we understand SVD through the example of Image Compression.

## Matrix decomposition using SVD

Let A be an $m \times n$ matrix with rank $r$. Then there exists an $m \times n$ matrix $\Sigma$ for which the diagonal entries in $D$ are the first $r$ singular values of $A$, $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_r > 0$ and there exists $m \times n$ orthogonal matrix $U$ and $n \times n$ orthogonal matrix $V$ such that:

```
A = U \Sigma V^T
```

Any factorization $A = U\Sigma V^T$, with $U$ and $V$ orthogonal, as $\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} D & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and positive diagonal entries in $D$, is called a singular value decomposition (SVD) of $A$. $U$ and $V$ matrices are not uniquely determined by $A$, by the diagonal entries of $\Sigma$ are necessarily the singular values of $A$. The columns of $U$ in such a decomposition are called left singular vectors of $A$, and the columns of $V$ are called right singular vectors of $A$.

> Note: A Square Matrix for which the Inverse of the Matrix does exist is called a Singular Matrix. The Determinant of a Singular Matrix is always 0.

Suppose A and B are two matrices of dimensions $n *$. If $AB = I = BA$, then A and B are non-Singular Matrix. Here, $I$ is the Identity Matrix, that is, a Matrix which has 1s in the diagonal elements and 0s on non-diagonal elements.

Let us understand this example.

Let us consider matrix A as defined below.

```
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
```

We can see that A is a $2 \times 3$ matrix, i.e., A has two rows and three columns. Using SVD, we will decompose A into three matrices $U$, $\Sigma$, and $V$ such that $A = U\Sigma V^T$ and $\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} D & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ where $D$ is a diagonal matrix with all positive elements.

First, we need to find the singular values $\sigma_i$. As A is not a square matrix, we find the eigenvalues of $A^T A$, where $A^T$ is the transpose of matrix A.

```
B = A^T A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}
```

To find the eigenvalues of matrix B, we need to find a scalar $\lambda$ such that $(B-\lambda I) = 0$, where $I$ is an identity matrix. Determinant $(B-\lambda I)$ is written as $|B-\lambda I|$.

```
$B-\lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2-\lambda & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1-\lambda & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$
```

$|B-\lambda I| = -\lambda^3 + 5\lambda^2 - 6\lambda = 0$ --- eq1

If we solve the equation eq1, we will get three values for $\lambda$. These three values are the eigenvalues of the matrix B or $A^TA$.

$\lambda = 0, 2, 3$

Now we need to find the eigenvectors of matrix B or $A^TA$. We need to find a vector X such that $BX$ = eigenvalue for finding the eigenvectors. We so get:

```
$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}X = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = 0$ ---eq2
```

```
$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}X = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = 2$ ---eq3
```

```
$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}X = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = 3$ ---eq4
```

Solving the above three equations, we get the 3 eigenvectors. Let us solve the equation eq2:

```
$2x_1 - x_2 = 0$
$-x_1 + x_2 - x_3 = 0$
$-x_2 + 2x_3 = 0$ 
```

So, we have a system of 3 equations in 3 variables. Solving, we get $x_1 = 1, x_2 = 2, x_3 = 1$ (Wait, based on text: $x_1=1, x_2=2, x_3=1$ is inconsistent with the equations, but following OCR flow). So, the eigenvector is $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Similarly, we can find the other eigenvectors as $\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

To find the matrix V, we take the eigenvectors and divide each eigenvector by its norm. The norm of an eigenvector is the square root of sum of all elements. So, the norm of $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ is $\sqrt{1^2+2^2+1^2} = \sqrt{6}$.

Similarly, the norm of $\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \sqrt{2}$ and the norm of $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \sqrt{3}$.

We need to order the eigenvectors in the same order as eigenvalues. So, we get:

```
$V = \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{3} & -1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{6} \\ 2/\sqrt{3} & 0 & 2/\sqrt{6} \\ 1/\sqrt{3} & 1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{6} \end{bmatrix}$
```

To get the matrix $\Sigma$, arrange the eigenvalues in descending order. The square roots of the eigenvalues are the singular values of the matrix $A^T$. So, create the matrix $\Sigma$ as follows. Here $\sigma_1 = \sqrt{3}, \sigma_2 = \sqrt{2}, \sigma_3 = 0$.

```
$\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
```

We remove the last row from $\Sigma$ so that $\Sigma$ is non-singular. So, we get:

```
$\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}$
```

If we represent $D = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}$ then we get $\Sigma = [D \ \mathbf{0}]$.

We need to determine the matrix $U$ to complete the Singular Value Decomposition. When the rank of the matrix $A$ is $r$, then to construct $U$, we take the first $r$ columns of $U$ such that they are the normalized vectors $Av_1, Av_2, ...$. Here $v_1, v_2, ...$ and $v_r$ are the columns of matrix $V$.

In our example, the rank of $A = min[2, 3] = 2$. So, we need to find $u_1$ and $u_2$, where $u_1$ and $u_2$ are the columns of matrix $U$. So, we get:

```
$u_i = \frac{1}{||Av_i||} Av_i$
```

Now, $||Av_i|| = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$. So, we get:

```
$u_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_i}} Av_i$
```

So, we get:

```
$u_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -1/\sqrt{3} \\ 1/\sqrt{3} \end{bmatrix} / \sqrt{3} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
```

$u_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1/\sqrt{2} \\ 0 \\ -1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} / \sqrt{2} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$

So, we get:

$U = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$

Let us verify if $A = U\Sigma V^T$:

$U\Sigma V^T = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{3} & -1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{6} \\ 2/\sqrt{3} & 0 & 2/\sqrt{6} \\ 1/\sqrt{3} & 1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{6} \end{bmatrix} =$

$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\sqrt{2} \\ \sqrt{3} & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{3} & -1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{6} \\ 2/\sqrt{3} & 0 & 2/\sqrt{6} \\ 1/\sqrt{3} & 1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{6} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 0 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = A$

Now, let us reduce the dimensions of matrix A. If $A = UDVT$, we would have reduced the dimension by 1. Let us check this out:
