# 10.2.18 Visibility and Discovery

The visibility concern manifests when technologies become so ingrained into daily life that they disappear from users. If you cannot see a technology, how do you know what it might be doing? For example, with voice response technology such as a smart speaker, when you talk to the device, do you know if it is the only system listening, and do you know if the sounds it hears are stored somewhere for eternity and linked to you?

The discovery trust concern stems from the fact that the Internet was built almost entirely on the TCP/IP protocol suite, with HTML for web sites running on top of TCP/IP. Standardized communication port numbers and agreed web domain names enabled consistent operation regardless of the computer or router manufacturer. This structure has not extended to IoT devices, because generally do not have the processing power to support it. This has enabled a vast number of possible interactions among various versions of software and hardware from many different sources. These interactions are prone to security and reliability problems.

# 10.3 Introduction of NIST 8222 (DRAFT)

The IoT is being utilized in almost every aspect of life, although this fact is often unknown and not advertised. The incorporation of IoT into everyday processes will continue to emerge.

According to Forbes magazine (Columbus, 2017), there will be a significant increase in spending on the design and development of IoT applications and analytics. Furthermore, the biggest increases will be in the business-to-business (b2b) IoT systems (e.g. manufacturing, healthcare, agriculture, transportation, utilities), which will reach $267 billion by 2020. In addition to b2b, smart products are becoming more prevalent as smart homes, smart cars, smart TVs, even smart light bulbs, and other basic commodities. In other words, products that can sense, learn and react to user preferences are gaining acceptance and are deployed in modern living.
