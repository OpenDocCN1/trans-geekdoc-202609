# 5.4.4 BUILDING ATTACK

When a house or organization is infected with malware, the malware can be transmitted through smart device communication. If a hacker attacks an area of building successfully, it can be said to have the potential to infect the entire organization. Residents can be filmed/recorded by hacked smart TVs and infected AI speakers can eavesdrop on personal conversations. Smart video conferencing devices can record the content of meetings between employees in the company. These are all examples of privacy violations.

At the hardware level, is the device itself that attacks as supply chain compromise, pre-OS boot, and firmware corruption are likely to occur. In this environment, or other scenarios, attacks on the network, for example, MITM, can occur because it is a networked environment. In addition, since smart homes or offices are relatively narrow spaces, data can be stolen from the network or prevent devices from operating properly through signal jamming attacks.

# 5.4.5 PRIVACY BREACH

Hacking a data system in a smart city that uses Big Data to provide citizens with convenient services is on a much larger scale of risk due to information leakage. Cyberattacks on sensitively sensitive data such as city administration, health-care, and finance can be devastating to city operations. A perfect example is in 2017, a database of hospital 'Atrium Health' was hacked in the United States, and 2.65 million personal records were leaked (Milliard, 2018). When a user’s credentials are hijacked, a security break-in can occur as the key to access the data is acquired. There is also a possibility that attack techniques related to data capture at the edge/cloud level may be applied.
