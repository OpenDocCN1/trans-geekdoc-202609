# IoT in Healthcare

Digital surveillance has been deployed as part of these measures to:

+   i. Track confirmed and potentially impacted cases with the virus,
ii. Enforcement lockdown when necessary, and
iii. Generate a much-needed source of data and statistics to the authorities.

The IoT community has been stimulated with the COVID-19 outbreak to conceive solutions to combat the pandemic. IoT protocols (e.g., Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), Near-Field Communications (NFC), Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), global positioning system (GPS), and Wi-Fi) are receiving great attention for providing solutions spanning the spectrum from a biosensor capable of detecting the SARS-CoV-2 virus in the air (Qiu et al., 2020) to the rapidly emerging digital surveillance technologies to track individuals and crowds. A digital surveillance for an individual relies on unique identifiers that are either temporarily (RFID tags) or permanently assigned to a person like identifiers, or to a sample individual's locations, either on short or long temporal scales for authorities to keep track of the citizens or residents.

At the most general level, surveillance of humans can be defined as “regard and attention to others (whether a person, group, or an aggregate with a national census) or factors presumed to be associated with this. A central feature gathering some form of data connectable to individuals (whether uniquely identified or as a member of a category)” (Marx, 2015).

Our review identified 64 digital tracking measures deployed in 38 countries (see: https://bit.ly/2Zpmhg). The textual analysis of publications on “Digital Tracking for COVID” shows that two classes of technologies have been utilized to establish digital tracking:

+   i. Mobile technologies (e.g., tracking bracelets, cameras with thermal sensors, drones), and
ii. Mobile technologies (e.g., smartphones with inbuilt location sensors, contact-tracing mobile-based applications).

Despite the avalanche of emerging surveillance technologies to combat the pandemic, there is still a little knowledge of how these could affect society. How widely the contact-tracing mobile applications are being used? What type of data will they collect, and how will data be saved? Whom is it shared with? And are there policies in place to prevent abuse?

# 9.3.2.1 Digital Tracking with Mobile Technologies

The sphere of IoT mobile technologies during the pandemic includes variations of the usage of electronic bracelets, deployment of cameras equipped with thermal sensors and facial recognition software, surveillance drones used to monitor mass gatherings, to even CCTV networks in a bid to help enforce curfews. Our review identified at least 15 of the non-based measures that were taken in 12 countries (see: https://bit.ly/2Zpmhg). For example, in Hong Kong, all people traveling from China, Taiwan, and Macau received a bracelet to track their movements.
