# 8.3 THE ACM AND IEEE COMPUTER SCIENCE KNOWLEDGE AREAS

The ACM and IEEE Computer Society have a long record of sponsoring efforts to establish international curriculum guidelines for academic programs in computing. These guidelines are approximately a 10-year cycle starting with the publication of Curriculum 68 (Atchison et al., 1968) 50years ago. The IEEE/ACM Curriculum Guidelines for Undergraduate Programs in Computer Science was published in 2013 (Force, 2013). In Computer Science, one can view the body of knowledge (BOK) as a specification of the content to be taught and a curriculum as an implementation. As shown in Table 8.1, the IEEE/ACM BOK is composed of 18 KAs.

KAs are not intended to be in one-to-one correspondence with a course that is part of a curriculum. A set of topics was recommended from Voas and Laplante to be considered when creating new computer science curricula, or the recommendations can be used to modify an existing computer science curriculum (Voas & Laplante, 2017). Further, if the curriculum is focused on CPS issues rather than IoT, modifying a systems engineering, electrical engineering, or mechanical engineering curricula might be worth pursuing as well.
