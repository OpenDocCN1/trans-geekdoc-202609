# IoT in Healthcare

+   Smart Thermometers: This device tracks temperature and medication times. This can provide an individual data to help determine if they are improving and provide medication tracking – which can be difficult for someone who is ill and lives alone.

+   Smart Asthma Monitoring: Patch devices (containing sensors) worn by the patient to detect typical symptoms such as wheezing, respiration patterns, cough rate, heartbeat, and temperature. This can assist patients or their caregivers by providing notifications and reminders for medication dosing. With this information, a patient can also determine possible asthma triggers.

+   Heart Disease Wearables and Pacemakers: Heart monitoring sensors inserted into clothing fabric send real-time data to a server where algorithms run to determine if alerts need to be sent to a medical professional. In addition there are a Body Area Sensor (BAS) devices where data is used to predict cardiac arrest. This device also uses a smartphone-based heart rate sensor to detect a health crisis (Majder et al., 2019). The newest generation of pacemakers IoT architecture to monitor breathing, sinus rate, and blood temperature. Irregularities are detected and the patient's heart rate is alerted (slowing or speeding) depending on a patient's current activity level. Data can also be accessed through a mobile device to check battery life and any correlations between their heart pace and activity level (Horwitz, 2019).

+   Diabetes: As described in Chapter 1, a closed-loop insulin delivery system is an amazing device for type 1 diabetics who depend on insulin for survival. The IoT architecture consists of both a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) and an insulin pump that are worn by the patient. The CGM generally sends glucose level directly to the pump. That connection closes the loop to automatically deliver some of insulin needed to the patient. The patient still needs to dose insulin for food consumed (insulin amount based on food carbohydrates.)
