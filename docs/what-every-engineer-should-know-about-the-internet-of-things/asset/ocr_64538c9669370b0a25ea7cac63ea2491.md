In West Virginia, a judge has strapped ankle monitors to positively tested citizens who refuse to quarantine.

In Bahrain, electronic wristbands that are compatible with the country's coronavirus-tracing application "BeAware" have been used to ensure infected citizens remained quarantined. Individuals wearing the electronic bracelet must be connected to the application at all times via Bluetooth and GPS enabled to track movement.

Corporate IoT proximity detection and contact-tracing devices have also been developed. An example comprises the LoRaWAN devices, which detect proximity and contact-tracing among employees in offices and risk to trigger alerts to people they should keep a safe distance.

Surveillance with facial recognition cameras equipped with body temperature sensors has spread rapidly in China as the Chinese government is promoting the development of artificial intelligence (AI) that prioritizes the prevention of COVID-19 spread. Number of facial recognition cameras in use has indeed jumped 3.5 times in the past 3 years to reach 626 million deployed cameras in 2020. This motivated the government to regulate measures to ensure the privacy of the collected biometric data through the "Personal Information Security".

Hoping to stop symptoms of the virus among the public, the authorities in Dubai are also trying cameras fitted with facial recognition software and thermal imaging technology. Similar thermal surveillance cameras have also been tested in Bournemouth Airport, United Kingdom.

The resort city of Cannes on the Cote dAzurAzur tried innovative cameras in outdoor markets and on buses equipped with AI software that generates an automatic alert to authorities when breaches of the mask and distancing rules are spotted. The French firm "Datalacak" insists that its technology set apart from the kind of high-tech surveillance common in China as their system doesn't store any identity data, but only sends anonymized alerts to the authorities.

Surveillance drones with sensors and cameras have also been used to enforce lockdown. For example, the New York Police Department has been using aerial photography to monitor lockdowns.

# 9.3.2.2 Digital Tracking with Mobile Technologies

The smartphone-enabling technologies such as Bluetooth, RFID tracking, built-in sensors, and NFC have it to an integral part of IoT sphere and to be the most used devices in these environments. For the digital tracking purpose during the COVID-19 crisis, authorities have strongly relied on mobile technologies. We identified 50 mobile-related digital tracking measures used in 34 countries. These can be further mapped into two classes discussed below.

# 9.3.2.2.1 Authorities Access Individual or Aggregated Data Directly from Mobile Operators

At least in 11 countries, mobile operators were reported to share individualized location data with authorities to ensure lockdown compliance. For example, in New Zealand, residents arriving from overseas were tested by the police if they consent to be monitored via their cell phone.
