In this chapter, we present a case study for building the requirements and the architecture for an IoT-based Home Automation Management System (HAMS).

# 12.2 CASE STUDY DESCRIPTION: HOME AUTOMATION MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (HAMS)

Consider a business leader that primarily sells sensors and actuators for building automation. They have software applications that manage a network of these devices, but this constitutes a loss leader, i.e., they lose money on the software but it helps the sale of the hardware devices.

Now, the company realizes that the hardware is becoming commoditized, and over time, the profit margins on the sale of their hardware devices are going to shrink. To sustain their business long term, the company decides to create a new building automation system that will be profitable. They wish to accomplish this by doing two things:

+   1. Reduce internal development costs and
2. Expand the market.

The company wishes to build a single Home Management System that will manage the sensors and actuators. With that, the company's internal development costs can be reduced by replacing several of the existing applications with a single one, while the market expansion can be achieved by entering and emerging geographic markets and opening new sales channels in the form of supporting not only native sensors/actuators but also from different manufacturers.
