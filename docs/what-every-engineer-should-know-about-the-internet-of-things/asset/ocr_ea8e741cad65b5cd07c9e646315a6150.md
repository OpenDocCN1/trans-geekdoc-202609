The IoT in the education domain is not an exception. Incorporating IoT in education will generate a large volume of data. Hence, the need for analyzing and treating these data to capture information and trends emerges. The scalability concern is addressed in papers over the selected primary studies. In Jagapt et al. (2016), the authors address the issue of scalability in the context of providing personalized content to the students based on analyzing a large volume of collected student data and activity. They propose a design for a “social recommender” system that is based on Hadoop and its particular computing platform.

In Mahmood et al. (2017), the authors discuss scalability concern when designing a learning management system. Other studied that discussed the scalability issue are Lamri et al. (2014); Sandu, Costache & Basel (2015); Poi et al. (2013); Jurkovic et al., (2015); and Alvarez, Silva & Correa (2016). With scalability, concerns regarding the discussion on the cost of IoT technology in education also becomes significant. The main question that may arise is in the 20 runs, IoT devices and Big Data analysis will increase the already-divide into a two-class learning system: those who can disburse these technologies and those who cannot! At the same time, if going to school should be affordable for everyone, which schools will be able to buy and service their devices? The financial concern of IoT in education is discussed in four selected articles: Maleko, Hamilton & D’Souza (2012); Putjorn, Ang & Farzin (2015); Georgescu & Popescu (2015); and Pruet et al., (2015).

# 7.3.3 HUMANIZATION

There are questions on the moral rate that IoT may play in human lives, particularly in respect to personal control. Applications in the IoT involve more than computers interacting with other computers. Fundamentally, the success of the IoT will depend on how far the technologies are connected and on the humanization of the technologies that are connected (Tech, 2015). IoT technology may reduce people’s autonomy, shift towards particular habits, and then shifting power to corporates focused on financial gain. For the education system, this effectively means that the controlling agents are the organizations that control the tools used by the academic professionals but not the academic professionals themselves (Gubbi et al., 2013).

Dehumanization is discussed in selected papers: Murphy et al. (2015) and Lenz et al (2016). Many studies indicate that face-to-face interaction between students will not only benefit a child’s skills also positively contributes to the character building. The issue that may arise from increased IoT technologies in education is the partial loss of the social aspect of going to school. Conversely, using IoT in virtual learning environments can be of special support to students of special needs (e.g., dyslexic and dyscalculic needs, for example Lenz et al. (2016)).
