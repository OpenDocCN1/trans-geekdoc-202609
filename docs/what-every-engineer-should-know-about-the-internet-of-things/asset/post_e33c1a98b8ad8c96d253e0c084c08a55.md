# 8 IoT Education

## 8.1 INTRODUCTION

By 2025 there will be 75 billion connected devices (Newman, 2020). Every industry is realizing Internet of Things (IoT) can help make their systems and devices more effective and efficient, including healthcare, retail, logistics, and many. Thus, there is a clear urgency for an IoT workforce that has the skills and knowledge in the areas. A quick search on indeed.com for US jobs in this area produced 25,920 jobs when searching for ‘Internet of things’. Other search terms produced 16,840 with ‘IoT’; 5,314 with ‘IoT Systems Engineer’. It is important to note that those results did not include jobs in the areas of data analytics, algorithms, security, which are clearly important. In addition, the Bureau of Labor and Statistics predicts a 30% increase in these by 2026.

In this book, we cover the major areas that are using IoT (e.g., education, cities, homes, healthcare) but we need to address how we will teach the next generation of engineers that analyze, design, and implement these complex systems. We need a new engineering discipline for the 21st century as IoT engineering, IoT systems engineering, or network engineering. This is similar to that called for new engineering disciplines such as when electrical engineering emerged in the 19th century with the invention of the electric motor or as the Industrial Revolution called for a chemical engineering discipline to design the mass production of chemicals. Even more, we have experienced the Software Engineering discipline that emerged with the need for the development of complex software systems. The industry needs a complete physical system (CPS)/IoT curriculum that will require core set of competencies from multiple disciplines is already existing in our engineering and computer science programs.

The goal of this chapter is to present an analysis that determines the state of this educational need and to provide the knowledge to advance the effort in adapting IoT training within academic institutions to meet the urgent employment needs. This chapter presents an analysis of courses related to educating students to build IoT and results of a program review for CPS/IoT-related courses currently being offered at the top 50 universities ranked by Collegeice.net.
