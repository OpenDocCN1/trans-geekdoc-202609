# Blockchain Technologies and IoT

Since the first versions of the implementation of the Bitcoin blockchain, it was equipped with a real programming language, albeit at the assembler level. The Bitcoin transaction, which involves the transfer of cryptocurrency from one or more inputs to one more outputs, is in fact carried out by executing code written in this language, which takes care to verify the authenticity of the private keys in possession of carries the outputs. However, the language of Bitcoin is limited, does not include decision points or cycles, and is therefore Turing complete.

The introduction of Ethereum in 2015 filled this gap, introducing a blockchain and a low-level language, with various high-level and actually usable languages (among them we mention Solidity and Serpent, to code and execute Smart Contracts (Marches et al., 2020)).

The best-known real implementation of a Smart Contract was the DAO. It is an autonomous distributed venture project funding organization, launched in May 2015 with US150 million raised in the crowdfunding campaign. In June 2016, it was attacked and emptied of a third of the funds, corresponding to approximately US50 million in other cryptocurrency. The cause of this problem was an error in the writing of the Smart Contract, which left a "door" capable of recursively emptying all the collected.

The debate that followed centered on one the fact that the feature of the code that enabled the attack cycle did not respect the will of the programmers and was a matter, and on the other, the observation that, if the code is correct, freely examined and by the parties, this feature was also part of the contract, and the attack could not be considered a breach of contract. Since the very nature of Smart Contracts on blockchain does not provide for any authority with the power to intervene, it was the community of Ethereum developers and miners who decided by majority for the first hypothesis, to the fork. As already mentioned, there were those who did not accept the fork and continued with the old blockchain and the old rules, resulting in the generation of Ethereum Classic.

Despite the DAO's epic failure, the topic of Smart Contracts is more relevant than ever. There are many initiatives, on Ethereum, on extensions to the Bitcoin blockchain, on Ripple, or on ongoing implementations of new private blockchains, aimed at creating Smart Contracts systems. The most active sector is currently banking and finance (Dashkevich, Counsell & Estefanis, 2020), but also for other sectors, such as the Public Administration and the Internet of Things, we are actively working to find and experiment with Smart Contracts applications.

Possible application sectors are electoral systems (Caseleggio et al., 2021), domain name registration, financial markets (Dashkevich, Counsell & Estefanis, 2020), healthcare (Kassab et al., 2019), crowdfunding platforms (Fenu et al., 2018), intellectual property, supply chain (Marchesi, Mannaro & Porcu, 2021).
