# IoT Trust Concerns

+   b. Snapshots may be aligned to a clock synchronized within their own network (NIST, Weiss et al., 2015). A global clock may be burdensome for sensor networks that operate in the wild. Others, however, argue in favor of a global clock (Li, 2004). This publication does not endorse either scheme at the time of writing.

+   c. Data, without some 'agreed upon' time stamping mechanism, is limited or reduced value.

+   d. NoTs may affect business performance – sensing, communicating, and computing can speed or slow down a NotT's workflow and therefore affect the 'perceived' performance of the environment it operates in or controls.

+   e. Snapshots may be tampered with, making unclear when events actually occurred, not changing time (which is not possible) but by changing the recorded time at which an event in the workflow is generated, or computation is performed, e.g., sticking in a delay(y) function call.

+   f. Malicious latency to induce delays is possible and will affect when decision triggers are able to execute.

+   g. Reliability and performance of a NoT may be highly based on (e) and (f).
