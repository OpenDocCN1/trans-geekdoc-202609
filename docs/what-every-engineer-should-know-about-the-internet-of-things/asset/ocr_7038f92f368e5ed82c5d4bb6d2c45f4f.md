It is important to understand that the interstitial CGM reading lags behind the actual blood glucose reading because it takes time for the glucose level to reach the interstitial fluid. By interpreting the steepness of the slope, the pump algorithms account for the lag. On the glucose trend, the pump will automatically disperse either insulin or scale back on the basal insulin to address a spike in blood sugar or when sugar is dropping. The pump still needs to dose insulin for the food consumed (insulin amount varies on food carbohydrates). Thus, the closed-loop system still requires user entry when food is consumed. The exciting part is that even more advanced IoT devices for care of TID patients are being developed and tested to not only mean reporting but also maintaining a normal blood sugar range after meals and/activity. This peace of mind for parents as adolescent TID patients frequently forget to dose insulin for food consumed (Palaisait et al., 2021).

Education: The education industry is also integrating IoT into everything from inventory management to remote laboratories. Similar to the healthcare domain, there are IoT systems to manage and track resources such as portable projectors, laboratory equipment, and sports equipment. The collected data from tracking portable equipment can be used to determine patterns and trends. Other IoT educational devices use sensors to detect levels and manage environmental conditions (e.g., lighting/heat). The most innovative IoT systems in smart environments to support learning and improving the quality of education, for example, providing support to students with adapted learning resources by integrating/posting content to a task that is based on the student’s knowledge and level. Another example is virtual laboratories to provide accessibility for school districts that have a resource problem but can also provide personalized experience for the student by using data analytics to monitor a student’s progress and the curriculum accurately. This is covered in Chapters 7 and 8.

Smart Homes: Who would not want a refrigerator that restocks itself? aside from one of the authors’ mother, who LOVES the grocery store. This refrigerator does not exist yet; however, there is beer refrigerator that restocks itself! The smart refrigerator senses you as you move away from the device and automatically reorders from the vendor of your choice. Current smart home development falls into five categories: security design and management (e.g., security systems and management, device security, risk management, security architecture, application security, intrusion detection, encryption, authentication, and privacy); product (g., home comfort, gardening assistance, entertainment, applications to control household devices, smart home automation systems, smart home assistants, health notification/monitoring); activity and behavior patterns (e.g., identifying human activity and behavior patterns, models for planned behavior, and user location and discovery (ULD)); power efficiency (e.g., device/system power consumption/efficiency and energy optimization); and systems design, simulation (systems design, models, simulations, requirements, architectures, frameworks, cost models, performanceimproving algorithms, and interoperability solutions). More on this topic can be found in Chapter 6.
