# B.4 Proof of Theorem 4.16

The proof of this theorem is again similar to the proofs in the previous sections.

Proof We show that any optimal sequence \(\alpha^* \in \mathcal{A}^{(M, \infty)}\) only has elements in the set \(\{0, 1\}\). This is trivially satisfied for \(\mathbf{0}\), the only sequence in \(\mathcal{A}^{(0, \infty)}\), and thus is optimal.

For \(M > 0\), the proof proceeds by contradiction by assuming that there is an optimal sequence, \(\alpha^* \in \mathcal{A}^{(M, \infty)}\), for which there exists a \(\tau\) such that \(\alpha^*_\tau > 1\). We will arrive at a contradiction to the claim that this \(\alpha^*\) achieves an optimal displacement within \(\mathcal{A}^{(M, \infty)}\) by instead considering the equivalent (with respect to the distance function) sequence \(\alpha^{opt}\), which is identical to \(\alpha^*\) except that a zero is inserted after the \(\tau\)th element and all subsequent elements are shifted to the next index; i.e., \(\alpha^{opt}_t > 1\) and \(\alpha^{opt}_{\tau+1} = 0\). As shown in Lemma 4.11, removing (or inserting) a zero elements in the sequence does not affect the distance function \(D(\cdot \cdot)\). Thus, the sequence \(\alpha^*\) achieves the same distance as the sequence \(\alpha^{opt}\).

We show that there is an alternative sequence, whose distance exceeds that of \(\alpha^{opt}\); i.e., \(\alpha^{opt}\) is not optimal. Formally, we first assume that

\(\exists \alpha^{opt} \in \mathcal{A}^{(M, \infty)} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \forall \alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{(M, \infty)} \quad D(\alpha^{opt}) \geq D(\alpha) \quad \text{(B.5)}\)
\(\text{and} \quad \exists \tau \in \Re \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} > 1 \wedge \alpha^{opt}_{\tau+1} = 0. \quad \text{(B.6)}\)

Now we consider an alternative sequence \(\alpha' \in \mathcal{A}^{(M, \infty)}\) that shifts 1 unit from \(\alpha^{opt}_{\tau}\) to \(\alpha^{opt}_{\tau+1}\)

\(\alpha'_t = \begin{cases} \alpha_t^{opt}, & \text{if } t < \tau \\ \alpha_t^{opt} - 1, & \text{if } t = \tau \\ 1, & \text{if } t = \tau + 1 \\ \alpha_t^{opt}, & \text{if } t > \tau + 1 \end{cases} \).

By design, \(\alpha^{opt}\) and \(\alpha'\) are \((\tau, 1)\)-differing sequences and thus we can compute the difference in their distances by applying Lemma B.1 to yield\(^3\)

\(\Delta_{\alpha^{opt}, \alpha'} = \frac{ \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha'_{\tau} \right) \cdot \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} \cdot \alpha^{opt}_{\tau+1} - \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} \right) \cdot \alpha'_{\tau} \cdot \alpha'_{\tau+1} }{ \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} \right) \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha'_{\tau} \right) \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau+1} \right) }\)
\(= \frac{ \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} \right) \cdot \left( 1 - \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} \right) }{ \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} \right)^2 \left( \mu_{\tau-1}^{(\alpha^{opt})} + \alpha^{opt}_{\tau} - 1 \right) },\)
