the more concise form of the constant is given by

```
\alpha_{\infty, \epsilon} = \frac{2}{1 + \epsilon} \exp \left( \ln(\epsilon) \cdot \left( \frac{\epsilon}{1 + \epsilon} \right) \right). \qquad \text{(D.8)}
```

Again, as shown in the proof above, \(\alpha_{\infty, \epsilon} > 1\) for \(\epsilon < 1\).

# Proof of Theorem 8.13

For this proof, we build on previous results for \(K\)-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) hyperspheres. The proof is based on a covering number result from Wyner (1965) that first appeared in Shannon (1959). This result bounds the minimum number of spherical caps required to cover the surface of a hypersphere and is summarized in Appendix A.2.

Proof of Theorem 8.13 Suppose a query-based algorithm submits \(N < D + 1\) membership queries \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{x}^{(N)} \in \Re^D\) to the classifier. For the algorithm to be \(\epsilon\)-optimal, these queries must constrain all consistent classifiers in the family \(\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{\text{convex}, \text{"+"}}\) to have a common point among their \(\epsilon\)-IMAC sets. Suppose that all the responses are consistent with the classifier \(f\) defined as

```
f(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} +1, & \text{if } A_2(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^4) < C_0^- \\ -1, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad \text{(D.9)}
```

For this classifier, \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) is convex since \(A_2\) is a convex function, \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^+} (A_2) \subseteq \mathcal{X}_f^+\) since \(C_0^+ < C_0^-\), and \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^+} (A_2) \unknown \subseteq \mathcal{X}_f^+\) since \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) is the open \(C_0^-\)-ball, whereas \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^+} (A_2)\) is the closed \(C_0^-\)-ball. Moreover, since \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) is the open \(C_0^-\)-ball, \(\nexists \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}_f^-\) such that \(A_2(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^4) < C_0^-\) therefore \(\text{MAC}(f, A_2) = C_0^-\), and any \(\epsilon\)-optimal points \(\mathbf{x}' \in \epsilon\text{-}IMAC^*(f, A_2)\) must satisfy \(C_0^- \le A_2(\mathbf{x}' - \mathbf{x}^4) \le (1 + \epsilon) C_0^-\).

Now consider an alternative classifier \(g\) that responds identically to \(f\) for \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{x}^{(N)}\) but has a different convex positive set \(\mathcal{X}_g^+\). Without loss of generality suppose the first \(M \le N\) queries are positive and the remaining are negative. Let \(\mathbb{G} = \text{conv}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{x}^{(M)})\); that is, the convex hull of the \(M\) positive queries. We assume \(\mathbf{x}^4 \in \mathbb{G}\), since otherwise, the malicious classifier can construct the set \(\mathcal{X}_g^+\) as in the proof for Theorems 8.7 and 8.6 and achieve \(\text{MAC}(f, A_2) = C_0^+\), thereby showing the desired result. Otherwise when \(\mathbf{x}^4 \in \mathbb{G}\), consider the points \(\mathbf{z}^{(i)} = C_0^- \frac{\mathbf{x}^{(i)}}{A_2(\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - \mathbf{x}^4)}\); i.e., the projection of each of the positive queries onto the surface of the \(\ell_2\) ball \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^-} (A_2)\). Since each positive query lies along the line between \(\mathbf{x}^4\) and its projection \(\mathbf{z}^{(i)}\), by convexity and the fact that \(\mathbf{x}^4 \in \mathbb{G}\), the set \(\mathbb{G}\) is a subset of \(\text{conv}(\mathbf{z}^{(1)}, \mathbf{z}^{(2)}, \dots, \mathbf{z}^{(M)})\)—we refer to this enlarged hull as \(\hat{\mathbb{G}}\). These \(M\) projected points \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^M\) must form a \(K\)-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) of the \(C_0^-\)-hypersphere as the loci of caps of half-angle \(\phi_\epsilon^* = \arccos\left(\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\right)\).
