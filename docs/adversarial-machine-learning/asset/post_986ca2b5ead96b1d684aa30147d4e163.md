
for \( p = \infty \). Based on this upper bound on the \( MAC \) of \( g \) and the \( MAC \) of \( f \) (i.e., \( C_0^- \)), if there is a common \( \epsilon \)-\( IMAC \) between these classifiers, it must satisfy

\( (1 + \epsilon) \ge \begin{cases} \left( \frac{D-K}{D} \right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}, & \text{if } 1 < p < \infty \\ \frac{D}{D-K}, & \text{if } p = \infty \end{cases} . \)

Solving for the value of \( K \) required to achieve a desired accuracy of \( 1 + \epsilon \) yields

\( K \le \begin{cases} \frac{(1+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{p-1}} - 1}{(1+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}} D, & \text{if } 1 < p < \infty \\ \frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon} D, & \text{if } p = \infty \end{cases} , \)

which bounds the size of the \( K \)-covering of \( \mathbb{X} \) required to achieve the desired multiplicative accuracy \( \epsilon \).

For the case \( 1 < p < \infty \), Lemma A.2 shows there must be

\( M \ge \exp \left\{ \ln(2) \cdot D \left( 1 - H \left( 1 - (1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{1-p}} \right) \right) \right\} \)

vertexes of the hypercube in the \( K \)-covering of \( \mathbb{X} \) to achieve any desired accuracy \( 0 < \epsilon < 2^{\frac{p-1}{p}} - 1 \), for which

\( \delta = \frac{(1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{p-1}} - 1}{(1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}} < \frac{1}{2} \)

to satisfy the condition required by the lemma. Thus, this theorem is applicable for any \( \epsilon < 2^{\frac{p-1}{p}} - 1 \). For example, for \( p = 2 \), the theorem is applicable for any \( \epsilon < \sqrt{2} - 1 \). Moreover, since \( H(\delta) < 1 \) for any \( \delta < \frac{1}{2} \),

\( \alpha_{p, \epsilon} = \exp \left\{ \ln(2) \left( 1 - H \left( \frac{(1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{p-1}} - 1}{(1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}} \right) \right) \right\} > 1 \)

and

\( M \ge \alpha_{p, \epsilon}^D . \)

Similarly for \( p = \infty \), applying Lemma A.2 requires \( M \ge 2^{D(1-H(\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}))} \) to achieve any desired accuracy \( 0 < \epsilon < 1 \) (for which \( \epsilon / (1 + \epsilon) < 1/2 \) as required by the lemma). Again, by the properties of entropy, the constant \( \alpha_{\infty, \epsilon} = 2^{(1-H(\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}))} > 1 \) for any \( 0 < \epsilon < 1 \) and \( M \ge \alpha_{\infty, \epsilon}^D \).

It is worth noting that the constants \( \alpha_{p, \epsilon} \) and \( \alpha_{\infty, \epsilon} \) required by Theorem 8.12 can be expressed in a more concise form by expanding the entropy function (\( H(\delta) = -\delta \log_2(\delta) - (1 - \delta) \log_2(1 - \delta) \)). For \( 1 < p < \infty \) the constant is given by

\( \alpha_{p, \epsilon} = 2 \cdot \left( 1 - (1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{1-p}} \right) \cdot \exp \left( \ln \left( \frac{1}{1 - (1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{1-p}}} \right) \cdot (1 + \epsilon)^{\frac{p}{1-p}} \right) . \quad (\text{D.7}) \)

In this form, it is difficult to directly see that \( \alpha_{p, \epsilon} > 1 \) for \( \epsilon < 2^{\frac{p-1}{p}} - 1 \), but using the entropy form in the proof above shows that this is indeed the case. Similarly, for \( p = \infty \)

the more concise form of the constant is given by

\[
\alpha_{\infty, \epsilon} = \frac{2}{1 + \epsilon} \exp \left( \ln(\epsilon) \cdot \left( \frac{\epsilon}{1 + \epsilon} \right) \right). \qquad \text{(D.8)}
\]

Again, as shown in the proof above, \(\alpha_{\infty, \epsilon} > 1\) for \(\epsilon < 1\).

## Proof of Theorem 8.13

For this proof, we build on previous results for \(K\)-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) hyperspheres. The proof is based on a covering number result from Wyner (1965) that first appeared in Shannon (1959). This result bounds the minimum number of spherical caps required to cover the surface of a hypersphere and is summarized in Appendix A.2.

Suppose a query-based algorithm submits \(N < D + 1\) membership queries \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{x}^{(N)} \in \mathbb{R}^D\) to the classifier. For the algorithm to be \(\epsilon\)-optimal, these queries must constrain all consistent classifiers in the family \(\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{\text{convex}, \text{"+"}}\) to have a common point among their \(\epsilon\)-IMAC sets. Suppose that all the responses are consistent with the classifier \(f\) defined as

\[
f(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} +1, & \text{if } A_2(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^*) < C_0^- \\ -1, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad \text{(D.9)}
\]

For this classifier, \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) is convex since \(A_2\) is a convex function, \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^+} (A_2) \subseteq \mathcal{X}_f^+\) since \(C_0^+ < C_0^-\), and \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^+} (A_2) \unknown \subseteq \mathcal{X}_f^+\) since \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) is the open \(C_0^-\)-ball, whereas \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^+} (A_2)\) is the closed \(C_0^-\)-ball. Moreover, since \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) is the open \(C_0^-\)-ball, \(\nexists \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}_f^-\) such that \(A_2(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^*) < C_0^-\) therefore \(\text{MAC}(f, A_2) = C_0^-\), and any \(\epsilon\)-optimal points \(\mathbf{x}' \in \epsilon\text{-}IMAC^*(f, A_2)\) must satisfy \(C_0^- \le A_2(\mathbf{x}' - \mathbf{x}^*) \le (1 + \epsilon) C_0^-\).

Now consider an alternative classifier \(g\) that responds identically to \(f\) for \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{x}^{(N)}\) but has a different convex positive set \(\mathcal{X}_g^+\). Without loss of generality suppose the first \(M \le N\) queries are positive and the remaining are negative. Let \(\mathbb{G} = \text{conv}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{x}^{(M)})\); that is, the convex hull of the \(M\) positive queries. We assume \(\mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{G}\), since otherwise, the malicious classifier can construct the set \(\mathcal{X}_g^+\) as in the proof for Theorems 8.7 and 8.6 and achieve \(\text{MAC}(f, A_2) = C_0^+\), thereby showing the desired result. Otherwise when \(\mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{G}\), consider the points \(\mathbf{z}^{(i)} = C_0^- \frac{\mathbf{x}^{(i)}}{A_2(\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - \mathbf{x}^*)}\); i.e., the projection of each of the positive queries onto the surface of the \(\ell_2\) ball \(\mathbb{B}^{C_0^-} (A_2)\). Since each positive query lies along the line between \(\mathbf{x}^*\) and its projection \(\mathbf{z}^{(i)}\), by convexity and the fact that \(\mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{G}\), the set \(\mathbb{G}\) is a subset of \(\text{conv}(\mathbf{z}^{(1)}, \mathbf{z}^{(2)}, \dots, \mathbf{z}^{(M)})\)—we refer to this enlarged hull as \(\hat{\mathbb{G}}\). These \(M\) projected points \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^M\) must form a \(K\)-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) of the \(C_0^-\)-hypersphere as the loci of caps of half-angle \(\phi_\epsilon^* = \arccos\left(\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\right)\). If not, then there exists some point on the surface of this hypersphere that is at least an angle \(\phi_\epsilon^*\) from all \(\mathbf{z}^{(i)}\) points, and the resulting \(\phi_\epsilon^*\)-cap centered at this uncovered point is not in \(\hat{\mathbb{G}}\) (since a cap is defined as the intersection of the hypersphere and a halfspace). Moreover, by definition of the \(\phi_\epsilon^*\)-cap, it achieves a minimal \(\ell_2\) cost of \(C_0^- \cos \phi_\epsilon^*\). Thus,

if the adversary fails to achieve a \(\phi_\epsilon^*\)-K-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) of the \(C_0^-\)-hypersphere, the alternative classifier \(g\) has \(MAC (g, A_2) < C_0^- \cos \phi_\epsilon^* = \frac{C_0^-}{1+\epsilon}\) and any \(\mathbf{x} \in \epsilon\text{-}IMAC^{(*)} (g, A_2)\) must have

\[
A_2 \left( \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^* \right) \le (1 + \epsilon)MAC < (1 + \epsilon)\frac{C_0^-}{1 + \epsilon} = C_0^-
\]

whereas any \(\mathbf{y} \in \epsilon\text{-}IMAC^{(*)}(f, A_2)\) must have \(A_2 \left( \mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x}^* \right) \ge C_0^-\) . Thus, there are no common points in the \(\epsilon\text{-}IMAC^{(*)}\) sets of these consistent classifiers (i.e., \(\epsilon\text{-}IMAC^{(*)} (f, A_2) \cap \epsilon\text{-}IMAC^{(*)} (g, A_2) = \emptyset\) ), and so the adversary would have failed to ensure \(\epsilon\)-multiplicative optimality. Thus, an \(\phi_\epsilon^*\)-K-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) is necessary for \(\epsilon\)-multiplicative optimality for \(\ell_2\) costs. Moreover, from our definition of \(\phi_\epsilon^*\), for any \(\epsilon \in (0, \infty)\), \(\phi^* \in \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\) and thus, Lemma A.1 is applicable for all \(\epsilon\). From Lemma A.1, to achieve an \(\phi_\epsilon^*\)-K-covering of \(\mathbb{X}\) requires at least

\[
M \ge \left( \frac{1}{\sin \phi_\epsilon^*} \right)^{D-2}
\]

queries. Using the trigonometric identity \(\sin \left( \arccos(x) \right) = \sqrt{1 - x^2}\), and substituting for \(\phi_\epsilon^*\) yields the following bound on the number of queries required for a given multiplicative accuracy \(\epsilon\) :

\[
M \ge \left( \frac{1}{\sin \left( \arccos \left( \frac{1}{1+\epsilon} \right) \right)} \right)^{D-2}
\ge \left( \frac{(1 + \epsilon)^2}{(1 + \epsilon)^2 - 1} \right)^{\frac{D-2}{2}} .
\]
