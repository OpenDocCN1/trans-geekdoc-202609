# 4.2.2 Formal Description of the Attack

Having described the problem and the assumptions made under it, a formal analysis can be conducted to reveal optimal attack strategies. This formal analysis begins with a formalization of the problem setting and of the objective. We refer to the number of *attack points* used at the \( t^\text{th} \) retraining iteration as \( \alpha_t \) and the optimal (under prescribed conditions) number of attack points to be used at the \( t^\text{th} \) iteration as \( \alpha_t^* \). We use \( \aleph \) to denote the natural numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., \( \aleph_0 \) to denote the non-negative integers 0, 1, 2, 3, ..., and \( \Re_{0+} \) to denote the non-negative reals. Unless specifically mentioned otherwise, \( \alpha_t, \alpha_t^* \in \aleph_0 \) although later in the text we consider sequences in the non-negative reals, which we differentiate notationally by using \( \beta_t, \beta_t^* \in \Re_{0+} \), respectively.

Along with \( \alpha_t \) and \( \beta_t \), we also define the space of possible attack sequences. Formally, we define \( \mathcal{A} \) to be the space of all legitimate sequence of attack points; that is, \( \mathcal{A} = \{ \left( \alpha_t \right)_{t=1} \mid \forall t \, \alpha_t \in \aleph_0 \} \) (in this space, any attack of a finite span is represented by concatenating an infinite trailing sequence of zeros). Similarly, we define the space of attacks of finite duration and limited size as \( \mathcal{A}^{(M,T)} = \left\{ \left( \alpha_t \right)_{t=1}^T \mid \forall t \, \alpha_t \in \aleph_0 \, \wedge \, \sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t \le M \right\} \); the space of attacks of a finite duration, \( T \), but unlimited size as \( \mathcal{A}^{(\infty,T)} \); and the space of attacks of a limited total size, \( M \), but unlimited duration as \( \mathcal{A}^{(M,\infty)} \). Finally, the analogous continuous versions of these spaces are denoted by \( \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{B}^{(\infty,\infty)}, \mathcal{B}^{(M,\infty)}, \), and \( \mathcal{B}^{(M,T)} \) and defined by replacing \( \alpha_t \) with \( \beta_t \in \Re_{0+} \) in the corresponding definitions of \( \mathcal{A} \).

With the notion of an attack sequence, we now formalize the notion of optimal strategies by reexamining the objective of the attacker. The attacker wishes to maximize the displacement alignment \( \rho \left( \, \cdot \, \right) \) as described in Definition 4.2, which was shown in Corollary 4.8 to depend solely on the attack sequence. The objective function is defined with respect to a given attack sequence \( \boldsymbol{\alpha} \in \mathcal{A} \) according to Equation (4.10) as

\( D \left( \boldsymbol{\alpha} \right) = \sum_{t=1} \frac{\alpha_t}{\mu_t} = \sum_{t=1} \delta_t \left( \boldsymbol{\alpha} \right) \quad \text{(4.11)}\)

\( \delta_t \left( \boldsymbol{\alpha} \right) = \frac{\alpha_t}{\mu_t}, \quad \text{(4.12)}\)

where \( \mu_t = N + \sum_{\ell=1}^t \alpha_\ell \) from Equation (4.7) and the function \( \delta_t \left( \cdot \right) \) assesses the *contribution* due to the \( t^\text{th} \) iteration of the attack, which depends on only the first \( t \) elements of the attack sequence. The goal of the attacker is to maximize this objective function \( D \left( \cdot \right) \) with respect to constraints on the size and duration of the attack.

DEFINITION 4.10 *Optimality*: An attack sequence \( \boldsymbol{\alpha}^* \in \mathcal{A}^{(M,\infty)} \) against a hypersphere with \( N \) initial non-attack points is an optimal strategy that uses a total of \( M \) attack points if \( \forall \boldsymbol{\alpha} \in \mathcal{A}^{(M,\infty)}, \quad D \left( \boldsymbol{\alpha} \right) \le D \left( \boldsymbol{\alpha}^* \right) \). The optimal distance achieved by such a sequence is denoted by \( D_N^* \left( M, \infty \right) \), where \( \infty \) here represents the infinite attack duration. This optimality can be achieved for the attacker by solving the following program for \( \boldsymbol{\alpha}^* \)::

\( \boldsymbol{\alpha}^* \in \underset{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}{\text{argmax}} \, D \left( \boldsymbol{\alpha} \right) \quad \text{(4.13)}\)
\(\text{s.t.} \quad \boldsymbol{\alpha} \in \mathcal{A}^{(M,\infty)}\)
