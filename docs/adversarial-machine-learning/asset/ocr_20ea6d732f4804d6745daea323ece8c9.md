# LEMMA 8.4 For all \(C > 0\), if there exists some \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}_f^-\) that achieves a cost of \(C = A_1^{(\mathbf{c})}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^4)\), then there is some feature \(d\) such that a vertex of the form of Equation (8.9) is in \(\mathcal{X}_f^-\) (and also achieves cost \(C\) by Equation 8.1).

Proof Suppose not; then there is some \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}_f^-\) such that \(A_1^{(\mathbf{c})}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^4) = C\) and \(\mathbf{x}\) has \(M \ge 2\) features that differ from \(\mathbf{x}^4\) (if \(\mathbf{x}\) differs in one or no features it would be of the form of Equation 8.9). Let \(\{d_1, \dots, d_M\}\) be the differing features, and let \(b_{d_i} = \text{sign}(x_{d_i} - x_{d_i}^4)\) be the sign of the difference between \(\mathbf{x}\) and \(\mathbf{x}^4\) along the \(d_i^{\text{th}}\) feature. For each \(d_i\), let \(\mathbf{w}_{d_i} = \mathbf{x}^4 + \frac{C}{c_d} \cdot b_{d_i} \cdot \mathbf{e}^{(d_i)}\) be a vertex of the form of Equation (8.9) that has a cost \(C\) (from Equation 8.1). The M vectors \(\mathbf{w}_{d_i}\) form an M-dimensional equi-cost simplex of cost \(C\) on which \(\mathbf{x}\) lies; i.e., \(\mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i \cdot \mathbf{w}_{d_i}\) for some \(0 \le \alpha_i \le 1\). If all \(\mathbf{w}_{d_i} \in \mathcal{X}_f^+\), then the convexity of \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) implies that all points in their simplex are in \(\mathcal{X}_f^+\) and so \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}_f^+\), which violates the premise. Thus, if any instance in \(\mathcal{X}_f^-\) achieves cost \(C\), there is always at least one vertex of the form Equation (8.9) in \(\mathcal{X}_f^-\) that also achieves cost \(C\).

As a consequence, if all such vertices of any \(C\) ball \(\mathbb{B}^C(A_1)\) are positive, then all \(\mathbf{x}\) with \(A_1^{(\mathbf{c})}(\mathbf{x}) \le C\) are positive, thus establishing \(C\) as a lower bound on the \(MAC\). Conversely, if any of the vertices of \(\mathbb{B}^C(A_1)\) are negative, then \(C\) is an upper bound on \(MAC\). Thus, by simultaneously querying all \(2 \cdot D\) equi-cost vertices of \(\mathbb{B}^C(A_1)\), the adversary either establishes \(C\) as a new lower or upper bound on the \(MAC\). By performing a binary search on \(C\) the adversary iteratively halves the multiplicative gap until it is within a factor of \(1 + \epsilon\). This yields an \(\epsilon\)-\(IMAC\) of the form of Equation (8.9).
