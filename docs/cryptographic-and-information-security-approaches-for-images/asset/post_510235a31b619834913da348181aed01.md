
## 1.5 Basics of Watermarking

Nowadays, billions of bits of data are acquired in a portion of a second. What's more, because of widespread acceptance of the Internet alongside rapid development of multimedia technology, users are able to use digital information (images, video and audio files, web distributed digital repositories and libraries). Copying digital information is a very sensitive issue in the digital world. Digital watermarking is a solution for these issues as it has different applications like copyright protection, authentication, secret communication, and identification. E-commerce, E-voting, medical safety, broadcasting monitoring, military and ordering can also be ensured by digital watermarking. A watermark stays in place, same as the cover image, regardless of the possibility that it is copied. Consequently to guarantee ownership the watermark is removed and tested. Digital watermarking is the process of embedding a watermark in a multimedia object. This object might be image, sound, video or any digital content with the aim of hiding data. Robust watermarking methods ought to be chosen for strong watermark embedding [4–13].

### 1.6.1 Basic Terminologies of Watermarking

The general definitions of some common terms used in the area of watermarking are listed below:

- **Watermark**: The data to be hidden. The term watermark additionally contains an insight that the concealed data is transparent like water.
- **Cover Media/Data**: The media used for conveying the watermark. Sometimes the terms original media, cover media and host media are also used to express it.
- **Watermark Data**: The digital medium which contains the watermark.
- **Extraction**: The technique used for extracting the embedded watermark from the watermark in question.
- **Detection**: The technique used for identifying whether the given media contains a specific watermark.

### 1.6.2 Qualities of Digital Watermarking

The requirements for image watermarking can be dealt with as qualities, properties or characteristics. Different applications request different properties of watermarking. Requirements of image watermarking vary and result in different design issues depending upon image watermarking applications and purpose. These requirements should be considered while designing watermarking system. There are fundamentally five requirements:

#### 1.6.2.1 Fidelity

Fidelity is considered as a measure of perceptual transparency or imperceptibility of a watermark. It implies the similarity of un-watermarked and watermarked images. This aspect of watermarking exploits the limitation of human vision. Watermarking should not present noticeable artifacts as it decreases commercial acceptance of the watermarked image.

#### 1.6.2.2 Robustness

Watermarks should not be removed deliberately or inadvertently by basic image processing operations. Thus, watermarks should be robust against a variety of such attacks. Robust watermarks are designed to oppose typical processing. On the other hand, fragile watermarks are designed to reveal any attempt to change digital content.

#### 1.6.2.3 Data Payload

Data payload is also called capacity of watermarking. It is the maximum measure of data that can be hidden without degrading image quality. It can be assessed by the measure of concealed information. This property describes how much data should be embedded as a watermark, so that it can be effectively detected during extraction.

#### 1.6.2.4 Security

A secret key must be used for embedding and detection processes in the event that security is a major concern. There are three kinds of keys used as a part of watermark frameworks: private-key, detection key and public key. Hackers should not be able to remove watermarks with hostile approaches to reverse engineering the algorithm.

#### 1.6.2.5 Computational Complexity

Computational complexity shows the measure of time watermarking algorithms take to encode and decode. To guarantee security and legitimacy of a watermark, more computational complexity is required. On the other hand, real-time applications require both speed and efficiency.

## 1.7 Watermarking Attacks

There are various, possibly malicious, deliberate or accidental attacks that a watermark is likely to be subject to. The availability of the broad assortment of images dealing with sensitive items make it possible to perform attacks on the quality of the watermarking structures. The purpose of these attacks is to prevent the watermark from playing out its intended purpose. A brief introduction to various types of watermarking attacks is given below:

### 1.7.1 Elimination Attack

Elimination attack hopes to remove the watermark data from the watermarked content. Such attacks exploit how the watermark is regularly an additional signal added in the host signal.

### 1.7.2 Intrusion Attack

Intrusion attacks are those which add additional confusion to the watermarked content. Lossy compression, quantization, cropping, denoising, demodulation, averaging, and noise addition are a few instances of this class of attacks.

### 1.7.3 Shape Attacks

All operations that impact the geometry of the image, for instance, flipping, rotation, trimming, etc., should be observable. A cropping attack from the right-hand side and the bottom of the image is an example of this attack.

### 1.7.4 Security Attack

If the watermarking computation is known, an aggressor can furthermore endeavor to perform modifications to render the watermark invalid or to assess and change the watermark. For this circumstance, we examine an attack on security. The watermarking computation is seen as secure if the embedded information can't be destroyed, detected or fabricated.

### 1.7.5 Protocol Attack

The protocol attacks neither go for destroying the embedded information nor at devastating the identification of the embedded information (deactivation of the watermark). Instead, they exploit semantic deficiencies of the watermark's use. For this reason, a solid watermark must not be invertible or to be recreated. A copy attack, for example, would go for recreating a watermark from one media into another without knowledge of the secret key.

### 1.7.6 Cryptographic Attacks

Cryptographic attacks deal with the breaking of the security. For instance, finding the secret watermarking key utilizing an exhaustive and effective strategy is a cryptographic attack. Another case of this sort of attack is the oracle attack. In the oracle attack, a non-watermarked object is made when an open watermark detector device is accessible. These attacks are like the attacks utilized as a part of cryptography.

### 1.7.7 Dynamic Attacks

Here, the hacker tries intentionally to remove the watermark or basically make it imperceptible. This is a major issue in copyright protection, fingerprinting or copy control.
