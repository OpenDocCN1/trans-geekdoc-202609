
Similarly, robustness criteria are likewise unique, since steganography is fundamentally concerned about identification of the concealed message while watermarking concerns potential removal by a pirate. Steganographic communications are normally point-to-point (amongst sender and receiver) while watermarking systems are generally one-to-many.

### 1.17.2 Cryptography versus Watermarking
Cryptography is the most widely recognized technique for ensuring digital content and is most well-developed sciences. However, encryption can’t enable the owner to monitor how a legitimate user handles the content after decoding. Digital watermarking can protect content even after it is decoded.

### 1.17.3 Cryptography versus Steganography
Cryptography is the study of securing information, while steganography deals with hiding messages so only the sender and the receiver understand that the message even exists. In steganography, only the sender and the receiver know the presence of the message, however in cryptography the presence of the encrypted message is clear to the world. Along these lines, steganography avoids the suspicious attention drawn to the hidden message. Cryptographic procedures try to guarantee the content of a message, while steganography uses techniques that would hide both the message and the content. By combining steganography and cryptography one can achieve better security. In essential words, cryptography is tied in with securing the content of messages (their meaning) and steganography is tied in with camouflaging the presence of messages.

## 1.18 Combination of Different Security Techniques

### 1.18.1 Joined Cryptography and Steganography
Both systems can be combined to give one more level of protection. The message can be first encrypted using cryptography to encode the given information. This encrypted message at that point can be embedded in a cover media using steganography. This combined approach will fulfill the three objectives of information hiding: security, capacity, robustness.

### 1.18.2 Joined Watermarking and Steganography
To secure the validity of the information, watermarking can be applied to it. This watermarked record can be embedded in the cover image by using a stego-key and transmitted over the communication medium. At the receiver end, the data can be first decoded using the reverse system and after that it can be verified for its authenticity using the watermarking. This combined approach will fulfill the four objectives of information hiding: security, capacity, vigor, and detectability.

## 1.19 Cybersecurity vs. Network Security vs. Information Security
We are in a period where organizations are more digitally advanced than any other time in recent history, and as technology enhances, associations’ security postures must be improved, too. Failure to do so could result in an expensive data breach, as we’ve witnessed with numerous organizations. Threat actors are pursuing any kind of association, so with a specific end goal to ensure your business’ information, cash, and reputation, it is important that you invest resources into an advanced security framework. However, before you can begin building up a security program for your association, it’s important that you understand the diverse sorts of security and how they all work together.

### 1.19.1 Information Security
Data or information security (otherwise called InfoSec) guarantees that both physical and digital information is protected from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, interruption, modification, review, recording or destruction. Data security differs from cybersecurity in that InfoSec intends to keep information in any form secure, though cybersecurity ensures just digital information. If your business is beginning to build up a security program, data security is the place you should initially start, as it is the foundation for information security.

When InfoSec specialists are creating policies and techniques for a successful data security program, they utilize the CIA (Confidentiality/Secrecy, integrity and availability) triad as a guide.

The CIA triad has become the true standard model for keeping your association secure. The three crucial principles help build an excellent set of security controls to safeguard and protect your information.

### 1.19.2 Cybersecurity
Cybersecurity, a subset of data security, is the act of protecting your association’s systems, PCs, and information from unauthorized digital access, attack or harm by executing different procedures, technologies and practices. With the countless sophisticated threats of actors focusing on a wide range of associations, it is important that your IT infrastructure is secured constantly to keep away a full-scale attack on your system as well as risks exposing your organization’s information and reputation.

When digital threat actors focus on your association, they investigate your business, as well as your workers, too. They realize that workers outside of IT security aren't as aware of digital threats, so they execute cyber attacks that target human vulnerabilities. Through the process of social engineering, threat actors manipulate individuals into giving the access to sensitive data.

As a business leader, it is your duty to build a culture of security awareness and fill in the gaps in your group's cybersecurity knowledge and understanding. It's essential that your workforce is educated on cybersecurity threats, so it will be less likely for an employee to succumb to an attack. Give your workers the necessary training and technology to strengthen your association's human firewall and reduce the likelihood of a digital attack.

### 1.19.3 Network Security
Network security, a subset of cybersecurity, aims to ensure any information that is being sent through devices in your system is guaranteed that the data isn't changed or captured.

When your system security is compromised, your first priority is to get the attackers out as fast as possible. The more they remain in your system, the more time they have to take your private information. According to Ponemon Institute's 2013 Cost of Data Breach study, barring disastrous or mega information security breaches, the average cost of a data breach for each compromised record in the U.S. is $188. The average total cost to an association in the U.S. is more than $5.4 million. The best technique for reducing the total cost is by getting the attackers out of your system at the earliest opportunity (Figure 1.12).
