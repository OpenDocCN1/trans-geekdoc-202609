and Arnold transform is given by Equation 2.4

$$\begin{pmatrix} x_{n+1} \\ y_{n+1} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_{00} & a_{01} \\ a_{10} & a_{11} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x_{n} \\ y_{n} \end{pmatrix} \mod N \tag{2.4}$$

where a00 × a11 + a01 × a10 = ±1, which makes the scrambling reversible.

It can be applied to scrambled digital images sized N × N by the discrete form in Equation 2.5:

$$\begin{pmatrix} x_{n+1} \\ y_{n+1} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x_{n} \\ y_{n} \end{pmatrix} \mod N \tag{2.5}$$

where (x_n, y_n) ∈ [0, N − 1] × [0, N − 1]

is the pixel coordinate of the plain image’s; N × N is the dimension of the input plain image; (x_{n+1}, y_{n+1}) is the new coordinate for pixel in the resulting image. This method scrambled the pixel positions for a number of times to scramble the image well enough [6].

The digital image can be seen as a two-dimensional matrix. When the size of the image is N, then we have N × N elements, the subscript x, y stand for the position of pixel, x, y ∈ 0,1,2..., N − 1. Let x, y corresponds to the x, y of Arnold scrambling, for each pair x, y, after all complete Arnold scrambling, become x′ and y′, which is equivalent to the original image of the point from (x, y) moved to the (x′, y′), so realizing the movement of pixels in the image, the image with Arnold scrambling traverse all the points to complete a picture of Arnold scrambling.
