These days, society is increasingly encompassed by the circle of the information age, which is driven by human resources and in which data is considered extremely valuable. Information data exists and is used in different forms as financial, official (documents), military, and political. The security of this data during exchange and storage, and in routine practice is vital because its compromise may lead to the disclosure of marketing, financial loss, military top secrets, and even the death toll.

Cryptology is a major way used in the study of data security. Three most important kinds of security are provided by cryptology using suitable and sound designed cryptosystems.

These frameworks are known as confidentiality, integrity, and authentication. Confidentiality is offered by ensuring that secret information is kept private from unauthorized disclosure. Integrity is offered by ensuring the secret information has not been changed, even accidentally, during creation or storage. Authentication is the process of verifying that the sender of the information is properly identified and legitimate.

The encryption schemes are often classified by several aspects, for example, the logic of their key distribution and the size of their input stream. Symmetric encryption schemes have a common secret key assigned to senders and receivers, while asymmetric cryptosystems use different keys for encrypting and decrypting given information. Symmetric cryptosystems have two main branches, block or stream ciphers, where the data to the cryptosystem takes the form of either blocks or continuous bit streams, respectively. Another type of encryption scheme is a hash function, which compresses data in a process form for integrity or authentication.

Cryptosystems are key targets for an attacker wanting to compromise the secret information protected by a security algorithm. In line with the three kinds of security requirements mentioned above, the typical motivations of an attacker are to reveal secret data, to illegally and maliciously modify data, and to falsely assume an identity. Besides, an attacker may attempt to remove evidence, or even add counterfeit evidence that an event or transaction has occurred.

Compromising a cryptosystem which is intended to secure the secret data can either directly allow these events to happen, or eventually weaken another element of the scheme to enable these moves to later make input. Prevailing available cryptanalytic attacks against cryptosystems have adapted to being unbeaten under these conditions.

The overall strength of an encryption algorithm depends on the strength of the individual components, for example, the authentication scheme, the secret key management scheme, the data storage plan, the cryptosystem, the configurations and techniques, and so on. Similarly, the overall strength of a security system depends on the strength of its individual components. A flaw in any of the individual processes may lead to a catastrophic breakdown in the whole security system.
