# WHERE IS YOUR INTERNET OF THINGS DIGITAL EVIDENCE LOCATED?

With cloud evidence, the investigator should understand where IoT device stores its evidence. Unlike the cloud, physical devices can be accessed, triaged, and examined in the traditional manner. If the logs of the IoT device are not stored in the device, inquiries may be made of the system administrator or device manufacturer to locate them. Reading the device's End User Licence Agreement (EULA) may also provide some direction.

In summary, IoT evidence may be stored in the device, or on a remote server operated by the IoT device manufacturer.

# LAWFUL SEIZURE OF INTERNET OF THINGS EVIDENCE

As with all evidence, seizure must be lawful. A complainant in most instances, will be able to provide authority in instances but must be aware of the value of IoT-connected devices when petitioning a court for a search warrant or civil court order. If it is likely to provide evidence and examination can be justified by the court official, explain its relevance and seek authority to seize and examine the device.

The key point of this chapter is to look broadly when seeking evidence in a digital investigation. Society becomes connected digital fingerprints are being left in numerous locations, providing evidence toward identifying a suspect. Many IoT devices have little or no-security which can become a very valuable attack vector for the cybercriminal, leading to network intrusions through unexpected devices—such as smoke detectors, webcams, refrigerators, and televisions—which are connected to a network that is unpatched and not secure.

As the lack of security provides opportunity to the cyber- or internal criminal, so it also provides opportunity to the investigator, as the breadcrumbs of evidence these devices will leave in and around the criminal scene will provide the opportunity to uniquely identify a person and place them at the crime scene at the time of the activity under investigation.
