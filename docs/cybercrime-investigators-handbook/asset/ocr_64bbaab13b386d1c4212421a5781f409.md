Obtaining cloud-based evidence from a jurisdiction where the EU means that the investigator must navigate European privacy directives as well as host country legislation. As an example, France has the Data Protection Act of 2004, which is administered by the Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés (CNIL), as well as the EU Data Protection Directive to protect data in their jurisdiction. This legislation places additional responsibilities on parties seeking to remove data from other countries, including potentially being required to seek separate notification and/or authorization from the CNIL prior to the data being removed from France should the data owner not have informed consent or the recipient country not have a level of data protection that is recognized by French authorities as being equivalent to their domestic legislation at the French or EU level. ³³ Removing any data from the EU requires legal advice from your own resources and preferably the CSP.

Encryption in a cloud-based environment may also present an issue for investigators. It is a security provision recommended by CSPs. Encryption will be particularly hurdles when the data is sought from a party who is not the data owner, as the CSP may not hold the key to decrypt the data.¹¹¹ The key may be held by the owner or user of the data and not be accessible to the CSP.¹¹⁷ Due to the increasing number of high-visibility data breaches being reported in the media, consumers are turning to encryption to protect their data in the event of a breach.
