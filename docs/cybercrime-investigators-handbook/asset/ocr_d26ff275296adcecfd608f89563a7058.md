# 4 Introduction

Although this book makes some references to material from third parties, it is not intended to be an academic book. This is because much of the material is from academic literature or web sources, but from the experience of the author as a cybercrime investigator. The major exception to this is Chapter 12, which relies on evidence from the author's doctoral thesis on cybercrime investigation in a cloud-computing environment and where academic references from a literature review are noted. Where explanations are provided, in the glossary, they are largely kept at a low-level technical definition to allow those new to this field of work to understand the material and its relevance without having to learn a whole new language technology.

Due to the dynamic nature of evidence, advances in technology, and the evolution/legal/court decisions, this book is not intended to be an exclusive guide in every legal jurisdiction or to cover every potential cyber event. Where material in this book conflicts in any way with the laws of your jurisdiction, the legal environment(s) you operate in will always take precedence. The book intends, to provide critical thinking among management, IR team managers, and investigators should be identified and subsequent evidence need to be presented in court.

This book contains many of the steps a cybercrime investigator will take, from the initial identification of a cyber event to considering a prosecution. There are many lists of things the investigator may consider. These are not exhaustive lists and are provided to expand the thinking as to what to do, where evidence may reside, and how to legally obtain and manage it. Use this book as a prompt and as a definitive step-by-step template, as each cyber investigation is different and each jurisdiction has its own legal requirements.

The lists in this book provide a handy point of direction in each stage of the investigation. As you will discover, at each stage there are many things to do and no one can remember them all time. So, the lists are provided as a memory prompt of things to consider and apply the circumstances, legislation in your jurisdiction, and your experience. Not all items in the lists will be relevant in all instances. The explanations are in plain language and technical terms are kept to a minimum to assist your understanding of new concepts.
