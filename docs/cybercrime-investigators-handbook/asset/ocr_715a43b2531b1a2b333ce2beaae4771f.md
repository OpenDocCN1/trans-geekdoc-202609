This means organizations subject to a cyber-attack that wish to find information about who is behind the attack will need to hire an experienced cyber investigator (scarce and very expensive) or investigate the matter themselves. Alternatively, they will not conduct an investigation and instead focus on increasing security.

The decision by victims to not investigate acrime is made for many reasons, including the time and money to be expended on an investigation, the focus of the business being directed on the investigation, the internal disruption it causes, and the reputational harm when the community finds the company security has been breached and all the data entrusted to them are stolen. Also, directors would not look forward to the day that they stand before a public annual general meeting and explain to the shareholders that all the company data was on their watch and that they have made no effort to recover it or who did it.

To members of an incident response (IR) team or the cyber investigator, responding to an attack is often an inexact science as the attackers’ motives and skill levels vary. Whereas an attack against a single desktop computer may be easily contained and investigated, an attack against a complete distributed corporate network will require significant resources and an experienced team to protect the company, their data, and clients. As the method varies, the investigation strategy will not necessarily follow the exact path each time.

Investigating a cyber-attack may be a critical part of the continuation of the business. When the attack is discovered, a mixture of panic, stress, anxiety, and fear is seen among staff, and those tasked to mitigate and eradicate the attack may make the future of the company rests upon their shoulders. Many employees will be concerned as to their personal future, as they will be familiar with the stories of businesses hit by a cyber-attack that no longer exist months later. Staff members of the organization being interviewed as a part of the incident response may also feel that they are held responsible and the interview is a method of laying blame at their feet.

Why conduct an investigation and gather evidence? Why should a company start investigating the crime and try to track down the offender? With the proliferation of the instances of cybercrime, there is an expectation among the community that those who are entrusted with the PII take their responsibilities seriously and ensure their data is secure.
