A definition of cloud forensics is:

[The] process of identifying, labeling, recording and acquiring forensic data from the possible sources of cloud in the cloud.⁹

Although the Association of Chief Police Officers (ACPO) provides guidelines about the manner in which forensic evidence is obtained in traditional examinations where access to the electronic storage device is achievable,⁴¹ it does not provide specific guidelines as to the collection, analysis, and presentation of digital data secured from a cloud environment. Specific case law and rules of evidence directly attributable to forensic computer examinations are still evolving, providing limited direction to examiners and investigators.

The CSA has stated that conducting forensic examinations in the multitanted, highly virtualized environment that cloud computing is a complex matter and current forensic techniques are immature. It also states that strong links between the practice of digital forensics and the legal system, as established rules of evidence place restrictions on the acceptable manner in how digital evidence is obtained and presented. A paper from its Incident Management and Forensics Working Group titled “Mapping the Forensic Standard/IEC 27037 to Cloud Computing” surveys the issues relating to forensic examinations being conducted in cloud-computing environments.³

There will be increasing security challenges to users of the cloud due to the distributed, virtualized nature of the cloud. This in turn create new challenges for digital investigators, especially as the capacity to obtain, preserve, and analyze potential digital evidence is a critical path of business and the investigative process.

This is because the servers are finely configured for functionality and stability and are not designed for digital investigators of various standards to use any of the multitude of forensic tools on the market.
