# Enhancing deep learning-based field reconstruction with a differentiable learning framework

Accepted: 21 May 2025

Published online: 22 July 2025

Achieving accurate reconstructions of complex high-dimensional fields from sparse sensors remains a long-standing challenge. Frequently, reconstruction performance is mainly constrained by models and placement. The placement of sparse and prohibitive sensors restricts information quality, resulting in formidable reconstruction tasks. Despite deep learning-based models having made strides, they typically lack the ability to co-optimize neural network parameters versus low-dimensional sensor placement further poses significant difficulties. Here we present a general bilevel differentiable learning framework that effectively integrates models with sensor placement optimization (DSPO), enabling search for placement and accurate global field reconstruction. Within this framework, models are complemented with a differentiable operator to achieve the differentiability of placement. A gradient-based optimizer empowers models by dynamically updating placement. The alternating optimization strategy is adopted to efficiently solve the joint optimization. We demonstrate the efficiency and generalizability of the DSPO on baseline models across various scenarios, including periodic and cyclic physical fields, regular and irregular grid datasets, and noisy and nameless observations. Our results show that the DSPO significantly improves the reconstruction accuracy of models and robustness against baseline models comparable with the state-of-the-art performance. Our framework provides a new general paradigm for the use of neural networks and placement optimization techniques for real-world applications.

To address these issues, we propose a general bilevel framework that integrates reconstruction models with sensor placement (SPO) for global field reconstruction. The operator is introduced into the bilevel framework without introducing any additional parameters. Gradient-based bilevel optimization is used to train and test models. Our framework has three major contributions: (1) We introduce an SPO-based framework for joint reconstruction and sensor placement. (2) By alternately optimizing different variables (blocks and placement), the alternating optimization strategy is designed to effectively resolve the trade-off between reconstruction quality and sensor numbers. (3) A differential operator is introduced to handle the non-differentiability of the global field with respect to placement. This enables an end-to-end bilevel framework to dynamically optimize sensor placement with global observations and their derivatives, which effectively handles both regular and irregular field densities. This framework can serve as a general paradigm, offering high generality and flexibility for various applications. This work is the first and systematic study on using NN-based models across various scenarios, including periodic and cyclic fields, regular and irregular grids. Throughout this article, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework by applying it to various scenarios.

The reconstruction model aims to capture the features of the global field $\mathbf{y}$ from a number of sensor observations $\mathbf{s}$ at $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}, i=1,...,n$, where n denotes the number of points in the horizontal/vertical direction of a high-resolution grid, and m denotes the number of sensors. For, the SPO models can be formulated as a bilevel optimization. The objective is to enhance the reconstruction quality by optimizing models and placement within a unified framework. The goal of this study is to propose a general bilevel learning framework that efficiently optimizes sensor placement and reconstructs global fields, namely, respectively. A bilevel SPO framework is defined as follows (1):

$$\boldsymbol{\theta}^* = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \mathcal{L}_f(\mathbf{y}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{s}; \boldsymbol{\theta}))), \text{ where } \boldsymbol{\phi}^* = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\phi}} \mathcal{L}_s(\boldsymbol{\phi}; \boldsymbol{\theta})$$

where $\theta$ denotes the global field, $\phi$ denotes the train/validation parameters of NN-based models, and the $\mathcal{L}$ denotes the objective function (criterion) to evaluate the reconstruction performance. Reconstruction error is considered as the criterion in this work.
