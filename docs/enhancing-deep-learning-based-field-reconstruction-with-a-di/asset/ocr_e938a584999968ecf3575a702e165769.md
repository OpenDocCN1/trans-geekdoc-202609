To investigate dependence of DSPO for periodic physical problem, we evaluated reconstructed performance of DSPO and the two baselines RS and US. The results are shown in Fig. 2. RS exhibits large variance, under varying numbers of sensors, DSPO consistently outperforms RS and US in all scenarios. (a) DSPO outperforms RS and US in all models. (b) DSPO shows comparable performance with MLP. Additionally, compared with U.S., DSPO outperforms PODN and Sensiver by 1-2 orders of magnitude. We also show the absolute errors of MLP, PODN, and CNN. Models using DSPO show significantly lower errors, especially in the flow direction. The optimized sensor distribution is shown in Fig. 2b. The advantage of optimized sensor placement consistently covers the wake area. More importantly, Fig. 2c also illustrates the importance of the rank of optimized placement. Removing sensors from the wake area

results in a greater decrease in reconstruction performance. This result highlights the ability of DSPO to capture flow field characteristics and is more beneficial for flow field reconstruction. Comparisons of DSPO with more extensive SPO baselines are shown in Supplementary Table 1. DSPO provides lower reconstruction errors compared to other methods. This demonstrates that algorithm sensor placement can be better for the point of high-dimensional NN parameters and lower sensor variables. DSPO can achieve better results than DSPO-1. Demonstrating the superiority of the proposed method, the computational complexity of the algorithm is shown in Supplementary Fig 2a. These results span computational complexity, structural characteristics and their unique advantages, making them suitable for their application scenarios.
