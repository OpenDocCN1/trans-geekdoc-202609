Another strategy for discovering a problem is to look for unique cases, both
good and bad. Highlighting abnormal cases is a great way of understanding
how things work, but it's important first to define the most common
occurrences to have an accurate idea of what inmates an abnormal case.
Returning to our farmer friend, a powerful wind descended upon the orchard,
causing the fruits to fall off the trees. Most of the trees lost some bit of fruit.
The dropped apples be to be close to the base of the tree. Another small
grouping of trees appeared to lose a lot of fruit, and the drops were typically
scattered from the trees. It is possible that some weird conditions
worsened the situation in this particular spot? Or is it a matter of chance that
a few trees in the same location lost some fruit than would be normal.
An organized count of lost fruit underneath a random sample of trees would
help respond to this question. The bulk of the following trees would perhaps
have each the same amount, but more importantly, “typical” group
deliver a yardstick against which we could determine what would
actually count as unusual. Once you find an unusual set of cases that is
beyond the limits of typical, you can shift your attention to these to try
understand the anomaly.
A third approach to define data problems is to determine the risk and
uncertainty. It is always important to eliminate uncertainty because of the way risk impact
the things you do. At home, at school, at work, life is full of risks. Making a
decision or failing to decide invites a chain of events that may result in
som good or not so good. It's hard to say, but in general, you prefer to
arrow things down in a way that optimizes the probability of a bad one. To
accompl this, you need to make helpful decisions, and better decisions
need to cut down on uncertainty. By asking questions related to risks and
uncertainty, a data scientist can zero in on the issues that matter. You can
even refer to the previous two strategies-asking about stories that demand
professional wisdom and about anomalies.
