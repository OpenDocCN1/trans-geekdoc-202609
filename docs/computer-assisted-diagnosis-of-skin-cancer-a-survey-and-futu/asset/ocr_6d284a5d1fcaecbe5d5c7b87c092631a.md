# ## 2. Complications in the diagnosis of skin lesions

The different image forms and sources are responsible for the difficulties in diagnosing skin lesions. Human skin color can seem very different from person to person, making skin detection a complicated and challenging operation. Fig. 2 illustrates the different visual properties of skin lesions’ images present several issues, including (a) blood vessels (b) low contrast (c) ruler in skin images (d) Bubbles (e) hair artifacts, and (f) non-uniform border.

The location, size, and shape of lesions vary enormously. For accurate analysis, the majority of image analysis algorithms necessitate image pre-processing. During image acquisition, artifacts called noise are added to the images. The presence of noise and artifacts may affect the ability to identify lesion images. Hair artifacts, bubbles, and blood vessels are among the examples. It can be difficult to obtain precise images of the border of skin lesions during the preprocessing stage for straightforward asymmetry prediction. Low contrast from nearby tissues might occasionally exist, which adds to the difficulties.
