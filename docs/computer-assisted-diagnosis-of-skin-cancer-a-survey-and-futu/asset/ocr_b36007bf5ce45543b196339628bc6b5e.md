# 3.2. Pre-processing

Pre-processing of an image is an important stage used in diagnosis since it removes noise and improves the content of the processed image. The pre-processing is used to eliminate unwanted artifacts like noise, blood vessels, hair, skin lines, texture, and air bubbles. These abnormalities limit border identification, resulting in accuracy loss and higher computing costs. As a result, various pre-processing procedures are necessary to eliminate these anomalies to permit segmentation and classification. Preprocessing is mostly used to enhance images for more efficient processing and reliable feature recognition. This stage aims to acquire an input image for attaining a grayscale image by removing noise and converting it to binary. It includes different techniques such as contrast enhancement, and filtering used to eliminate any undesired features (such as hair) from the image. Additionally, this procedure removes unnecessary noise and improves the image quality for the identification of the focal areas in the lesion image. Table 1 gives the description and categories of various preprocessing along with their categories.

+   * **Image enhancement**: Image Enhancement is a critical method for improving the image’s visual look; it’s described as the "superior" transformation form for future automatic detection procedures. As a result, there are three types of image augmentation i.e., Image-scaling, contrast adjustment, and color space transformation.
+   * **Image restoration**: The method for recovering a deteriorated image from a fuzzy and noisy one is known as image restoration. It is capable of restoring degraded photos in a variety of methods. Image degradation can be produced by several factors, that includes imaging system flaws, poor focusing, motion, and so on, all of which cause an image to become noisy or blurry. Because damaged images lead to fault detection, it’s critical to understand the types of noise in an image before choosing the best denoising method. The different types of Categories are Restoration from noise (mean filter, adaptive filter, order statistics filter), Restoration from blur.
+   * **Removing thick hairs**: Thick hairs are a frequent impediment to automated analysis of tiny skin lesion images, and they can cause the segmentation process to be misled. To eliminate dense hairs in skin cancer images, researchers used mathematical morphology approaches such as Curvilinear structure recognition, inpainting-based method, automate software known as Dullazor, and the Top Hat transform in combination with the bicubic interpolation method. The procedures are utilized to create hairless images.

The images produced by preprocessing steps of the skin cancer diagnosing systems can be distinguished from those produced by other systems.
