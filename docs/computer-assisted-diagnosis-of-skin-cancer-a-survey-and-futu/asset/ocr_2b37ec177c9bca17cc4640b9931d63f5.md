# 6.2. Unstable datasets

In the real world, skin cancer datasets are significantly skewed. The quantity of images in databases for each kind of skin cancer varies. E.g., these datasets have several images of various forms of cancer but just some images of rare skin cancers, making image generalization challenging.

# 6.3. Inadequate access to high-performance hardware

To obtain the various characteristics of a skin lesion image, the deep learning programmer needs the most powerful system resources with powerful GPU capacity, which is demanding for improved skin cancer detection. The lack of appropriate computer hardware requirements is a major issue in training for the detection of skin cancer.

# 6.4. Skin cancer images with minor interclass variation

Healthcare images have comparatively minimal inter and intra-class variation, for example, the cancerous and non-cancerous images are significantly low variation than the horse and zebra images. Many skin lesions are so similar that they are harder to identify. Because of the limited diversity, image analysis and classification become incredibly tough.

# 6.5. Investigation of genetic and environmental factors

Different genetic factors of skin cancer, such as fair complexion, eye color, red hair, body moles, and any medical history have been identified as genetic causes of cancer. These factors and environmental factors such as high exposure to Ultraviolet radiation, the possibility of developing skin cancer increases. These parameters can be used with current deep learning algorithms to boost performance.

# 7. Future recommendations

There are many studies in the area of skin cancer detection, however more recent studies have focused on increasing the performance and accuracy of skin cancer detection. To achieve these goals, sophisticated pre-processing and segmentation techniques are used. Additionally, the accuracy of classification operations is being impacted by recent deep learning research. The primary objectives of this type of study are advancements in scalability and flexibility during a skin cancer diagnosis. In the diagnosis of skin cancer by dermatologists, computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) has emerged as one of the main research areas. The fundamental idea behind CAD is to offer a result of data as a second opinion to help dermatologists analyze images by increasing the consistency and accuracy of diagnosis and also by speeding up image reading. A self-assessment tool like this one could be used in the future to automatically identify people who need treatment. To standardize screening recommendations, it would potentially be feasible in the future to create a skin cancer screening registry.

# 7.1. Gathering of data and quality control

The collection of relevant data and quality criteria of the data are the first steps in the CAD process. CAD systems include electronic health records (EHRs) as a crucial component. Eliminating bias in the training data is one of the crucial data gathering concerns. The efficiency of the CAD system could be severely hampered by the presence of bias in such data. Some other issue is related to data imbalance, improper sampling generates a problem of class imbalance. As a result, CAD systems would need to process enormous volumes of clinical data to arrive at conclusions about skin diseases that are statistically significant.
