## 3.2. Pre-processing

Pre-processing of an image is an important stage used in diagnosis since it removes noise and improves the content of the processed image. The pre-processing is used to eliminate unwanted artifacts like noise, blood vessels, hair, skin lines, texture, and air bubbles. These abnormalities limit border identification, resulting in accuracy loss and higher computing costs. As a result, various pre-processing procedures are necessary to eliminate these anomalies to permit segmentation and classification. Preprocessing is mostly used to enhance images for more efficient processing and reliable feature recognition. This stage aims to acquire an input image for attaining a grayscale image by removing noise and converting it to binary. It includes different techniques such as contrast enhancement, and filtering used to eliminate any undesired features (such as hair) from the image. Additionally, this procedure removes unnecessary noise and improves the image quality for the identification of the focal areas in the lesion image. Table 1 gives the description and categories of various preprocessing along with their categories.

- **Image enhancement**: Image Enhancement is a critical method for improving the image’s visual look; it’s described as the "superior" transformation form for future automatic detection procedures. As a result, there are three types of image augmentation i.e., Image-scaling, contrast adjustment, and color space transformation.
- **Image restoration**: The method for recovering a deteriorated image from a fuzzy and noisy one is known as image restoration. It is capable of restoring degraded photos in a variety of methods. Image degradation can be produced by several factors, that includes imaging system flaws, poor focusing, motion, and so on, all of which cause an image to become noisy or blurry. Because damaged images lead to fault detection, it’s critical to understand the types of noise in an image before choosing the best denoising method. The different types of Categories are Restoration from noise (mean filter, adaptive filter, order statistics filter), Restoration from blur.
- **Removing thick hairs**: Thick hairs are a frequent impediment to automated analysis of tiny skin lesion images, and they can cause the segmentation process to be misled. To eliminate dense hairs in skin cancer images, researchers used mathematical morphology approaches such as Curvilinear structure recognition, inpainting-based method, automate software known as Dullazor, and the Top Hat transform in combination with the bicubic interpolation method. The procedures are utilized to create hairless images.

The images produced by preprocessing steps of the skin cancer diagnosing systems can be distinguished from those produced by other systems.

## 3.3. Segmentation techniques for analysis and diagnosis of skin lesion images

Segmentation is a critical phase in the diagnosis of skin lesions, it’s a crucial step in analyzing skin lesion images by isolating the unhealthy from the healthy, this method gets an area of interest. Recently, more emphasis has been placed on the segmentation process. Many segmentation methods or algorithms have been explored in the literature. These techniques address several shortcomings connected with typical medical division processes. However, no one strategy can be considered superior. Such solutions are only applicable to certain images and applications. Thresholding, region growth, merging and splitting, clustering, edge detection, and model-based image division are different techniques of segmentation. All segmentation techniques are based on the following important factors: levels of intensity, similarity, and discontinuity. This section goes over several strategies for segmenting skin lesions images handcrafted features-based approaches, such as threshold-based methods, are among these strategies, intelligence-based supervised segmentation, edge, and region-based methods. Deep learning and Artificial Neural Network algorithms are examples of intelligent-based systems.

### 3.3.1. Traditional intelligence based methods of segmentation

Artificial Intelligence-based systems can do an analysis of the image by reasoning, learning, and perceiving images in large datasets that already exist. Skin lesion segmentation may be accomplished in a variety of ways, both supervised and unsupervised, using existing datasets to train and learn the system. Intelligent-based methods of segmentation such as ANN, fuzzy C-means, and Genetic algorithms are commonly used and solutions have also incorporated deep learning technologies. Furthermore, Table 2 describes various traditional techniques of segmentation.

- Threshold-based methods: Threshold-based methods can be used to create binary pictures from a grayscale image. Segmentation is used to create binary pictures from colored images. Segmentation is the process of assigning two or more classes to each pixel in the original image. When there are more than two classes, the outcome is usually a series of binary images. In image processing, thresholding is used to divide a picture into smaller segments, or chunks, by defining their boundaries with at least one color or grey scale value. Eq. (1) shows the Thresholding of an image where T is a threshold value
  g(x, y) = { 0, f(x, y) < T
              1, f(x, y) ≥ T } (1)

- Edge or boundary based segmentation methods: Consistency is essential to region-based methods. Assuming that all pixels in a region must be of the same gray level, these algorithms break the image into smaller subregions. The term "region-based technique" refers to methods that use comparable patterns in the intensity values of nearby pixels as a starting point. The algorithm's goal is to group areas based on their structural or functional roles, which is the cluster’s purpose.

- Region-based segmentation: A pixel must conform to certain established constraints in this segmentation process to be classified into related pixel regions. Based on particular criteria, an image is segmented into separate groups or regions of comparable pixels. Its core principle is that pixels nearby inside the same region have the same value.

- Laplacian of Gaussian Filter method: The Laplacian Filter is a second-order or second-derivative picture enhancing technique that draws attention to areas with rapid changes in intensity. It’s very good at detecting minute features in an image. A Laplacian Filter will improve any feature with a strong discontinuity. Eq. (2) shows the Laplacian second derivative of the linear differential operator where function f denotes the image.
  Δ²f = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y² (2)
