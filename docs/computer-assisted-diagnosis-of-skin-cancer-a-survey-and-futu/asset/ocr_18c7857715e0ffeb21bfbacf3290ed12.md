ABSTRACT

Skin cancer is amid the most frequent types of cancer, accounting for approximately 2 to 3 million cases being diagnosed each year worldwide. Abnormal cell development on the skin causes skin lesions and manual inspection of skin lesions is a difficult, challenging, instinctive, and tedious task. Computer Aided Diagnosis (CAD) techniques can assist doctors to enhance their investigation skills and reduce the time it takes to get a precise diagnosis. Furthermore, the lack of advanced, user-friendly CAD techniques has raised serious concerns about the noninvasive, precise, and rapid identification of diseases. CAD systems can help to make an early diagnosis of skin lesions to plan timely treatment schedules for the patients to increase their survival rates. However, due to the distinctive and complex properties of skin lesion images, examination of skin lesion images still poses significant difficulties. The motivation behind this study is to discuss several preprocessing, segmentation, and classification strategies for analyzing skin lesions to differentiate between cancerous and non-cancerous images. The primary goal is to provide an overview for naive researchers to commence their research in this field. Moreover, this manuscript will also highlight open challenges and future recommendations which further calls the distinct researchers to begin their research in this domain.

# 1. Introduction

Skin cancer grows when normal cells are converted into tumor cells that generally grow from a precancerous lesion to a malignant tumor. The most common type of cancer is skin cancer, non-melanoma and melanoma are the two main categories of skin cancers. These cancer subtypes are mostly distinguished by where they develop in the skin layers. Melanoma is the type of skin cancer that records around only one percent of all types of skin cancers, but that is the dominant reason for death from skin cancer. According to the National Cancer Institute and the American Cancer Society, there would be around 19,18,030 cancer cases diagnosed, equating to around 5250 new cases per day, and skin cancer would cause 1,08,480 new cases and 11,990 deaths in the United States in 2022 [1]. Recent advancements in computer vision using machine learning approaches have resulted in significant advancements in computer-assisted diagnostic and detection systems for timely detection. Manual screening and visual inspection have long been used to diagnose and identify skin cancer disorders.
