+   - **ResNet**: This architecture is also called a deep residual network with numerous residual networks that serve as the design’s fundamental elements for constructing a complete continuous network. These remaining modules are stacked one on top of the other [15]. Deeper neural networks are constructed without the use of shortcuts by stacking new layers on top of each other. This architecture incorporates hundreds of residual layers which are used for training the network and improving performance. It is less computational than other networks that have been proposed. ResNet-X stands for a residual deep neural network with X layers, such as ResNet-152 refers to a Resnet that has 152 levels.

+   - **ResNeXt**: It is an evolution of the Inception Network in that it incorporates cardinality to utilize the concept of divide, transform, and merge in a strong yet simple manner. The number of transformations in a set is what determines its cardinality. The split, transform and merge block integrated VGG and GoogleNet by restricting resolution to 3*3 filters [16]. It also made use of residual learning to speed up network convergence on both the deep and wide scales. Adding more cardinality improves performance enormously, leading to a brand-new, better-performing model.

+   - **Deep reinforcement learning**: This network has been thoroughly trained from the base, starting with random behavior and progressing to a truth knowledge base gained through experience. It’s a mixture of reinforcement and deep learning that uses fewer computer data and resources [17]. The representative may train from its surroundings and apply what it has learned to any sequential choice task, such as image analysis. AlphaGo and AlphaGo Zero are two examples. Due to the application of far more principled algorithms, AlphaGo Zero takes far less computing than prior versions while still performing at a much higher level.

+   - **Advanced inception network**: All three versions of Inception i.e V3, V4, and ResNet are part of this network. They are enhanced versions of Googlenet, Inception-V1, and Inception-V2, respectively. Due to Inception-lowering V3’s processing costs, deep networks no longer have to sacrifice generality. Large filters were replaced with small and asymmetrical filters by employing 1 × 1 convolution as the bottleneck (1 × 7 and 1 × 5).

+   - **DenseNet**: DenseNet, like ResNet, is a neural network designed to address the vanishing gradient issue. While ResNet uses additive identity transformations to keep track of information, DenseNet uses cross-layer connections and feeds the information forward in a feed-forward manner. Previous layers feature maps as inputs into future ones, taking advantage of dense blocks [18].

+   - **Xception**: Deeply separable convolution is used in this extreme Inception design. The original inception block is expanded to control computational complexity, and separate spatial dimensions (e.g., 1 × 1, 5 × 5, 3 × 3) are substituted with 3 × 3 and 1 × 1 convolutions. The network’s computational efficiency improves due to the separation of the spatial and feature [14].

+   - **Squeeze and Excitation Network**: Hu et al. outlined in their paper, the Squeeze and Excitation Network (SE-Network). To aid with object recognition, they devised a new building element called a “channel block.” Before the convolution stage in any CNN design, this layer is required. In this paradigm, the two most important processes are squeeze and excitation. When compared to the Squeeze operation, the convolution kernel gathers local information but does not consider feature context. By generating feature map statistics, the network has evolved into a more powerful architecture, making it particularly useful for low bandwidth devices such as mobile phones [19].

+   - **Residual Attention Neural Network (RAN)**: Wang et al. develop this Network to enhance feature representation. Its purpose of adding attention to CNN was to develop an object-aware network. With the attention module, RAN is a feedforward CNN built from residual blocks. The attention module’s trunk and mask branches learn from the bottom up. The attention module integrates two independent learning algorithms that combine fast feedforward processing and top-down attention feedback [20].

+   - **Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)**: CBAM created by Woo et al., is attention based CNN that sequentially implies attention maps through utilizing feature map attention and spatial attention to discover the precise feature maps, as opposed to the Squeeze and Excitation Network, which still perceives feature-maps in classification task without taking the spatial information of the actual object into account [21].

+   - **MultiResUNet**: Ibtehaz and Rahman introduced an enhanced MultiResUNet that is suggested to detect tissue from biomedical images. The ResPath component improves communication between encoding and decoding blocks and reduces semantic inconsistencies, whereas the anti-aliased convolution and MultiRes modules were built to be sensitive to high-frequency semantics, such as tissue borders. During the training phase, a mixed loss function is employed to concentrate on irregular or discontinuous boundaries [22].

+   - **INet**: Khedkar et al. iNet, which was stimulated by an Inception, collects different features by adding the feature-maps of the prior layers. INet employs max-poolings with stride 1 i.e., two overlapped max polling, to extract the sharpest features. INet, which expands receptive fields by gradually growing the size of the kernel in convolutional layers rather than downsampling [23].
