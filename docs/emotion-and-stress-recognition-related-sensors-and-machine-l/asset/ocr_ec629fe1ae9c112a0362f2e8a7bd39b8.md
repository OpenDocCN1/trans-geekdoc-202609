**Abstract:** For future healthcare applications, which are increasingly moving towards out-of-hospital
or home-based caring models, the ability to remotely and continuously monitor patients' conditions
effectively are imperative. Others, emotional state is one of the conditions that could be of
to interest to caregivers. This paper presents a preliminary study to develop a wearable
device that is a low cost, single channel, dry contact, in-ear EEG suitable for non-intrusive monitoring. All aspects of the designs, engineering, and experimenting by applying machine learning for emotion
classification are covered. Based on the valence and arousal model, the device is able to
classify emotion with 71.70% (valence), 72.98% (arousal), and 53.72% (all
emotions). The results are comparable to those from the more conventional EEG headsets
at T7 and T8 scalp positions. These results, together with its carphone-like wearables, suggest its
pential usage especially for future healthcare applications, as home-based or tele-monitoring
systems as identified.

**Keywords:** EEG; in-ear EEG; emotion classification; emotion monitoring; elderly caring; outpatient
caring; machine learning



As societies around the world increasingly face with the issue of aging population, how to take
care of these elderly people becomes an important challenge. This is especially true for the
ffort-fortunate ones who live alone. In order to ensure their physical and mental well-being and provide
ememergency assistance, monitoring technology could potentially be part of the solution. Particularly,
wearable devices or smart sensors could be employed for effective and practical monitoring. Part of
the carer's concerns, emotional state is one of the factors that reflects mental states and can
greatly impact decision-making [1]. Emotion monitoring could therefore be used as another piece
for elderly and remote-care supporting systems.
