To classify in these systems, researchers have three data collection (i.e., measurement) strategies at their disposal [5]: (i) neurological/physiological measurement, which uses sensors to detect changes in the user's body; (ii) subjective self-reporting by questionnaires, diaries or interviews; and (iii) behavioral measurement is based on expert observations of the participant's behavior. While these approaches have their specific advantages and disadvantages, as Kim and Femaser [6] suggest, a physiological approach is considered to be more objective. In our literature, we found that a large boey of AC studies exists on classifying emotions from physiology (EEG) [8-9], heart rate (GSR) [10,11], and [12,13]. However, a higher validity can be achieved by combining more than one measurement strategy. For example, a viable approach, which is employed in this study, is to combine a physiological approach with self-reporting, the latter is used to verify the existence of the target.

Accurate classification of boredom is considered of particular importance because boredom affects multiple facets of life. In a published report by United States Air Force, unmanned pilots' reactions were longer when they felt bored [14]. Furthermore, boredom is connected to serious issues such as cardiovascular disease [15]. Additionally, it can have negative effects on learning [16-19]. If computing devices could accurately classify the occurrence of the user's boredom and administer a suitable questionnaire to compensate for it, they could be used to tackle the aforementioned boredom-related issues.

Previous studies have built classification models using different physical data and performed a joint analysis of both data shown in Table 1. To the best of our knowledge, no previous studies have used EEG and GSR data for boredom. In this study, we performed collecting EEG and GSR from 28 participants who also answered a questionnaire surveying their perceived level of boredom. Based on the collected data and questionnaire results, we ran an test of 19 machine learning algorithms and selected the best three candidate models with 100 iterations of 10-fold cross validation. The performance with a mean accuracy of 79.98% (min: 71.43%, max: 93.93%) was obtained using a Multilayer perceptron (MLP) model. Furthermore, we analyzed the features to investigate the correlation between EEG data, GSR, and boredom. In this study, there has three major contributions: (i) revealing a correlation between EEG, GSR, and boredom; (ii) conducting a reliable performance comparison between machine learning algorithms through repeated cross validations; and (iii) proposing a robust boredom classification model based on MLP.
