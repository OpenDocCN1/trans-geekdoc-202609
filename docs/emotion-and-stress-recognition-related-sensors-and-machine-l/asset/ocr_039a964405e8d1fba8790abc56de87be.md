Emotions can be recognized in various ways. The most well-known models for emotion are the 'discrete emotion model' proposed by Ekman [3] and the 'emotional model' proposed by Lang [4]. The discrete emotion model categorizes emotions into six basic emotion states: surprise, anger, disgust, happiness and sadness and fear. These emotions are universal, biologically experienced by all humans and accepted such in the research community. In contrast to the discrete emotional model, the dimensional model assumes that the emotions are a combination of several psychological dimensions. The most well-known dimensional model is the 'valence-arrousal dimensional model'. The valence represents a form of pleasure and ranges from negative to positive. However, the arousal increases the physiological and/or psychological level of being awake and from low to high [5].

Overall, researchers in the field have used two major approaches to recognize emotions. The first one consists of features-based approaches and the second one uses Deep Learning (DL). In features engineering, human emotion recognition involves several steps ranging from collecting sensor data to a final conclusion about the current emotion status. The steps thereby involved are the following: (1) preprocessing of the raw data from sensors for handling completeness, eliminating noise and redundancy, and (2) feature extraction which means extracting the main characteristics of/from the raw signals (3) dimensionality reduction to decrease the number of features to increase their quality and reduce the computational effort for the classification and (4) classification by machine-learning and reasoning techniques to recognize the effective emotion.

On the other hand, DL does not require the feature engineering/extraction step, due to the fact that it learns features and/or implicitly (within the training phase) [9]. Therefore, they have shown promising results while using a combination of different physiological signals for human emotion recognition [10,11].

Additionally, DL showed promising results in other research fields for different applications, such as identification of gas mixture [12], classification of tea specimens [13] and cardiac arrhythmia detection [14,15].

Generally, subject-independent emotion recognition is a challenging field due to the fact (a) physiological expressions of emotion depend on age, gender, culture and other social factors [16], and (b) it also depends on the environment in which a subject lives, (c) the subject-independent nature of human emotion recognition which means that the system is based on a group of subjects and tested on another group, (d) the lab-setting independent nature of emotion recognition is related to the fact that the classifier will be trained locally using sensors of a given lab-setting and after testing collecting different datasets that are based on different settings. The motivation for developing a generalized model that collecting data time for each subject is not a realistic task and is far from the practicality.
