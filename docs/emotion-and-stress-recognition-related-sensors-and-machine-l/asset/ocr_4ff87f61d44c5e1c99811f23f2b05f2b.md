A vital part of the analysis is the visualisation of different levels: First, on the individual level, the data from different sensors measuring the same physiological parameter at the same time provide a direct comparison between the two-time series of interest. This enables reaching on the sensors' measuring behaviour. Second, on the collective level, the consolidation of global metrics across all participants provides useful insights into the participants' individual behaviour (physical constitution, individual baseline of skin conductance, etc.).

These complementary visualizations enable a flexible method of interpretation. For instance, it allows starting the interpretation on the individual level on a particular pair of physiological parameters (e.g., HR, GSR) and the corresponding exploratory plot as shown in Figure 7b, then rolling-up the R² matrix together with the cross-correlation (Figure 7a) and comparing the result between participants. Furthermore, it allows checking whether particular pair of participants has a functional relationship and that relationship is stable among other participants using the MIC–R² individual matrix (Figure 11). The focus on a particular pair of participants and participant can be connected to the similarity measures, namely the Fréchet distance and the DTW distance (Figures 12 and 14). Another method of interpretation is to begin at the collective level using the MIC–R² cluster matrix of pairs of participants (Figure 10), then drilling-down on a specific parameter of interest using the MIC–R² individual matrix (Figure 11) and contextualizing this with the exploratory plots shown in Figure 5.

This kind of visualisation provides the central advantage regarding sensor benchmarking from a “big picture”, to serve as a baseline for visual analysis of the correlations between the measurements of one parameter by two different sensors (each row in the matrix) and the correlations between the different measurements for a single participant (column in the matrix). Furthermore, the matrix allows the simple assessment of each single cell to trace back particularities of each measurement to a test person, which makes it easier to see anomalies that may be caused by usage errors, a user's characteristics, sensor failures, or violations of the benchmark protocol.
