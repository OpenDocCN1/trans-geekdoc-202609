## 4.2 Emotion Recognition

A database with 28 typically developing children was used to analyze the performance of the proposed system for five emotions recognition [17]. This database contains appearance vectors that were computed on ROIs (see Figure 4), which correct placements were carefully verified by a trained expert. The effectiveness of PCA for five emotions classification was evaluated here adaptive transform under controlled head-pose and imaging conditions may be sufficient to capture features of expressions [9]. Figure 7a shows the average performance by PCA for different components. Note that non-significant difference (p < 0.05) for 60 components was achieved. Similarly, non-significant difference was obtained using PCA with 60 components plus other classifiers, such as LDA or SVM, as shown in Figure 7b. For instance, LDA using diagonal covariance matrices achieved Kappa values of 81.3% and 77.9%, respectively, and 78.5% for SVM. The resulting matrix has low cost and can be easily embedded in N-MARIA hardware for online emotion recognition during child-robot interaction. Moreover, PCA and LDA have been successfully used in other studies [16,17,20,23], achieving promising results. Then, we selected PCA with 60 components plus LDA on full covariance matrix as the best set for five emotions recognition

It is noting that PCA improved the performance of system for subset S06 (78.10% and ACC of 73.11% and Kappa of 65.09% for FNCA), as shown in Figure 8a,b. This improvement may be caused by PCA for feature extraction under head-pose and imaging conditions [9]. Also, notice that for subset S22 the lowest performance was achieved by applying PCA (ACC of 49.27% and Kappa of 36.75%) and FNCA (ACC of 49.98% and Kappa of 37.78). However, higher values were achieved for a total of 14 children by applying PCA or FNCA, being the highest ACC 99.78 for subset S26. Labelling feature vector of emotions is a challenge, when labels are applied over periods of time, some delays between perception and manual label assignment may exist. Similarly, a visual stimulus may produce different facial emotions, thus the manual labeled procedure is subjective. Then, an automatic method for feature labeling may be suitable to obtain reliable labels and a classification model for LDA.

On the other hand, Figure 8 shows that four emotions (disgust, fear, happiness, and surprise) were successfully recognized by both PCA and FNCA, achieving TPR > 85%, whereas sadness was classified with less sensitivity (TPR of 74.69% and 76.15% for FNCA and PCA, respectively) by the proposed system. As a highlight, low values of FPR (≤5.40%) were obtained to recognize all studied emotions. In general, the results achieved that PCA and FNCA are interchangeable to obtain the features that increase the emotion discrimination by discovering those regions that are informative in terms of expressions [9]. Similar to other studies [52], the sadness emotion was less recognized (ACC < 77%) than the other emotions, as each child expressed sadness in a different manner, producing a large intra-class variability for sadness.

Many researchers [1] have conducted studies to infer facial emotions over visual images using only three of the basic emotions, specifically happiness, surprise and sadness, as these are easier to recognize for humans, and have been successfully used to generate expressions across portraits. Similarly, other studies using visual images report the recognition rates for both fear or disgust can be very low, in a range from 40 to 50%.
