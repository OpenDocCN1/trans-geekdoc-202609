### Appendix A.1. Time-Domain

A total of five measures were considered in the time-domain, directly or indirectly extracted from the QRS analysis:

- The unprocessed signal [53,55] and the R Amplitude (RA) (amplitude of the different waves in each window). The SSSPs and the baseline parameters were calculated for these measurements.
- The interval between successive Rs (RR) (time between successive waves) [35,49,80]. Apart from the SSSP and the baseline, some special features have been extracted from the measurement:
- Deep Breathing (DBD), calculated as the difference between the maximum RR and minimum RR in the window [81,82].
- Ratios maximum RR vs. minimum RR, that is, RRmax/RRmin and RRmin/RRmax under study.
- Log variation in the RR under study, calculated as the quotient between the DBD and the mean value of the RR. This measure is related to the parasympathetic nervous system during spontaneous ventilation [84].
- Modal Value (MV), defined as the most frequent value in the RR intervals [40].
- Load Index (LI), based on the ratio between the number of occurrences of Modal Value and DBD [40].
- NN50, determined as the number of successive RR pairs differing by more than 50 ms [40,42,48].
- pNN50, obtained dividing NN50 by the total number of RR intervals [40,42,48].
- Approximate Entropy (AE), originally proposed in [85], and applied to physiological data [39,42].
- Root Mean Square of Differences (RMSD), determined by calculating the square root of the mean squared difference between consecutive intervals [34,48,49]. The RMSD is used to estimate the high-frequency beat-to-beat variations that represent regulatory activity [48].
- Two parameters of Detrended Fluctuation Analysis (DFA) [85]. These two parameters (DFA1 and DFA2) were used to quantify the fractal-like properties of the heart period time series [39].
- Heart Rate (HR), measured as the number of pulses per minute (bpm). It is calculated as the inverse of the RR interval. This parameter is highly important, as it is related to physical exercise, sleep, food intake, and increase or decrease on the speed of the body [34].
- Heart Variability (HRV), which has been widely used to extract information about the status of the autonomic system [23,88]. The work provides a review of this parameter [23,35,36,39,41,42,45,48,50,51]. We decided to obtain the HRV as proposed in [26], where HRV is sampled at 256 Hz. Once obtained, it is possible to extract various features using the SSSFs and the baseline parameter. Many other measures were found in the literature such as SDNN, SDANN [34], however we did not use these measurements because they require at least 5 minutes to be considered, since they are often calculated over 24-h period.

### Appendix A.2. Frequency-Domain

The main group of measurements is evaluated in the frequency domain through the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT). The main measurements taken in this part were:

- Power Spectral Density (PSD) of the HRV signal is obtained using Welch's method [23,38,42]. From this parameter, several frequency bands were considered: Very-Low frequency (PSD-VLF), from 0.0033–0.08 Hz; Low Frequency (PSD-LF) from 0.05–0.08 Hz; Mid Frequency (PSD-MF) from 0.08–0.15 Hz; High Frequency (PSD-HF) from 0.15–0.5 Hz.
- Power Bands. 
- These values were established taking into account several papers such as [23,26,34,36–40]. The parameters from these spectral measurements were the SSSFs, the baseline parameter, and a set of specific parameters related to ratios between the average power for the different frequencies: HF/LF, LF/HF, MF/HF, (LF+MF)/HF, HF/TF, being TF the total power in all frequencies [23,43,44,46,47,49].

### Appendix A.3. Mixed Domain

There were also two parameters taken from relationships between time and frequency parameters, defined as Coefficients of Variance (CCV). The CCVs considered were the CCV-LF and the CCV-HF [39], and they were calculated as the square root of LF or HF power divided by the average HR.

## Appendix B. Features from the TEB Signal

The measurements used to extract the most relevant information from TEB signal follow a structure similar to the one described in the ECG signal, being divided into the time domain, frequency domain, and mixed domain features.

Figure A2 shows the features extracted from the TEB signal. There are 60 time domain features, 89 frequency domain features, and 2 mixed domain features. Therefore, the total number of features calculated to characterize the body signal is 151.

### Appendix B.1. Time Domain

- TEB-Signal: The 13 SSSPs and the baseline parameter aforementioned are calculated from the TEB-Original signal. Apart from these parameters, the area was also calculated, using an approximated segment-integral of the measurements via a trapezoidal method with unit spacing.
- TEB-RF: the original signal is low-passed (LF block) with a cutoff frequency of 3 Hz, using an FIR filter with order $N_1=100$. The 13 SSSPs and the baseline parameter are calculated.
- TEB-RF: Additionally, another original signal is obtained from TEB-LF. This allows the use of Interpolated Impulse Response (IRF) filters [90]. The output of this anti-aliasing filter is applied to a band-pass filter with cutoff frequencies of 0.1 Hz and 0.5 Hz with a stretch factor of $SF=10$ and an order $N_2=5 \times f_{TEB} - N_1 = 400$, (where $f_{TEB}=100$ Hz).
