Respondents have been found to report minor or positive events in response to questions designed to elicit negative and undesirable events [9].

Psychological methods alone are not enough to assess stress-related conditions [10]. Stress can be quantified objectively by bio-markers like electroencephalography (EEG), galvanic skin response, and electrodermal activity [11]. Recently, wearable systems were developed that can record electro-physiological signals (such as EEG and heart rate variability) to detect stress [12]. EEG is one of the most common source of information for studying brain function [13–17]. The oscillations generated by the variation of electric potential in the brain are recorded using low resistance electrodes placed on the scalp [18]. It is a widely used noninvasive method for its excellent temporal resolution, ease of use, and low cost. EEG signals are categorized by their frequency bands including delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma. Each frequency band can be used as a discriminating feature for different brain states [19]. There are methods reported to quantify human stress in response to induced stressors using EEG recordings. In comparison, the classification of long-term or chronic stress EEG has not been widely used.

# Our Contributions

In this study, the problem of long-term human stress is recognized by using labels and expert evaluation, which has not been explored before. We hypothesized that wearable sensors (such as EEG for recording activity using EEG electrodes) could be used for identifying chronic stress, without inducing a stimulus. Thus, our experiments involving a psychology expert for labeling stress and chronic stress is beneficial for such classification. It is important to note that we did not use any stimulus in our study to detect stress that can be administered for detecting stress in daily life routine. Two groups of participants were included the stress group and the control group. A total of forty five features were extracted from EEG signals in frequency domain to classify these two groups. Discriminating features were selected using a statistical significance test. Five machine learning classifiers including SVM, Naive Bayes (NB), KNN, logistic regression (LR), and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) were used to classify human stress using the selected features. Due to limitations of the data size and nature of the signals, deep-learning-based systems were not suitable for the task at hand. Therefore, we concentrated on machine learning classifiers that are more suitable for the task that we solve. The summary of our findings in this study is as follows:

+   1. We used EEG signals from 33 participants in closed eye conditions using a five-channel EEG headset for long term stress classification (no stimulus used to induce stress) and found that among different feature, three frequency domain features were statistically significant in stress and control groups.
2. To the best of our knowledge, this is that the stress level of participants was labeled by a psychology expert in an EEG-based study. We showed its feasibility with a validated set of experiments.
3. The conventional machine learning classifiers suite well to long-term human stress classification and give better performance using psychological expert labeling.
