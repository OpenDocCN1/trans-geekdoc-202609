The experiments have been replicated on images of the whole face, to study the contribution of the mouth; the resulting confusion matrix is shown in Figure 9. Results can be summarised in the following points:

- The network that provided the best performance was the same as the one for mouth-based ER, i.e., InceptionResNetV2.
- Anger was the most difficult emotion to classify.
- The performance improvement was only 5%, with an accuracy rate of 84.4%, proving the consistent contribution of the mouth for ER.![](img/601340ca744b738277eef45ff127492a_436_0.png)

In this work, we presented the consolidated results for the approach of mouth-based emotion recognition, gotten through the comparative analysis of four different CNNs using transfer learning. As described in Section 5, we obtained InceptionResNetV2 an accuracy of 79.5% on the validation set. With respect to the full face, results show a loss of only 5%, which in our opinion is offset by the advantages of our method. Moreover, focusing on a smaller area requires less computational capabilities. Straightforward applications of our approach for healthcare management, and automated supervision of clinical patients, the system can support patients by using ER when human assistance is not available, e.g., during the night or wards, for advanced detection of initial discomfort or state, and raising a signal letting the sanitary staff be informed promptly, avoiding the cases to nurses. Our approach could easily extended to the scenario previously described, upon availability of proper datasets. An additional application could be the early recognition of depressive states or people with autism spectrum. Our system could set up a channel for information.
