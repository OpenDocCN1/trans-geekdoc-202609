When using a z-score normalization, we observed that the accuracy for intra-subject models was generally better, except in the DREAMER database, which showed very poor results in all cases. This was despite considering fewer training data. In general, the accuracy of inter-subject models that use z-score standardization remained close to 50% in most cases, which is consistent with the data topology shown in Figure 1, in which samples are grouped by subject by their emotional label. On the contrary, the intra-subject models showed reasonable accuracies that are consistent with results reported in previous works [18,33,34], ranging from 0.54 to 0.66 in the DEAP and MAHNOB databases.

When using proposed data transformation, a significant performance improvement was achieved with regard to the z-score normalization. The results are clearly outperformed in all cases. On occasions, the inter-subject model on the normalized data performed better than the corresponding intra-subject model classification accuracy, close to that in other repositories. After a clear performance advantage, results reported in Table 3 show a comparable performance between using an intra-subject and the suggested data transformation. However, the proposed approach can be used for previously unseen subjects without adding additional data available for that specific individual, and offers a performance which better than that by using a typical z-score normalization.

The behavior reported is consistent with the plots in Figures 1 and 3. When the subject-based data transformation was not applied, the intrinsic subject-dependent component in the signal dominated the data topology, the intra-subject model performed reasonably well, this component equally affected all samples and part of the subject-related component, but it did not cancel it completely. The remnant component can easily be observed in Figure 3, in the form of small clusters of samples that belong to the same individual.
