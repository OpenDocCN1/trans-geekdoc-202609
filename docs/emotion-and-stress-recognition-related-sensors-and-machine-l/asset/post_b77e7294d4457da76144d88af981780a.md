In Ref. [62], the authors used a parrot-inspired robot (KiliRo) to interact with ASD children by simulating a set of autonomous behaviors. They tested the robot for five consecutive days in a clinical facility. Children’s expressions while interacting with the robot were analyzed by the Oxford emotions recognition API, allowing them to make an automated facial detection, emotion recognition and classification system.

Some works, as in Ref. [69], show the use of deep learning to detect the child face and the visual attention on a robot during CRI therapy. They used the robot NAO from Softbank Robotics, with two low-resolution cameras that were used to take pictures and record videos. They also used the detection and tracking system-built in NAO for the clinical experiments. In experiment they initiated some robot movements. The total of 6 children participated in the experiment, which had 14 encounters over a month, and each experiment started 7 days after the preliminary encounter, in order to avoid the novelty effect in the results. Different learning techniques and classifiers were used, and they could reach an accurate children attention rate of 59.2.

Deep-learning-based approaches have been promising for emotion recognition, determining features and classifiers still being studied for real-time embedded systems because of their computational complexity and high degree of accuracy [70], although for these systems the methods for feature extraction and classification are designed by the programmer and not to increase performance [71]. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that conventional approaches require relatively lower computing power and more deep learning-based approaches [101]. Gabor factors are very popular for facial expression classification and face recognition, due to high dimensionality [72,73], but the computational complexity and memory requirement make them less suitable for real-time implementation.

Our system is composed of low-cost hardware and methods of low-computational cost for visual and thermal image processing, and recognizes emotions, 85.75% of accuracy. For our system, we proposed a method based on probability error to accurately locate subject-specific landmarks, taking into account the trained expert. As a highlight, our proposal can find frame-to-frame the best located facial ROI using the Viola-Jones algorithm, and adjust the location of its surrounding facial ROI. Another novelty, our proposal based on probability error showed robustness and accuracy to locate facial ROIs on thermal images, which were collected through a database of five facial emotions. We extended an extension to infer unknown emotion generated while the children interacted with the social robot, using our recognition system based on PCA and LDA, thus, achieving that results with the written reports of children.

As limitation, our system is not able to track head movements, thus as done by Ref. [74], we can make robust of our proposal for facial landmarks in uncontrolled scenarios, such as mobile applications for child and social robot interaction. Generally, facial emotion datasets with basic emotions contain only adult participants, but there are very few databases collected on developing children (aged between 7 and 11 years) through infrared camera, containing the basically. Then, it is a challenge to use high quality of examples during the training stage of a recognition system to infer emotions of children with age range from 7 to 11 years while they interact with a robot for. In addition, more testers with a higher number of volunteers must be performed, including ASD children.

# 6. Conclusions

A low-computational system for emotion recognition in an unobtrusive way was proposed in this study, which is composed of low-cost cameras, making possible its extension into research developing countries. As a first, our proposal was tested on visual and thermal images of children interacting with the mobile robot N-MARIA, achieving promising results (85.77% accuracy) to locate face landmarks, as well to recognize (or) five emotions. All children had a hopeful interaction with the robot, which demonstrated our system is useful to stimulate emotions in children, and to trigger a profitable interaction with them. In future work, this proposal will be integrated in N-MARIA, aiming to know the children's emotion and making decisions based on emotions. Additionally, other methods will be explored for tracking, in order to reduce the influence of head-pose during emotion recognition. unsupervised methods for automatic label assignments and classifier learning will be evaluated in database to obtain a robust recognition system for processing patterns of high uncertainty, such as facial emotions and expressions.
