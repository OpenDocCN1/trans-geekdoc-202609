Several studies have been addressed to recognize some of the six accepted emotions by psychological theory: surprise, fear, anger, happiness and sadness [10,53–59]. In fact, facial motion and the tone of the speech have shown in recognition systems their relevant role to infer these aforementioned emotions, achieving accuracy (ACC) from 80% to [56–58] and from 70% to 75% [31,56,59]. However, it is necessary to measure, by optical flow, the movements of facial muscles through markers located on the face [53,57,59]. However, this non-contact technique may not be comfortable to study or infer emotions of children with ASD, as these children present high sensitivity.

Following this approach of non-contact techniques, there are also APIs (Application Program Interface) such as the “Emotion API” from Microsoft or the Oxford API [61,62]. Some works, utilize other image processing techniques, such as finding the region of the therm within a temperature range to detect the face [18]. Another approach is to keep the face in a fixed position using a support for chin or headset device [41,63]. Furthermore, in Ref. [64] the authors show a solution using neural network and supervised shapes classification methods applied to facial thermogram.

Emotion recognition systems based on IRTI have, in fact, shown promising results. Table 5 shows some studies that use ROI replacement and further analysis, although using other techniques in comparison with our proposal. Unfortunately, the results are presented as somewhat, and it is not possible to make a fair comparison among them, due to the different units used in the studies. In Ref.[18] the authors proposed techniques for selecting ROI and classifying emotions using a FLIR A310 camera. To detect the face, the thermogram's temperatures between 32 °C to 36 °C were used to define the face. The ROIs were further calculated by proportions based on the head's width. All other points were considered background. For emotion classification, the system was calibrated using a baseline (neutral state) that contrasted the induced emotion by fuzzy algorithm, and thus calibrate the induced image. Using the baseline, the temperature is inferred by IF-THEN rules to classify the thermal images for the induced emotions: joy, disgust, anger, fear and sadness. Next, a top-down hierarchical classifier was used to analyze the emotion classification, reaching 89.9% of success rate.
