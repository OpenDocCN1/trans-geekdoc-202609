With the recent development of mobile sensors for ECG recording, HRV analysis and stress studies have been actively carried out. However, due to the inherent limitations of HRV analysis, which requires sufficient data to observe the variability, the longer the time window of the ECG record for an HRV analysis the more accurate statistical characteristics be. In other, it is difficult to perform an HRV analysis with short-term ECG measurements. Some research has demonstrated the limited window required for an HRV analysis. Carr et al. [7] recommended at least a 5 min ECG series be used. To do so, computational algorithms such as the Pan-Tompkins algorithm [8] can be used. These algorithms require various preprocessing steps, such as filtering and QRS detection. Similarly, the classical HRV approach requires preprocessing steps with a limited window.

To recognize stress states, previous studies [9–11] have reported classical HRV analysis using machine learning algorithms, such as the support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), adaptive boosting (AB), and logistic regression (LR) methods, along with the ECG and other physiological signals. Other physiological signals include skin conductance (SC) [9,11], respiration [9,11], and skin temperature [11].

Table 1 shows a summary of previous studies which used conventional machine learning algorithms, as well as DNNs. The column "Window length" refers to the duration of the ECG measurement used to extract the HRV parameters. The column "Performance" indicates to the accuracy of the classifier shown in Table 1. Most studies used more than one classifier, but only those that gave the best results. The study [11] used different stressors to the other studies [10], including the stress color word test (SCWT), mental arithmetic (MA), and counting tasks. They simulated an experiment where ECG was recorded on two different days. Castella et al. [10] designed an experiment where the participants were undergoing a university examination, and the next day was after a vacation. These studies (which were performed in a laboratory-controlled environment) showed an accuracy of 85%, outside of the accuracy reached only 80% [10]. These results might indicate the limitations of the stressors used in the laboratory environment and of the conventional machine learning methods. A conventional machine learning method based on HRV features involves not only the preprocessing of the ECG signals, but also feature selection among the HRV parameters.
