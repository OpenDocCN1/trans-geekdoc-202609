# 2.5. Music Stimuli

As mentioned above, in this experiment the key in provoking emotions is music. For this reason, eight music pieces have been specifically composed by a professional musician for this experiment. These compositions reflect some musical styles that older people heard when they were young (more than 30 years ago). This was the first time the participants heard each of these original pieces. All eight pieces are characterized by a same melody and eighty variations according to musical styles. The duration of each variation was 60 s. Table 1 shows the eight variations of four music genres, with each genre two musical styles. They were “rock/jazz” (twist and swing), “Cuban” (bolero and habanera), “Spanish folklore” (pasable and Murcian jota) and “flamenco” (fandango and pentera), respectively.

The musical genres used in this experiment and their repercussion in the region of Murcia are briefly described below. First, flamenco, has been widely disseminated on the radio and only through simple songs, is a deeply rooted genre in Spain. The most cheerful, folkloric and festive flamenco styles were adopted, such as the Fandango and the Pentera, relegating everything to the “jondo” singing to a secondary position [46]. Secondly, Spanish Folklore, mainly linked to moments of celebration, is characterized by its joyful and festive character. Also anchored in popularity, its simplicity and the repetition of melodic-rhythmic elements give off energy and vitality. It is linked to dancing as a couple, allowing one to enjoy the social atmosphere and to relate to the music and the corte.

On its side, Cuban evokes silent listening without movement or slow dancing in couples with direct physical contact. This musical genre has also been adopted by classical music and has been expanded mainly by the cinema and the radio to its sentimental character. Finally, jazz and rock’n roll imply a new way of listening and relating to music. The orchestration of this music adds instruments and sounds unknown in their culture was new to the participants. This music relies on simple and repetitive structures, as well as on melodic improvisation through instrumental or vocal solos. The design of this music is also new, in pairs but without physical contact, and with very rhythmic movements that sometimes are perceived as transgression.

# 2.6. Experimental Design

An appropriate experimental design is fundamental to achieving results. The E-Prime software has been chosen to create the basic design of the experiment. The software is the most widely used in the field of psychology for setting up experimental trials. In fact, E-Prime is a very robust software tool for our proper study, since it allows us to randomize and synchronize musical pieces that are played to the participants. Furthermore, it makes it possible to add the SAM questionnaire and to control/record different parameters that we will exploit the EDA signals acquired during music performance.
