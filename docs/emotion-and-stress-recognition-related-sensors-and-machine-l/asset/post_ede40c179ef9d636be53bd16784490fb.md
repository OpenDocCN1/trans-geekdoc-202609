
(iii) Characterising the results for diverse classifiers, sensor modalities and feature set combinations for ER accuracy and F1-score; (iv) Exploration of an extended feature set for each modality and (v) Systematic analysis of multimodal classification in DF and FF approaches. The study revealed that FF is the most competitive technique in terms of classification accuracy and computational complexity.

Moving away from the affective computing research that has mostly used two-dimensional (2D) displays to elicit emotional states, [10] adopted an immersive virtual reality (VR) approach. This allowed the researchers to simulate environments in closed laboratory conditions with high levels of presence and interactivity. We presented a systematic view of the emotion recognition research undertaken with physiological and behavioural measures using head-mounted displays. The devices highlighted the evolution of the field, gave a clear perspective of the use of aggregated analysis and revealed the current issues and guidelines for future research works.

Focusing on affective computing, which is an artificial intelligence area of study that recognizes, interprets, processes and simulates human affect, some of the pertinent scientific literature on affecting computing from 2015 to 2020 was presented in [17]. The paper presented trends and compared algorithms in new implementations from a computer science perspective. The survey provided an overview of datasets, emotion elicitation methods, feature extraction and selection, classification algorithms and performance evaluations.

## Facial Expression Recognition

Building upon deep learning techniques, facial expression recognition (FER) was addressed in [18]. The authors tackled the challenging issues of: (i) diversity of factors, which are unrelated to facial expressions (ii) the lack of training data for FER and (iii) the intrinsic analysis of existing facial datasets. The deep transfer learning to FER was compensated by a loss function weighted-cluster loss used during training.

In [19], the authors revisited the analysis of pain-related facial expressions by proposing an end-to-end approach based on attention networks for the analysis and recognition of pain-related facial expressions. The method proposed by the authors combined both spatial and temporal aspects of facial expressions through a weighted aggregation of attention-based neural networks that use sequences of Motion Images (MHIs) and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) was used to achieve pain recognition.

Building around human-computer interaction (HCI) setting, [21] addressed the challenging issue of dialogue-based HCI relevant emotional and cognitive loads by using a multimodal dataset for affective computing research. The dataset used an experimental mobile and interactive sensor design that was implemented based on a generic framework. The work consisted of six sequential experiments including Interest, Overload, Normal, Easy, Understand, and Frustration.

Facial-landmark detection was recognized in [21] in a multistage architecture. The first stage was to obtain pixel-level information. The second stage was concerned with integrating obtained information with spatial relationships between each key point in global-context information. The paper considered a pipeline architecture consisting of two main components: (i) a deep network for local-context subset used to generate detection heatmaps via fully convolutional Datasets with additional kernel convolutional filters and (ii) a dilated skip convolution subnet using a combination of dilated convolutions and skip-connections networks used to refine the local appearance heatmaps.

Building around the Child-Robot Interaction (CRI), [22] proposed a system for emotion recognition in children by recognizing faces using both visual (RGB–red, green and blue) and Infrared Thermal Imaging (IRTI) cameras. Building upon the Viola-Jones algorithm on colours to detect facial regions of interest (ROIs), the proposed paper as a new contribution the computation of the error probability for each ROI over thermal images, using a reference frame manually marked by a trained expert, in order to choose that ROI better-placed according to the expert criteria. The results: (i) show that the proposed approach for ROI locations track facial landmarks with lower errors than respect to the traditional Viola-Jones algorithm and (ii) that the proposed system integrated to a social robot to infant emotions during a child-robot interaction.

A comparison of machine-learning algorithms applied to the recognition of emotion and intensities was proposed in [23] as a solution to the lack of encoding the intensity of observed facial emotion and multifarious in existing emotion recognition systems. The work compared several algorithms, (i) Gabor filters, a Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG), and Local Binary Pattern (LBP) for feature extraction and (ii) Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and Nearest Neighbour Algorithm (KNN) for classification. The experiment suggested that the category could be studied in real-time behavioural recognition.

A transfer learning approach was adopted for mouth-based emotion recognition in [24]. The study was predicated on the fact that there were only a few datasets available in practice and some included emotional expressions by actors, instead of adopting real-world categorisation. Even when the face was only visible from an unfavourable perspective, the transfer learning approach allowed the authors to use feature training. This minimized the effort of training a network from scratch and resulted in an dynamic emotion recognition when taking into account not only new scenarios but also modified situations to the initial training phase. As presented in the paper, the transfer learning and the underlying method provided the relevance of emotion detection in the complex process of emotion recognition.

The authors in [25] proposed a method to emotion recognition in the aviation domain with the goal of filling space of pilot emotions and their reactions during flight procedures such as take-off, climbing, descending, descent, flight approach and landing. Building around a sensing architecture and a set of simulated experiments, the study showed that it was possible to recognize emotions from different pilots in various environments.

As we alluded in introduction, assistive technology is a research field with a number of challenges. Some of these are present in this Special Issue, which we will foster more research. Other fields were not covered, leaving room for new ideas to be discovered in this field.
