# 2. Related Work

Hemispheric specialization is a major concern in neuro-physiological research. Generally, a healthy brain at rest has a balanced level of activity in both hemispheres of brain [20]. The left hemisphere is associated with the processing of positive emotions, while the right hemisphere is associated with the processing of negative emotions [21]. The extent of asymmetry has been suggested under various conditions of chronic stress [22]. Frontal asymmetry is highly related to post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) [23]. The results in [24], have shown that major depression (MDD) group is significantly right lateralized to controls, and both MDD and PTSD displayed more-than-frontal activity.

Recently, the feasibility of using EEG in multilevel mental stress has been demonstrated [19], where alpha rhythm at the pre-frontal cortex was suggested as a bio-marker. A machine learning framework using EEG was proposed in [25], where stress was induced by the Mental imaging stress task (MIST), SVM, NB, and LR classifiers were used to classify the stress level of participants. The EEG of participants in resting-state was recorded under negative, positive, and neutral stimulus using soundtracks from the affective subjective (IADS-2) dataset [26]. Stress detection by frontal alpha asymmetry was performed using the DEAP dataset, and classification was performed using SVM, KNN, and fuzzy KNN [27]. In [28], 11 participants were analyzed for the identification of long-term stress [10], including several mothers of children with mental disability (stress group) and four mothers of healthy children (control group).

A variant of the trior social stress task (TST) was used to assess stress in 49 participants [29]. The samples of salivary control and resting state EEG based alpha asymmetry were assessed before after performing TST. The frontal and parietal alpha symmetry was used to classify depression in elderly people [30]. The correlation between frontal and parietal alpha asymmetry, the geriatric scale, and the mental state examination were revealed. A high activity at the frontal and occipital lobes was observed on the visual input of negative images [31]. The frontal activity was shown to decrease due to a stressful arithmetic task [32]. In [33], low beta waves predicted by closed eye condition were found to be a strong predictor of stress, where stress score was predicted by multiple linear regression. The pre-frontal gamma power i, the ratio of gamma band and slow brain rhythms, was proposed as a bio marker for identification of stress [34,35].

The studies presented here can be grouped as either short-term or long-term stress. Short-term stress is measured using a stress inducing task, while long-term stress is measured without performing task. Different techniques have been applied to measure stress, but most of these require human intervention. Among different physiological measures, EEG has the potential to be used as a measure of stress in daily life. This is due to the fact that EEG headsets are becoming commercially available for observing brain activity in an easy and cost effective manner. The proposed study uses EEG signals with a commercially available EEG headset to identify long-term stress without relying on stress-inducing tasks.

# 3. Methodology

We devised a supervised machine learning model for the classification of human stress (Figure 1). A total of 33 volunteers participated in this study. The resting state EEG data for each participant were acquired using an EmotionIV Insight headset (https://www.emotiv.com/insight/) in a closed eye condition for three months. After EEG signal recording, participants were asked to fill in the PSS-10 questionnaire followed by an interview with the psychology expert. The average time for the interview was 25 min. Based on the PSS scores and interview, the psychology expert grouped each participant either the stress or the control group.
