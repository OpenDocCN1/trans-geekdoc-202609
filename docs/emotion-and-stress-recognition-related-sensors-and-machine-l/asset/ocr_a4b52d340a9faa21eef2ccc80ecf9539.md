As the deep neural network (DNN) approach has recently demonstrated outperforming pattern recognition accuracy [5], some [12,13,16–21] have utilized DNNs for biomedical engineering applications, including heart arrhythmia classification, medical image classification and enhancement, stress detection, and other medical diagnoses. U-net [21], which consists of a convolutional neural network (CNN), forms an autoencoder skip-connection between the encoder and decoder. This architecture could be used in medical imaging applications, including augmentation, classification, and detection. Hann et al. [16] achieved a performance in arrhythmia detection using deep convolutional neural network comparable to that of cardiologists.

Huang et al. [12] proposed the DeepCNN method, which detected stress using an ultra-short (10 s) ECG. They used raw ECG signals for the inputs of the DNN without extracting the HRV parameters. A model was configured with one CNN and two long short-term memory (LSTM) [22] models. They suggested selecting the hyperparameter values for the convolution filter and pooling window width, respectively of 0.6 and 0.8 s. Both are capable of covering the QRS-complex of HRV and pooling window width. They designed an experiment for inducing mental and physical stresses there were 20 and 30 participants, separated into two. In the two cases, the method [12] reached 87.3% and 73.96% accuracy. Saed et al. [13] suggested a multitask convolutional neural network (MT-CNN). Raw physiological signals with the heart rate (HR) derived from an ECG and SC were fed to the MT-CNN to detect stress. There was no mention of how long the duration of the measurement was, but 300 samples of ECG signals were reported. They achieved 0.918 for the area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC).

These studies still issues remain unresolved. One of these is the complexity of the classification steps. Steps are involved using conventional machine learning algorithms, Some of these used in conventional methods: Preprocessing, feature selection, classifier training, and classification. Preprocessing, R-peak extraction, is required to extract the proper R-peak and calculate the HRV. Most of these studies used other physiological signals (e.g., respiration, SC, and ST) as well as an ECG, and need to select proper features from both physiological signals and the HRV parameters. These processes make it difficult to apply in a practical environment, from the perspective of real-time classification. Additionally, a person must measure for at least 1 min to detect a stress event, even though it is a short-term HRV analysis.

In this paper, we suggest a DNN model to detect stress with a raw ECG signal, which recognizes the limitations mentioned above. An end-to-end method using a raw ECG signal does not require preprocessing (i., filtering and R-peak extraction). This method does not require feature selection. The contribution of ours is a method for training the DNN with a pretrained model. The DNN requires a large amount of data to train the model, but it is difficult to acquire a large set of physiological signals. We trained the proposed model based on a pretrained model, which learned a large amount of data [14]. We evaluated the performance of the proposed model by calculating evaluation metrics (e.g., accuracy and area under the curve) which are widely used in the evaluation of DNN models. We also assessed the proposed model by comparing it with conventional machine learning methods [9–11] which used only the ECG to detect stress, and with other DNN methods
