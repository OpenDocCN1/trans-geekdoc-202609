Overall four emotion classification accuracies were approximately 53.72% for in-ear EEG and 58.12% for T7 T8 EEG. Valence classification accuracies were 71.07% and 69.85% for in-ear and T7 T8 EEG, respectively. Arousal classification accuracies were 72.89% and 78.7% for in-ear and T7 T8 EEG, respectively. These comparable accuracies indicate that in-ear EEG has potential for emotion classification as T7 and T8 electrodes.

From the main results, in-ear EEG was verified to be highly correlated to the nearby T7 and T8 scalp signals (correlation between T8 and in-EEG was approximately 0.853). This was expected as the 10-20 system scalp positions of T7 and T8 are just above left and right ears, respectively. They are in close proximity to ear channels. The results also correspond to the finding in previous work [17,37].

DEAP data analysis shows that using single electrode at T7 or T8 could achieve valence and arousal accuracies above 70 percent. This is comparable to classification accuracies obtained from multiple electrodes.

The results show in-ear EEG accuracy was comparable to T7 and T8 (71.07% for valence and 69.85%, 72.89% and 78.7% for arousal). The emotion and arousal accuracies of in-ear EEG were slightly lower than those of T7 and T8 (53.72% and 58.12%).

Furthermore, the differences in valence and arousal accuracies between in-ear EEG, T7, and T8 are not statistically significant (p-values = 0.74, 0.99, and 0.65, respectively). From the above findings, in-ear EEG could be highly correlated to T7 and T8. Their classification results are also compatible.

In terms of wearability, in-ear EEG could be set up within five minutes and put on by users. During experiments most subjects did not complain of being uncomfortable and it is unaffected by sweat, which makes it suitable for long term monitoring. The additional benefits of in-ear EEG are also its compatibility and familiarity to users. Earphones have been used for many years and wearing earpiece is considered normal, so in-ear EEG could allow the user's acceptance much easier than scalp electrodes. Another benefit of in-ear EEG is the signal has less artifacts from movement. With in-ear EEG that fits in the ear canal, contacts between electrode and skin could easily loosen. Earbud redesign is needed to fill a gap in the middle to let sound through.

Despite its potential, the in-ear EEG monitoring device would need to be further developed to be more practical. An additional feature required is wireless connectivity, possibly via Bluetooth. This would make it more convenient to use without cumbersome wires [31].

## 6. Conclusions

An in-ear EEG device was developed. Earphone was used as the in-ear EEG main body. Silver-adaptive fabric was used as an in-ear EEG electrode. The in-ear EEG signals were verified to be close to T7 and T8 on MMN ERP responses, with a correlation of approximately 0.8530. The emotion classification results were approximately 71.07% for valence, 72.89% for arousal, and 53.72% for four emotions, compared to those of the DEAP classification results using T7 and T8, which were 69.85% for valence, and 78.7% for arousal, with the accuracy for classifying four simplified emotions were about 58.12%. Classification accuracies between in-ear EEG, T7 and T8 electrodes, were not statistically significant. These results together with its earphone-like wearable design, suggests its potential for healthcare applications, such as home-based or tele-monitoring systems.
