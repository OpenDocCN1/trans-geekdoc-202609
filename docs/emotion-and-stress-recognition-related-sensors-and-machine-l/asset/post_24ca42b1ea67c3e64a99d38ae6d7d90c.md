**Abstract:** Many previous studies have identified that physiological responses of a driver are significantly associated with driving stress. However, research is limited to identifying the effects of traffic conditions (low vs. high traffic) and road types (highway vs. city). The objective of this study is to quantify the relationship between driving stress and traffic conditions and road types. In this study, electrodermal activity (EDA) signals of a male driver were collected in real road driving for 60 min a day for 21 days. To classify the levels of driving stress (low vs. high), two separate models were developed by incorporating the statistical features of the EDA signals, for traffic conditions and the other road types. Both models were based on the application of EDA features with the logistic regression analysis. City driving turned out to be more stressful than highway driving. Traffic conditions, defined as traffic also significantly affected the stress level of the driver, when using the criteria of the vehicle speed of 40 km/h and standard deviation of the speed of 20 km/h. Relevance to industry: The classification results models indicate that the traffic conditions and the road types are important features for driving stress and its related applications.

The participant of the experiment was a healthy 35-year-old Korean male with a height of 176 cm and a weight of 67 kg. The driver was well equipped with the driving route and had driving experience of than 15 years.

The Empatica E4 wristband (EDA sensor) was used for physiological signal collection in our experiment. The wristband [42] is a wearable and wireless device designed for comfortable, continuous, and real-time data acquisition in daily life. It contains three sensors: (1) PPG sensor that detects cardiovascular features as blood volume pulse and heart rate variability, (2) EDA sensor that measures an arousal of a sympathetic nervous system and deliver features related to stress, engagement, and excitement, and (3) three-axis accelerometer that captures motion-based activity. The wristband operates either in streaming mode for real-time data viewing on a mobile device, using Bluetooth Low Energy or in recording mode, using its internal memory.
