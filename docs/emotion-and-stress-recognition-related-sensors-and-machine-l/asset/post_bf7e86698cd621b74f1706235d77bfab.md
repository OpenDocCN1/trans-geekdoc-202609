Among the 720 segments, one of the ECG segments was significantly distorted by a motion noise; we excluded this segment and its label for further analysis. To evaluate the performance of a model, five-fold validation was used on both machine learning models and DeepER. This method commonly evaluates the predictability of a model [5]. The 719 segments were randomly shuffled and split into five folds. Furthermore, five-fold cross validation was applied for evaluation. The use of this cross validation indicates, that the segments extracted from a subject can be in both test and training set. Because of the similarity between the test set and training set, this might lead to higher accuracy on the testing set.

We calculated the average performance of the machine learning models, for comparison of purposes. The random forest (RF) yielded the highest accuracy (71.8 ± 2.3%), F1 score (0.67 ± 0.04), and AUC (0.80 ± 0.02). This was followed by the decision tree (DT), SVM, KNN, and the logistic regression (LR) showed the lowest performance. This highlights that different models can give different performance, even when trained on the same handcrafted dataset, and that we need to find the most suitable model for each problem. In addition, the fact that the RF and LR demonstrated the highest and lowest performance, suggests that an ensemble model can be used for recognizing stress. However, the RF's AUC was less than 0.9, so it cannot be considered to be highly accurate [24].

Turning to the performance of the proposed DeepER, we found that it had the highest accuracy (83.9 ± 2.3%), F1 score (0.81 ± 0.05), and AUC (0.92 ± 0.01). Compared with RF, its average accuracy was 12.1% (p-value < 0.05 with paired t-test), its average F1 score was 0.14 (p-value < 0.05 with paired t-test), and its average AUC was 0.12 higher (p-value < 0.05 with paired t-test), clearly indicating that our deep learning approach was a substantial improvement. In addition, DeepER's AUC was greater than 0.9, so we can conclude that it is highly accurate for recognizing stress [24]. These results suggest that our deep learning approach is a promising option for recognizing stress. Loss and accuracy information of the proposed DeepER Net during training is shown in Figure S1.

## 4.2. Visualization

Although numerous studies have crafted machine learning models based on handcrafted features, Table-4 shows that data-driven features can capture more general information than handcrafted ones. Visualizing the activation is a potentially useful way to further analyze results, as it can help researchers understand how the network is making decisions and find new stress-related features. Here, we selected the network trained during the first fold of cross validation and a sample of the ECG and RESP data. Then, after calculating the activation in both parts of the network, we compared the first batch-normalization layer's output with the activation after the first ReLU. Because we used zero-padding the convolutional layer to maintain the input length, we also added zero-padding to the first batch-normalization layer's output. The activations of the ECG and RESP networks are shown in Figures 4 and 5, respectively.

Visualization is a promising approach to finding evidence for how networks make decisions. Of the various visualization tools, we elected to look at the activation of the network's neurons to identify which ECG and RESP patterns they focused. As Figure 4 shows, the neurons were activated around ECG QRS and T waveforms. These are unique patterns, specific to ECG data, and the network's convolutional layers were able to consider changes in their shape and amplitude. Likewise, Figure 5 shows that the network was able to extract patterns around the RESP peaks and troughs.

These findings indicate that our network can extract a more comprehensive range of features than simple handcrafted waveform (e.g., R-peaks), frequency-domain, or time-domain features. This is because the network learned meaningful statistical features from the data. From this point of view, we can understand why the network performed better than the machine learning models (Table 4). We can therefore conclude that this deep learning approach is more promising than the previously proposed machine learning approaches.

Consequently, we used this optimized network structure as the basis for our proposed DeepER Net.
