# 2. Related Works

Regarding human emotion recognition based on EDA sensors which can be embedded in smart wearable devices, few works have been published so far. However, in [17], they proposed a system to recognize the driver's emotional state after transforming the EDA signals using a short-time Fourier transform. They described three classes: neutral-stress, neutral-anger, and stress-anger.

Furthermore, in [18], they applied a complex physiological electroactivity (cvEDA) framework and clustering algorithms to classify the arousal and valence levels induced by affect stimuli.

In the literature, it has been proven that the stimuli play an important role to increase the EDA response which helps to make the emotion recognition process less complex [19]. Furthermore, other works showing results when EDA responses are modulated by musical emotional [20,21]. Consequently, this result encourages researchers to work on classifying arousal and valence levels induced by auditory stimuli.

In [22], authors used the AVEC 2016 dataset [23,24], they proposed a deep-learning model that consists of a CNN followed by a recurrent network and fully connected layers. They showed that an end-to-end learning approach directly drawing on raw signals can replace feature engineering for emotion recognition purposes.

Moreover, the use of different physiological signals has been previously involved [25,26]. However different types of sensors on the human body is not preferred nor well-accepted. In [27], authors used different types of sensors, ECG (Electrocardiogram), EDA and ST (Skin Temperature) through a hybrid neural network which combines four channels valence and arousal, mainly, high valence high arousal, high valence low arousal, low valence high arousal, and low valence low arousal. In [25], authors combined dataset during racing conditions. The emotional classes identified are high stress, low stress, disappointment, and euphoria. Support vector machines (SVMs) and adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) have been used for the classification.

In [27], the reported results using only EDA to recognize four different sets, joy, anger, sadness, pleasure using 193 features and a music and and based on genetic algorithm and the K-neighbor methods.

The major limitations in the state-of-the-art can be summarized in three major points. First, the limitation regarding recognizing human emotions based on EDA signals (i., published works do not comprehensively the lab-setting independence of emotion classifiers). Second, the limitation concerning human subject-independence of emotion recognition (i., published works do not comprehensively the subject-independence property of emotion classifiers for EDA signals). Third, most related works do focus on classifying only 2 (active/positive) emotional states.

In this work, we focus on the second and the third limitation, due to the fact that classifying human emotion with different lab settings is a research question which may need to adjust the raw data in a feature-engineering level which is not the focus of this work CNN does the desired features internally as it is a deep-learning model.
