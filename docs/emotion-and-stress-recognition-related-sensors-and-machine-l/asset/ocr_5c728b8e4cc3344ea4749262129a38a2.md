# 4.2. Testing the Models in the Wild

As mentioned, we had two types of labels in the laboratory environment stress detection experiment: PSS-5 self-reports and the known context (the stress level). On the other hand, in the wild, we had only self-reports of individuals, which was among the reasons why daily life stress detections were low [34], and there is still room for improvement in daily life stress detection research.

We used library truth labels whereas developing machine learning models for laboratory environments and testing in daily life. The LDSR model was trained with the laboratory self-report labels, whereas the LDKC was trained with the known context labels. We expected that self-report based labeling would have higher performance than context-based labeling because it was more coherent with the daily self-reports. Participants might experience different stress than the known context implied, which reduced the performance of the LDKC. We further developed a DDSR model that was solely trained and tested with daily life data. We compared three models in Tables 4–7. As expected, the LDSR model had the better performance. In the laboratory, self-reports were more reliable because the environment was controlled. Furthermore, choosing the self-report label resulted in better performance compared to that with the known context labels. In most of the cases (20 tests), a combination of modalities outperformed the single modality done alone.
