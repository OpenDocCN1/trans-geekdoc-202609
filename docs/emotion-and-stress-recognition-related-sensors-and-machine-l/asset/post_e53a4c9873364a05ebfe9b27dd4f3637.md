**Abstract:** The detection of emotions is fundamental in many areas related to health and well-being. This paper presents the identification of the level of arousal (EDA) through a commercial device. The objective was to recognize arousal and create future features that help them improve mood, contributing to reduce depression and anxiety. To this end, elderly people in the region of Murcia were exposed to listening to various musical genres (flamenco, Cuban folk and rock/jazz) during their youth. Using the method of deconstruction of the EDA, two different studies were conducted. First, of a purely natural study, based on the search for statistically significant differences for a series of temporal, morphological, statistical and frequency features of the processed signals. It was found that Flamenco and Spanish Folk presented the highest number of statistically significant parameters. In the second study, a wide range of classifiers was used to analyze the correlations between the EDA-based arousal level around the participants' responses to the level of arousal subjectively felt. In this case, it was obtained that the best classifiers are support vector machines, with 87% accuracy for flamenco and 83.1% for Spanish Folk, by K-nearest neighbors with 81.4% and 81.5% for Flamenco and Spanish Folk again. These results reinforce the notion of familiarity with musical emotion induction.

**Keywords:** arousal; aging adults; musical genres; electrothermal activity

Understanding and recognizing human emotions has been identified as a major interest in smart systems [1–5]. Such systems are being applied in many fields like well-being and healthcare [6–11], safe driving [12], smart cities [13] and smart environments [14,15]. Pleasure, arousal and dominance are independent dimensions to describe people' state of feeling [16,17]. Arousal was conceived as a mental activity to describe the state of feeling along a single dimension ranging from sleep to frantic excitement and to adjectives such as stimulated-relaxed, excited-calm and wake-awake to define arousal [18].

The arousal level changes constantly, and it has a profound influence on performance during everyday activities [19]. Fluctuations in arousal are regulated by the autonomic nervous system, which is mainly controlled by the balanced activity of the parasympathetic and sympathetic systems [20]. Electrodermal activity (EDA; or skin conductance) has also frequently been used as a measure of arousal. The advantage of EDA is that it is unambiguous, given that it is innervated by the sympathetic nervous system (SNS) [21]. Within the domain of emotion research, physiological measures such as EDA, heart rate, and body temperature have been frequently used as correlates of emotional arousal. Among these, EDA is a preferred measure as it is highly sensitive and under control of the sympathetic nervous system and is therefore largely involuntary. Furthermore, a relationship between EDA as indicator of arousal and experienced arousal has been demonstrated [22–26]. This paper introduces arousal detection from EDA using musical stimuli. Several studies have reported that using music to elicit emotions is one of the most effective methods [27–30]. Music plays a key role in most people's lives, have frequently being used to explore and regulate emotions. The proposal is linked to our current research elicitation of emotions in elderly [31–35].

Specifically, over 60 years old from the region of Murcia, were recruited to listen to a series of musical pieces similar to those played in their younger years to study the level of arousal produced by each genre. The use of original music fragments provides a high level of control over the independent variables on the dependent ones [27]. Experimental control is especially important when analyzing physiological responses like EDA [36]. The signals collected during the experiment were used in conjunction with a SAM questionnaire to undergo a couple of studies. One alone and the second, based on classifiers, examined possible correlations between the objective detection of the arousal from processed EDA signals and the level of arousal objectively perceived by participants when answering the SAM.
