## 4.2. Testing the Models in the Wild

As mentioned, we had two types of labels in the laboratory environment stress detection experiment: PSS-5 self-reports and the known context (the stress level). On the other hand, in the wild, we had only self-reports of individuals, which was among the reasons why daily life stress detections were low [34], and there is still room for improvement in daily life stress detection research.

We used library truth labels whereas developing machine learning models for laboratory environments and testing in daily life. The LDSR model was trained with the laboratory self-report labels, whereas the LDKC was trained with the known context labels. We expected that self-report based labeling would have higher performance than context-based labeling because it was more coherent with the daily self-reports. Participants might experience different stress than the known context implied, which reduced the performance of the LDKC. We further developed a DDSR model that was solely trained and tested with daily life data. We compared three models in Tables 4–7. As expected, the LDSR model had the better performance. In the laboratory, self-reports were more reliable because the environment was controlled. Since stress detection systems have lower accuracies in the wild when compared to laboratory environments, there is need to develop new techniques to improve their performance. In this study, we examined the effect of developing ML models in different environments and with varying truth labels. To the best of our knowledge, this was the first work to examine all possible combinations of perceived stress measurements for daily and laboratory settings with different ground truth labels. We used EDA, HRV, ST, and ACC signals (ST and ACC were used for artifact detection and removal) in our unobtrusive stress detection system. We first trained and tested our system in the laboratory environment. We obtained a maximum of 94.4% accuracy with HR, 86.70% with EDA, and 92.30% with HRV + EDA, which showed that our system detected the stress levels in the laboratory successfully. These results were aligned with the literature [14]. Choosing the ground truth as self-reports while training the ML model always achieved higher accuracies than using the known context labels, which could be explained by the fact that stress levels (i.e., known context) might not represent the perceived stress of participants. We further looked up to daily life environments and tried the DDR model, which achieved 68.30% accuracy with HRV, 63.60% accuracy with EDA, and 71.40% accuracy with multimodal HRV + EDA. We applied the model in the laboratory with self-reports and showed that the performance increased with all types of physiological signals for daily life.
