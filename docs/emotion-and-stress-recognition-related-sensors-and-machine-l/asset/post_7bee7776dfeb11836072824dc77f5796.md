
We can see that weighted-cluster loss effectively solved the imbalanced problem in the AffectNet dataset. The FER performance with minor emotions dramatically improved. For instance, recognition accuracy of the transferred ResNet-50 model for the contest improved approximately by 1000%—from 6% when fine-tuned using the softmax loss, to 59% when fine-tuned using the proposed weighted-cluster loss. For the disgust, it improved by 150%—36% when fine-tuned using the softmax loss to 54% when fine-tuned using the proposed weighted-cluster loss. This is because the loss function penalizes misclassifying samples from these classes. The performance with major emotions (e.g., happy and neutral) slightly improved. The same trend can be found in case of the transferred ResNet-50 model where the recognition accuracy of class intent improved about 50% (from 10% to 53%) and that of class disgust improved about 150% (from 30% to 47%).

It is worth noting that although the weighted-cluster loss serves to increase the inner-class differences, the differences between some emotions such as happy vs. neutral, surprise vs. anger still remain to some extent. This is due to the natural similarity between these emotions. For reason, there were up to 16% samples of the neutral and 15% samples of happy class that was misclassified by the contest model 10. In the opposite direction, there was a high percentage (e.g., 11% and 12%) samples of the class misclassified as the neutral and happy class, respectively. Similarly, in model 10, 8% samples of surprise was misclassified as the fear, and 13% of the fear class was misclassified as the surprise class while disgust and anger were fairly recognized at the rate of 15% and 22%.

## 4.5. Threats to Validity

- Threat to internal validity: Threat to internal validity errors when implementing the codes and conducting experiments. Although implementations and experiments were carefully verified, errors are possible.
- Threat to validity: Threats to extend the generalization to other datasets of the results in this paper. Applying the proposed methods in paper to additional datasets will reduce this threat.
- Threats to validity: Threats to consider the appropriateness of the benchmark algorithms and evaluation metrics. For the most part, the proposed FER outperformed its counterparts on the same test dataset. The evaluation metrics used for validating methods (i.e., accuracy, F1-Score, Kappa score, AUC, and AUC-PR) are common in machine learning studies to evaluate the performance of classification models.

## 5. Conclusions

This paper proposed a facial expression recognition model based on deep learning and a novel weighted-cluster loss to mitigate the shortage of unbalanced data. SE-ResNet-50 model which is prepared for face identification is fine-tuned to recognize eight facial expressions in AffectNet data. This not only helps to save computing resource but also alleviate the shortage of training data used in the fine-tuning phase to handle the high imbalance in data distribution of AffectNet data. Multiple methods have been used to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed model. Experimental results on the test indicate that the proposed model can outperform its counterparts which use softmax loss or center loss. Moreover, using generative adversarial networks (GAN) to generate more data is considered our future work.
