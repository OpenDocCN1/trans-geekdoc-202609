The emotion recognition reaches different levels of accuracy. The tests of the produced output models showing that the lowest recognition errors were reached when all data were considered or when the GSR datasets were omitted from the model training. It also showed that the emotion *surprised* was the easiest to recognize, having a mean RMSE of 0.13 and mean MAE of 0.01; while the emotion *sad* was the hardest to recognize, having a mean RMSE of 0.82 and mean MAE of 0.08. When considered only higher emotions by time, the match accuracies were between 55% and 100%. It can be partially explained by the amount of emotion detected by the Face Reader, which identified emotions *happy*, *surprised* and *scared* presented more instances than the experiments.

As of future work, we intend to execute more emotion recognition tests, omitting the datasets (matching with the higher emotions by time), to optimize the total accuracies. Also, we want to optimize the quality of the emotion dataset, processed by the Face Reader, and then obtain higher accuracies and lower error levels. Increasing the number of features is another improvement that can be applied in the future; it would require more data for training during the recognition phase.
