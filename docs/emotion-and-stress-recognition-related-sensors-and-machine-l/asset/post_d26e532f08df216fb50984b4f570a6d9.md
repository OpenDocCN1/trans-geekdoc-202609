## 2.2. User Profile

The considered users (not real pilots) of both genders between 21 and 24 years old. Considering the 13 valid flights, nine were executed by male users and four were executed by female users.

Regarding the user experience in experimental context, one male user reported to have a more deep experience in flight simulator; the other male users reported to have experience with electronic devices and all female users reported to low experience in flight simulators and electronic devices.

## 2.3. Acquisition Devices

The multimodal data acquisition was based on Heart Rate (HR), Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) and electroencephalography (EEG). The emotion monitoring system includes a set of sensors such as: two shimmer3-GSR+, one MedLab-Pearl100 and one Enbio-N8.

two shimmer3-GSR+ units the devices used to, acquire the GSR data and to act as an auxiliary head shaking indicator, using its embedded accelerometer. It includes: 1 channel GSR (Analog); the measurement range: 10 x 4.7 MO (0.2-100 μS); frequency range: DC-15.9 Hz; protection RF/EM interference; multichannel input: 2 channel analog/I2C; digital input: 3.5 MHz MSP430 CPU with a precision clock subsystem; 10D inertial sensor with accelerometer integrated, gyroscope, magnetometer and altimeter; low power consumption, weight and small form; also perform the analog to digital conversion and readily connects via Bluetooth or local storage via micro SD card. Furthermore, it is also highly configurable which can be used in a variety of data scenarios [2].

The HR data was acquired by the MedLab-Pearl100 device. It is considered an excellent artifactbuffering device includes: a compact, portable and attractive design; crisp, easily readable TFT colour display; reliably measures SpO2; pulse rate, and pulse strength; integrated 100 h trend memory; integrated context support; intuitive, multi-language user interface; works on mains and integrated battery; full alarm system usable from neonates to adults [27].

To acquire the EEG, the Enobio Toolkit was used. It is a wearable toolkit with a wireless electrophysiology sensor system for the recording of EEG. Using the Neuroelectronics headband toolkit (having several dry and wet electrodes), the Enobio-N8 is ideal for out-of-the-lab applications. comes integrated with an intuitive, powerful user interface for configuration, recording and visualization of 24 bit EEG data at 500 sampling rate, including spectrogram and 3D visualization in real time of spectral features. It is ready for research or clinical use. In addition to EEG, triaxial accelerometer data is automatically collected. You can also use a microSD card to save data offline in Holer mode; and like Shimmer device, it can use Bluetooth to transmit real time data too [28].

## 2.4. Facial Emotion Sensing

During the experiment, users' faces and the flights along the experiments were recorded and outputs were processed after the experiment. To do this, two softwares were used: the OBS Studio, to record the flight and face time in a synchronized manner, and the software Face Reader v.7, a software marked by Noldus (www.noldus.com) to recognize the emotions based on the face recording. The last one considers seven emotions: neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, scared and disgust.

In offline analysis, processing the emotions and emotional data were omitted. In these experiments, the Face Reader output a neutral as a emotion along each flight, which seems unrealistic because it also omitted the emotions of another relevant emotions. It was confirmed by the users; they noted not to feel neutral most of the time. For this reason, we decided to omit the neutral emotion in work. Regarding the disgust emotion, it was also omitted due to not directly related to the flight context as confirmed by the users who said that did not feel disgust along the flights.

## 2.5. Physiological Sensing

The proposed multimodal sensing considered three methods: Heart Rate (HR), Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) and Electroencephalography (EEG). To acquire these, Ag/Ag/Cl dry electrodes and one earplug were used: eight electrodes placed on the scalp (EEG), one placed on the earlobe (EEG), one placed on the earlobe (HR) and two on the hand of the user (GSR).

The GSR data is based on Electrodermal Activity (EDA) and refers to the electrical resistance between sensors when a weak current occurs. This is typically acquired from hands or fingers. In this work, it was acquired by Shimmer3-GSR+ unit which can measure activity, emotional engagement and psychological arousal in lab and remote scenarios. It was recommended that these electrodes were kept immobile during the experiment to avoid additional motion artifacts.

Regarding EEG, some studies showed that it is difficult to find the user's specific region where brain activity is sufficiently high to detect emotional states [32,33]. According to several studies, the prefrontal cortex or frontal lobe (near the front of the head) is more involved with cognition and decision making from emotional responses [34]. The 10-20 system was used to describe and apply location of scalp. This way, to better detect, electrodes were placed where: Fp1 (channel 1), F3 (channel 2), C3 (channel 3), T7 (channel 4), Fp2 (channel 5), F4 (channel 6), C4 (channel 7) and T8 (channel 8). The EEG reference electrode (EEG) was placed on the user's earlobe. It frequently aimed to use beta rhythms (or band) in this experiment, considering brain signals between 10 and 30 Hz.

## 3. Feature Extraction

Feature extraction is the last step before data recognition or classification. It is very important in pattern identification, classification, modeling and general automatic recognition. Its importance is fundamental to minimize the loss of important information embedded in the data [37] and to also optimize a dataset, giving clearer information to recognize any pattern.

This work uses different feature extraction techniques using the technique to acquire the physiological data (e.g., HR, GSR and EEG). Its extraction was executed after the processing phase which prepared the data to have clearer features. It was applied over time and frequency contexts better described in the next section.

### 3.1. Features Description

In this work, we extracted 15 features based on time and frequency. Each feature was chosen to each dataset characteristics as presented in Table 1, which describes all features and the corresponding datasets. If the dataset needed a frequency analysis, it was applied through its features using EEG datasets which filtered and other analysis in frequency and time.
