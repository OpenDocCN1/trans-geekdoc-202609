# 3.2. Evolution of the Research

Figure 2 shows the number of papers published each year which included the topics virtual reality and emotion analysis. This number of studies was calculated based on all the papers screened. In the 1990s, the average number of papers published annually was 6.4, the first being in 1995. In the 2000s, the average number of papers published increased to 26.3, from 2010 to 2014, the average multiplied by three to 77.4. In the last five years, the curve has grown exponentially to 203 in 2019, and 278 in 2020.

# 3.3. Analysis

Figure 3 depicts the evolution in the number of papers analysed in the review based on the emotion analysis. Until 2015, the majority of the papers analysed arousal-related emotions, mostly arousal, anxiety and stress. From that year, some experiments started to analyse-related emotions, such as valence, joy and sadness, but the analysis of arousal-related emotions still predominant. 50% of the studies used CRA (ar 38.1% [54] and anxiety 11.9% [125]), and the other 50% used basic or complex emotions (stress 23.8% [112], anxiety 16.6% [109], fear near 11.9% [43], awe 2.4% [121], calmness 2.4% [135], joy 2.4% [135], pleasantness 2.4% [6] and sadness 2.4%].

# 3.4. Implicit Techniques, Features and Participants

Figure 4 shows the evolution of the number of papers analysed in the implicit measures used. The used HRV (73.8%) and EDA (59.5%). However, the majority of the studies used ANS to analyse emotions. The majority of the studies that used HRV very few features from the frequency domain, such as HF, LF or HF/LF [119,126] and 2 non-linear features, such as Poincare and Poincaré [65,105]. Of the studies used EDA, the majority used tonic skin conductance (SC) [116] but some also used tonic (SCL) [54] or phasic activity (SCR) [124]. In recent years, EEG has increase, with 6 papers being published (14.3%), and the CNS has started to be, in combination with HMDs, to recognise emotions. The analyses that have been used ERP [138], power spectral density [140] and connectivity [65]. EMG (11.9%) and RSP (9.5) were also used, mostly in combination with HRV. Other implicit measures used were eye-tracking, gait patterns and salivary cortisol responses.
