# 3.4. Data Annotation

In addition to the basic annotation, from the experimental design and application log files, the dataset is enhanced by various semi-automatic generated labels. The basic annotation contains the exact timing information at millisecond of the beginning and end of all sequences: Timestamps when each search item was presented, and when a subject presented the solution, whether the question was correct or wrong and all information of the given subjective feedback. These mostly technical annotations do not necessarily contain emotional information, although they can give hints on where the probability of emotional reactions rises, in case of timeouts or wrong answers, or during maximum loads.

The semi-automatic labels are generated by our data driven affective learning approach, as presented in [62]. The basic assumption of this approach is sparseness of emotions in audio and video modalities. We figured that in HCI scenarios, users mostly tend to have emotions only in a few situations, or show them only in sparsity.

# 4. Discussion and Summary

The resulting multimodal uilmMAC database from our emotional and cognitive load scenario conducted in a interactive HCI setting is a valuable contribution to research fields related to multimodal affective computing and machine learning applications in HCI. Summarized, the main contributions of our work include the following:

- Dataset for affective computing: We designed and acquired the uilmMAC dataset for emotional and cognitive recognition. The dataset consists of six different senses, including states Interest, Overload, Normal, Easy, Underload, and Frustration.
- Multimodal interactive: it contains highly multimodal (biosignals, videos, audios, and Kinect), mobile (standing, walking, freely moving positions with physiological sensors), and interactive (HCI via via natural speech) emotional-cognitive HCI scenario.
