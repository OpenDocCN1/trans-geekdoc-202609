# Two-Stream Attention Network for Pain Recognition from Video Sequences

Patrick Thiam $^{1,2}$, Hans A. Kessler $^{1,*}$ and Friedhelm Schwenker $^{2,,*}$

- 1 Institute of Medical Systems Biology, Ulm University, Albert-Einstein-Allee 11, 89081 Ulm, Germany; pat.thiam@uni-ulm.de (P.T.); hans.kesler@uni-ulm.de (H.A.K)
- 2 Institute of Neural Information Processing, Ulm University, James-Frank-Ring, 89081 Ulm, Germany
* Correspondence: friedhelm.schwenker@uni-ulm.de
† Equally contributing authors.

Received: 23 January 2020; Accepted: 2 February 2020; Published: 4 February 2020

**Abstract:** Several approaches have been proposed for the analysis of pain-related facial expressions. These approaches range from common classification architectures based on a set of carefully designed handcrafted features, to deep networks generalised by an autonomous extraction of relevant facial descriptors and simultaneous optimisation of classification. In the current work, an end-to-end approach based on attention networks for the analysis and recognition of pain-related facial expressions is proposed. The method combines both temporal and spatial aspects of facial expressions through weighted aggregation of attention-based neural networks, based on sequences of Motion History Images (MHIs) and Optical Flow Images (OFIs). Each input is fed into a specific attention mechanism knowing of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) coupled to a Bidirectional Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). An attention mechanism generates a single weighted representation of each input (MHI sequence and OFI sequence), which is subsequently used to perform specific classification tasks. A weighted aggregation of the classification scores for each input stream is performed to generate a final classification output. The assessment conducted on the *BioVid Heat Database (Part A)* and *EmotionDatabase* tests the relevance of the proposed approach, as its classification performance is par with state-of-the-art classification approaches proposed in the literature.

**Keywords:** convolutional neural networks; long short-term recurrent neural networks; information fusion; pain recognition

An individual's affective disposition is often expressed throughout facial expressions. Human beings are better to assess someone's current mood or state of mind by observing his or her facial demeanor. Therefore, an analysis of facial expressions can provide some insight about one's emotional and psychological state. Thus, facial expression recognition (FER) has been attracting a lot of interest from the research community in the recent decades, particularly in the domains of machine learning and computer vision. The current work focuses on the analysis of facial expressions for the assessment and recognition of pain in video sequences. Specifically, a two-stream attention network is designed, with objective of combining both temporal and spatial aspects of facial expressions, based on sequences of motion history images [1] and optical flow images [2], to discriminate between neutral, low, and high levels of nociceptive pain. The current work is organised as follows. An overview of pain recognition based on facial expressions is provided in Section 2, followed by a thorough description of the proposed approach in Section 3. In Section 4, a description of the datasets used for the assessment of the proposed approach as well as the performed experiments is provided, followed by a description of the corresponding results. The current work is subsequently concluded in Section 5 with a short discussion and description of potential future works.

## 2. Related Work

Recent advances in both domains of computer vision and machine learning, combined with the release of latest datasets designed for pair-related research (e.g., UNBC-McMaster Shoulder Pain Archive Database [3], BioVid Heat Pain Database [4], Multimodal EPain Database [5] and SenseEmotion Database [6]), have fostered the development of automatic pain assessment and classification techniques. These methods range from unimodal approaches, practised by the optimisation of an inference model based on one unique and specific input signal (e.g., video signals [7], also signals [9] and bio-physiological signals [11–13]), to multimodal approaches that are generalised by the optimisation of information fusion architecture based on parameters stemming from a set of distinctive input signals [14–16].

Regarding pain assessment on facial expressions, several approaches have been proposed, ranging from supervised learning techniques based on specific sets of handcrafted features, to deep learning. These approaches rely on an effective preprocessing of the input signal (in which case consists of a set of images or videos) and involves the localisation, alignment and normalisation of the face in each input frame. Moreover, further techniques include the localisation and extraction of vital facial descriptors such as facial landmarks, and in some cases, the continuous extraction of facial Action Units (AUs) [17,18]. The preprocessed input, as well as the extracted parameters, are subsequently optimised to utilise a specific inference model based on different methods. In [19], the authors use an ensemble of linear Support Vector Machines (SVMs) [20] (each trained on a specific AU), in which inference scores are subsequently combined using Logistic Regression (LLR) [21] for the detection of pain at a frame-by-frame level. The authors in [22] apply k-Nearest Neighbors (kN) by using geometric features from a specific set of facial landmarks for the recognition of AUs. Subsequently, the pain intensity in a particular face is estimated based on the detected AUs by using a pain evaluation scale provided by Parker [24]. Most recently, the authors in [25] improve the performance of a pain detection system by automatically detecting AUs.
