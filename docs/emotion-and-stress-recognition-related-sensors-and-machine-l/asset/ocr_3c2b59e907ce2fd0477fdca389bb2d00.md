In the literature, numerous works are found that these three signals are used to detect stress, emotions, and activity. For instance, ECG is affected by these factors, the heart rate is directly related to the body and mental condition [20–21]. In this sense, Heart Rate Variability (HRV) has been widely used to extract information about the status of the autonomous nervous system and emotions [23]. On the other hand, TEB can be used as an indicator of the breathing function, and it has been used in different uses for activity recognition [24] and stress detection [25]. EDA measures the activity of sweating glands on the skin which are directly controlled by the sympathetic nervous system, and can also be used for emotion recognition [26–29].

However, few papers provide studies including these three signals with the same purpose, comparing the signals under study and determining which physiological signals provide more relevant information about the individual activity. For instance, the features from TEB acquired together with the ECG and the heart sound can be used to study reactivity during emotional activation and in men [24]. Numerous features have been found for this purpose in the literature, but there is not a clear rule of which ones are more relevant for a given problem. The larger the number of features, the greater the generalizations problem, the less the ability to handle unseen data [31]. Selecting a subset of features remains mandatory for many activity recognition.

Taking all into account, the present paper aims at assessing the utility of features extracted from ECG, TEB, and EDA in activity recognition systems. These physiological signals have been recorded using sensorized garments with wearable instrumentation. We intend to recognize four activities: emotional activity, mental activity, physical activity, and resting. The paper is structured as follows: Section 1 introduces the problem tackled in this paper; Section 2 is a review of the literature about physiological signals, window length, and possible classifiers. Section 3 summarizes the sensors used to acquire the signals and the mental states that are considered; Section 4 presents the experiments carried out; Section 5 includes the results; Section 6 presents the main conclusions. A Appendix A–C are also included with a detailed description of the considered features extracted from the acquired signals.

# 2. Background

In this study several features have been analyzed: (a) the physiological sensing mode (ECG, TEB and EDA), (b) the window length, (c) the features extracted from each signal, (d) the number of features to obtain the best results, and (e) the type of classifier.

+   * Selection of physiological sensing modality. In this, we compare the physiological signal under study and determine which physiological signals provide more information about the individual activity. The signals used are ECG, TEB, and EDA. It is possible to find numerous works in which these signals are used to detect stress, emotions, and activity in the literature. The ECG signal is used in some papers as such [23], where the obtained results suggest that positive emotions lead to activation in HRV, which may be beneficial in some illness treatment [19,31,32].

TEB is also used in some papers, but it is less useful than ECG and EDA signals. The work [25] demonstrated that its use is effective to detect stress.
