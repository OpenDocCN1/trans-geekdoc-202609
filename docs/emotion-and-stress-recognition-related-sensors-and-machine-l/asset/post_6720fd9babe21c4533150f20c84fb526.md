# Multimodal Approach for Emotion Recognition Based on Simulated Flight Experiments

Válber César Cavalcanti Roza 1,2* and Octavian Adrian Postolache 1

+ 1. Instituto Universitário de Lisboa (ISCTE-IUL) and Instituto de Telecomunicações (IT-FTUL), Av. das Armadas, 1649-06 Lisbon, Portugal; octavian.postolache@gmail.com
2. Universidade Federal do Rio Grande do Norte (UFRN), Av. Sen. Salgado Filho, 3000, Candelélia, Natal, RN 59064-71, Brazil
* Correspondence: valbercesar@gmail.com

Received: 18 October 2019; Accepted: 9 December 2019; Published: 13 December 2019

**Abstract:** The present work tries to fill the gap regarding the pilots’ emotions and bio-reactions during flight procedures such as takeoff, climbing, cruising, descent, initial approach, approach and landing. a set of experiments were developed, involving several simulated flights ($N_{flights} = 13$) using the Microsoft Flight Simulator Edition (FS-SE). The approach was carried out with eight beginners on the flight simulator ($N_{pilots} = 8$). It is shown that it is possible to recognize emotions from different pilots in flight, comparing them and previous emotions. The cardiac system sensed by Heart Rate (HR), Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) and Electroencephalography (EEG), were used to extract emotions, as well as the intensities of emotions detected from the pilot. We also considered five emotions: happy, sad, angry, surprise and scared. The emotion recognition is based on Artificial Neural Networks and Deep Learning techniques. The Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) were the methods used to measure the quality of the regression output models. The tests of the produced output models showed that the lowest errors were reached when all data were considered or when the GSR datasets were omitted from the model training. It showed that the emotion *surprised* was the easiest to recognize, having a mean RMSE of 0.13 and mean MAE of 0.01; while the emotion *sad* was the hardest to recognize, having a mean RMSE of 0.082 and mean MAE of 0.08. When we considered only the higher intensities by time, the most accuracies were between 55% and 100%.

**Keywords:** emotion recognition; physiological sensing; multimodal sensing; deep learning; flight simulation

With the growth of air safety and accident prevention, in the mechanical-structural and avionics aspects, a gap of probable cause of accidents emerges, which can justify the occurrence of unwanted situations. This can be referred as the relationship between emotions and aviation accidents caused by human failure.

The development of research about the relation between emotions and aviation accidents is quite new and is mainly based on preliminary and final accident reports. It was important to show the real need of improvements and strategies regarding emotion effects in risky situations of a real flight, on take off, approach and landing.

To know how are the studies of emotions over the aviation contexts, we first need to understand emotion definitions. Emotion is led by the brain and can be the result of chemical processes that join internal and external factors to produce an output or response that reflects an emotional state [1]. This response can also reflect physiological changes in human body [2]. Some emotions e.g., the primary emotion "anger" plays a fundamental role in kernel cases, such as fear and trust, that are directly related to protection, defense and maintenance of life.

Several methods and techniques can be applied to perform emotion recognition through the use of a couple of hardware devices and software such as:
- analysis of emotional properties based on physiological data, such as ECG and EEG [3];
- unified system for effective discrimination of positive and negative emotions based on EEG data [4];
- automatic recognizer of the facial expression across the eyes and forehead based on Electroencephalography (EEG) giving support to emotion recognition task [5];
- use of GSR and EEG data to study the effectiveness of Matching Purge (MP) algorithm in emotion recognition, using PCA to reduce the features and Probabilistic Neural Network (PNN) as the recognition technique [6];
- emotion recognition system based on physiological data using ECG and respiration (RESP) data, recorded simultaneously by a physiological monitoring device based on wearable sensors [7];
- emotion recognition using EEG data and also performed an analysis about the impact of positive and negative emotions using SWM and RBF as the methods [8];
- new approach to emotion recognition based on EEG and classification method using Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) with features analysis based on Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) [9];
- an application stores physiological data based on HR, ECG, Sp2 and GSR, that were created when the users advertised about smoking campaigns [10];
- experiments based on flight simulator to develop a multimodal architecture to recognize emotions using three different techniques for biosignal acquisitions [11];
- multimodal system to identify emotions using acquisition techniques, based on photo presentation methodology [12];
- real-time interface with emotion recognition that depends on the need for skill development to support a change in the interface paradigm to one that is more than human [13];
- recognize emotions through physiological sensing using a multiple-function-layer classifier of stacked autoencoder (MSEAE) [14].

In addition, it is also possible to present research that is more related to emotion recognition e.g., the use of the Friedman test to evaluate whether the work on emotion and emotional identification influences the levels of anxiety and depression [15];
- emotion recognition system based on cross-correlation and the Flow database [16];
- derived features based on bi-spectral analysis for quantification of emotions using a Valence-Arousal emotion model, to get a way of gaining phase information by detecting relationships between features components and characterization of the non-Gaussian information from EEG data [17];
- a novel real-time subject-dependent algorithm using Stability-trace Correlation Coefficient (ICC) with the most stable features that gives a better accuracy than other algorithms when it is crucial to have only one training session [18];
- analysis of emotion recognition techniques used in existing systems to enhance recognition on the improvement of tuning adaptation [19];
- and the ensemble deep learning framework by integrating multiple stacked autoencoder to reduce the model complexity and improve the recognition accuracy using physiological features [20].

In the present work, we studied the multimodal or multisensory architecture, processing, these emotions. These "pilots" were created by the beginner users of a flight simulator (not real pilots), following a sequence of steps during the flight experiments. The result of this work can also be applied to various workplaces and contexts e.g., administrative sectors [21], in aviation companies/schools [11] and in urban areas [16], among others.

## 2. Multimodal Sensing

The proposed system utilizes several sensors to capture physiological signals:
- Heart Rate (HR) measured via a wearable chest strap.
- Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) captured through skin conductance sensors.
- Electroencephalography (EEG) to monitor brain activity.

The data from these sensors are synchronized and processed to identify emotional states during flight.

## 2.1. Experiment

A total of 13 simulated flights ($N_{flights} = 13$) and eight beginner users on flight simulator ($N_{pilots} = 8$) were considered, using the Microsoft Flight Simulator Edition (FS-SE). These flights were used as: RC1, RC2, RC3, GC1, GS1, LS1, LS2, VC1, VC2, CR1, CR3 and CL3.

The proposed experiment corresponds to the human behaviour of the users (pilot in) some flight tasks such as:
- Take off (Task 1)
- climbing (Task 2)
- flight (cruise navigation) (Task 3)
- descent (Task 4)
- approach (Task 5)
- final (Task 6)
- landing (Task 7)

The data from these sensors are synchronized and processed to identify emotional states during flight.
