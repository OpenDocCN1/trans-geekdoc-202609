provided a 5 min rest between the mental arithmetic tests. To measure the mental arithmetic induced mental stress in the participants, we used two questionnaires, including self-assessment manikin (SAM) [24] and distress thermometer (DT) [25]. The self-reports were written after the first rest period and after two repetitions of the tasks. The mental arithmetic experiments were approved by the institutional review board at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (2017-030).

# 2.2. Data Preprocessing and Annotation Proportions

We performed some preprocessing procedures before the data sets for training the neural network. As the ECGs of the two data sets were recorded by different sensors, we scaled them to the same range (0-1) by z-score normalization,

```
$x_i^s \leftarrow \frac{x_i^s - \mu^s}{\sigma^s}, \quad \text{for } 0 \leq i \leq n\ns \in [Driving, MA]$
```

where $n$ denotes the number of tasks for each stress and window. We calculated the mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$), with all the data for each stress (i.e., driving and the mental arithmetic). We normalized the ECG using both the mean and standard deviation. After normalization, the data set was downampled to match the sampling rate of the mental arithmetic data set, which needed a fixed input dimension, due to using the same neural network for training the model and detecting the stress on the two different data sets. The ECG signals were sliced into 10 s, 30 s, and 60 s to detect stress in short-term windows. The reason for selecting a short-term window was to recognize stress in real-time.

We needed to annotate the data with specific labels to train a neural network by supervised method. We segmented the data set, based on the boundaries of driving on the highway, driving on the city, and resting. The ECG measurements recorded during driving on the highway and on the city were labeled as stress and rest. In the case of the mental arithmetic data set, we labeled the ECG measurements recorded during the mathematical task as stress and others as rest. The other ECG measurements, recorded during the rest period, were annotated as rest.

Table 2 shows the number of the data sets and their label distribution for each window. The number of data labeled as stress in the driving was much more than labeled as rest, while the mental arithmetic data set showed a balanced distribution. The drivers took for approximately 30 min, including an initial and final rest, but they drove for 45 min, resulting in an unbalanced distribution. The participants who took the mental arithmetic test were exposed to the same amount of time for the stress task and resting: 10 min. The driving data set and the mental arithmetic data set contained over 72,000 and 16,000 ECG cycles, respectively.
