This work highlights the use of immersive VR, in particular using head-mounted displays, in emotion recognition combination with implicit measures. It provides a clear perspective based on a systematic review and aggregated analysis, focusing on the role that VR might play as an elicitation tool in the coming years.

The evolution of scientific VR and emotions has grown exponentially, to more than 200 papers per year (Figure 2). However, the performance improvements in the last few years in the latest generation of HMMDs, in terms of resolution, field of view, immersion levels and the fall in their price, has boosted their use in emotion-recognition research. With VR's increased application in recent years in other areas, such as rehabilitation, neuropsychology and therapy [2], results suggest that the 2010s were the decade of rapid growth of VR in emotion research using implicit measures, and the 2020s might be the decade when the future fails. Environmental simulations might, in the future, go beyond the paradigm of non-immersive/video-based 2D images to immersive scenarios, where subjects feel a strong sense of presence and can interact with the stimuli presented.

Regarding HMDs and implicit measures in emotion analysis, there is no consensus about the use of CMA [92] or the Ekman theory of basic emotions [91], both approaches are used in 50% of the research (Figure 3). The differences in the frameworks used caused some disparities in comparing the results of different studies. The majority of the studies (80.5%) included analyses of [54], or high-arousal-discrete emotions, such as stress [112], anxiety [119] and fear [43]. On the other hand, only 29.9% of the studies analyzed emotions closely related to valence, such as we [121], calm [93], joy [108] and sadness [135]. Therefore, the challenge of effective affective computing using HMDs is in its first growth phase, and physiological dynamics, in particular, are under-searched. Recent research since 2017 has started to address this [65,139], a dimension of the CMA not addressed in general affective computing research using pictures or videos [143] has also not been in HMD set-up. In terms of the implicit measures used to recognise emotions through HMDs, ANS measures are used most: HRV (73.8%) and EDA (55.9%), many times used in combination. However, until 2016, the majority of the papers used only HR and SC (Table 2), in combination with EMG and GSR. From 2016, the research started to include HRV frequency domain and non-linear dynamics analyses [105,119], and EDA analyses, such as CDA, dividing the signals into tonic and phase components [64]. In terms of the CNS, EEG research has been undertaken since 2016, including ERP [138], power spectral density [140] and functional connectivity analysis [65].
