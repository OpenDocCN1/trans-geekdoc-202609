The sets-ups used, mostly screens, are non-immersive devices, which provide only a low level of presence in subjects [30]. Therefore, the stimuli do not evoke the feeling of “being there”, which is needed to analyse emotions in simulated real-world situations. In addition the stimuli are non-interactive, so do not allow the subjects to intervene in the scene, which would open the possibility to recognise emotional states during interactive tasks. These limitations can be overcome by using immersive VR as a new emotion elicitation method. Since the year 2000, VR has increasingly been used as affective stimulus, however the majority of the studies have applied classical statistical methods, such as hypotheses and correlation, to analyse subjects' physiological responses to emotions [104]. In recent years, research has started to apply affective computing paradigms with VR as the emotion elicitation method, combining implicit measures with machine-learning methods to develop automatic emotion recognition models [105].

This paper provides a systematic review of the literature on the use of head-mounted displays in implicit measure-based emotion recognition research, and examines the progress of the research field, the emotions elicited, the data analysis, the set-ups and the validations performed.

# 2. Materials and Methods

Data Collection

We adopted an adapted version of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [106]. This includes steps to identify literature, to screen the identified literature, to check the eligibility of the screened literature and finally, to synthesize the literature. The search was queried using the following string: carried out on 25 March 2020. TITLE-ABS-KEY (“virtual reality”) OR “head-mounted display”) AND TITLE-ABS-KEY (“emotion** OR “affective”) AND DOCTYPE (AR or re). The keywords virtual reality head-mounted display include all the studies on VR and, in particular, that all used HMDs. In addition, the keywords ‘emotion* OR ‘affective* include all the papers related to emotion. The combination of both requirements limited the search to journals and reviews (snowballing). A total of 1424 records were identified. 14 additional records were found from other sources.

The screening and eligibility checks were undertaken as follows: (1) first, by investigating titles and abstracts, 13 records were excluded. (2) The manuscripts were specially screened for a thematic match with virtual reality as emotion elicitation. A total of 1157 records were excluded for not matching the topic, and 3 records were because they were unavailable. (3) We investigated 265 records to exclude those that did not fit, using a specific rejection order- that is, if they used HMDs, we moved to the next criterion, implicit measures, if they used implicit measures, we moved on to the last criterion, the analysis of an emotion. 132 records were rejected for not using implicit measures, 68 for and 23 for not analysing an emotional dimension. Finally, 42 studies were included in the analysis which used virtual reality displayed in an HMD, in combination with implicit measure to analyse or recognise emotional states.
