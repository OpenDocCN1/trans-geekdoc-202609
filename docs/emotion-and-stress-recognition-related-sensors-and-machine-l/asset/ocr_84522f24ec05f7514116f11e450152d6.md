# # 3. Results

# ## 3.1. Traffic Conditions

A combination of 40 km/h of average speed and 20 km/h of standard deviation, which exhibit relatively higher accuracy, sensitivity, and positive predictive value, was selected. The mean value of four measures at the combination were the highest. It is highlighted in Table 3. From Table 3, we can see that a few cases have an accuracy of 40/h of 80%. However, we cannot select them due to low sensitivity and predictive value, which are under 50%. Low sensitivity shows that a large number of cases at 40 km/h high stress are not recognized, and the low predictive value indicates the low effectiveness of the model in detecting high stress. Thus, considering all the effective parameters of the developed model, the most accurate criterion of low high traffic was found with average speed of 40 km/h and standard deviation of 20 km/h: Classification model accuracy = 80.3%; sensitivity = 85%; specificity = 78%; and positivity = 70%. Based on these results, we can conclude that traffic conditions (low high traffic) are an important element to classifying stress levels (low high stress). In addition, we predicted that traffic conditions (average speed < 40 km/h and SD < 20 km/h) could be a clear threshold of stress levels (low high stress), compared to the other 19 conditions in Table 3.

The mathematical rule of the developed model for the classification of stress levels was based on the probability concept. High stress was recognized by the model if the probability was greater than 0.5. Otherwise the case was classified as low stress.

The goodness of fit measure evaluates the ability of developed models to explain variation in the dependent variable (high and low stress). As such, pseudo-R-squared are usually used in logistic regression analysis. In the presented study pseudo-R-squared of Cox and Snell Nagelker were obtained using IBM SPSS Statistics Software (Version 23). For traffic condition model Cox and Snell and Nagelker R-squares are 0.323 and 0.432 respectively.
