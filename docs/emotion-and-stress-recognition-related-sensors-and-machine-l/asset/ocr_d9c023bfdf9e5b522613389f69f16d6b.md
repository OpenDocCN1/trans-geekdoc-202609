# # Emotion and Stress Recognition Related Sensors and Machine Learning Technologies

Kyondhere Kyamakya 1*, Fadi Al-Machot 2, Ahmad Haj Mosa 1, Hamid Bouchachia 3, Jean Chamberlain Chedjou 1 and Antoine Bagula 4

+   1 Institute for Smart Technologies, Universitat Klagenfurt, A9020 Klagenfurt, Austria; ahmad.haj@mospac.com (A.H.M.); Jedchau@au (J.C.J)
Department of Applied Informatics, Universitat Klagenfurt, 9020 Klagenfurt, Austria; Fadi.AlMachot@auau.com Machine Intelligence Group, Bournemouth Bournemouth BH125JB, bouchachia@bournemouth.uk
2 ISAT Laboratory, University of the West Cape, 7535 Belleville, South Africa; bhaglia@uwc.ac.za
Correspondence: kyondhere.kyamakya@auau.com

Scientific technologies are gaining momentum in today's information-society, where different technologies are used to collect data from such systems and this data to make useful insights about daily activities. These systems range from driver-assistance systems, to medical-monitoring systems, to emotion-aware intelligent systems, to collaborative robotics systems. They are built around (i) intuitive technologies such as EEG, electrodermal activity and skin conductivity and (ii) noninvasive technologies that use piezo-vibration sensors, facial expression cameras, hair vibration sensors and bone vibrational sensors. In our daily lives, there are many types of issues relating to the design and development of stress-related systems, which they rely on the design of emotion and stress classification from physiological signals.

Issues can be viewed from various perspectives: (i) quality and reliability of data sensor, (b) performance comparison in tasks of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-measure; (c) robustness of sub-independent recognition; (d) portability of the classification systems to different environments and (e) the estimation of the state for dynamic systems.

This Special Issue of the Journal on Emotion and Stress Related Sensors and Machine Learning Technologies emerges as a crucial need for massive deployment of innovativeing systems. These technologies are being applied in assistive systems in different domains and parts of the world to address challenges that could not be addressed with the advances made in past. The Special Issue includes 25 papers submitted to release for papers. The high number of submissions to this Special Issue is an indication of the momentum of the current research in this field. Mission is not driven only by technology but also by human needs. This Special Issue includes papers that present concepts, frameworks, architectures and ideas on sensing technologies and machine-learning techniques. They are relevant in tackling the following challenges: (i) the field readiness and use of intuitive sensors and data for capturing biosignal, including EEG sensor, ECG and electroactivity sensors; (ii) the quality assessment and management of sensor data, (iii) data preprocessing, filtering and calibration techniques for biosignals; (iv) the field readiness and use of non-invasive sensor technologies, including Visual sensors, Acoustic sensors, vibration sensors and Piezzo-electric sensors; (v) emotion recognition using machine learning and networks; (vi) area stress studies; (vii) the use of operational data in emotion recognition, including dataset generation principles and concepts, quality assurance and emotion elicitation materials and contents; (viii) machine-learning techniques for robust emotion recognition,
