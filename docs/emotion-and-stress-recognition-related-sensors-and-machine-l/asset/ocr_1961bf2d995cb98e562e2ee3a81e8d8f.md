# 4.1. Predictability of Stress State Depending on Traffic Conditions and Road Type

External driving conditions have a decisive influence on the emotional state of the driver and the occurrence of traffic accidents [44,45]. Based on this, two driving conditions of traffic congestion and road type were chosen and compared in this study. Simplified traffic conditions criteria, which characterize traffic congestion, were found using various measures and a standard deviation of average speed of 20 km/h. If this criterion is met, then we can say that it corresponds to potential traffic congestion and in this moment, the driver is in a high stress state. To confirm this, a statistical model was developed. We showed the classification accuracy of 80.3%. From the detected EDA features, the number of occurrences, duration, and amplitude of peaks and valleys were found as significant predictors for the analyzed model. The method for determining stress dependency on the road type shows the classification accuracy of 22.9% with a significant predictor of Min OD. The goodness of model fit is assessed using various measures and in modern literature there is no consensus which one is better [46]. In our study we evaluated using Cox and Snell Nagelkerke pseudo-R-squares, which show improvement of the constant-only model (null model) after applying predictors Cox and Snell pseudo-R² is not to reach “1” for the perfect model, in turn Nagelkerke R² is adjusted Cox and Snell R² and its maximal value can be extended to “1” [46]. Obtained results for traffic condition model that show difference between 32.3% (Cox and Snell) and 43.2% (Nagelkerke) in low and high stress level, in turn, road type model explain difference 37.4% (Cox and Snell) and 51.8% (Nagelkerke) of value (Table 7). In this view, there is no consensus on how to interpret the values of pseudo-R-squares, but some sources [47,48] evaluated the Cox & Snell level higher than 0.3 and Nagelkerke higher than 0.35. Based on this, our models have a good level of comparison with the observations, but the road type model has higher values and it fits observations. The comparison of both methods is shown in Table 7. We also compared the performance of other classifiers using 10-cross-validation and separation of training (70%) and testing (30%). As shown, the overall result shows that (6) has the best performance, and 89.46% and 85.22% in training/testing method, respectively. Surprisingly, multi-layered perceptron is the area under the ROC curve (AUC values) of 88.50, 85.70 and 79.10, in 10-cross-validation and 10-fold-validation respectively. Neural network-based method shows the overall performance. Thus, the tree-based method is better than neural network-based method in this type of data.

Sensitivity and specificity reflect how well the model developer is able to grasp high stress and low stress, respectively. A positive predictive value indicates the exactness of high stress. Experimental results show that the overall model of the road type dominates, but it can predict the stress more easily in the traffic-condition model. We use the AUC value which reflects the overall performance better as a single value.
