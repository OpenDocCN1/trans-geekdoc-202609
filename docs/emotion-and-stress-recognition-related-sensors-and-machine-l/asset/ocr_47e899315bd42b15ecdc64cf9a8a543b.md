The data collected in the experiments conducted in the laboratory be used for developing models in the lab to improve the low occuracies. There is a limited number of studies using laboratory data for creating models with higher performance in daily life. Li et al. [27] built their model in the lab, verified performance in the lab with different participants, and later verified it the test again with new subjects. They used mental tasks to induce stress and seven-point Likert self-reports as labels in the laboratory (LDSR model type). They evaluated their system performance using the Elastic-net regression technique. The correlation results of predicted labels and true self-reports were 0.72 and 0.56 respectively. Gjorski et al. used a continuous stress detection scheme, which consisted of a base detection mechanism using the activity recognizer for identifying the contextual information and a real-life stress detection mechanism that utilized the result from the laboratory detector for real-life stress detection [25]. While training the model, they used only the context label in the laboratory environment (LDKC-type of model). They did not collect self-reports during these tests. The classification accuracy of their stress detection system built using context information in our taxonomy were increased to 98% by adding the activity type. Since different methods were created for each particular paper in the literature (see Table 1), one could not infer the success of a method over other methods. Therefore, there is a clear need for comparing the different proposed techniques for stress detection in the laboratory and daily life settings with unobtrusive devices.

# 3. Methodology

3.1. Laboratory Data Collection
nWe collected laboratory data from the participants during a psychological experiment using our implementation of the Trial Stress Test (TST) [35]. The social test has been proven to be an effective way of inducing stress after the baseline condition we created at the beginning of the experiment. This experiment was conducted on 14 participants who were university students between 20 and 25. There were nine male and female participants.

The experiment, which took approximately 1 hour, had the following steps:

+   1. Setup
2. Pre-stress measurements (baseline)
3. The TST (Trier Social Stress Test) (inducing stress)
4. Post-stress recovery measurements (recovery)

The communication language between interviewers and the participants was Turkish. Please note that the mother of all participants was Turkish. In addition to that, they knew English as a foreign language. This circumstance affected stress induction.
