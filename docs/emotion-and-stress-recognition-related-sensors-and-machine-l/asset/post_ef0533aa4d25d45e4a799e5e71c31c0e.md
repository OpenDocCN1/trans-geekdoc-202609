# Activity Recognition Using Wearable Physiological Measurements: Selection of Features from a Comprehensive Literature Study

Inna Mohino-Heranz 1,*, Roberto Gil-Pita 1, Manuel Rosa-Zurera 1 and Fernando Seoane 2,3,4

- 1 Department of Signal Theory and Communications, University of Alicante, 28805 Alcaena de Madrid, Spain; roberto.gil@uah.es (R.G.-P); manuel.rosa@uah.es (M.R.-Z)
- 2 Clinical Science, Technology, Karolinska Institutet, 17177 Stockholm, Sweden; fernando.seoane@uio.se
- 3 Department Biomedical Engineering, Karolinska University Hospital, 14186 Stockholm, Sweden
- 4 Swedish School of Textiles, University of Bor, Sweden

* Correspondence: Received: 12 November 2019; Accepted: 11 December 2019; Published: 13 December 2019

**Abstract:** Activity and emotion recognition based on physiological signal processing in healthcare is a relevant field, promising future applications such as health at work or preventive care. This paper carries out a deep analysis of features proposed to extract information from electrocardiogram, thoracic impedance, and electrodermal activity. The activities analyzed are neural, emotional, and physical. A total of 33 features are used for activity recognition, performing a comprehensive study taking into consideration the accuracy, feature calculation, window length, and type of classifier. Feature selection to know the most relevant features from the complete set is implemented using a genetic algorithm with different number of features. This study allowed us to determine the best number of features avoiding over-fitting, and the best subset of features among those proposed in the literature the lowest error probability.

**Keywords:** activity recognition; physiological signals; electrocardiogram; thoracic impedance; electrodermal activity

Activity can be defined as the state or quality of being active, which implies that the activity can be emotional, intellectual, etc. Typical activity recognition systems on daily life activities such as walking, running, scrubbing [1–5], mental tasks [6,7] or emotion recognition [8]. Activity-state recognition systems can be helped to prevent human tasks in many professional activities such as first responders, crane operators or drivers. The present work aims at studying several features in the literature to characterize the signals of electrocardiogram, thoracic impedance and electrodermal activity, whose objective is to recognize four different activities: emotional, physical, and neural.

Currently, there are different methods for detecting activity. For instance, Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) [9,10] in combination with Global Positioning System (GPS) for outdoor applications [11] or sensor devices for smart homes [12] for physical activity. On the other hand, speech and gestures are used for activity detection [13–16]. Another alternative is physiological signals captured through sensors located on the body. Wearable biomedical sensing through smart cloth [17,18] allows the recording of physiological measurements such as the Electrocardiogram (ECG), the Thoracic Electrical Impedance (TEB) or the Electrodermal Activity (EDA), among others, which contain not only information about specific body functions and physiological states, but also valuable information about emotional state, mental load and physical activity [19].

In the literature, numerous works are found that these three signals are used to detect stress, emotions, and activity. For instance, ECG is affected by these factors, the heart rate is directly related to the body and mental condition [20–21]. In this sense, Heart Rate Variability (HRV) has been widely used to extract information about the status of the autonomous nervous system and emotions [23]. On the other hand, TEB can be used as an indicator of the breathing function, and it has been used in different uses for activity recognition [24] and stress detection [25]. EDA measures the activity of sweating glands on the skin which are directly controlled by the sympathetic nervous system, and can also be used for emotion recognition [26–29].

However, few papers provide studies including these three signals with the same purpose, comparing the signals under study and determining which physiological signals provide relevant information about the individual activity. For instance, the features from TEB acquired together with the ECG and the heart sound can be used to study reactivity during emotional activation [24]. Numerous features have been found for this purpose in the literature, but there is not a clear rule of which ones are relevant for a given problem. The larger the number of features, the greater the generalization problem, the less the ability to handle unseen data [31]. Selecting a subset of features remains mandatory for many activity recognition.

Taking all into account, the present paper aims at assessing the utility of features extracted from ECG, TEB, and EDA in activity recognition systems. These physiological signals have been recorded using sensorized garments with wearable instrumentation. We intend to recognize four activities: emotional activity, mental activity, physical activity, and resting. The paper is structured as follows:
- Section 1 introduces the problem tackled in this paper;
- Section 2 is a review of the literature about physiological signals, window length, and possible classifiers.
- Section 3 summarizes the sensors used to acquire the signals and the mental states that are considered;
- Section 4 presents the experiments carried out;
- Section 5 includes the results;
- Section 6 presents the main conclusions.
Appendix A–C are also included with a detailed description of the considered features extracted from the acquired signals.

## 2. Background

In this study several features have been analyzed:
(a) the physiological sensing mode (ECG, TEB and EDA),
(b) the window length,
(c) the features extracted from each signal,
(d) the number of features to obtain the best results,
(e) the type of classifier.

### Selection of physiological sensing modality.

In this, we compare the physiological signal under study and determine which physiological signals provide more information about the individual activity. The signals used are ECG, TEB, and EDA. It is possible to find numerous works in which these signals are used to detect stress, emotions, and activity in the literature. The ECG signal is used in some papers as such [23], where the obtained results suggest that positive emotions lead to activation in HRV, which may be beneficial in some illness treatment [19,31,32].
