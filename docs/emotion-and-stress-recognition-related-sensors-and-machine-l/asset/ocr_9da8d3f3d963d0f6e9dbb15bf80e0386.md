# 3. Physiological Parameter of Interest and Sensors used for Benchmarking

Here, we describe the physiological parameters we investigated, and the sensors used to measure them. We investigated three sensors and four physiological parameters (Table 1):

+   - HR: heart rate, i.e., heartbeat frequency, unit: beats per minute
- IBI: inter intervals, also known as the RR interval, the time between two R-peaks in the ECG's QRS complex, unit: milliseconds
- ECG: electrocardiogram, i.e., electrical activity of the skin, unit: volt
- GSR: galvanic skin response, i.e., the level of electric conductance of the skin, unit: microSiemens [µS]

The VariPort (http://www.bisigma.com) is a small, lightweight, and highly flexible recording system that is used for multi-channel physiological recordings in laboratory and ambulatory studies. The standard version of the device can record up to 16 signals from pre-amplifiers (e.g., electromyography, electrocardiography, electro activity, or respiration). The device has two-marker buttons that can be used to detect significant events occurring over time, resulting in an additional channel of data. We used these buttons to mark changes in the activity phases (for details refer to Section 4.1). Available sensors are either wire-connected to the device or are directly integrated into the device. The recorded data are stored on an SD card inside the device. The VariPort allows setting sampling rates for different channels, effectively reducing the required storage, especially in slowly changing signals (e.g., such as the skin conductance). For rapidly changing signals, such as ECG, sampling rates up to 1024 Hz can be set. Since the VariPort is the platform used for scientific studies at our Psychology Department, it is the gold standard for our benchmarking. In the remaining part of the paper, the VariPort sensor is called VP.



The Zephyr BioHarness 3 (https://www.zephyrany.com/) is a multivariable monitoring device with a chest belt that measures a wide variety of physiological parameters. The BH is a certified medical device (FDA Class II). Due to its design as a chest belt, the BioHarness 3 can measure ECG, RR intervals, respiration frequency, and other parameters such as 3D acceleration on a single sensor platform. Furthermore, parameters such as heart rate can be derived from the directly measured parameter, for instance, from ECG (HR within 0–240 BPM and an accuracy of ±1 BPM). The sampling rate for ECG is 250 Hz. The BH and the smartphone are connected wirelessly via Bluetooth. The raw data are accessible via a free SDK in binary format, and the device has extensively tested and validated in practical applications [41,42]. In the remaining part of the paper, the Zephyr BioHarness 3 sensor is called BH.
