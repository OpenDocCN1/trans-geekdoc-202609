It is shown in this that the alpha asymmetry of the brain can be considered a potential marker for the recognition of chronic stress in humans. We observed (Table 5) that the classification accuracy using beta and gamma oscillations was lower compared to alpha asymmetry. Whenever a combination of alpha asymmetry from beta and gamma oscillations was used, the decision boundaries changed. Due to this, the classification accuracy was lower compared to the case when alpha asymmetry was individually used as a feature. The labeling should be performed by using a hybrid method (psychology expert and PSS scores) for training the system in a supervised manner. Due to the limited size of the data, we have shown that MST is the only class of neural network-based classifier that can fit to the task of stress classification. For deeper networks, we would need more recordings of EEGs.

In this paper, two different labeling methods were used for the classification of long-term stress in humans using EEG signals. Fifty-four sensory features were analyzed for the classification of chronic stress, and alpha asymmetry was used as a discriminating factor when using expert's value as ground truth. The PSS scores, when used for labeling, returned no significant features. Furthermore, it is evident from our experimental results that SVM and LR gave the highest accuracy (85.20%) for classification. We also observed that the stress group was better classified compared to the control group irrespective of the classifiers. Finally, we established that alpha asymmetry can be used as a potential marker for the classification of long-term stress with SVM. To the best of our knowledge, no previous EEG-based studies have used a psychology expert for labeling of groups for long-term stress. In the future, more features and participants will be considered for the analysis. With the availability of more data, deep learning strategies can be applied for potentially improved methods.
