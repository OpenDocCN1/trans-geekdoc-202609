# ## 5. Experimental—Study 3

In this study, the goal is to demonstrate the robustness of our model. Therefore, we conducted an in-field study where we recorded data over a day from users performing their everyday tasks. Similar to the previous studies, the University of Auckland Human Participants Committee approved the protocol.



We recruited 10 participants (7 males and 3 females) aged 25 and 34 ($M=29.9; SD=3.4$). Among these participants, four were from the first study, another four from the second study and two were newly recruited. A requirement for our participant selection was a foot size that matched the prototype. In addition, the participant had to work in an office and spend the majority of their time in a sitting position (>70%). Apart from this, the inclusion/exclusion criteria remained similar to previous studies.



The study was conducted on a particular day that the participants expected to have some periods of acute stress. The stress tasks included working for a deadline, debugging a firmware/software, having a meeting with their supervisor, writing a paper for an upcoming submission, Having lunch/coffee with friends and having casual chats with friends were activities that possibly relaxes stress. We began at 09:45 a.m. time at the participants' office space. After explaining the study procedures, filling consent forms and collecting demographic data, the experimenter installed the participants to wear the StressFoot and E4. At 10:00 a.m., the experimenter initiated apps for data collection and asked participants to ask them to report their current stress level, energy level and how pleasant they felt on a 7-point Likert scale. Then we asked them to continue their work. As we asked participants to fill out two forms, a calendar application reporting the time and duration of tasks performed, as well as a questionnaire. The were the only tasks we asked the participants to perform hourly 8 h from 10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. To ensure the participants would remember to report and fill out the calendar and the form, they received reminders via visual and audio pop-ups at the end of each hour. The questionnaire asked the participants to rate their perceived stress level, energy level, as well as how pleasant they felt during last hour. Part of the questionnaire was to fill a NASA TLX, which was used to calculate the overall workload.
