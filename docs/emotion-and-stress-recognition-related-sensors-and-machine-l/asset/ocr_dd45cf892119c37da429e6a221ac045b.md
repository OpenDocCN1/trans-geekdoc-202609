As for the induction of emotional states, many studies focusing on basic emotions in discrete (i.e., fear, anger, joy, sadness, surprise or disgust) or dimensional (i.e., valence, arousal, dominance) models. These emotional states are especially induced using standardized pictures [32,33] for instance from the International Affective Picture System (IAPS) [34] or relying on audiovisual stimuli [35] as movie clips [36,37] or music clips. Emotional states can also be induced by game scenarios asking the user to perform a task [39]. This elicitation method is especially useful for the induction of HCI relevant emotional states as Frustration and Interest [40]. These states are relevant in designing efficient and easy-to-use interactive systems, in interactive, educational and social practices [42], or in therapeutic settings providing tailored feedback to reduce frustration states [43].

Taylor et al conducted a study to induce Frustration based on the inclusion of latency between the user's touch and the reaction of the breakout engine [44]. A more recent study on Frustration is given by Aslam et al examining the effects of any factor on HCI elements of Frustration and disappointment [45]. For the induction of frustration, the authors asked the subjects to fill in a registration form, which fails twice based on intended systems, before it succeeds in the third time. Additionally, Lisner et al designed an experiment for the induction of six emotions including classified results physiological via wearable computers and human-robot interaction, Liu et al provided a comparative study of machine learning methods using physiological signals for the recognition of five different emotions including Frustration [47].

In their article “Interest–the curious emotion”, Silva focuses on the role of Interest in learning and motivates its creative resources in cultivating knowledge and expertise, and [48]. Ree et al present a concept of Interest in three ways: as a basic emotion, and as an emotion schema [49]. They explain the importance of Interest in educational settings as a mean to motivate high-quality engagement that leads to positive learning outcomes and as an motivational and cognitive resource that leads to better performance. According to Ellsworth, Interest is related to the universality of a positive state which may also lead to curiosity and hope, while lack of control can reduce results in Frustration. The recognition of Frustration and the system reaction can reduce resignation [50]. Thus, in a HCI context, providing excitement through an appropriate degree of urgency might increase Interest, while providing a level of controllability, preventing implicit except systems can reduce frustration. The recognition of turning it into a Interest state are critical aspects for avoiding negative consequences and valuable for enhancing positive interaction effects.

Despite many studies investigating emotional and cognitive states, particularly Overload, Underload, Frustration, and Interest, the measurement still poses many challenging issues especially with respect to multimodal, mobile and temporal acquisition. Additionally, requiring the validation of the experimental design, most of the studies limit their to one subjective modality. Further, previous studies restrict their induction to either cognitive or emotional elicitation and relate both into one single dataset. In this paper we focus on these issues and present a dataset for affective computing research, based on systematic induction of cognitive load (Overload, Underload) and emotional states related to HCI (Interest, Frustration) as well as a neutral and transient state (1) designed and acquired in a mobile HCI setting, (2) based on multimodal sensor data, (3) involving temporal acquisition via three different modalities. Combining these challenging issues related to mobile, multimodal, temporal, and validated acquisition into one dataset for both cognitive and emotional states are the main contributions of this work.
