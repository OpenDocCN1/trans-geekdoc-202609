# # How Laboratory Experiments in the Wild Can Be Exploited for Monitoring Stress: A Bridge Between Laboratory and Daily Life

Yekta Said Can*, Dilamura Gokay, Dilrubra Reyyan Kılıç, Deniz Ekiz, Niaz Chalabianoo and Cem Esoy

Computer Engineering Department, Bogazici University, Bebek, 34342 Istanbul, Turkey; dilat.gokay@boun.tr (D.G.), dilrubra@boun.tr (D.R.K.), deniz.ekiziz@boun.tr (D.E.); niaz.chalabianoo@boun.tr (N.C.); eros@boun.tr (C.E.)

+   * Correspondence: yekta.can@boun.tr

Received: 3 January 2020; Accepted: 1 February 2020; Published: 4 February 2020

**Abstract:** Stress leads to poor well-being, and has effects on life quality and health. Society may have significant benefits from daily life stress detection system using unobtrusive wearable devices physiological signals. However, the performance of these systems is not accurate when used in unrestricted daily life compared to the systems tested in real-life and laboratory conditions. To test our stress level detection system that processes physiological signals, features and applies machine learning classifications, we used a laboratory experiment and ecological momentary assessment data collection with smartwatches in daily life. We investigated the effect of different labeling techniques and different training and testing environments. In the laboratory environments, we had controlled situations, and we could validate the perceived stress from self-reports. When machine learning models were developed in the laboratory instead of training them with the data coming from daily life, the accuracy of the system when tested in daily life improved significantly. The subjectivity effect coming from the self-reports in daily life be eliminated. Our system obtained high stress level detection accuracy results compared to most of the previous daily life studies.

**Keywords:** smart band; stress recognition; physiological signal processing; machine learning



Stress, an ever-growing issue in modern societies, has become an inseparable part of people's daily-lived continuously. Increasingly increasing workload, tight deadlines, and the resulting time pressure to control the increasing stress levels. Stress is an organism's reaction mechanism to stress. In a stressful state, certain control systems in the human body, as the autonomic nervous system (ANS), act unconsciously to control the responses by regulating some bodily functions. This mechanism has been constantly improved throughout human evolution, to create prompt reactions in life-threatening situations [1]. Stress symptoms can be measured and observed in numerous ways. The sympathetic nervous system (SENS) kicks the stress reaction, which will appear in the form of psychological, physiological, and behavioral interactions [1]. The bidirectional effects of the impact on the body and vice versa are also the major hallmarks of the autonomic nervous system (ANS), which has evolved in a way to have a direct role in human life and survival [2]. The autonomic nervous system (ANS) is divided into the sympathetic nervous system (SENS) and the parasympathetic nervous system (PSNS).
