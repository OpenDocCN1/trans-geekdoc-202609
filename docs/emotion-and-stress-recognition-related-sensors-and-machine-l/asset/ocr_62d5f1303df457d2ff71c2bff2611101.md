# 5.2.1. Linear Regression and Coefficient of Determination

The first statistic of interest is the coefficient of determination R², which quantifies the variance of the two given parameters that can be explained by a linear regression model. In addition to the R² of individual parameters, as shown in the scatter-plots (refer to Section 5.1, Figures 4b and 5b), we investigate all patterns among participants and explore the corresponding R² pattern. This pattern can be derived from the R² matrix shown in Figure 6. Furthermore, for each group of parameters, e.g., all IBI related parameter, we calculate the total average per participant in order to get an impression of the impact of each participant's overall measurement accuracy (GSR base level, skin contact of electrodes, etc.).

Figure 6. R² matrix of parameters and participants; detail (a) complements Figure 4, detail (b) complements Figure 5; total average of individual participants is shown in the last column, total average of individual parameter pairs is shown in the first row of each parameter group; red indicates high correlation, blue indicates low correlation.

In the upper half of the R² matrix, patterns of equal-type parameters, measured by different sensors (or derived from another of the same sensor) show a high linear relationship between the majority of the participants. This relationship also indicates that these parameters are robust when measured from a measuring point of view. However, the matrix also shows cases with no relationship at all, as for instance IBI derived from ECG BH (moving average version), IBI derived from ECG VP (moving average version). This might indicate that one of the sensors had no proper contact between the electrodes and the skin used to collect valid data.

The matrix shows that GSR in general, and IBI measured by Empatica E4, tend to have rather low or even no correlation, while some participants demonstrate the exact opposite (complete instance RP 4-11 and RP 2-5).

Note that the R² matrix in Figure 6 is organized as follows: for each group of parameters, the top row shows the highest correlation among participants, while the bottom row shows the lowest correlation among all parameters.
