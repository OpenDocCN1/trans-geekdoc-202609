First, the proposed model achieved the highest performance in all evaluation metrics, outperforming its counterparts. In detail, model 10 (the proposed ResNet50 fine-tuned using the proposed joint-cluster and weighted-softmax) achieved recognition accuracy of 60.70%. The second best (i.e., model 8) by approximately 1% in terms of recognition accuracy. As reported in [5], the average agreement between two human annotators (randomly chosen out of 12 annotators) on a part of the AffectNet data was 65.56%, which may be considered the maximum achievable recognition accuracy. This emphasizes that recognizing human emotions from facial expressions in a real-world scenario is a challenging task, and the newly proposed weighted-cluster loss function is capable of addressing challenging factors such as subtle facial appearance, head pose, illumination, and occlusion.

In terms of F1-score, the proposed model (model 10) surpassed its counterparts. In imbalanced tasks (e.g., facial recognition tasks on an unbalanced dataset like AffectNet), the weighted average of precision and recall, gives a better measure of incorrectly classified cases that the author created. In addition, model 10 also achieved the best kappa, alpha, and AUC-PR values, which outperforms most other loss functions. Similar to FER systems, this shows that our proposed FER model is effective when it comes to measuring the reliability of automated facial expression recognition in real-world scenario. It should be noted that model 8 achieves the best AUC on the positive class (High AUC) at the cost of higher false negative rate. On the other hand, the proposed model (model 10) tries to reduce the false negative rates while maintaining good performance on the positive class.

Second, under the supervision of the same losses, transferred FER models performed better than FER models that trained from scratch (i.e., models 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5; models 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10, respectively). This shows the benefit of transfer learning where the pre trained model, with the ability to learn features for face identification, can be transferred to recognize facial expressions to improve the recognition performance. The proposed model (model 10) which integrated both transfer learning and weighting achieved the highest performance.

Next, the performance of model 1 and model 6 (models using conventional softmax loss) is lower than the performance of model 5 and model 10 (models using weighted-cluster loss) by margin (50.65% and 52.22% vs. 56.27%, 60.70%). This is because the softmax loss is incapable to handle the imbalance data problem which leads to the poor performance of FER on minor classes (e.g., contempt, disgust). In contrast, the proposed weighted-cluster loss alleviated the imbalanced problem. This help improve performance of small classes. In addition, center-based models (models that were fine-tuned using joint center loss with either softmax or weighted-softmax) performed worse than the models that were fine-tuned using only softmax loss or weighted-softmax loss. The recognition accuracy of model 7 which uses softmax loss is only 46.07%, which is lower than that of model 6 (using softmax loss). Similar phenomena can be found in the case of model 9. Model 9 that using center loss with weighted-softmax loss achieves lower accuracy than model 8 that using only weighted-softmax. This shows that the existing center loss is not suitable for tracking data imbalance
