
## 3.2. Decision Fusion

In contrast to FF, from each sensor signal, a feature is extracted and used independently to train and learn a classifier, so that each modality returns a set of predicted labels. With k modalities, k classifiers will be created, k predictions per sample. The resulting predictions are combined to yield a final result, in our case, a weighted majority voting system. In this voting system, the ensemble decides the class that receives the highest number of votes taking into account all sensor modalities, and a weight (W) per modality to give the most competent classifiers a greater power for the final decision. The weights were chosen according to the classifier accuracy on the validation set. In case of a draw in the class prediction, the selection is random.

## 3.3. Classifier

To perform the classification, SL classifiers were tested: K-Nearest Neighbour (k-NN), Decision Tree (DT), Random Forest (RF); Support Vector Machines (SVM), AdaBoost (AB), Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB), and Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA). For more detail regarding these, the author refers to [48] and references therein.

A comprehensive study of these classifiers and parameter tuning was performed using 4-fold Cross (CV) to ensure a meaningful validation and avoid overfitting. The value of 4 was selected to optimize the number of iterations and the homogeneity in the classes in the training and test set, as some of the datasets used were highly imbalanced. The performing classifier was chosen using Leave-One-Out (LOO) to be incorporated into the FF and DF frameworks.

To obtain a measurable evaluation of the model performance, the following metrics are computed: Accuracy—Precision—Recall—F1-score—the harmonic mean of precision and recall [49]. Nomenclature: TP—True Positive; FP—False Positive; FN—False Negative.

### 4.2. Signal Pre-Processing

The raw data recorded from the sensors usually shows a low signal-to-noise ratio, thus, it is generally necessary to pre-process the data, namely filtering to remove motion artefacts, outliers, and further noise. Additionally, since different modalities were acquired, further filtering specifications are required according to each sensor modality. Considering what is typically found in the state-of-the-art [1], the filtering for which each modality was performed as:

- Electrocardiography (ECG): Finite impulse response (FIR) band-pass filter of 0.5 and 3-45 Hz cut-off frequency;
- Electrodermal Activity (EDA): Butterworth band-pass filter of order 4 and 0.1 Hz-0.35 Hz cut-off frequency.
- Respiration (RESP): Butterworth band-pass filter of order 4 and 1-8 Hz cut-off frequency.
- Blood Volume Pulse (BVP): Butterworth band-pass filter of order 4 and 1

After noise removal, the data was segmented into 40 s sliding windows with 75% overlap. Lastly, the data was normalised per user, by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation, to values between 0-1 to remove subjective biases.
