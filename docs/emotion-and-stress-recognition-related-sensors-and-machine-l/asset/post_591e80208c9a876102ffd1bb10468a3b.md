## 1. Stress Detection

Addressing the issue of stress as a naturally occurring psychological response, [1] proposed a new way of discriminating between acute stress and relaxation by using movement and posture characteristics of the foot. The authors used machine-learning techniques to build models that were used to assess the validity of their method based on data collected from 23 participants performing tasks that induced stress and relaxation. Data collected from an additional sample of 11 participants accuracy of 87%. External validity was also demonstrated by conducting a study with 10 participants who demonstrated the robustness of the results.

The research in [2] contributed to bridging the gap between laboratory experiment and daily life activities. The authors used a laboratory experiment and momentary assessment-based data collection with smartwatches in daily life to promote a stress detection system. The system extracts physiological signals, features and applies machine-learning techniques to classify the levels of stress. The study revealed that the accuracy of the system when tested in daily life improved significantly when machine-learning models were trained in the laboratory instead of with daily life.

In [3], regression and classification models were created for stress detection using both personal and user-independent models' experimentation. The paper used the stress detection dataset AffectiveROAD, which contained data gathered using Empatica E4 sensor and also continuous target variables—a feature that is missing in the other-stress detection dataset. The classification models used for stress detection were Random Forest and Bagged tree ensemble. From conducted experiments and using the AffectiveROAD dataset, the study revealed that regression models outperform classification models when classifying as stressed or not-stressed.

The research done in [4] revisited stress using EEG as an objective measure for cost-effective and personalized stress management in situations where mental health facilities are not available. The study conducted by the paper considered: (i) a scenario in which long-term stress was classified with machine-learning algorithms using state EEG recording and (ii) the labelling for the stress and control groups were performed using two widely accepted clinical practices: expert evaluation. Support vector machine was found by the authors to be the most suitable algorithm for long-term human stress when used with the alpha asymmetry feature.

## 2. Wearable Body Sensors

The main objective of [5] was to study electroencephalography (EEG) and galvanic response (GSR) together for boredom classification, with the objective of using the potential features of the associated data for these features on emotion classification. The authors investigated the combined effect of these features on boredom classification by: (i) collecting EEG and GSR data from 28 participants using off-the-shelf sensors; (ii) labelling the collected samples using the participants' questionnaire-based testimonies of the various boredom levels experienced; (iii) using the collected data to initially train 30 models with 19 machine-learning algorithms and select the top three candidate classifiers and (iv) tuning the hyperparameters and validating the final models through 1000 iterations of 10-fold cross validation to ensure the robustness of the test results. The work revealed the efficiency of multipilayer perceptrons compared to machine learning. It also showed the correlation between boredom and the connected features of EEG and GSR.

The research in [6] addressed the issues of features extraction from Electroencephalography (EEG) signals and emotional aspects by considering both intra-subject and inter-subject approaches to EEG-based affect detection. Using three public repositories, the paper analysed both modelling approaches and showed the subject's influence on the EEG signals is significantly higher than that of the emotion, (i) the subject's influence on the EEG signals should be accounted for and (ii) a data transformation that seamlessly integrates individuals into an inter-subject approach should be performed to improve the classification process.

In [7], the authors suggested a classification method for detecting stressed states based on electrocardiogram (EEG) and a method for training a neural network (DNN) with a smaller dataset. The work built an end-to-end architecture to detect stress using EEG, using a multistage architecture that includes convolutional layers. Two kinds of datasets were used to train and validate the model, which were: a dataset and a smaller mental arithmetic. A transfer learning method was then used to train the proposed model with a small dataset. It is shown in the paper that (i) based on receiver operating curves, the proposed model performs better than conventional methods and (ii) compared with other DNN-based methods. The results revealed that the transfer learning method improves accuracy.

The issue of recognizing mental stress with EEG-recognition network was addressed by proposing a new stress-detection algorithm that uses multiple physiological signals, such as electrocardiogram (EEG) and respiration (RESP) signals to achieve end-to-end deep learning in [8]. The study mimicked workplace stress by using tasks as stressors, with each stress followed by relaxation task(s). It also provided experimental results demonstrating its superiority over conventional machine-learning models.

The authors in [9] focused on the field readiness of low-cost wearable devices, which are increasingly used in research as well as personal and private purposes. The goal was to evaluate the accuracy of these devices in comparison to well-calibrated, high-quality devices used in laboratories for physiological and medical research. The study demonstrated an approach for quantification of the accuracy of low-cost wearables in comparison to high-quality laboratory devices providing freedom for physiological measurements. The benchmark covered the entire workflow from sensor data acquisition to interpretation of correlation and similarity. The study showed that the benchmarked wearables provide physiological measurements, such as heart rate and heartbeat interval, with an accuracy close to those of the professional/higend sensors. It also revealed that accuracy varied more for parameters such as galvanic skin responses.

In [10], the issue of patient monitoring was revisited with the perspective of developing a wearable device with low cost, single channel, dry and suitable for in-ear EEG for nonintrus monitoring. The paper covered all aspects of the designs, engineering and permitting. By applying machine learning for emotion classification, it was revealed that the proposed device was able to classify basic emotion with results that were comparable to those measured from the more conventional EEG headsets at T and T8 scale positions.

In [11], a deep analysis of features presented to extract information from the electrocardiogram, thoracic electrical bioimpedance and electrodermal signals was carried out a focus on activities such as neutral, emotional, mental and physical. The study tested of 533 features for activity recognition. A comprehensive study was then taking into consideration the prediction accuracy, feature calculation, window length and type of classifier. This study enabled the determination of the ideal number of features and the best subset of features among those presented.

## 3. Dermatological Sensors

The association between the stress and road types was associated in [12], the study used electrodermal activity (EDA) for 60 min and over 2-day period. Two separate models were used to classify the levels of stress (low/high). The classification performance of ER benchmarking datasets in the arousal/valence space showed that traffic conditions and road types were important.

The work done in [13] introduced the issue of Active and Living environments for elderly/disabled people and the subjectivity of results using a machine-learning model on a specific group of people. The study used Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture to provide robustness-related results, revealing that a robust classification of human emotion was possible even without involving physiological signals.

The research in [14] presented the identification of the level of arousal in older people by monitoring their electrodermal activity (EDA) through a commercial device. The objective was to use the influence of a musical genre on emotional induction in order to recognize arousal and hence create future therapies that can help improve their mood. This can ultimately contribute to the reduction of depression and anxiety. Using methods based on the process of decoding the EDA signal, two different studies were carried out, the first being a purely statistical study based on the search for statistically significant differences for series of temporal, morphological, statistical and frequency features of the processed signals. The second study was a machine-learning using a range of classifiers to analyze the possible correlations between the level of arousal basically to the participants' responses. While the first study showed that Flamenco and Spanish Folklore presented the highest number of statistically significant parameters, the second study showed that the best classifiers were support vector machines, with high accuracy for Flamenco and bulky, followed by K-nearest neighbors.

Motivated by the limitations of emotion recognition systems in terms of lack of systematic analysis regarding the selection of classifiers to use, sensor modalities, range and expected accuracy, and many others, the works in [15] contributed to the body of work in machine learning by presenting a systematic study across five public datasets used in Emotion Recognition (ER) with the objective of evaluating de-identification through Supervised Learning (SL). The work considered: (i) Classification performance analysis of ER benchmarking datasets in the arousal/valence space; (ii) Summarising the ranges of the classification accuracy reported across the existing literature.
