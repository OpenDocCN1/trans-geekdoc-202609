Numerous studies have analyzed brain activity under stress conditions, which are induced by a task such as impromptu speech, examination, mental task, public speaking, and the cold press test [38–43]. These studies evaluate short-term stress using EEG, whereas the classification of long-term stress using EEG has not been widely investigated. In Table 7, studies involving EEG to classify human stress are present for comparison. It is observed that different stress-inducing tasks were used such as driving simulation, examination, and mental arithmetic tasks. Specialized instruments like MIST and Stroop tests were also used to induce stress. For chronic stress there could be stressors that affect the physical, emotional, or behavioral being of a human being. Therefore, it is proposed that recording state EEG for stress classification is a better choice without inducing stress. The number of participants in such studies varied from 5 to 42. The SVM and NB were used as classifiers in most of them. SVM was found to be the most efficient classifier, with a maximum accuracy of 90%, stress was induced by mental arithmetic task. In [10], the resting EEG was recorded for five minutes and a linear analysis was performed but no classification algorithm. In [4], chronic stress was classified with an accuracy of 90% using EEG recordings from eight electrodes and stress-inducing condition.

The difficulties of using EEG in stress studies, where the use of EEG is vital and has a clear meaning when mental stress originates in the brain. Furthermore, we used long-term stress system, and we did not have any stress inducer in our study (unlike other EEG- and HRV-based studies); EEG, can be a modality of choice for our experiments and we show its effectiveness with our experimental results. Although EEG has not been widely used for long-term stress classification in clinical practice, our proposed method attempts to establish this approach. It has been shown that conditions such as anxiety, tension, and depression decrease as frontal asymmetry shifts to the right hemisphere of the brain gaining significance to EEG later [22]. It was demonstrated that variations in the beta activity [31] and pre-frontal gamma [34] contribute to stress assessment. Hence there is evidence suggesting that these oscillations in the pre-frontal brain region can be used for assessment of stress using EEG recordings.
