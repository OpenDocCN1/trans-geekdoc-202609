# 3.1. Participants

The participants had to be at least 18 years of age, English speaking and able to provide consent. In addition, participants needed to have a foot size of UK 9-10 to match the size of the prototype. Participants with prolonged foot-related injuries were excluded. We recruited 23 healthy participants (12 Males and 11 Females) aged between 21-32 years old (M =26.4, SD = 3.17).

# 3.2. Apparatus

To collect the data, we used the StressProt features [85] the Empica E4 wristband was used to collect the participant’s EDA. All the study tasks were executed and generalised on a 13” Macbook Pro, which was connected to an extended LED monitor (see Figure 4).

# 3.3. Procedure & Tasks

After explaining the aim of the study, participants were required to fill out consent forms. Participants were informed that the study aims to explore behavioural changes while performing tasks which induce stress and relaxation. We collected demographic data and asked the participant to wear the wearables. The participants were asked to sit in-front of the Macbook Pro with an extended display as shown in Figure 4. We allowed the user to adjust the chair and the distance of the table according to their personal preference. During the study, they were allowed to cross their legs. However, them not to take the foot out. Once the setup was complete and the user was comfortable, we asked them to complete four tasks. The first, the second and fourth tasks intended to create relaxation. The order remained consistent for all participants. The participant had to fill a questionnaire. On a 7-point Likert scale, the users had to rate their perceived stress level, energy level and how pleasant they found the task. A NASA Task Load (NASA TLX) [86] was conducted to calculate the overall workload per task.

# 3.3.1. Task 1 [Stroop]: Stroop Color and Word

The Stroop color and word test is a common stress test (Stroop Test) [87] in many previous works [10,88,89]. For our study, we customised an open source Matlab-based Stroop Tool to display four tasks (“Red”, “Magenta”, “Green” and “Blue”) on the screen. Participants were de-facto asked to type the first letter of the colour (‘R’, ‘M’, ‘G’, ‘B’). After each answer, the program provides the result by displaying the word “correct” or “incorrect”.
