
# EEG Based Classification of Long-Term Stress Using Psychological Labeling

**Abstract:** Stress research is a rapidly emerging area in field of electroencephalography (EEG) signal processing. The use of EEG as an objective measure for cost effective and personalized stress management becomes important in situations like nonavailability of mental health factors. In this study, long-term stress was classified with machine learning algorithms using resting EEG signals. The labeling for the stress and control groups was performed using two currently accepted practical practices: (i) the perceived scale score and (ii) expert evaluation. Domain features were extracted from five-channel EEG recordings in addition to the frontal temporal alpha and beta asymmetries. The asymmetry was computed from four channels and used as a feature. Feature selection was also performed to identify statistically significant features to classify long-term human stress when used alpha asymmetry as a feature. We found that support vector machine was best for classifying both stress and control groups (i-test). It was observed that the expert evaluation-based labeling method improved the classification accuracy up to 85.20%. Based on these results, it is concluded that alpha asymmetry may be used as a biomarker for stress classification, when labels are assigned using expert evaluation.

**Keywords:** long-term stress; electroencephalography; machine learning; perceived stress scale; expert evaluation

The response of the human body to a demand for change is considered as stress [1]. A balance exists between the sympathetic and parasympathetic arms of the autonomic nervous system in human people. A fight-or-flight response is invoked when there is a threat to survival. Daily stress routine does not only damage life, however, the fight-or-flight response may still be invoked. A persistence of this short-term stress for a longer duration can cause lasting effects on the neurology of an individual and may lead to depression [2]. Long-term stress is a better predictor of depressive symptoms as compared to short-term stress [3]. Long-term stress is a risk factor for many health conditions such as cardiovascular diseases [4,5].

The prevention of depression requires a timely detection of long-term stress symptoms. Conventional methods and analysis of hormones such as cortisol and alpha-amylase are widely used in long-term stress studies [6]. These are practical but they are affected by various factors, such as language and objectivity. For instance, the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS) is a widely used questionnaire to measure the level of chronic stress, validated extensively diverse samples [7]. Though in general, a self-administered checklist cannot equal the precision of an interviewer trained to elicit events of critical to examine stress. Such interviews have shown to provide substantially better information in comparison to relatively unassisted self-reporting mechanisms [8]. Respondents have been found to report minor or positive events in response to questions designed to elicit negative and undesirable events [9].

Psychological methods alone are not enough to assess stress-related conditions [10]. Stress can be quantified objectively by bio-markers like electroencephalography (EEG), galvanic skin response, and electrodermal activity [11]. Recently, wearable systems were developed that can record electro-physiological signals (such as EEG and heart rate variability) to detect stress [12]. EEG is one of the most common source of information for studying brain function [13–17]. The oscillations generated by the variation of electric potential in the brain are recorded using low resistance electrodes placed on the scalp [18]. It is a widely used noninvasive method for its excellent temporal resolution, ease of use, and low cost. EEG signals are categorized by their frequency bands including delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma. Each frequency band can be used as a discriminating feature for different brain states [19]. There are methods reported to quantify human stress in response to induced stressors using EEG recordings. In comparison, the classification of long-term or chronic stress EEG has not been widely used.

## Our Contributions

In this study, the problem of long-term human stress is recognized by using labels and expert evaluation, which has not been explored before. We hypothesized that wearable sensors (such as EEG for recording activity using EEG electrodes) could be used for identifying chronic stress, without inducing a stimulus. Thus, our experiments involving a psychology expert for labeling stress and chronic stress is beneficial for such classification. It is important to note that we did not use any stimulus in our study to detect stress that can be administered for detecting stress in daily life routine. Two groups of participants were included the stress group and the control group. A total of forty five features were extracted from EEG signals in frequency domain to classify these two groups. Discriminating features were selected using a statistical significance test. Five machine learning classifiers including SVM, Naive Bayes (NB), KNN, logistic regression (LR), and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) were used to classify human stress using the selected features. Due to limitations, we used the alpha asymmetry as a feature.
