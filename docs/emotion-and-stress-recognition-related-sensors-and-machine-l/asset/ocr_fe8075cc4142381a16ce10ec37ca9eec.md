Many research to recognize facial emotion by contact-free strategies have proposed automatic methods for both face and ROI detection over visual and thermal images, as constructing an effective face representation from images is a crucial step for successful action analysis, in order to recognize facial emotions. In this field, there is the Facial Action Coding System (FACS), which is a taxonomy of human facial expressions to facilitate human annotation of facial behaviour [9,14,33]. For instance, a total of 32 facial muscle movements, termed Action Units (AUs), and 14 other descriptors to mention possible actions are specified, which are used by automatic methods to locate facial landmarks and ROIs. These regions are used based on geometric [9,10,15] and appearance features to discriminate emotions [9,14]. Appearance features use textural information by considering the intensity of the pixels, whereas geometric representations ignore texture and describe shape [9,15]. Here, we review of the state-of-the-artaches focuses on appearance features on the face, which are generally computed by dividing the face region into regular grid (holistic representation). Appearance features can be encoded to encode low-level or high-level information. For example, low-level information can be encoded through low-level histograms that are computationally simple and ideal for real-time applications.

In the authors use the Haar classifier for face detection, which is widely applied, due to its high accuracy and real time performance [32]. They extracted appearance features from the global face region by using Local Binary Pattern (LBP) that take care of min changes of facial expression for different emotions [9,34], followed by Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction, to improve the speed of computation in real time during six emotions (anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, and surprise).
