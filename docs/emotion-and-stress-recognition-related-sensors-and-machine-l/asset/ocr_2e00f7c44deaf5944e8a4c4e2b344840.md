The research in [6] addressed the issues of features extraction from Electroencephalography (EEG) signals and emotional aspects by considering both intra-subject and inter-subject approaches to EEG-based affect detection. Using three public repositories, the paper analysed both modelling approaches and showed the subject's influence on the EEG signals is significantly higher than that of the emotion, (i) the subject's influence on the EEG signals should be accounted for and (ii) a data transformation that seamlessly integrates individuals into an inter-subject approach should be performed to improve the classification process.

In [7], the authors suggested a classification method for detecting stressed states based on electrocardiogram (EEG) and a method for training a neural network (DNN) with a smaller dataset. The work built an end-to-end architecture to detect stress using EEG, using a multistage architecture that includes convolutional layers. Two kinds of datasets were used to train and validate the model, which were: a dataset and a smaller mental arithmetic. A transfer learning method was then used to train the proposed model with a small dataset. It is shown in the paper that (i) based on receiver operating curves, the proposed model performs better than conventional methods and (ii) compared with other DNN-based methods. The results revealed that the transfer learning method improves accuracy.

The issue of recognizing mental stress with EEG-recognition network was addressed by proposing a new stress-detection algorithm that uses multiple physiological signals, such as electrocardiogram (EEG) and respiration (RESP) signals to achieve end-to-end deep learning in [8]. The study mimicked workplace stress by using tasks as stressors, with each stress followed by relaxation task(s). It also provided experimental results demonstrating its superiority over conventional machine-learning models.

The authors in [9] focused on the field readiness of low-cost wearable devices, which are increasingly used in research as well as personal and private purposes. The goal was to evaluate the accuracy of these devices in comparison to well-calibrated, high-quality devices used in laboratories for physiological and medical research. The study demonstrated an approach for quantification of the accuracy of low-cost wearables in comparison to high-quality laboratory devices providing freedom for physiological measurements. The benchmark covered the entire workflow from sensor data acquisition to interpretation of correlation and similarity. The study showed that the benchmarked wearables provide physiological measurements, such as heart rate and heartbeat interval, with an accuracy close to those of the professional/highend sensors. It also revealed that accuracy varied more for parameters such as galvanic skin responses.

In [10], the issue of patient monitoring was revisited with the perspective of developing a wearable device with low cost, single channel, dry and suitable for in-ear EEG for nonintrus monitoring. The paper covered all aspects of the designs, engineering and permitting. By applying machine learning for emotion classification, it was revealed that the proposed device was able to classify basic emotion with results that were comparable to those measured from the more conventional EEG headsets at T and T8 scale positions.
