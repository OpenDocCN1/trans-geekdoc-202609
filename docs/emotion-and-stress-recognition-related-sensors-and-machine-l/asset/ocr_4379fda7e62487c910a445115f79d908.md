To calculate the DE, RASM, and DASM features, we use the formulae proposed by Zheng et al. [4], which are replicated in Equations (3)-(5).

```
DE = \frac{1}{2} \log 2\pi e\sigma^2,
```

```
DASM = DE_{(left)} - DE_{(right)},
```

```
RASM = DE_{(left)} / DE_{(right)}.
```

In Equation (3), $\sigma^2$ is the variance of the bandpass-filtered EEG data of each frequency band, and $\pi$ and $e$ are constants. Furthermore, as we chose, DASM and RASM are based on DE and utilize the electrical current asymmetry between the electrodes of the EEG sensors. In this, we use the asymmetry between the electrodes and the values from each electrode at the same time to train classification models.

As explained in Section 4.1, we set the window size to 7 for feature extraction. Therefore, 70 units of ABP data and 70 units of NABP data of each frequency band, and 1540 units of EEG data of each electrode were extracted from each participant. We calculated the average and the standard deviation for 70 units of ABP and NABP and used the results as features. In calculation of DE, we used all 1540 units of EEG data. In more detail, we applied the bandpass filter on EEG data. Regarding the frequency range filtering, we filtered the frequency bands of our EEG sensor (Section 3.2).

As a result of feature extraction, we secured 40 features from ABP and NABP (five frequency bands times two electrodes times statistics). Moreover, 10 features were extracted from DE (five frequency bands times two electrodes). Finally, as indicated by Equations (4) and (5), RASM and DASM utilize local symmetry for each electrode and DE value. Thus, five features were secured from RASM and DASM (five frequency bands).



As mentioned in Section 3.2, we used MV and SR features for the GSR sensor. Additionally, normalization of MV with feature scaling was also performed (see Equation (2)). As a result, normalized MV (NMV), and SR were secured from the GSR data. Similar to the feature extraction of the EEG data, we calculated the average and the standard deviation for each feature of the GSR data and used them as the final values. Consequently, six features in total were secured from the GSR data.

In this study, we considered a wide range of machine learning algorithms secured by Weka as candidate algorithms. These candidates were used for initial testing to select the best algorithms for hyperparameter tuning.
