Alongside handcrafted feature-based approaches, several techniques based on deep neural networks have also been proposed for the assessment of pain induced facial expressions. Such approaches are generalised by the joint extraction of relevant descriptors (from the preprocessed raw data) and optimisation of an inference model, based on neural networks in an end-to-end manner. In [36–38], the authors propose a hybrid neural network pain detection architecture by the combination of a feature embedding network consisting of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) [39] with a Short-Term Memory [40] Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), to enhance detection of spatial and temporal aspects of pain facial expressions in videos. [41] propose similar approach by combining a CNN with a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), and using a Variable-State Conditional Random Field (VRS-CRF) [42] instead of a similar approach in [36,37], however, this feature embedding CNN is coupled to two distinct LSTM networks. The outputs of the LSTM networks are further combined to perform classification of the pain intensities in videos. Furthermore, Zhou [44] propose a Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (RCNN) [45] architecture for the continuous expression of pain intensity in videos at the frame-levels, where Wang [46] propose a transfer learning approach, consisting of the regularisation of a feature network, which is subsequently applied to a pain intensity regression task.

The current work focuses on the analysis of facial expressions for the discrimination of neutral, low and high levels of nociceive pain in videos. Thereby, an end-to-end hybrid neural network generalised by the integration of spatial and temporal information at both the representational level of the input data (OFI and MHI) and the structural level of the proposed architecture (hybrid CNN-LSTM) [47] are integrated into the proposed architecture to generate an aggregated representation of the input data based on estimation of the representativeness of each input frame, with the corresponding level of nociceive pain. An extensive assessment of the proposed architecture is performed on both BioVid Heat Pain Database (Part A) [4] and SenseEmotionDatabase [6].

# 3. Proposed Approach

A video scene can be characterised by its spatial and temporal components. The spatial component represents the appearance (i.e., texture, shape and form) of each face, whereas the temporal component represents the perceived motion over consecutive frames due to dynamic changes of the character's appearance through time. Most of the deep neural networks designed for the assessment of pain-related facial expressions generate spatio-temporal descriptors of the input data in two stages: firstly an embedding network (which is commonly a pre-trained CNN) extracts appearance descriptors from the individual input frames (which are greyscale or colour), and a recurrent neural network is subsequently used for the integration of the input's temporal aspect based on sequences of previously extracted appearance features, generating spatio-temporal representations of video sequences for classification or regression tasks. Therefore, both the temporal and spatial aspects of video sequences are integrated uniquely at the structural level (e.g., the architecture of the neural network) of such sequences. The current approach extends this technique by specifically integrating motion information at the representational level (e.g., input data) of the architecture throughout sequences of motion history images [1] and optical flow images [2].
