# Dilated Skip Convolution for Facial Landmark Detection

**Seyha Chim, Jin-Gu Lee and Ho-Yun Park \***

School of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Chung-Ang University, 84 Heukseokro, Dongiak-gu, Seoul 06974, Korea; sehyachim@cau.ac.kr (S.C.); dlwism21@cau.ac.kr (J-G.L)

* Correspondence: hoyun@cau.ac.kr; Tel: +82-10-3354-9180

Received: 26 September 2019; Accepted: 22 December 2019; Published: 4 December 2019

**Abstract:** Facial landmark detection has gained enormous interest for face-related applications due to its success in facial analysis tasks such as facial recognition, cartoon generation, face tracking and facial expression analysis. Many studies have been proposed and implemented to deal with the challenges of localizing facial landmarks from given images, including large appearance variations and partial occlusion. Studies have differed in the way they use the facial appearances and shape information of images. In our work, we consider facial information within both global and local contexts. We aim to obtain pixel-level accuracy for localizing spatial relationships between each key point in the first stage and integrate global-context information in the second stage. The pipeline of our architecture consists of two main components: (1) a network for local-context subnet that generates features via fully convolution filters; (2) a dilated skip subnet—a combination of dilated convolutions and skip-connections—that are in charge of the appearance. Through this proposed architecture, we demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging datasets—including LFPW, HELEN, 300W and AFLW—by leveraging fully convolutional networks, skip connections without further post-processing.

**Keywords:** face landmark detection; fully convolutional networks; skip connections; dilated convolutions

In computer vision, facial landmark detection is known as alignment and is a crucial part of face recognition. Algorithms attempt to predict facial landmark coordinates that vary owing to head movements and facial expressions. These landmarks are located at major parts of the face, such as the contours of the nose, eyes, and corners of the mouth. In review, facial landmark detection has sparked much interest as it is a prerequisite for many vision applications, including facial recognition [2], face tracking [3,4], face modelling [6] and human-computer interactions [7]. In recent years, considerable research works [8–10] have developed networks to predict facial landmark location more accurately under challenging conditions, such as large appearance variations, occlusion and difficult illumination. Facial landmark detection is classified into three types: holistic, constrained local model (CLM), and regression-based. Among these, regression-based approaches [5–11] have demonstrated superiority in efficiency and accuracy even in challenging scenarios. Regression-based methods contain two stages: early and updated. The key points are located on the face in the early stage and gradually refined in the updated stage. This points out two main issues of this approach. Commonly, the face is initially determined by a bounding box. In the case it fails to detect the face, accuracy declines. Another issue is that algorithms apply a fixed number of predictions, so it is impossible to judge the quality of landmark prediction and adapt to different image tests.

Before the success of deep learning [9–12] for computer vision problems, scale-invariant transformation (SIFT) was used to learn criteria for models. The algorithm iteratively refines models until convergence is reached. Recently, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) have dominated the field of facial landmark detection. Deep learning models have been used to outperform SIFT methods, which used hand-crafted features. In contrast, SIFT is not robust to non-linear transformations and is unsuitable for face data with large shape variations. Consequently, deep learning has attracted more attention than SIFT for applications. In research [15], a probabilistic model for facial landmark detection captured shape variations by poses and expressions. Also, [16] proposed to extract independent deep features from fully convolutional networks (FCNs) and refine landmark locations by recurrent attention-refinement (RAR) networks. In the early stage, the network employed deep methods to regress key points directly on images that are highly non-linear and difficult to estimate.

The research in [17] argued that learning to extract extractive features from images yields more advantages over direct mapping. It applies a method based on heatmap regression on individual points and mentions that adding several large convolutions (e.g., 3×3 kernel convolution) would improve performance, although this increases the number of parameters and makes optimizations difficult. To address this problem, [18] proposed dilated convolutions to increase the effective receptive fields without additional parameters. However, applying heatmap regression methods in a network of large convolutional enhances the performance. We propose an end-to-end model that leverages Dense networks (DenseNet) [19] that use heatmap regression to learn deep feature maps from the image. Moreover, inspired by [18], we carefully designed a network that can extract more complex dependencies by building skip-connections in the stacked dilated convolutions. In doing so, we expect that our network will obtain different sizes of receptive fields and informative feature maps, which will boost prediction accuracy.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:

- To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to perform DenseNet as local detector with heatmap regression to predict dense heatmaps from the image.
- We designed a dilated skip connection (DSC) network that can refine the estimated heatmaps of the key points by combining a stack of dilated convolutions and a skip-connection method.
- We developed a robust method to estimate the initial facial shape to work in challenging conditions.
- We evaluated our framework's performance with other state-of-the-art networks on LFPW [20], HELEN [21], 300W [22] and AFLW3D [23] datasets.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. First, a summary of our paper is given in Section 2. Next, we detail our proposed methodology in Section 3. Then, the results of our experiments are presented in Section 4. Finally, the conclusions are drawn.

## 2. Related Works

Facial landmark detection is divided into three types of global model: holistic, constrained local model (CLM) and regression-based. Holistic methods build a global model to learn the facial appearance and obtain shape information during training to estimate the best fits of any given test image via the model parameters. CLM methods use independent local appearance information around each landmark with a global face shape for facial landmark detection, outperforming holistic methods for capturing illumination and occlusion. Unlike the first two methods, which build a global shape model, regression-based methods directly map the facial appearance and regress the landmark locations between individual inputs and outputs.

Regression-based methods have recently demonstrated outstanding performance compared with holistic and CLM methods. Regression-based methods effectively build a parametric space or approach model to extract feature maps from an image and infer a shape. Regression functions focus on holistic picture details, subsequently updating these features using finer image details to provide more accurate predictions. Using typical approaches, [5,11] proposed a regression function to predict landmark coordinates from shape feature maps from the input. Subsequently, [24] proposed a combined regression network to initially detect facial landmarks and refine landmark locations using progressively finer details; [25] proposed a cascade autoencoder network to produce finer images from low resolution input images; [26] proposed multiple regressions to learn discriminative features from each facial landmark. Extending this work, [27] proposed a two-step facial segmentation to estimate head pose, gender and expression. The system first segmented faces into small regions for hair, skin, eyes, mouth and so forth; then classified these regions using support vector machines (SVMs). The [27] process is effectively an extended version of the FACSEG dataset [3] represented images in the spatial domain with high time-frequency resolution. Then they extracted useful feature maps from the decomposed image and employed supervised learning to classify expressions in the images. Ref [3] applied stationary wavelet entropy to extract features in the frequency domain followed by a single layer feedforward network using the gradient-free optimizer. Similarly, [4] proposed bidirectional wavelet to extract multi-scale information and fuzzy SVM classifiers. Heatmap regression was used to estimate human pose; [29,30] and detect facial landmarks; [8–10]. Ref [29] employed multiple regressions to predict human poses. The regressor crops the input to focus only on the human, requiring computational resources for background accuracy. This only reduces the number of parameters but also increases accuracy since there is no information loss compared with using pooling layers to reduce data size. Ref [30] proposed a stacked hourglass network to capture information from local to global scale and enable the network to learn shape relationships between joints.
