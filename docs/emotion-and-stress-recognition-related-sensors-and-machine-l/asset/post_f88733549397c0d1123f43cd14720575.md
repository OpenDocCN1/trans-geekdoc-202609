## 4.5. Classification

We performed a comprehensive set of experiments to test and validate our proposed model using five classifiers, KNN, SVM, LR, and MLP. These classifiers were used with alpha asymmetry, beta, and gamma waves from channel AF3 as features to classify long-term stress. Each combination of the selected features was analyzed with each of the classifiers. The results of these classifiers in terms of average accuracy are shown in Table 5. We used 10-fold validation in our experiments since our dataset was limited, where in each fold 90% of the data were used for training and 10% for testing and reporting the average values of parameters across all 10 folds. The hyper parameters for classifiers used in our experiment were chosen using a grid search.

We observed that the classifier accuracy was high whenever alpha asymmetry was either as a single feature or in combination with other features. The SVM- and LR-based classifiers gave the highest accuracy when alpha asymmetry was used as a feature. The performance evaluation parameters for these classifiers are given in Table 6. We also observed that SVM and LR have very similar values for kappa statistic and F-measure. SVM may have a slightly lesser absolute error of 0.15 than that of logistic regression with a value of 0.22, LR has RMAE of 0.36 than that of SVM i.e., 0.38. The overall classification accuracy of both classifier is similar. Overall, we concluded that SVM may be the better choice for an asymmetry for system recognition.

Numerous studies have analyzed brain activity under stress conditions, which are induced by a task such as impromptu speech, examination, mental task, public speaking, and the cold press test [38–43]. These studies evaluate short-term stress using EEG, whereas the classification of long-term stress using EEG has not been widely investigated. In Table 7, studies involving EEG to classify human stress are present for comparison. It is observed that different stress-inducing tasks were used such as driving simulation, examination, and mental arithmetic tasks. Specialized instruments like MIST and Stroop tests were also used to induce stress. For chronic stress there could be stressors that affect the physical, emotional, or behavioral being of a human being. Therefore, it is proposed that recording state EEG for stress classification is a better choice without inducing stress. The number of participants in such studies varied from 5 to 42. The SVM and NB were used as classifiers in most of them. SVM was found to be the most efficient classifier, with a maximum accuracy of 90%, stress was induced by mental arithmetic task. In [10], the resting EEG was recorded for five minutes and a linear analysis was performed but no classification algorithm. In [4], chronic stress was classified with an accuracy of 90% using EEG recordings from eight electrodes and stress-inducing condition.

The difficulties of using EEG in stress studies, where the use of EEG is vital and has a clear meaning when mental stress originates in the brain. Furthermore, we used long-term stress system, and we did not have any stress inducer in our study (unlike other EEG- and HRV-based studies); EEG, can be a modality of choice for our experiments and we show its effectiveness with our experimental results. Although EEG has not been widely used for long-term stress classification in clinical practice, our proposed method attempts to establish this approach. It has been shown that conditions such as anxiety, tension, and depression decrease as frontal asymmetry shifts to the right hemisphere of the brain gaining significance to EEG later [22]. It was demonstrated that variations in the beta activity [31] and pre-frontal gamma [34] contribute to stress assessment. Hence there is evidence suggesting that these oscillations in the pre-frontal brain region can be used for assessment of stress using EEG recordings.

It is shown in this that the alpha asymmetry of the brain can be considered a potential marker for the recognition of chronic stress in humans. We observed (Table 5) that the classification accuracy using beta and gamma oscillations was lower compared to alpha asymmetry. Whenever a combination of alpha asymmetry from beta and gamma oscillations was used, the decision boundaries changed. Due to this, the classification accuracy was lower compared to the case when alpha asymmetry was individually used as a feature. The labeling should be performed by using a hybrid method (psychology expert and PSS scores) for training the system in a supervised manner. Due to the limited size of the data, we have shown that MST is the only class of neural network-based classifier that can fit to the task of stress classification. For deeper networks, we need more data.

In this paper, two different labeling methods were used for the classification of long-term stress in humans using EEG signals. Fifty-four features were analyzed for chronic stress, and alpha asymmetry was used as a discriminating factor. PSS scores, when used for labeling, returned no significant features. Furthermore, it is evident from our experimental results that SVM and LR gave the highest accuracy (85.20%) for classification. We also observed that the stress group was better compared to the control group irrespective of the classifiers. Finally, we established that alpha asymmetry can be used as a potential marker for SVM. In the future, more features and participants will be considered. With the availability of more, deep learning strategies can be applied.
