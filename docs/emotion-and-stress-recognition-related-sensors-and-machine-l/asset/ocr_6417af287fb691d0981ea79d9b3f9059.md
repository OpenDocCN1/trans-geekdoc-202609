cases, the VP time series were “leading”, which may indicate that the response characteristics of the VP
sensors are more sensitive compared to the others.

Measuring the strength of association between pairs of physiological parameters was of particular
interest. Here, we contrasted the coefficient of determination R² with the MIC (Figures 10 and 11).
In words, we confronted a statistic that measures linear relationships with a statistic
measures all types of functional relationships, including linear ones, and thereby classified
the relationship as ‘false linear’, ‘true linear’, or ‘functional but not linear’. The results are outstanding;
on the, some related examples have been confirmed by a purely
data-driven approach (for instance, relationships between IBI and VLF, LF, HF); on the other hand,
so, relationships that were expected to be linear are in fact not linear or functional. For instance,
the relationships between GSR measured by E4 and GSR measured by VP (both filtered and moving
averaged versions).

# ## 7. Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we performed a benchmark of two wearable physiological sensors (Zephyr BioHarness 3
and Empatica E4) by comparing their measurements (heart rate, inter-interval, and galvanic skin
s, and derived heart rate variability parameters) to highly-calibrated high-end professional
equ. In our study, we used the measurements from 18 participants to compare the correlations
(Pearson’s r), cross-correlations at different temporal lags from –15 to +15 sec, the (sub-)linearity
of functional dependencies (MIC), the difference of two measurement time series with respect to
geometric structure (Fretchet distance), local time series similarities (moving window), and time series
similarity with respect to their temporal alignment (DTW).

The results of our study show that the cardiovascular parameters yield very high similarities
the low-cost wearable and the calibrated professionals. Although cardiovascular
parameters are simple to measure (technically and professionally-wise), the similarities are
remarkable. For GSR, our experiment resulted in lower similarities, which may be caused by a
factors like different measurement methods, different placement of the sensors (hand palm vs. wrist),
conductance characteristics between surface (use of electrolyte gel or not), and others.
it Should be noted that the use of tonic electrolyte gel is a scientific standard for measurement
of electro activity [52] and we used the Variport GSR measure but not the other devices.

We demonstrated that our methodological approach to quantify insights into the relationships
both the individual and the aggregated level can enable different points of view on the same data and thus more
holistic interpretation for the benchmark of physiological sensors. Our research contributes to
she holistic interpretation in ways: the only the essential
one are presented in this paper 1) the confrontation of the coefficient of determination R²
the Maximal Information Coefficient MIC, in particular, the classification of non-linear correlations,\sand the quantification of the signals’ temporal and geometric similarity based on well-established
distance metrics (DTW and Fretchet distance).

Our future work will focus on two main research challenges. First, to continue fine-tuning
the methodology and integrate additional similarities, for instance, the Time Warp Distance
(TWED) [53]. Second, to evaluate the transferability of the methodology to other
 benchmarking challenges, not necessarily physiological measurements. In the long run we
to expand the methodology to the geospatial domain, i.e., integrating the location in addition
to the timestamp and the measurement of mobile sensors. This approach will likely warrant an
additional field that addresses the suitability of measurement devices and measurement
on moving objects, e.g., persons riding a bicycle or walking, and relating sensor data to subjective
experience-report result.
