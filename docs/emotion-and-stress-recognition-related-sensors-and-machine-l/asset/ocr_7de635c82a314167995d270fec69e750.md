Increasing the threshold would make the model stricter when determining stress states, while lowering the threshold would make it more generous. These suggestions suggest that we could change the threshold based on individual stress responses, Alternatively, personalized models could be developed by fine-tuning the network on data from a single individual. Unlike with conventional learning approaches, there is no need to retrain the network from scratch, so it can be trained rapidly and avoid over-fitting issues.



The reasons for using multiple physiological datasets are as follows. First, a small number of subjects can cause over-fitting that reduces generalization performance. Over-fitting issues can be overcome by increasing the amount of data (e.g., involving more subjects or augmenting the data) or using different types of data. Because the number of subjects is limited, each person's stress responses may vary slightly, leading to the problem of inter-variability, which has the effect of lowering multiple data related to stress could help to reduce the problem.

However, using many different types of data could reduce the stress recognition system's usability by requiring a variety of monitoring devices to be worn to collect all the physiological signals, which would be burdensome in practice. Researchers should consider the trade-offs involved usability and performance.



Our study has many limitations: the experimental setting and the use of a respiration monitoring device. Although the setting was intended to simulate a real workplace, the experiments were conducted in a more controlled manner recruiting working subjects is difficult and an uncontrolled experimental setting would reduce the quality of the data. Once we have established our stress model, we plan to perform experiments in a real workplace setting. In this study, we used a chest strap-based wearable device to measure the physiological signals, but we are aware that such devices can be hard to wear in the workplace and plan to use a patch-type ECG device and a wearable device to measure RESF in a later study.

# 6. Conclusions

In this study, we have proposed the first end-to-end deep learning approach to stress recognition on EEG and RESP data. Our protocol involving collecting EEG data and recording subjective stress scores the subjects conducted alternating stress and relaxation tasks. With this multiple dataset, our proposed DeepNet performed better than conventional learning methods that require the extraction of handcrafted features. By visualizing the network's activation, we found that neurons are being activated by unique and specific patterns. In conclusion, we believe that our proposed DeepNet will be of benefit to people who suffer from stress in the workplace.
