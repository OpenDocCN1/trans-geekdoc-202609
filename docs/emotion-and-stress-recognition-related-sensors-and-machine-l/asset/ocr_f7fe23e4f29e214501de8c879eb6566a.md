Emotions are communicated via external (facial or body expressions (alterations in heart rate (HR), respiration rate, perspiration, and others) and internal body expressions. These manifestations occur naturally and unconsciously, and their septic modulation can be used to influence the subjects' current emotional state. In a acquired systematic setting, it could be possible to influence the subjects' mood for the following day and their health condition.

External physical manifestations (e.g., facial expressions) are easily carried through a camera; however, present they low reliability which depend heavily on the user environment (if the subject is alone or in a group setting), or cultural background (if the subject grew in a society promoting the externalisation or internalisation of emotion), and can be faked or manipulated according to the subject goals, compromising the assessment of the true emotional state [2]. On the other hand, for internal physical manifestations, these constraints are less prominent, as the subject has little control over his body states. Alterations in physiological signals are not easily controlled by the subject, these entitle a more authentic insight into the subjective emotional experience.

Given these considerations, our work aims to perform a comprehensive study on emotion recognition using physiological data, such as Electrocardiogram (ECG), Electrodermal Activity (EDA), Respiration (RESP), Blood Volume Pulse (BVP). This choice of modalities is due to three factors: (1) Data can be extracted easily from portable, discrete wearable technology, rather than more intuitive sensors (e.g., Electroencephalography (EEG), or Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fINIRS)); (2) Widely reported in the literature state-of-the-art; (3) We use public-state datasets to evaluate two major techniques: Feature Fusion (FF) versus Decision Fusion (DF) on a feature-based representation, exploring also an extensive set of features compared to previous work. Furthermore, in our model, the user's emotional response is assessed on the two-dimensional space: Valence (meaning unpleasant or pleasant) and Arousal (meaning emotional intensity level).

# State of the Art

In literature, human emotion processing is generally described using two models: One deconstructing emotion in categories divided into basic/primary (arriving from innate, and in response to "fight-or-fight" behaviour) and complex/secondary emotions (deriving from cognitive processes) [3,4]. On the other hand, the second model quantifies emotions into continuous dimensions. A popular model, proposed by Lang [5], uses valence (unpleasant-pleasant level) versus arousal (activation level) two-dimensional model [6], which we adopt in this work. Concerning affect, it is generally performed through film snippets [6], virtual reality [7], music [8], recall [9], or stressful environments [6], with no commonly established norm which is the optimal methodology for ER recognition.

Regarding the automated recognition of emotional states, it is usually performed based on two methodologies [1 Traditional Machine Learning (ML) techniques [12–14]; Deep learning approaches [15–17]. Due to the limited size of datasets used, most of the work focuses on traditional ML algorithms, in particular Supervised Learning (SL), such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) [18–20], k-Nearest Neighbour (KNN), Decision Trees (DT) [24,25], and others [26,27].
