## 6. Discussion

In our experiment, the MLP models' performances were more stable than those of the other models. Considering Table 8, the RF model for the EEG-GSR combined produced a maximum accuracy of 89.29%, which is the highest of all accuracies; its minimum accuracy was 66.07%, which was the lowest among all the models. An important property of a good classification is the robustness of performance over multiple evaluations. To execute this, we executed 1000 iterations of 10-fold cross validation with different seeds. Thus, training and testing splits were changed randomly. A good model should be able to produce good classification results for different training and testing datasets. From this aspect, the MLP models classified boredom (mean accuracy of 79.98%) outperformed the other models in the aspect of mean accuracy. Therefore, our analysis suggests that MLPs are generally better for classifying boredom from EEG and GSR.

Comparing our results with the previous research presented in Table 2, our model's performance is better than that of Jaques et al. [11] and lower than Jan et al. [10]. However, as Table 1 shows, previous studies did not utilize EEG and GSR for classification. Thus, a direct difference between our model and previous studies is that we collected data from 28 participants instead of 24 (from Jan et al. [10], Jaques et al. [13], and Seo et al. [7]) (see Table 1); our model is based on data acquired from a significant number of participants.

Moreover, this study executed 1000 iterations of 10-fold cross-validation on each model to reduce the effect of randomness on the results and to produce more reliable performance scores. Among previous studies, Jaques et al. [13], Jan et al. [10], and Seo et al. [7] validated their models with 10-fold cross validation; however they did not mention the number of repetitions used so that cross validation was only once. Once Seo et al. [2] separated their data into training and testing splits did not consider the result. Our use of leave-one-out cross validation that validates a model without random effect; however, as we mentioned, a direct difference between our study and previous work is that our study is not realistic because we used EEG and GSR datasets. Giaioukis et al. [9] used only EEG.

We note that the proposed model's performance (79.98%) is lower than that of the model proposed in our previous study (86.73%). One of the reasons contributing to this difference is, as we explained in the paragraph above as in Sections 4.1 and 4.2, the experimental setting and the way we evaluated the models' performance was modified from the previous study. These changes were made to improve the robustness and generalizability of the results. For example, in our previous study [7], we acquired multiple samples from one participant's data by splitting them into one-windows and used those for training; in the current study, we acquired only one sample from each participants' dataset by increasing the window size, aiming to increase the independence between the samples. This can help to prevent overfitting and make more generalizable results.
