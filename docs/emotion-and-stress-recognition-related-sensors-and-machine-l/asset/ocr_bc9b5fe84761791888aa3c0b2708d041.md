# # Two-Stream Attention Network for Pain Recognition from Video Sequences

**Abstract:** Several approaches have been proposed for the analysis of pain-related facial expressions. These approaches range from common classification architectures based on a set of carefully designed handcrafted features, to deep networks generalised by an autonomous extraction of relevant facial descriptors and simultaneous optimisation of classification. In the current work, an end-to-end approach based on attention networks for the analysis and recognition of pain-related facial expressions is proposed. The method combines both temporal and spatial aspects of facial expressions through weighted aggregation of attention-based neural networks, based on sequences of Motion History Images (MHIs) and Optical Flow Images (OFIs). Each input is fed into a specific attention mechanism knowing of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) coupled to a Bidirectional Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). An attention mechanism generates a single weighted representation of each input (MHI sequence and OFI sequence), which is subsequently used to perform specific classification tasks. A weighted aggregation of the classification scores for each input stream is performed to generate a final classification output. The assessment conducted on the *BioVid Heat Database (Part A)* and *EmotionDatabase* tests the relevance of the proposed approach, as its classification performance is par with state-of-the-art classification approaches proposed in the literature.

**Keywords:** convolutional neural networks; long short-term recurrent neural networks; information fusion; pain recognition



An individual's affective disposition is often expressed throughout facial expressions. Human beings are better to assess someone's current mood or state of mind by observing his or her facial demeanor. Therefore, an analysis of facial expressions can provide some insight about one's emotional and psychological state. Thus, facial expression recognition (FER) has been attracting a lot of interest from the research community in the recent decades, particularly in the domains of machine learning and computer vision. The current work focuses on the analysis of facial expressions for the assessment and recognition of pain in video sequences. Specifically, a two-stream attention network is designed, with objective of combining both temporal and spatial aspects of facial expressions, based on sequences of motion history images [1] and optical flow images [2], to discriminate between neutral, low, and high levels of nociceptive pain.
