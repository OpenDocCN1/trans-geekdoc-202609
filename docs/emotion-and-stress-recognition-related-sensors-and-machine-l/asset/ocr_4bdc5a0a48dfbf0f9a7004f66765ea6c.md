# 3.2. Daily Life Data Collection and Emotional Momentary Assessment

After the controlled room experiments were finished, we gave the Empatica E4 devices to all participants. They were told to wear the Empatica E4 devices for twelve hours per day, between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m., for seven days. These days were not necessarily consecutive. We applied the EMA to collect information about the subject's stress level [39]. EMA involved the repeated sampling of subjects' current behaviors and experiences in real time [39]. EMA aims to minimize bias, maximize ecological validity, and allow the study of micro processes that influence behavior in real-world contexts. We implemented an online version of the PSS-5 questionnaire. After each hour, we asked the participant to fill in the EMA. In order to make the collection of self-reports, we sent emails every seven days at the end of each hour when they were wearing the wristband. In order, the participants were reminded to fill in the PSS-5 at 12 p.m., 3 p.m., and 9 p.m. every days. The EMA was delivered to the participants via a survey app. The app was available on desktop and mobile browsers. The link to the EMA was delivered through e-mail. Each email was sent to remind participants to fill the questionnaire with PSS-14 and for ambulatory assessments. In total, we collected 103 h of physiological data and 388 EMAs (60 EMAs in total), in the lab.

The procedure of the methodology used in this study was approved by the Institutional Review Board for Human Subjects of Bojacki University Approval Number 2018/16. Prior to the data acquisition, each participant received a consent form that explained the experimental procedure and its benefits and implications to both society and the subject. The procedure was also explained verbally to the subject. All of the data were stored anonymously.

# 3.3. Stress Recognition Framework

In order to propose an unobtrusive stress wristband for everyday use, we used an Empatica E4 [40] comfortable wristband, which has a variety of sensors such as the three-dimensional accelerometer (32 Hz), the continuous heart rate monitoring unit based on the skin temperature (4 Hz), and the EDA sensors (4 Hz). The major difference between the wristbands and contact sensors used in hospital settings is the vulnerability to the motion artifacts due to their design and attachment. A daily life suitable and comfortable stress detection system should consider these artifacts. Thus, it should have a preprocessing unit that detects and removes the artifacts from contact and motion. In daily life, the HRV and EDA can change due to high-intensity activity in short periods. Therefore a single sensor-based system can fail. Hence, a stress recognition system suitable for daily life should be multi-modal in terms of the collected data. We used robust preprocessing and feature extraction modules from our previous work [18] for this purpose (see Figure 3).
