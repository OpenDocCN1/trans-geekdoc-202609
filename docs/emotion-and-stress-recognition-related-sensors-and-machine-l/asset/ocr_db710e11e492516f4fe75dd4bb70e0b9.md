# 3.2. Databases Considered

Training of ML algorithms on the type and size of database used. A low number of images in a database is used for training, can cause under-fitting. To counter this, we need a large database for training the ML algorithms. The estimations and results are remarkably affected by the use of larger databases over the smaller ones; for more accurate results a database is required in abundance. Emotion classification and their intensity estimations require a vast and varied dataset for validation and testing. The images used for intensity estimation and recognition of emotion are spontaneous and needed

+   - Posed Databases: For capturing extreme emotions. The disadvantage is the artificial human behavior.
- Spontaneous Datasets: Natural human behavior. However, it is extremely time-consuming for capturing the right emotion.

A close, relationship exists between the models used for the intensity of facial emotions and the databases used. Mainly five databases CK [2,6], JFFE [64], B-DE and an in-house database of 200 images (images of each AU of ethnic/racial) taken in an uncontrolled environment through a web camera. Here is a brief description of each dataset:

+   1. JFFE: The database contains 213 images of 7 facial expressions (6 basic facial expressions + 1 neutral) by 10 Japanese female models. Each has been rated on 6 emotion adjectives by 60 Japanese subjects. (Posed – no AUs)
2. DISFA: In this database the images were acquired using PIGrey stereo imaging system at high resolution (1024 × 768). Each has 66 facial landmark points manually scored by two human FACS experts. The database also includes 66 landmark points of each emotion in the database. (Spontaneous–AUs present AU1, AU2, AU4, AU5, AU6, AU9, AU13, AU17, AU20, AU26)
3. CK: In the released portion of the Cohn-Kahn AU-Coded Facial Expression Database are 100 university students. They ranged from 18 to 30 years. Fifty-five percent were female, 15 percent were African-American, and three percent Asian or Latino. Subjects were instructed by an experimenter to perform a series of 23 displays single AU and combinations of AUs. (Posed: AUs present AU1, AU2, AU4, AU5, AU27)
4. BU-DE: Includes 100 subjects with 2500 facial expression images. The BU-DE database is available to the research community (e.g., areas of interest come from affective computing, computer vision, human interaction, biomedicine, law-enforcement, and psychology). The database contains 88 subjects (56% female, 44% male), ranging from 18 to 70 years, of various ethnic/racial nationalities (White, Black, Asian, Middle-East, Indian, and Latino). Posed: AUs present AU1, AU2, AU4, AU5, AU6, AU9, AU15, AU17, AU20, AU25)
5. Our dataset: consists of 200 images (images of each of the 5 basic emotions considered) taken in an uncontrolled environment through a web camera. The reason for collecting the dataset in an uncontrolled environment was that the intensity of facial emotions is to be measured in an everyday environment for real-time applications. The dataset consists of probe and gallery images which are taken over a month. There are in total 200 probe images and 100 gallery images from the same subjects of different emotions which are used for validation of the algorithm purposes. The images are in different lighting and conditions to see the effects of these on our proposed method. (No AUs present)
