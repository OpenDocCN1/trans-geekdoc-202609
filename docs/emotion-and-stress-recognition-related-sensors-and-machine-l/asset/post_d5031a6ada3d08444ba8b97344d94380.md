Additionally, analyzing the results of the state-of-the-art, feature engineering for subject-independent and subject-dependent human emotion detection on EDA does not lead to high performance in, when the number of classes is higher than two. This is because extracting the synthetic patterns which are part of each emotion is difficult. Furthermore, when trying to overcome this by analyzing more basic features such as level, response amplitude, rate, rise and turnover, they discard facile elicited behavior which might improve emotion recognition. It has been proven in this work that DL can recover this drawback quite well.

Regarding the point of testing the proposed model using different datasets from different labs, because human emotions do not form similar patterns, Consequently, the research community should develop generalized models to recognize human emotions, where such elicitations and physiological sensors are different from the ones involved in the initial training. Dealing with such research has an important impact for human support in smart environments in different environments.

Concerning, human emotion recognition with different lab-settings, in [30], authors showed that adjusting and manipulating the feature space to bring different datasets to a homogenous feature space as a pre-processing step may increase the overall performance when datasets come from different labs.

Moreover, in [47], they checked the ability of 504 school children aged between 8 and 11 years old to recognize the emotions of facial expressions based on pictures. The overall performance approximately 86% to recognize anger, fear, sadness, happiness, disgust and neutral facial expressions. It is impressive to show that the proposed EDA-based emotion recognition system is close to the performance of human capability to interpret the facial expressions.

This study can be considered to a basic contribution in terms of overcoming the generalization problem for human emotion recognition. The aim was to show the feasibility and the possibility of building generalized models for relevant application contexts. Furthermore, this studied the less intensive sensors based on statistical analyses in real-life datasets and reviewed various state-of-the-art approaches to human emotion recognition in smart environments.

Additionally, emotion recognition is a cornerstone of advanced intelligent systems for monitoring a subject's comfort. Thus, subject's emotion and stress level is a key component for the future of smart AAL environments.

In our future work, we will focus on human emotion recognition using EDA with different lab-settings, we will try to build a generalized approach which should be trained using lab-settings X and tested using lab-settings Y. Additionally, we plan to combine Stacked Sparse Autoencoders with CNN. Moreover, CNN essentially learns local (spatial) features. On the other side, RNN does in essence rather learn temporal features. Consequently, combining both neural network concepts will result in a neuro-processor which can learn both contextual dependencies (i.e., spatial and temporal) from input local features. As a result, such a combination potentially improve the overall performance.
