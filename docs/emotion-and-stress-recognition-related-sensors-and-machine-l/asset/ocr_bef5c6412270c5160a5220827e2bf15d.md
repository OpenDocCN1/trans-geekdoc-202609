Figure 1 shows the result produced by t-SNE method on the three databases used in this work. We samples from each subject using a different color marker, to observe that EEG data samples from the same subject are topologically close to each other in the 2-D space. Plots reveal that the contribution of the subject to the EEG signal is clearer higher than the effect of the emotion, a fact which has been extensively explored in biometrics (e.g., [61–63]).

Although it seems that the topological structure of the maps presented in Figure 1 is not the best for the construction of inter-subject models, other previous works have obtained positive results when applying subject-independent models under a typical classification setting. For example, the effect recognition reported in [35] refer to accuracy of 0.62 in valence and arousal, using a SVM with a Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel. However, they used an imbalanced dataset, with a proportion of 56–44% in arousal and 61–39% in valence. It is possible that the positive accuracy reported is in part due to this fact, rather than to the existence of emotion-evoked specific EEG patterns that are represented by multiple subjects.
