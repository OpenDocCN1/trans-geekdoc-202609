The overall reported system accuracy results range from 53% to 90% for the classification of one or more emotions. However, there is likely a gap between real-world applications performed in real time, which presents numerous challenges compared to experiments conducted in a laboratory. Some authors suggest that training datasets be generated on a larger scale to overcome these challenges. Indeed, we believe that larger datasets could catalyze the research in this field. It is worth noting that a similar dynamic played out in the area of image recognition, which experienced a rapid expansion due to the generation of massive databases. Nevertheless, this effort for EEG datasets would likely require collaboration between various research groups to achieve emotions triggered by elective methods.

Overall, we believe systems should be trained with larger samples (and per subject), plus use of real-time data. With improved datasets, unsupervised techniques could be implemented to obtain comprehensive models. Moreover, these robust systems might help for transfer learning, i.e., general models that can be successfully applied to particular individuals.

In recent years, EEG-based BCI emotion recognition has been a field affecting that has generated much interest. Significant advancements in the development of low-cost EEG devices with increasingly better usability have encouraged numerous research studies.

In this article, we reviewed the algorithms and processes that can be part of EEG-based BCI emotion recognition systems: (1) Emotion election, (2) signal acquisition, (3) feature extraction and selection, (4) classification techniques, and (5) performance evaluation. For our survey of this topic, we mined different datasets and 60 selected studies carried out under a computer science perspective to gain insight into the state of the art and suggest possible future research.

As in this review, computational methods do not have standards for various applications. Research is continuing to evolve in this field with great effort. The study of the relationship between brain signals and emotions is a complex problem, and new methods and implementations are continuously presented. We expect that many of the existing challenges will be solved and will pave the way for a vast array of applications using EEG-based emotion recognition.
