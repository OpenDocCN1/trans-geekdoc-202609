We further aook up to daily life environments and tried the DDR model, which achieved 68.30% accuracy with HRV, 63.60% accuracy with EDA, and 71.40% accuracy with multimodal HRV + EDA. We applied the model in the laboratory with self-reports and showed that the performance increased with all types of physiological signals for daily life stress recognition (7% for HRV, 14.8% for EDA, 2.8% for HRV + EDA). We also investigated that the mean accuracy was enhanced with the LDKC model for HRV + EDA and EDA-based stress recognition. The classification performance of the proposed system changed significantly based on the labeling methodology. Models trained in the laboratory for daily life stress detection outperformed ones trained with daily life data. We achieved the best results (73.81%) in the DDR model, where the daily life stress detection system was trained in the laboratory with self-reports. This demonstrated that we could increase the accuracy of the system by training the model in the laboratory with the same kind of ground truth since also that self-reports provide a more robust framework for all types of sentence labeling approaches. Multi-modal sensing showed that the performance of the LDKC was better than the DDR model and what of the DDR in the multimodal context. On the other hand, the accuracies of the LDKC model were lower than those of the DDR results only when a single modality was used. We could find that the DDR method suffered from relatively noisy labels and data changed when using different types of data in training and testing (known context labels in the laboratory) for the low performance of LDKC models. RV and SVM classifiers outperformed others, and were aligned with the daily life stress recognition mentioned in the Related Work Section. Feature and modality selection is vital for achieving better performance. We selected the best features: five of them from EDA, and all of them from HRV, which also suggested that multi-modal sensing is crucial for daily life stress detection. In most of the cases (15/20), the combination of modalities increased the performance of the system. In the remaining tests, anticorrelations between the features of different modalities might be the cause of lower accuracies. There is still room for improvement for daily life stress recognition. Moreover, our study was without limitations. In order to generalize the conclusions, additional studies based on larger heterogeneous samples are needed. As future work, we plan to develop perceived stress models to overcome the problem of self-reports. We will try to exploit surveys and daily-session questionnaires of individuals to prevent the bias caused by biased self-reports.
