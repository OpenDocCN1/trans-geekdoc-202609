#  2.3. Experiment Structure

The experiment consists of six induction sequences, separated by subjective feedback related to the actual sequence, and followed by a respiration baseline and a summary of the achieved results in that. While the experimental sequences are used to induce cognitive and emotional states, the subjective feedback is used for the validation of the induction. These are described in details in the following subsections. Prior to the experiment, each subject received an introduction and instructions to the experimental steps in form of a PowerPoint presentation and was asked to fill three questionnaires related to: (1) emotion regulation based on the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) [53,54]; (2) emotional control based on the Trait Emotional Questionnaire (TEIQ-SF) [55,56]; and (3) personality traits based on the Test Item Personality Scale (TIPS) [57,58]. These questionnaires are also used as further evaluation of the stability of the induction procedure.

#  2.3.1. Induction Sequences

Six sequences of different difficulties, with four tasks assigned, for the induction of six different emotional and cognitive load states. All tasks within a sequence have the same or comparable difficulty levels. The first inductive sequence is designed to induce Interest and is of more difficulty to gain the users' interest and familiarize them with the search task procedure. The Interest sequence has 40 tasks and is designed with a map of 3 x 3 and 4 x 4 matrices, and 10 time per task to give the answer. The second sequence is designed to induce Overload and consists of 40 tasks with a 6 x 6 matrix and short time of 6 s per task to provide an answer. The third sequence has a Normal difficulty and is defined with 40 tasks with 4 x 4 matrices. This Normal sequence is the neutral (cognitive and emotional) state to be considered as baseline between the sequences. The fourth sequence is implemented as an Easy sequence with 40 tasks with 3 x 3 matrices and very long time of 100 s for responding. In order to induce Underload, the fifth sequence is defined as a repetition of the previous Easy sequence of low difficulty, with 40 tasks with 3 x 3 matrices and 100 s to provide an answer. This originates from the idea that repeating a well-known task in the same way twice in row, generates a state of boredom and leads to Underload.
