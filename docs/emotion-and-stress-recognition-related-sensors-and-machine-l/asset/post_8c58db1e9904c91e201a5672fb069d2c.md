## 4. Future Work

Datasets developed for specific applications use passive methods to provoke emotions, such as IAPS, IADS, music videos, and film clips. Public databases, such as DEAP and SEED, use emotion elicitation through music videos and film clips, respectively. Few implement active methods for provoking emotions, such as video games and flight simulators.

Going forward, we expect the generation of datasets that use active elicitation because these techniques simulate "real life" events better, and are more effective at emotion induction. However, the implementation of such studies requires more complex experimental setup.

Furthermore, the study of individual emotions has been recently trending. Some works include fear detection, an algorithm has applications in phobia investigation, and other psychiatric disorders. It is worth mentioning that our survey found that negative emotions are more challenging to detect positive ones.

We did not find in the elicitation of the EEG-based emotion recognition of feelings that combine positive and negative effects at the same moment, instance, bittersweet feelings. These mixed emotions are interesting because they are also related to the study of high creative performance [141].

Feature extraction and selection are EEG-BCI system components, which are continuously evolving. They are designed based on a profound approach to understanding of the brain’s biology and physiology. The development of novel features is a topic that can contribute significantly to the improvement of emotion recognition systems. For instance, time-domain features are combined with frequency-frequency characteristics, channel location, and connectivity criteria. The development of novel feature extraction methods includes asymmetry discoveries in different functioning brain segments, new electrode locations that provide more information, connectivity models (between channels), and correlations needed for understanding functionality.

These evolving features contain EEG signals and their frequency bands are related to multiple functional and connectivity considerations. The study of the relationship between EEG and biological or psycho-emotional elements should imply going forward. Improved features could better capture individual emotion dynamics and also correlate characteristics across individuals and sessions.

A particularly interesting trend in feature extraction is to use deep neural networks. These systems receive raw data to avoid loss of information and take advantage of the neural networks functioning to obtain relevant features automatically.

The overall reported system accuracy results range from 53% to 90% for the classification of one or more emotions. However, there is likely a gap between real-world applications performed in real time, which presents numerous challenges compared to experiments conducted in a laboratory. Some authors suggest that training datasets be generated on a larger scale to overcome these challenges. Indeed, we believe that larger datasets could catalyze the research in this field. It is worth noting that a similar dynamic played out in the area of image recognition, which experienced a rapid expansion due to the generation of massive databases. Nevertheless, this effort for EEG datasets would likely require collaboration between various research groups to achieve emotions triggered by elective methods.

Overall, we believe systems should be trained with larger samples (and per subject), plus use of real-time data. With improved datasets, unsupervised techniques could be implemented to obtain comprehensive models. Moreover, these robust systems might help for transfer learning, i.e., general models that can be successfully applied to particular individuals.

EEG signals are reliable information that cannot be simulated or fabricated. To decode EEG and relate these specific emotion to a complex problem. Mapping with specific brain structures because different emotions activate the same brain locations, or conversely, a single emotion can activate various structures.

In recent years, EEG-based BCI emotion recognition has been a field affecting that has generated much interest. Significant advancements in the development of low-cost EEG devices with increasingly better usability have encouraged numerous research studies.

In this article, we reviewed the algorithms and processes that can be part of EEG-based BCI emotion recognition systems: (1) Emotion election, (2) signal acquisition, (3) feature extraction and selection, (4) classification techniques, and (5) performance evaluation. For our survey of this topic, we mined different datasets and 60 selected studies carried out under a computer science perspective to gain insight into the state of the art and suggest possible future research.

As in this review, computational methods do not have standards for various applications. Research is continuing to evolve in this field with great effort. The study of the relationship between brain signals and emotions is a complex problem, and new methods and implementations are continuously presented. We expect that many of the existing challenges will be solved and will pave the way for a vast array of applications using EEG-based emotion recognition.
