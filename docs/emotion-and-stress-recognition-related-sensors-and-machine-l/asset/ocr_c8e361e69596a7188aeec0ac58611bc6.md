computed and subsequently used to generate a single weighted representation of the input sequence.
The self-attention weights are computed as follows,

```
$a_k = elu(W_kh_{h,j} + b_k) \quad (17)$
```

```
$a_k = \frac{exp(a_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^{l} exp(a_i)} \quad (18)$
```

where $W_k$ are the weights to specify the input feature representation $h_{k,j} = [\overrightarrow{h_{k,j}}, h_{k,j}]$ and $elu$ represents the exponential linear activation function [58], which is defined as

```
$elu(x) = \begin{cases} a \cdot (exp(x) - 1) & \text{if } x < 0 \ x & \text{if } x \geq 0 \end{cases} \quad (19)$
```

with $a=1$. Each self-attention weight expresses the relevance of a specific image for the emotional state expressed within the video sequence. Thereby, relevant images should be significantly computed with higher weight values as irrelevant images. The final representation of the input sequence is computed by performing a weighted aggregation of the BiLSTM output $[h_{1,j}, h_{2,j}, \dots, h_{l,j}]$ based on the computed self-attention weights $[a_k]_{k=1}^l$ as follows,

```
$h_j = \sum_{k=1}^{l} a_k \cdot h_{k,j} \quad (20)$
```

and is further used to perform the classification task.

In the following section, a description of the experiments performed for the evaluation of the proposed approach is provided. The datasets used for the evaluation are briefly described, followed by a description of the conducted data preprocessing steps. The experimental settings as well the performed experiments are described subsequently. This section is finally concluded with a description and discussion of the experimental results.



The presented approach is evaluated on both BioVid Heat Pain Database (Part A) (BVVD) [4] and the SenseEmotion Database (SEDB) [6]. Both datasets were designed with the principal goal of fostering research in the domain of face recognition. In fact, several healthy participants were subjected to a series of individually heated-induced painful stimuli, using the exact same procedure. The BVVD consists of 87 individuals subjected to four individually and gradually increasing levels of heat-induced stimuli ($T_1, T_2, T_3$ and $T_4$), the SEDB consists of 40 individuals subjected to three individually and gradually increasing levels of heat-induced stimuli ($T_1, T_2$ and $T_3$). Each level of heat-induced pain stimulation was randomly elicited for 20 times for the BVVD and 30 times for the SEDB. Each elication lasted 4 s, followed by a recovery period of a length of 8 to 12 seconds which a baseline $T_0$ ($32^\circ C$) was reached. While the elicitations were performed uniquely on one specific hand for the BVVD, the experiments were conducted twice for the SEDB, with the elicitation performed on one specific arm (left forearm and right forearm). Therefore, with the inclusion of the baseline temperature $T_0$, the dataset dataset consists of a total of $87 \times 20 \times 5 = 8700$ samples, whereas the SEDB consists of $40 \times 30 \times 4 = 4800$ samples.
