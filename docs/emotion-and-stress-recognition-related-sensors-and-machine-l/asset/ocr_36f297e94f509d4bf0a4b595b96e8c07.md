Other researchers [41,56,57] have detected some types of muscle tension by analysing keyboard and mouse control. An increased pressing pressure and a greater contact with the surface of the mouse has been observed in stressed conditions [41]. # 1.1. Facial Expressions

In recent years, identifying affective states based on facial expressions has been widely explored in the area of affective computing [58]. Technology-wise, vision-based camera tracking, including cameras [59], are the most commonly used techniques in identifying facial expressions [60]. A recent work [61] identified stress and anxiety using facial cues, which the authors' induced stress by internal and external factors. All videos recorded were analysed and a machine learning model scored between 80-90% accuracy in recognizing stress. Although vision-based identification is demonstrably effective, unfavourable light conditions and movement artifacts easily affect detection.

Other researchers identify facial expressions on skin-contact electrodes, such as piezoelectric sensing [62], EM [63,64], capacitive sensing [65,66] and electric field sensing [67]. These researches demonstrate the detection of facial gestures, such as frowning, eye wink, eye-down, mouth movements, et and on emotions such as frustration, confusion and interest engagement. Although the identification of emotions and facial expressions seem reliable, the outcome may differ in real-life scenarios where facial expressions are known to deceive easily [68].
