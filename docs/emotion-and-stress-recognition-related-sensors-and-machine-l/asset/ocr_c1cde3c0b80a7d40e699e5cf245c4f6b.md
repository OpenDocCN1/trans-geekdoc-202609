Stress detection based on wearable sensors have been studied a lot, more than detection of any other state. For instance, in [6] a classifier to detect high and low stress, as well as, non-stressful situations to monitor lately conditions based on wearable wrist-wrap sensors. The result of the paper was these two classes be detected with an accuracy of 85%. Moreover, in [7] a binary classifier to stress and non-stress state was trained, and it was noted that stress can be detected using sensors of commercial smartwatch. There are also other studies showing that stress detection based on classification models can be performed with high accuracy using user-independent models (see for [8,9]). However, according to some studies, the accuracy can be further improved by using models based on personal data [10,11].

What is noticeable that most of the stress and affect detection studies are based on classification methods commonly used in human activity recognition, for instance [6,12]. These methods include feature extraction using sliding window technique and usage of the same classifiers. The reason for this is that probably the devices used in both studies are similar, or at least partially, as they are both using wearable devices and the data obtained by the sensors of these devices. While the recognition accuracy in stress detection articles are based on classification of discrete target variables. The problem is that stress and affect are not discrete phenomena as for the level of stress of a person can be high or low between anything. In [5], it was suggested that discrete classification could be transformed as continuous phenomenon based on posture. Thus, also in this case, the original classification for stress/non-stress would be based on discrete target values. In fact, the problem is that affect recognition studies is already decided in the data gathering phase by transforming a continuous phenomenon into discrete due to difficulty to gather continuous target variables. Of course, continuous phenomenon can be modeled using regression option, and this type of problem simpleification can cause problems in the modelling phase. In fact, this means that a problem which was originally recognized as classification problem. Therefore, a better option would be to be based on stress and affect detection to continuous target values.

While the most of the stress studies are based on discrete target variables, there are some attempts to gather and analyze continuous target variables for stress level. In [13], stress while driving a car was studied. In the study, continuous target variables for stress level were created based on video recorded to analyze driver driver’s facial expression, body motion and road conditions. Another study, where continuous target variables for stress detection were gathered in [14,15]. Furthermore, in [14] the study concentrates on analyzing stress while and during the data gathering, driver constantly estimated/his own stress level. While, while in these two datasets contain continuous target values, in both cases the final analysis is based on discrete targets created based on continuous. On the other hand, there are also studies where stress and affective states have analyzed using regression model (see for instance [16,17]), these are rare compared to studied based on classification methods.

As stress is not discrete, it should be analyzed based on continuous target variables. This means that of classification, the data should be analyzed using regression methods. This article is the first in one where regression and classification models for stress detection are properly compared to experiment methods really perform classification method. In addition, the cons pros of classification and regression models are discussed. Moreover, user-independent and personal recognition models are presented. The study is based on public dataset making the reproduction of the presented results easy.
