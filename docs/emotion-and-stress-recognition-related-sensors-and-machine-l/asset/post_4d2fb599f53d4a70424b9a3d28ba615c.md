## 2.8. Feature Extraction and Analysis

As commented, to establish if there are differences between the EDA signals produced during the listening to the different music tracks, the $SCR_{Driver}$ has been used. Figure 3 shows the feature extraction and analysis process, which aim to assess those features (metrics) that characterize the signals. The SCR driver, obtained through the deconvolution process described above, is decomposed into a series of temporal, morphological, statistical and frequency features. These features are stored on a feature sheet for later analysis investigate if there are differences in the arousal basis of each feature for each of the musical genres.

### Feature Extraction

Notice that the human reaction against a specific stimulus is usually expressed as a peak or a burst of peaks in $SCR_{Driver}$ as per the level of alertness involved. From a physiological perspective, the reactions against the stimulus are plotted on the signals as peaks proportional to the intensity, length and number of emotional events. The greater the disturbance, the greater peak height produced in the SCR data. The number of peaks in $SCR_{Driver}$ increase when the stimulus is maintained over time, which produces a series of sequences.

Table 2 details the several features selected to characterize the different segments of the $SCR_{Driver}$. These features, which have been applied in previous works [40,41,48], allow us to quantify each signal.

## Table 2. Features obtained from skin conductance response (SCR)

| Analysis | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Temporal | M, SD, MA, MI, DR, D1, D2, D1M, D2M, D1SD, D2SD |
| Morphological | AL, IN, AP, RMS, IL, EL |
| Statistical | SK, KU, MO |
| Frequency | F1, F2 |

The temporal parameters are the mean value (M), standard deviation (SD), maximum and minimum peak value (MA and MI), and dynamic range (DR) establishing the difference between maximum and minimum. These parameters can provide globally significant feedback about the average and variability of the data sets. They provide specific information about a higher or lower reaction obtained through the data, which may vary by the nature of the stimulus. Other temporal parameters used are the first and second derivative (D1, D2), which mean (D1M, D2M) and their standard deviations (D1SD and D2SD). The use of these parameters is due to the fact that if the stimulus induces a greater skew than it is less. It is, therefore, necessary to establish a criterion of speed and acceleration in the response. The time needed in the recovery produces a more opposite sign gradient.

Within the morphological features there are length (AL), area (IN), normalized mean power (NP), root mean square (RMS), perimeter and area ratio (IL). These parameters observe the need to understand morphological differences in the shape of the SCRs. They are not only to be studied, but also the general morphology of the signals are of interest. Statistical features 예를 skew (SK), kurtosis (KU) and momentum (MO). These supply information about the distribution and variability of the data series. For the frequency domain the fast Fourier transform (FFT) for bandwidths F1 (0.1, 0.2), F2 (0.2, 0.3) and F3 (0.3, 0.4) has been chosen. Using these parameters enables any variation in the frequency domain of the stimulus.

## 3. Results and Discussion

This section presents the results obtained in the experiment, broken down into two different studies. In the first study, a series of statistical tests were conducted to determine whether there are significant statistical differences for each the temporal, morphological, statistical and frequency features described in the EDA signals produced for each of the music genres. The objective was to identify the variations in arousal during the music genre, as well as specify which features confirm a significant statistical difference.

The second study consisted of analyzing whether there is a clear correspondence between the responses given by the participants in the SAM activation and the physiological EDA signals acquired during the listening tasks. To this end, objective information on each of the EDA segments associated with each music genre was linked to the response to the SAM question. Several classifiers were used to quantify whether there are differences between low and high excitation states. Our purpose was to check whether these classifiers can classify the states with good accuracy.

For the statistical analysis of both studies IBM SPSS version 23 was used. Please note that in all cases a p-value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.
