# 5. Conclusions and Future Work

Over the past decade, the field of affective computing has grown, with many datasets being created [6–95], however, a consolidation is lacking concerning: (1) What are the ranges of the expected performance; (2) The definition of the best sensor modality, SL classifier and features per modality for ER; (3) Which is the best technique to deal with multimodality and limitations (FF or DF); (4) Selection of the classification model. Therefore, in this work, we studied the recognition of low emotional response in two dimensions: Arousal and Valence, for five publicly available datasets commonly found. For this, we used physiological data easily measured from pervasive technology, namely ECG, RESP and BVP data. Then, to deal with multimodality, we used two techniques: FF and DF.

We extend the state-of-the-art with: (1) Benchmarking the ER classification performance for SL, FF and DF in a systematic way; (2) Summarising the accuracy and F1-score (3) Systematic analysis of SL classifiers and extended feature for each modality. We were able to obtain significant results that are new in literature for the selected datasets. Experimental results showed that FF is the most competitive technique.

For future work, we identified the following lines: (1) Acquisition of data for the development of a subject-dependent model, since emotions are highly subject-dependent resulting, according to literature [11], in a higher classification performance; (2) Grouping the users by clusters in FF and in consideration when considering emotional detection; (3) As stated in Section 4.3 we used a SFS methodology to select the best feature set to use in all our tested techniques, however, it is not optimal, so the classification results using additional feature selection techniques should be tested; (4) Lastly, our work is highly conditioned to Deep Learning techniques, in an approach which concerns the exploration and comparison of feature engineering and the data representation learning approaches, with emphasis on performance and explainability aspects.
