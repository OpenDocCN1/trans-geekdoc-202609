
# A Wearable In-Ear Device for Emotion Monitoring

Chanavit Athavhip1, Setha Pan1,* and Pasin Israsena2

Received: 30 July 2019; Accepted: 10 September 2019; Published: 17 September 2019

**Abstract:** For future healthcare applications, which are increasingly moving towards out-of-hospital or home-based caring models, the ability to remotely and continuously monitor patients' conditions effectively are imperative. Others, emotional state is one of the conditions that could be interest to caregivers. This paper presents a preliminary study to develop a wearable device that is a low cost, single channel, dry contact, in-ear EEG suitable for non-intrusive monitoring. All aspects of the designs, engineering, and experimenting by applying machine learning for emotion classification are covered. Based on the valence and arousal model, the device is able to classify emotion with 71.70% (valence), 72.98% (arousal), and 53.72% (all emotions). The results are comparable to those from the more conventional EEG headsets at T7 and T8 scalp positions. These results, together with its carphone-like wearables, suggest its usage especially for future healthcare applications, as home-based or tele-monitoring systems as identified.

**Keywords:** EEG; in-ear EEG; emotion classification; emotion monitoring; elderly caring; outpatient caring; machine learning

As societies around the world increasingly face with the issue of aging population, how to take care of these elderly people becomes an important challenge. This is especially true for the fortunate ones who live alone. In order to ensure their physical and mental well-being and provide emergency assistance, monitoring technology could potentially be part of the solution. Particularly, wearable devices or smart sensors could be employed for effective and practical monitoring. Part of the carer's concerns, emotional state is one of the factors that reflects mental states and can greatly impact decision-making [1]. Emotion monitoring could therefore be used as another piece for elderly and remote-care supporting systems.

Emotion itself is very complex [2]. There are several methodologies for the kinds of emotions, emotion recognition can be fairly straightforward. For research purposes, several models have been proposed that can be categorized into two approaches: defining basic emotions and using a dimensional model. The most widely used approach is the basic emotions (i.e., anger, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, and surprise) generally used in facial expression recognition [3]. For the second approach, the common dimensional model is characterized by two main dimensions (i.e., valence and arousal).
