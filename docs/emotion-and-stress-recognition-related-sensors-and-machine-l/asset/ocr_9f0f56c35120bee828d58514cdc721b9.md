To achieve this state, the participant remains silent looking at a black screen before the first piece of music. At the same time the experiment was being conducted, the EDA signals were continuously collected, making possible further segmentation, preprocessing and analysis of the signal.



As discussed, EDA has been measured by a non-invasive device. The E4 Empatica bracelet measures the skin conductance (SC) in the form of EDA signals. These measurements are composed of two signals: a first signal that varies wildly, called the driver or skin conductance (SCL), and a second that varies rapidly, the phase driver or skin conductance (SCR). The SCL signals establish the base level of the signal, while the SCR is directly associated with the activity of the sweat motor system in, is directly associated with the parasympathetic nervous system.

Within the process of processing the EDA signals, different phases are crossed when the signals are transformed. These phases are usually preprocessing, filtering, artifact removal and discrete convolution. The preprocessing is in charge of establishing the segments in each of the phases of the experiment. Then, it is necessary to filter the SC signals to eliminate the artifacts and interference recorded during the acquisition phase. In our case, two different filters have been used: a low-pass filter with a 4 Hz cutoff frequency, and a Gaussian filter to smooth the signal. The next step is the deconvolution to separate the SCR from the SCL signals. This method makes it possible to minimize the effects that rate, sex and age contribute to the SC signal. For this phase, the MATLAB Ledalab library 3.4 has been successfully used [47]. Mathematically, the sudomotor nerve activity is considered a Driver containing a train of impulses that develop over time. This response is integrated in SC and consequently also in SCL and SCR. The result is represented by a convolution (*) of the driver with the impulse-response function (IRF), which describes the flow of the impulse response over time, as shown in Equation (1).

```
SC = SCR_Driver * IRF
```

The SC signal is composed of signals SCL and SCR, as shown in Equation (2).

```
SC = SCL + SCR
```

```
SC = (SCL_Driver + SCR_Driver) * IRF
```
