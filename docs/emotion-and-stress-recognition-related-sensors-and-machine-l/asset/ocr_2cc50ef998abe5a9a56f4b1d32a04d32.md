To record the vehicle movements and on-road travels, a standard on-board diagnostic system (OBD-II) was used. OBD-II is a computer-based system built into modern passenger cars, which monitors emission controls, and performance of the engine and detects malfunctions. OBD-II systems provide access to the health of a vehicle along many parameters and sensors from the engine control unit (ECU). The OBD-II system offers valuable information, including diagnostic codes, when troubleshooting problems [43]. The technical characteristics of OBD-II can be found in the standard sampling protocols for interfaces.

A mid-sized sedan, Hyundai Grandeur (Azera in the U.S.) 6th generation (Hybrid), was used in this study. The humidity values inside the vehicle were maintained between 1016 and 1030 Mbar

# 2.3. Experimental Conditions

In this study, an experiment was conducted during real driving between the cities of Incheon and Seoul in Korea, which is a 55-kilometer route consisting of five segments: City 1 (Incheon in Korea), Highway 1, Highway 2, Highway 3, and City 2 (Seoul in Korea) with two Tollgate points (TG) (Figure 2). The total time for one-way drive was approximately 60 min. During this route, two types of data were collected, as EDA data from the Empatica E4 sensor (sampling rate: 4 Hz) and signal measured by OBD-II.

All studied data were collected for a month from 13 December 2017 to 13 January 2018. Datasets for 25 weekdays were obtained. Data from four days were excluded because they were inaccurate and unclear, as unrecorded time data, device failure and zero values of EDA data. The total number of datasets (days) used in the analysis was 21.
