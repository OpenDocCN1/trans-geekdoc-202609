## Preface to "Emotion and Stress Recognition Related Sensors and Machine Learning Technologies"

Emotions reflect how we feel about something in various circumstances of daily life. For example, facial expressions are one of the ways to identify emotions through some form of nonintrusive visual or acoustic sensors. Besides nonintrusive physiological sensors can be used to collect data, out of which emotion and/or stress can reliably and robustly be extracted through appropriate machine learning schemes. Examples of such sensors are: pulse rate-measuring devices, electroencephalography (EEG), electrocardiogram (ECG), respiration rate measurements (RESP), etc.

Indeed, adequately recognizing human emotions through the use of electronic machines has always been a challenge although very fascinating. There are numerous applications and use cases in which reliable emotion information can be a cornerstone for related value-added services. A positive trend is that the relevant physiological sensors are becoming cheaper and more accurate over time.

Human emotion recognition systems are of high relevance for various fields of application which include, amongst others, robotics, various areas of medicine, industry, quality control, visual inspection, surveillance, driving assistance systems, etc.

In this book, 25 different contributions involving either visual or physiological sensors are provided. The book is divided into four parts:

- (1) Stress level recognition
- (2) Wearable body sensors
- (3) Dermatological sensors
- (4) Facial expression recognition

This book will be a valuable read for research experts and professionals in the various above-cited application fields/areas in which human emotion-related information is of precious use. Further, this book will also be very inspiring for students in machine learning and artificial intelligence.

Scientific technologies are gaining momentum in today's information-society, where different technologies are used to collect data from such systems and this data to make useful insights. These technologies are being applied in different domains and parts of the world to address challenges that could not be addressed with past. This Special Issue of the Journal on Emotion and Stress emerges as a crucial need for massive deployment of innovative systems. They range from assistive systems, medical-monitoring, to emotion-aware systems. They are built around (i) the field readiness and use of intuitive sensors and data for capturing biosignal, including EEG sensor, ECG and electroactivity sensors; (ii) the quality assessment and management of sensor data, (iii) data preprocessing, filtering and calibration techniques for biosignals; (iv) the field readiness and use of noninvasive sensor technologies, including Visual sensors, Acoustic sensors, vibration sensors and Piezzo-electric sensors; (v) emotion recognition using machine learning and networks; (vi) area stress studies; (vii) the use of operational data in emotion recognition, including dataset generation principles and concepts, quality assurance and emotion elicitation materials and contents; (viii) machine-learning techniques for robust emotion recognition,

Issues can be viewed from various perspectives: (i) quality and reliability of sensor, (b) performance comparison of accuracy, recall, and F1-measure; (c) robustness of independent recognition; (d) portability of the systems to different environments and (e) the estimation of the state for dynamic systems.
