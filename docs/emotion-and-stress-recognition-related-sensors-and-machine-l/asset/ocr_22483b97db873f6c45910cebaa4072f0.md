# 4.2. Limitation of This Study and Future Research

The presented research is the result of a long-term study stress stress based on physiological signals. Even though developed classification models showed good results, there are a few limitations, which need to be addressed in the future. First, a shortcoming of this study is the number of participants, which was limited to one driver. The authors compensated for it by increasing the experimental period to one month to obtain the optimal number of driving datasets for analysis. Second, the temperature in the car was not regulated during the experiment. It may need to be controlled in future studies because this condition can possibly affect the psychological state and sweeping intensity of the driver. Third, the traffic flow theory [26] will be used in future research. From this theory, the speed of vehicles, traffic flow, density, road capacity, distance between vehicles will be considered to extend the developed methods. Fourth, the accuracies of models obtained can be improved by increasing their sensitivity to emotional states of the driver, such as anger, stress, etc., as discussed in the previous studies [24,50].

Few additional observations will need to be considered in the future. For example, direct data evaluation shows that EDA are doubled in the morning routine (EDA=0153). A higher EDA level was observed in the morning session on 12 January 2018, which was the coldest observed day with a temperature of -14 C. The experiment was repeated. Potentially, this indicates that weather conditions and the time of day affect the driver's EDA signal, and these factors will be considered in the future. This and further research can find many applications, including the development of wearable devices containing physiological sensors.

# 5. Conclusions

This study presented the findings on the level of driving stress depending on road types, using the EDA signals. The study confirmed that EDA is a significant indicator of the psychological state and an effective tool to determine the stress levels in real driving conditions. Advantages of the developed method are that developed models have an ability to predict stress levels under actual driving conditions with over 80% accuracy. The developed models can also be used to classify stress in different traffic situations and different road types. The definition of a traffic jam state in driving situation is one of the most important contributions of this study. One of the characteristics of the method is the ability to classify the stress level without considering the state of the driver. With the preliminary intervention, the proposed model and the experimental procedure are expected to be easily reconfigured by other researchers. One of the disadvantages of the proposed model is that the traffic congestion concept needs to be defined and improved more specifically based on traffic flow theory.
