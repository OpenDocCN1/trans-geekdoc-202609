Larger kernel receptive fields can enhance network capability to capture spatial context, which is beneficial to reconstruct large and complex edges. However, ordinary convolutions require a large number of parameters to expand the receptive fields. In contrast to dilated convolutions, dilated convolution has zero-padding inside its kernels, injecting zeros into defined fields to expand receptive field size, as shown in Figure 2. Thus, dilated convolutions can view larger input portions without requiring a pooling layer, resulting in no spatial dimension loss and reduced computational time.

For semantic segmentation, Reference [35] proposes a new convolutional architecture that fully exploits dilated convolutions for multi-scale context aggregation. Reference [36] proposes simple yet effective, gridding methods by studying the deconvolution of dilated convolutions. In these studies, dilated convolutions replace the upsample layers to keep the output resolutions the same as the input. For other tasks such as audio generation [37], video modeling [38] and machine translation [39], dilated convolutions are used to capture global views of inputs with few parameters. WaveNet [37] recognizes speech from audio waveforms. The dilation factor in Reference [37] is doubled, starting from 1 to a fixed number for every forward layer; then, the pattern is repeated.

Figure 2 illustrates how dilated convolutions enlarge the receptive fields by altering dilation factors (d). When dilation factors are increased exponentially, the pixels between the original kernel elements progressively grow; this causes the receptive field to expand. In Figure 2a, a dilated field of 1 (Dilated convolution) is performed in a 3x3 on a feature map. We observed that the 1-Dilated convolution is the same as the 3x3 standard convolution. When the dilation factor is set to 2 as shown in Figure 2, the region of the receptive field increases dramatically to 7x7 pixels. In Figure 2c when the dilation factor is changed to 4 and the receptive field is 15x15 pixels. In Figure 2, the group of red boxes represented by the gray area and the blue boxes indicates the meaning of the dilation factors that are applied to the kernel. The most important factor is the number of space pixels between the original kernel elements. In our work, we track 7 dilated convolution layers to perceive a wider range for capturing global contexts of input feature maps.
