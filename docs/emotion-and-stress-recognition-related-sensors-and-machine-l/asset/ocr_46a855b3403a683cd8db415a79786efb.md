Another research [68] locates facial points using Gabor filters based boosted classifiers, which the authors used an adaptive version of Viola-Jones algorithm, using GentleBoost of Adaabo, to detect the face. Also, Gabor wavelet was used for feature extraction, detecting 20 ROIs that represent the facial points. All detection was made automatically and contact-free using the iris and mouth. These parts were detected by dividing the image into two regions, and calculating proportions to find those regions (iris and mouth). From this, all ROIs were used to recognize emotions. Their success rate was high, the algorithm achieved 93% of success rate using the Kohon-Kanade database, which has expressiveness of 200 people. Although Gabor wavelet transform is a representative method to extract local features, it takes a long time and has a large dimension.

Another method was proposed in Ref. [65], where a deep Boltzmann machine (DBM) was used to recognize emotions from facial images, using a previous database and the participation of 38 adult volunteers. Their evaluation of finding the emotion value, could be positive or negative, and their accuracy reached 62.9%. In their study, since the face and the background had different temperatures, they were split by using the Otsu threshold algorithm to binarize images. Then, the projection (both vertical and horizontal) were calculated to find the largest gradient and detect the face boundary.

Additionally, a model for expression recognition using thermal images of an adult volunteer was proposed in Ref. [66]. These authors used feature extraction for the volunteer's facial images through PCA to recognize five emotions (happiness, anger, sad, and neutral). As highlight, that proposal reached an accuracy of 77%, in which, they evaluated and trained the system with a set of images, used PCA to reduce the dimensionality, and distance to recognize emotions.

In Ref. [63], authors were able to achieve 81.95% of accuracy using histogram feature extraction combined with SVM over thermal images of 22 volunteers in the Kotani Thermal Face database, and four classes studied: They used preprocessing techniques to prepare the image using Viola-Jones and for further image de-noising the image and using the Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization. For ROI detection, a ratio-based segmentation was used.

Moreover, the recognition of baseline and affective states was carried out in Ref. [41], to detect the face, they used a headset to keep it in position, in addition to a reference point (located on the top of the head), which was about 10 °C colder than the skin temperature. To find the ROIs, the reference point was used, and a radiometric threshold was applied. In case of loss of the reference point, it was manually corrected by the researchers.

Another study [67] applied ITTI in a female adult volunteer, and Neural Networks and Backpropagation were used to recognize emotions, such as happiness, surprise and neutral state, reaching an ACC of 90%. To find the face they used segmentation, and the Fever's data was used in the binary image with the center of gravity of the human body. Then, after segmenting the image, the positions of the face based on FACS-AU were used to determine the variation and, thus, emotion.

Another work [61] showed a several approaches regarding emotion recognition and facial detection as machine learning and geometric feature-based process, in addition to SVM and a diversity of other classifiers. They also present the use of Micro Holens (MHL) for detecting human emotions by using an application that has been built to use MHL to detect faces and recognize emotion of feeling face. The set of emotions were composed of happiness, sadness, anger, surprise and neutral. Additionally, they used a webcam to detect emotions and compare with the result using MHL.
