## 3. Datasets

This study uses public datasets (MAHOB and DEAP) of physiological signals to test our proposal for a robust emotion recognition system. However, for both the EDA related data will be used in the experiments for this paper.

The dataset is called MAHOB and was collected by Soleymani Mohammad et al. [31]. The data is related to different physiological signals.

The data was collected from 30 healthy young adults who participated in the study. 17 of the participants were female and 13 of them were male. Their age varied from 19 to 40. The participants were 20 emotional video clips which were evaluated in terms of both valence and arousal using the Self-Assessment Manikins (SAM) questionnaire [32]. SAM is a prominent tool that visualizes the degree of valence and arousal by manikins. The participants distinguished a scale from 1 to 9, see Figure 1.

![](img/601340ca744b738277eef45ff127492a_414_0.png)

**Figure 1.** Self-assessment manikins scales for valence (above) and arousal (below) [32].

In the experiments for MAHOB, electroencephalogram (EEG), blood volume pulse (BVP), respiration pattern, skin temperature, electromyogram (EMG), electrocogram (EOG), electrocardiogram (ECG), and EDA of 30 participants were collected.

DEAP [33] is a multimodal dataset used to analyze human emotional states.

The stimuli used in the experiments were chosen in different steps. First, they selected 120 initial stimuli that were selected both semi-automatically and manually. Second, a one-minute highlight part was specified for each stimulus. Third, through a web-based subjective assessment experiment, 40 final stimuli were chosen.

During the physiological experiment, 32 participants evaluated 40 videos via a web interface used for subjective emotion assessment in terms of levels of arousal, valence, like/dislike, and familiarity. The age of participants between 19 to 37. Considering the classes/labels for DEAP, we considered the same classes as in Section 4.1.

In the experiment, electroencephalogram (EEG), BVP, response pattern, ST, electromyogram (EMG), electrooculogram (EEG), electrocardiogram (EC), and EDA of 32 participants were collected.

## 4. Classification using a Convolutional Network–CNN

In this section, we present, the labelling of EDA signals, the design details of the proposed CNN for emotion classification, and the evaluation metrics and results.

First, raw data of EDA were scaled so that the distribution is centered around 0, with a standard deviation of 1.1. After normalization, these stated [34] valence and arousal are identified for emotion classification.

The valence of 1-5 were mapped to "negative" and 6-9 to "positive", respectively. The arousal scale of 1-5 was mapped to "passive" and 6-9 to "active", respectively.

- High Valence/High Arousal (HVHA). This class includes emotions such as happy and excited.
- High Valence/Low Arousal (HVLA). This class includes emotions such as relaxed.
- High Valence/High Arousal (LVHA). This class includes emotions such as angry.
- Low Valence/Low Arousal (LVLA). This class includes emotions such as sad.

To perform the classification, we propose a deep architecture that consists of multiple layers followed by subsampling and ends with a fully connected layer. As illustrated in Figure 2.

![](img/601340ca744b738277eef45ff127492a_414_0.png)

C is the convolution layer, P is the max-pooling layer and SELU is the Scaled Exponential activation function.

The convolutional layers generate feature maps using SELU. The first layer has 16 nodes, the second layer has 32 nodes, the third layer has 64 nodes, the fourth layer has 128 nodes. The output layer is SoftMax.

A grid search has been used to fine-tune the CNN hyperparameters and to find the optimal number of filters and layers. We used GridSearchCV [37]. We have provided a dictionary of hyperparameters that should be checked during the performance evaluation. By default, the search uses one thread, but it can be configured to use all available cores to increase the calculation time. The Scikit-learn class has been combined with K to find what are the best hyperparameters. Additionally, cross is used to evaluate each individual model and the default of 10-fold cross-validation has been used.

All the previous results have been obtained using the following computer platform: Intel Core7-7820HK Quad-Core 2.90 GHz, 8 GB DDR4 RAM, NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 with 8 GB dedicated memory.

Additionally, we examine several classifiers to compare the performance of the existing models with that of the proposed one. In particular, Support Vector Machine (SVM) [38], K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) [39], Naive Bayes [40] and Random Forest [41] are used for benchmarking.
