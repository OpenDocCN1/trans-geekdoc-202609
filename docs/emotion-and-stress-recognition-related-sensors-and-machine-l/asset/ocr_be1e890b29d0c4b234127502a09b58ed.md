PhysNet [2] offers access to a large number of physiological records recorded under various conditions. We selected the driver data set [4] from the free accessible databases. It consists of various physiological records, ECG, electrocardiography (EMG), and respiration signals, which were recorded under the conditions of driving and resting. Healey et al. [14] tried to monitor real-world stress during driving situations. Among the recorded physiological records, we chose the ECG, which was sampled at 49 Hz. Modified load II electrodes were used to measure the ECG. Sixteen participant's records were uploaded to PhysNet. We excluded 2 subject's data, which did not contain a record of the marker for when the participants changed the driving region to highway or city. Finally, we selected 14 subject's records for our use in study. All the selected records included approximately 50-85 min of ECG measurements during driving and resting. Due to differences in traffic conditions, the total duration of experiments differed by subject. The participants were asked to make a rest for 15 min before and after driving.



Seventeen people (6 female and 11 male, 27.9 ± 3.3 years) participated in our experiment. We designed an experiment to induce mental fatigue in the participants using a mental task. The mathematical tests were developed based on Mental Imagery Stress Test (MIST) to elicit two levels of stress in mental. To do so, we simplified the MIST paradigm by two levels of difficulty. The participants had to solve the arithmetic problems and keep to answer the questions. The problems consisted of two levels: Moderate and high. The moderate level includes addition and multiplication operations of the high level, four types of arithmetic operations used. All participants ECG data was sampled at 26 Hz and measured by a T-REX TRX10000 sensor, the electrodes were placed in a modified load II configuration, which was the same as for the driving data set [14]. First, we measured the baseline ECG for 5 min while the participants took a rest. After the baseline measurement, the participants took the mental test twice (5 min each) at a moderate and a high level. They encountered more complicated mathematical problems during the high level test.
