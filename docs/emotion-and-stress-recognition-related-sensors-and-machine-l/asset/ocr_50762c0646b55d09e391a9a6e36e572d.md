**Abstract:** The goals of this study are the suggestion of a better classification method for detecting stress states based on raw electrocardiogram (ECG) data and the training for a deep neural network (DNN) with a smaller data set. We suggest an end-to-end architecture to detect stress using raw ECG. The architecture consists of successive stages that contain convolutional layers. In this study, two of data sets are used to train and validate the model. A driving data set and a arithmetic data set, smaller than the driving data set. We propose a transfer learning method to train a model with a small data set. The proposed model shows better performance, based on receiver operating curves, with conventional methods. The proposed model improves accuracy from 87.9% to 90.1%. The transfer learning method improves accuracy by 12.01% and 10.06% when 10 and 50% of ECG signals, respectively, are used in the model. In conclusion, our model outperforms others and so, our model can significantly contribute to mobile healthcare for stress management in daily life.

**Keywords:** stress; electrocardiogram; deep neural network; convolutional neural network



As interest in health concerns, the importance of stress management has grown. As many people are exposed to stress environments, they are more likely to suffer from physical and mental disorders. Stress has been shown to cause diseases such as depression, asthma, and autoimmune diseases [1]. To observe the changes in body caused by stress, many researchers have focused on physiological signals, such as electrocardiography (ECG) signals and galvanic skin response [2]
When a person receives stress stimulation, his/her autonomic nervous system reacts to the stress, which results in physiological changes [3]. Among physiological signals, the ECG enables us to observe how our bodies react to stress. An ECG is an electrical signal which is generated by heart activity. An ECG has three components: the P-wave, QRS-complex, and T-wave. Among them, the time-intervals between successive R peaks are used to calculate the heart rate variability (HRV) [4]. The HRV can be represented by various parameters that have been calculated along the time, frequency, and non-linear domains. These HRV parameters have been used for stress recognition [5–7].
