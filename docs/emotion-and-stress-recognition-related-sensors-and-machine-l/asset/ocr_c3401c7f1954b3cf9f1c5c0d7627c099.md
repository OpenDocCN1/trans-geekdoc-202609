# # Dilated Skip Convolution for Facial Landmark Detection

**Seyha Chim, Jin-Gu Lee and Ho-Yun Park \*

School of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Chung-Ang University, 84 Heukseokro, Dongiak-gu, Seoul 06974, Korea; sehyachim@cau.ac.kr (S.C.); dlwism21@cau.ac.kr (J-G.L)

* Correspondence: hoyun@cau.ac.kr; Tel: +82-10-3354-9180

Received: 26 September 2019; Accepted: 22 December 2019; Published: 4 December 2019

**Abstract:** Facial landmark detection has gained enormous interest for face-related applications due to its success in facial analysis tasks such as facial recognition, cartoon generation, face tracking and facial expression analysis. Many studies have been proposed and implemented to deal with the challenges of localizing facial landmarks from given images, including large appearance variations and partial occlusion. Studies have differed in the way they use the facial appearances and shape information of images. In our work, we consider facial information within both global and local contexts. We aim to obtain pixel-level accuracy for localizing spatial relationships between each key point in the first stage and integrate global-context information in the second stage. The pipeline of our architecture consists of two main components: (1) a network for local-context subnet that generates heatmap features via fully convolution filters; (2) a dilated skip convolution subnet—a combination of dilated convolutions and skip-connections—that are in charge of refining the local appearancemaps. Through this proposed architecture, we demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging datasets—including LFPW, HELEN, 300 and AFLW2000-3—by leveraging fully convolutional DenseNets, skip-connections and dilated convolution without further post-processing.

**Keywords:** face landmark detection; fully convolutional DenseNets; skip-connections; dilated convolutions



In computer vision, facial landmark detection is known as alignment and is a crucial part of face recognition operations. Its algorithms attempt to predict the locations of the facial landmark coordinates that vary owing head movements and facial expressions. These landmarks are located at major parts of the face, such as the contours of the nose, chin, eyes, and corners of the mouth ([1]) in review. Facial landmark detection has sparked much interest recently as it is a prereite for many vision applications, including facial recognition [2], facial recognition [3,4], face merging [2,5], face modelling [6] and human-computer interactions [7]. In recent years, considerable research works [8–10] have developed remarkable networks to predict facial landmark location more accurately under challenging conditions, such as large appearance variations, face occlusion and difficult illumination. Facial landmark detection is classified into three types of methods: holistic, constrained local model (CLM), and regression-based. Among these, regression-based approaches [5,11] have demonstrated superiority in efficiency and accuracy, even in challenging scenarios. Regression-based methods contain two stages: early and updated. The incentive key points are located on the face in the early stage and gradually refined in the updated stage.
