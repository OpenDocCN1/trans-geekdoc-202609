## Materials and Methods

An experimental mobile interactive and multimodal emotional-cognitive load scenario was designed and implemented for the induction of cognitive and emotional states in an HCI setting. Based on this mobile and interactive scenario, multimodal data were acquired using the University of Ulm Multimodal Affective Corpus (uulmMAC). The basic concept of our cognitive load scenario follows a generic setup by Schüssel et al. who proposed a gamified setup for the exploration of various aspects with potential influence on users' way of interaction [51]. The generic scheme is, however, an abstract foundation for HCI exploration with no specific field. The induction of emotional and cognitive states depends on various factors related to the specific nature of human reactions [52]. For our research focusing on emotional and cognitive states in-life HCI, the development of the current experiment required further developments with an in-depth analysis and re-implementation of the generic paradigm such to match the requirements of cognitive load and affective states. The main contributions include the design of the interaction sequences scheme including cognitive, emotional and neutral states (Section 2.2), the development of the emotional structure (Section 2.3) and the software implementation and platform embedding (Section 2.4). Furthermore, for the experimental data acquisition, we developed and implemented a technical infrastructure with multimodal systems for the experiment and recording setup (Section 2.5).

### 2.1. Participants and Consent

The uulmMAC dataset consists of two homogenous samples of 60 participants (30 females, 30 males; 17-27 years; mean age = 21.65 years, SD = 25) with a total of 100 recording sessions (N = 100) about 45 minutes each. The 60 subjects were recruited and distributed at the University of Ulm. The first sample includes 40 subjects who underwent one measurement, while the second sample consists of 20 subjects who underwent three measurements. The three different measurements were acquired at different times with one week of time-interval between. The second sample allows for instance the investigation of attentional research questions. While both subjects underwent exactly the same experiment they slightly differ modality acquisition: the first sample includes facial electromyography (EMG) measurements, allowing better conditions for the analysis of facial expressions via data. Both subjects are evenly balanced between male and female. All subjects gave their informed consent for inclusion before they participated in the experiment and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Ulm: Project C4 - SFB RRR62.

In summary, the original dataset of uulmMAC consists of 100 individual recording sessions: The first sample with 40 recording sessions (40 subjects x 1 measurement) and the second sample with 60 recording sessions (20 subjects x 3 measurements).

### 2.2. The Interaction Scheme

The goal of the experiment was the induction of various dialog-based cognitive and emotional states in a real HCI environment. Furthermore, the participants were asked by the system to solve a series of cognitive games in order to test their reaction to various cognitive tasks, varying from high interest and overcoming boredom and frustrating levels. The aim of each game task was to identify the single one that is unique in shape and color (i.e., the number 2 in Figure 1), the number 36 and the based on a visual search task. The difficulty was set by adjusting the number of objects, shapes and colors shown per task as well as the available time given to solve that task. Thus, cognitive Overload was induced by increasing the task field objects and decreasing the available time, while cognitive Underload was induced by decreasing the task field objects and increasing the available time. Further, for each individual task, the student could earn a certain amount of money (up to ten cents) according to the individual speed of the response. The amount of reward money earned for solving a task was increasingly reduced, the longer the student needed to answer. If the given answer was incorrect, the participant received no reward at all for that particular task. Figure 1 shows screenshots of the visual search task.

### 2.3. Experiment Structure

The experiment consists of six induction sequences, separated by subjective feedback related to the actual sequence, and followed by a respiration baseline and a summary of the achieved results in that. While the experimental sequences are used to induce cognitive and emotional states, the subjective feedback is used for the validation of the induction. These are described in details in the following subsections. Prior to the experiment, each subject received an introduction and instructions to the experimental steps in form of a PowerPoint presentation and was asked to fill three questionnaires related to: (1) emotion regulation based on the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) [53,54]; (2) emotional control based on the Trait Emotional Questionnaire (TEIQ-SF) [55,56]; and (3) personality traits based on the Test Item Personality Scale (TIPS) [57,58]. These questionnaires are also used as further evaluation of the stability of the induction procedure.

### 2.3.1. Induction Sequences

Six sequences of different difficulties, with four tasks assigned, for the induction of six different emotional and cognitive load states. All tasks within a sequence have the same or comparable difficulty levels. The first inductive sequence is designed to induce Interest and is of more difficulty to gain the users' interest and familiarize them with the search task procedure. The Interest sequence has 40 tasks and is designed with a map of 3 x 3 and 4 x 4 matrices, and 10 time per task to give the answer. The second sequence is designed to induce Overload and consists of 40 tasks with a 6 x 6 matrix and short time of 6 s per task to provide an answer. The third sequence has a Normal difficulty and is defined with 40 tasks with 4 x 4 matrices. This Normal sequence is the neutral (cognitive and emotional) state to be considered as baseline between the sequences. The fourth sequence is implemented as an Easy sequence with 40 tasks with 3 x 3 matrices and very long time of 100 s for responding. In order to induce Underload, the fifth sequence is defined as a repetition of the previous Easy sequence of low difficulty, with 40 tasks with 3 x 3 matrices and 100 s to provide an answer. This originates from the idea that repeating a well-known task in the same way twice, generates a state of Underload. Finally, the sixth sequence is intended to induce Frustration.

| Sequence | Induction | Number of Tasks | Search Field | Time (s) to Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sequence 1 | Interest | 40 | 3 x 3 and 4 x 4 | 10 |
| Sequence 2 | Overload | 40 | 6 x 6 | 6 |
| Sequence 3 | Normal | 40 | 4 x 4 | 10 |
| Sequence 4 | Easy | 40 | 3 x 3 | 100 |
| Sequence 5 | Underload | 40 | 3 x 3 | 100 |
| Sequence 6 | Frustration | 40 | 3 x 3 and 4 x 4 | 10 |

*(Every) Sequence is induced by subjective feedback, respiration baseline, and results summary. The user-system interaction during all tasks is a mobile interaction conducted via speech while the participants could freely move in the room (standing position). The walking area is limited to 1 m × 3 m, represented by an electrostatic mat to prevent any signal caused by electrostatic interference.*

### 2.3.2. Subjective Feedback

In order to evaluate the validity of the induction paradigm, various kinds of subjective feedback are implemented, including *Free Speech, SAM Ratings,* and *Direct Questions*. These are presented on the screens as shown in Figure 2. After each of the six accomplished sequences, the participants provided a series of information about their current state in three different ways; (1) expressing in open words via *Free Speech* of 12 s duration how they felt during that sequence, (2) rating their emotions via Self-Assessment-Manikin *SAM Ratings* and (3) answering *Direct Questions* regarding the assessment of their performance. The aim of this subjective feedback is to determine the current subjective emotional state experienced in that particular sequence, which, turn, can be used as ground truth to validate the induction paradigm. While the Free feedbacks are given via natural speech, logging of the SAM Ratings and Direct Questions was carried out per mouse-click to ensure correct logging documentation. The user was thereby guided and instructed by the system via speech output. The user-system interaction modality (mouse, speech or both) within the experiment is part of the technical implementation as described in Section 2.4.
