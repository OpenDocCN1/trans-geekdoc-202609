This method was used in the human detection area, further by using object detectors and finally, here for analyzing and representing the facial emotions. The descriptor HOG can quickly and efficiently describe the local shape and appearance of objects by counting the orientations of gradients in a local position of the images. In this study, the images are divided into small cells, and for every cell the histogram of the gradient is calculated. This is done to represent spatial information of the face. For every image, in our study, 48 cells are constructed of every image by building a cell with 18 × 16 pixels. A horizontal gradient filter [-1 0] was applied with 59 orientation bins in the study. Final done was the concatenation of the HOG descriptors of each cell to form a HOG feature vector (size of 2832 (48 × 59)).

The Laplacian eigenmap algorithm was used in our study to reduce the dimensionality of the data. Furthermore, the high-dimensional data was mapped to a 29-dimensional space. Discriminative hyper planes are found by the SVM classifiers the highest margin for dividing the data that belongs to the different classes. however, several kernel types can affect the efficiency of the SVM classifier. ## 3.2. Databases Considered

Training of ML algorithms on the type and size of database used. A low number of images in a database is used for training, can cause under-fitting. To counter this, we need a large database for training the ML algorithms. The estimations and results are remarkably affected by the use of larger databases over the smaller ones; for more accurate results a database is required in abundance. Emotion classification and their intensity estimations require a vast and varied dataset for validation and testing. The images used for intensity estimation and recognition of emotion are spontaneous and needed:

- Posed Databases: For capturing extreme emotions. The disadvantage is the artificial human behavior.
- Spontaneous Datasets: Natural human behavior. However, it is extremely time-consuming for capturing the right emotion.

A close, relationship exists between the models used for the intensity of facial emotions and the databases used. Mainly five databases CK [2,6], JFFE [64], B-DE and an in-house database of 200 images (images of each AU of ethnic/racial) taken in an uncontrolled environment through a web camera. Here is a brief description of each dataset:

1. JFFE: The database contains 213 images of 7 facial expressions (6 basic facial expressions + 1 neutral) by 10 Japanese female models. Each has been rated on 6 emotion adjectives by 60 Japanese subjects. (Posed – no AUs)
2. DISFA: In this database the images were acquired using PIGrey stereo imaging system at high resolution (1024 × 768). Each has 66 facial landmark points manually scored by two human FACS experts. The database also includes 66 facial landmark points.
3. CK [2,6]: The database contains 213 images of 7 facial expressions (6 basic facial expressions + 1 neutral) by 10 Japanese female models. Each has been rated on 6 emotion adjectives by 60 Japanese subjects. (Posed – no AUs)
4. B-DE: The database contains 213 images of 7 facial expressions (6 basic facial expressions + 1 neutral) by 10 Japanese female models. Each has been rated on 6 emotion adjectives by 60 Japanese subjects. (Posed – no AUs)
5. In-house database: The database contains 200 images (images of each AU of ethnic/racial) taken in an uncontrolled environment through a web camera.
