For a long time, most emotion recognition studies have focused on using facial expressions and for continuous monitoring purposes, these approaches may not be the most suitable, as they may suffer from practical issues, such as ambient light and noise. For camera-based facial privacy is also a concern. Alternatively, physiological signals, such as galvanic skin response (GSR), electrocardiogram (EEG), skin temperature (ST), and electroencephalogram (EEG), which occur continuously and are harder to conceal. As emotions are thought to be related to activity in brain areas that direct, motivate behavior, and determine the significance of what is going on us, EEG, which is the signal from fluctuations in the brain that are generated at the level of cellular membranes [6], has especially of interest.

Emotion classification has been shown to achieve high accuracy [1,7–16]. In reality, these conventional channel EEG headsets are not suitable for continuous monitoring due to their size and setup difficulty. The EEG recording device used for emotion monitoring should be small, little time to set up, and be comfortable to wear.

For requirements, an in-ear EEG which an EEG recording device introduced by Looney et al. [2012] could be of interest. The potential benefits of using an in-ear type include that it does not obstruct the visual fact. It is also robust, as it is generally fixed inside the ears. And as it is similar to devices people use, such as earphones, and earplugs. It is unlikely to encounter sweat, and also user-friendly for setup and maintenance. Unlike scalp EEG, which may require some experienced assistants to help, in-ear EEG also has drawbacks. An in-ear EEG has much fewer electrodes and a much smaller area than the scalp EEG. So, its application is more convenient than that of the scalp EEG.

Our work was aimed at building an in-ear EEG device and evaluating it in terms of signal quality classification compared to those methods (i.e., T7 and T8 from the international 10-20 system [18]). The international 10-20 system is an internationally recognized system for labelling scalp locations for EEG measurement. The T7 position is located above the left ear, while T8 is positioned above the right ear. The prospect of an in-ear usage for emotion classification was investigated by experiments.
