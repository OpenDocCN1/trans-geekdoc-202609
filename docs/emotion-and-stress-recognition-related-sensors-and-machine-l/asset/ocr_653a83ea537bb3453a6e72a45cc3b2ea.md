# 2. Multimodal Sensing System

The multimodal sensing approach is not a new architecture or new method to aim for a recognition system, it is a more robust and powerful approach to be applied in situations in which a low amount of inputs (channels) are not sufficient to reach a good recognition accuracy along the time. This approach is on several channels (inputs) that may come from different sources of data. It is sometimes challenging for researchers during the time and multiple sampling rate synchronization.

For research based on contexts like emotion recognition based on physiological data, it is not recommended to use only one data e.g., heart variability, to accurately detect emotions, because it can reflect emotions only in strong or intense emotional situations [25]. According to some studies, when an extended period of physiological data is considered, better results can be reached.

# 2.1. Experiment

A total of 13 simulated flights ($N_{flights} = 13$) and eight beginner users on flight simulator ($N_{pilots} = 8$) were considered, using the Microsoft Flight Simulator Steam Edition (FSX-SE). These flights were used as: RC1, RC2, RC3, GC1, GS1, LS1, LS2, VC1, VC2, CR1, CR3 and CL3.

The proposed experiment corresponds to the human behaviour of the users (pilot in) some flight tasks such as: Take off (Task 1), climbing (Task 2), flight (cruise navigation) (Task 3), descent (Task 4), approach (Task 5), final (Task 6) and landing (Task 7). The environment's setup from the main equipment, the result of the initial Proof of Concepts (PoCs). Several improvements from these PoCs were: a large screen to improve the immersive experience during the simulation; addition of a computer to run the flight simulator and record results; the user only use the joystick during the experimental flight and must use only one hand to control the flight; the GSR sensors were placed on the hand, i.e., without movements to avoid motion effects; a microcontroller was used to acquire the HR data from HR device (e.g., Arduino board); two sensors were to receive HR and GSR data from Bluetooth communication, and another to receive the Bluetooth data from EEG device; also a video camera was used to record the users' body movements.

The users were trained before the experiment regarding flight tasks and procedures. During the main experiment, they had no contact with the experiment supervisor, who only interfered before and after each simulation. It was also recommended to the users to talk and make the hand with GSR sensors.
