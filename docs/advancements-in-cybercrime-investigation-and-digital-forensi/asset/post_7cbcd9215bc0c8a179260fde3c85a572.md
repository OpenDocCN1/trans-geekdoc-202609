## 8.3 NEED FOR CYBERSECURITY IN WORLD WIDE WEB

The business world isn’t new to data breaches and cyber threats. We have already witnessed the Yahoo data breach in 2016, which turned into a massive catastrophe. The changes introduced by the pandemic, including remote working, more agencies moving online, and switching to digital payments, have given rise to even additional digital threats.

For many small companies, the switch to digital was surprising and provided little time to reinforce existing security features or enforce new processes. Even larger brands found it difficult to protect data while managing remote teams and using new software programs, including Trello, Slack, Flock, and more. In 2020, Microsoft suffered a malicious attack that compromised over 280 million Microsoft customer records that included

electronic mail addresses, IP addresses and support case information. A major incident happened in April of 2020 when websites hosted by San Francisco International Airport suffered a cyber-attack in which hackers tried to obtain login credentials.

The recent growth in data breaches has caused a marketplace surplus in stolen identities. Normally, this will result in marketplace saturation; however, because of the number of possible fraud schemes, the use of stolen identities has continued growing, older identities still have resale value. With the stolen data traded mainly over the Internet, these developments have been reflected in changes to the web marketplace for stolen identities. The online market for stolen identities has evolved over the course of three generations. First-generation data breaches were exceptionally small, with the early Internet used primarily to facilitate the purchase instead of the sale of the identities. The second generation saw the development of an Internet-based wholesale marketplace and, with the advent of larger breaches, specialization acting in the fraud cells. The third and present generation is showing market maturity further, with the differentiation of wholesale and retail markets. The dark net and cryptocurrencies have caused direct-to-consumer sales of identities, which has had a widespread effect on the marketing and pricing of stolen identities.

The rapid shift to digital has uncovered many vulnerabilities that firms currently face. Based on the World Economic Forum’s Global Risks Report (2021), the cyber threat is among the top global threats that need to be countered urgently.

Digital transformation has not only eliminated certain physical barriers but has also blurred boundaries among countries. While this is ideal for companies seeking to operate in a global market, it has also created more threats of cross-border cyber-attacks. While ransomware, phishing, and social media hacking attacks continue to rise, organizations need to prioritize data security. This is essential because it is legally required as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) asks businesses in Europe to protect personal data.

Moreover, any breach of protection isn’t only detrimental to the corporation’s image and has a financial implication. A business that has a well-protected cybersecurity system will have the consumer’s trust as well. This ensures the protection of any sensitive data, durability, and smooth functioning of the organization.

If you believe that it is only the most influential countries and governments that need to be looking for cyber-attacks, then think again. The corporate sector is equally at risk and needs to ensure that it isn’t

prone to hazards. This is why corporations need to take appropriate steps to put special protection checks in place. Also, it’s not just the large organizations but companies of all sizes that need to put strong defenses in the face of cyber threats. While the future is unpredictable, it is always wise to have a plan of action to protect the organization’s sensitive data before any considerable harm happens.

When most of the transactions are happening electronically, they’re also becoming the source of crime. Lots of data shared, money transferred are the target of cybercriminals. All organizations are growing their technical base, increasingly depending upon the Internet for transactions without studying the threat associated with the technology. Cybercriminals manipulate financial data, can transfer electronic ownership, interrupt communications with personnel or business partners, steal intellectual property, harm a company’s reputation, or bring e-commerce (or an entire business) to a close.

E-commerce business is distinct in operations, so it has specific challenges and risks as the absence of physical presence increases the possibilities of frauds and crimes. There are many more reasons in India for rampant e-commerce cyber frauds. The cost of cybercrime will increase as more business functions move online and as consumers and groups around the world are gathering on cyberspace. The risk of intellectual property theft will increase, resulting in counterfeit products. The use of technology theft causes enormous loss to the parent organizations because it lessens the rate of return on the improvements. The government must take the strong initiative to combat the challenges of cybercrime; otherwise, this will negatively impact the business. Globally cybercrime losses are more than 400 billion dollars every year.
