A summary of the results of this ANOVA is shown in Table 16.3, where we see that duration has a significant effect. As videos increase in length, so do viewers' feelings of discomfort indicated by ratings of unpleasantness. The difference in unpleasantness ratings was not significant; therefore, we cannot conclude that increased delay lowers ratings of unpleasantness in the population sample. Furthermore, the nonsignificant AC interaction means we cannot conclude that the delay of test affects the rate of increase of unpleasantness ratings with duration, despite the somewhat steeper slope in the immediate condition in Figure 16.1. It is possible that these effects are present in the population, but if so, we will require more participants and items to have sufficient power to determine this.

# 16.4.3 Estimating General $\omega^2$ (Omega-Squared)

A measure of the contributions of our factors to the data is desirable and required by most methods. The expected mean squares in Table 16.2 lead directly to one measure. Continuing with our example of the experiment in which videos were rated for unpleasantness, we calculated estimates of general $\omega^2$ for (A), delay (C), and their interaction. Although there are several definitions of omega-squared, we defined $\omega^2_s$ as the ratio of the variance of the source of interest divided by that variance plus all variances plus error variance. The variance and omega-squared estimates are contained in Table 16.4.
