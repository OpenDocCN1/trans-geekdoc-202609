In general, we have an $acn$ Latin square, with $n$ participants in each row of the square at each level of $C$; there are $ac$ participants, each tested in $a$ blocks. In the present example, $a=3, c=2, n=4$, so that there are 24 participants and a total of 72 scores. Table 15.10 presents the design and the means for the participants in each cell of the design.^2$ The mean indicates that the context supporting the less common $(A_1)$ results in longer reading times. We also find that poor readers are slower than good readers, although the difference varies only slightly as a function of context. Finally, we note that reading times differ slightly over lists (the blocks), but the differences are small. Significance tests are needed but we must first consider the sources of variance and their expected means.

The analysis is summarized in Table 15.11 for the case where $B$ is assumed to have random effects. As indicated in the SV column and expected means of Table 15.11, both row and between-cells residual (BCR) terms reflect possible $AB$ interaction effects. The $C \times Row$ and $C \times BCR$ reflect possible $ABC$ interaction effects. This is also indicated in the SV and EMS means of Table 15.11. These interaction effects are again a function of the confounding variables inherent in block designs; that, designs where participants are not tested in the complete set of combinations of $A$ and $B$.
