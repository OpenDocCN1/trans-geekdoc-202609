Blocking requires more time, effort, and often expense than random assignment. That will cost in more powerful tests and more precise estimates of effects only to the extent that the concomitant variable is a good predictor of performance.

# 12.4 The Analysis of Covariance

In both the blocking design and the analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), a concomitant variable is used to account for variance that would otherwise be treated as random (i.e., error). As we have seen, the blocking design reduces error by incorporating the concomitant variable, B, into the design and extracting variance associated with B from the error term. In ANCOVA, the concomitant variable, X, is not incorporated into the experimental design; rather, participants are assigned randomly to conditions without regard to their scores on X. However, the correlation of X with the dependent variable, Y, is used to remove from the total variance that the resulting, adjusted SS is then partitioned into total variability attributable to A and S/A. Thus, ANCOVA is like the blocking design in that it also uses the concomitant variable to reduce the error variance; however, it accomplishes the reduction statistically of incorporating X into the design of the experiment. ANCOVA is best developed within a regression framework and therefore we postpone most details until Chapter 24. For now, we will present ANCOVA conceptually, using a numerical example.
