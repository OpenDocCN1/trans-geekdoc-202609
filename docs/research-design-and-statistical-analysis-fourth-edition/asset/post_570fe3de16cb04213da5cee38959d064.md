## 16.4 Estimating $\omega^2$ (Omega-Squared)

A measure of the contributions of factors to the data is desirable and required by most methods. Continuing with our example of the experiment in which videos were rated for unpleasantness, we calculated $\omega^2$ for (A), delay (C), and their interaction. Although there are several definitions of omega-squared, we defined $\omega^2$ as the ratio of the source of interest divided by that variance plus all variances. The variance and omega-squared estimates are contained in Table 16.4. From the results in Panel b, it is clear that only duration makes more than negligible contribution to the variance.

## 16.5 An Alternative Design: The Latin Square

### 16.5.1 The Experimental Design

So far, we have assumed a design in which the 15 videos are divided into three sets of five. These sets are assigned to durations in a way that the assignment is balanced over all participants. As discussed in Chapters 12 and 15, the Latin square design is potentially more efficient, capable of reducing error variance to the order of testing.
