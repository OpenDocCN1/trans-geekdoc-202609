# 8.5.2 Estimating Strength of Association in the Population: $\omega^2$ (Omega-Squared)

Where $\eta^2$ describes the sample- and design-specific strength of association between the dependent and independent variables by forming a ratio of sum of squares, $\omega^2$ is a measure of the strength of association in the population; unlike $\eta^2$, it is a ratio of population variances:

```
$\omega^2 = \frac{\sigma_A^2}{\sigma_\epsilon^2 + \sigma_A^2}$
```

The numerator of the ratio is the variance of the treatment population means (the $\mu_i$) or,

```
$\sigma_A^2 = \frac{\sum_1^a (\mu_i - \mu)^2}{a}$
```

The denominator of $\omega^2$ is the total population variance; that is, the treatment error variance, $\sigma_\epsilon^2$, plus the variance of the treatment population means, $\sigma_A^2$. Thus, $\omega^2$ assesses the magnitude of the treatment effect relative to the total variance in the design.
