# 19.2.2 A Numerical Example

The standard regression equation (Equation 19.1) has two parameters about which we might want to draw inferences: $eta_0$ and $eta_1$. The value of the population regression line, $eta_0$, is the conditional mean of Y when X has a value of zero. Estimates of $eta_0$ are not always of interest, but we will see in Chapter 22 there are circumstances in which they may be useful. In contrast, the slope of the population regression line, $eta_1$, is always of interest. If it differs from zero, there is a linear relationship between X and Y. More importantly, the value of the slope tells us much, on the average, Y increases (decreases) with an increase of one unit in X.

As an example, consider the data presented in Table 19.1 and Figure in 19.1. 20 participants each look at a computer screen and are presented with an array of letters. They are asked to indicate whether a specified target letter is present in the group. Groups of five participants are assigned to sizes of two, four, six and eight letters, and the times (in milliseconds) to respond correctly when the target letter is recorded. Each participant completes one data point. Generally, when the letter arrays are larger, it takes longer to respond to the presence of a target letter, so the data are reasonably well fit by a linear equation. Here the assumption that X is a fixed-effect variable is satisfied because the array sizes are chosen by the researcher and can be measured without error.
