# 12.3.3 Relative Efficiency

We have presented an argument that the error term will be smaller and, consequently, will be higher in the $T \times B$ design than the CR design. However, the argument was based on a single example, so it is fair to question the generality of conclusion. Will the $T \times B$ design have more power than the corresponding CR design? If not, what factors determine the relative power of the two designs? When the $T \times B$ design has more power, is the advantage worth the time, effort, and effort spent in executing the design the trouble? Key to answering these questions is the concept of relative efficiency (RE).

This condition will hold if there is a correlation between the blocking variable and the dependent variable. Typically, it is a simple matter for a researcher to identify a blocking variable that is related to the dependent variable, the efficiency of the $T \times B$ design will generally be greater than that of the CR design.

# 12.3.4 How Many Blocks?
