# Research Design and Statistical Analysis

The $\mu_{jk}$ may vary for any of three reasons; namely, they correspond to different levels of $A$ and different combinations of those levels. We can represent this idea by the following identity:

```
$(\mu_{jk} - \mu) = (\mu_j - \mu) + (\mu_k - \mu) - [(\mu_{jk} - \mu) - (\mu_j - \mu) - (\mu_k - \mu)] \quad (9.3)$
```

A simpler notation

```
$$\mu_{jk} - \mu = \alpha_j + eta_k + (\alpha\beta)_{jk} \quad (9.4)$
```

Equation 9.4 states that $\mu_{jk} - \mu$ is a sum of three effects. The first of these, $\alpha_j$ is the *main effect of treatment* $A_j$; its value represents the extent to which the average score in the population defined by the treatment $A_j$ differs from the mean of all the scores in the $ab$ populations. Similarly, $eta_k$ is the main effect of treatment $B$ and reflects the extent to which the average score in population $B$ differs from the average of all scores in the $ab$ populations. Finally, $(\alpha\beta)_{jk}$ is the *interaction effect* of $A$ and $B$. The interaction is the difference between $\mu_{jk}$ and $\mu$ that remains after removal of the main effects of $A$ and $B_j$; that is,

```
$(\alpha\beta)_{jk} = (\mu_{jk} - \mu) - (\mu_j - \mu) - (\mu_k - \mu) \quad (9.5a)$
```

This interaction is more often represented by simplifying the right-hand term in Equation 9.5a:

```
$(\alpha\beta)_{jk} = \mu_{jk} - \mu_j - \mu_k + \mu \quad (9.5b)$
```

From Equation 9.4, we can substitute for $\mu_{jk} - \mu$ in Equation 9.2. Recognizing that nuisance variances will also contribute to the observed data, we have the structural model underlying tests of hypotheses for the two-factor design:

```
$Y_{jk} - \mu = \alpha_j + \beta_k + \varepsilon_{jk} \quad (9.6)$
```

In reality, the variability among the populations has four possible sources: the effects of manipulating A; the effects of manipulating B; the joint, or interaction, effects of A and B; and error component. A detailed summary of the model components and related assumptions is presented in Box 9.1.
