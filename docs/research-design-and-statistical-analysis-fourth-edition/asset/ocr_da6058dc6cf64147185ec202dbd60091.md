Given these assumptions, we can apply the multiplicative law to compute the probability of each sequence of outcomes because each sequence corresponds to a joint event. We assume trials are independent, so the probability of any sequence may be found by multiplying probabilities. The probability of the sequence $<C_i, C_j, C_k> = (.25)(.25)(.25) = .015625$. The probability of the sequence $<E_i, E_j, E_k> = (.75)(.75)(.75) = .4218$. The probability of each single sequence corresponding to one correct and two errors is greater than .1055. The four sequences are mutually exclusive and we are interested in any one of them occurring, so the additive law may be used to compute the probability of the compound event. Summing up the probabilities of the four possible sequences gives us a value of .420 for the probability of one correct in four trials. By the same approach, we can compute that the probability of two correct in four trials is .219, and the probability of three correct in four trials is .0469.
