Sequential methods provide more powerful tests, when hypothesis tests are conducted. However, sequential methods are not applicable to the construction of confidence intervals because there is no ordering within a set of confidence intervals. Finally, we emphasize that contrasts should be planned whenever possible because a set of planned contrasts is always smaller than a set of unplanned contrasts. Thus, tests of planned contrasts generally have more power than tests conducted on other kinds of families. But perhaps the most important benefit of planning contrasts is that the careful thought that is required to specify the research questions will probably lead to research designs that are more closely focused on those questions.
