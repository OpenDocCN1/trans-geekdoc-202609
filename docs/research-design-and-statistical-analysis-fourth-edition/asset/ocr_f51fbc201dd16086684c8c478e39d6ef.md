# 11.2 Introduction to the Experiment

The effects of the organization of factual material upon the memory have been studied by several researchers. In one classic study (Myers, Pozdek, & Coulson, 1973), participants read a series of 25 sentences, each of which related the name of one of five fictional countries with an attribute (e.g., climate, principal industry, language). The sentences were presented to participants in five paragraphs, with each paragraph on a separate page. For the Name organization group, a paragraph consisted of five sentences describing five attributes of a single country; each attribute was from a different category. For the Attribute organization group, each paragraph presented attributes for a single category for each country; a page might state the different climates of the five countries, or the different languages. For the Random organization, the 25 facts were randomly placed, to a paragraph. Subjects read each paragraph for 40 seconds and after reading all five paragraphs, wrote down as many of the 25 facts as they could recall. The Attribute organization resulted in significantly better recall than either of the two types of organization; there was little difference between the Name and Random results.
