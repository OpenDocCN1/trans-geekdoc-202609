A second possible F may be constructed, using the same four MS terms:

```
$$F_i^2 = \frac{MS_{SA} + MS_{SAB}}{MS_{AB} + MS_{SA}} $$ (14.5)$
```

The rationale, is that expectations of numerator and denominator differ only with respect to the null hypothesis component. In Equation 14.5 the ratio of EMS is

```
$$(2)(\sigma_A^2 + \sigma_{SAB}^2) + b_A^2\sigma_{AB}^2 + bn\theta_A^2$$
$$(2)(\sigma_A^2 + \sigma_{SAB}^2) + b_{SA}^2 + n\sigma_{AB}^2$$
```

which equals 1 if the null hypothesis $(\theta_A^2=0)$ is true. The ratio of EMS terms equals 1 is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for the ratio of mean squares to be distributed as F. An important condition is that both the numerator and denominator must be distributed as $\chi^2$ variables divided by their degrees of freedom. Satterwaite (1946) has shown that under the usual assumptions of analysis of variance, a linear combination of mean squares has approximately this same sampling distribution.

The denominator of $F_i'$ and the numerator and denominator of $F_i''$ are special cases of combinations of mean squares (CMS) that have the general form

```
$$CMS = w_1V_1 + w_2V_2 + \dots + w_kV_k + \dots + w_KV_K$$ (14.6)$
```

where $w_k$ is any real number, and $V_k$ is either a mean square or a variance. Such terms have degrees of freedom that are more complicated than the usual ones. The general form of the degrees of freedom for any CMS is

```
$$df_{CMS} = (CMS)^2 / \sum_k w_k^2V_k^2 / df_k$$ (14.7)$
```

where $df_k$ are the degrees of freedom associated with the $k$th mean square. $F_i'$ in Equation 14.4, the numerator degrees of freedom are $a-1$. The denominator of $F_i'$ is

```
$$CMS = (1)(MS_{SAB}) + (1)(MS_{SA}) + (-1)(MS_{SAB})$
```

and inserting this the degrees of freedom for each of the three mean squares into Equation 14.7, the denominator of $F_i'$ are

```
$$df_{denominator} = \frac{(MS_{SA} + MS_{SAB} - MS_{SAB})^2}{MS_{SA}^2 / df_{SA} + MS_{SAB}^2 / df_{AB} + MS_{SAB}^2 / df_{SAB}}$$ (14.8)$
```

For $F_i''$ the appropriate df for their numerator are

```
$$df_{num} = \frac{(MS_{SA} + MS_{SAB})^2}{MS_{SA}^2 / df_A + MS_{SAB}^2 / df_{SAB}}$$ (14.9)$
```
