# Box 15.1 Quasi-F Test of A for the Data of Table 15.1
(B Nested in Levels of A)

To test the effects of mood (A),

$F' = MS_{SA} / (MS_{SA} + MS_{B/A} - MS_{SB/A})$

Substituting the MS values from Table 15.3 into this equation, we have

$F' = 1,073,858 / (98,662 + 260,647 - 41,107) = 3.375$

The degrees of freedom for the numerator of this test are $a - 1 = 2$ and the denominator  
degrees of freedom are

```
$df_{den} = \frac{(MS_{SA} + MS_{B/A} - MS_{S\times B})^2}{df_{SA} + MS_{B/A}/df_{B/A} + MS_{S\times B}/df_{S\times B}} = \frac{(98,662 + 260,647 - 41,107)^2}{12 + 14 / 260,647^2/84} = 15.88$
```

Based on 3 and 16 $df_t$, the $F'$ value of 3.375 is significant at $a = .05$; therefore, we can conclude that the moods displayed in the faces of people affects the time required to categorize the factors.

In other words, the $EMS indicates that the means of the levels of $A$ may differ for four distinct reasons: measurement error $(\sigma^2_{ij})$; the possibility that the effects of $A$ may differ after participants $(beta^2_{SA})$; the possibility that the means across the different levels of $A$ may differ ($nb\theta^2_{\lambda}$); and the possibility that the means across the different levels of $A$ may differ ($nb\theta^2_{\lambda}$); that is, there is an effect of $A$.

The EMS shows that no single source of variance provides an appropriate error term to test $A$. Rather we must form a combination of mean squares to create the error term, then the relevant calculations are presented in Box 15.1, using the MS values from Table 15.3. Researchers often fail to appreciate the importance of computing quasi-F ratios. This approach can be done by averaging over items so that each participant has a single mean score at each treatment level, and then using the means as input to the ANOVA.
