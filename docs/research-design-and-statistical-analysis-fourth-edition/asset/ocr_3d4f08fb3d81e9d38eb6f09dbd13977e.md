The Mann-Whitney U test rests on the assumption that the two treatment distributions have the same shape and the added limitation that confidence intervals are difficult to construct. However, if the assumptions are met and all that is required is a significance test, this will provide a powerful alternative to the t test when data are not distributed and the population distributions are assumed to be the same shape. When the data distribution is symmetric and long-tailed, the trimmed t test will often be most powerful and has the added advantage that confidence intervals can easily constructed.

# 6.6.2 The Assumption of Homogeneity of Variance

It is common to encounter violations of the assumption that variances are the same in both experimental conditions. The consequences of such violations depend on a couple of considerations.

# Consequences of Violating Assumption of Homogeneity of Variance

The denominator of the equation for the two-sample Student’s test is based on the pool of two variance estimates (see Equation 6.4); the underlying assumption is that the two group variances estimate the population variance. If this is not true – if the population variances are heterogeneous – then the sampling distribution of the t statistic may not have a t distribution.
