# 9.6.2 Omega-Squared for the Multi-Factor-Between-Subjects Design

Although $\omega^2$ is easily calculated and interpreted, it is an overestimation of the population variance attributable to a factor. As we discussed in Chapter 8, $\omega^2$ is more satisfactory in that respect. Most commonly, partial $\omega^2$ has been reported. In a multi-factor design in which we wish to measure the effect of factor A, this parameter would be defined as

```
$$\omega_P^2(A) = \sigma_A^2/(\sigma_A^2 + \sigma_e^2)$
```

However, we proceed to again follow the approach suggested by Olejnik and Algina (2003). Therefore we will focus our discussion on general $\omega^2$. Because our estimate of $\omega_P^2$ requires estimates of any main or interaction effects, assuming that all levels of all variables have been arbitrarily selected; that these are fixed-effect variables, regardless of whether they are extrinsic or intrinsic. For such between-participants designs, the general formula is

```
$$\delta_{Effect}^2 = df_{Effect} \times (MS_{Effect} - MS_{cells}) / N$$
```

where N is the total number of scores. For example, for a three-factor-between-participants design, the estimate of the variance of the AB interaction effects is

```
$$\delta_{AB}^2 = (a-1)(b-1)(MS_{AB} - MS_{ABC})/abc$
```
