Method 3: Main effects not adjusted

| SV | df | SS |
|---|---|---|
| A | a - 1 | $SS_A = R_A^2 SS_S$ |
| C | c - 1 | $SS_C = R_C^2 SS_S$ |
| AC | $(a-1)(c-1)$ | $SS_{AC} = (R_{A,AC}^2 - R_{A,AC}^2) SS_S$ |

| Residual | N - ac | $SS_{residual} = (1 - R_{A,AC}^2) SS_S$ |

Hypotheses tested:

+   A: $\mu_1 = \mu_2 = ... = \mu_a$ where $\mu_{j.} = \frac{1}{n_j} \sum y_{ij}$ is the weighted mean of the means for the c cells in the jth row of A
C: $\mu_{*1} = \mu_{*2} = ... = \mu_{*c}$ where $\mu_{*k} = \frac{1}{n_k} \sum y_{ik}$ is the weighted mean of the means for the a cells in the kth row of C
AC: $\mu_{jk} - \mu_{j.} - \mu_{*k} + \mu_{**} = 0$ for all $j, k$

Usage: This method tests main effects against the weighted row and column means. These tests may be desirable if the cell frequencies are proportional to the corresponding population sizes. This method is also known as Yates's method for proportional cell sizes.

Finally, under what conditions Method 2 be used? The Method 2 approach that has been favored by some statisticians (e.g., Cramer & Appelbaum, 1980) corresponds to a hierarchical series of tests that starts with higher-order effects. The rationale that main effects are not very meaningful in the presence of an interaction, this approach first tests the interaction by the model
