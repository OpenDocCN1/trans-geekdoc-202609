# 11.4.3 A Planned Contrast

The experimenter predicted that delay would have a different effect for the Attribute condition compared to the other conditions, although the direction of the effect was not clearly specified. A planned contrast to test the prediction is

```
$$\hat{\psi} = (day3 - day1)_{Attribute} - \frac{(day3 - day1)_{Name}}{2} + (day3 - day1)_{Random}$
```

Substituting the means from Table 11.1, we find $\hat{\psi} = 4.2$, and applying Equation 10.5, $t(189) = 1.79$. The p-value is found using a distributional calculator. Because the test is two-tailed, we calculated the probability of a t at least large as 1.79; in R, the command `2*(1-pt(1.79, 189))` reveals $p = .074$. We conclude that the experiment provides no clear evidence in favor of the hypothesis that effect of test delay is different for the Attribute organization than the average of the Name and Random organizations.

# 11.4.4 Post Hoc Tests and Confidence Intervals

Turning to the effect of organizations, the results of the Tukey HSD test in Table 11.3 reveal that the significant variance among the Three organization is largely due to the advantage of the Attribute condition over the Mean and Random conditions; the difference between the latter two means is not significant. The confidence intervals can be obtained stated earlier in Box 10.3. The formula for the bounds and its application to the Attribute-Name contrast is

```
$CI = \hat{\psi} \pm (q_{0,a,b,n-1} / \sqrt{2}) (MS_{S/AB} \sqrt{2 / ab})\n=5.39 \pm (3.35 / \sqrt{2})(1.103)$
```

Completing the calculation, the lower bound of the confidence interval is 2.79 and the upper bound is 8.00.

Because statistical significance does not always mean that an effect is of practical or theoretical importance (particularly when the large number of error degrees of freedom provide power to detect even very small–perhaps uninteresting–effects), we can compute estimates of Cohen’s d from the output in Table 11.3. Each value is the mean divided by the square root of 40.16, the mean square. For the three contrasts in Table 11.3, they are 0.85, 1.16, and 0.31. The estimates of d are quite large for comparisons of the Attribute mean with the other two organizational means, and relatively small for the comparison of the Name and Random means, providing further confirmation that the significant variance of the organization is largely due to the differences between the Attribute and the other two means.

Other contrasts are possible. Noting that the Name and Random organizations are similar to each other but markedly lower than the Attribute mean, we might wish to conduct a post hoc test of the difference between their average and that for the Attribute participants.
