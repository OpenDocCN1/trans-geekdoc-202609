We should emphasize that when we test the interaction between two predictors, we must include both predictors in the regression equation with the product of the two predictors, as we did in Equation 22.10. That is, to test the interaction, we evaluate the effect of the product term after the effects of the predictors that are the constituents of the product term have been partial out by including them in the equation (see, for example, Cohen, 1978). It is not appropriate to test the interaction by including only the product term in the regression equation—although this is a common mistake. # 22.8.3 Testing the Interaction Between Two Predictors

In Chapter 20 we regress TC on age and BMI for women aged 20–65 and obtained the equation

```
$$\widehat{TC} = 92.239 + 1.737 \text{ age} + 1.474 \text{ BMI}$$
```

This is the equation of a two-dimensional plane in the three-dimensional space that has its axes predicted TC, age, and BMI (see Figure 20.8). Because this equation is additive, if BMI is held constant, a 1-year increase in age corresponds to an increase of 1.737 units in predicted TC, TC when BMI is held constant, also, no matter what value BMI is held constant. Also, if age is held constant at any value, a one-unit increase in BMI corresponds to a 1.474-unit increase in predicted TC. However, it is possible that Equation 22.13 is unrealistic: the rate of change of predicted TC with age may differ for different values of BMI, or equivalently, the rate of change of predicted TC with BMI may differ for different values of age. We can investigate the possibility that age and BMI interact by creating a new variable age × BMI that is the product of age and BMI, and regressing TC on age, BMI, and age × BMI. As can be seen in the R output of Figure 22.8, the resulting equation is

```
$$\widehat{TC} = -70.241 + 5.209 \text{ age} + 7.844 \text{ BMI} - 0.135 \text{ age} \times \text{BMI}$$
```

The coefficient of the product term is significant; $t(186) = -2.546, p = .012$. We can understand the interpretation of this coefficient, -0.135, by grouping terms and rewriting Equation 22.14

```
$$\widehat{TC} = -70.241 - 94.612 \text{ sex} + (5.209 - 0.135 \text{ BMI}) \text{ age}$$
```

and as

```
$$\widehat{TC} = -70.241 + 5.209 \text{ age} + (7.844 - 0.135 \text{ age}) \text{ BMI}$$
```

Now we see that, according to Equations 22.15 and 22.16, the rate of change of predicted TC with BMI is a linear function of age, $7.844 - 0.135 \text{ age}$, and the rate of change of predicted TC with age is a linear function of BMI, $5.209 - 0.135 \text{ BMI}$. When we plot Equation 22.14 (see Figure 22.9), we see that because we included the product term, we no longer have a plane, but rather curved surface on which the slope for one predictor decreases as the other predictor increases. The slope of predicted TC with age decreases by 0.135 for each one-unit increase in BMI, and the slope of TC with BMI decreases by 0.135 for each one-unit increase in age.
