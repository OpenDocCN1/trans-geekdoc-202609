If the Xs are random variables, we assume 2 and 3, and further assume that the distributions of the predictor variables are independent of $\epsilon$.

# 21.3 Testing Assumptions and Checking for Outliers and Influential Data Points

In Chapter 19 we considered how to check assumptions and detect outliers and influential data points in bivariate regression. We extend the discussion to multiple regression. Whether X is fixed or random, we assume that the following population model is

$Y = eta_0 + eta_1X_1 + eta_2X_2 + ... + eta_pX_p + \epsilon$$

and that the error components associated with each of the Ys are normally and independently distributed with mean 0 and variance $\sigma_e^2$. As we discussed in Chapter 19, we would like to check for the assumptions of linearity, homoscedasticity, normality, and independence.

# 21.3.1 Checking Assumptions by Using Residuals

We can visually check several assumptions by the residuals, $e$ - the closest we come to observing the error components, $\epsilon$. We can see whether the distribution of residuals is approximately normal, and we can generate scatterplots of $e$ against $Y$ to determine whether there are systematic tendencies to violate assumptions (as in Figure 19.5). Under our usual assumptions, the residuals should act like normally distributed error. If there seems to be systematic variability in the residuals, then perhaps the model has not been properly specified – perhaps additional variables should be added to the equation.

The histogram and scatterplot for the regression of $TC$ on $age$ and $BMI$ are presented in Figure 21.1 and Figure 21.2. The standardized residuals have an approximately normal distribution. Also, although the scatterplot of $e$ vs Yhat indicates that there are outliers that deserve to be looked at more closely, there are no strong suggestions of curvilinearity or heteroscedasticity.
