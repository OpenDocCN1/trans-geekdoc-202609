# 9.8.2 Two Types of Sums of Squares

There are three approaches available in most software packages for analyzing data when effects vary because cell frequencies are unequal. We will briefly describe each.

*Type III sums of squares* involve adjusting each mean and interaction effect for the others of the others. For example, the adjusted $SS_A$ would consist only of the area labeled $t$ in Figure 9.6b. The Type III analysis is the default in SPSS. It waits all cell means equally and therefore is appropriate if it is assumed that the sampled populations are of equal size and the unequal cell frequencies reflect change variance. Type III sums of squares should be calculated when the data comes from true experiments in the independent variables have been manipulated, and the loss of data is due to factors such as the random failure of participants in various conditions to appear for the experiment.

*Type II sums of squares* involve adjusting each effect of interest at the same or lower order, and for higher-order effects that do not include the effect of interest. In the two-factor design, this requires adjusting the sum of squares for each main effect for ability due to the other main effect, but not for the interaction. In Figure 9.6b, the Type II $SS_A$ would be represented by the areas labeled $t$ and $u$. This analysis implies that there are no interaction effects. Therefore, the interaction mean square can be averaged ("pooled"; see the next section) with the within-cell error term, yielding more degrees of freedom and potentially more power.
