# 19.4 Regression Analysis in Nonexperimental Research

In the regression model introduced in Section 19.2.1, X is assumed to be measured
without error. In other words, the values of X scores are known. This condition
gill generally be fully satisfied when X is an independent variable that is manipulated
in an experiment. We can consider X to be a fixed-effect variable in the search experiment
example because the researcher selects the array sizes to use. When a statistical inference
is made, conclusions are drawn about the populations of Y scores at these selected levels of
X. However, regression is commonly used with data collected in nonexperimental research
where both X and Y take on values that can vary from sample to sample and are therefore
both random variables. For example, in the Seasons study that we have referred to through-
out the book, a sample consisting of individuals between the ages of 20 and 70 was selected
from the membership of health maintenance organization and many variables were
measured. In the previous section we considered the regression of cholesterol level (Y) on age
(X) from the Seasons data set. What should we do if both X and Y are random variables?

The usual strategy is to continue treat X as though it was fixed; is to make our
inferences on the values of X that were observed in the sample. In this case, we
can use the samples for hypothesis tests and confidence intervals as the fixed-X
case we discussed earlier. However, if we do so, we must remember that our inferences
end only to those that have the same distribution of X scores as the current sample.
Treating age as a fixed-effect variable in the Seasons data set when interpreting the results
t the test of equality of regression slopes for men and women in the previous section does
not represent a great limitation on the inference. The age distributions for men and women
are very similar and are fairly flat between the ages of 30 and 70. Moreover, age is one vari-
able that we can usually expect to obtain without (much) error.
