- Statistical inference for multiple regression.
- Prediction in regression.
- Power in regression.

## 21.2 Inference and Assumptions

As was the case for bivariate regression, the validity of our inferences rests upon the following:

1. The errors are normally distributed.
2. The errors are independent.
3. The variance of the errors is constant (homoscedasticity).
4. No predictor variable is completely redundant with others.

In multiple regression, we assume the model is

$$Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_1 + \beta_2X_2 + \dots + \beta_pX_p + \epsilon$$

where $\epsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$.

### 21.3.1 Checking Assumptions by Using Residuals

We can visually check several assumptions by the residuals, $e$ - the closest we come to observing the error components, $\epsilon$. We can see whether the distribution of residuals is approximately normal, and we can generate scatterplots of $e$ against $Y$ to determine whether there are systematic tendencies to violate assumptions (as in Figure 19.5). Under our usual assumptions, the residuals should act like normally distributed error. If there seems to be systematic variability in the residuals, then perhaps the model has not been properly specified – perhaps additional variables should be added to the equation.
