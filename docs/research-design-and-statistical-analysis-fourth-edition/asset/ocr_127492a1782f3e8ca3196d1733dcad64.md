Although we used a small square for our example, generally it is wise to use squares with at least five levels of the Latin factors.

If the Latin square is the design of choice, the data should be properly analyzed. We have found that in many, perhaps most, instances counteract the assignment of factor levels and then analyzed the data as if the design were a standard repeated-measures design, or a mixed design if there was a between-participants factor.
