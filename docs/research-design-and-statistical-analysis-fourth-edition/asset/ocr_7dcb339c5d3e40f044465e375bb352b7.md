# 13.10 The Problem of Missing Data in Repeated-Measures Designs

Missing data can be a problem in any kind of research. They are particularly troublesome in repeated-measures designs when the general linear model (GLM) modules of the statistical packages are used to conduct ANOVAs, as cases with missing scores in any condition are completely dropped from the analysis.

There are many possible causes of missing data. There may be some equipment failure, or a participant may not show up for a session or may fail to respond to a questionnaire or stimulus presentation. What are to do? If we drop participants who have scores in most but not all conditions, or if a condition contains too many missing observations, a good deal of effort and a lot of information may be lost. More importantly, cases with complete data may differ systematically with missing data. If missing data occurs nonrandomly, conducting analyses with only complete cases will provide biased estimates of population parameters and standard errors thereby invalidate our inferences.

We can distinguish among three types of missing data: missing not at random (MNAR, or nonignorable missing data), data missing at random (MAR), and data missing completely at random (MCAR).
