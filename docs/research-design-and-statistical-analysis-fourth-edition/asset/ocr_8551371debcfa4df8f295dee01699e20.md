Let's consider each expected mean square in turn to understand the information provided by $MS_A$ and $MS_{S/A}$. $E(MS_A)$ states that the between-groups mean square, $MS_A$ estimates error variance, $\sigma^2_\epsilon$, plus $n$ times the treatment population means, $	heta^2_A$ (if there is any effect of the treatment). This should make intuitive sense when you examine the formula for $MS_{S/A}$:

```
$MS_{S/A} = \frac{n\sum_j(\bar{Y}_j - \bar{Y}_{.})^2}{a-1}$
```

Equation 8.7 states that $MS_A$ is the variance of the condition means times the sample size, $n$. Even if there are no differences between the treatment population means, the mean would differ just by chance because there are individuals with different characteristics in each group. The error variance, $\sigma^2_\epsilon$, reflects this. If there is any effect of the treatment, the $\mu_j$ will differ and their variability will also be reflected in the value of $MS_{S/A}$. $E(MS_{S/A})$ states that the within-groups mean square, $MS_{S/A}$, is an estimate of error variance. Again, this result may be understood intuitively by examining how $MS_{S/A}$ is calculated:

```
$MS_{S/A} = \frac{SS_{S/A}}{a(n-1)} = \frac{\sum_j\sum_i(Y_{ij} - \bar{Y}_j)^2}{a(n-1)}$
```

Equation 8.8 can be rewritten as

```
$MS_{S/A} = \frac{1}{a} \left[ \frac{\sum_i(Y_{ij} - \bar{Y}_j)^2}{n-1} \right]$
```
