Assumptions about the distribution of the terms that were in Equation 13.2 are unchanged. The interaction effect associated with the $ith$ cell is defined as

```
$(\eta\alpha)_{ij} = (\mu_{ij} - \mu_j) - \eta_i - \alpha_j$$
$$= (\mu_{ij} - \mu) - (\mu_{i, \cdot} - \mu)$$
$$= \mu_{ij} - \mu_i, \mu_j + \mu$$ (13.5)
```

Because the independent variable, A, is a fixed-effect variable, the sum of the interaction effects for any participant in an experiment equals 0; i.e., $\sum_j(\eta\alpha)_{ij}=0$. However, because participants are random-effects variable, and therefore only $n$ of the infinitely possible population effects at any level of A are sampled $(\eta\alpha)_{ij}$ rarely equal zero, although the expectation of all population values is zero; i.e., $E(\eta\alpha)_{ij}=0$. These properties of the $S \times A$ interaction effects have implications for the EMS, and therefore for the $F$ tests. We consider this matter.
