# 21.8 Automated Procedures for Developing Prediction Equations

Sometimes we want to predict some criterion of interest by developing a regression equation that contains a subset of the potentially useful predictor variables that are available. In predicting, we are normally concerned with the accuracy of the predictions and with the costs involved in making them. If our only concern was accuracy, then for large samples we would be inclined to include as many valid predictors as possible in the regression equation; on the other hand, concerns about simplicity motivate us to use fewer predictors. Because in many types most of the predictor variables are correlated with one another, including all of them in a regression would not be cumbersome but would also introduce a good deal of redundancy. Several automated procedures have been developed to allow a compromise between these procedures. These procedures forward selection, backward elimination, and stepwise regression; all are commonly available in statistical software. For example, in SPSS, the “Method” defaults to “Enter,” which includes all variables, but can be changed to “Forward,” “Backward,” or “Stepwise” from the main linear regression window.

Using these automated procedures, it is often possible to select a subset of the predictors that account for nearly as large a proportion of the variance in Y as the pool of predictors. Before deciding the methods, we should emphasize that these automated procedures have been developed solely to produce the best prediction equations according to certain criteria. These equations need not be best – nor good – in any explanatory or theoretical sense. Running an automated regression routine may be useful for predicting outcomes within a sample but is a poor way to develop theory. Recall our discussion of cross-validation techniques and why they are so important: The best-fitting regression equations are sample specific and may have little ability to predict outcomes accurately in any sample or in the population (see Section 20.4.4). Thus, we recommend that cross-validation be used to validate a predictive model that is generated by any of the automated procedures discussed next.

# 21.8.1 Forward Selection

In the forward selection, the regression equation is built up one predictor variable at a time. On the first step, the predictor that has the highest correlation (positive or negative) with the criterion variable selected. If it fails to meet the criterion for inclusion, the procedure ends with no predictors in the equation, and the final equation is $Y = Y$. If the first predictor meets the criterion and is added to the equation, a second predictor is selected and tested to determine whether it should be entered into the equation. The second predictor considered is the one that would result in the greatest increment in $R^2$ if added to the equation.
