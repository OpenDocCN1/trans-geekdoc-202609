+   2. A third type of error rate, the *false discovery rate (FDR)*, is the proportion of null hypothesis rejection decisions that are Type 1 errors (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995). Controlling the *FDR* at, say, .05, requires a compromise between limiting the *EC* to .05 and limiting the *FWE* to .05. Unless all null hypotheses are true, controlling *FDR* entails a *FWE* that exceeds .05.

+   3. Šidak (1967) proposed that $H_i: \psi_i = 0$ be rejected if $p \leq 1 - (1 - FWE)^K$. Because $1 - (1 - FWE)^K > FWE / K$, this method has more power and a narrower confidence interval than the original Dunn-Bonferroni procedure. However, the difference is very small.
