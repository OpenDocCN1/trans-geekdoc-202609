18.8 [Correlations with sums of variables] Sometimes a correlation has been computed between some variable X and another variable T which is the sum of a number of variables including X (e.g., T = X + Y). Under these circumstances, we can expect a positive correlation between X and T even if X is not related to Y because X is part of T. Show that in general

```
$r_{XT,T-X} = \frac{r_{XT}-r_{X}^2}{\sqrt{(11-r_{X}^2)(1-r_{T}^2)-r_{XT}^2}}$
```

where $r_{X,T-X}$ is the correlation between X and the part of T not containing X.

18.9 [Correlations with difference scores] Note that the previous question has implications for the interpretation of correlations involving change or difference scores. Suppose that X refers to preset and posttest scores. Therefore, $T - X$ refer to change scores. If we assume that $s_{pre} = s_{post}$ in the exercise 18.8 reduces to

```
$r_{X,T-X} = r_{pre,change} = \frac{r_{pre,post}-1}{2(1-r_{pre,post})}$
```

We would not expect a perfect correlation for a lot of reasons, including random error. Suppose $r_{pre,post} = .70$. What do we expect for $r_{pre,change}$? Remember, by regression toward the mean, would we expect the correlation between preset scores and change scores to be negative.

18.10 [Correlations with sums of variables] A researcher tries to develop a questionnaire measure some personality trait. The instrument is made up of many items, each of which is scored numerically. The total score, $T$, is supposed to represent the degree to which a person has the trait. The researcher likes the instrument but it will be time-consuming to administer all of it, so she arbitrarily divides the instrument into two parts, each containing half the items. Let's refer to the score on one of the halves as $X$ and the score on the other half as $Y$ (so $T = X + Y$). She finds that the correlation between X and $T$ is high ($r_{XY} = .7$) and concludes that the correlation between the scores on the two halves of the instrument ($r_{XY}$) must be pretty high, so that she can get by using only one of the halves to measure the personality trait. If we assume the correlation of X and Y are equal, what is $r_{XY}$?
