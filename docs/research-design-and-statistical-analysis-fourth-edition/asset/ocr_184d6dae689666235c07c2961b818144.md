# 9.3.4 Mean Squares (MS), Expected Mean Squares (EMS), and F Ratios

As the in-one factor, the MS of Table 9.5 are ratios of SS to df. Conceptually, however, the means for simple effects are functions of variances. For example, $MS_A$ is the variance of the $a$ marginal means in the $A$ conditions, multiplied by $nb$, the number of scores upon which each is based. $MS_B$ is the variance of the $b$ marginal means in the $B$ conditions, multiplied by $na$, the number of scores upon which each is based. The error mean square, $MS_{SI/AB}$ is an average of the within-cell variances; it can be calculated as as

```
$MS_{SI/AB} = (1 / ab) \sum_{k=1}^{ab} s_{ik}^2$
```

where $s_{ik}^2$ is the variance of the $n$ scores in the cell defined by $A_i$ and $B_k$. All three ratios are justified by the ratio of mean squares (EMS; see Panel $b$ of Table 9.5). As we stated in Chapter 8, the ratio of two means follows that the numerator and denominator MS must have the same expectation when the null hypothesis corresponding to the numerator is true.

The EMS are derived by assuming the structural model of Equation 9.6, independence of the scores, and homogeneity of the population variances. In addition, if the populations are normally distributed and the null hypothesis is true, the ratio of mean squares is distributed as $F^4$.

We now have both a conceptual framework and formulas on which to base tests of hypotheses about main and interaction effects. We apply this framework to the analysis of the verification test (IVT) data in Table 9.6.
