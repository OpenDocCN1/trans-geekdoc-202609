# Multi-Factor Repeated-Measures and Mixed Designs 371

As an example of a mixed design, Table 14.5 (the data are also in the table 14\_5 Mixed Data (on the website)) presents a data set for a hypothetical experiment in which one group of students was taught algebra by a standard instructional method ($A_1$), a second group received additional problems from a computer that provided immediate feedback ($A_2$). All three groups were tested at the end of the instruction, and then once every two weeks until different tests had been given. Assuming that the tests were easy enough so that any differences could be attributed to the passage of time. This design permits us to compare the instructional methods ($A$) and the test course ($B$) of performance the end of instruction for each method. Notice that the design is a mixture of the between-participants design of Chapter 8 and if we average the four test scores for each participant, we have a between-participants design and can conduct the ANOVA exactly as in Chapter 8. If on the other hand, we retain the four test scores but ignore the instructional method, we have an $S 	imes B$ design in which 18 participants have scores at the four levels of B.
