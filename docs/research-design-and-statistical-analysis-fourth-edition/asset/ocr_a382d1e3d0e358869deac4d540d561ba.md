# 14.5.1 S × A × B Design With Two Random-Effects Factors

In Section 14.4, we described a humans experiment in which both participants and words (stimuli) would be considered random-effects factors. Imagine that we conducted such an experiment involving n participants, b words, and a fonts. A summary of the appropriate approach of this design is presented in Table 14.10. The sources of variance and their de are what you would expected, the expected means for S, A, and SA are unexpected from the designs considered to this point. We will work through the unexpected variance mean square for A to illustrate the application of the rules for this term.

There are eight variance components in this design: measurement error, σ², variability to participants, σ² variability by words, σ² variability by B, and with AB, variability by the interaction of words with A, σ², and variability associated with the fixed-effects factor A, θ². The coefficients associated with each of these variance components is, as always, the product of the number of levels of all the factors that are not represented in the sample on variance term (e.g., for SB). Consider how the rules apply to the generation of the expected mean square for the effect of interest, A.
