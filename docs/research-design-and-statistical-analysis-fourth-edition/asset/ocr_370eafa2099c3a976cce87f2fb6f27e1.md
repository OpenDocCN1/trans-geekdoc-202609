Note that the AB interaction contributes to A source of variance *only when B has random effects*, the rules of thumb dictate. This is the reason for the difference between the two panels in Table 15.8.

In the detection experiment in which the number of distract elements was varied and counterbalanced over blocks of trials, it is reasonable to assume that trials have fixed effects. In case, the expected mean squares are in the left panel of Table 15.8 and the error term for testing A (number of elements) and B (trial blocks) are the same error term in the mixed designs of Chapter 14 (remember that $SS_{WSR} = SS_{S/G} = SS_{B/G}$). This would be the case for any design in which the blocking is assumed to have fixed effects.

In other experiments, recall scores might be obtained for lists that have been studied for various amounts of time, or comprehensibility ratings might be recorded for sets of pictures varying in syntax, or brain activity might be recorded for emotional content. In these examples, the blocks in Table 15.6 represent sets of stimuli. It is reasonable to assume the stimuli were randomly sampled from some large population and randomly divided into several sets. Suppose each group has a different pairing of set with study time or emotional content, with the pairings arranged so that the design forms a Latin square, as in Table 15.6. The appropriate error term for testing A (e.g., study time or emotional content) is the BCR source.

Table 15.9 presents two F and p-values based on the design of Table 15.6. The tests consider whether B has fixed effects or that B has random effects. As the EMS of Table 15.8, shown, when the effects of B are assumed to be fixed the test of the test variable, A, is the same as in the mixed design of Chapter 14. The within-participants error term is appropriate for testing both A and B main effects, as well as providing a test of the interaction (AB)' of A and B.

When B is assumed to have random effects, following the rules set in Chapter 14, $\sigma_{AB}^2$ contributes to E($MS_A$).
