Similarly, $SS_c$ is a times the sum of the squared deviations of the trial (C) around the grand mean, so we can write the degrees of freedom as $(a - 1)$ for each of the independent variables. The *residual* sum of squares is obtained by subtraction of the three sums of squares for treatments, rows, and columns from the total sum of squares. The *residual df* effects are also calculated as a difference: $df_a^2 - 1$, minus the sum of the $df$ for the main effects, $3(a-1)$, $(a-1)^2$. Note that we have not mentioned the calculation of SS terms for any interaction, because the analysis assumes there are no interactions between the variables. If that assumption is wrong, any interaction variability will contribute to the residual variance with the consequence that the three main effects will be biased. We will have to say more about calculations in an extended discussion of this design and the related analysis in Chapter 15.
