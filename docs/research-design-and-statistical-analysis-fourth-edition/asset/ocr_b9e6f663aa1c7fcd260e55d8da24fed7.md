Next, according to both approaches, we must decide on the criterion for how unlikely a result must be before we are willing to reject the null hypothesis; the criterion is called the "significance level" of the test. In theory, the significance level, $\alpha$, may be set at different values, and the value chosen for a particular situation may depend on a variety of considerations. In a pilot experiment, a researcher may select a relatively lenient criterion, perhaps $\alpha = .10$. In contrast, if the efficacy of some new drug treatment is being tested against an established drug that has already proven effective, a researcher might decide on a stricter criterion for rejecting the old treatment in favor of the new, perhaps $\alpha = .001$.
