The variances of the posttest scores for low, medium, and high ability groups are 8.67, 6.92, and 9.67, respectively; the within-group variance is 8.42 (i.e., $MS_{S(AB)}$). However, if the 12 scores were not grouped by pretext level, their variance would be 12.63 (i.e., $MS_{S(A)}$). Thus, the error variance will be smaller in a treatments $	imes$ blocks design than in a completely randomized design, provided that the pretest scores are correlated with the posttest scores.

To further understand the effect of blocking, we consider the average result of many independent replications of two experiments differing in design. Table 12.1 presents the average sums of squares and mean squares for 5,000 independent replications of the experimental designs under consideration. The results in the table were obtained by using a simulation to draw 5,000 samples of 36 pretext and posttest scores in a focus with which the correlation between the two measures was 0.4. In the CR design, the 36 posttest scores were then divided randomly among the three treatment levels; in the blocking design, the posttest scores were divided among four blocks in order of their pretext scores, and then randomly among the treatments within each block. To simulate treatment effects, a constant was added to all posttest scores at a given treatment with a different constant for each level. An ANOVA was then performed for the data in each design and the proportion of the 5,000 samples that resulted in rejection at the .05 level was considered. The results presented in Table 12.1 are averages over the 5,000 replications of the simulated experiment. There are several points to note about the results:

+   1. The average $SS_{total}$ is virtually identical for the two designs, the very small difference due to chance variability involving a finite number of samples.

+   2. The treatment variance, $SS_A$, is smaller in the blocking design than in the CR design. To see why, consider the expected mean square for treatments, we saw in Chapter 8, with 12 scores at each level $E(MS_A) = \sigma^2 + 12\sigma_A^2$; as the preceding numerical example demonstrated, we expected the error variance, $\sigma^2$, to be smaller in the T $	imes$ B design than in the completely randomized design. Therefore, we expect $MS_A$ to be smaller in the blocking design.

+   3. The $T 	imes B$ design partitions both the $df$ and $SS$ for error in the CR design ($df_{S/A}$, $SS_{S/A}$) into variance portions for the $B$, $AB$, and $S/AB$ terms. The reduction is proportionally larger than the reduction in $df$, resulting in a smaller MS for error in the $T 	imes B$ design ($MS_{S/AB} < MS_{S/A}$).

+   4. Power of significant results is greater in the $T 	imes B$ ANOVA, despite the fact that the $MS_A$ and the error $df$ – affect that power – are smaller in that design.
