However, we may wish to ask whether there would still be a correlation even if the effects of age could be controlled or partialed out of both variables. How can we measure the relationship between size and verbal ability that is not contaminated by the effects of chronological age? By the best way is to collect data from samples of children who are all the same age. However, we can also attempt to control statistically for the effects of age in a child of varying sizes by partialing correlation.

If we use notation $r_{XY} = Corr(X, Y)$ to stand for the correlation between X and Y, then $r_{XY|W}$, the partial correlation between X and Y with the effects of W partialed out, is given by

```
$r_{XY|W} = Corr(X|W, Y|W)$
```

In this expression, $X|W = X - \hat{X}$, where $\hat{X}$ is the value of X predicted from the regression on W; therefore $\hat{X}$ is the part of X that is not predictable from W with linear regression. Similarly, $Y|W = Y - \hat{Y}$ where $\hat{Y}$ is the residual when Y is regressed on W. It is possible to express $r_{XY|W}$ in terms of the simple correlations between X, Y, and W as follows:

```
$r_{XY|W} = \frac{r_{XY} - r_{X|W}r_{Y|W}}{\sqrt{(1-r_{X|W}^2)(1-r_{Y|W}^2)}}$ (18.9)
```

Suppose X represents shoe size, Y represents verbal ability, and W represents age. If the correlations of size and verbal ability with age .7 ($r_{XW} = r_{YW} = .7$), $r_{XY|W}$ would have a value of $(.5 - .49)/(1 - .49) = .02$. In other words, if we take into account the relationship between shoe size and age, and verbal skills and age, the correlation between shoe size and verbal ability virtually disappears.

More one variable may be partialed out of a correlation. Suppose that we wish to partial out the effects of variables W and Q from the correlation between X and Y. The partial correlation $r_{XY|WQ}$ is given by $Corr(X|WQ, Y|WQ) = Corr(X-\hat{X}, Y-\hat{Y})$ where X is the prediction of X based on a linear regression equation that contains both W and Q (we will cover this type of regression in Chapter 20), and $\hat{Y}$ is the corresponding prediction of Y. The same logic holds no matter how many variables there are to be partialed out; the partial correlation can always be obtained by correlating the two sets of residuals that result when X and Y are regressed on using statistical packages that either produce the partial correlations directly or provide the appropriate residuals that then be corrected. If the raw data are not available but the first-order correlations are, Equation 18.10 indicates how to find the partial correlation between X and Y, partialing out W and Q. In effect, Equation 18.9 is used to remove the effects of $Q$ from $r_{XY}$, $r_{X|W}$ and $r_{Y|W}$, and is then used to remove the effects of W.

```
$r_{XY|WQ} = \frac{r_{XY} - r_{X|WQ}r_{Y|WQ}}{\sqrt{(1-r_{X|WQ}^2)(1-r_{Y|WQ}^2)}}$ (18.10)
```
