Finally, a common situation in which a researcher wants to assess the stability of a correlation in a group of individuals over time. These correlations are dependent because they are measured on the same individuals, and they include no overlapping data (a variable measured at Time 1 will not be identical to that variable measured at Time 2). The function cocor.dep.groups.nonoverlap in the [cocor] package performs the calculations requested by Steiger (1980). This function takes a series of observed correlations in a particular order as input, followed by the sample size and any desired options (e.g., conf). More, suppose that we are comparing the correlation of variables A and B N times at 1 and 2. The command cocor.dep.groups.nonoverlap(rA1B1,rA2B2,rA1A2,r2B1,r1B2) in the [cocor] package computes several related z tests and Zou’s (2007) confidence interval.

# 18.2.9 Some About Tests That Compare Correlations

In Sections 18.2.6–18.2.8, we discussed how to test whether two correlations differ in size. However, given that we have previously emphasized that correlations are dependent on sample characteristics such as the variance of X and Y, we should give serious thought to exactly what we are testing when we compare two correlations. Suppose, for example, we find that the correlations between income and years of education are significantly different for people living in two different states. This means there is a stronger relationship in one group than the other. However, the correlations may differ between the two groups, rather than because the rate of change of income with years of education is different. Also, a researcher who wishes to compare correlations should carefully evaluate whether constraints on the sampling of observations might have resulted in a biased estimate of rho for either or both coefficients (see Section 17.7).

Our second point is that we are often more interested in the slope of the regression line than the strength of the linear relationship. If our concern about how much income changes with additional education and which this differs across groups, we should compare regression slopes, not correlation coefficients. Just because the correlations are significantly different for the two groups does not necessarily mean that the slopes will be. Conversely, finding that the slopes are significantly different does not necessarily mean that the correlations are significantly different.
