The difference between the ns needed to achieve power in the between- and within-participants factors reflects the fact that variability greater between participants than within participants. Furthermore, this depends on the size of the correlation of scores across repeated-measures. As ρ increases, the n needed to achieve a particular level of power to test B decreases. Contrast, as ρ increases, the n needed to achieve a particular level of power to test the between-participants factor, A increases. This pattern occurs because each participant's score is the average of b scores, and the variance of the population average is the sum of the variances plus a covariance term (see Appendix 5.1). Both effects can be observed by substituting values of ρ in the G*Power screens for the between and within factors.
