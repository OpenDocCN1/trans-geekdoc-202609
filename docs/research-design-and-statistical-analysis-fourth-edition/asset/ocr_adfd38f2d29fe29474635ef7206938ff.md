Once we conclude that the population variances are not equal, the next question is: What shall we do about it? One response is to seek a transformation of the data that yields homogeneity of variance on the scale rooting the transformation. As discussed in Chapter 6, data transformations can make it difficult to interpret the results; in practice, they are used infrequently. A second possibility is to consider an alternative to the usual F test. We consider this approach.

# Dealing With Heterogeneity of Variance: Welch's F Test

Several alternatives to the standard F test of the equality of a population means have been proposed (Alexander & Gordon, 1994; Brown & Forsythe, 1974b; James, 1951, 1954; Welch, 1951), but no one test is under all conditions. When the data are normally distributed and as equal, most of the procedures are reasonably robust with respect to Type 1 error rate; however, the standard F is slightly more powerful if the population variances are equal. When the variances are not equal, the choice of test depends upon the degree of skew and kurtosis, which others are, the degree of heterogeneity of variance, the relation between group sizes and group variances, and the total N (Clinch & Keselman, 1982; Coombs, Olftman, 1996; Cribbie et al., 2012; Grissom, 2000; Les-Keselman, & Keselman, 1996; Tomarker & Serlin, 1986).

Although there is rarely a clear-cut for any given data set, simulation studies have concluded that the Welch test, F<sub>w</sub> provides a good alternative when both Type 1 error rates and power are considered (Delace, Ley, Mora, & Lakens, 2019; Lik et al., 1996). Fww performs well relative to various competitors when are highly skewed (skew > 2.0) or group sizes are less than 10 (Lik et al., 1996; Tomarker & Serlin, 1986). Furthermore, the test is readily available in statistical packages. For example, in R we use the Welch test in the [oneway] package; in SPSS, simply click the “options” button for “Welch test,” and continue as normal. Alternatively, the equations for Welch’s F test are in Box 8.3.
