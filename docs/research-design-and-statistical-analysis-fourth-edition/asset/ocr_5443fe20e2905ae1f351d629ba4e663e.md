To use effective coding, if one of the factors is continuous (i.e., age), it must be entered as a covariate in the model for the General Linear Model.

In R, dummy coding is the default: the reference cell is set to the first level of each factors (e.g., $A_1C_1$ in Table 23.2) the regression coefficients using dummy coding are shown in Figure 23.4. The mean of $A_1C_1 = 52.0$, the value of the intercept. The other coefficients are added to the intercept other cell means, analogous to Equations 23.1–23.3. For example, the mean of $A_2C_1$ is 64.3, $A_2C_1 = 52.0 + 14.5 - 3.0 + 1.0 = 64.5$.

To use effective coding in R, we must specify the contrasts for both factors in the analysis: lm(data = dat, Y ~ A*C, contrasts = list(A = contr.sum, C = contr.sum)). For the data in Table 23.2, the regression coefficients using effective coding are shown in Figure 23.5. The intercept is the mean of group, 66.667. The effect of being in cell $A_1$ is to shift the grand mean by the sum of the coefficients of $A_1$ and $C_1$, and their interaction, $A_1C_1$, are given by the coefficients: $67.667 - 8.833 + 1.500 = 52.0$. The coefficients of cells having the highest level of each factor (i.e., any cell $A_i$ or $C_j$) are found by recalling that the effect of $A$ and $C$ sum to zero. This mean that the effect in cell $A_iC_j$, for example, is a deviation from the grand mean equal to the effect of $A$ minus the sum of the effects of $C$ and $C_j$, and their interactions with $A_i$: $67.667 - 9.0 - 1.5 - 6.333 + 14.833 = 64.5.
