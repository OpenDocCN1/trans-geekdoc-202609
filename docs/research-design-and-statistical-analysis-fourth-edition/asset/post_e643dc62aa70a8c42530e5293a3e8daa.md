## 9.4.2 Extending the Wiley-Voss Experiment

To illustrate the concepts and analyses, we add a hypothetical third factor to the design of the Wiley-Voss experiment. Assume that there are only two levels of instruction (I), Summary and Argument, and two formats (F), Text and Web. Further assume that the participants are older adults and young adults; call this factor Age (A). Assume that there are 10 scores in each factor; i.e., $n=10$ and $N=80$. For the means of the hypothetical experiment are presented in Table 9.11. The means are presented as having a value of 154, but the relevant variance conditions on which that value is based have been omitted. Note that we are dealing with the simplest case of a three-factor design, one where there are only two levels of each of the three variables. The simplicity of the design enables us to concentrate on basic concepts. The design has the added advantage that it is very common research design.

## Main Effects

For example, the test of format source of variance involves a comparison of the text and marginal means. These means are obtained by averaging over the four combinations of instructions and age. As can be seen in the right-hand column in the bottom panel of Table 9.11, the text and web means are 72.375 and 86.125. ## Multi-Factor Between-Participants Designs

To test this null hypothesis, we use the $F_{statistic}$ by dividing the mean square of the factor by the mean square of the error. In the case of the interaction, we use the $F_{statistic}$ by dividing the mean square of the interaction by the mean square of the error.

The $F$-statistic for the interaction is calculated as:

$F = \frac{MS_{interaction}}{MS_{error}}$

In this case, the $F$-statistic for the interaction is:

$F = \frac{24.55}{14} = 1.75$

This $F$-statistic is not significant at the $\alpha = .05$ level, which means there is no significant interaction between instruction and age.
