For multiple regression, in addition to the plot of residuals against predicted scores, we would like to see how the residuals vary as a function of each predictor, *taking the other predictors in the equation into consideration*. Note that SPSS* will readily provide these partial regression plots for

each predictor in the equation if we check Produce all partial plots in the optional Linear Regression: Plots dialog box; they are also straightforward to generate using R.

Let’s be more concrete in our description of partial regression plots. Suppose we have a criterion variable, Y, and predictor variables, $X_1$, $X_2$, and $X_3$. We can obtain a partial regression plot for each of the predictor against $X_i$. The steps to get the partial regression plot of Y against $X_i$ when $X_j$ and $X_k$ are also in the equation are the following:

- 1. Regress Y on $X_2$ and $X_3$ and obtain the residuals for this regression, $e_{Y,23}$.
- 2. Regress $X_1$ on $X_2$ and $X_3$, and obtain the residuals for this regression, $e_{1,23}$.
- 3. Now generate the scatterplot for $e_{Y,23}$ vs $e_{1,23}$.

These partial regression plots have the following useful properties:

- *If we regress $e_{Y,23}$ on $e_{1,23}$ the slope is equal to $b_{Y,1,23}$, the partial regression coefficient for $X_1$ in the equation is obtained when Y is regressed on $X_1$, $X_2$, and $X_3$. The partial regression of Y on $X_1$ will not generally have the correct partial slope.*
- The simple correlation between $e_{Y,23}$ and $e_{1,23}$ is equal to the partial correlation between Y and X, with the other predictor variables in the equation partially out.
- The residuals from the regression line in this plot are identical to those obtained when Y is regressed on all three predictors.
- *If Y and $X_i$ are not linearly related, the partial regression plot will show both the linear and nonlinear components of the relation, *controlling for the other predictors*. This should help us decide whether to add additional variables or other terms as $X_i^2$, to the regression equation.*
- *We can also use these partial regression plots to determine whether the variability of the residuals varies as a function of $X_i$ in the presence of the other predictors in the equation, violating the assumption of homoscedasticity.*

The partial regression plots for *TC* and *age* and for *TC* and *BMI* are displayed in Figures 21.3 and 21.4. There are some outliers and there also seems to be an increase in the variability of the TC residuals for larger values of the age residuals, suggesting a violation of the homoscedasticity assumption. As we noted in Section 19.9, this violation might be addressed with a weighted-squares regression that is beyond the scope of this book. It is also possible that the relationship between *TC* and *BMI* may differ for younger and older participants, so we may wish to consider whether there is an $age \times BMI$ interaction, this is a topic we will take up in Chapter 22.
