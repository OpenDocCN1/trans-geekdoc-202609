The study is a specific example of a general design in which there are n participants, of whom are nested with b different stimuli at each a levels of A; in the prior example, n = 8, a = 3 (moods), and b = 5 (faces within each mood). B is nested within levels of A (i.e., B/A) and is assumed to have random effects; A is assumed to have fixed effects. The distinction between fixed and random effects in this example stems from the fact that we have purposely chosen the three moods to be included in the experiment but have randomly sampled faces representing each mood. In what follows we develop the analysis of data from this hierarchical design.

# 15.2 Partitioning Sum of Squares and Degrees of Freedom

In the example represented by the data of Table 15.1, there are a x b, or 3 x 5, pictures of faces so there are ab-1, or 14, degrees of freedom for items. The part of variability the scores on the ab items is due to the moods. This accounts for a-1, or 2, of the 14 df for items. The remaining ab-1, or 12, df for the B/A term (within moods). In addition, each of n, or participants is shown all of the pictures representing all three moods, participants cross with A: SA, with (n-1)(a-1) = 14 df. Participants also are nested within levels of A and are written SIB/A. This notation expresses that the combination of participants and items is different for each of the levels of A. The SB interactions each have (n-1)(b-1) = 28 df, and there are three levels of A in which those interactions nest, so the SB/A df = 3(28) or 84. Summing all these degrees of freedom, 2 + 12 + 14 + 84, 7 more for the participants, equals 119, accounting for the total degrees of freedom in the experiment, nab-1.
