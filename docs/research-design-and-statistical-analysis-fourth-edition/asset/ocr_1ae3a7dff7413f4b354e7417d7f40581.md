Table 20.3 ANOVA table for multiple regression

| sv | df | SS | F |
|---|---|---|--|
| Regression | $p$ | $\Sigma(\hat{Y}-ar{Y})^2$ or $R^2_{Y,1,...p}SS_{reg}$ | $R^2_{Y,1,...p}SS_{reg} / MS_{residual}$ |
| Residual | $N-1-p$ | $\Sigma(Y-\hat{Y})^2$ or $(1-R^2_{Y,1,...p})SS_{Y}$ | $(1-R^2_{Y,1,...p})SS_{Y} / (N-1-p)$ |
| Total | $N-1$ | $SS_{Y} = \Sigma(Y-ar{Y})^2$ | |

where $SS_{regression} = R^2SS_{Y}$ and $SS_{residual} = (1-R^2)SS_{Y}$. It is convenient to express the partitioning of variability in an ANOVA table the form of Table 20.3. $SS_{Y}$ is associated with $N-1$ df because one $df$ is used to estimate the population mean. Of $N-1$ df, $p$ are associated with the regression sum of squares because coefficients for each of the $p$ predictors are estimated. The remaining $N-1-p$ $df$ are associated with the residual $SS$. Note that when there is only one predictor, $N-1-p=N-2$, the result is presented for bivariate regression.

Under standard assumptions that the regression coefficients $eta_1, \beta_2, ..., \beta_p$ will be estimated in Chapter 21, if the p population regression coefficients are all 0, the ratio

> $$\frac{MS_{regression}}{MS_{residual}} = \frac{R^2SS_Y/p}{(1-R^2)SS_Y/(N-1-p)} = \frac{R^2/p}{(1-R^2)/(N-1-p)}$ (20.3)

will be distributed as $F$ with $p$ and $N-1-p$ $df$. Therefore, the relation of mean squares tests the null hypothesis that $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = ... = \beta_p=0$. In Section 20, we will regress TC on age and BMI score for the 181 female-identified *Seasons* participants with data on all three measures (where $p=2$ and $N=181$), and we find that $R=.522$. Substituting the square of this value into Equation 20.3, we have

> $F = \frac{.522^2/2}{(1-.522^2)/(181-2)} = 33.33$

# 20.4 Partitioning $SS_{regression}$

In bivariate regression, $SS_{regression}$ corresponds to the variance in Y accounted for by the single predictor, $X$; in multiple regression, $SS_{regression}$ corresponds to the variance in Y accounted for by the entire set of $p$ predictor variables in the regression equation. We are often interested in trying to identify the contributions of the different $X$ variables to the prediction of $Y$. The pattern of correlations among the predictor variables has direct implications for the partitioning of $SS_{regression}$ into the variance components associated with each predictor. General patterns of relationships among the predictors may be distinguished.
