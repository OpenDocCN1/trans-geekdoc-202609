+   1 This assumption means that the selected levels of A are the levels of interest; we cannot generalize our conclusions to other possible levels of A from our statistical analysis. We will consider random effects beginning in Chapter 13.

+   2 The arguments are true from a statistical perspective, and conclusions about the presence or absence of an interaction on the dependent variable (DV) may be made with confidence. However, as well as other disciplines, the dependent measure may use an indirect way to assess the underlying, latent, variable of interest. For example, we use reaction times (RT) as a measure of memory. The function mapping the latent variable to the DV is unknown, and a significant interaction in the DV does not necessarily entail a significant interaction in the latent variable. Caution is needed when drawing conclusions about interaction of the experimental factors; e.g., Loftus (1978) and Waginers, Kryppendorf, Christensen and Jersion (2012) for detailed discussion.

+   3 Note that the mean square for the relation is related to the variance of the cell means, but not simply as is for the main effects.
