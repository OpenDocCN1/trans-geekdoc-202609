> Note: The standard error for these tests = $\sqrt{MS_{error}(2/66)}$ the use of the error mean square from the ANOVA results based on the assumption of homogeneity of variance, and it is the number of scores at each organizational level and the mean of the subsequent levels. Similarly, in R, the command `contrasts(datOrg) <- contr.helmmert(3)` assigns contrasts to the Org factor of the data frame called dat, which has three levels. In this case, the comparisons are between each level of the factor and the mean of the previous levels, thus R's "contr.helmmert" contrasts are sometimes reverse Helmert or difference contrasts. Using named contrasts like this requires careful attention to the levels within the factor of interest. Additionally, the meaning of "helmert" (and other named contrasts) can vary across software, it is wise to confirm that the contrasts being calculated are those you intended.

The results of the Helmert contrasts are summarized in Table 11.4. The significance level reported here is not adjusted for the fact that the test was post hoc. Therefore, to evaluate significance, use the Scheffé criterion as in Box 10:8.

- 1. From Table 11.4, calculate t by dividing the contrast by the standard error (6.37, .955) = 6.67.
- 2. Compare the computed value $t = 6$ with $\sqrt{df_1 \cdot F_{0.5,df_1,df_2}} = \sqrt{2F_{0.5,189}} = 2.47$. The result is significant.
- 3. The confidence interval also fail to take into consideration that the test is post hoc. From Chapter 10, the bounds are:

CI bounds = $\hat{\phi} \pm s_s = 6.37 + (.955)(2.47) = 4.01, 8.73$

Because the contrast was selected after viewing the results, the bounds are based on the family of possible contrasts and are therefore somewhat wider than those reported by software (Table 11.4). We should also note that the weights for the first contrast were 1, -5, and -5. Had we used other weights, the bounds would have required adjustment. If we used 2, -1, and -1, the values of the contrast and its standard error would have been twice in Table 11.4. Accordingly, the bounds would have been divided by two to return to the original data.

There is one more point to notice about these Helmert contrasts: they form an orthogonal set. With three levels of factor, there are two Helmert contrasts, level 1 versus the mean of levels 2 and 3, and level 2 versus level 3; the contrast weights are 1, -5, .5 and 0, 1, -1. Using Equation 10.23, we can confirm their orthogonality: (1)(0) + (-5)(1) + (-5).(-1) = 0. Because they are orthogonal contrasts, the SS neatly partition the SS for the Organization factor in the:

$SS_{AVNR} = \frac{(6.371)^2}{1^2 + (-5)^2 + (-5)^2} = 1785.944$

$SS_{NVR} = \frac{(1.95)^2}{(1)^2 + (-1)^2} = 125.485$

Summing, we find 1785.944 + 125.485 = 1911.429, which is within rounding error of $SS_O = 1912$ shown in Table 11.2.

To sum up, the statistical analyses demonstrate that the type of organization influences recall; recall is better in the Name or Random conditions, which do not differ.
