For procedures like forward selection

Wilkinson (1979) has discussed the case in which a subset of k predictors is chosen, where 1 < k < m, and has provided tables for the 95th and 99th percentile points of the sample R² distribution in forward selection based on simulations (other tables and tables of this problem can be found in Hocking, 1983; Rencher & P, 1980; and Wilkinson & Dallal, 1982). These tables are more conservative than the F tables. With N = 35 and α = .05, if all members of a set of predictors variables are to be included in the regression equation, it is appropriate to use the F test for R² significance. When this is done, it is possible the R² exceeds .26 to reject the hypothesis that the population multiple correlation coefficient is 0. If the four predictors are to be selected from a larger set of 20 predictors by a forward selection procedure, according to Wilkinson's tables, the sample R² exceeds .51 to reject the null hypothesis. Many researchers do not seem to be aware of this problem; for a sample of 66 papers that reported forward selection analyses according to the usual F tests, Wilkinson found that 19 were not significant when his tables were used.

# 21.8.2 Backward Elimination

As you may expect, backward elimination is the opposite of forward selection. Whereas forward selection begins with no predictors in the equation and adds them to the equation by one, backward elimination begins with all the predictors in the equation and removes them one by one until the final equation is obtained. At each step, the predictor in equation causes the smallest change in R² is tested to determine whether it should be removed from the equation. Again, the criterion for removal is generally stated in terms of the significance level of a partial F test. If the selected variable is removed, another predictor is selected and tested on the next step. The procedure terminates when a predictor has been selected for testing it does not meet the criterion for elimination; the final equation includes that predictor and all the other predictors remaining in the equation at that point.
