Two important things about conditional probabilities should be noted. First, people have a tendency to confuse conditional probabilities with joint probabilities. Look carefully at Equations 3.6 and 3.9. The conditional probability p(A | psychology major) is the probability of selecting a slip of paper that is labeled “A” if the selection is made from the 60 slips labeled “psychology.” The joint probability p(A and psychology) is the probability of selecting a slip labeled both “A” and “psychology” if selection is randomly made from all 100 slips of paper. A conditional probability must be at least as as – and generally larger than – the set from which we sample a subset of the entire sample. For example, when we calculate p(A | psychology), we divide by only the number of psychology majors, a number less than the size of the total sample. Bear in mind, although joint and conditional probabilities are not the same, they are related, as can be in Equation 3.9.

If they are mutually exclusive, p(E₁) and p(E₂) = 0, and p(E₁ | E₂) = 0. We introduced the concept of independence in discussing concepts for analyzing the structure of events. At that time, we said that events are independent if the occurrence of one event provides no information about the likelihood of the other event. Now that we have introduced the concept of conditional probabilities, we can state the concept of independence more precisely: E₁ and E₂ are independent if p(E₁ | E₂) = p(E₁); i.e., that is, if the probability of E₁ is the same whether or not E₂ occurs.

We may ask such questions as “Getting a grade of A independent of the major of the student?” This is another way of asking whether the probability of getting an A is the same for linguistics majors and psychology majors. If there is independence, p(A | linguistics) = p(A | psychology) = 15/60 = .25, p(linguistics | A) = 12/40 = .3, and p(A) = .27. For this data set, getting A is not independent of being a psychology or linguistics major because p(A | psychology) is not equal to p(A | linguistics); therefore, <A, psychology major> and <A, linguistics major> are pairs of events that are not independent. On the other hand, p(B | psychology) = p(B | linguistics) = p(B), so that for this data set, getting a grade of B is independent of the student’s major.
