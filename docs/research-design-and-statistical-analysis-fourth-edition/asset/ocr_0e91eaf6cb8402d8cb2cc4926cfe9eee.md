+   4. Conduct a priori calculations. We have emphasized how statistical software as G*Power 3.1 can be used to determine the number of participants necessary to achieve a specified level of power for finding significant results, given a specified effect size. If we fail to perform these calculations and perform a study with too few participants, the result will be an effort of effort and resources without much chance of finding significant results. Also, if we bear our calculations on a measure of effect size obtained from a small pilot study, we must remember that the point estimate for the effect size is likely to be larger than the population size, and that the confidence interval for this measure may also be large, resulting in large confidence intervals over the estimated number of participants. We can estimate these by limiting the required N at the upper and lower ends of the confidence interval for the effect size.

+   5. Always explore the data. Before conducting any statistical tests, always generate a thorough description of the data and consider it carefully. Statistical software provide many graphical display options that allow us to summarize and display data. We check for the presence of extreme outliers and data points that may have influence on the results. We must make sure that results do not occur because of anomalies from malfunctions in procedure or computer glitches during the data analysis. If we have outliers that cannot be explained by equipment failures or transcription errors, in many cases we can fall back on robust procedures/or determine the sensitivity of our findings to these questionable data points by determining whether we come to the same conclusions regardless of whether these points are included. We should also check to see if there are missing data that might bias our results.

+   6. Correlations without scatterplots is misleading. The Pearson correlation coefficient is a measure of the extent that two variables are linearly related. However, there may be a nonlinear component to the relationship between two variables in addition to, or instead of, a linear one. It is possible that there is an important systematic relationship between two variables even though their correlation is very small. Correlations are also very sensitive to the presence of outliers. If it must be important to discuss correlations, we must look at the relevant scatterplots. Moreover, we must take note the level of analysis: Combining different groups can grossly distort the correlation that may be present within each group.

+   7. Do not categorize inherently continuous independent variables. Because some researchers are more comfortable with t χ tests and ANOVA with regression, we see the use of median splits (i.e., placing all values below the median in one category and all values above the median in another) to turn inherently predictor variables into dichotomous ones. This procedure away away information and categorizes observations in an arbitrary way. For example, the smallest value and the value just below the median are placed in the same category even though they may be very different, but the value just above it are placed in different categories even though they may be similar. Dichotomizing tends to reduce statistical power (cf. Irwin & McClelland, 2001)
