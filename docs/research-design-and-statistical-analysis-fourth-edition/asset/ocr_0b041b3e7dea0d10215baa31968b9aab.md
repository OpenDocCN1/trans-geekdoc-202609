# 17.4 Linear Relationships

A straight line can be represented by an equation of the form

```
Y = b_0 + b_1X
```

where $b_0$ and $b_1$ are constants because all points (X, Y) that satisfy this *linear equation* fall on a straight line. The constant $__1$ is the *slope* of this line and indicates the rate of change of Y with X. We can see from the equation for a straight line that for every-unit increase in X, Y changes by $b_1$ units. The constant $b_0$ is the *Y-intercept*, the value of Y when X is zero. This equation describes a perfect linear relationship between X and Y. In fact, perfect linear relationships do not occur in behavioral data, but there are many occasions when a line provides a good approximation of the relationship between two variables.
