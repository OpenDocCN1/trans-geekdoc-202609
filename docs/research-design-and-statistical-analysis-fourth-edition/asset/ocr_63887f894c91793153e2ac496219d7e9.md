The number of contrasts associated with a factor in the experimental design should be constrained by the df with that source of variance: Testing more contrasts than there are $df$ always means that there is some redundancy in the questions, in the sense that the contrasts must share some variance (pieces of the pie overlap). Also, as the number of contrasts increases, the power of each one decreases because the FWE must be controlled.

Summarizing, we have developed the idea that a sum of squares based on multiple degrees of freedom can be broken into independent parts, of which each may be tested as a separate contrast. One way to conduct such analyses is to define $a-1$ orthogonal contrasts, testing them either with $t$ or an $F$ ratio. No matter what they are tested, contrasts partition the SS for some of variance into independent components, and if there are $a-1$ orthogonal contrasts then together account for all the variance associated with the source.

# 10.12 Summary

This chapter developed the following points:

+   - Contrasts are specific comparisons on a set of means that allow researchers to pose detailed questions of a data set. They capture components of the source of variance we intend to analyze. If a set of contrasts is orthogonal, they partition the variance associated with the source of variance, asking logically independent questions.
- Procedures for conducting hypothesis tests and constructing confidence intervals on contrasts are straightforward test procedures in Chapter 6.
- In an experimental design of any complexity, there are many possible contrasts that might be of interest to a researcher. The probability that least one Type 1 error will occur in a set, or of contrasts increases as a function of the size of the family. It is therefore important to control the probability of a Type 1 error across a family (i.e., FWE).
- Just as there are many types of human families, there are many kinds of families of contrasts. These include the family of comparisons prior to data collection; the family of all possible contrasts of members of group mean; the family of all comparisons of experimental groups with a group mean; and the family of post hoc contrasts determined after viewing the data.
- Different methods have been developed for controlling the FWE, depending on the kind of family and on whether intervals are desired. Some of these methods involve the usual $t$ statistic, or its close relation, the Studentized range statistic, or when heterogeneity of variance is suspected – Welch’s $t$.
