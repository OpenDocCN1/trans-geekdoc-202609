## 15.2.5 Estimating Omega-Squared in the Hierarchical Design

As in previous chapters, we wish to estimate the proportion of variance due to the factor of interest that is relative to variances due to participants and intrinsic factors, as well as error variance. Therefore, we define omega-squared

$$ \omega_e^2(A) = \sigma_A^2 / (\sigma_A^2 + \sigma_a^2 + \sigma_S^2 + \sigma_{SA}^2) \text{ (15.1)} $$

Assuming $n$ participants, levels of A, and $b$ levels of B, our estimates of the parameters in Equation 15.1 are

$$ \sigma_A^2 = \frac{a-1}{a} \frac{MS_A - (MS_S + MS_{B/A} - MS_{S\times B/A})}{bn} $$
$$ \sigma_S^2 = (MS_S - MS_{S\times B/A})/ab $$
$$ \sigma_{SA}^2 = (MS_{SA} - MS_{S\times B/A})/b \text{ (15.2)} $$

### 15.3.2 Partitioning Sum of Squares and Degrees Freedom

As our analysis of the data in Table 15.1, we have two-participants sources of variance: A (difficulty level) and B/A (problems nested within difficulty level). In the present design, we have added the between-participants variable, C (experience), so participants are now nested within levels of C. The SV column in the ANOVA of Table 15.5 includes these four sources of variance (C, S/C, B/A) as well as additional interaction terms. The AC term is the interaction of difficulty level with experience, and $SS_{AC}$ is calculated in all. Because all problems are present at both levels of experience, C also crosses with the problems, giving rise to the CB/A term.

### Box 15.2 Quasi-F Test of C for the Data of Table 15.4 (C Is Between Participants, B Is Nested in Levels of A)

To test the effect of experience (C),

$$ F' = MS_{SIC} / (MS_{SIC} + MS_{C,B/A} - MS_{Residual}) $$

Substituting values of mean squares from Table 15.5 into the preceding equation, we have

$$ F' = 94.70 / (5.22 + 2.81 - .26) = 12.19 $$

The numerator degrees of freedom is $c-1$, and the denominator degrees of freedom are $\frac{MS_{SIC} + MS_{C,B/A} - MS_{Residual}}{F'}$.
