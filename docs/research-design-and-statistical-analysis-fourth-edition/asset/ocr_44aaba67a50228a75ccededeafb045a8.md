The second measure of effect size in the correlated-scores design is $d = (\mu_1 - \mu_2)/\sigma$; The population means are estimated by the corresponding sample means and $\sigma$ is estimated by averaging the two within-condition variances and taking the square root of the root. Using the Tc change score to illustrate, first converting the standard deviations to variances by squaring, we compute our estimate of the pooled standard deviation by $\sqrt{(2,246.521+4,111.725)/2} = 56.384$. Then, $\hat{d} = (224.509-208.1694)/56.384=.29$, a relatively small effect.

Cohen preferred d over t, because values of the former are comparable to those obtained from experiments involving the same conditions but in an independent-groups. How ever, dz is more directly linked to power calculations, as we will discuss in Section 6.8.2. In any event, the two scores are closely related; if $s_{diff}$ is the standard deviation of the difference scores and $s_{pool}$ is the square root of the average within-condition variance, then $\hat{d} = (s_{diff}/s_{pool})\hat{d}_c$.
