# 6.8 Deciding on Sample Size

Too many experiments waste time, effort, and resources because the researcher has failed to collect enough data to detect sufficient differences that may be present in the population. Power estimates should be used when deciding on the sample size. Analyses that do have been referred to as a priori or prospective analyses.

6.8.1 A Priori Power Analyses in the Independent-Groups Design

How might we decide on the sample size of a planned experiment with two independent groups? When we begin by deciding on the smallest effect size that would be of interest. For example, if a current drug is known to reduce total cholesterol, say 20 mg/dl of blood, then these reductions would not be marketable. We also need to decide on an acceptable level of power to detect that smallest effect size of interest. Understanding the role of these two requirements in calculating power explains how t is distributed when the null hypothesis is false. The relevant distribution is the noncentral t distribution and we discuss it next. Following that discussion, we will consider a specific example of its application to the decision over sample size.
