# HTTPS

According to Google (https://blog.chromium.org/2023/08/towards-https-by-default.html) about 90% of Internet connections are made over an HTTPS connection, and that percentage is growing over time. When HTTPS is enabled and enforced as intended, it ensures that users can confirm that the URL they are connecting to is what it is displayed to be. It also means the data transferred by the involved nodes is encrypted between source and destination. This is true when the digital certificate involved in the HTTPS connection is valid and trusted. If the involved digital certificate is fraudulent or issued by an untrustworthy certification authority (CA), then the involved server site or application could be fraudulent.

When browsing the Internet (or even the local network), everyone should try to ensure they are connecting over trusted HTTPS connections using trustworthy certificates.
