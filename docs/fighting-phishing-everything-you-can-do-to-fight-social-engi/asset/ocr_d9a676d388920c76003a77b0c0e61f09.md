The string of characters following the p= variable is the DKIM public key. A single domain can have multiple DKIM keys. This is very common with big domains, like Google and Microsoft, and with domains with multiple email servers. There can also be multiple DKIM keys when one older key is nearing its expiration date (or has expired) and another key is now being used to sign current emails.

Figure 7-11 shows KnowBe4's DKIM record downloaded from DNS. The k= variable indicates what digital signature algorithm is being used, and p= variable shows the public key in base64 binary format. An email agent receiving a DKIM-signed email from KnowBe4 could download KnowBe4's DKIM public key and use it to verify the DKIM signature of any email sent by KnowBe4.
