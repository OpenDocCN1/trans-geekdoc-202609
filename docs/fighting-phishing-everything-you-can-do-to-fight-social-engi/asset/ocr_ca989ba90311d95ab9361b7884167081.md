# Summary

DMARC, SPF, and DKIM are global anti-phishing standards that can be used to prevent domain spoofing in emails. DMARC may become a US national or regulatory requirement in the near future. SPF works by verifying the 5321.MailFrom email address, and DKIM works by verifying the 5322.From email address. SPF does this by using a specialized DNS record. DKIM uses DNS along with digitally signing sent emails. DMARC allows senders to choose the treatment (e.g., accept, reject, or quarantine) for emails claiming to be from their domain and to select reporting modes. DMARC, SPF, and DKIM have been successful in stopping most email domain spoofing. However, phishers have largely adopted DMARC, SPF, and DKIM, so most phishing emails will pass the checks. Still, when DMARC, SPF, and DKIM are used, the phishers cannot as easily use or spoof the domains of well-known brands and domains.
