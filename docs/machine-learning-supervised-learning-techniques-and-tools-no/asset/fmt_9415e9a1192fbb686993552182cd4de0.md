Truncated regression is a special case of a general problem known as non-random sample selection that occurs when the sample available for estimation is not random.

Heckman devised a two-stage procedure for estimating models in the presence of incidental truncation that overcomes the problem of selection bias:

Stage 2. Using the selected sample (s=1) we estimate by OLS the regression of Y on X and ![image](d2d_images/image246.png).

This procedure yields consistent and asymptotically normal estimators.

A test for the existence of sample selection bias consists of testing in the second stage the hypothesis of `ρ = 0` using the t-statistic of ![image](d2d_images/image619.png). When `ρ ≠ 0` the standard errors of the 2nd stage are not valid and must be corrected.

We have assumed that X is a subset of Z because if we inappropriately exclude any element of X we may have inconsistent estimators. It is important that Z contains at least one element that does not belong to X. This is not essential for the identification of `β` as the non-linearity of ![image](d2d_images/image619.png) is sufficient for this purpose.

But if we have additional elements, the correlation of ![image](d2d_images/image619.png) with X decreases and we will obtain more precise estimators of the `β`. Moreover, if in the first stage we do not include additional items, the significance of ![image](d2d_images/image619.png) in the second stage may be due to sample selection or misspecification.

In addition to using the 2-stage procedure, this type of models can be estimated by MV, although it is more complicated because we need the joint distribution of Y and s.
