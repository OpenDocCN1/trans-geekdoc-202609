# NB Features/Directions SB (km)

19.16 Main intersection of old Nelson. On NE corner, picture an old Victorian home with widow’s walk, not relocated but bulldozed. Fuel up at the gas stations on two other corners. Funeral on SW corner, now passed on itself. At the corner, an unblazed side trail descends trail-compass-north to lake shore in 65 m. The concrete steps barely holding on between tree roots, beside road on your right, once descended to homes on either side at the edge of the lake, the Lugg family farm now under the lake. The farm house moved up the hill behind you was at the time occupied by Ila Lugg Wiley, who in 70's fought to move the village as a community, not just the individual residents. She lived a long full life nearly 100 years. Imagine her daughter as a little girl, learning to ride a bicycle, straying from the road into a tree, now visible as a large stump in the lake. The fertile bottom land on both sides once grew tobacco. Or turn uphill from the corner on an overgrown gated road to reach new highway in 104 m.

19.70 Parking lot, 6.4 miles from PA 49 west of Lawrenceville. South end of skiable, accessible to backcountry, Moccasin Trail section. Unblazed trail across PA 49 trail-compass-south leads from here to Nelson in 220 m. Waterfall began when 1936 cut through a long bend in the river. Floods in 1946 finished creating the island opposite the viewpoint. Fast water, high flow over the rocks to deep pool. Swimming, or climbing down from the vista to the fast eroding riverbank is prohibited. Look closely at the soil exposed in the cut above. Alternating layers of sand, silt, and clay, varves, show this was the bottom of a glacial lake much larger and higher than today’s reservoir. Thanks to Mallory of Lawrenceville for geological information.

# SECTION A: GREENWOOD SPUR
PA 305 TO DETWEILER JUNCTION
ROTHROCK STATE FOREST

Greenwood Furnace State Park is on PA 305 between Belleville and McAlevys Fort. Detweiler Junction is reached from Bear Meadows Road at the junction with Detweiler Road. The orange blazed Standing Stone Trail connecting the Greenwood Spur to the Tuscarora Trail begins on the south side of PA 305 across the park office.

Water is available at several springs along this section as well as at the state park. Features of the spur are the historic sites at Greenwood Furnace, Alan Seeger Natural Area and Greenwood Fire Tower on top of Broad Mountain.

There is primitive camping at the Maggi Cutoff and along Detweiler Run but camping is prohibited in Alan Seeger NA. Intermediate access to the spur is available via Seeger and Stone roads. The Greenwood Spur, the first major side trail in the Mid State system, was opened in 1974.
