Although the voluntary, willing permission MSTA has secured individual and corporate landowners takes various forms, common rules for hiking MST across private land are:

+   - Stay on trail. Leash your dog (not exceeding 2 m) and clean up any waste left behind.
- Do not use any vehicles, ATV's, horses, or bikes (all of which the owner probably uses himself to be very careful you're not talking to the owner if you confront a rider, he pays the taxes and the mortgage and you don't).
- Respect owners' property and privacy (a similar trail was closed when hikers set up a picnic lunch on the front lawn of the owners' house, and refused to move when the owner asked them to).
- Don't pick up anything or leave anything behind (this goes for all trails, but especially on private land where one indiscretion can result in the trail being closed forever).
- No camping (exception: as of the date of publication, camping for one night only is allowed at two locations noted in this Guide on Wool-rich, Inc. Property, and one location on section 18 in Tioga County, ONLY).
- No fires.
- No alcohol, and certainly no illegal substances.
- If the blazes and signs disagree with the Guide andor maps, follow the blazes and posted instructions!
- Stay off private land during posted hunting season closures: closures are noted where possible in this Guide.
- Even when not closed, strongly consider avoiding private land during spring and fall turkey seasons, and fall/winter deer and bear seasons.
- No hunting (that's for you, the owners' cousins probably hunt it.

The Golden Rule: Be courteous of the rights of private landowners-just as you would expect an uninvited guest on your own property to behave.
