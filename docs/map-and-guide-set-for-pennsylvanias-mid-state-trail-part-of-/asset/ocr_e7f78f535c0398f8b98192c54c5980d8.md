# WATER

The ridge route of much the MST means springs and streams are few and far between. It is frequently necessary to find water on side trails both marked and unmarked. In many cases these junctions have been signed but distances and directions to the water supply are given only in this guide. "Reliable" is used here to mean a water source that does not dry up in August and September. Probably none of these springs or streams could pass the presumptive tests of public health departments. Giardia is known to be present in several water sources near the MST. MST springs are certified only to contain a substantial proportion of dihydrogen monooxide when flowing (H$_2$O).

Water that presumably does meets is available from pumps or faucets at Stone Valley, Penn Roosevelt, Greenwood Furnace, Poe Valley, Poe Paddy, R.B. Winter, Ravensburg, Camp Kline, Little Pine, Black-well Canoe Access, Hills Creek, Ives Run, Tioga Heritage, and Cow-anesque. But only in season. The fastest method to purify water for drinking is a submicron filter of which there is a bewildering variety available. Boiling water in the field to purify it is recommended only by those who haven't tried it.

Hikers are strongly urged to carry one or more canteens, water bottles, hydration bladders, or a water bag on the MST.

Winter, Ravensburg, Little Pine, and Hills Creek at the prevailing state park fees and only in season.
