The other fire was on Fourth Mountain between Fleet Foot and North Meadows Trails. The chestnut oaks were killed but some pines survived. The burn area is in no hurry to come back to trees and has become a large patch of huckleberry and Mountain Laurel. Fleet Foot Trail has vanished in the brush which encroaches on the main trail as well. Keeping Fourth Mountain open is another challenge as brush grows fast in the open sunlight. Bears are attracted to the berry patch.

Neither of these fires should have happened. Open fires at primitive camp sites are prohibited during periods of high fire danger. These dates are March 1st to May 25th and October 1st to December 1st. At other times use common sense in building ground fires. Try to find a spot that has been used before. Close in your camp fire with rocks, remove all flammable material from the immediate area and keep your fire small. Don’t leave your fire unattended, not even for a couple of minutes. An unexpected gust of wind could take it completely out of your control by the time you get back to camp. Make sure by pouring water on it that camp fire is completely out before you break camp. The Mid State Trail Association urges you to use a backpacking stove (Optimus, Primus, Svea, MSR, etc.) as they present a minimal fire hazard. If campfires are built on the top of narrow ridges they can be seen for great distances. When these fires are reported forestry has to check them out on the ground. Please refrain from building fires in such locations. It makes a lot of extra work for forestry and doesn't earn us any friends.
