# NOTICE TO ALL TRAIL USERS

Every effort has been made by the Mid State Trail Association to provide the most accurate and up-to-date information about the Mid State Trail in this publication. However, conditions on the trail are constantly changing. Acts of nature, forestry practices, and human developments are only a small number of events that necessitate the rerouting of the Mid State Trail. Changes to the route described within this publication can and will occur over time and, therefore, no published route can be regarded as precisely accurate at the time you read this notice. Hikers using this publication should do so at their own risk and the use of this publication should be in conjunction with current maps, as well as a compass or GPS, and with common sense precautions. Always tell someone of your hiking plans. Visit the Mid State Trail website (http://www.hike-mst.org) for the most recent information regarding trail reroutes.

# PREAMBLE

The first edition of this trail guide was printed in 1972. Already the name had been changed from Central Allegheny (It was in the Appalachian Ridge and Valley Region NOT the Alleghenies and the name produced confusion with that trail that crosses Pennsylvania) to Mid State Trail in compliance with OUTDOOR RECREATION HORIZONS, a planning document issued in 1970 by the Department of Forests and Waters. See map on page 137 of this document for a good deal of foreshadowing of the current route of the longest trail in Penn's Woods.

The 1973 edition was the first metric and the last self-publishing edition. The 1974 to Fifth editions were held together with little brass clips allowing the insertion of new pages as the trail was relocated repeatedly during these years but the guides were printed commercially. The 6th and 7th editions were stapled and the 8th was the first to be printed by Mid State Trail Association. The 9th edition became a real paperback book. The 11th edition was the first to cover the trail through the entire state, from Maryland to New York. This edition is the 12th publication of the Guide to Pennsylvania Mid State Trail and incorporates the many spurs that offer additional access to the Mid State Trail, as well as providing elevation profiles of the various trail sections.

# INTRODUCTION

The Mid State Trail (MST) is an old fashioned wilderness foot path across Pennsylvania from Bedford County in the south to Tioga County. Features of the MST are its many views and side trails, its sense of isolation and remoteness, and its use of metric measure. Unique features are the tunnel under Paddy Mountain and a portion of the Greatland Indian Path near Ravensburg State Park. The latter is believed to be one of two or three bits of unaved Indian paths on public lands in Penn Woods. The Burnt Cabins to Franktown Path is crossed in SGL 118.

The Mid State Trail Association (MSTA) is an organization of trail overseers and others formed in 1982 and dedicated to the completion, maintenance, and preservation of the Mid State Trail System.

Most of the Mid State Trail is on public lands including State Forests, State Game Lands and State Parks. The MST links or traverses state game lands, two scout camps, four state wild areas, three state forest picnic areas, five state forests, eight state parks, ten state forest natural areas, the Woolrich factory outlet store and.

# PRIVATE LAND

Some parts of MST, especially those closer to Maryland and New York borders, cross private land. Although voluntary, common rules for MST:

- Stay on trail. Leash your dog and clean up any waste left.
- Do not use any vehicles, ATVs, horses, or bikes.
- Respect owners' property and privacy.
- Don't pick up anything or leave behind.
- No camping (except at two locations noted in this Guide).
- No fires.
- No alcohol, and certainly no illegal substances.
- If the blazes and signs disagree with the Guide, follow the blazes and posted instructions!
- Stay off private land during posted closures: closures are noted where possible in this Guide.
- Even when not closed, strongly consider avoiding private land during spring and fall seasons.
- No hunting.

The Golden Rule: Be courteous of the rights of private landowners as you would expect an uninvited guest on your own property to behave.

Finally, if you do not intend to follow all of these rules while passing through private land on the MST, please hike another trail so that polite, quiet hikers can continue to enjoy in perpetuity of the diversity of features along MST.

# FOOT PATH ONLY

The main trail and side trails are described in both northbound and southbound directions in a table format. The trail description is separated into twenty sections divided by paved roads or highways (expect the division between secs. 8 and 9). Crossings of roads that can be navigated by an ordinary car at sometime in the year are noted as intermediate access points. The MST is still a rugged and demanding trail.

In Rothrock State Forest the MST consists largely of ridge top trail built since 1969 by the Hiking Division of the Penn State Outing Club and other groups. East of Stillhouse Hollow in Bald Eagle, Tiadhton, and Tioga State Forests the MST follows many old trails linked with lengths of new trail. Other groups which have helped build the MST include convict laborers from Huntington State Prison, Standing Stone Garden Club trail crews, CETA, Girl Scouts, Youth Conservation Corps, Boy Scouts, adjudicated juveniles, community service workers, PSU Faculty Women's Club Hiking Interest Group, Bucknell University Outing Club, Susquehanna University Outing Club, Asaph Trail Club, as well as volunteers of the Keystone Association and of course the Mid State Trail Association.

The Mid State Trail was designed, built, and is maintained for foot travel only.
