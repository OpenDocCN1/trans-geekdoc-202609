If you are navigating using a Global Positioning System (GPS) device, you can probably set it to read your position directly in UTM. If you do this sure the units of length are not any other units, and that the datum is NAD 1983 not, say, NAD 1927.

If you are navigating using a compass after the batteries in your GPS unit go dead, you should have prepared first by finding out your magnetic declination relative to the UTM grid moves every year and no magnetic declination is shown on the MSTA maps. Remember the MSTA map north is not true but UTM grid north – hence you need to add the local grid and the magnetic declination to find the declination you will use for your hike. The UTM grid should you have read to this point not knowing what “declination” is, consult any standard reference or the Internet to learn map and compass use.

Topographic contour lines at 50 or 25 meter intervals relate to mean sea level are shown on the maps. Reading contour line topographic maps is an art that can be easily learned from numerous sources. When you compare MSTA maps to other topographic maps, be aware that 50 meters is a much greater interval than most other maps you will see. In other words, when the contour lines get close together on an MSTA map, the terrain is truly steep – but if the contour lines are far apart, that doesn’t mean the terrain is flat.
