## 3. Experimental procedure

The printed sensor by AJP process for experiments was a typical strain gauge, as shown in Fig. 4. Figs. 4(a) and 4 (b) show the design and the fabricated shape of the printed sensor, respectively.

The sensor was printed using the AJ5X aerosol jet printer from Optomec.

![](img/66c56d2f673c4ec909c972f118c5aaf4_2_0.png)

![](img/66c56d2f673c4ec909c972f118c5aaf4_2_1.png)

Fig. 4. Printed strain gauge by AJP process: (a) sensor design and (b) printed sensor.

The ink used is the NovaCentrix Metalon JS-B25P, a suspension of silver nanoparticles in an aqueous solvent. The ink is maintained at 25 °C by the water bath and a cooler. The ultrasonic transducer atomizes the ink particles into an aerosol cloud. The carrier gas stream, which is nitrogen gas, entrains the aerosolized ink and carries it to the virtual impactor, where excess carrier gas is diverted away, concentrating the ink particles. The aerosol stream then travels to the print head. The sheath gas enwraps the ink forming a collimated stream of ink particles. The focused ink aerosol would impact the substrate and deposit the ink particles. The substrate is a polyimide film and the nozzle used has a 200 μm diameter orifice. After the sensor was printed, it was sintered in a laboratory furnace for 30 min at 180 °C. Silver conductive epoxy was then applied to connect some wires to the sensor's contact pads. These wires were then connected to a DC power supply, where a current of 0.1 A was applied to the sensor.

The VarioCAM HD Head 600 thermographic camera from InfraTec was used as the thermographic camera to capture the thermal data during the heating process caused by the current passing through the sensor. The sensor was then put under a confocal microscope from Keyence to examine the morphology of the printed lines.

### 4.2. Repair of defective region

To repair the printed sensor with defective regions, additional material is deposited on the defective region of the sensor using the AJP process. During the flowing current into the repaired sensor, the temperature distribution of the sensor is investigated by the thermographic camera. Fig. 7 shows thermographic images for the repaired sensor. The previously defective regions with relatively higher temperature distributions are repaired, displaying uniform temperature distribution. The lack of hot spots shows that there is no localized heat generation from low resistance of the material, indicating the cross-sectional area of the defective region has increased. The uniform temperature distribution shows that the printed lines have uniform cross-sectional area and electric resistance. From this result, it is revealed that the defective region of the printed sensor is properly repaired by the proposed inspection and repair method.

The repaired sensor is observed by the confocal microscope, as shown in Fig. 8. Through the comparison of Fig. 6(c) and Fig. 8(b), it is observed that the mean height of lines of the printed sensor increases from 8.0 to 10.0 μm to 14–16 μm through the repair of the printed sensor. The increase in height of the defective regions in the printed lines leads to an increase in the cross-sectional area of these regions, thus repairing the defective areas. From these results, it is noted that the electric resistance of the printed sensor is reduced by additionally deposited materials of the repaired sensor.

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method has the capability of identifying defective regions to improve the quality of the printed sensor by repairing printed sensor.

## 5. Conclusions

A novel quality inspection method using infrared imaging and a machine learning approach was proposed to identify the defective regions in the printed sensor by AJP process. From the analysis of the confocal microscope images, it was revealed that the increased electric resistance, which was induced by the reduced cross-sectional area of the defective region, causes increase in temperature at the hot spots. The hot spots were assumed to be potentially defective regions. A support vector machine algorithm was adopted to differentiate defective and non-defective regions. The defective printed sensor was repaired through the deposition of additional material on the defective region using the AJP process. The temperature distribution and formation of the repaired sensor were examined. From the results of the examination, it was demonstrated that the defective region with a relatively higher temperature distribution was eliminated. In addition, it is revealed that the height and the cross-sectional area of the repaired region were recovered to those of non-defective printed lines. Based on the above results, it was shown that ML-assisted quality inspection and closed-loop quality enhancement framework can improve the quality of the printed sensor by the AJP process.

In the future, additional data collection is needed to improve the applied machine learning algorithm, and the data collected will be incorporated into a feedback control system in the AJP apparatus, such that defects can be detected and automatically repaired.
