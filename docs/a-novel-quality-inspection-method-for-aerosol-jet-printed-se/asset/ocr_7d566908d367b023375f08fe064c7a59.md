# 3. Experimental procedure

The printed sensor by AJP process for experiments was a typical strain gauge, as shown in Fig. 4. Figs. 4(a) and 4 (b) show the design and the fabricated shape of the printed sensor, respectively.

The sensor was printed using the AJ5X aerosol jet printer from Opto-

mec. The ink used is the NovaCentrix Metalon JS-B25P, a suspension of silver nanoparticles in an aqueous solvent. The ink is maintained at 25 °C by the water bath and a cooler. The ultrasonic transducer atomizes the ink particles into an aerosol cloud. The carrier gas stream, which is nitrogen gas, entrains the aerosolized ink and carries it to the virtual impactor, where excess carrier gas is diverted away, concentrating the ink particles. The aerosol stream then travels to the print head. The sheath gas enwraps the ink forming a collimated stream of ink particles. The focused ink aerosol would impact the substrate and deposit the ink particles. The substrate is a polyimide film and the nozzle used has a 200 μm diameter orifice. After the sensor was printed, it was sintered in a laboratory furnace for 30 min at 180 °C. Silver conductive epoxy was then applied to connect some wires to the sensor's contact pads. These wires were then connected to a DC power supply, where a current of 0.1 A was applied to the sensor.

The VarioCAM HD Head 600 thermographic camera from InfraTec was used as the thermographic camera to capture the thermal data during the heating process caused by the current passing through the sensor. The sensor was then put under a confocal microscope from Keyence to examine the morphology of the printed lines.
