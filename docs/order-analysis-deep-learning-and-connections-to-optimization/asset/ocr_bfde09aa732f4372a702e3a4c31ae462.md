We discuss a very simple example.

**Example 2.8 ([74, Example 3.2])** For the cone $C := \mathbb{R}_+^+$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$, it is well-known that $C$ coincides with its dual cone $C^*$, i.e., $C$ is self-dual. For the $-C$ representing functional $\psipsi \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$

$$\psi(y) := \max\{y_1, y_2\}$ for all $y = (y_1, y_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2$,

we obtain the subdifferential

$$\partial\psi(0_{\mathbb{R}^2}) = {\ell \in \mathbb{R}^2 \| \psi(y) \geq \ell^T y \forall y \in \mathbb{R}^2}$

$= {(\ell_1, \ell_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \| \max\{y_1, y_2\} \geq \ell_1 y_1 + \ell_2 y_2 \forall (y_1, y_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2$.

By a simple calculation, we find that the inequality

$$\max\{y_1, y_2\} \geq \ell_1 y_1 + \ell_2 y_2$ for all $(y_1, y_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2$

is equivalent to the condition $(\ell_1, \ell_2) \in \text{conv} \{ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix} \}, i.e.,

$cone(\partial\psi(0_{\mathbb{R}^2})) = cone = \text{conv} \{ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix} \} = \mathbb{R}_+^+ = C^*$.

This example shows that the inclusion in the assertion of Theorem 2.2 can also be an equality.
