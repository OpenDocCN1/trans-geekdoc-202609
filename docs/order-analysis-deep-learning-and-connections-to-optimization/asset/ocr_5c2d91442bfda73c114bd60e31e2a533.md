Note that the modulus of continuity is generally used to quantitatively measure uniform continuity (see [98, p. 239]).

The following theorem is presented without proof:

Theorem 6.1 (Approximation Property of ReLU Deep Feedforward Neural Networks [127, Theorem 1.1]) Let the continuous function $g \in C([0,1]^d)$ be arbitrarily chosen, and let $s, t \in \mathbb{N}$ and $p \in [1, \infty)$ be numbers. Then there exists a real-valued function $f$ in the class of all functions generated by a ReLU deep feedforward neural network with width $\alpha$ max $\{d t^{-\frac{1}{2}}, t+1\}$ and depth $2s+eta$, where

```
$$\alpha := \begin{cases} 12 & p \in [1, \infty) \ 3^{d+3} & p = \infty \end{cases}$$
```

and

```
$$\beta := \begin{cases} 14 & p \in [1, \infty) \ 14+2d & p = \infty, \end{cases}$$
```

so that

$$\|g-f\|_{L^p((0,1)^d)} \le 19\sqrt{d}\omega_g(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}).$.$(6.2)$

**Remark 6.1 (Special Classes of the Modulus of Continuity)** In Theorem 6.1, the upper bound in inequality (6.2) uses the modulus of continuity $\omega_g$. It is known (see, for example, [98, p. 239]) that the inequality $\omega_g(\varepsilon) \le c\varepsilon$ for some constant $c \in \mathbb{R}$ describes the Lipschitz continuity of $g$ and the inequality $\omega_g(\varepsilon) \le c\varepsilon^{\\eta}$ for $\eta \in (0,1]$ describes the Holder continuity of $g$.

# 6.5 Supervised Learning

A neural network describes a large class of functions, but for a specific problem, we need to specify the unknown weights and biases in this network. This can be done by *learning* from a given dataset, also a *training dataset*. Such a training dataset consists of inputs (such as features) and correct outputs. The computation of appropriate weights and biases of a deep neural network on a training dataset is *supervised learning*. In this chapter, we restrict ourselves mainly to methods of unconstrained optimization. In [67]).
