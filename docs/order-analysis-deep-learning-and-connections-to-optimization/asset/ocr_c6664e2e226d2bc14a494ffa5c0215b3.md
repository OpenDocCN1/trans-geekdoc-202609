**Proposition 1.8** Let $X$ be a real linear space, and let $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ be a convex functional. Then at every $\bar{x} \in X$ and in every direction $h \in X$, the directional derivative $f'(\bar{x})(h)$ exists.

**Definition 1.24** Let $(X, \|\cdot\|)$ be a real normed space, and let $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ be a convex functional. For an arbitrary $\bar{x} \in X$, the set $\partial f(\bar{x})$ of all continuous linear functions $\ell$ on $X$

```
$f(x) \ge f(\bar{x}) + \ell(x - \bar{x}) \text{ for all } x \in X$$
```

is called the *subdifferential* of $f$ at $\bar{x}$. A continuous linear functional $\ell \in \partial f(\bar{x})$ is called a *subgradient* of $f$ at $\bar{x}$ (see Fig. 1.13).
