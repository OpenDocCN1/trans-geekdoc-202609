We discuss a very simple example.

**Example 2.8 ([74, Example 3.2])** For the cone $C := \mathbb{R}_+^2 in $\mathbb{R}^2$, it is well-known that $C$ coincides with its dual cone $C^*$, i.e., $C$ is self-dual. For the $-C$ representing functional $\psi: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$

$$\psi(y) := \max\{y_1, y_2\} \text{ for all } y = (y_1, y_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2,$$

we obtain the subdifferential

$$\partial\psi(0_{\mathbb{R}^2}) = \{\ell \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid \psi(y) \geq \ell^T y \forall y \in \mathbb{R}^2\}$$

$= \{(\ell_1, \ell_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid \max\{y_1, y_2\} \geq \ell_1 y_1 + \ell_2 y_2 \forall (y_1, y_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2\}.$$

By a simple calculation, we find that the inequality

$$\max\{y_1, y_2\} \geq \ell_1 y_1 + \ell_2 y_2 \text{ for all } (y_1, y_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2$$

is equivalent to the condition $(\ell_1, \ell_2) \in \text{conv} \{ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \}$, i.e.,

$cone(\partial\psi(0_{\mathbb{R}^2})) = \text{conv} \{ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \} = \mathbb{R}_+^+ = C^*$.

This example shows that the inclusion in the assertion of Theorem 2.2 can also be an equality.

**Proposition 2.15 ([74, Proposition 3.9])** Let $C$ be a convex cone of a real normed space $(Y, \|\cdot\|)$, and let $\psi$ be a $-C$ representing functional with the property that $y \in Y$ have $y \in -C \iff \psi(y) = 0$.

Then $0^* \in \partial\psi(0_Y)$.

**Proof** every $y \in Y$, we have $\psi(y) \geq \psi(0_Y) + 0^*(y)$ implying $0^* \in \partial\psi(0_Y)$.

**Theorem 2.3 ([74, Theorem 3.2])** Let $C$ be a convex cone of a real normed space $(Y, \|\cdot\|)$, and let $\psi$ be a $-C$ representing functional with the property that for $y \in Y$ we have $y \in -C \iff \psi(y) = 0$. Let $\psi'(0_Y)$ denote the directional derivative of $\psi$ at $0_Y$. Under the additional assumption $\ell \in C^* \exists \alpha_{\ell} > 0 : \alpha_{\ell}(y) \leq \psi'(0_Y)(y)$ for all $y \in Y$, then holds $C^* \subseteq \text{cone}(\partial\psi(0_Y))$.

**Proof** Let $\ell \in C^*$ be arbitrarily chosen. By assumption (2.15), there exists $\alpha_{\ell} > 0$ so that $\alpha_{\ell}(y) \leq \psi'(0_Y)(y)$ for all $y \in Y$. With Theorem 1.3 (a), we obtain $\alpha_{\ell} \in \partial\psi(0_Y)$ implying $\ell \in \frac{1}{\alpha_{\ell}}\partial\psi(0_Y)$. Consequently, we have $\ell \in \text{cone}(\partial\psi(0_Y))$.

**Corollary 2.1** Let the assumptions of Theorems 2.2 and 2.3 be fulfilled. Then we have $C^* = cl[\text{cone}(\partial\psi(0_Y))]$.

**Proof** By Theorem 2.3, we obtain $C^* \subseteq \text{cone}(\partial\psi(0_Y)) \subseteq cl[\text{cone}(\partial\psi(0_Y))]$ and Theorem 2.2 shows $cl[\text{cone}(\partial\psi(0_Y)) ] \subseteq C^*$, which completes the proof.
