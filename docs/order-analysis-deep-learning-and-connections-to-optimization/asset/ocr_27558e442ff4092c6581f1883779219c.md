# 6.6 Gradient Methods

# Algorithm 6.4 (Stochastic gradient method)

```
1 Input: $f_1, \dots, f_p : \mathbb{R}^n 	o \mathbb{R}$, starting vector $x^0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$,
2 maximal iteration number $i_{max}$.
3
4 $i := 0$ % initialization
5 while $i \le i_{max}$ do
6 $k := 	ext{randi}(p)$ % random integer (uniformly sampled in $\{1, \dots, p\}$)
7 $d^i := -
abla f_k(x^i)$ % iteration direction
8 choose $\lambda_i > 0$ % step size
9 $x^{i+1} := x^i + \lambda_i d^i$
10 $i := i + 1$
11 end while
12
13 Output: $x^1, x^2, \dots$
```

# Remark 6.7

+   (A) 6.4 does not specify the step size $\lambda_i$ in line 8. In practice, a step size formula as $\lambda_i = \frac{1}{i}$ for $i \in \mathbb{N}$ is used, or one works with constant step sizes such as 0.1, 0.01, or 0.001. A good choice of $\lambda_i$ actually depends on the optimization problem under consideration.

+   (B) The previous algorithm only works with one gradient $
abla f_k(x^i)$ at an iteration $x^i$ and ignores the other gradients $
abla f_j(x^i)$ for $j \in \{1,\dots,p\}\{k\}$. Because of this lack of information, one cannot assume that the iteration direction used is particularly good. However, one can easily see the descent of the objective function continuous after several iterations.

Next, we could cite by Garrigos and Gover [42] on the convergence rate of the stochastic gradient method under appropriate assumptions.

**Theorem 6.4 (Convergence of the Stochastic Gradient Method [42], Theorem 5.7])** Let the functions $f_1, \dots, f_p : \mathbb{R}^n 	o \mathbb{R}$ with $n, p$ be differentiable, convex, and bounded from below, and let the objective function $f : \mathbb{R}^n 	o \mathbb{R}$ be defined by (6.17). For each $j \in \{1,\dots,p\}$, the gradients $
abla f_j$ be Lipschitz continuous, i.e., there exists Lipschitz constant $L_j > 0$ such that

$$
abla f_j(x) - 
abla f_j(y)\|_2 \le L_j\|x - y\|_2$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$.

The objective function $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex for $\mu > 0$ (Definition 1.25). Furthermore, let $(x^i)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence generated by Algorithm 6.4 with a constant step size $\lambda$ ( $\lambda_i = \lambda$ for all $i \in \mathbb{N}$) satisfying $0 < \lambda < \min\{\frac{1}{\mu}, L_{\max}\}$ with $L_{\max} := \max_{1\le j\le p} L_j$.
