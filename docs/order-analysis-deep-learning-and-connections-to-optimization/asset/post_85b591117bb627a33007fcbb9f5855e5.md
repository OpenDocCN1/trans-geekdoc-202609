## 2.9 Separation Results

By definition, $-C$ representing functionals are qualified for simple separation results.

**Proposition 2.17** ([74, Proposition 3.11]) Let Assumption 2.1 be satisfied, let $\psi$ be a $-C$ representing functional, and let $S$ be a nonempty subset of $Y$. Then

- (a) $S \cap C = \emptyset \iff \psi(-y) > 0 \ \forall y \in S$.
- (b) for an arbitrary $\tilde{y} \in S \cap C$:
$S \cap C = \{\tilde{y}\} \iff \psi(-y) > 0 \ \forall y \in S, y \neq \tilde{y}$.

**Proof**

(a) The assertion is a direct consequence of the definition of the functional because

> $S \cap C = \emptyset \Rightarrow -S \cap (-C) = \emptyset \Rightarrow \psi(y) > 0 \ \forall y \in -S$.

which leads to the assertion.

(b) With the equivalence

> $S \cap C = \{\tilde{y}\} \Leftrightarrow (S\setminus{\tilde{y}}) \cap C = \emptyset$,

the assertion follows from part (a).

**Proposition 2.18** ([74, Proposition 3.12]) Let $C_1$ and $C_2$ be convex cones in a real linear space $Y$ with $C_1, C_2 \neq \{0_Y\}$, and let $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ be $-C_1$ and $-C_2$ representing functionals, respectively.

```
$C_1 \cap C_2 = \{0_Y\} \Leftrightarrow \psi_2(-C_1) > 0 \ \forall c_1 \in C_1\setminus\{0_Y\\}$
\Leftrightarrow \psi_1(-C_2) > 0 \ \forall c_2 \in C_2\setminus\{0_Y\\}$
\Leftrightarrow (y \neq 0_Y, \psi_1(-y) \le 0 \Rightarrow \psi_2(-y) > 0)$
\Leftrightarrow (y \neq 0_Y, \psi_2(-y) \le 0 \Rightarrow \psi_1(-y) > 0$.
```

**Proof** The first and second equivalences follow from Proposition 2.17 (b). The third equivalence can be deduced from the first one because $C_1=\{y \in Y \mid \psi_1(-y) \leq 0\}$ or

$y \in C_1\setminus\{0_Y\} \Leftrightarrow \psi_1(-y) \leq 0 \text{ for } y \neq 0_Y$.

The last equivalence is a result of the second one.

### Section 2.1
Bishop-Phelps cones introduced by Bishop and Phelps [18] (see also the paper [112]). For detailed information about the important class of Bishop-Phelps cones, compare Jahn [68] and Hahn [53]. Lorentz cones (see Example 2.1) play an important role in second-order programming. For literature on second-order programming, see Ben-Tal and Nemirovski [13, 3] and Alizadeh and Goldfar [5]. An overview of translation functions were given by Tammer and Weidner [132] (compare the references). Early results on translation functions were studied by Hamel [56]. The relationships between several scalizing functions were studied by Bouza, Quintana, and Tammer [22]. There are many papers on generalization of scalizing functions and their use in optimization. For instance, a recent paper by Ansari and Sharma [7] studies the properties of generalized function introduced by Xu and Li [142]. And an extension of the Gerstewitz functional and its application in optimization is given by Köbis, Le, and Yao [89], among others.

### Section 2.7
It is obvious for a penalty functional for the negative convex cone that all zeros of this functional equals the negative convex cone. But it is also known from Bishop-Phelps theory that in special the zeros of a Bishop-Phelps functional equal the negative convex cone; a comprehensive study of such special cases was carried out by Ha and Jahn [54]. It was shown in [54, Lemma 2.1] that a Bishop-Phelps cone $C(\ell)$ is given by an equation and only if $\|\ell\|_{Y^*} = 1$. In the norm the continuous linear functional $\ell$ plays an important role in the theory of Bishop-Phelps cones.
