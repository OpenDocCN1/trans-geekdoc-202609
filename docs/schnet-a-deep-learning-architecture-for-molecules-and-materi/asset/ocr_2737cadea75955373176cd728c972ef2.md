SchNet shows significant advantage of the additional force information, reducing energy and force errors by 1-2 orders of magnitude compared to energy only training on the small training set. With 50,000 examples, the improvements are less apparent as the potential energy surface is

already well-sampled at this. On the small training set, SchNet outperforms GDML on the more flexible malonaldehyde and ethanol, while GDM reaches lower errors on the MD trajectories that include all aromatic rings. A potential reason is that GDML defines orders of atoms in the molecule, while the SchNet architecture is invariant to indexing which constitutes a greater advantage in the more flexible molecules.

While GDML is more data-efficient than a neural network, SchNet is scalable to larger datasets. It obtains MAEs of energies and forces of 0.23 kcal/mol and 0.33 kcal/(mol/$	ext{\AA}$), respectively. Remarkably, SchNet performs better using the combined loss with energies and forces over 1000 reference calculations than training on energies of 50,000 examples.
