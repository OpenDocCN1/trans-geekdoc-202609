SchNet shows significant advantage of the additional force information, reducing energy and force errors by 1-2 orders of magnitude compared to energy only training on the small training set. With 50,000 examples, the improvements are less apparent as the potential energy surface is already well-sampled at this. On the small training set, SchNet outperforms GDML on the more flexible malonaldehyde and ethanol, while GDM reaches lower errors on the MD trajectories that include all aromatic rings. A potential reason is that GDML defines orders of atoms in the molecule, while the SchNet architecture is invariant to indexing which constitutes a greater advantage in the more flexible molecules.

While GDML is more data-efficient than a neural network, SchNet is scalable to larger datasets. It obtains MAEs of energies and forces of 0.23 kcal/mol and 0.33 kcal/(mol$\text{\AA}$), respectively. ## E. Application to molecular dynamics of C$_{20}$-furanide

After demonstrating our SchNet on the benchmark set, we performed a method selection using ML-driven MD simulation of C$_{20}$-furanide. This mid-sized molecule has a complex potential field that requires to be described with normal modes and degrees of freedom. Here we use SchNet to perform analysis of basic properties of C$_{20}$ when including quantum effects. The reference data generated by running molecular MD at 300 K in the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) level of theory used the Perdew-Burke$^{39}$ exchange-correlation functional and Zhang-Schffler$^{40}$ method. Further details about the simulations can be found in Appendix B.

By training SchNet on DFT at the PBE+vdW levels, we reduced the simulation time by orders of magnitude from 11 hours using 32 cores to 10 ms using NVIDIA GTX1080. This allows us to perform long simulations with DFT accuracy at low cost, which is making this feasible.

To obtain accurate energy and force predictions, we first perform a method selection on the reference data. We use 20k calculations for the training set, 4k samples for early stopping, and the test report on the remaining data.
