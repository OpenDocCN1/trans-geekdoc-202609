In the case of typical automotive parts, body assembly is carried out through processes such as spot welding after hot stamping. At this time, as the thickness of the residual oxide layer increases, the quality of welding can deteriorate rapidly. Therefore, for the hot stamping of uncoated boron steel, it is necessary to control the atmosphere to minimize the amount of oxygen in the environment during formation. Otherwise, surface oxide layer removal through processes such as shot peening will be required, which increases manufacturing costs and becomes an obstacle to using uncoated boron steel sheets. However, in actual component manufacturing processes, controlling the oxygen level close to a vacuum to suppress the oxide layer is highly challenging.
