
is called *the generalized Fermi information entropy*. For this function, the domain of definition is the *m*-dimensional parallelepiped `𝒩` = [`0`, `G`], i.e., the set of admissible macrostates. If the parameters `a` and `G` satisfy the conditions

$$
\begin{array}{r} {G_{k}a_{k} = \text{const},\text{\quad\quad}N_{k} \ll G_{k},} \\ \end{array}
$$

then `H[F]`(`N`) can be transformed into the entropy function

$$
\begin{array}{r} {H_{B}(\mathbf{N}) = - {\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}N_{k}}\ln\frac{N_{k}}{a_{k}G_{k}e},} \\ \end{array}
$$

which is called *the generalized Boltzmann information entropy*. This function is defined on the nonnegative orthant `𝒩` ∈ *R*[+](*m*), which represents the set of admissible macrostates.

The random distribution of the indistinguishable particles over the subsets in the state space consumes *r* types of resources (energy, time, finances, etc.). The available quantities of these resources are `q[1]`, …, `q[r]`. Resource consumption is characterized by consumption functions `Φ[1](t, N)`, …, `Φ[r](t, N)`, which have parameters `t` (specific consumption rates) and depend on the macrostate vector `N`.

As the distribution process requires resources, it is possible to realize only those macrostates that satisfy the conditions

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{N}) = q_{i},\quad i = \overline{1,s};\text{\quad\quad}\Phi_{i + s}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{N}) = q_{i + s},\quad i = \overline{1,r-s}.} \\ \end{array}
$$

These constraints define the so-called resource set `𝒟`. The intersection of this set with the corresponding sets of admissible macrostates gives *the admissible set*

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{N}{\cap \mathcal{D}}.} \\ \end{array}
$$

Using the above concepts and terminology, we can state *the generalized variational principle* as follows. *The macrostate that is realized in the macrosystem corresponds to the constrained maximum of the generalized information entropy*, i.e.,

$$
\begin{array}{r} \left. H(\mathbf{N})\Rightarrow\max\text{~subject~to~}\,\, \mathbf{N} \in \mathcal{F}. \right. \\ \end{array}
$$

## 5.2 Entropy-Robust Estimation Algorithms for Probability Density Functions

The estimation algorithms of the PDFs form the core of the randomized machine learning procedure. They are oriented towards the corresponding classes of the randomized parametric models, the RPM-Orig and RPM-Rel models. The two classes of randomized models have technical differences. First, some estimation algorithms for the RPM-Orig model will be considered, including the general problem statement and the structure of the resulting solutions. Then these estimation algorithms will be adapted to the RPM-Rel model.

### 5.2.1 Estimation algorithms for RPM-Orig model

According to (5.4, 5.12), the observable output of the RPM-Orig model at a time instant `t[j]` is described by

$$
\begin{aligned} {\mathbf{v}(t_{j}) = \mathbf{y}\lbrack t_{j}} & \left| \, P(\mathbf{a})\rbrack + {\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}\lbrack Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})\rbrack, \right. \\ \left. \mathbf{y}\lbrack t_{j}\, \middle| \, P(\mathbf{a})\rbrack = \mathcal{B}\lbrack\mathbf{x} \right. & \left. ((t_{j},\tau_{l}),\, \middle| \, l = \overline{1,(j-1)}),\mathbf{a}\rbrack, \right. \\ & {j = \overline{1,s}.} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

(5.19)

Here, `ℬ` denotes a matrix functional of dimensions (*m* × *n*) that characterizes an existing relation between the model's output `y(t[j])` and the array composed of the input vectors `x(t[j], τ[1])`, …, `x(t[j], τ[(j − 1)])`. The measurement errors are simulated by the noise vectors `${\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}.$` The RPM-Orig model output (5.2.1) depends on the randomized parameters `a` with a PDF `P(a)` and also on the randomized noise vectors `${\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}$` with a PDF `$Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)});$` see (5.2.1). The available prior information about these PDFs (if any) is represented in the form of prior PDFs `P[0](a)` and `$Q_{j}^{0}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}).$` By assumption, the PDFs belong to the class `ℂ¹` of all continuously differentiable functions.

*For the RPM-Orig model (5.4), the entropy-robust estimation problem of the PDFs of its parameters and measurement noises* is formulated as follows:

* maximize the generalized Boltzmann information entropy functional [[149](#refbib.xhtml#r149)]

$$
\begin{aligned} {\mathcal{H}\lbrack P(\mathbf{a}),\overline{Q}(\xi)\rbrack =} & {- {\int_{\mathcal{A}}P}(\mathbf{a})\,\ln\frac{P(\mathbf{a})}{P_{0}(\mathbf{a})}\, d\,\mathbf{a} -} \\ {- {\sum\limits_{j = 1}^{s}\,}{\int_{\Xi_{j}}Q_{j}}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})} & \left. \ln\frac{Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})}{Q_{j}^{0}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})}d{\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}\Rightarrow\min_{P,Q_{1},...,Q_{s}} \right. \\ \end{aligned}
$$

(5.20)

* subject to:

  * –the normalization conditions for the PDFs, i.e.,

$$
\begin{array}{r} {{\int_{\mathcal{A}}P}(\mathbf{a})d\mathbf{a} = 1,\quad{\int_{\Xi_{j}}Q_{j}}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})\,\ d{\overline{\xi}}^{(j)} = 1,\quad j = \overline{1,s},} \\ \end{array}
$$

(5.21)

and

  * –the balance constraints for the *k*-means of the output of the RPM-Orig model (5.14) and the measurements of the object's output, i.e.,

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\mathbf{m}^{(k)}\lbrack P(\mathbf{a}),Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}),j\rbrack = \mathbf{y}(t_{j}),\quad j = \overline{1,s}.} \\ \end{array}
$$

(5.22)

Due to (5.13), the vector `$\mathbf{m}^{(k)}\lbrack P(\mathbf{a}),Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}),j\rbrack$` in these equality constraints has the components

$$
\begin{aligned} & {m_{i}^{(k)}\lbrack j\rbrack = \left\lbrack {\int_{\mathcal{A}}P}(\mathbf{a})y_{i}^{k}\lbrack t_{j}\, \middle| \, P(\mathbf{a})\rbrack\, d\mathbf{a} \right\rbrack^{1/k} +} \\ & {\text{\quad\quad} + \left\lbrack {{\int_{\Xi_{j}}\,}Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})\,{\lbrack\xi_{i}^{(j)}\rbrack}^{k}\, d\xi_{i}^{(j)}} \right\rbrack^{1/k},} \\ & {\quad\text{\quad\quad}\text{\quad\quad}i = \overline{1,m},\, j = \overline{1,s}.} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

(5.23)

Consider the case in which the balance constraints involve the 1-mean of the RPM-Orig model output:

$$
\begin{aligned} \left\lbrack {\int_{\mathcal{A}}P}(\mathbf{a})\mathbf{y}\lbrack t_{j}\, \middle| \, P(\mathbf{a})\rbrack\, d\mathbf{a} \right\rbrack & {+ \left\lbrack {{\int_{\Xi_{j}}\,}Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})\,{\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}\, d{\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}} \right\rbrack = \mathbf{y}(t_{j}),} \\ & { \quad j = \overline{1,s}.} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

(5.24)

In this optimization problem, the functional constraints extract the subset `$\Omega\lbrack P(\mathbf{a}),Q(\overline{\xi})\rbrack$` of all admissible functions, where `$Q(\overline{\xi}) = \left\{ Q_{1}({\overline{\xi}}^{(1)}),\ldots,Q_{s}({\overline{\xi}}^{(s)}) \right\},$` from the class `ℂ¹`. In fact, the problem (5.2.1)–(5.24) is a functional entropy-linear programming problem [149]. It belongs to the class of the Lyapunov-type optimization problems [77, [174](#refbib.xhtml#r174)], in which the admissible set of functions is described by a system of linear integral functionals.

*For the RPM-Orig model, the entropy-robust RML algorithm* has the form

$$
\begin{array}{r} {\left\lbrack {P(\mathbf{a}),Q(\overline{\xi})} \right\rbrack^{\*} = \arg\max_{\lbrack P(\mathbf{a}),Q(\overline{\xi})\rbrack}\left\{ \mathcal{H},\, \middle| \,\lbrack\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{a}),\mathcal{Q}(\overline{\xi})\rbrack \in \Omega \right\},} \\ \end{array}
$$
