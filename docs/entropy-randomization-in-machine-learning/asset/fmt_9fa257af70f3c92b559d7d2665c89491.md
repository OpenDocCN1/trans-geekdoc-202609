The probabilistic properties of the randomized original parameters are described by the PDF *P*(**a**). The models of this class will be referred to as the *RPM-Orig* models.

$$
\begin{array}{r}

\end{array}
$$

(5.2)

\begin{array}{r}

\end{array}

The probabilistic properties of the randomized relative parameters are characterized in terms of the PDF *W*(**u**). The models of this class will be mentioned as the *RPM-Rel* models.

## 3. Measurement error generator (MEG)

is intended to simulate the measurement errors in output data. This device produces the ensembles of random vectors with certain probabilistic properties. It consists of a vector randomizer (*VR*) with a standard PDF and a converter (*C*) of these vectors into the ones with the given PDFs. The standard PDF is the density of the random vectors that have the uniform distribution on the unit nonnegative *m*\-dimensional cube. The converter transforms these vectors into the random vectors with a PDF that is defined by the machine learning of the model.

Thus, the MEG outputs the random noise vectors $\overline{\xi}(t_{j}) = {\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}, \,(j = \overline{1,s}\,),$ where ${\overline{\xi}}^{(j)} \in R^{m}.$ In the same way, describe the noise arrays that appear as the result of data accumulation using a matrix $\left. \Xi = \lbrack\,\xi_{ij}\, \middle| \, i = \overline{1,m};\, j = \overline{1,s}\,\rbrack. \right.$

The elements of the noise vectors are assumed to be independent and belonging to the intervals

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\overline{\xi}}^{(j)} & {\in \mathcal{K}_{j} = \lbrack{\overline{\xi}}_{-}^{(j)},{\overline{\xi}}_{+}^{(j)}\rbrack,\quad j = \overline{1,s},}
\\
\Xi & {\in \mathcal{K} = \otimes_{j = 1}^{s}\mathcal{K}_{j}.}
\\
\end{aligned}
$$

(5.7)

It is often useful to introduce the relative nonnegative vectors ${\overline{\eta}}^{(j)}$ that take values within the unit nonnegative *m*\-dimensional cube ℰ[*j*] = \[**0**, **1**\]. In addition, define the auxiliary variable matrix $\left. E = \lbrack\,\eta_{ij}\, \middle| \, i = \overline{1,m};\, j = \overline{1,s}\,\rbrack. \right.$ The transformation of the random vectors ${\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}$ into the vectors *η*^((*j*)) runs as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{{\overline{\xi}}^{(j)} = {\overline{\xi}}_{-}^{(j)} +} & {K_{j}\,{{\overline{\eta}}^{(j)}},\quad K_{j} = \text{diag}\,\lbrack\xi_{i, +}^{(j)} - \xi_{i, -}^{(j)}\rbrack,}
\\
& {\, i = \overline{1,m};j = \overline{1,s}.}
\\
\end{aligned}
$$

(5.8)

The probabilistic properties of the noises are characterized by the PDF $Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})$ defined on 𝒦[*j*] (5.7) or by the PDF $G_{j}({\overline{\eta}}^{(j)})$ defined on the unit cube ℰ. If these density functions are specified, then the MEG produces the ensembles of random vectors that simulate

* the original noises

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathcal{Q}_{j}\lbrack Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})\rbrack = \left\{ {\overline{\xi}}^{(j)}\lbrack Q_{j}({\overline{\xi}}^{(j)})\rbrack \right\},\text{\quad\quad}j = \overline{1,s};}
\\
\end{array}
$$

(5.9)

* the nonnegative relative noises

$$
\begin{array}{r}
{\mathcal{G}_{j}\lbrack G_{j}({\overline{\eta}}^{(j)})\rbrack = \left\{ {\overline{\eta}}^{(j)}\lbrack G_{j}({\overline{\eta}}^{(j)})\rbrack \right\},\text{\quad\quad}j = \overline{1,s}.}
\\
\end{array}
$$

(5.10)
