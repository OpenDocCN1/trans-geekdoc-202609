$(\varepsilon^0 + \Delta_{im} - \varepsilon)a_{im} + \sum_{j\neq i} t_{im}^{im'}a_{m'} = 0 \quad [9]$

where

$$\Delta_{im} = \langle im | \sum_{j\neq i} V_{ij} | im \rangle = \sum_{j\neq i} \phi_{im}(\vec{r}-\vec{R}_i)V_{ij}(\vec{r}-\vec{R}_j)\phi_{im}(\vec{r}-\vec{R}_i)d^3r \quad [10]$

$t_{im}^{im'} = \langle im|V_{i'}|im' \rangle = \int \phi_{im}^*(\\vec{r}-\vec{R}_i)V_{ii'}(\vec{r}-\vec{R}_{i'})\phi_{im'}(\vec{r}-\vec{R}_{i'})d^3r \quad [11]$

The $\Delta_{im}$ integrals shift the energy of the reference atomic levels $\varepsilon^0$, and $t_{im}^{im'}$ integrals mix into molecular states. From the set of Eqs. [9], one arrives at a $5N \times 5N$ determinant from which the electronic levels of the clusters can be obtained.$^{12}$ The $5N$ atomic $d$ states $5N$ electronic densities distributed between the two extremes $\varepsilon_a$ and $\varepsilon_b$. The lowest level, with energy $\varepsilon_b$, corresponds to the form of $d-d$ bonds between most pairs of atoms. In going from $\varepsilon_b$ to $\varepsilon_a$, the number of antibonds increases. At $\varepsilon_a$, the electron density decreases along bonds between most pairs of atoms (in antibonding states, densities of the free atoms). The energy broadening can be viewed as resulting from a resonance between the atomic $d$ levels. From experience acquired in metals, it is known that the $d$ band width $W = \varepsilon_a - \varepsilon_b$ is larger than the shift $S$. The shift allows the electrons to hop from atom to atom through the cluster (or through the lattice in the solid). The shift is the energy difference between the atomic level $\varepsilon^0$ and the average band level $(\varepsilon_a+\varepsilon_b)/2$. Typical values in metals are $W=5-10$ eV and $S=1-2$ eV.

Atomistic simulations usually require the calculation of the total energy of the system. The band energy is evaluated by integrating the density of electronic states $D(\varepsilon)$

$E_{band} = \int^{\varepsilon} \varepsilon D(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon \quad [12]$

The part of the energy not included in $E_{band}$ can be modeled by pairwise repulsive interactions

$E_{rep} = \sum_{i\neq j} U_{ij} \quad [13]$
