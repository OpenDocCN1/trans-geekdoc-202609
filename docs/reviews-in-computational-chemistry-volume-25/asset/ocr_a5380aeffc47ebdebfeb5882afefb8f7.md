An alternative approach involves integrating out the degrees of freedom located above the top layer in the simulation.^76 The elimination of the degrees of freedom can be done within the context of the Kubo theory, or more precisely the Zwanzig formalism, which leads to effective (potentially time-dependent) interactions between the atoms in the top layer.^77-80 These effective interactions include those mediated by the degrees of freedom For periodic solids, a description in reciprocal space decouples with wave vector q as far as the static properties are concerned. This description in turn implies that the computational effort also remains in the order of L⁻¹ in L, provided that use is made of the Fast Fourier for the transformation between real and reciprocal space. The description is exact for purely harmonic solids, so that one can mimic the static contact mechanics between a purely elastic lattice and a substrate with one single layer only.^81

The possibility exists of including dynamical effects (plus random forces at finite temperatures).^77-80 However, it may not be advisable to take advantage of this possibility, as the simulation would become increasingly slow with increasing number of time steps. The simulation will slow considerably in higher dimensions because of the nonorthogonality of the dynamical coupling in reciprocal space.

To be specific let $u_{qiq}(t)$ denote the α component of the displacement field with wave vector q and eigenmode i at time t. In the absence of external forces, which can simply be added to the equation, the equation of motion for the coordinates that are not thermodynamically explicitly $\hat{u}_{qiq}(t)$ would read as:

```
$$\hat{u}_{qiq}(t) = -G_{qiq}u_{qiq}(t) + \int_{-\infty}^{t} dt' \sum_{q'=1}^{3} \gamma^{q'iq}(t-t')\hat{u}_{q'iq}(t') + \Gamma^{q'iq}(t')$ [37]$
```

where $G_{qi}$ are the (static) Green's functions, or effective spring constants, associated with eigenmode i and wavelength $\lambda$. The knowledge of these functions enables us to work out the static contact mechanics. The time-dependent-damping coefficients $\gamma^{q'iq}(t-t')$ in Eq. [37] reflect the dynamical coupling between various eigenmodes. No reason exists why this coupling should be diagonal in any of its indices, and thus the terms related to dynamics increases memory requirements and slows down the speed of the calculation tremendously, i.e., beyond the expense of approximating a semi-infinite solid by a discrete, elastic lattice of size $L^3$. Included in Eq. [37] are random forces $\Gamma^{q'iq}(t')$, which must be used at finite temperature to counterbalance the time-dependent damping term. The random and damping terms have to be chosen such that satisfy the fluctuation-dissipation theorem.^39
