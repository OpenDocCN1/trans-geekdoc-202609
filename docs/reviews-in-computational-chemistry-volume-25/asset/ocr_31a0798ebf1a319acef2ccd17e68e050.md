In summary, the works of Guirado-López et al.⁶⁹ and of Wan et al.⁴⁸ have shown the importance of the orbital contribution to the magnetic moment of nikel clusters. The TB method provides a convenient framework to understand the variation of µ(NiX) with cluster size, and this understanding is good, although not perfect. In fact, the work of Andriot and Men⁷⁷ formulated in the TB framework, while supporting the idea that the enhanced orbital moments are aligned parallel to the spins in Ni clusters, also raises the possibility that these states are energetically unfavourable. This may be attributed to the interplay between the action of the spin-orbit interaction HSO, which favors the alignment of L along the direction of S, and the action of the crystal field, which tends to align L along the easy magnetization axis (in the magnetization is easier along a particular direction of the crystal). # Chromium and Iron Clusters

Chromium is an antiferromagnetic metal in the bulk phase, and the calculations of Cheng and Wang⁷⁵ show that the Cr clusters also have a strong tendency toward antiferromagnetic spin ordering (although the fact that the number of atoms makes the distribution of the magnetic moments more complex). Small Cr clusters are “special” compared with the other 3d metals.⁷⁵⁷⁶ The electronic structure of the atom 3d⁴¹ has unpaired electrons. This half-filled electronic configuration leads to d–d bonding in Cr₂, with a bond length of 1.68 Å, which is very short compared with the interatomic distance of 2.50 Å in Cr metal. The dimer is a closed shell molecule with a strong sextuple bond.⁷⁵ The strong binding arises from the filling of the 3d-bonding molecular orbitals: σ₄π₄δ₀σ⁶(¹Σ⁺). The electronic structure of the dimer is robust and controls the growth of the small clusters.
