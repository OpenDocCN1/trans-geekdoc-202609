The crystal potential stabilizes the d and s states by different amounts, because of their different degree of localization. This process, plus partial hybridization between the s and d shells, leads to charge transfer from s to d states, and number of s electrons for clusters and metals is close to 1 per atom. Assuming that the d orbitals are atomic-like, Hund's rule requires the majority 3d up sub-band to be fully occupied with five electrons per atom, the minority 3d down sub-band has to be occupied with two, three, and four electrons per atom in Fe, Co, and Ni, respectively. The difference, in the direction of spin up and spin 3d electrons per atom is $n_{\uparrow}(1)=3, n_{\downarrow}(1)=2$, 1 for Fe, Co, and Ni, respectively, and the magnetic moments per atom are $\mu_b(Fe)=3\mu_b, \mu_b(Co)=2\mu_b, \mu_b(Ni)=1\mu_b$. These simple estimates are close to the actual moments of very small clusters. In comparison, the magnetic moments of the bulk metals, $\mu(Fe)=2.2\mu_b, \mu(Co)=1.7\mu_b$ and $\mu(Ni)=0.64\mu_b$, and their noninteger values are caused by the partial delocalization of the 3d electrons. The exchange interaction between these delocalized electrons (known as itinerant exchange) also contributes to the mutual alignment of the atomic moments.

Experiments have been performed to reveal how the magnetic moments evolve as the number of atoms in the cluster increases. That evolution is much smaller and has unexpected effects. The clusters in a molecular beam are free from any interaction with a matrix. So it is possible to measure their intrinsic magnetic properties. The magnetic moment can be determined by an experimental technique similar to that used by Stern and Gerlach to demonstrate the quantization of the components of the angular momentum in the early days of quantum theory. In this, experimental can investigate the dependence of the cluster's magnetic moment as a function of the cluster size. The clusters interact with an inhomogeneous magnetic field $B$ and are deflected from their original trajectory. The deflection $l$ of a cluster moving with a velocity $v$ in a direction perpendicular to the field gradient (defined as the z direction) is given by

```
$l = K\frac{M(B)\partial B}{\partial z}}{mv^2} [1]$
```

where $m$ is the cluster mass, $\partial B/partial z$ is the gradient of the magnetic field, and $K$ is a constant that depends on the geometry of the apparatus. This equation shows that the deflection is proportional to the cluster magnetization $M(B)$, which is the time-averaged projection of the magnetic moment $\mu$ of the particle along the field gradient direction.
