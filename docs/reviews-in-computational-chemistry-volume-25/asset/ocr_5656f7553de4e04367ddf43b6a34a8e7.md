The quantities $b_n(q)$ are the scattering factors of the different scatterers in the sample. For X-ray scattering, momentum transfer $q$ dependence of the form factor of the electronic clouds must be taken into account. For neutron scattering, the form factors reduce to $q$-independent scattering lengths, $b_n(q)=b_n$. Neutron scattering studies of the melt structure are typically performed on deuterated samples, i.e., where all H atoms have replaced by D atoms, for deuterium and carbon atoms, coherent scattering domains (they have the same coherent scattering lengths, whereas hydrogen atoms scatter incoherently. X-ray scattering is most sensitive to the positional correlations of the heavy atoms in the sample with their associated electron clouds.

Performing neutron scattering on deuterated chain in a protracted matrix (or vice versa; both provide the same contrast) gives the single-chain structure factor,

```
$S_{ch}(q) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n,m}^N b_n b_m \langle e^{iq\cdot(\hat{r}_n-\hat{r}_m)} \rangle \quad [60]
```

where now the sum runs only over all monomers of a single chain.

When we think of situations involving bead-spring models, all scatterers can be assigned the same scattering lengths [that are absorbed into arbitrary units for $S(q)$], and for united atom like the one used for PB, we can consider scattering from the united atoms in the same way. This simplifies the scattering functions of Eqs. [59] and [60] to be

```
$S_m(q) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{n,m}^M \langle e^{iq\cdot(\hat{r}_n-\hat{r}_m)} \rangle \quad [61]
```

and

```
$S_{ch}(q) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n,m}^N \langle e^{iq\cdot(\hat{r}_n-\hat{r}_m)} \rangle \quad [62]
```

The structure factors are Fourier transforms of radial pair-distribution functions for the complete melt or the single chain, respectively,

```
$S_m(q) = 1 + 4\pi \int_0^{\infty} r^2 g_m(r) \frac{\sin(qr)}{qr} dr \quad [63]
```

```
$S_{ch}(q) = 1 + 4\pi(N-1) \int_0^{\infty} r^2 g_{ch}(r) \frac{\sin(qr)}{qr} dr \quad [64]
```
