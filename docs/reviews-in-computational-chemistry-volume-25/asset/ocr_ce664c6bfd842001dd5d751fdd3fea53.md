However, both methods make approximations in the treatment of the electronic correlations, and because of those approximations, there are conflicting cases that resist a conclusive analysis; the magnetic ordering in Mn2 is a good example.

The well-known ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic orderings of many materials in the bulk phase become more complex in clusters. For instance, for a material with a tendency to antiferromagnetic ordering of the atomic spins, a perfect antiferromagnetic configuration is not possible in a triangle with the geometry of a triangle in the same direction. This simple example of frustration is induced by the finite number of atoms of the system. This type of frustration occurs in many clusters with a tendency to antiferromagnetic ordering. Sometimes the magnetic frustrations cost a sizable amount of energy and the magnetic moments reread pointing towards different directions in space to reduce the cluster energy; this is called a noncollinear configuration. Current improvements in the theoretical tools allows one to study noncollinear magnetic ordering in clusters, and this is one of the recent trends in the literature. It is expected that the results of some previous calculations and the interpretation of some experiments will have to be revised in light of possible nonlinear arrangements. Many experiments, so far, have been interpreted by taking into account the spin magnetism only. However, recent work points out the importance of orbital magnetism and of the spin coupling. A good example is the deep insight into evolution of the magnetic moment of nickel clusters or the cluster size obtained by taking into account the effects of orbital magnetism.48 However, the general relevance of this effect is not yet assessed and more work is required.

To summarize, one can note that the magnetic characteristics of small clusters of the transition metals vary in nonmonotonous way as a function of the number of atoms in the cluster. This nonscalable behavior is what makes small clusters interesting and complex at the same time, offering possibilities for future technological applications.
