# Using a GA in Combination with Another Technique

GAs are often combined with other AI or non-AI methods, with the role of the GA being to find some optimum set of parameters. Gributs and Burns⁵ used a GA to select a set of wavelengths or wavelengths⁶ would provide a parsimonious estimate of the properties of interest in a study of NIR spectra, Damascus and Saraf⁷ combined a GA with a neural network to predict the product mix from crude distillation unit of a petroleum refinery. Adjustable parameters determine the structure and performance of neural networks,⁸ so it is natural to consider using a GA to select the best neural network geometry and minimize development time.

# Biomedical Applications

Biomedical spectra are extremely complex. Hyphenated techniques such as MS–MS can generate databases that contain hundreds of thousands or millions of data points. Reduction of dimensionality is a common step preceding data analysis because of the computational overheads associated with manipulating large datasets⁹ To verify the large datasets provided by biomedical spectra, some form of feature selection¹⁰ is almost essential. In sparse data, many combinations of attributes may separate the samples, but not every combination is plausible.

Prankeviciane et al.¹¹ have assessed the NMR spectra of pathogenic fungi and human biofluids, finding the spectral signature that comprises a set of attributes to uniquely identify and characterize the sample. This use of GAs effectively reduces the dimensionality of the data, and can speed up later processing as well as make it more reliable.
