As pointed out in the previous section, the Chebyshev operator can be viewed as a cosine propagator. By analogy, the energy wave function and the spectrum can also be obtained using a spectral method. More specifically, the spectral density operator can be defined in terms of the conjugate Chebyshev angle $(\theta)$^{:128,132}$

```
$$\delta(EI - \mathbf{H}) = \frac{1}{\pi \sin \theta} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (2 - \delta_{k0}) \cos kE \cos k\theta \n= \frac{1}{\pi \sqrt{1 - E^2}} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (2 - \delta_{k0})T_k(E)T_k(H) \quad [62]$
```

where the angles in the first equation map energy and Hamiltonian, respectively, defined as $\mathbf{q}_0$, one can expect to obtain a "filtered" vector near $E$: Applying the delta operator onto an arbitrary initial vector $\mathbf{q}_0$:

```
$$\eta(E) \equiv \delta(EI - \mathbf{H})\mathbf{q}_0 = \frac{1}{\pi \sin \theta} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (2 - \delta_{k0}) \cos k\theta\mathbf{q}_k \quad [63]$
```

where the Chebyshev vectors $\mathbf{q}_k$ are generated using Eq. [56]. In other words, the energy domain vector is a cosine Fourier transform of the Chebyshev vectors. Compared with time propagation, the Chebyshev recursion is much more efficient because it can be carried out exactly using Eq. [56], whereas the time propagator has to be approximated.

A desirable feature of Eq.[63] is that one can scan the energy axis looking for eigenvectors in spectral regions of interest. In the limit where a sufficiently large number of terms in Eq. [63] is included, $\eta(E)$ will be the (normalized) eigenvector when the energy variable $(E)$ hits an eigenvalue $(E)$. All eigenvectors can be obtained from the same set of Chebyshev vectors because the Chebyshev vectors are energy global, i.e., independent of $E$ or $0$. Thus, all information about the energy is contained in the expansion coefficients. In reality, the infinite sum in Eq. [63] is always true and $\eta(E)$ will only approximate an eigenvector when the width of the truncated delta filter is narrower than the spectral density of the region.
