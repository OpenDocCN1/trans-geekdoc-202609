Among the advantages of DPD over Langevin dynamics are conservation of momentum and the ability to properly describe hydrodynamic interactions with longer wavelengths,${47,48}$ ensures that “macroscopic” properties are less affected by DPD than with Langevin dynamics. To illustrate this point, it is instructive to study the effect that DPD and Langevin thermostats have on a one-dimensional, linear harmonic chain with nearest-neighbor coupling, which is the simplest model to study long vibrations.

The Langevin function $L$ of harmonic chain without thermostats is by

```
$L = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{m}{2}\dot{x}_i^2 - k(x_i - x_{i-1} - a)^{2} \quad [19]$
```

where $a$ is the lattice constant and $k$ is the stiffness of the springs. Periodic boundary conditions are employed after a distance $Na$. The equations of motion at zero temperature with damping are

```
$m\ddot{x}_i + \gamma\dot{x}_i = -k(2x_i - x_{i+1} - x_{i-1})$ (Langevin) \quad [20]$
$m\ddot{x}_i + \gamma(2\dot{x}_i - \dot{x}_{i+1} - \dot{x}_{i-1}) = -k(2x_i - x_{i+1} - x_{i-1})$ (DPD) \quad [21]$
```

As usual, it is possible to diagonalize the equations of motion by transforming them into reciprocal space. The equations of motion of the Fourier transforms $\tilde{x}(q, \omega)$ then read as follows:

```
$-m\omega^2\tilde{x} + i\omega\gamma\tilde{x} + 4\sin^2(qa/2)k\tilde{x} = 0$ (Langevin) \quad [22]$
$-m\omega^2\tilde{x} + 4\sin^2(qa/2)i\omega\gamma\tilde{x} = 0$ (DPD) \quad [23]$
```

Thus, although Langevin and DPD do not alter the frequencies of the chain, i.e., their $q$ dependence is $\omega_0(q)=\sqrt{4\sin^2(qa/2)/m}$, the quality factor $Q$, defined as the ratio of the frequency to the damping, differs between the two methods, as shown in Eq. [24]

```
$Q(q) = \frac{\omega_0(q)}{\gamma}$ (Langevin)
$Q(q) = \frac{\omega_0(q)}{\gamma} 4\sin^2(qa/2)$ (DPD) \quad [24]$
```

In the long wavelength limit where $q \rightarrow 0$, Langevin dynamics will always be overdamped, whereas DPD dynamics will be underdamped, provided that the system is not intrinsically underdamped, as would be the case in the vicinity of a continuous phase transition. Although this example was based on calculations of a linear harmonic chain, the results suggest that DPD has little effect on dynamical quantities that couple to long wavelengths. The bulk viscosity of a system depends on the precise choice of $\gamma$ only in a negligible manner as long as $\gamma$ is reasonably small.$^{48}$

In some cases, it may be beneficial to work with Langevin wavelength modes modes equilibrate notoriously slowly. Consider the case in which an elastic solid is pressed onto a fractal surface. Because the DPD thermostat barely damps long-range oscillations, we must expect a lot of bouncing before the center-of-mass of the top finally comes to rest. Conversely, Langevin dynamics can lead to faster convergence because the thermostat couples more to long-wavelength oscillations, as shown in Figure 11. When the Langevin thermostat is used, the system quickly reaches its mechanical position, whereas the DPD-thermostatted system is strongly underdamped, despite fact that the damping coefficient was 10 times larger for DPD than for Langevin.
