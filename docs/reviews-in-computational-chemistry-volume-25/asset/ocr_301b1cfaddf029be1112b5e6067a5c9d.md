The fifth term in the Hamiltonian of Eq. [57] is added to account for the electronic spillover at the surface.$^{21}$ One extra orbital with symmetry ($s'$ orbitals) is added to each surface $f$ and located in the vacuum region near the atom. This $s'$ orbital interacts with the $s$ orbital of the same surface atom through the hopping integral $t'_f$ and the occupation of the $s'$ orbitals represents the spill. The hopping integral is parameterized such that $t'_f(Z_i) = V_{sc}\sqrt{Z_{max}-Z_i}$, where $Z_{max}-Z_i$ is the local defect in the atomic coordination ($Z_{max}$); 12 for a fcc solid and $V_{sc}$ is the hopping strength. The last term in Eq. [57], which accounts for the orbital shifts from interlayer Coulomb interactions, is related to the last two terms in Eq. [16].$^{48}$ restricted the orbital shifts to the surface atoms $f$.

The eigenvalue equation corresponding to the Hamiltonian of Eq. [57] can be solved by an iterative procedure for any orientation of the spin magnetization (identified as the zz direction). The self-consistent density matrix is then employed to calculate the local spin and orbital magnetic moments. For instance, the local orbital moments at different atoms are determined from

```
$$\mu_{i,orb} = \sum_{\sigma} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} mn_{im\sigma}(\sigma)d\epsilon \quad\quad [66]$
```

where $m$ refers to the quantum number. The spin magnetic moment $\hat{\mu}_{spin}$ and the orbital magnetic moment $\hat{\mu}_{orb}$ of the cluster are calculated as a vector average of the atomic moments. The total magnetic moment $\hat{\mu}$ is obtained as the vector sum of $\hat{\mu}_{spin}$ and $\hat{\mu}_{orb}$. The self-consistent solution of the Hamiltonian generates a non-uniform and non-collinear distribution of spin and orbital moments; however, it was found by Wan et al. that nonlocality is very weak in this case. Also, the spin-orbit interaction can generate anisotropy, by a comparison of clusters with spin along the three principal axes revealed small energy differences of less than 0.005 eV/atom. The results reported immediately before correspond to spin orientation along the largest inertia axis.
