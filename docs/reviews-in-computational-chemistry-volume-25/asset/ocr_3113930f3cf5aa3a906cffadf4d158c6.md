# 164 The Reactivity of Energetic Materials at Extreme Conditions

One of the most difficult parts of this problem is describing the EOS of the fluid components accurately; Because of its simplicity, the Becker-Kistiakowski-Wilson (BKW)^21 EOS is used in many applications involving energetic materials. Numerous parameter sets have been proposed for the BKW EOS,22–25 Koury and Sours have critically reviewed these sets by comparing their predictions to a database of detonation tests. They concluded that the BKW EOS does not model the detonation of a copper-lined charge adequately. The BKWC parameter set^26 confirms this partially through multivariate parameterization techniques. However, the BKWC parameter set is not reliable when applied to explosives that are very high in hydrogen content.

It has long recognized that the validity of the BKW EOS is questionable.^12 This is particularly important when designing new materials that may have unusual elemental compositions. Efforts to develop better EOSs have been largely based on the concept of potential models. With potential models, molecules interact via idealized spherical particles. Statistical mechanics is employed to calculate the EOS of the interacting mixture of effective particles. Most often, the exponential-6 (exp-6) potential is used for the pair interactions:

```
$V(r) = -rac{\varepsilon}{\alpha-6} \exp\left[-\alpha\left(\rac{r}{r_m}\right)^m\right] \quad [1]$
```

Here, r is the distance between particles, r_m is the minimum of the potential well, ε is the well depth, and α is the softness of the potential well. The Jacobs-Coerwthwaite-Zwissler (JCZ3) EOS was the first successful model based on a pair potential that was applied to detonation.^27 This EOS was based on fitting Monte Carlo simulation data to an analytic functional form. Ross, Rees and others successfully applied a soft-sphere EOS based on perturbation theory to detonation and shock problems.^10,28–30 Computational cost is a significant difficulty with an EOS based on fluid perturbation theory. Byers^31 developed an analytic representation of the Kang et al. EOSs using Chebyshev polynomials. The accuracy of the Byers Brown EOS has been evaluated by Charlet et al.,^12 these authors concluded that Ross's approach is the closest. Fried and Howard^32 have used a combination to generate highly accurate EOS for the exp-6 fluid.

The exp-6 model is not well suited to molecules with large dipole moments. To account for this, Rees^39 used a temperature-dependent well depth c(T) in the exp-6 potential to model polar fluids and phase separations. Fried and Howard have developed an effective cluster model for HF.^33 The effective cluster model is valid for temperatures lower than the variable well-depth model, but it employs two more adjustable parameters than does the latter.
