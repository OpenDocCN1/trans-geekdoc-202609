The second important innovation is that an efficient preconditioner ((EI− H₀)⁻¹) is used to accelerate the convergence of the QMR solution of the linear equation. An attractive characteristic of PIST is its weak dependence on the spectral range of the Hamiltonian,⁸² which allows one to focus on a particular spectral window of interest. Recall that the convergence rate of the original Lanczos algorithm is inversely proportional to the square root of the spectral range,⁴¹²,⁴² which may result in numerical inefficiency for problems with large spectral ranges.

The key element of PIST is the judicious choice of H₀, which should be sufficiently close to the true Hamiltonian and easily to construct the preconditioner. Several publications by Poirier and Carrington were devoted to the choice of H₀¹. These authors observed that with a good choice of H₀ the number of vector-matrix multiplications in the converging eigenpairs can be substantially less than what is needed by the Lanczos algorithm. We point out that the design of the zeroth-order Hamiltonian may be system dependent and requires intimate knowledge of the structure of the Hamiltonian.

# Transition Amplitudes

In chemical physics, knowing of the eigenpairs of the relevant Hamiltonian is often insufficient because many processes transition between states. In such cases, the transition amplitudes between these states under a quantum mechanical propagator may required to solve the problem:

```
$C_{mm'} = \langle\chi_m|U(\mathbf{H})|\chi_{m'}\rangle = \chi_{m'}^T U(\mathbf{H})\chi_m$ [39]
```

U(H) is a function of the Hamiltonian as the time propagator and $|\chi_m\rangle$ are prespecified states. Their examples include absorption/emission spectra, Raman cross sections, correlation functions, rates, and S-matrix elements for reactive scattering.¹³⁶ A special case of Eq. [39] is recognized for transitions between molecular quantum states caused by interaction with an external source, which can be another molecule during a collision event or an electromagnetic field in a laser-molecule interaction. Under such circumstances, the total Hamiltonian is the sum of the molecular Hamiltonian H₀ and its interaction with the external V:

```
$H = H_0 + V$ [40]
```

Here, $\chi_m$ are eigenvectors of the molecular Hamiltonian: $H_0\chi_m = E_m^0\chi_m$.
