DFT studies of the adsorption of $NH_3$ on $Ni_N$ clusters with $N=1$–$4$ also indicate that the adsorbed molecules have a significant effect on the magnetism: a decrease of the Ni moments is predicted, which is completely quenched when the number of $NH_3$ molecules equals the number of Ni atoms.$^{128}$ The nitrogen atom binds directly to a Ni atom, and the quenching of the magnetic moment of Ni is from the short distance between the Ni and N atoms in Ni, the Ni-N bond. When the number of molecules is larger than the number of Ni atoms, the Ni-N bonds become stretched because of steric hindrance. Once Ni–N distances exceed the critical distance of 3.59 a.u., magnetism appears.

Adsorbed species can also increase the magnetic moments of ferromagnetic clusters. The magnetic moments of free and hydrogenated iron clusters are quenched by Knucklebein$^{129}$ are shown in Figure 14. The Fe clusters become saturated with a layer of dissociatively chemisorbed hydrogen under the conditions of the experiment. For most clusters sized, the $Fe_NNH_m$ clusters have larger magnetic moments than the corresponding pure $Fe$ clusters, and the enhancement is particularly large between $N=13$ and $N=18$. This result contrasts with analogous studies for Ni clusters; in this case, quenching of the magnetic moments is observed after generation.$^{129}$

## DETERMINATION OF MAGNETIC MOMENTS BY COMBINING THEORY AND PHOTODETACHMENT SPECTROSCOPY

The measurement of the magnetic moment of very small clusters is not simple. In such cases, the total magnetic moment is also small and the deflection in the magnetic field may lie within the error of the experiment. Motivated by this difficulty, an alternative method to determine the magnetic moments has been proposed by Khanna and Jena$^{130}$ based on combining calculations for the neutral and negatively charged (ionic) species, $X_N$ and $X_A$, respectively, with electron photodetachment experiments for the anionic cluster. Let us consider a ferromagnetic anionic cluster that has $n$ unpaired spins, and, thus, a magnetic moment $\mu_B$ and multiplicity $M=n+1$. When an electron is detected from the anion, the neutral cluster has a multiplicity of $M+1$ if the electron is removed from the minority band, or $M-1$ if the electron is removed from the majority band. The measured photoelectron energy peaks can be compared with theoretical calculations where one firstly determines the ground state of the anion, including its spin multiplicity $M$, and the energy for the transition to the neutral species with multiplicities $M+1$ and $M-1$ at the anion geometry. Quantitative agreement between the calculated energies and the observed spectral peaks indicates that the calculated multiplicity must be correct$^{130}$.
