We determined the free energy barrier for dissociation by defining a free energy surface for the oxygen-hydrogen distances, viz. W(r) = -k_BT In [g(R_{OH})], where W(r) is the free energy surface (potential of mean force). The results are shown in Figure 11.

The free energy barrier can then be defined as the difference between the first minimum and the second maximum in the free energy surface. The free energy barrier is 11 kcal/mol at 34 GPa and 8 kcal/mol at 115 GPa. The remainder of the results discussed below are for the "S" simulations.

We now analyze the chemical species prevalent in water at these extreme conditions by defining instantaneous species on the O-H bond direction. If distance is not a cut-off value r_c we count the atom pair as being bonded. Determining all bonds in the system gives chemical species at each point in time. Species with lifetimes less than an O-H vibrational period (10 fs) are "transient" and do not represent bonded molecules. The optimal cut-off r_c between bonded and nonbonded species is given by the location of the maximum in the free energy surface.83

Using the free energy maximum to define a bond cut-off provides a clear picture of qualitative trends. As expected from a g(R_{OH}), at 34 GPa, the free energy peak is found at 1.30 Å, which is approximately the same value obtained from simulations of ambient water. At 75 GPa, the free energy peak maintains almost the same position but broadens considerably. At 115 GPa, the peak has sharpened once again, and the maximum is now at 1.70 Å.
