The basic qualitative prediction of MCT is, upon lowering the temperature or increasing the density of a melt, one observes a separation of scales between the microscopic dynamics and the structural relaxation leading to a two-step decay of all relaxation functions. One imagines the molecules to be trapped within a cage formed by their neighbors for some time span between the short times dynamics and the large-scale structural relaxation that comes when particles leave their cages. The long-time behavior is the structural (or $\alpha$) relaxation and the plateau regime between vibrational dynamics, and this $\alpha$-relaxation is termed the $\beta$-relaxation in the theory. For a well-developed intermediate plateau regime MCT predicts solutions of the type

```
$S(q,t) = f_q^2 + h_qG(t,\sigma)$ [48]
```

in a time window $t_0 \ll t \ll t_0'$, where $f_q^2$ is the nonergodicity parameter, $h_q$ is some wave-vector dependent amplitude, $G$ is a $q$-independent scaling function of time, and $t_0$ is the microscopic scale. The singularity parameter $\sigma \propto 1-\frac{1}{f}$ measures the distance from the singularity representing the "ideal" glass transition. For $\sigma \geq 0$, one has

```
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}S(q,t) = f_q^2 + h_q\sqrt{/(1-\lambda)} + O(\sigma)$ [49]
```
