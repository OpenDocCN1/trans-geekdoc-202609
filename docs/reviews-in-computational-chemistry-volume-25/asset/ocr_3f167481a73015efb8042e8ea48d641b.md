In the works of Lylin et al. and of Zon and de Leew, MCT was to provide a consistent description of the coherent as incoherent scattering functions over a temperature range between $T_c$ and $1.2T_c$. The value of the critical temperature Zon and de Leew obtained for PB was $T_c \sim 162K$, which is about 50 K below the experimental value. Their conclusions agree with those derived for the bead-spring model. The relaxation in polymers is more stretched than in the bead-spring model. This stretching results in a smaller value of the von Schweidler de-coupling exponent ($b=0.46$ for the PE-like model vs. $b=0.75$ for the bead-spring model) as well as for the KWW stretching exponent ($\beta \sim 0.4$ for the PE-like model vs. $\beta \sim \infty$), which are intercalated by the MCT prediction $eta_q \to b$ for $q \to \infty$. The result for the KWW event for the chemically realistic simulation agrees with typical values in neutron scattering or dielectric experiments. In contrast, the result for the bead-spring model is much larger.

We therefore conclude that differences between bead-spring and chemically realistic models can be attributed to either the differences in packing that we discussed above or the presence of barriers in the dihedral potential in atomic models. There has been extensive effort in recent years to use coordinated experimental and simulation studies of polymer melts to understand the connection between polymer motion and conformational dynamics. Although no experimental method directly measures conformational dynamics, several experimental probes of molecular motion are spatially local or sensitive to local motions. Coordinated simulation and experimental studies of local motion in polymers have been conducted for dielectric relaxation$^{152-158}$ dynamic neutron scattering,$^{157,159-164}$ and NMR spin-lattice relaxation.$17,152,165-168}$ A particularly important outcome of these studies is the improved understanding of the relationship between the probed motions of the polymer chains and the underlying conformational dynamics that leads to observed motions.
