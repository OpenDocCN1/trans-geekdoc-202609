# COMPUTING THERMODYNAMICS FROM FREE VOLUMES

As discussed, the intuitive notion that there should be a connection between the statistics of the free volumes of a fluid and its measurable macroscopic properties has a long history in studies of the liquid state. In fact, it turns out that this connection is precise in the case of the thermodynamics of the single-component hard-sphere fluid. Hoover, Ashurst, and Grover⁷⁷ and Speedy⁸² have provided in independent derivations that predict the relationship between the hard-sphere compressibility factor Z = P/ρk_BT and the geometric properties of its free volumes, as follows:

```
Z = 1 + \frac{\sigma}{6}\sqrt{\frac{s_{i}}{v_{i}}}$$
```

where σ represents the hard-sphere diameter and the angle brackets indicate an ensemble average. A more general form of this relationship, which applies to multicomponent hard-sphere systems with either additive or nonadditive diameters, has been derived more recently by Cory and Bowers⁸³.

From a computational perspective, the expression in Eq. [8] provides a straightforward test for the free-volume algorithm discussed above. In particular, the right-hand side of Eq. [8] can be computed from the configurations of hard spheres generated in a molecular dynamics simulation, whereas the lefthand side can be computed directly from measurement of the hard-sphere collision rate in the same simulation. Sastry and coworkers⁷¹ carried out that test, the results of which are be seen in Figure 8. As can be seen, the agreement is excellent.
