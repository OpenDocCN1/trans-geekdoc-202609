The nature of the spin arrangement depends sensitively on the interatomic distances. A comparative study of Cr, Mn (Mn clusters will be studied in the next section), and Fe was made by Fujita⁷ by assuming a pentagonal pyramid structure for the three clusters and including the variation of the interatomic distance $d$ from the bulk value $d_{bulk}$ up to a value 20% lower than $d_{bulk}$. The results are summarized in Figure 11. For Cr with $d_{bulk}$, the magnetic moments are arranged in a coplanar, noncollinear configuration; that is, the vector moments lie on the same plane but point in different directions. When $d$ decreases, the magnetic moments are ordered in a parallel (P) fashion. The situation is in Mn for interatomic distances close to $d_{bulk}$ that is, the cluster shows a coplanar configuration of the spins. However, a noncoplanar configuration first appears when $d$ decreases, which changes to an antiparallel (AP) configuration with a further decrease of $d$. Finally the arrangement of the spins in Fe is parallel for $d \approx d_{bulk}$ and $d \approx 0.8$ $d_{bulk}$. ## Manganese Clusters

Manganese is the 3d metal with the smallest bulk modulus and cohesive energy. It has a complex lattice structure with several allotropic forms. Some of these bulk phases are antiferromagnetic, monolayers$^{81}$ and supported clusters$^{82}$ exhibit nearly degenerate ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states. The dimer is also peculiar.$83}$ In contrast to other transition elements, the bond length of Mn2 is larger than the nearest-neighbor distance in the bulk. In addition, its binding energy is between 0.06 and 0.6 eV, which puts Mn2 in a category similar to van Waals molecules. The properties arise from the electronic configuration of the atom, $3d^54s^2$. The electrons of the half-filled $3d$ shell are well localized and do not interact with those of the other atom in the dimer. Binding arises from the interaction between the filled $4s^2$ shells. A nonmetal-to-metal transition occurs as the Mn clusters reach a critical size. From experiments of the reactivity with hydrogen, Parks et al.$84$ have suggested that this transition occurs at $\text{Mn}_{16}$. The large magnetic moment of the free atom (1 $\mu_B$) and the weak interaction between the atoms in the dimer lead to an interesting magnetic behavior for Mn clusters.

Some measurements of the moments of Mn clusters containing fewer than ten atoms have been performed for clusters embedded in matrices. Electron spin resonance (ESR) for Mn2 in condensed gas matrices yield an antiferromagnetic configuration, but Mn2 is ferromagnetic, with a total magnetic moment of 11 $\mu_B$.$^{85}$ A moment of 20 $\mu_B$ has been measured for Mn2 in a silicon matrix. Mn2$^{86}$ embedded in inert gases has a moment of 25 $\mu_B$, although the cluster actually could be larger.$87$ We close by noting that neutral Mn3 is antiferromagnetic, whereas the other Mn clusters are ferromagnetic.

The computational results on Mn clusters are also controversial. An early Hartree–Fock study of Mn3 predicted a $^5A_1'$ ground state resulting from the antiferromagnetic coupling of the localized spins$^{88}$ Fujita and Yamaguchi$^{89}$ used DFT to study clusters of size Mn2 to Mn7. The interatomic distances were optimized for constrained geometries, and all clusters were predicted to show antiparallel spin alignment. Nayak and Jena$^{90}$ optimized the structures of clusters with N ≤ 5 at the LSDA and GGA levels of theory. Only the GGA reproduce some of the observed features of Mn2, namely a bond length longer than the nearest-neighbor distance in the bulk and a small binding energy (the calculated bond length is 6.67 a.u., and the binding energy is 0.06 eV). The cluster is predicted to be ferromagnetic with a total magnetic moment of 10 $\mu_B$. The binding energy increases in Mn2^+$, and the bond length decreases relative to Mn2 because the electron is removed from an antibonding orbital. The total magnetic moment of Mn2^+$ is 11 $\mu_B$, in agreement with the experimental estimation for rare gas matrices.

## Magnetic Properties of Atomic Clusters of the Transition Elements

The optimized geometries of Mn3, Mn4, and Mn5 obtained by Nayak and Jena$^{90}$ are an equilateral triangle, a Jahn-Teller distorted tetrahedron, and a trigonal bipyramid. The strength of the bonding increases relative to the dimer by $s-d$ hybridization. The predicted geometries are consistent with those deduced from experiments in matrices. The hyperfine pattern observed for Mn4 embedded in a silicon matrix$^{86}$ indicates that the four atoms are equivalent, or would occur in a tetrahedron. The triangular bipyramid is one of the possible structures of Mn3 consistent with the ESR measurements$^{87}$ The calculated interatomic distances decrease substantially from Mn2 to Mn3, which signals the onset of delocalization and hybridization between atomic orbitals at various sites. But the most striking property of these clusters is their ability to retain their atomic moments. Mn3, Mn4, and Mn5 in their ground state are predicted to be ferromagnetic, with moments of 5 $\mu_B$ (low-lying isomers are also ferromagnetic). Experiments for thin layers support the possibility of magnets$^{91,92}$

The calculations of Peterson$^{93}$ provide insight into the magnetism of small clusters. These authors studied Mn2 using LDA and GGA functions and consider the manganese dimer is ferromagnetic with a total moment of 10 $\mu_B$, a bond length of 4.3 a.u., and a binding energy of 0.99 eV. They also found an antiferromagnetic state whose, a binding energy of 0.54 eV and a bond length of 5.13 a.u., are closer to those of Mn2 in condensed gas matrices. A plausible resolution of Mn3 found by Peterson et al.$^{97}$ is that the ferromagnetic state is the true ground state of Mn3; that the interaction with the condensed gas matrix may stretch the bond, which leads to the appearance of antiferromagnetic state in the embedded cluster. However, recent calculations by Yamamoto et al.$^{94}$ using a high-level ab initio method (second-order perturbation theory)$^{95}$ MCQPT2 predict antiferromagnetic coupling for the Mn dimer.

Larger clusters were also studied by Peterson et al.$^{93}$ Mn3 has different magnetic states in energy. The ground state is isosceles triangle in a ferromagnetic configuration with a total moment of 15 $\mu_B$. A frustrated antiferromagnetic state also exists with the atomic spins of the shorter side of the triangle antiferromagnetically coupled to the third atom, whereas the first two are antiferromagnetically aligned (perfect antiferromagnetism is impossible in the triangular structure because the moments of two atoms necessarily point in the same direction); this represents a frustration of the tendency to antiferromagnetism). This state, with a magnetic moment of 5 $\mu_B$, is only 0.014 eV above the ground state. Mn4 is a tetrahed with a total moment of 20 $\mu_B$. The calculations predict a trigonal bipyramid as the ground state of Mn5 with a net moment of 23 $\mu_B$, which is lower than the measured value of 25 $\mu_B^{87}$. Trigonal bipyramid and square pyramid clusters with moments of 25 $\mu_B$ were found, 0.62 eV and 1.20 eV above the ground state, respectively.
