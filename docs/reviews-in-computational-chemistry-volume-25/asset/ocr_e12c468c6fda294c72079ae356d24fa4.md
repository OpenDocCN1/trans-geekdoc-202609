# Magnetic Properties of Atomic Clusters of the Transition Elements

The optimized geometries of Mn3, Mn4, and Mn5 obtained by Nayak and Jena$^{90}$ are an equilateral triangle, a Jahn-Teller distorted tetrahedron, and a trigonal bipyramid. The strength of the bonding increases relative to the dimer by $s-d$ hybridization. The predicted geometries are consistent with those deduced from experiments in matrices. The hyperfine pattern observed for Mn4 embedded in a silicon matrix$^{86}$ indicates that the four atoms are equivalent, or would occur in a tetrahedron. The triangular bipyramid is one of the possible structures of Mn3 consistent with the ESR measurements$^{87}$ The calculated interatomic distances decrease substantially from Mn2 to Mn3, which signals the onset of delocalization and hybridization between atomic orbitals at various sites. But the most striking property of these clusters is their ability to retain their atomic moments. Mn3, Mn4, and Mn5 in their ground state are predicted to be ferromagnetic, with moments of 5 $\mu_B$ (low-lying isomers are also ferromagnetic). Experiments for thin layers support the possibility of magnets$^{91,92}$

The calculations of Peterson$^{93}$ provide insight into the magnetism of small clusters. These authors studied Mn2 using LDA and GGA functions and consider the manganese dimer is ferromagnetic with a total moment of 10 $\mu_B$, a bond length of 4.3 a.u., and a binding energy of 0.99 eV. They also found an antiferromagnetic state whose, a binding energy of 0.54 eV and a bond length of 5.13 a.u., are closer to those of Mn2 in condensed gas matrices. A plausible resolution of Mn3 found by Peterson et al.$^{97}$ is that the ferromagnetic state is the true ground state of Mn3; that the interaction with the condensed gas matrix may stretch the bond, which leads to the appearance of antiferromagnetic state in the embedded cluster. However, recent calculations by Yamamoto et al.$^{94}$ using a high-level ab initio method (second-order sidegenerate perturbation theory)$^{95}$ MCQPT2 predict antiferromagnetic coupling for the Mn dimer.

Larger clusters were also studied by Peterson et al.$^{93}$ Mn3 has different magnetic states in energy. The ground state is isosceles triangle in a ferromagnetic configuration with a total moment of 15 $\mu_B$. A frustrated antiferromagnetic state also exists with the atomic spins of the shorter side of the triangle antiferromagnetically coupled to the third atom, whereas the first two are antiferromagnetically aligned (perfect antiferromagnetism is impossible in the triangular structure because the moments of two atoms necessarily point in the same direction); this represents a frustration of the tendency to antiferromagnetism). This state, with a magnetic moment of 5 $\mu_B$, is only 0.014 eV above the ground state. Mn4 is a tetrahed with a total moment of 20 $\mu_B$. The calculations predict a trigonal bipyramid as the ground state of Mn5 with a net moment of 23 $\mu_B$, which is lower than the measured value of 25 $\mu_B^{87}$. Trigonal bipyramid and square pyramid clusters with moments of 25 $\mu_B$ were found, 0.62 eV and 1.20 eV above the ground state, respectively.
