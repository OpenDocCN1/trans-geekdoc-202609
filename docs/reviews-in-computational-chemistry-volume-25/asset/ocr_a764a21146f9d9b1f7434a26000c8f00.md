To measure such time scales, the free-volume autocorrelation function $C_{v_i}$ can be calculated.$^{75}$ This quantity, defined as

```
$C_{v_i}(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \langle v_{v_i}(t)v_{v_i}(0) \rangle / \langle v_{v_i}(0)v_{v_i}(0) \rangle$ [9]
```

where $\delta_{v_i}(t) = v_i(t) - \langle v_i \rangle$ is the deviation of $i$'s free volume at time $t$ from its average value. This autocorrelation function characterizes the dynamic manner in which a particle's free volume loses correlation with its initial value over time because of thermal fluctuations. The decay of the correlator can be used to identify the "persistence" times of free volumes $	au_b^{75}$ which are displayed in Figure 10(a) as a function of the interparticle attraction. As expressed, the persistence time of the free volumes increases rapidly as the strength of attractions is increased. KGT$^{75}$ also noted that the simple scaling relationship $D = A(v_i^2)^{2/3}/	au_i$, is capable of capturing the diffusion coefficient's nontrivial dependence on attractive strength.
