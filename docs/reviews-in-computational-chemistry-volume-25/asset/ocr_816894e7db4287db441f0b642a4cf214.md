The hopping integrals $t_{ij}^eta$ between orbitals $\alpha$ and $eta$ at equilibrium are assumed to be independent of spin and are well fitted to reproduce the first-principles band structure of the metal at the observed lattice constant. The variation of the hopping integrals with the interatomic distance $R_{ij} = |R_i - R_j|$ is often assumed to follow a power law $(R_0/R_{ij})^{l+1}$, where $R_0$ is the equilibrium-neighbor-distance in the bulk solid and $l$ are the angular momentum of the two orbitals, $\alpha$ and $eta$, involved in the hopping.$^{16}$ An exponential decay is sometimes used instead of the power law.

The spin-dependent diagonal terms $\varepsilon_{ij\sigma\sigma'}$ contain all of the many-body contributions. In a mean field approximation, these environment-dependent energy levels can be written as

```
$$\varepsilon_{ij\sigma\sigma'} = e_{ij}^0 + \sum_{\beta\sigma'} U_{ij\beta\sigma'} \Delta v_{ij\beta\sigma'} + \sum_{j+1} \frac{e^2}{R_{ij}} \Delta v_j + Z_i \Omega_\sigma$
```

where $e_{ij}^0$ are the reference orbital energies. These energies could be atomic levels, but most the bare orbital of the paramagnetic metal are taken as reference energies. The second term gives the shifts of the energies caused by intraatomic Coulomb interactions. The intraatomic Coulomb integrals

```
$U_{ij\sigma\sigma'} = \int \frac{\psi_{i\sigma}(\vec{r})\psi_{j\sigma'}(\vec{r})}{\|}{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'\|} \psi_{i\sigma'}(\vec{r}')\psi_{j\sigma}(\vec{r}') d^3r d^3r'$
```

give the electrostatic interaction between two charges corresponding to the orbitals $|i\sigma\rangle$ and $|j\sigma'\rangle$ on the same atom. The quantity $\Delta v_{ij\beta} = v_{i\beta} - v_{\beta}$, where $v_{i\beta} = (n_{i\beta})$ is the average occupation of the spin-orbital $|i\beta\rangle$ and $v_{\beta}$ is the corresponding occupation in the paramagnetic solid for the bulk metal. The intraatomic Coulomb integrals $U_{ij\beta}$ can be expressed in terms of more convenient quantities, the effective integrals $U_{ij\beta} = (U_{\|i\beta|} + U_{\|j\beta|})/2$ and the exchange integrals $J_{ij\beta} = U_{\|i\beta|} - U_{ij\beta}$. Then, the intraatomic term of Eq. [16] splits into two contributions

```
$\sum_{\beta\sigma'} U_{ij\beta\sigma'} \Delta v_{ij\beta} = \sum_{\beta} U_{ij\beta} \Delta v_{ij\beta} + \sum_{\sigma} \frac{J_{ij\beta}}{2} \mu_{\beta}$
```

where $\Delta v_{ij\beta} = \Delta v_{i\beta} + \Delta v_{j\beta}$, $\mu_{\beta} = \Delta v_{i\beta} - \Delta v_{j\beta}$, and $\sigma_{\sigma}$ is the sign function $(z_1 = -1, z_2 = +1)$. The first term $U_{\|i\beta|}$ refers to the Coulomb interaction between electrons with opposite spin and $U_{\|j\beta|}$ to the interaction between electrons with the same spin.
