## Relativistic AO Basis Sets

It is not possible to use normal AO basis sets in relativistic calculations: The relativistic contraction of the inner shells makes it necessary to design new basis sets for this effect. Specifically designed basis sets have been constructed using the DKH Hamiltonian. These basis sets are of atomic orbital (ANO) type and are constructed such that semi-core electrons can also be correlated. They have been given the name ANO-RCC (relativistic with core correlation) and cover all atoms of the Periodic Table.

- They have been used in most applications presented in this review. ANO-RCC are all-electron sets. Deep core orbitals are described by a minimal basis set and are kept frozen in the wave function calculations. The extra cost compared using effective potentials (ECPs) is therefore limited. ECPs, however, have been used in some studies, and more details will be given in connection with the specific application. The ANO-RCC basis sets can be downloaded from the page of the MOLCAS quantum chemistry software (http://www.teekom.lu.se/molcas).

## THE MULTIPLE METAL–METAL BOND IN $Re_2Cl_8$ AND RELATED SYSTEMS

In 1965 Cotton and Harris examined the crystal structure of $K_2[Re_2Cl_8]\cdot2H_2O$^{39}$ and reported a surprisingly short Re–Re distance of 2.24 \AA. This was the first reported of a multiple bond between two metal atoms and the $Re_2Cl_8$ ion has since become the prototype for this family of complexes.

Cotton analyzed the bonding using simple MO theory and concluded that a quadruple Re–Re bond was formed.$^{39,40}$ Two parallel $ReCl_4$ units are connected by the Re–Re bond. $d_{x^2-y^2}$, $p_x$, and $s$ orbitals of the valence shell of each Re atom form the $\sigma$ bonds to each Cl atom. The remaining $d_{z}$ and $p_z$ orbitals relative to the Re–Re axis, $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ with $\pi$ symmetry, the $d_{xy}$ with $\delta$ symmetry form the quadruple Re–Re bond. The system contains one $\sigma$ bond, two $\pi$ bonds, and one $\delta$ bond between the Re atoms. Because there are eight electrons ($Re^{3+}$ is $d^5$) to occupy these orbitals, the ground state will be $\sigma^2\pi^4\delta^2$. The presence of a $\delta$ bond explains the eclipsed conformation of the ion. In a staggered conformation, the overlap of $\delta$ atomic orbitals is zero and the $\delta$ bond disappears. The visible spectrum was also reported in these early studies. The notion of a quadruple bond is based on the inherent assumption that four orbitals are doubly occupied. Today we know that this is not the case for metallic bonds. The true bond order depends on the relation between the occupation of bonding and antibonding orbitals, respectively. Such description is, however, only possible if a quantum chemical model is used that goes beyond the single configuration. The CASSCF method has been used to study this system, and it has demonstrated that the true bond order between Re atoms is closer to three.

Because $Re_2Cl_6-}$ is such an important entity in inorganic chemistry, we decided to study its structure and electronic spectrum using multfigurational quantum chemistry.$^{41}$ Scalar relativistic effects and spin-orbit coupling were included in this study. The geometry of $Re_2Cl_6-}$ was obtained at the CASPT2 level of theory, and several excited states were calculated using an active space formed by 12 active electrons (12/12) (reported in Figure 3). It comprises one $\sigma$, two $\pi$, and one $\delta$ Re–Re bonding orbitals and the corresponding antibonding orbitals, and two $\delta$ bonding orbitals and the corresponding antibonding orbitals. They are nicely paired such that the sum of the occupation numbers for the Re–Re bonding and antibonding orbitals of a given type is almost exactly two. orbitals are located mainly on Cl, whereas the antibonding orbitals have large contributions from 3d. The occupation of $\pi$ bond is low, and these orbitals are almost empty and may be used as acceptor orbitals for electronic transitions. The strongest bond between the two atoms is the $\sigma$ bond, with an occupation number of $nb=1.92$ of bonding and $nh=0.08$ of antibonding. We estimate the effective bond order as $(nb-nh)/(nb+nh)$, and for $\sigma$ bond, we obtain the value 0.92. The corresponding value for $\pi$ bond gives an effective bond order of 0.54. Adding up these numbers results in a total bond order of 3.20 for $Re_2Cl_5$. The main reduction of the bond order from 4.0 to 3.2 is due to $\delta$ bond. Note that the calculation of natural numbers by CASSCF description of $Re_2Cl_5$ deviate from zero and is indicative of the limit. Vertical excitation energies and oscillator strengths have been determined by the CASPT2 level with the inclusion of spin-orbit coupling.
