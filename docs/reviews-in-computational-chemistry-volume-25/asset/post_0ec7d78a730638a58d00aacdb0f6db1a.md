## CHAPTER 1

### Determining the Glass Transition in Polymer Melts

Wolfgang Paul

Institut für Physik, Johannes Gutenberg-Universität, Germany

#### INTRODUCTION

In the last 15 years, computer simulation studies of the glass transition in polymer melts have contributed significantly to advance our understanding of this phenomenon, which is at the same time of fundamental scientific interest and great technical importance for polymer materials, most of which are amorphous or at best semi-crystalline. This progress has been possible, on one hand, because of improved models and simulation algorithms and, other hand, because of theoretical advances in the description of the structural glass transition in general.¹ Much of this development has been mirrored in a series of conferences on relaxations in complex systems the proceedings which might serve as a good entry point into the literature on the glass transition in general.,³

Instead of providing a detailed overview of all simulation work on the glass transition in polymer melts, this review has two goals. The first goal is to provide a novice to the field with the necessary background to understand the model building and choice of simulation technique for studies of the polymer glass transition. In particular, a novice modeler needs to be aware of the strengths and limitations of the different approaches used in simulation of glass-forming polymers and to be able to judge the validity of the original literature.
