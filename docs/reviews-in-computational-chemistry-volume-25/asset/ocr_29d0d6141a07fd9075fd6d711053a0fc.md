The energy of the bond in the Cr(I) compound PhCrCrPh is as large as that of the formally Cr(0) compound, Cr₂, despite the decreased bond order. In the study of Cu₂O₂, the CASSCF/CASPT2 method is unsatisfactory. This and related methods motivate the extension of the CASSCF/CASPT2 method to handle larger active spaces.

The chemical bond in systems containing actin atoms, in particular uranium, was also addressed. A formal quantum bond was found for the uranyl diatomic molecule U₂ with a unique electronic structure involving six-electron bonds ferromagnetically coupled, which results in a high spin ground state. It was questioned whether the U₂ unit could be used as a central moiety in inorganic complexes similar to those explored by Cotton et al. for transition metal dimers. Corresponding chlorides anding were found to be stable units with multiply bonded U(III) ions. It might be possible to use the elusive U(I) ion in metal-metal bonding involving organic ligands in parallel to the recently synthesized ArCrAr compound. Many challenges exist, and issues remain open. The interplay between theoreticians and experimentalists will certainly enhance the possibilities for further progress in transition metal and actinide chemistry.
