For a polymer chain, the long time limit in Eq. [67] means that one has to be able to simulate the model system for times on the order of several Rouse times, or to put it in enough away, for the chains to diffuse over a spatial range a few times their size. This simulation is possible for a bead-spring model down to lower temperatures, but for a chemically realistic model with reasonably long chains, one typically cannot perform such lengthy simulations. Super-cooling the bead-spring melts below its crystallization temperature (which is $T=0.76$, see the section on thermodynamic properties) and a large decrease in the self-diffusion coefficient is observed (see Figure 9). The temperature dependence below $T=1$ is compatible with a Vogel–Fulcher law with a vanishing self-diffusion coefficient at $T_0=0.32\pm0.02$. Note, however, that for this coarse-grained model, which is much easier to simulate than chemically realistic models, the information on the chain center of mass diffusion from the simulation in a (meta)-stable equilibrium is limited to temperatures above approximately $1.44T_0$, which makes the deduction of $T_0$ from these data a risky extrapolation. We will comment on the MCT fits in this figure later in the chapter.
