# DETERMINATION OF MAGNETIC MOMENTS BY COMBINING THEORY AND PHOTODETACHMENT SPECTROSCOPY

The measurement of the magnetic moment of very small clusters is not simple. In such cases, the total magnetic moment is also small and the deflection in the magnetic field may lie within the error of the experiment. Motivated by this difficulty, an alternative method to determine the magnetic moments has been proposed by Khanna and Jena$^{130}$ based on combining calculations for the neutral and negatively charged (ionic) species, $X_N$ and $X_A$, respectively, with electron photodetachment experiments for the anionic cluster. Let us consider a ferromagnetic anionic cluster that has $n$ unpaired spins, and, thus, a magnetic moment $\mu_B$ and multiplicity $M=n+1$. When an electron is detected from the anion, the neutral cluster has a multiplicity of $M+1$ if the electron is removed from the minority band, or $M-1$ if the electron is removed from the majority band. The measured photoelectron energy peaks can be compared with theoretical calculations where one firstly determines the ground state of the anion, including its spin multiplicity $M$, and the energy for the transition to the neutral species with multiplicities $M+1$ and $M-1$ at the anion geometry. Quantitative agreement between the calculated energies and the observed spectral peaks indicates that the calculated multiplicity must correct$^{130}$.

The Khanna-Jena method has been applied to $Ni_S^{131}$ shows a prominent peak at 10.80 eV and a minor peak at 2.1 eV. A careful investigation was performed using DFT with the GGA for exchange and correlation of the equilibrium spectra of anionic $Ni_S$ corresponding to spin multiplicities $M=2, 4, 6, 8,$ and $10$, and of neutral $Ni_S$ with spin multiplicities $M=1, 3, 5, 7,$ and $9$. The ground state of the neutral cluster is a square pyramid with spin multiplicity $M=7$ (total magnetic moment of $6\mu_B$). This is almost degenerate, with an isomer having the structure of a distorted triangular bipyramid and $M=5$ ($4\mu_B$). In the case of $Ni_S$, the structure, for all of the spin multiplicities, is a distorted square. The ground state has $M=8$, and this can only arise by adding an electron to the majority-spin band of neutral $Ni_S$ with $M=7$ (which is precisely the ground state of $Ni_S$). The structure of $Ni_S$ with $M=6$ has an energy only 0.05 eV above the ground state, so both isomers with $M=6$ and $8$ are expected to exist in the beam.
