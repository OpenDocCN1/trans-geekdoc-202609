The enhancement of $\mu_{orb}$, in the clusters with respect to the bulk moment $\mu_{orb}$ results from several correlations changes related to the local environment of the atoms.⁶⁹ The first contribution is the reduction of the local coordination number leading to an increase of the spin polarizations, which spin-orbit interactions enhance large orbital moment. The second contribution is the distribution of intraatomic Coulomb interaction, which favors the occupation of states with high $m$ and contributes to the enhancement of $\mu_{orb}$ in clusters. The final contribution is the presence of degeneracies in the spectrum of one-electron energies that allow for a very effective spin-orbit moment that enhances $\mu_{orb}$.

The magnetic moments of the Ni clusters are decreasing from surface atoms.⁴⁸,⁶⁹ The analysis of Wan et al. indicates that the orbital and spin moments of cluster atoms with atomic coordination 8 or larger are similar to those in the bulk $(\mu_{spin} \approx 0.55 \mu_{B}$, and $\mu_{orb} \approx 0.05 \mu_{B}$)⁷³ that is, the orbital moment is almost quenched for internal cluster atoms. In contrast, there is a large enhancement of the spin and orbital moments for atoms with coordination less than 8. This enhancement increases with the coordination deficit, and it is larger for the orbital moment. ## Chromium and Iron Clusters

Chromium is an antiferromagnetic metal in the bulk phase, and the calculations of Cheng and Wang⁷⁵ show that the Cr clusters also have a strong tendency toward antiferromagnetic spin ordering (although the fact that the number of atoms makes the distribution of the magnetic moments more complex). Small Cr clusters are “special” compared with the other 3d metals.⁷⁵⁷⁶ The electronic structure of the atom 3d⁴ has unpaired electrons. This half-filled electronic configuration leads to d–d bonding in Cr₂, with a bond length of 1.68 Å, which is very short compared with the interatomic distance of 2.50 Å in Cr metal. The dimer is a closed shell molecule with a strong sextuple bond.⁷⁵ The strong binding arises from the filling of the 3d-bonding molecular orbitals: σ₄π₄δ₀σ⁶(¹Σ⁺). The electronic structure of the dimer is robust and controls the growth of the small clusters.
