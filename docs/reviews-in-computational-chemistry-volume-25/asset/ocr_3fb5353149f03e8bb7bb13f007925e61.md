The dissociation of $U_2Cl_{16}$ to $2 UCl_3$ has also been studied. UCl$_3$, unlike $U_6$, is known experimentally$^{101}$ and has been the subject of previous computational studies.$^{102}$ Single-point CASPT2 energy calculations have been performed at the geometry, as reported in Ref. 102, namely a pyramidal structure with a U–Cl bond distance of 2.55 Å and a Cl–U angle of 95 degrees. $U_2Cl_6$ was found to be about 20 kcal/mol more stable than $U_2Cl_3$ moieties.

$U_2Cl_6$ is the analog of $Re_2Cl_6$. The structure for $U_2Cl_6$ has been optimized using an active space formed by 6 active electrons in 13 active orbitals, assuming $D_{4h}$ symmetry. As in $U_2Cl_6$, the molecular orbitals are linear combinations of $U 7s, 7p, 6d$, and $5f$ orbitals with Cl $3p$ orbitals. The ground state of $U_2Cl_6$ is a singlet state with an electronic configuration of $(F\sigma)^6(F\pi)^8$. The molecule possesses a U–U triple bond. The U–U bond distance is 2.59 Å, and the U–U angle is 111.2 degrees. $U_2Cl_6$ is compared with $Re_2Cl_6$ in terms of molecular bonding, in the sense that the bond in $Re_2Cl_6$ is formally a quadple bond, because $Re^{3+}$ has four electrons to form the metal-metal bond. Only a triple bond can form in $U_2Cl_6$ because only three electrons are available on each $U^{3+}$ unit.

Based on several experimental reports of compounds in which uranium is bound to a carbon atom, we have considered the possibility that the UUCC core containing two U++ ions could be incorporated between two sterically hindered ligands. We performed a theoretical study of a hypothetical molecule, namely UPPh (Ph=phenyl), the uranium analog of the bulky terphenyl ligands, PhCrCrPh.$^{102}$ We have chosen to mimic the bulky terphenyl ligands, which could be potentially promising candidates for the stabilization of multiply bonded uranium compounds, using the simple phenyl molecule. We demonstrate that UPPhPh could be a chemical entity with a singlet ground state. The CASSCF method was used to generate molecular orbitals and reference functions for CASPT2 calculations. The structures of two isomers were initially optimized by DFT, namely the bent planar UPPhPh (Isomer A, Figure 7) and the linear isomer (Isomer B, Figure 8). Starting from a transbent planar structure, the geometry optimization for isomer A predicted a rhomb structure (a bis(phenyl) structure), belonging to the $D_{2h}$ point group and analogous to the structure of the experimentally known species $U_2H_2$.^{91}$ Linear B also belongs to the $D_{2h}$ point group. CASPT2 geometry optimizations for several electronic states of various spin multiplicities were performed on selected structural parameters, namely the U–U and U–Ph bond distances, whereas the geometry of the phenyl fragment was kept fixed.
