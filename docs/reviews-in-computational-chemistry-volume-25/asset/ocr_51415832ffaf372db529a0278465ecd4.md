In fact, β-HMX often transforms into δ-HMX before reacting violently.89 Manaa et al.20 have conducted quantum-based molecular simulations of the chemistry of HMX and nitromethane90 under extreme conditions, which are similar to those encountered at the C-J detonation state. They studied the reactivity of (1.9 g/cm³) HMX at 3500 K for reaction times up to 55 ps, the “Self-Consistent Charge Density-Tight-Binding”.91 Stable products are formed rapidly (in a less than 1 ps) in these simulations. Plots of chemical speciation, however, indicate a time greater than 100 ps is needed to reach chemical equilibrium. Reactions occur rapidly in these simulations because the system is “preheated” to 3500 K. In a detonation, on the other hand, a temperature drop to 3500 K would only be found after stable product molecules have been formed. The initial temperature of untreated nitromethane, after being shocked, has increased to 1800 K.13 HMX has a similar initial temperature to that of nitromethane. Nonetheless, the simulations of Manaa et al. provide useful insight into the chemistry of dense, hot energetic materials, which demonstrate that they are a useful complement to more traditional gas phase calculations.

Numerous experimental characterizations of the decomposition products of condensed-phase HMX exist at low temperatures (i.e., < 1000 K, well below temperature).54,92–100 These studies tend to identify final products (such as H₂O, N₂, H₂, CO and CO₂) from the surface burn, and the authors aspire to establish a global decomposition mechanism. Similar experimental observations at detonation conditions (temperatures 2000–5000 K and pressures 10–30 GPa) have not been realized to date, however. Computer simulations provide the best access to the short time scale processes occurring in these regions of extreme conditions of pressure and temperature.101 In particular, simulations employing many-body potentials102,103 or tight-binding models have emerged as successfully demonstrated in the synthesis of shocked hydrocarbons.104,105

Lewis et al.106 calculated four decomposition pathways of the α-HMX polymer: N–NO₂ bond dissociation, HONO elimination, C–N bond sc, and ring fission. Based on energetics, it was determined that N–NO₂ dissociation was the initial mechanism of decomposition in the gas phase, whereas they proposed HONO elimination and C–N bond sc to be favorable in the condensed phase. The most recent study of Chakraborty et al.52 using density functional theory (DFT), reported detailed decomposition pathways of β-HMX, which is the stable polymer at room temperature. It was concluded that HONO elimination (4 HONO) and subsequent decomposition into HCN, OH, and NO are the most energetically favorable pathways in the gas phase. The results showed that the formation of CH₂O and N₂ could occur preferably from secondary decomposition of methylenitramine.
