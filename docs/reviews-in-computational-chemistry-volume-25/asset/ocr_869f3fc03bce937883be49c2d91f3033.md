The source of emerging heterogeneity (slow movers and fast movers) in the conformational dynamics of amorphous polymers when decreasing the system’s temperature remains unknown. In light of the packing arguments underlying MCT, it is reasonable to associate differences in rates with the local packing environment, a dense environment for slow dihedrals and a looser environment for faster dihedrals. The work by Jin and Boyd¹¹⁵³ in this direction and efforts by de Pablo’s group¹¹⁹⁷ trying to relate heterogeneous dynamics to inhomogeneous stress distributions have so far been inconclusive. Phenomenologically, it is clear that conformational transitions become increasingly self-correlated with decreasing temperature. (Here, correlation is defined in dihedral until another transition (usually back to the original state) before a neighboring torsion undergoes a transition.) It has been observed for several polymer melts¹⁹³,¹⁹⁴ that the probability of self-correlation increases dramatically with decreasing temperature. Self-correlation may increase because of the ineffective of conformational transitions to induce relaxation with decreasing temperature: A relatively few transitions back-and-forth rapidly between two conformational states can contribute significantly to the rate of conformational transitions, but they will contribute little to the experimentally observable local polymer relaxations.

The homogenization (or equilibration) process of the torsional transitions can be examined in even more detail. For example, one can differentiate between a first regime where every dihedral becomes mobile and is able to visit other isometric states, and a second regime where the frequency of these visits approaches its thermal equilibrium value. Bedrov and Smith¹¹⁸,¹⁹⁹ have analyzed the time scales for these regimes recently modified using PB-models and have shown that the first regime corresponds to the time scale of the dielectric β relaxation, while the second regime follows the α-relaxation time scale. This study is a first inroad into a mechanistic understanding of dielectric processes based on MD simulations.
