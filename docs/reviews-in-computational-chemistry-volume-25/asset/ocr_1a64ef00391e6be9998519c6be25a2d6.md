However, the direct Coulomb repulsion tends to suppress charge redistribution among atoms and to approximate local charge neutrality (i.e., $\Delta v$ is small). The direct and exchange integrals, $U_{ij}$ and $J_{eta ij}$, are usually parametrized. The difference between $s-s$, $s-p$, and $p-p$ direct Coulomb integrals is neglected by writing $U_{sp} = U_{pp}$, and it is assumed that $U_{sd} = U_{dd}$. The ratio $U_{sd} : U_{dd}$ of the magnitudes of $U_{sd}$, $U_{dd}$, and $U_{dd}$ can be taken from Hartree-Fock calculations. The absolute value of $U_{dd}$ can be estimated by independent method, from instance, atomic spectroscopic data. Typical values for $U_{sd} : U_{dd}$ ratios are $0.32 : 0.42 : 1$ for Fe and $U_{dd} = 5.40$ eV$^{15}$ The direct Coulomb integral between $d$ electrons, $U_{dd}$, determines $U_{sd}$ and $U_{sd}$. The magnetic properties of clusters are not very sensitive to the precision of $U_{dd}$ because charge transfer $\Delta v$ is typically small. In most cases, all exchange integrals involving $s$ and $p$ electrons are neglected and the $d$ exchange integral $J$ is determined to reproduce the bulk magnetic moment. Typical $J$ for Cr, Fe, Ni, and Co are between 0.5 and 1.0 eV$^{15,17,18}$
