# CLUSTERS OF THE 4d elements

The 4d metals are nonmagnetic in the bulk phase. However, the free atoms are magnetic, and consequently, it is reasonable to expect that small clusters of these elements could be magnetic. Experiments$^{5,106}$ show that Rh clusters with less than 60 atoms and Ru and Pd clusters with less than 12 atoms are magnetic. Several calculations have investigated the magnetism of those clusters assuming model structures. Patterns across the cross of the periodic table have been shown by performing DFT calculations for six-atom clusters with octahedral structure$^{107}$ and the magnetic moments and the largest are given in Table 2. All clusters, except $Y_6$, $Pd_6$, and $Cd_6$, have finite magnetic moments and the largest occur for Ru, $Rh_6$ ($1.6 \mu_B$/atom and $0.99 \mu_B$/atom). The large moments of these two clusters arise from the fact that the density of electronic states shows a large peak in the region of the Fermi level. A small exchange splitting (the shift between $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ spin sub-bands) produces a sizable difference between the populations of electrons with $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ spins. $Ru_6$, $Rh_6$, and $Nb_6$ have the largest exchange splittings. The Fermi levels of the bulk metals lie in a dip of the $d$ contrast. The main contribution to the DOS comes from the $d$ electrons, which gives support to models in which the effect of the $sp$ electrons has been neglected.
