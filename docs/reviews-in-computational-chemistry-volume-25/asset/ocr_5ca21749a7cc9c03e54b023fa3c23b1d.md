The structural relaxation time scale, however, also determines relaxation processes on large length scales, for example, the decay of the Rouse modes.¹135,136 In Figure 13 we see that the time scales of the first five Rouse modes follow the predicted relaxation times over a certain temperature window above $T_c$. When one comes close to $T_c$, the system does not actually freeze. Instead, relaxation processes not considered in the idealized MCT take over. The glassy freezing enters into the Rouse picture only through the temperature dependence of the segmental friction $\zeta(T)$, following the temperature dependence of the $\alpha$-relaxation time scale.

One also finds that the result does not depend on the $\alpha$-exponent (and consequently all other exponents) path one follows to reach the state-point by $(T_c, ho_c)$.^{134}$ which conforms to another prediction by MCT. In summary, MCT has been found to be consistently applicable to the glassy slowdown in the bead-spring polymer model over a narrow range of temperature above $T_c$. What is the reason underlying the applicability of MCT, considering the fact that the theory was developed for simple liquids and has no connectivity built in?
