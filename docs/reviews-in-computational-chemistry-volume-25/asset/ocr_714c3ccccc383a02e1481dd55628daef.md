This guideline is only a general one, opposed to a strict principle, and one should test the convergence of $\sigma_{sys}$ with respect to $E_{cut}$ before performing any serious first-principles tribological simulations.

A related issue originates from the fact the quality of a plane-wave set is dependent on the volume of the cell. In most variable-cell AIMD simulations performed with plane-wave basis sets, the number of plane waves is determined for the initial cell volume and held fixed through the simulation. That, as the cell changes shape and size, the number of plane waves remains constant, and the quality of the basis set, is more closely related to $E_{cut}$, is not constant throughout the simulation. This inconsistency can lead to problems when calculating *relative* energies, which govern chemical reactions. Essentially, a relative energy is the difference between the absolute energies of two systems, and if the systems are treated at the same level of theory, any errors in the absolute energies of the individual species because of the quality of the basis set will roughly cancel. On the other hand, if one system is treated with a higher quality basis set, these errors will no longer cancel, but instead become incorporated into the relative energy. These errors can become significant if the cell undergoes a substantial change which may occur during a tribological simulation. Two popular approaches exist for minimizing the errors caused by quality during variable-cell AIMD simulations. The first approach is to set $E_{cut}$, which determines the number of plane waves considered in the calculation, high enough to ensure that the absolute energies are sufficiently converged for all structures within the range of volumes that one would anticipate during the simulation. The second approach involves altering the number of plane waves that are explicitly considered during the simulation that $E_{cut}$ remains constant with changes in volume. We note that regardless of which approach one uses it is necessary to ensure that the basis set is large enough to yield an accurate $\sigma_{sys}$. An advisable strategy involves performing the AIMD simulation with a basis that is large enough to yield converged results for the pressure and exploring the energetics of observed processes in greater detail through a second set of calculations with a basis set that yields converged energies. Such a strategy has been used in static quantum calculations of phase transitions in $CO_{2}^{73}$ and could also be used by researchers to study tribochemical reactions.
