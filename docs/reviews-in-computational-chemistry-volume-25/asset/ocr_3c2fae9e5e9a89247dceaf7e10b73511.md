In particular, both the position and the intensities of the spectral lines can be obtained by efficient recursive methods such as RGGM and SLP without resorting to the explicit calculations of the eigenvectors.${108-115}$

An important problem in molecular spectroscopy is the assignment of vibrational states, assuming the system is in the regular regime, which is trivial to do if the eigenvectors are known. However, it has been shown that even without the eigenvectors, important information about the eigenvectors can be extracted to an unambiguous assignment of quantum numbers. Two strategies have been proposed and demonstrated to accomplish this assignment. The first is based on a perturbative scheme to compute the expectation values of the vibrational operator for a particular mode,$^{121}$ as described above. Such an operator could be chosen as the one-dimensional vibrational Hamiltonian $((1/2\mu)\delta^2/\partial R^2+(k/2)(R-R_0)^2)$ or the squared displacement from equilibrium $(R-R_0)^2$, which is amenable to both the Lanczos algorithm and the Chebyshev method LSFD, which is especially efficient for assigning normal quantum number near the minimum. The second strategy takes advantage of the SLP by computing the overlaps of the eigenvectors with a set of prescribed target functions. For example, the target functions could be chosen to be Gaussian functions at large R values, which have large overlaps with highly excited local-mode states.${103,117,198}$ This latter approach is particularly effective in "fishing out" eigenvectors with well-defined characteristics.
