The first principle is that the local magnetic moments of the atoms decrease when the number of neighbors (local coordination) decrease. The second principle is that the average magnetic moment of the cluster decreases when the intermediate distances decrease, which occurs because the width of the $d$ band increases. In metallic clusters, the average atomic coordination number increases with $N$. The average nearest-neighbor distance $d$ also increases with $N$, from the value for the molecule $d_{mol}$ to the value for the bulk $d_{bulk}$. The two effects oppose each other, and for that reason, the behavior of $\tilde{\mu}(N)$ in a growing cluster is complex. For $N\le20$, the geometries used to perform the calculations of the magnetic moments are obtained from molecular dynamics simulations using a many-atom potential based on the TB, with parameters fitted to the properties of $Ni_{12}$ and $Ni_{13}$. The geometries for $N=5-16$ are shown in Figure 4, which shows a cluster of icosahedral growth.

A qualitative agreement exists between the experimental and theoretical magnetic moments of small clusters. The TB calculations predict pronounced local minima at $Ni_6$ and $Ni_{13}$ and a local maximum at $Ni_8$. $Ni_{13}$ is an icosahedron with an internal atom at its center. The local atomic coordination of the surface atoms in $Ni_{13}$ is $Z=6$. On the other hand, $Ni_{12}$ and $Ni_{14}$ contain some atoms with coordination smaller than 6, and this leads to an increase of the local magnetic moments of those atoms.
