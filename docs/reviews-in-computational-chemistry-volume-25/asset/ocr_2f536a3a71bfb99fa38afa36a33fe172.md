# Quantum Mechanics and Eigenproblems

The fundamental equation in quantum mechanics, namely the time-independent Schrödinger equation,

```
H|\Psi> = E|\Psi>                               [1]
```

suggests that many problems in chemical physics can be reformulated as the solution of the corresponding eigenfunctions or their generalized counterparts. Examples of such problems include the Hartree-Fock and configuration interaction (CI) equations in electronic structure theory,ro-vibrational spectra of molecular systems,3-5 resonances in scattering,7 and in photodissociation. In addition, eigenproblems for other Hermitian operators in quantum mechanics are also common and can be handled using similar strategies.
