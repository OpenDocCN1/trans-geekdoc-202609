# Why Does the Genetic Algorithm Work?

The problem tackled here is simple, and it may seem that there is nothing terribly about what has been done. Nevertheless, though simple may seem, the algorithm can be applied successfully to problems that are more challenging than aligning a set of dipoles, as the examples in the next section will illustrate. The algorithm works, how does this happen?

The theory of evolutionary algorithms is increasingly extensive, detailed, and in some areas, however, a simple insight into how the genetic algorithm creates good solutions is offered by the building-block hypothesis. Although this hypothesis is not regarded in the AI community as a complete explanation, it provides an acceptable qualitative picture of how evolutionary methods can discover optimum solutions.

Let return to Table 1. In strings 2 and 7, there is one section in each string, shown in where neighbors are nearly aligned; alignment, in one case with two neighbors having angles close to 90° and in the other having two angles near 270°, rises to a low (favorable) interaction energy for that part of the string, and it is this that we can anticipate appear in the optimum solution. These regions in the strings construct “building blocks” for the construction of a good solution. If strings 2 and 7 are chosen as parents for the next generation, there is a chance that the mating operator will bring the two strings together for crossover. If this happens, these two building blocks might find themselves in the same, new string, which will contain at least two sections that will help improve (i.e., lower) the energy.
