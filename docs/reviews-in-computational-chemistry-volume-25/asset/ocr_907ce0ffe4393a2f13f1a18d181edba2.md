Heterogeneity here is defined as some dihedrals exhibiting faster motion than average dynamics, whereas others (chemically equivalent) dihedrals exhibit much slower motion than average. The mean transition time, which follows Arrhenius temperature dependence, is sensitive to fast events. For example, a few dihedrals may exist in a system, with the remaining being quiescent. This system can yield the same conformational transition time as a system in which all dihedrals have average dynamics. Heterogeneity in conformational dynamics can be quantified by measuring the distribution of waiting times for a number of transitions. For PB, as shown in Figure 24,¹ observes an increase in probability for very short waiting times, which is from correlated transitions. Furthermore, a long time tail develops, indicating the existence of very slow dihedrals. These results are compared in the figure with the expected Poisson behavior of independent random events. At high temperatures, the distribution approaches the expected Poisson behavior. When reducing the temperature, the distribution becomes increasingly heterogeneous.
