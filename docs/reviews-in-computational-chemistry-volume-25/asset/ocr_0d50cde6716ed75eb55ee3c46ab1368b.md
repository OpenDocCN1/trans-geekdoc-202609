# CONCLUSIONS

Exploring the nature of the chemical bond has a central issue for
toretical chemists since the dawn of quantum chemistry 80 years ago. We
now have a detailed understanding of what the electrons are doing in mole-
c on both a qualitative and a quantitative basis. We also have quantum
chemical methods that allow us to compute, with high accuracy, the properties
of chemical bonds, with bond energies, charge transfer, and bond bonding.
in recent years, it has been possible to extend these methods to treat bonding
involving atoms from the lower part of the Periodic Table.

In this chapter we illustrated how the CASCF/CASPT2 method can be
used to explore the nature of such chemical bonds. Classic cases are the Re–Re
ultiple bond in Re$_2$Cl$_6^-$, and the Cr–Cr bond ranging from the quadruply
bonded Cr(II)–Cr(II) moiety to the formal hexane bond between two neutral
chromium atoms. The bonding between the $3d$ electrons is weak and should
