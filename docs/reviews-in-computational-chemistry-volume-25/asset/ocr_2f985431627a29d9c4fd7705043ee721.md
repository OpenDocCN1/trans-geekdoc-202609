The ground state of the uranium atom is $(5f)^3(6d)^1(7s)^2$, $^5L_6$ with four unpaired electrons that can form a radical bond. The double occupancy of the $7s$ orbital, however prevents the unpaired orbitals from coming to contact with the bonds. On the other hand, a valence state with six unpaired electrons is 0.77 eV above the ground level: $(5f)^3(6d)^2(7s)^2$, $^7\mathcal{M}_6$. A hexaple bond could form in principle if it is strong enough to overcome the needed atomic promotion energy of 1.54 eV. However, there is one obstacle to form the bond. The $7s$ and $6d$ orbitals can expected to overlap more strongly than the $5f$ orbitals. In particular, the $5f$ orbitals, which are occupied in the free atom, will have little overlap. Thus, there must be a transfer of electrons from $5f$ to $6d$ to form a strong bond. As we see, this competition between the atomic configuration and the ideal molecule determines the electronic structure of the uranium dimer. To proceed further with the analysis, one needs to perform explicit calculations, and such calculations were done using a basis set ANO type, with inclusion of scalar relativistic effects, ANN-RCC, of the size: $9s8p65d2fg1lb^6$. As pointed out, 13 active orbitals on each atom are involved in the bonding ($5f, 6d, 7s$). This would yield an active space of 26 orbitals with 12 active orbitals, an impossible calculation, so the number of trial calculations were performed using smaller active spaces. The results had one important feature: They showed that a triple bond was formed involving the $7\sigma_g$ and $6d\pi$ orbitals. The occupation numbers of these three orbitals were close to two with small occupation of the corresponding bonding orbitals. It was therefore decided to leave these orbitals inactive in the CASSCF wave function and to remove the antibonding counterparts $7\sigma_g$ and $6d\pi_g$. This approximation should work well near the equilibrium bond, but course it prevents the calculation of full potential curves.

With six electrons and six MOs removed from the active space, one is left with 6 electrons in 20 orbitals, a calculation that could be performed easily. Several calculations were done with different space and spin symmetry of the wave function. The resulting ground state was found to be a triplet with all six electrons having parallel spin, and the orbital angular momentum was high with $\Lambda = 11$. Spin-orbit calculations showed that the spin and orbital angular momenta coincided to form an $\Omega = 8$ state. The final label of the ground state was $^7O_8$.
