Numerically, resonance states can be considered eigenstates of a complex-symmetric Hamiltonian with a negative imaginary potential $H = H - iV^{137-139}$ This approach is related to the more complex scaling method in which the dissociative coordinate is rotated into the complex plane.$208,209}$ The diagonalization of the complex-symmetric Hamiltonian matrix yields eigenvalues in the form of $E - i\Gamma/2$, where $E$ and $\Gamma$ represent the position and width of the resonance, respectively. In the limit of isolated resonances, the lifetime of the resonance is given by $1/\Gamma$. Like the real-symmetric case, the size of the matrix limits the applicability of direct diagonalization methods.

This is because the multiple copies of an eigenpair do not converge to machine precision.

A general non-symmetric eigenproblem can be solved recursively using the Arnoldi method.$^{211}$ The Lanczos algorithm for real-symmetric problems, the Arnoldi recursion generates a set of orthonormal vectors to span the Krylov subspace. Instead of a tridiagonal matrix, it yields an associate matrix in the Hessenberg form, which can be diagonalized. The Lanczos algorithm can also be modified for such problems. Instead of orthonormal vectors, the nonsymmetric Lanczos recursion generates vectors for two Krylov subspaces.$^{25}$ This-called dual Lanczos algorithm returns a generally nonsymmetric tridiagonal matrix.$^{34,212}$ Both methods are discussed in Saad$^{24}$ Although these recursive methods are amenable to complex-symmetric problems, their applications to chemical physics have only been attempted recently.
