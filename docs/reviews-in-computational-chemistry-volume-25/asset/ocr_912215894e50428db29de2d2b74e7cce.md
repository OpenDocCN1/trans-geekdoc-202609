The calculated magnetic moments of Figure 2 reveal a decrease of $	ilde{\mu}$ for sizes up to $N \approx 28$, followed by a weak increase between $N \approx 28$ and $N = 60$. This behavior is primarily a result of the variation of the average coordination number $	ilde{Z}$ which increases smoothly with $N$ up to $27$ and then drops.$^{46}$ By extrapolating the smooth behavior of $	ilde{Z}(N)$ to sizes greater than $N=27$, it was found that for $N$ between 27 and 54, the actual values of $	ilde{Z}(N)$ fall below the extrapolated values. Between $N=27$ and $N=30$ and then begins to increase after $N=30$. The break in $	ilde{Z}$ at $N=27$ suggests a flattening of $	ilde{\mu}$, which is confirmed by the calculations. This break in the coordination number is caused by a structural transition in the icosahedral clusters.$^{51}$ which occurs precisely $N=28$. Starting with the first complete icosahedron shown in Figure 4, atoms can be added on top of this 13-atom core in two different angular faces (F sites) and vertices (V sites). These F and V sites provide a total of 32 sites $(20+12)$, and full coverage produces a cluster with 45 atoms; this type of decoration can be denoted FC (face centered) as it emphasizes the coverage of the icosahedron. Alternatively, decorate edges (E sites) and vertices (V). These E and V sites provide a total of 42 sites $(30+12)$, and completion of this layer leads to the next Mackay icosahedron with 55 atoms; these structures are multilayer icosahedral (or MIC) structures. FC growth is favored at the beginning of building a shell, up to a point when a transition occurs because MIC growth becomes preferred. The cluster size for the transition depends slightly on the details of theatomic interactions in different materials. For Ni clusters, it occurs between $N=27$ and $N=28$.

The calculated minimum of $	ilde{\mu}$ at $N=55$ corresponds with a minimum in the measured magnetic moment at $N=56$. Also, the calculated minimum in the region $Ni_{28-37}$, can be correlated with the broad experimental minimum of $	ilde{\mu}$ in that region. The experiments also show a weak minimum at Ni$_{19}$, which can be tentatively assigned a double icosahedron structure (an icosahedron covered by an FC cap formed by six atoms, one in a vertex and the others in the five associated F sites)$^{52}$, although this local minimum does not show up in the calculations. Another weak feature is the drop of $	ilde{\mu}$ between Ni$_{22}$ and Ni$_{23}$, which has a counterpart in the structure of Ni$_{23}$ results by covering an icosahedron with two FC caps; its structure can be viewed as three interpenetrating double icosahedra. One may conclude with confidence that the minima displayed by the measured magnetic moments provide some support to a pattern of icosahedral growth.
