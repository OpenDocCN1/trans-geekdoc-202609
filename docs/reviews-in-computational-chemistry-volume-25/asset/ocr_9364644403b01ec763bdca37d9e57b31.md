We have studied triatomic compounds of general formula XUY, where X, Y = C, N, O, and U is the uranium in the formal oxidation state 4+, 5+, or 6. We have determined the vibrational frequencies for the electronic ground state of NUN, NUO, UO$^{2+}$, and UO$^{61}$ and have compared them with the experimental measurements performed by Zhou and coworkers.$62$

The electronic ground state of UO were found to be extensively.$^{63-65}$ The ground state was found to be $(5f\Phi)(7s)$,^3\Delta$ state. The lowest of grade symmetry, $^3H_{6g}$, corresponding to the electronic configuration $(5f)^2$ was found to be 3300 cm$^{-1}$ above the ground state. The computed energy levels and oscillator strength were used for the assignment of the experimental spectrum,$^{66,67}$ in energy ranges up to 32000 cm$^{-1}$ above the ground state.

The reaction between a uranium atom and a nitrogen molecule N$_2$ leading to the formation of the triatomic molecule UUN was investigated.$^{68}$ The system proceeds from a neutral uranium atom in $(5f)^3(6d)(7s)$,^3L$ state to the linear molecule UUN, which has a $^1\Sigma_g^+$ ground state and a final U(VI) oxidation state. The effect of spin-orbit coupling was estimated from points around the reaction. The system proceeds from a quinter state for U + N$_2$, via a triplet transition to the final closed shell molecule.

An eventual barrier for the insertion reaction is caused primarily by the spin-orbit coupling energy. The lowest electronic states of the CuO molecule were studied.$^{69}$ The ground state of linear CuO was found to be a $\Phi_2$ (a $\Phi$ state with the total angular momentum $\Omega$ equal to two). The calculated energy separation between the $\Sigma_2^+$ and the $\Phi_2$ states is -0.36 eV at the geometry of the $\Sigma_2^+$ state [(C-U) = 1.77 Å and (U-O) = 1.80 Å], and -0.55 eV at the geometry of the $\Phi_2$ state is the ground state of CuO. These results do not confirm the experimental results,$^{70}$ supported by some DFT calculations.

According to the results of Andrews and co-workers, the ground state of CuO shifts from a closed ground state to a triplet state, when going from a Ne matrix (analogous to free CuO) to an Ar matrix. Other groups are also working on the topic,$^{71}$ which remains under debate.

For the systems we described, a multifururational treatment is needed, especially in the case of UUO, where the ground state is not a closed shell and several electronic states are lying close to the ground state. In general the ground state and low-lying states of these systems are described in a satisfactory way in comparison with experiment with the CASSCF/CASPT2 approach, whereas the high-lying excited states are less accurate with experiment, because it becomes difficult to include all relevant orbitals in the active space.
