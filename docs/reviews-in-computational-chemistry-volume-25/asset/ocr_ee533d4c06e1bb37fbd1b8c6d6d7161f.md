# INTRODUCTION

In the last 15 years, computer simulation studies of the glass transition in
polymer melts have contributed significantly to advance our understanding of
this phenomenon, which is at the same time of fundamental scientific interest
d of great technical importance for polymer materials, most of which are
amorphous or at best semi-crystalline. This progress has been possible, on
tone hand, because of improved models and simulation algorithms and,
tother hand, because of theoretical advances in the description of the
structural glass transition in general.¹ Much of this development has been mir-
rored in a series of conferences on relaxations in complex systems the proceed-
ings which might serve as a good entry point into the literature on the glass
transition in general.²,³

Instead of providing a detailed overview of all simulation work per-
g on the glass transition in polymer melts, this review has two goals.
t first goal is to provide a novice to the field with the necessary background
to understand the model building and choice of simulation technique for stu-
dies of the polymer glass transition. In particular, a novice modeler needs to be
are of the strengths and limitations of the different approaches used in
t simulation of glass-forming polymers and to be able to judge the validity of the
original literature.
