The density of electronic states can be written

$D(\varepsilon) = \text{Tr}\delta(\varepsilon - H) \quad [A.2]$

where $\text{Tr}$ indicates the trace of the operator $\delta(\varepsilon - H)$. The moments $\mu^{(p)}$ of the density of states are defined as

$$\mu^{(p)} = \int e^{ip}D(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon = \text{Tr}H^p \quad [A.3]$$

These moments can be calculated using the tight binding approximation. Introducing a complete set of atomic orbitals $|x\rangle$ satisfying the equations

$[T + V_{ij}(\tilde{R}_i - \tilde{R}_j)]\phi_{i_j}(\tilde{R}_i) = \varepsilon_{ij}\phi_{i_j}(\tilde{R}_i) \quad [A.4]$

the moments $\mu^{(p)}$ can be calculated by expanding the trace

$$\mu^{(p)} = \sum_{i_1,i_2...i_p} \langle\phi_{i_1}|H|\phi_{i_2}\rangle\langle\phi_{i_2}|H|\phi_{i_3}\rangle\dots\langle\phi_{i_p}|H|\phi_{i_1}\rangle \quad [A.5]$$

and keeping only two-center-neighbor integrals. In addition, integrals $\langle\phi_j|V|\phi_i\rangle$ will be neglected in comparison with those of type $\langle\phi_i|V|\phi_j\rangle$. The sum in Eq. [A.5] goes over all paths of length $p$ that start and finish at a given atom, such that the electron hops between nearest neighbors.

If we work with the local (and orbital-dependent) density of states $D_{i_2i_2}(\varepsilon)$, moments $\mu^{(p)}$ can also be calculated

$$\mu_{i_2i_2}^{(p)} = \int e^{ip}D(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon = \sum_{i_1,i_2...i_p} \langle\phi_{i_1}|H|\phi_{i_2}\rangle\langle\phi_{i_2}|H|\phi_{i_3}\rangle\dots\langle\phi_{i_p}|H|\phi_{i_1}\rangle \quad [A.6]$$

Equation [A.6] shows a connection between the local bonding of an atom and its electronic structure. The density of states is then calculated from all the moments $\mu^{(p)}$. This theory offers a promising way of calculating the density of states. Many numerical methods are unstable, although the recursion method of Haydock$^{20}$ works efficiently. In this Green's function method, the local density of states is written in terms of the local Green's function $G_{i_2i_2}(\varepsilon)$ as

$D_{i_2i_2}(\varepsilon) = -\frac{1}{\pi} \lim_{\eta\to0} \text{Im}G_{i_2i_2}(\varepsilon + i\eta) \quad [A.7]$
