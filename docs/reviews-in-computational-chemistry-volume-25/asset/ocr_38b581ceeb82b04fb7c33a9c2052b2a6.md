# Tight Binding Approximation for the $d$ Electrons

The orbitals of the $d$ states in clusters of the $3d, 4d,$ and $5d$ transition elements (or in the bulk metals) are fairly localized on the atoms as compared with the $sp$ values of comparable energy. Consequently, the $d$ states are not much perturbed by the cluster potential, and the $d$ orbitals of one atom do not strongly overlap with the $d$ orbitals of other atoms. Intraatomic $d-d$ correlations tend to give a fixed integral number of $d$ electrons in each atom $d$-shell. However, the small interatomic $d-d$ overlap terms and $s-d$ hybridization introduceatomic charge fluctuations in each $d$ shell. In fact, a $d$ orbital contribution to the conductivity of the metals and to the low temperature specific heat is obtained only by starting with an extended description of the $d$ electrons.

The partially filled $d$ band of the transition metals, or the $d$ states in clusters, are described well by the tight binding (TB) approximation$^{11}$ using a linear combination of atomic $d$ orbitals. The basic concepts of the method are as:

+   (1) The lattice potential $V$ (or the cluster potential) is written as a sum of atomic potentials $V_i$ centered on the lattice sites $i$.
(2) The electronic states in the cluster (or in the solid metal) are expressed as a linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO).

```
$$\Psi(\varepsilon) = \sum_{i,m} a_{im}|im\rangle\quad\quad [8]$
```

The sum in $m$ goes from 1 to 5, because there are five different atomic $d$ orbitals $\phi_m$. In the usual notation, these orbitals are labeled $d_{xy}, d_{xz}, d_{yz}, d_{z^2-y^2},$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$. The normalized orbitals are eigenfunctions of the atomic Hamiltonian $T+V$ with energy $\varepsilon^0$. As a first approximation, the overlap integrals of the atomic orbitals across neighboring sites can be neglected.
