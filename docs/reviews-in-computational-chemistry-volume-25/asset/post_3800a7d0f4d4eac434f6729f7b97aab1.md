## FILTER-DIAGONALIZATION

Spectral methods, whether based on time or Chebyshev propagation, converge slowly because of the uncertainty principle. This behavior stems from the fact that the time or Chebyshev propagation is energy global, providing a uniform convergence in the entire energy range. Nehauser suggested an elegant scheme that combines propagation with diagonalization to overcome this shortcoming. The central idea is to first construct a set of primitive energies in a specified energy window via a propagation, which is denoted as filtering. These primitive bases need not be well resolved in energy. Consequently, the propagation length can be shortened significantly by solving a small local generalized eigenproblem via direct diagonalization. The original *filter-diagonalization (FD)* method of Nehauser was formulated based on time propagation and the time-conjugacy. It has been extended to other types of propagation.
