# Self-Assembled Monolayers

Self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) are highly ordered organic films that have been proposed as low-friction, protective coatings for devices such as MEMS/NEMS and computer drives.^{96 The influence of the chemical details of the SAM, such as chain length and chemical nature of the terminal group, have been investigated both experimentally^{97,98} and theoretically.^{99 As anticipated, it has been found that these chemical details have a significant effect on the frictional properties of SAMs. For instance, certain types of ends promote adhesion between two SAMs, increasing friction. Recent simulations have shown that the frictional aspects of SAMs are not only influenced by the chemical details of the film, but they can also be affected by the degree of order within the SAM.

In their study, Park et al.^{101} investigated the frictional properties of fluorine-terminated SAMs grafted to gold surfaces. The properties of the system were investigated by sliding SAMs past one another at velocities in the stick-slip regime under various external loads. Two types of fluorine-terminated SAMs emerged from the simulations. One exhibited low shear strength, whereas the other showed a much higher resistance to shear. Considering pressures above 400 MPa, we found that the low-shear SAM had $\mu_k \approx 0.12$, whereas the friction coefficient of high-strength SAM was approximately zero. The authors of this study investigated the origins of these differences.

The differences in the frictional aspects of the two SAMs were found to arise from differences in the structures of the films. Specifically, the high-strength SAMs exhibited a high degree of order, such that each terminal group of the SAM on the opposing surface was perfectly aligned. Thus, sliding chains of one SAM will lock with all of those on the opposite surface, which leads to a stick condition and high friction. The low-shear SAM, on the other hand, exhibited less order and only a fraction of the terminal groups would, lead to lower friction. Thus, the differences in the order of the SAMs account for the differences in the shear strengths of the films.
