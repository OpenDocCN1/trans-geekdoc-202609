Table 1 gives for the average distance, number of bonds per atom, the magnetic moment, and the binding energy of $Mn_2$ to $Mn_6$. Also are given the two spin gaps $\Delta_1 = E_{6}^{majority} - E_{6}^{minor}$ and $\Delta_2 = E_{6}^{majority} - E_{6}^{minor}$, which represent the energy required to move an electron from one spin-band to the LUMO of the other. The two spin gaps must be positive for the system to be magnetically stable.

In a Stern-Gerlach deflection experiment, Knickbeam$^{96}$ measured the magnetic moments of free $Mn_n$ clusters for sizes between $N=11$ and $N=99$. The magnetic moments were obtained from Eq.[2] using superparamagnetic behavior. The moment $\mu$ shows local minima for $N=13$ and $N=19$, which showed isoshedral growth in that size range; for larger sizes, $\mu$ shows a minimum in the range $Mn_{32}-Mn_{37}$ and a broad maximum in the range $Mn_{7}-Mn_{56}$, followed by a minimum at $Mn_{57}$. The maximum value of the magnetic moment found in the experiment was $\mu(Mn_{15})=1.4$ $\mu_B$, which is substantially smaller than the calculated moments in Table 1, and this is puzzling. The interpretation of the experimental results has been given by Guevara et al.$^{97}$ They performed TB calculations for Mn clusters for $Mn_{42}$ using several structural models (icosahedral, bcc, and fcc), and they obtained several magnetic solutions for each cluster size and structure. In general, the magnetic moments led to the suggestion that the structures are mainly icosahedral for $N<30$, and bcc structures compete with icosahedral structures for larger clusters. Jena and coworkers$^{98,99}$ arrived at similar conclusions for the magnetic ordering: Non-ferromagnetic ordering is responsible for the small moments measured for the Mn clusters.
