## Local Spin-Density Approximation (LSDA)

As in the non-spin-polarized case, the main problem with the spin-polarized method comes from our limited knowledge of exchange–correlation energy functional $E_{xc}[\rho_{\alpha\beta}]$, which is not known. However, $E_{xc}[\rho_{\beta}]$ is well known for a homogeneous gas of interacting electrons that is fully spin-polarized, i.e., $n(\vec{r}) = n_-(\vec{r}) = 0$ (and, of course, for a nonhomogeneous electron gas, with $n_+(\vec{r}) = n/2$; see above). As a result, von Barth and Hedin$^{30}$ proposed an interpolation formula for the exchange–correlation energy per electron in a partially polarized electron gas (the z-axis is taken as the spin quantization direction)

```
$e_{xc}(n,\xi) = e_{xc}^c(n) + [e_{xc}^F(n) - e_{xc}^c(n)]f(\xi) \quad [47]$
```

where the function $f(\xi)$ gives the exact spin dependence of the exchange energy

```
$f(\xi) = \frac{1}{2}(2^{1/3}-1)^{-1}\{(1+\xi)^{4/3} + (1-\xi)^{4/3} - 2\} \quad [48]$
```

In Eq. [47], $e_{xc}^c(n)$ and $e_{xc}^F(n)$ are the exchange–correlation energy densities for the nonpolarized (paramagnetic) and fully polarized (ferromagnetic) electron gas. The form of both $e_{xc}^c(n)$ and $e_{xc}^F(n)$ have been conveniently parameterized by von Barth and Hedin. Other interpolations have also been proposed$^{24,33}$ for $e_{xc}(n,\xi)$. For the homogeneous electron gas can be used to construct an LSDA

```
$E_{xc}[\rho_{\alpha\beta}] = \int n(\vec{r})e_{xc}(n(\vec{r}),\xi(\vec{r}))d^3r \quad [49]$
```

```
$V_{xc}^c(\vec{r}) = -\frac{\delta E_{xc}[\rho_{\alpha\beta}]}{\delta n(\vec{r})} \quad [50]$
```

## NONCOLLINEAR SPIN DENSITY FUNCTIONAL THEORY

In many systems of interest, the spin density is collinear; that is, the direction of the magnetization vector $\vec{m}$ is the same at any point of space. There are other systems, however, in which the direction of $\vec{m}$ changes in space, a well-known example being the $\gamma$-phase of solid Fe$^{34}$. Noncollinear magnetic configurations occur easily in systems with low symmetry or those that are disordered$^{35,36}$. One can then see the occurrence of noncollinear spin arrangements in clusters of the transition metals. Generalized LSDA calculations allowing for noncollinear magnetic structures have been performed for solids$^{37–39}$.

Implementation of the noncollinear formalism for clusters has been more recent$^{40,41}$ and uses as a basis the LSDA. When a exchange-correlation correlation functional is used, the key idea was introduced by von Barth and Hedin.$30$ One can divide the volume of the system into small independent boxes and consider that within each small box the electrons form a spin-polarized electron gas, whose densities are $n_{\uparrow}(\vec{r})$ and $n_{\downarrow}(\vec{r})$, the two real and positive eigenvalues of the spin-density $\rho_{\alpha\beta}$ matrix at $\vec{r}$. At each point one can then use a local coordinate system such that the $z$-axis coincides with the direction of the local spin. In this way, we can use the LSDA exchange and correlation functionals and calculate the exchange-correlation potential in this local diagonal frame. This strategy provides a local magnetization density approximation, which is similar in spirit to the LSDA. In the LSDA, the exchange-correlation potential is calculated by assuming the system behaves locally as a spin-polarized electron gas.
