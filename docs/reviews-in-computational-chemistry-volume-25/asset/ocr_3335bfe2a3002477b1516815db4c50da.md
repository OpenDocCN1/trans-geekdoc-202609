Despite the simplicity of the above scheme, however, a straightforward implementation in finite-precision arithmetic may cause severe problems in calculating the overlaps. The only circumstance that permits the direct application of Eq. [46] in practical calculations is when there is only a single covered copy, whose normalization is always maintained in finite-precision arithmetic.$^{38}$ When converted copies appear, the normalization of the approximate eigenvectors may not hold because of the loss of global orthogonality among the Lanczos vectors arising from round-off errors. Tests have shown that the norm of the approximate eigenvectors $\|b^{(K)}\|^2$ fluctuates over multiple converted copies.$^{38}$ On the other hand, any converted eigenvector $\mathbf{z}_i$, is nonetheless a good approximation of the true eigenvector of $H(\mathbf{b}_n)$ for a normalization constant.

Despite these problems, it was realized that one can still compute the overlaps accurately by using the formula:$^{38}$

```
$$\zeta_{m,n}^{(K)} = \sum_{i} |c_{m,i}^{(K)}|^2/N_n^{(K)}$ \quad [48]
```

where the sum runs over all converted copies $\{E_i^{(K)}\}$ of the true eigenvalue $E_n$. This is possible because of a remarkable observation by Chen and Guo on the Lanczos algorithm in finite-precision arithmetic;$^{38}$ i.e., the sum of the norms of all converted copies of an eigenvalue equals the following numbers:

```
$$\sum_{i} |\mathbf{b}_i^{(K)}\|^2 \cong N_n^{(K)}$ \quad [49]
```

For real-symmetric systems, the above relation holds up to machine precision despite the fact that individual copies are not normalized.$^{38}$ It also works reasonably well for complex-symmetric Hamiltonians.$^{102}$ Unfortunately, there has not yet been a formal proof of this striking observation of the Lanczos algorithm.

Equation [48] allows for the calculation of the squared overlaps, which are often sufficient for many problems. However, when the sign of an overlap is needed, it should be chosen to be of the so-called “principal copy,” which has the largest $|z_i|$ in the group. In many applications, the actual sign of the overlaps is not important as long as the same “principal copy” is used throughout the calculation.

The efficiency of the LLP method can be further improved. According to Eq. [46], both the $\zeta_{m,k}$ and all the Lanczos eigenvectors $\mathbf{z}_k$, are needed. Although the latter can be obtained by QL, its explicit calculation is unnecessary, as noted by Chen and Guo.$^{100}$ In particular, the overlaps $\zeta_{m,j}^{(K)}$ can be obtained efficiently and directly without the explicit calculation of the Lanczos eigenvectors, which can lead to substantial savings for recursions.
