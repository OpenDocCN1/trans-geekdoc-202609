## MEASUREMENT AND INTERPRETATION OF THE MAGNETIC MOMENTS OF NICKEL CLUSTERS

## Interpretation Using Tight Binding Calculations

Bloomfield et al.$^3$ have performed accurate measurements of the magnetic moments of size-selected $Ni_N$ clusters with $N$ between 5 and 700. Their results up to $N=60$ are plotted as black dots in Figure 2. The average magnetic moment $\bar{\mu}$ of the cluster shows an overall decrease with increasing cluster size, while oscillations are superposed on this decreasing behavior. In the small size range, for $N<10$, where $\bar{\mu}$ decreases most rapidly, there is a local minimum at $Ni_{16}$ and a local maximum at $Ni_8$. Thereafter, $\bar{\mu}$ displays a deep minimum for $Ni_{13}$ and another minimum at $Ni_{56}$. The latter is so close to $Ni_{55}$ that it is tempting to conclude that the Ni clusters grow following an icosahedral pattern (clusters with perfect icosahedral structure and two shells, shown in Figure 3, have 15 and 55 atoms, respectively)$^{44}$. A third important minimum occurs around $Ni_{34}$. The magnetic moment goes through a broad maximum between $Ni_{13}$ and $Ni_{34}$, and again between $Ni_{34}$ and $Ni_{56}$.

Theoretical studies attempting to rationalize the behavior of the magnetic moment of $Ni_N$ clusters$^{45–48}$ have relied on the tight binding method. The magnetic moments calculated by Aguilera-Granja et al.$^{46}$ for sizes up to $N=60$ are plotted in Figure 2. The calculations used the theory described above, with some simplifications. Local charge neutrality was assumed, leading to the following expression for the environment-dependent energy levels:

```
$\varepsilon_{r\sigma} = \varepsilon_{r\sigma}^0 + z_{\sigma}\sum_{\beta} \frac{J_{r\mu\beta}}{2} + Z_{r}\Omega_{\sigma}$ [56]
```

which is simpler than the expression in Eq. [16]. Two principles are useful when interpreting these results. The first principle is that the local magnetic moments of the atoms decrease when the number of neighbors (local coordination) decrease. The second principle is that the average magnetic moment of the cluster decreases when the intermediate distances decrease, which occurs because the width of the $d$ band increases. In metallic clusters, the average atomic coordination number increases with $N$. The average nearest-neighbor distance $d$ also increases with $N$, from the value for the molecule $d_{mol}$ to the value for the bulk $d_{bulk}$. The two effects oppose each other, and for that reason, the behavior of $\tilde{\mu}(N)$ in a growing cluster is complex. For $N\le20$, the geometries used to perform the calculations of the magnetic moments are obtained from molecular dynamics simulations using a many-atom potential based on the TB, with parameters fitted to the properties of $Ni_{12}$ and $Ni_{13}$. The geometries for $N=5-16$ are shown in Figure 4, which shows a cluster of icosahedral growth.

A qualitative agreement exists between the experimental and theoretical magnetic moments of small clusters. The TB calculations predict pronounced local minima at $Ni_6$ and $Ni_{13}$ and a local maximum at $Ni_8$. $Ni_{13}$ is an icosahedron with an internal atom at its center. The local atomic coordination of the surface atoms in $Ni_{13}$ is $Z=6$. On the other hand, $Ni_{12}$ and $Ni_{14}$ contain some atoms with coordination smaller than 6, and this leads to an increase of the local magnetic moments of those atoms. Consequently, the compact structure of $Ni_{13}$ explains the minimum of $\tilde{\mu}$ occurring at cluster size. $Ni_6$ is an octahedron formed by atoms with coordination $Z = 4$. In $Ni_7$, which has the structure of a flattened pentagonal bipyramid, the coordination of two atoms increases to $Z = 6$ and remains 4 for the rest of the atoms. Ni$_8$ has four atoms with coordination $Z = 5$ and five atoms with coordination $Z = 4$, which leads to a mean coordination number that is slightly smaller than in $Ni_7$. The coordination increases again in $Ni_9$. This behavior of the mean coordination number would lead one to a maximum of $\mu$ in $Ni_8$, which is indeed observed in the experiments and a minimum at $Ni_7$. The observed minimum and the calculated minimum occur at $Ni_6$, and the reason for this is that the larger average-neighbor-distance $d_h$ has a local maximum at $Ni_7$ counteracts the increase of the coordination number when going from $Ni_6$ to $Ni_7$ and produces the minimum of $\mu$ at $Ni_6$. To summarize, the oscillations of the average magnetic moment in Ni clusters can be explained by two purely geometrical effects: (1) compact clusters, which with high average coordination number, have small values of $\mu$; and (2) clusters with large interatomic distances have large $\mu$.
