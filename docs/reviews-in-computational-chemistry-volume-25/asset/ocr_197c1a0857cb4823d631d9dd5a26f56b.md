The remaining $d_{z}$ and $p_z$ orbitals with $\sigma$ symmetry relative to the Re–Re axis, the $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ with $\pi$ symmetry, the $d_{cy}$ with $\delta$ symmetry form the quadruple Re–Re bond. The system contains one $\sigma$ bond, two $\pi$ bonds, and one $\delta$ bond between the Re atoms. Because there are eight electrons ($Re^{3+}$ is $d^5$) to occupy these MOSs, the ground state configuration will be $\sigma^2\pi^4\delta^2$. The presence of the $\delta$ bond explains the eclipsed conformation of the ion. In a staggered conformation, the overlap of the $\delta$ atomic orbitals is zero and the $\delta$ bond disappears. The visible spectrum was also reported in these early studies. The notion of a quadruple bond is based on the inherent assumption that four orbitals are doubly occupied. Today we know that this is not the case for weak inter-metallic bonds. The true bond order depends on the relation between the occupation of the bonding and antibonding orbitals, respectively. Such description is, however, only possible if a quantum chemical model is used that goes beyond the single configuration Hartree-Fock model. The CASSCF method has been used to study this system, and it has demonstrated that the true bond order between the Re atoms is closer to three to four.

Because $Re_2Cl_6^{2-}$ is such an important entity in inorganic chemistry, we decided to study its structure and electronic spectrum using multiconfigurational quantum chemistry.$^{41}$ Scalar relativistic effects and spin-orbit coupling were included in this study. The geometry of $Re_2Cl_6^{2-}$ was obtained at the CASPT2 level of theory, and several excited states were calculated at this geometry. The calculations were performed using the active space formed by 12 active electrons in 12 active orbitals (12/12) (reported in Figure 3). It comprises one $\sigma$, two $\pi$, and one $\delta$ Re–Re bonding orbitals and the corresponding antibonding orbitals, and two Re–Cl $\delta$ bonding orbitals and the corresponding two antibonding orbitals.
