# 210 Magnetic Properties of Atomic Clusters of the Transition Elements

The angles $	heta(\tilde{r})$ and $\phi(	ilde{r})$ are calculated in such a way that $U$ diagonalizes the density matrix

```
$U(	ilde{r})ho(	ilde{r})U^+(	ilde{r}) = \begin{pmatrix} n_{\uparrow}(	ilde{r}) & 0 \ 0 & n_{\downarrow}(	ilde{r}) \end{pmatrix} \quad [52]$
```

$U^+(\tilde{r})$ is the adjoint (or Hermitian conjugate) of $U(\tilde{r})$. The exchange-correlation potential is then calculated in this local reference frame, in which it is a diagonal operator with components $V_{sc}^{\uparrow}$ and $V_{sc}^{\downarrow}$, and it must be transformed back to the original reference frame. The local spin rotation angles $	heta(\tilde{r})$ and $\phi(\tilde{r})$, the local azimuthal and polar angles of the magnetization density vector, are computed through the requirement of having the off-diagonal elements vanish in the matrix of Eq. [52]. The result is

```
$\phi(\tilde{r}) = -tan \frac{\text{Im}\rho_{\uparrow}(	ilde{r})}{\text{Re}\rho_{\uparrow}(	ilde{r})} \quad [53]$
```

```
$\theta(\tilde{r}) = tan^{-1} \frac{2[\text{Re}\rho_{\uparrow}(	ilde{r})]^2 + [\text{Im}\rho_{\uparrow}(	ilde{r})]^2]^{1/2}}{\rho_{\uparrow}(	ilde{r}) - \rho_{\downarrow}(	ilde{r})}} \quad [54]$
```

This leads to an exchange-correlation potential in the form of a $2 \times 2$ Hermitian matrix in space

```
$V_{xc}(\tilde{r}) = \frac{1}{2}(V_{sc}^{\uparrow}(\tilde{r}) + V_{sc}^{\downarrow}(\tilde{r}))\mathbf{I} + \frac{1}{2}(V_{sc}^{\uparrow}(\tilde{r}) - V_{sc}^{\downarrow}(\tilde{r}))\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot d(\tilde{r}) \quad [55]$
```

$d(r)$ is the unit vector in the direction of the magnetization $m(r)$. The presence of the second term in Eq. [55] effectively couples the $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ components of the spin. To interpret the magnetic properties, one can use the spin-density matrix of Eq. [35] to compute the magnetization density $m(r)$. Local magnetic moments $\mu_{at}$ can be associated with individual atoms by integrating each component of $\m(r)$ within spheres centered on the ions. A reasonable choice for the radius of those spheres is one half of the smallest interatomic distance in the cluster. This process overlaps between neighbor spheres; however, some interstitial space remains between spheres, and one should be aware of the fact that atom-centered spheres contain about 80–90% of the magnetization. It is worth stressing again that the method explained above has assumed local exchange-correlation functionals. A route that includes density gradients has been explored by Capelle and Gross.$^{43}$
