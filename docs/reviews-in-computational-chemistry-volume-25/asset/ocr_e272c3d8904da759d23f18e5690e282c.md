# Measurement and Interpretation of the Magnetic Moments

# Density Functional Calculations for Small Nickel Clusters

Small clusters of transition elements have been primarily studied using DFT the calculations became very time consuming for large clusters. Reuse and Khanna$^{61}$ have calculated $\mu$ for $Ni_N$ clusters with $N=2-6$, and 8. They found $\mu(	ext{Ni}_6) < \mu(	ext{Ni}_8)$ and $\mu(	ext{Ni}_{13}) < \mu(	ext{Ni}_8)$, which agrees with the experimental trend; however, the magnetic moments of $	ext{Ni}_6$ and $	ext{Ni}_8$ were nearly equal whereas the experiment indicates a larger $\mu$ for $	ext{Ni}_8$ (Fig. 2). Bouarab et al.$^{45}$ performed TB calculations with the same structures and interatomic distances used by Reuse and Khanna. Their magnetic moments differed by no than $0.06$ $\mu_B$ from the TB values of Fig. 2. Therefore, the differences between TB and DFT results were to be mainly ascribed to the treatment of electronic interactions.$^{46}$ Desmarais et al.$^{62}$ have studied $	ext{Ni}_2$ and $	ext{Ni}_8$. The same value $\mu=1.14\mu_B$ was obtained for the ground state and for all its low-lying isomers. Similarly, an average moment $\mu=1.0\mu_B$ was obtained for the ground state and for the low-lying isomers of $	ext{Ni}_8$. The sensitivity of the magnetic moments to atomic structure in $	ext{Ni}_2$- and $	ext{Ni}_8$, also found for $	ext{Ni}_4^{61}$, is striking. Reddy et al.$^{63}$ have calculated the magnetic moments for sizes up to $N=21$. For $N \le 6$, they employed ab initio geometries, and for $N>6$, geometries were optimized with the Finn–Sinclair potential.$^{64}$ Compared with the experiment, the calculated magnetic moments at different atomic sites within model were substantially lower, and infrequent discrepancies in the evolution of $\mu$ and $	ext{Ni}_{55}$ clusters with fcc structure and bulk interatomic distances. The octahedral shape was assumed for $Ni_{119}$ and the cubocthedral shape for $	ext{Ni}_{55}$. No significant differences were found between the magnetic moments on different surfaces, but the moments of atoms in the layer immediately below the surface were $0.2$ $\mu_B$ smaller than the surface atoms. The average magnetic moments $\mu(	ext{Ni}_{19})=0.58\mu_B$ and $\mu(	ext{Ni}_{55})=0.73\mu_B$ are significantly smaller than the measured moments. Pacchioni et al.$^{66}$ calculated the electronic structure of $	ext{Ni}_6$, $	ext{Ni}_{13}$, $	ext{Ni}_{19}$, $	ext{Ni}_{38}$, $	ext{Ni}_{55}$, and $	ext{Ni}_{17}$. Icosahedral structures were assumed for $	ext{Ni}_{13}$, $	ext{Ni}_{55}$, and $	ext{Ni}_{17}$ and structures with $	ext{O}_h$ symmetry for $	ext{Ni}_6$, $	ext{Ni}_{13}$, $	ext{Ni}_{19}$, $	ext{Ni}_{38}$, $	ext{Ni}_{44}$, $	ext{Ni}_{55}$, and $	ext{Ni}_{79}$ (in most cases, fragments of an fcc crystal). The convergence of $\mu$ to the bulk limit was not observed, despite the width of the $3d$ band being almost converged for $N=40-50$.
