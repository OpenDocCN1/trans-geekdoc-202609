# Local Spin-Density Approximation (LSDA)

As in the non-spin-polarized case, the main problem with the spin-polarized method comes from our limited knowledge of exchange–correlation energy functional $E_{xc}[ho_{\alphaeta}]$, which is not known. However, $E_{xc}[ho_eta}]$ is well known for a homogeneous gas of interacting electrons that is fully spin-polarized, i.e., $n(\vec{r}) = n_-(\vec{r}) = 0$ (and, of course, for a nonhomogeneous electron gas, with $n_+(\vec{r}) = n/2$; see above). As a result, von Barth and Hedin$^{30}$ proposed an interpolation formula for the exchange–correlation energy per electron in a partially polarized electron gas (the z-axis is taken as the spin quantization direction)

```
$e_{xc}(n,\xi) = e_{xc}^c(n) + [e_{xc}^F(n) - e_{xc}^c(n)]f(\xi) \quad [47]$
```

where the function $f(\xi)$ gives the exact spin dependence of the exchange energy

```
$f(\xi) = \frac{1}{2}(2^{1/3}-1)^{-1}\{(1+\xi)^{4/3} + (1-\xi)^{4/3} - 2\} \quad [48]$
```

In Eq. [47], $e_{xc}^c(n)$ and $e_{xc}^F(n)$ are the exchange–correlation energy densities for the nonpolarized (paramagnetic) and fully polarized (ferromagnetic) electron gas. The form of both $e_{xc}^c(n)$ and $e_{xc}^F(n)$ have been conveniently parameterized by von Barth and Hedin. Other interpolations have also been proposed$^{24,33}$ for $e_{xc}(n,\xi)$. For the homogeneous electron gas can be used to construct an LSDA

```
$E_{xc}[ho_{\alphaeta}] = \int n(\vec{r})e_{xc}(n(\vec{r}),\xi(\vec{r}))d^3r \quad [49]$
```
