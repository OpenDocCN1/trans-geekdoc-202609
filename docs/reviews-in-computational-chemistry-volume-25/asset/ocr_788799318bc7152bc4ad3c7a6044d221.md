In summary, from the latest experimental$^{103}$ and theoretical$^{98-102}$ works, a clearer picture of the magnetic properties of Mn clusters is emerging. The smaller cluster sizes ($N \le 6$), a strong competition exists between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic ordering of the atomic moments, which results in a near degeneracy between the two types of ordering. The calculations of Bovadova-Parvanov et al.${101}$ clearly illustrate this competition. Mn$_n$ is ferromagnetic, with a total $\mu = 10\mu_B$, but an antiferromagnetic state lies only 0.04 eV above it energy. Mn$_7$ is ferromagnetic with $\mu=10\mu_B$, but an antiferromagnetic state with a similar triangular structure and a moment of $5\mu_B$ exists only 0.05 eV higher in energy. The ground state of Mn$_4$ has a tetrahedral structure and is ferromagnetic with $\mu=20\mu_B$, but antiferromagnetic states with a similar tetrahedral structure exist 0.11 eV and 0.24 eV higher in energy, respectively. Mn$_5$ is antiferromagnetic with $\mu=3\mu_B$ (the structure and distribution of atomic moments are shown in Figure 13), but ferromagnetic and two other antiferromagnetic states lie within a small energy range of 0.05 eV above the ground state. The ground state, with $\mu = 0.6\mu_B$, could explain the result obtained in the Stern-Gerlach experiments of Figure 12.
