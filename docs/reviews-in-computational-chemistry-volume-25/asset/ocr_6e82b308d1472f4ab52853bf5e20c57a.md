One can observe in Figure 2 that the magnitude of the spin moments obtained by Aguilera-Granja et al.$^{46}$ is good, but Wan et al.$^{48}$ suggested that the result of Aguilera-Granja et al. is caused by the parameterization. Figure 8 shows the spin, orbital, and total magnetic moments obtained by Wan et al. Except for the smallest clusters, $\mu_{orb}$ varies between 0.3 and 0.5 $\mu_B$/atom, which represents a large enhancement (6 to 10 times) with respect to the orbital moments in Ni bulk. The orbital moment of the free Ni ($d^8s^1$) atom is $\mu_{at,orb}(Ni)=2\mu_B$. Therefore, a large part of the quenching of $\mu_{orb}$ is already manifest in the smallest clusters, as full rotational symmetry is lost. However, a substantial enhancement of $\mu_{orb}$ with respect to the bulk orbital moment is observed at $Ni_{60}$. The orbital moments depend on the choice of the correlation parameter $U$, $U=2.6$ eV gives the best agreement with the experiment. The positions of the minima depend on U, except the four minima indicated by the vertical lines in Figure 8. A comparison with the experiment is provided in Figure 9. The qualitative trend up to $N=28$ is reproduced approximately by the calculations. In the size range $9 \le N \le 28$, the calculated moments are in reasonable agreement with the experimental moments measured by Apsel et al.$^3$ (the discrepan-pancies between the two sets of experimental results are largest between $N=10$ and $N=20$). The calculated moments for $30 \le N \le 38$ are larger, by 0.1-2 $\mu_B$/atom, than those of Apsel et al., and they display the minimum at $Ni_{34}$.
