## Influence of s Electrons

An alternative model for explaining the behavior of the magnetic moment of Ni clusters has been proposed by Fujita. The interest of this model is because it may contain some ingredients required to explain the observed maxima of $\mu$. However, as a function of N, the model cannot predict the minima. It is intriguing that the observed maxima of $\mu$ are at N=8 and near N=20 and N=40. These numbers immediately bring to mind well-known shell closing numbers of alkali clusters. Electrons have never as independent particles in an effective potential with spherical shape in the alkali clusters. In that potential, the energy levels group in shells with degeneracy up to 2(J+1) caused by angular momentum and spin considerations. A realistic view of the effective potential, obtained for instance with the jellium background for positive ion charge,⁴ produces a sequence of electronic shells 1s, 1p, 1d, 2s, 2p, 2f, 2g, ..., where the notation for angular momentum character of the shells is given in capital letters (S, P, ...) to avoid confusion with s, p, and d orbitals. Clusters with closed electronic shells are special. These “magic” clusters contain a number of valence electrons $N_{magic}$=2, 8, 18, 20, 34, 40, 58, 66, ... have been observed for noble metal clusters.⁹ The configuration of free noble metal atoms are 3d¹⁰s, 4d¹⁰5s, and 5d¹⁰6s for Cu, and Au, respectively, and the interpretation of magic numbers in clusters is that these effects are caused by external shells.

In a similar way, the model for Ni clusters distinguishes between the magnetic moment of the cluster, and delocalized molecular orbitals derived from the atomic 4s electrons, it neglects hybridization between the 3d electrons and the delocalized 4s electrons. The delocalized 4s electrons are treated as moving in an effective harmonic potential. The energy levels of the delocalized 4s electrons lie just above the Fermi energy in many Ni clusters. But, as N grows, the states progressively move below the 3d band. The existence of some 4s states below the 3d band causes the presence of holes at the top of the minority-spin 3d band (the majority-spin 3d band is filled). The number of 3d holes is equal to the number of 4s states buried below the 3d band, because the total number of valence electrons for Ni atom is 10. The model assumes that the transfer of 4s states to below the 3d band abruptly when the number of delocalized 4s electrons is enough to completely fill an electronic shell in the harmonic potential. As a consequence of the stepwise motion, there is a sudden increase in the number of holes at the top of the minority-spin 3d band of the cluster, and because the number of holes is equal to the number of unpaired electrons in the cluster, an abrupt increase of the magnetic moment $\mu$ then occurs. The maxima of the magnetic moment observed in the Ni experiments near N=20 and N=42 could be related to this effect, because of electronic shells in s-like electrons at N = 20 and N = 40. On the other hand, the model predicts the maxima and the minima of $\mu$ that are too close, because of the assumption of the sudden transfer of a whole shell of electrons when the conditions of shell closing are met. This contrasts with experiment, where the maxima and the minima are well separated.

## Measurement and Interpretation of the Magnetic Moments

### Density Functional Calculations for Small Nickel Clusters

Small clusters of transition elements have been primarily studied using DFT the calculations became very time consuming for large clusters. Reuse and Khanna¹ have calculated $\mu$ for $Ni_N$ clusters with N=2-6, and 8. They found $\mu(\text{Ni}_6) < \mu(\text{Ni}_8)$ and $\mu(\text{Ni}_{13}) < \mu(\text{Ni}_8)$, which agrees with the experimental trend; however, the magnetic moments of $\text{Ni}_6$ and $\text{Ni}_8$ were nearly equal whereas the experiment indicates a larger $\mu$ for $\text{Ni}_8$ (Fig. 2). Bouarab et al.⁴ performed TB calculations with the same structures and interatomic distances used by Reuse and Khanna. Their magnetic moments differed by no than $0.06$ $\mu_B$ from the TB values of Fig. 2. Therefore, the differences between TB and DFT results were to be mainly ascribed to the treatment of interactions. Desmarais et al.² have studied $\text{Ni}_2$ and $\text{Ni}_8$. The same value $\mu=1.14\mu_B$ was obtained for the ground state and for all low-lying isomers. Similarly, an average moment $\mu=1.0\mu_B$ was obtained for the ground state of $\text{Ni}_8$. The sensitivity of the magnetic moments to atomic structure in $\text{Ni}_8$, also found, is striking. Reddy et al.³ have calculated the magnetic moments for sizes up to $N=21$. For $N \le 6$, they employed ab initio geometries, and for $N>6$, geometries were optimized with the Finn–Sinclair potential. Compared with the experiment, the calculated magnetic moments at different sites were substantially lower.
