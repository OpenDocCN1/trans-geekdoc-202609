The first example is from Starr et al.⁷⁶ who used the algorithm of Sastry and coworkers⁷¹ in their molecular dynamics simulation study of a super-cooled polymer melt. One of their main findings was that a quantitative connection exists between the average free volume and the dynamics of that system. Starr et al. demonstrated that the temperature dependence of the average monomer free volume (defined with a corresponding temperature-dependent Boltzmann diameter⁸) is well described by a power law that extrapolates to zero free volume at the same temperature that the dominant relaxation of the melt diverges. This simulation seems entirely consistent with the historical notion that the glass transition may be related to a vanishing free volume, which is a central tenet of free-volume theories of vitrification.¹³

Refuting the generality of this finding, however, is the work by Kkelberg, Ganesan and Truskett⁷⁵ which demonstrates that static free volumes do not correlate strongly with dynamics. These authors also used the same algorithm from Ref. 71 in conjunction with molecular simulations to prove the connection between free volume and the self-diffusivity of model colloids that interact via polymer-induced depletion attraction (for a detailed discussion of the model, see Refs. 86 and 87).
