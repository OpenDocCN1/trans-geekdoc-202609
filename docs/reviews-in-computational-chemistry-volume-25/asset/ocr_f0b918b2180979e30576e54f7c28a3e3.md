The magnetic moment per atom $\mu$ decreases for increasing cluster atoms and converges to the bulk value for clusters consisting of a few hundred atoms. This convergence is fastest for Ni clusters. However, in the three cases shown, oscillations are superimposed on the global decrease of $\mu$. Apsel et al.$^3$ have also performed high-precision measurements of the magnetic moments of nickel clusters with $N = 5$ to $740$, and more recently, values have been reported by Nickelbein.$^4$

Experiments have also been performed for clusters of the $4d and 5d$ metals, which are nonmagnetic in the bulk.$^5$ Rhodium was the first case of a nonmagnetic metal in which magnetism was observed in clusters. Magnetic moments were observed for Rh clusters with fewer than 60 atoms, but larger clusters are nonmagnetic. $Rh_{n}$ clusters with ten atoms have magnetic moments $	ilde{\mu} \approx 0.8\mu_B$; $	ilde{\mu}$ decays quickly for clusters of size $N = 10$ and $N = 20$, showing, oscillations or sizable magnetic moments for $Rh_{15}$, $Rh_{16}$, and $Rh_{19}$. This behavior differs from that of $Fe, Co,$ and Ni, where the variation of $	ilde{\mu}$ extends over a much wider range of cluster sizes (cf. Figure 1). In contrast to rhodium, clusters of ruthenium and palladium (with $N = 12$ to more than 100) are reported to be nonmagnetic.$^5$
