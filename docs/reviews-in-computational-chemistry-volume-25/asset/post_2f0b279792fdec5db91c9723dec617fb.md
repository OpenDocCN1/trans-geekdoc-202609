# Development and Uses of Artificial Intelligence in Chemistry

Hugh Cartwright

University of Oxford, Oxford, United Kingdom

# INTRODUCTION

A decade ago, artificial intelligence (AI) was mainly of interest to computer scientists. Few researchers in the physical sciences were familiar with it. However, in recent years, AI has moved into the mainstream, becoming one of the most important tools for researchers.

In some respects, it is still difficult to pin down exactly what AI is, but it is becoming clear that it is worth investing time. An artificial intelligence program is a piece of software that can learn. This chapter outlines how some of the most used methods work and what they can achieve.

Most AI used in science lies within three areas: evolutionary methods, neural networks, and knowledge-based systems. Additional methods, such as automated reasoning, hybrid systems, fuzzy logic, and case-based reasoning, are also of interest, but this review will focus on the methods that seem to offer the greatest near-term potential.

# EVOLUTIONARY ALGORITHMS

# Principals of Genetic Algorithms

As the name suggests, the inspiration for evolutionary algorithms is the evolutionary behavior of natural systems. Of the various types of evolutionary algorithms that exist, genetic algorithms (GAs), which have been reviewed previously in this book series, are currently the most widely adopted, and it is on this method we shall concentrate.

The use of iteration in solving problems is of course not limited to evolutionary approaches. Where the GA differs from other iterative techniques is in its simultaneous manipulation of many possible solutions to a problem, rather than just one.
