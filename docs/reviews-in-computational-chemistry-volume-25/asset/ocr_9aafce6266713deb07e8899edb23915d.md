The dimer effect controls growth up to N = 11; those clusters are formed by dimers with strong bond lengths and one isolated atom (Cr5, Cr7, Cr9), or two isolated atoms (Cr10) bonded to adjacent dimers. The structure of Cr11 is similar to that of Cr10 with an atom at the cluster center. The dimer growth routes up to Cr11, at which point the bond lengths suddenly increase and dimer bonds can no longer be identified for N > 11. The arrows in Figure 10 indicate the orientation of the atomic spins. There is an anisotropic distribution of the magnetic moments, but the strong tendency to antiferromagnetic ordering is clear, especially as N increases.
