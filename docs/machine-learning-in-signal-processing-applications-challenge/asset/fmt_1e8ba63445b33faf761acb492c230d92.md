Generally, the raw ECG signals contain noises due to physical and technical factors. Such noises occur due to electricity line interference, baseline wander, amplifier noise, electrode contact, electromagnetic, and polarization effects \[[26](#ch12.xhtml#r12_26), [27](#ch12.xhtml#r12_27) and [28](#ch12.xhtml#r12_28)\]. Hence, the denoising of ECG signals is very important to carry out further analysis. ECG signal analysis involves three basic processes: signal denoising, signal wave identification, and heart disease classification, as depicted in [Figure 12.4](#ch12.xhtml#f12_4) below. Wavelet-based ECG signal decomposing, denoising, and reconstructing signals promises to be an efficient technique \[[29](#ch12.xhtml#r12_29), [30](#ch12.xhtml#r12_30) and [31](#ch12.xhtml#r12_31)\].

### 12.4.1 Mexican Wavelet Transform

Mexican wavelet is considered as the second-order derivative of the Gaussian function as given in Equation (12.7) below. This type of wavelet is popularly used for data classification problems in large-scale data analysis \[[32](#ch12.xhtml#r12_32), [33](#ch12.xhtml#r12_33) and [34](#ch12.xhtml#r12_34)\]. The chirp signal of the Mexican hat wavelet is given below in [Figure 12.5](#ch12.xhtml#f12_5). For this case study, the central frequency of the Mexican Hat wavelet is considered at 0.2 Hz.

$$W\\left( t \\right) = \\left( {1 - t^{2}} \\right)\*e^{- {(\\frac{t^{2}}{2})}}$$  
(12.7)

### 12.4.2 Morlet Wavelet Transform

The Morlet wavelet is one of the popularly used wavelets for handling classification problem in IoT signals \[[35](#ch12.xhtml#r12_35), [36](#ch12.xhtml#r12_36) and [37](#ch12.xhtml#r12_37)\]. Equation (12.8) given below represents the Morlet wavelet:

$$W\\left( t \\right) = \\frac{1}{\\sqrt\[4\]{\\pi}}\*\\left( {e^{i\\omega\_{0}t} - e^{- {(\\frac{\\omega\_{0}^{2}}{2})}}} \\right)\*e^{- {(\\frac{t^{2}}{2})}}$$  
(12.8)

Here, `ω0` represents the central frequency of the original wavelet. In this study experiment, `ω0` is considered at 0.8 Hz. [Figure 12.6](#ch12.xhtml#f12_6) below depicts the chirp signal of the Morlet wavelet.

The chirps of frequency B-spline are depicted in [Figure 12.7](#ch12.xhtml#f12_7) below. The central frequency is considered as 1Hz. The expression for this wavelet is given in Equations (12.9) and (12.10) below.

(12.9)
