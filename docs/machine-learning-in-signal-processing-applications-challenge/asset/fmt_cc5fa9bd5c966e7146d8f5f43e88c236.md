#### 3.5.2.6 Maximum Margin Lines

To set the extreme margins, two parallel lines (margins) are added, and the distance is maximized to the original decision line. The errors or misclassified points (errors), points between the margins and the line, are considered. Large margin decision lines incline to have a lower overview error. In other words, models having small margins incline to be less disposed to overfitting [[43](#ch03.xhtml#r3_43), [44](#ch03.xhtml#r3_44), [45](#ch03.xhtml#r3_45), [46](#ch03.xhtml#r3_46), [47](#ch03.xhtml#r3_47), [48](#ch03.xhtml#r3_48), [49](#ch03.xhtml#r3_49), [50](#ch03.xhtml#r3_50), [51](#ch03.xhtml#r3_51), [52](#ch03.xhtml#r3_52), [53](#ch03.xhtml#r3_53), [54](#ch03.xhtml#r3_54), [55](#ch03.xhtml#r3_55), [56](#ch03.xhtml#r3_56), [57](#ch03.xhtml#r3_57) and [58](#ch03.xhtml#r3_58)].

The calculation of maximization function is done as follows:

$$\begin{array}{l} {w_{0} + w^{T}\\, x_{\text{pos}} = 1} \\ {w_{0} + w^{T}x_{\text{neg}}\,\, = - 1} \\ \end{array}$$

Subtraction of expressions leads to

`⇒ w^(T)(x[pos]−x[neg])=2`

the vector length w,

$$\left\| w \right\| = \sqrt{\sum\limits_{j = 1}^{m}{w^{2}{}_{j}}}$$

The procedure leads to the margin error expression

$$\frac{w^{T}\left( {x_{\text{pos}} - x_{\text{neg}}} \right)}{\left\| w \right\|} = \frac{2}{\left\| w \right\|}$$

`x[pos]` is the positive hyperplane, and `x[neg]` is the negative hyperplane. In the above expression, the margin has improvised. In reality, solution to quadratic programming can be obtained by minimizing the reciprocal term

$$\text{Margin} = \frac{1}{2}\left\| w \right\|^{2}$$
