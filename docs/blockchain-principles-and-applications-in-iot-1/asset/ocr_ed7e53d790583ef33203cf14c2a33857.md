The digital signature is usually utilized for ensuring authentication, non-repudiation, identity verification, and integrity verification [22]. A digital signature is used to communicate and trust between nodes in a distributed network of blockchains.

In a cryptocurrency system, digital signatures are used in transactions to prove that the spender possesses the cryptotoken. The cryptotokens provide digital signatures in their new transactions using their corresponding private key to verify that they possess the cryptotokens. This process creates a chain of digital signatures. This process hashes the content of the prior transaction and the address of the next owner. The data list is digitally signed with its own private key is added to the end of the transaction and transmitted to the receiver. The receiver must check the information in order to prove the owner's information, and then verify the owner of the transaction using their public key. The present owner, prior owner, and next owner of the cryptotokens are all recorded in each transaction on blockchain. This creates a chain of ownership for each transaction. As a result, the entire transaction of cryptotoken can be traced, successfully preventing double payment and faked transactions [23].

The digital signature currently used in the most of blockchains is "Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA)" [24]. The specific curve used by-chains for ECDSA is secp256k1, and it is an elliptic curve that has been specified by NIST [26]. ECDSA is mostly implemented for blockchains, as the fact that its key sizes are so small and it has better computational efficiency than RSA.
