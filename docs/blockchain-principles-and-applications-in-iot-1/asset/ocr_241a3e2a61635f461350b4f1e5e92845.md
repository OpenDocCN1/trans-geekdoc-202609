NIST organized the First Post-Quantum Cryptography Conference in April 2018 and on 30 January 2019 NIST announced 26 algorithms as the Round 2 candidates. A second Post-Quantum Cryptography Conference was held in August 2019. Recently, NIST announced the Round 3 Finalists. For digital signature algorithms, the finalists are CRYSTALS-DLITHIUM, FALCON, and Rainbow. The finalists of key-establishment and public-key encryption are SABER, NTRU, CRYSTALS-KYBER, and Classic McEliece.

The competition is expected to be finished before 2024 and draft standards should be available around that time. Blockchain designers should consider including these quantum-safe digital signature algorithms in their implementations or future upgrades. Recall that adding new cryptographic algorithms to a blockchain requires soft forks.

# 13.6 Zero-Knowledge Protocols

Cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin provide pseudo-anonymity. That is, anybody can see every transaction in the blockchain but know who belongs to real life. In order to obtain anonymity, users use non-cryptographic techniques like mixing in n people together and sign a multi-signature transaction to hide the receiver and the sender of a single transaction. Such techniques are not always practical and requires trust amongst the participants. Two protocols, Zeroin [10] and Zerocash [11], incorporate anonymity at the protocol level using zero-knowledge protocols. The main idea behind zero-knowledge protocols is that one party can prove they know a value x without revealing it.

Zerocash protocol uses two coins A and B to obtain anonymity. A user obtains anonymity by destroying their A and making some amount of B coins. Although this provides anonymity, transaction amounts are still visible in Zeroin. The cryptographic building blocks of Zerocash is the use of RSA accumulators which require a one-time trusted setup. A trusted party is needed for the choice of two primes q and p to create N = pq and destroy q and p. Thus, the security depends on the IFP. Anybody can create new coins without being detected. Zcoin was the first cryptocurrency that used this protocol but then moved over the Sigma protocol [35], which removes the trusted setup feature of the Zerocash protocol.

To provide anonymity, Zeroc uses zk-SNARKs [12], zk-SNARKs make zeroknowledge proofs more compact and easy to verify. This efficiency removes the necessity of having a basing that is used in Zeroin. Moreover, public ledger no longer contains transaction amounts in Zerocash. However, over 1 GB of random/secret public parameters are needed to set up Zerocash compared to a single public number N that is used in Zeroin. How else zero-knowledge proofs or protocols can be used in blockchain technology remains an open problem.
