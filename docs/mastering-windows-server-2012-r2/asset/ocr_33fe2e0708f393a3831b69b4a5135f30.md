# PDC Emulator FSMO

Finally, there's the PDC emulator FSMO. It's a important one.

Arbitrarily dubbing an AD domain's DCs as the "primary" DC then makes sense.
And although an AD domain is in mixed mode, the PDC emulator FSMO is more than just an emulator; it's the only DC that can accept changes.

But does mean that a PDC emulator becomes relevant once you're in native mode and have no pdc2000 book around at all. The PDC emulator serves two extremely important functions. Since we covered replication in the book, you probably know that replicating AD changes can change time—some significant amount of time. So, suppose the following happens: you're in St. Louis and need your help desk, which is unknown to you, in Ottawa. The help-desk person changes your password, and it seems all be well.

But what did the help-desk person change your password? Well, she probably did on a DC that was physically close to her, in other words, a DC in Ottawa. So an Ottawa DC knows your new password. How long will it be before local St. Louis DCs know your new password? Well, it could be hours. Does that mean you'll have to twist your thumbs for a few hours waiting for your new password to find its way to Missouri? Well, if you were talking about any attribute besides the password, the answer would be yes—but passwords are special.

When an admin changes a password on some DC somewhere, that DC immediately contacts the system acting as PDC emulator FSMO for that. So, the PDC FSMO always knows the most up-to-date passwords. When you try to log into the domain, it is a local DC that tries to log in. As you tell you your new password, the local DC is inclined to decline your login, because the password that you offer doesn't match the DC has. But before declining your login, the DC connects to the PDC emulator FSMO for its domain and double-checks, and if the password that you give your DC matches the new one that the PDC has, then you're logged in.

This "high-priority replication" also occurs for one other user attribute—account locks. Thus, when a user forgets his password and retries to log on with the wrong password over and over, then not only needs a new password but he probably locks himself out of his account. So, when the administrator resets the user's password, the admin probably also has to unlock the account. Immediately replicating the new password without replicating the account wouldn't be very.

That's important job for the PDC FSMO. What's the other one? We'll cover that in an upcoming section, "Time Sync".

# Transferring FSMO Roles

If you want to transfer an FSMO assignment to another domain controller, you are going to transfer the role. This process is pretty simple. As you saw earlier, you can view the FSMO assignment in three MMC snap-ins:

+   The Active Directory Domains and Trusts snap-manage the Domain Master role.

+   The Active Directory Schema snap-manages the Schema master role.

+   The Active Directory Users and Computers snap-manage the PDC Emulator, RID Master, and Infrastructure Operations Master roles.
