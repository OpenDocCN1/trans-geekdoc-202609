# THE THIRD DC

Any domain starts with a single writable domain controller. Companies often add a second DC for routine fault tolerance in case the DC fails. Although you're not required to have two DCs before creating an RODC, this is what most companies will do. If there's only one writable DC and it fails, the RODC can't be used to seize FSMO roles and won't hold the majority of powers.

In Chapter 22, "Adding More Locations: Sites in Active Directory," you learned about adding sites in Active Directory for different geographical locations of your enterprise. Any users in the remote office would have to use the slow 256 Kbps WAN link to log onto their computers. You could improve their login times by adding a domain controller to the remote office and configuring a site within Active Directory.

Moving the DC to the remote office will certainly improve the login times for users. However, it presents a significant security risk if the DC in the remote office is stolen or compromised.

A social engineer would probably not succeed if he came to your headquarters and told people there to pick up the DC for its annual cleaning. However, employees at a remote office may be convinced by a cunning attacker that a DC does not need to be taken away to be "cleaned.".

If an attacker were able to gain unrestricted physical access to the DC, he could gain access to passwords of every, including administrator accounts. However, if an RODC is placed at the remote location instead of a reader DC, the attacker will not be able to access all the passwords in the domain because RODC holds only a limited amount of data that can be encrypted.

With BitLocker, the drives would be useless to someone who doesn't have access. Make sure to encrypt your RODCs using BitLocker to limit concerns of physical theft. Many small offices that have only a limited number of personnel dedicated to tasks and duties are known to have weak security policies. We control your environment whenever possible. Badge access to the room dedicated to IT infrastructure, encrypt your machines, and use RODCs instead of full-blown DCs when the situation arises.

Another great feature RODCs is Administrative Role Separation (ARS). ARS gives administrators the ability to delegate domain-like rights to any user or group in the environment.
