# # Managing Permissions

Share permissions are applied when a user accesses a file or folder across the network, but they are not taken into consideration when a user accesses those resources locally, as they would be when sitting directly at the computer or when using resources on a terminal server. NTFS permissions, in contrast, are applied no matter how a user accesses those same resources, whether they are connecting remotely or logging in at the console. So, when accessing files locally, only NTFS permissions are applied. When accessing those files remotely, the permissions of both share and NTFS permissions is applied by calculating the most restrictive of the two types. For more information about NTFS, see Chapter 13, "Files, Folders, and Basic Shares."

# ## Creating Share Permissions

Share permissions are possibly the easiest form of access control you will deal with in Windows Server. Remember that share permissions take effect whenever you travel to access a computer over the network. Consider share permissions to be a kind of access to a secure building. When you walk up to the front door and show your identification, the guard looks up your name and gives you "level 1 access," then your pass will get into every door at level 1—and nowhere else. Inside, if you get into a room with level 2 access, it won't work. By defining share permissions, you can control the access level for each person at the front door.
