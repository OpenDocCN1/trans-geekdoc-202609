.Test.com. Creating a domain causes AD to build a lot of data structures—a domain for Ecost.Test.com—causing more work for the global catalog, changes to the overall forest AD database, and so on. Imagine that two people both try to create a new domain named Ecost.Test.com at the same time could be a nightmare—the parent domain would be receiving conflicting requests to modify the AD database, there might be potential security issues, and it might keep the whole forest from functioning.

# Domain Naming: A FSMO Example

To avoid the situation of duplicate names and to ensure the forest structure, Microsoft developed one DC to act as sort of central clearinghouse for domain creation, whenever you attempt to create a new domain or new name AD, DS stops and locates the DC in the first forest that is the “keeper of the names.” That DC is the domain-naming FSMO or domain-naming operations master. If AD on the new DC does not contact with the domain-naming FSMO, then it refuses to go further.

Remember, if you have a worldwide enterprise with dozens of domains, hundreds of offices all around the world, thousands of domain controllers, and hundreds of workstations, there is one and only one computer that serves as the domain-naming FSMO. If it were, in the Okinawa office and you in the New York office sitting at a server to create a new domain in the forest, then your computer would be unable to proceed until it contacted the Okinawa computer and got its OK on building the new domain.

# Why Administrators Must Know about FSMOs

In general, you need to know that the DCs act as FSMOs in your forest much all, and you need to do a little planning about which DCs will be FSMOs, and you need to know how to assign a particular FSMO role to a particular DC.

That reminds us, how to manage the FSMO roles by hand. The AD automatically picks a DC to act in each FSMO role—the first DC that you install—but it’s not right enough to manage those roles alone. For example, consider this scenario. Your company decides to plan with AD and sets up this first DC on a “junk” machine in a test-lab, the 1 GHz system with 1 GB of RAM.
