Cyberwarfare is performed at the state level which is approved by the national security. As cyber-warfare security is related to national security, there are several queries that are raised with regards to performing these cyber-attacks. Those questions include,

- If it is moral for a state to initiate and infiltrate an adversary’s systems which have threatened its political and territorial integrity, if yes, to put forth the general rules and guidelines to be followed to perform those attacks.
- The ways by which both the electric power and the telecommunications department that serve the military functions and the civilian functions of a country be attacked by this cyber-warfare.
- Another significant question which was raised are the ways by which a country reacts to the cyber-attacks that are performed by another country.
- And if there are any chances that a country will be able to attack a third country whose computer networks have been compromised or exploited to facilitate the assault?

The second area where the ethical dilemma is faced involves acts that are carried out in the interest of a certain social cause or of a political party. “Hacktivism” is the term coined to represent this area of conflict since it signifies the convergence of hacking with activism.

If the attack is planned with the motive of causing significant harm to civilians and cause widespread terror, they are termed as “cyberterrorism.” It is a term coined to signify the confluence of cyber-attacks with terrorism. Cyberterrorism can be unambiguously labeled as heinous and immoral; however, hacktivism raises ethical dilemmas. For instance: can a cyber-attack be considered as ethical when the hackers take down a website which is mainly used to trade child pornography, or a cyber-attack which supports terrorist operations or performing attacks on stolen credit card numbers trafficking.

The ways how a hacktivist who protest against the practices and policies that are framed by the government to deface a website or to perform a web “sit-in.” The rights of a hacktivist to attack a vulnerable system which aids in disclosing the security holes which in turns aims in making the internet more secure.

The third area where the ethical dilemma is encountered is the ethics of cyberdefense, specifically known as the “strike back,” “active response” or “hack back.” The third area of cyber defense also demonstrates the ways by which the system administrators (victims) react and respond to the attack and to know if the attack is coming from systems that may themselves be victims of compromise. As most of the cyber-attacks are directed through the chains of compromised machines, can a victim “hack back” along the chain in order to determine the source?

However, rather than exploring for answers to the above questions, this chapter details the various ethical issues in each of these areas of cyberconflict and create a framework by which all the questions can be addressed effectively.

With the help of examples, principles have been explained; however, the aim here is not to reach a final moral conclusion. That would require a much more detailed examination of the specific cyber-attack taking into account the motive and the background.

The framework presented here is based on the international law of armed conflict. This law was formulated to acknowledge and redress the threats of armed attacks and the usage of primarily armed force. However, some efforts have been channeled into making it relevant in the domain of cyberconflict. For a better understanding of the framework, the law has been segregated into two parts:

- jus ad bellum, or the law of conflict management; and
- jus in bello, or the law of war.

Despite being stated as “laws,” the two segments of this law details more about the ethical behavior in a cyberconflict as these ethical behaviors act as the rules of law.

Ethics is a field of study that provides a guidance of what is right and what is wrong, governs a person’s behavior and helps in taking a moral stand. Ethics primarily examines the morality of social structures, policies, laws, and human behaviors of a nation. The primary work of ethicists is to justify their moral judgments by reference to ethical principles of theories which tries to retain and capture the moral intuitions about what is right and wrong. The most common theoretical approaches are of two types, namely:

- Consequentialism; and
- Deontology.

As per the consequentialist approach, actions that lead to bad consequences can be termed as wrong. Whereas, deontological approaches assume that the moral duties of an individual have to be viewed independently of the consequence of their actions, whether good or bad.

Mostly, ethical principles and legislations are interconnected with each other. But it is found that it is not possible for a legislation to function as a substitute for morality. It is for this reason that individuals and corporations are always required to consider not only the legality but also the morality of their actions. Ethical analysis of privacy and security issues in the field of Information Technology (IT) took place primarily in computer ethics that emerged in the late 1980s.

The moral or ethical analysis should be included to address these ethical problems and questions: analysis aimed at a study of why certain issues can be termed as morally right or morally wrong and resolving the same and also reasoned views behind making these judgments. The aim of moral analysis is to provide clear knowledge and bring an equilibrium between several rights, interests, values. ## 6.2. CODES OF ETHICS

The study on the code of ethics provides a broader social consciousness of “Cause No Harm” and improves the quality of work which in turn reduces the dangerous outcomes. The code of ethics can be studied in detail under two categories, they are:

- Association for Computing Machinery (ACM); and
- Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE).

> According to Landau in 2008, “An ACM member shall consider the health, privacy, and general welfare of the public in the performance of the members work. An ACM member, when dealing with data concerning individuals, shall always consider the issue of individual privacy and seek the following: to minimize the data collection; to limit authorized access to data; To provide proper security for the data; to determine the required retention period of the data; to ensure proper disposal of the data.”

The Institute for Certification of IT Professionals (ICCP) has a code of ethics IT professionals have to adhere to. The code underlines the importance of possessing high standards of skill and knowledge, necessitates the maintenance of confidentiality, and that the IT professional will recognize the public reliance upon the standards of conduct and established practices.

The code of ethics which the members of the Association of IT Professionals (AITP) are required to follow are:

- To promote management's understanding of information processing;
- To refrain from dishonest practices and act with dignity;
- Contribute to the best of their capacity in sharing IT knowledge;
- To maintain confidentiality;
- Base actions on ethical and moral principles that they have acquired in educational institutions;
- Be faithful to their employers and safeguard their interests; and
- To value their countries and the way of life of those countries.

The above seven values are the key concepts of the code of ethics that consist of justice, utility, respect for individuals, consistency, autonomy for all, integrity, and competence.
