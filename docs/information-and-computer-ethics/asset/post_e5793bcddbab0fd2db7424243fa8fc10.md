## 3.3. NORMATIVE ETHICAL THEORIES

Public perception of these standards varies; hence, the various normative theories of ethics emphasize on what they claim makes an action a moral action: on what things are good or bad, and what kind of behavior is right as opposed to the wrong (Figure 3.4).

Two normative theories are discussed in this chapter. They explain three different sets of ideas about ways of ethical living.

### 3.3.1. Deontological Theory

The terminology of deontology has been generated out of the Greek Deon, which means the responsibility or the compulsions, essentiality as well as with the process of binding. In general, such words, when get translated, turn out be as a duty, so the various theories that are grounded on the deontology try to establish what one’s moral duties are. Once these moral duties have been enumerated, those duties are the foundation of moral ‘rules.’

Deontological systems emphasize that the primary focus should be on confirming to duties and rules because that is where the moral worth of an action lies. Evaluating the consequences of the action is secondary.

Kant would have argued that for an action to be considered as moral, extrinsic reasons, such as personal gains, should be neglected and the action should be done solely as an obedience to a rule. In his example, Kant explains, a grocer cannot be called as kind, if he is good to his customer, just to earn their loyalty.

If he does consider being kind, his moral duty, any moral goodness of his action is negated. The grocer should not have an ulterior motive and should be kind because of its intrinsic goodness. It can be deduced that ethics based on the deontological possess the capability of leading the various individuals to perform in the manner, which tends to bring in the negative repercussions.

### 3.3.2. Consequential Theory

According to the Consequentialist theories of ethics, consequence of an action should be the basis to judge the moral worth of an action. Thus, in case of an action, being good or bad is generally the one, which helps in generating the great conclusions in totality, within the specified condition.

There are, therefore situations where a consequentialist would be prepared to lie if that was thought necessary to bring about the best consequence(s).

The theory that is grounded on the repercussions and that too well-known, is Utilitarianism has been acknowledged as the great instance of the act-consequentialism, wherein all the correct actions have been considered as those, which helps in maximizing the pleasure of the perceptive beings, which means that all the individuals who can reason, feel, think as well as possess an experience.

In comparison to this, the theories which are based on the deontology usually tend to possess their major concentration on the essential or important value belonging to the rule itself, within the ethics belonging to the consequences, the major focus is on the basic importance of such repercussions of the provided rule. No act can be categorically termed as right or wrong.

### 3.3.3. Teleological Theory

The term “Teleological” is derived from the Greek word “telos,” which means ‘goal,’ ‘end’ or ‘purpose.’ This word is already known to the people in relation with the Design Argument for the presence of God, which is also called as the Teleological Argument, as it claims that the purpose of God is visible in what is observed about the universe.

Within ethics, the basic conclusion, the objective or the generic reasoning, which all the researchers generally tend to find has been referred to as the provided obligations which are required in the process of achieving the specified objectives based on the morality or the ethics or the end results.

For the theories based on the teleological ethics, thus, in case one required to seek for the way as to how one should be conducting oneself in accordance with respect to the morality or ethics, then that person is required to make a decision as to what all objects they should be possessing in terms of the ethics. Such theories can also be considered as consequentialist and thus are being headed in the direction of the set objectives or the goals or the specified purpose, a theory based on the teleology, might also consider to have an achievement of the great repercussions in any of the provided condition. This will follow the simple reason that to achieve the best consequence in any particular situation will generally contribute to the overall goal.

### 3.3.4. Character-Based Theory

Many of the researchers have observed that the various theories based on the repercussions as well as the deontology are basically act-centered, and thus, they tend to label them as the specified acts, being positive or negative, correct or wrong.

Being a dissimilarity to this, the ethics or the morality which is based on the character, is considered to be agent-centered that means that the righteousness is not just pertaining within the act but within that specified person, wherein the researchers tend to label or observe them if the agent is a great or honorable person in accordance with their respective habits as well as by their character.

In the theory of Kant, a person doing good acts morally by habit is not considered as good morally, as the moral goodness needs the moral agent to resolve what should be done in accordance with the reason.

Having a consideration of an instance: there pertained a eulogy regarding a lady, some years back, in one of the provided communities belonging to the church, who had been addressed as being the most honorable person as she was good to everyone, habitually, in spite of judging them for who they are.

## 3.4. PROFESSIONAL ETHICS

The ethics belonging to the business or the corporate world presents with the advice regarding the connection between the various experts or the professionals in a way that they could be able to assist each other as well as the entire pertaining society in the way which is best suited, without the possession of any fear of the other professionals that tends to weaken them with the actions that are considered to be less ethical (Figure 3.5).

Codes similar to these are being accessible in various professions, and thus, are considered to be distinct from that of the moral codes that were supposedly utilized within the academics as well as the religion of the whole of the extensive society.

Ethical codes are more specialized than moral codes, more internally consistent, and typically simple to be applied by an ordinary practitioner of the profession, without the need for extensive interpretation.

The Hippocratic Oath is one of the most primitive codes of professional ethics that provided rules for the ethical performance of the physician so as not to harm their patients. The oath, as well as the complete code, is written in the first person.

The recent professional codes possess the identical characteristics or the attributes, presenting with the specification as to what all will be the predominant responsibilities as well as to whom the professional possesses, as clearly or tacitly as possible.
