### 1.2.2. The Product Ethics of Information Sector

IE is based on the premise that information may be considered as a product. Ethics encompasses moral issues that surface in the context of legislation, misinformation, accountability, propaganda, advertising, plagiarism, testimony, and liability among others and more generally of pragmatic rules of communication à la Grice. Kant's analysis of the immorality of lying is one of the best-known case studies in the philosophical literature concerning this kind of IE.

A classic example of how improper management of information can have disastrous results can be understood by studying the case of Trojan War where Laocoon and Cassandra were not successful in ensuring that the Trojans exercised proper caution against the Greeks.

“What is right?” and “What is wrong?” has been a pressing question for human beings for centuries. In their everyday life, differentiating right from wrong has been challenging for almost every man and woman. The outcome of this has been endless debates, conflict of opinions, deliberations, and arguments. These discussions have only intensified with the passage of time. The birth of “Information Society” and extraordinary progress in Information and Communications Technologies (ICTs), has added a new chapter in the on-going debate on what is right and what is wrong.

Information professionals now have a huge responsibility to differentiate right from wrong and also determine what is ethical and what is not. It is extremely challenging to determine if an action is ethical or unethical. Informational professionals are burdened with this responsibility, and they are now trying to come up with solutions in numerous ways.

Information has now proliferated in all branches of information. The widespread use of ICT tools and techniques have raised very pertinent points about security, accessibility, right to express, privacy, and other related questions. As a result of this, it is of vital importance for information professionals to resolve all issues related to the ethical use of information and determine new ways to assist their clients in a fair and ethical manner.

## 1.3. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN ETHICS AND INFORMATION ETHICS

Ethics often referred to as moral philosophy, is that division of philosophy that is concerned with systematizing, defending, and providing guidance on ethical and unethical conduct. Moral behavior merits doing the right things and taking the right action, irrespective of the situation. However, it is very difficult to decide on what is the right thing to do and what is wrong. Ethics is concerned with providing reasonable justification for our decisions. It evaluates if an action is moral or immoral.

The role of information in routine decision-making and the advancement of our society is significant. The absence of precise and timely information will make it very challenging to make the right choices and decide the course of action. Ethics facilitates in the establishment of a code of conduct that can be applied in particular situations. Ethical principles also aid in setting up standards that can guide information professionals.

The omnipresence of information in all fields of human exertions has given rise to new challenges for the human. The problem now is the presence of an exorbitant amount of information as opposed to the lack of it. In the present situation, it has become extremely difficult to use information judiciously to resolve our routine challenges and decision making.

IE is expected to pave the path for problem-solving and utilizing information productively. IE is concerned with the ethical conduct of the users of information. It reinforces their accountability and obligation. It deals with the moral questions emerging from the use of information and the related activities during its lifecycle including creation, protection, distribution, and possibly destruction.

The task of providing information is not very challenging and tough. There is now abundant ICT tools and techniques at our disposal. The information landscape has evolved into a very intricate and complicated information network. Also, the demand of information users all over the globe have now varied and broadened. These factors have complicated the situation. Hence, there is an additional endeavor, of distributing information to the right person at the right time in the right manner. And this is not an easy task anymore.

One disturbing fact is the presence of ‘information divide.’ This is a result of inequalities in wealth creation and distribution among the various countries. This has manifested in generation and use of information also.

## 1.4. NORBERT WIENER’S FOUNDATION OF INFORMATION ETHICS

In 1950, Wiener wrote a book on the probable effects of information technologies. It was called ‘The Human Use of Human Beings,’ and it covered fundamental human values like wealth, health, contentment, liberty, opportunities, and capabilities. The book is relevant even today. This decade can be called the “Internet Age” and is characterized by the perennial search for “global IE.”

The notions and measures suggested by Wiener can be used to recognize, evaluate, and find a solution to all social and ethical dilemmas that have arisen with the emergence of information technologies. These technologies encompass all mediums of information like computer, laptops, television, mobiles, books, and libraries, media, and journalism.

“IE is a very appropriate name for this branch of study founded by Wiener since his philosophy can be applied in all types of information technologies. Computer ethics has emerged from the field of IE that was founded by Wiener. Wiener evaluated many issues in his works many years ago that are pertinent even in the present era (see Wiener 1948, 1950; 1954, 1964):

> “Computers and unemployment, computers, and security, computers, and learning, computers for persons with disabilities, computers, and religion, information networks and globalization, virtual communities, teleworking, the responsibilities of computer professionals, the merging of human bodies and machines, ‘agent’ ethics, artificial intelligence (AI), and a number of other issues” (see Bynum, 2000, 2004, 2005, 2006).

Wiener’s work on IE was based on the branch of science referred to as Cybernetics. Cybernetics is concerned with the way human brains and electronic machines work and invent machines that can function like the human brain. Wiener identified “great principles of justice” that all civilizations should adhere to. He formulated a feasible approach to evaluate IE issues and find a solution for the same as and when they occur. Wiener did not intend to establish a new branch. Rather IE was founded incidentally as a branch of academic research.

The field simply emerged from the many ethical remarks and examples that he included in his writings on cybernetics. The term “cybernetics” was derived from the Greek word for the pilot of a ship. Wiener came up with this term for this new branch of science. However, the terms like “IE” and “computer ethics” which subsequently arose from his research.

Although he had published his book, The Human Use of Human Beings in 1950, that dealt with the moral and social consequences of cybernetics and electronic computers, Wiener did not call his work an offshoot of applied ethics. Neither did he describe what he was doing as the creation of a new branch of applied ethics nor did he give an explanation on the purpose of his work.

To understand Wiener’s foundation for IE, therefore, we must observe what he does in his works, rather than look for metaphilosophical explanations about his intentions. Wiener’s work on human nature focused on the physical anatomy of man and the power of human physiology in facilitating creative actions and opportunities for learning. He provided clarifications about this potential by comparing the physiology of humans and less evolved organisms:

> "Cybernetics takes the view that the structure of the machine or of the organism is an index of the performance that may be expected from it. The fact that the mechanical rigidity of the insect is such as to limit its intelligence while the mechanical fluidity of the human being provides for his almost indefinite intellectual expansion is highly relevant to the point of view of this book. Man’s advantage over the rest of nature is that he has the physiological and hence the intellectual equipment to adapt himself to radical changes in his environment. The human species is strong only insofar as it takes advantage of the innate, adaptive, learning faculties that its physiological structure makes possible.” (Wiener, 1954, pp. 57–58, italics in the original).

### 1.4.1. Wiener and His Account of a Good Life

According to Wiener, human beings are very intelligent and should exercise their decision-making power to control their own lives and live well. They must have the liberty to engage in innovative and malleable actions, and this should result in maximizing their full potential. This should be the objective of human lives. It is obvious that one person’s accomplishment will vary from any other individual’s accomplishments. This is because different human beings will have different levels of ability and aptitude.
