### 2.2.1. The Fundamental Character of Information Technologies

In the initial stage of the revolution of IT, Richard Mason had provided with the suggestion regarding the upcoming transformations or the changes within the information technologies, for instance: their respective parts or the roles in the effects of education as well as economy that would be necessitated the process of rethinking about the social contract.

Various researchers got really worried that they would further challenge the privacy, accuracy, property, and accessibility (PAPA) and to secure their society, they “... should frame a new social contract, one that assures everyone the right to complete their own human potential” (Mason, 1986, p. 11).

The legal theorist Larry Lessig provided them with the warning that the rate at which the changes are taking place in IT is so fast that it would be leaving the steady as well as the contemplative procedures of policies, related to the law and politics, behind, and thus, being an outcome of all these, such technologies would certainly become extralegal or lawless.

This is because of the fact that when a law is written to restrain, few forms of the copyright infringement enabled by a specific file-sharing technology, that technology has become outdated and users are on to something else that enables even more copyright infringement (Lessig, 1999). Information technologies store, communicate, organize, and synthesize the information. For instance, a book is a kind of information record, a telephone is utilized to communicate information, and the Dewey decimal system categorizes the information.

Ultimately, the major focus would not be just on how the IT possess an impact on the moral institutions but also how it would be leading to certain changes or transformations within the respective nature of the moral reasoning.

Many things have been discussed about the information, being the technology of morality as well as how the researchers would be programming those applications along with the robots to have an interaction with them, in a manner, which would be morally acceptable.
