
Information ethics (IE) is that branch of ethics which outlines the connection between the collection, organization, and use of information with the ethical standards and moral codes that govern the conduct of an individual in the society. It also highlights the development of computer ethics which has now become essential for every individual to understand. ## 1.1. INTRODUCTION

In common parlance and professional context, all written works on ethics present an incomprehensible range of insinuations and implications. Ethics and morals are two terms that are often used in place of each other. This is because the distinction between them is ambiguous. Morality describes customs, ideals, and principles, that define and bring about a change in the society and guides an individual or community to differentiate right from wrong. The language of ethics is how we translate these values and principles in our conversations.

Aristotle was the forerunner in establishing ethics as an indispensable aspect of morality. He founded ethics as an academic discipline. Rafael Capurro, the head of International Center for Information Ethics (ICIE), is of the opinion:

> "As self-referential process ethics is an unending quest on explicit and implicit use of the moral code that is to say of respect or disrespect, with regard to individual and social communication. In other words, ethics observes the ways we communicate with each other as moral persons and the ways this moral identity is understood. There is, indeed, no unbiased, ethical observer."

Information and communication technologies (ICTs) have overwhelmingly altered many facets of life, including the nature of entertainment, work, communication, education, health care, manufacturing, business, social relations, and conflict. ICTs have had, and continue to have, unfathomable, and profound impact on our moral lives and on present-day ethical debates (Floridi, 2006). A fundamental change can be seen, over the last decade, in the dissertation of experts in the field of information science.

The point of focus has shifted from a primary concern with technique (which includes topics like acquisitions and moving through cataloging and conservation to user education and beyond), towards other diverse issues in which the ethical dimension is more dominating (intellectual property, user privacy, and serving the socially excluded)

The last decade has witnessed a rudimentary change, in the desertions of specialists, in the field of information science. The focus of this chapter has shifted from a predominant concern with technique to an engagement with a range of issues in which there is an ethical dilemma involved. By techniques, we mean topics like acquisition, cataloging, and conserving to user education and much more than that. The range of issues that is the primary concern are intellectual property, user privacy, and catering to all strata of the society.

Only a critical examination of information science literature will reveal if this suggestion holds true or not. The evaluation would be a challenging task given that the majority of the professional conversations, be it written, spoken or electronic, is very technical and is not expected to change in the near future. The literature of theory still sets out to discuss information as a scientific phenomenon, rather than a product of moral action.

It is vital to analyze implicit values from different perspectives and standpoints before formulating and executing information policies. It was during the Second World War that IE got recognition in the academics. This academic field was not planned but was born inadvertently. Norbert Wiener, a philosopher, and a scientist, were working in collaboration on some cutting-edge technologies including the invention of digital computers and radar. They were also working on a novel antiaircraft cannon with the following features:

- The aircraft could detect the presence of an airplane;
- It could collect all necessary data about the airplane like its speed and flight path;
- It could envisage what position the airplane would be in a few seconds;
- It could estimate the target and calculate where to fire;
- It could have decision-making skills.

The actions were to take place almost immediately and that too without human involvement. Wiener had extraordinary insight and foresight. He had the foresight to realize that the new inventions in the field of science and technology that he and his colleagues were working on would have "enormous potential for good and for evil."

Wiener prophesied that the post-war period would be characterized by a significant presence of information technology (IT) in every aspect of life. The change would be as profound as the post-Industrial Revolution era in the nineteenth and early twentieth century. Wiener also foretold that new moral conflicts and opportunities would arise as a result of the second industrial revolution. This could be termed as the "automatic age."

In the current age, "IE" has been interpreted by different researchers working in fields like business ethics, information science, computer science, medical ethics, social epistemology and computer ethics in various ways. This Babel was inevitable considering that this is a new field and information is diverse in nature. The detailed scope and nature of IE have, unfortunately, caused some confusion. This situation could quickly be resolved with the help of a unified approach that can help to explain and relate the main senses in which IE has been discussed.

## 1.2. INFORMATION AS A RESOURCE ETHIC

IE was a broad term, in the 1980s, to discuss issues pertaining to information (or data) confidentiality, reliability, quality, and usage.

Information Society is a social order where economic and cultural activities are mostly comprised of creation, distribution, and manipulation of information. The emergence of information societies can be attributed to the advancement in the field of ICTs. The advent of information society has been characterized by fundamental moral challenges. The intricacies of these challenges are multiplying exponentially.

The most urgent need of the hour is to have an IE structure in place that can bring infosphere into existence. Infosphere is an environment which is populated by information, knowledge, and data as well as their related lifecycle activities like conception, distribution, security, storage, and obliteration after their intended use, etc. Mankind is expected to thrive in this new environment.

The purpose of having IE is to identify and resolve technical complexities that may arise in this new environment.

This field is new, and the problem it poses is also compelling. Also, the concept of information and its related phenomena are multifaceted.

The Earth is a dynamic planet, and it has undergone different stages of expansion, diminution, and globalization ever since it came into existence. This present era of globalization is apparently here to stay because it has been accompanied by phenomenal changes in transportation and communication all over the globe (Brandt and Henning, 2002).

The most notable feature of this era is the rapid movement of bytes and atoms. This movement has been inexpensive, dependable, constant, and universal since the last decade. This has resulted in short turnaround times for economic transactions, financial exchanges, social relations, movement of information and so on. The omnipresence of information has shrunk physical environment and human life.

### 1.2.1. Resource Perspective

From a "resource" perspective, the moral machine needs abundant information to function accurately. To consider information solely as a resource restricts the scope of its analysis. It is essential to exercise caution so that all ethical disclosure is not reduced to the nuances of higher quantity, quality, and intelligibility of informational resources. It cannot always be claimed more is better. Removal of information (sometimes obvious and conscious) can often make a substantial difference.

Whether the (quantitative and qualitative) presence or the (total) absence of information-as-a-resource is in question, it is obvious that there is a perfectly reasonable sense in which IE may be described as the study of the moral issues arising from "the triple-A" of informational resources.
