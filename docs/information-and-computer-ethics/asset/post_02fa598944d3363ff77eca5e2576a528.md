Netherlands revolutionized and topped the aspect of digital innovation in the 21st century with the concept of the smart electricity meters across their country. There is need to make the electricity grids more efficient in every house in Netherlands to meet the targets of reduction of carbon dioxide (CO2) from the environment by the year 2020.

The introduction of the smart electricity meters would have enabled Netherlands to top the digital network. The smart digital meters were made to analyze the electricity consumption in each household and understand the areas of high and low consumption. This would provide the citizens with the information regarding the different areas of electricity consumption and optimize their utilization, accordingly, and provide them with an opportunity to reduce their consumption and in turn minimize the electricity bill. The concept of smart meters was proposed in the parliament; however, the parliament did not approve the smart meter owing to the privacy and security constraints. This was another innovation which discontinued owing to privacy and security concerns.

The meter is known as the ‘spying device,’ which would be harmful to the circle of individuals private life as it would take photographs/picture of the utilization of electricity almost every seven seconds and store the respective data in the database. This data would be utilized by the companies to understand the functioning of the homes of the individuals.

The important personal life data like the movies watched by the individuals at a time would also be known with little effort. The proposal was brought to the Dutch parliament, but it was rejected by the upper house for security and privacy of the citizens. The rejection of the smart meters in the parliament of Netherlands acted as a lesson to the other European countries as most of the other European countries that were planning to implement the concept of smart meters have withdrawn the idea of the smart meters.

There is a different example like the smart meter/ spying device in Netherlands which is the introduction of electronic patient record system. The electronic patient record system took nearly ten years of research and development. However, after ten years of research, preparations, lobbying, stakeholder consultation and debates along with an investment of 300 million Euros the venture was banned in the parliament by the upper house because of privacy and security considerations. The novel ideas and innovations in the areas of electricity and health care sector would have enabled Netherlands to reduce the prices, improve their competence, become more sustainable with their objectives; however, the privacy and security concerns were the main reasons for hindrance in Netherlands for these aspects.

The health care sector would have seen major changes with respect to the maintenance of patient files and would have achieved better data security with respect to the patient data; however, the moral and ethical aspects have stopped the innovation in these areas.

These innovations would have been a huge revolutionary success if the engineers would have considered the moral and privacy aspects and combined these aspects into their innovations. The process of considering the moral and ethical aspects and giving them due importance while making an innovation is an important aspect of any innovation.

The major take-home point from these examples is that there is need to consider the moral and ethical values must be given importance as the “nonfunctional requirements,” at an initial stage of the development of a project. The moral and ethical values are called as the nonfunctional requirements and the storage, capacity, speed, bandwidth, compliance with technical standards and protocols are called as the “functional requirements.”

The smart meter and the electronic patient record system are undoubtedly the best innovative designs; however, any idea or design is considered to be a true innovation only if it has the capacity to fulfill all the requirements and enter into the market effectively. Both these projects failed because of lack of proper value sensitive thinking.

Value sensitive thinking is a process of understanding the different aspects of a design such as the efficiency, privacy, sustainability, and safety. A prior evaluation of all these factors and the impact of the innovation into the new market would save a lot of time, energy, and resources. If the engineers have carefully analyzed and understood these aspects, they should have integrated them into their innovations which would have made these innovations a great success.
