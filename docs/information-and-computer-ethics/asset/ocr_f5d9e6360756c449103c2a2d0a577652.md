The actions were to take place almost immediately and that too without
human involvement. Wiener had extraordinary insight and foresight. He
had the foresight to realize that the new inventions in the field of science
and technology that he and his colleagues were working on would have
“enormous potential for good and for evil.”

Wiener prophesied that the post-war period would be characterized by a
significant presence of information technology (IT) in every aspect of life.
The change would be as profound as the post-Industrial Revolution era in the
nineteenth and early twentieth century. Wiener also foretold that new moral
conflicts and opportunities would arise as a result of the second industrial
revolution. This could be termed as the “automatic age.”

In the current age, “IE” has been interpreted by different researchers
working in fields like business ethics, information science, computer science,
medical ethics, social epistemology and computer ethics in various ways.
This Babel was inevitable considering that this is a new field and information
is diverse in nature. The detailed scope and nature of IE have, unfortunately,
caused some confusion. This situation could quickly be resolved with the
help of a unified approach that can help to explain and relate the main senses
in which IE has been discussed.

# 1.2. INFORMATION AS A RESOURCE ETHIC

IE was a broad term, in the 1980s, to discuss issues pertaining to information
(or data) confidentiality, reliability, quality, and usage.

Information Society is a social order where economic and cultural
activities are mostly comprised of creation, distribution, and manipulation
of information. The emergence of information societies can be attributed
to the advancement in the field of ICTs. The advent of information society
has been characterized by fundamental moral challenges. The intricacies of
these challenges are multiplying exponentially.
