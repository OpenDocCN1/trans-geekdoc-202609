# Table 6.2: Benefits and Drawbacks of Using Non-State Actors in Cyberspace Operations

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Gaining the initiative – element of surprise | No direct control of non-state actors |
| Plausible deniability | Risk of unintended collateral damage |
| Can choose target and attack vector | Targeting of own resources |
| Determinate scale and duration of attack | Escalation to conventional war |
| Exploit legal uncertainties | Labeling as sponsor of terrorism |
| Possibility of rapid attacking-by-proxy | Backlashes (blackmailing etc.) |

Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/310827486_Non-State_Actors_in_Cyberspace_Operations/download

The above table gives a keynote of some of the significant “benefits and drawbacks” of performing a cyberwarfare, and how the employment of non-state actors is associated in the cyberspace operations.

# Benefits of Using Non-State Actors:

+   - The primary advantage for an attacker is that the attacks can be performed in a much-secured way which can result in huge surprises or high possibility of uncertainty. As the attacker is in a position of initiating the attack, the options left with the defender are only to respond to those attacks, which are mostly predicted by the attacker.
+   - The attacker has the advantage of precision of time, and the ability to choose the specific target as per their own picking, using attack methods that are most suitable. For an attacker to perform an attack, not many processes or systems are needed other than a computer system. On the other hand, a victim would require several protections or defense systems in order to overcome a cyber-attack. The defense materials that are required to shield the victim’s computer systems are exorbitantly expensive.
+   - The attacker can decide how to attack and the scale and duration of attack to have the desired impact. The responsibility of the attack can also be entrusted to a third party. This way they can amplify the impact of the act and also not risk direct attribution.
