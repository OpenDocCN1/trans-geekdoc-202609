# 6.6. NON-STATE ACTORS AND ITS INCREASE IN CYBERWARFARE

Armed forces and intelligence around the world have recognized the urgency to rapidly achieve a military cyberspace operation capability since the cyberwarfare has started to spread to more and more countries. During the year 2013, the Department of Defense of the U.S. sanctioned a major expansion of its Cybercommand.

Consequently, its size multiplied fivefold to almost 5000 troops and civilians. For well-developed nations the priority would be to build defensive capability, securing susceptible digital resources, such as command and control systems. Whereas developing countries may identify cyberspace operations as a striking technique of warfare, owing to the fact that is relatively low-cost method, and low risk-from from a political perspective to engage in a war against an opponent with kinetic battlefield dominance.

Cyberwarfare is expected to involve a plethora of methods. It could include destruction of vital infrastructures, cause critical injuries and loss to human lives, psychological techniques that could be disruptive.
