However, the drawbacks of using cyber-attacks are substantial. The downside to it is the lack of control over the extent of collateral damage and calculation of value of the same in the targeting process, whereas the data is more readily available in case of conventional warfare. The cyberspace is a highly sophisticated and interdependent in nature.

This makes it difficult to assess whether an attack in this space will not only cause disruptions in the military network but also cause collateral damage to civilians, non-combatant or possibly the person who is carrying out the attacks himself. Hence the political risk needs to be taken into consideration, and this is often high. The unintended consequences may cause conflict escalation, carries the risk of making the cause look less legitimate in front of the international community, receive negative responses from their own people and augment resistance in the nation that was attacked. These shortcomings will deter nations-states from using cyber-attacks.

# 6.6.1. Actors Involved in Cyberwarfare

Table 6.1: Main Non-State Actors in Cyberconflict

| Actor | Motivation | Target | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary citizens | None (or weak) | Any | Indirect |
| Script kiddies | Curiosity, thrills, ego | Individuals, companies, governments | Previously written scripts and tools |
| Hacktivists | Political or social change | Decision makers or innocent victims | Protests via web page defacements or DDoS attacks |
| Black-hat hackers | Ego, personal animosity, economic gain | Any | Malware, viruses, vulnerability exploits |
| White-hat hackers | Idealism, creativity, respect for the law | Any | Penetration testing, parching |
| Grey-hat hackers | Ambiguous | Any | Varying |
| Patriot hackers | Patriotism | Adversaries of own nation-state | DDoS attacks, defacements |
