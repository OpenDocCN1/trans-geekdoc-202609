# ## 4.2.2. The Meta-Ethical Thesis

There are acts in computer ethics that cannot adequately be characterized by the traditional concepts of obligatory, permissible, good, and supererogatory. The meta-ethical thesis, then, makes the very strong claim that the very meta- ethical foundation for general and applied ethical thinking is inadequate.

On the other hand, the researchers may contend that the theories regarding ethics that are present currently may prove to be enough to find solutions to the problems concerning certain areas of applied ethics, but turn inadequate to find solutions to the issues engaging the use of computers. Hence, IE is different in certain ways.

# ## 4.2.3. The Normative Thesis

Computer technologies lay down ethical issues that cannot, as a neutral matter, be sufficiently determined by choice of current ethical theories. The normative thesis, hence, puts forward a claim regarding the impartial reporting of the current set of first-principles – and not regarding the aptitude to apply those principles.

In particular, it states that not even a flawlessly smart viewer could properly gauge certain ethical matters merely by recourse to current first- principles since those ideologies do not completely cover those acts. Third, a person might contend that certain kinds of perceptions valuable in other parts of applied ethics are of partial value in the setting of computer ethics.

# ## 4.2.4. The Epistemological Thesis

The digital technologies put forward the kind of ethical issues that keep those analogies away that make it easy for the people to see the manner in which the theories concerning ethics and its primary principles, have an application in the other fields that concern the subject of applied ethics. The epistemological thesis, then, asserts no more than that the techniques that frequently help us in seeing how existing normative materials apply to specific problems are inadequate to help us with problems in computer ethics.
