### 1.6.1. International Laws

Over two hundred or more countries worldwide are interconnected by the internet. A significant question arises as to what laws would apply. For example, if an internet user in London wishes to protect their privacy, which law would protect his rights?

The local law of the UK would not apply to the rest of the world. There are a number of issues that require regulation over the internet like control of pornography, freedom of speech, protection of intellectual property, invasion of privacy. The task is even more daunting since so many countries are involved. When people of the two countries are involved in dealing over the internet, and one country finds the activity illegal, a question arises as to how the other country would deal with the matter.

### 1.6.2. International Cyber-Business

The globalization has driven the “cyberbusiness” worldwide, and it has grown exponentially. Countries with good infrastructure facilities have been able to leverage them to enjoy economic benefits. The countries that are not well equipped have not been able to do so. Some important issues that arise are:

- What will the outcome of the unequal expansion of “cyberbusiness” be?
- Will the laws and regulation of one country be binding on another?
- Will the disparity in income widen as a few countries become wealthier?
- Will all this result in political confrontations and war?

### 1.6.3. International Education

Information should be made available to everyone at an affordable price, be it downtrodden people in the developing nations or the rich. If this happens, for the first time ever, all humans on earth will be able to update themselves with the current affairs from a free press, will have access to artworks and documents from good libraries and museums all over the globe, will be able to understand traditions, customs, and political scenarios of all places on Earth.

The access to this “global education” has been unexpected and intense. The effect of this on political dictatorships, isolated communities, coherent cultures, religious practices, etc. has to be weighed. For example, if more recognized universities of the world start offering distance education programs over the internet, will that have an adverse impact on universities that are not that well recognized.

### 1.6.4. Rich and Poor Information

The disparity between developing and developed nation is widening. Even in the advanced countries, the gap between the poor and rich is alarming. A concern that arises is, when educational opportunities, business, and job prospects, services in the field of medicine, and other utilities on which humans depend, have to be accessed via the internet, the gap between the wealthy and the needy may further widen making matters worse.

## 1.7. FACTORS INFLUENCING INFORMATION ETHICS

There are many factors that impact and mold IE. There are some factors that have a humongous impact, and they are discussed below:

- **Globalization**: Globalization is undoubtedly the most significant development of this century. In this new era of globalization, there is widespread use of ICTs and openness in exchanging information. This has also given rise to conflicts among individuals and societies trying to proclaim supremacy over others.

- **More emphasis on individualism**: In today’s world, people often prioritize personal well-being over social good as they tend to be more self-centered. Consequently, the demand for customized and personalized services has increased.

- **Privacy and information security**: The whole world is now a big network. Hence an apprehension that arises is privacy and security of all the data held in public and private databases. Information repositories like websites have to be secured properly. There is a growing concern among people about the confidentiality of the personal information held in these databases. It is the responsibility of the providers and handlers of this information to keep them discreet and prevent them from falling into the wrong hands.

- Diversification of ‘information works’: The information professionals have a bigger responsibility than their forerunners. They have to play multiple roles apart from being just providers of information. They also become consultants, guides, coaches, technical specialists, translators, and others. They have to constantly upgrade their skills and competencies and acquire new ones to discharge their new responsibilities.

- The conflict between ‘right to information’ and ‘ethical use of information’: Countries all over the globe have passed laws to safeguard people’s rights to obtain and utilize information. For instance, India passed the ‘Right to Information Act’ in 2005, and Bangladesh passed it in 2009. Nevertheless, security concerns and other such reasons, restrict access to classified information by the general public. An information professional often faces dilemmas when it comes to choosing between ‘rights to information’ and ‘restricted use of information’.

- Access to information: Many libraries will also have to deal with the issue of universal access to information. According to Fernandez-Molina various public and professional declarations refer unequivocally to free and public access to information. For instance, individuals have the right to free and public access to information as per the British Library Association code of conduct.

It needs to be considered that if too much importance is given on providing service without charging for the same, it could result in a cash crunch for the information center and jeopardize its survival. It should be borne in mind that when a service is provided at no cost, its value is often depreciated. As a result, the respect and viability of the profession will be undermined.

- Intellectual property rights: Reproduction of information material through means like photocopying, faxing, scanning, and other such methods have become very easy with the invention of new technologies. Reproduction is now a hassle-free process, and this affects the rights of the holders of intellectual property detrimentally. It will now result in conflict between the information professional and owners of the information, with the former longing to obtain it at a low price and the latter wanting to protect his own interest.

Commoditization of information has happened due to information now being categorized as raw materials. Information is now traveling across various sectors and carries a price tag. This is making it hard for less affluent people to procure and utilize information.

> According to Schiller “In the reallocation of information resources now occurring throughout the economy from one set of users (the general population) to another (mostly corporate business users), one principle prevails. It is the market criterion – the ability to pay. This determines who will receive and who will be excluded from the benefits of the information lubricated economy.”

Evidently, information professionals now face new moral dilemmas in determining their roles and responsibility in this complex ‘infosphere’; it is equally difficult to assess their own capability to support the less privileged population to protect their right to free information and use it productively.

## 1.8. THE EXPONENTIAL GROWTH OF INFORMATION AND COMPUTER ETHICS

The conceptualization of a new academic field and its advancement is dependent on collaboration among scholars and supporting services from universities, research centers in the form of courses, conferences, and academic journals. The year 1958 was critical for information and computer ethics, in this respect. The strong foundation of the field was laid down by Johnson when he published his book Computer Ethics and a special issue of the journal Metaphilosophy in 1985. Moor wrote the article “What is Computer Ethics?” which was also a brilliant curriculum material for this field.

Maner had put in humungous efforts and had also influenced other people to contribute to this field. Consequently, a generation of passionate computer science and philosophy scholars was born. This paved the way for exponential growth. It has to be noted that Maner was way ahead of his time when he laid down the foundation for computer and IE in the 1940s and 50s. It was only much later in the 80s when scholars and social thinkers followed his lead and developed this new field of computer and IE.
