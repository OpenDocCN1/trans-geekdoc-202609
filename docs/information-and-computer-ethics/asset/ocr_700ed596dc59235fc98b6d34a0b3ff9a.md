But when it got sighted in accordance with the perceptions of the ethics or morality associated with the information and communication technology, being a track for the ethics belonging to the experts or the professionals or being the basic division related to the philosophy, grounded on the applied ethics, the positions held by Johnson could get challenged.

Some of the individuals, who were in support of this specified perception, having an inclusion of the practitioners of the computer sciences, for instance: Gotterbarn (1991), would certainly be responding to the forecasting or the prediction made by Johnson, by presenting with an indication regarding the various problems or issues being associated with it, having an involvement of the certain responsibilities for the provided experts or the professionals, who are operating within the facet of the computers would certainly not being distant in accordance with the future, ethics belonging to the computers will not be going away, for instance: the facet or the field belonging to the ethics being associated with the professionals.

But then, another question arises related to the ethics belonging to the information as well as the communication technology being the branch of the applied ethics.

Antonio Marturano (2000) has lately provided with the response to Johnson through a demand regarding the two pertaining ways with which she could be able to understand or interpret the claim made by her regarding the banishment of the computer ethics:

+   - As an autonomous discipline, computer ethics will disappear; and
+   - As an aspect of applied ethics, computer ethics will disappear.

In addition to challenges posed to Johnson’s prediction by Gotterbarn and Marturano, others would also likely disagree with Johnson.

It would surely appear that questions related to AI comprising moral responsibility will endure to rise in the coming future and that these AI related questions would benefit arguably from the analysis by computer ethicists.

Rather, it is believed that a better knowledge of how computer technology has emerged during the last fifty years can aid to recognize more clearly some of the issues and challenges that people will face in the future when they attain a state of more comprehensive ‘technological convergence.’
