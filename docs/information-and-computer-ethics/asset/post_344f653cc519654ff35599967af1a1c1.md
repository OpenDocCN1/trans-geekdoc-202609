### 2.6.3. Robotics and Moral Values

Information technologies have not been restricted to software implementations and virtual worlds. These technologies are directly interacting with the generations by the application of robotics. Robotics is an evolving technology, but it has produced a huge number of uses that have significant moral implications.

Various technologies like medical robotics, personal robotics, military robotics and the world of sex robots are some of the existing applications of robotics that express the moral commitments.

The launch of semi or fully autonomous machines in public life will not be that easy. Autonomous machines are those that take decisions with little or almost no intervention of humans.

The military robotics has been recognized to be one of the most ethical and charged applications of robotics. These days these machines are basically operated remotely (telerobots) or semi-autonomous, but over time it is possible for these machines to become progressively autonomous because of the requirements of modern warfare.

In the first ten years of war in the 21st century, the robotic weaponry has been involved in various killings of soldiers as well as noncombatants and this fact alone is of profound moral concern. Gerhard Dabringer has done various interviews with ethicists and technologists related to the inferences of automated warfare.

### 2.7.1. Information Technology as a Model for Moral Discovery

Patrick Grim has been a great follower of the notion that philosophy must apply different information technologies so as to illustrate and automate philosophical thought experiments.

Also, Peter Danielson (1998) has extensively written on this topic with his book *Modeling Rationality, Morality, and Evolution* with a lot of initial research in the computational theory of morality focused on utilizing computer models in order to explain the evolution of cooperation between simple software AI or ALife agents.

J. W. Sanders and Luciano Floridi argue that the information which is used in the computation theory can aid as a potential thought that can help in resolving few popular moral conundrums in philosophy, for example, the nature of evil (1999, 2001).

### 2.7.2. Information Technology as a Moral System

In the beginning of 20th century, John Dewey, the American philosopher proposed an inquiry theory based upon the instrumental utilization of technology. Dewey had an extensive definition of technology that further comprises machines, common tools and information systems like laws, logic, and language (Hickman, 1990).

Further, Dewey argued that nowadays people are in a kind of transactional relationship with these kinds of technologies inside which people are discovering as well as constructing the world (Hickman, 1990). This is a cooperative perspective to take as it permits the current generation to advance the idea that an IT of ethics and morality is conceivable.

As well as letting us to utterly take the idea that the transactions and relations between the human agents and those which occur between humans and their artifacts have essential ontological comparisons.

Though Dewey could just observe the upcoming revolutions in information technologies, his theories are very beneficial to the modern generation as he proposed that ethics is a kind of practice and resolving issues in ethics is similar to resolving issues in algebra. Ethics was not just a kind of theory only (Hickman, 1990).

If he is correct at his terms, then an exciting possibility evolves, such as the probability that morality and ethics are assessable issues and hence it must be possible to develop an IT that can further represent moral systems of thought.

The philosopher named as Mario Bunge in 1974, anticipated that we take the belief of a “technoethics” utterly arguing that the moral philosophers must compete with the ways the engineers deal with any issue or problem. Engineers never argue in context of reasoning by definite imperatives, but rather they utilize:

> ... the forms If $A$ produces $B$, and you value $B$, chose to do $A$, and If $A$ produces $B$ and $C$ produces $D$, and you prefer $B$ to $D$, choose $A$ rather than $C$.

In brief, the rules and regulations that have been set basically depend on the fact and value, various researchers have submitted that this is the only path moral rules ought to be formed, specifically as rules of conduct originating from scientific statements as well as value judgments.

Briefly, ethics could be perceived as a branch of technology. Taking this notion quite seriously indicates that the process of creating information technologies is also the process of developing specific moral systems in which human, as well as artificial agents, will interact by moral transactions occasionally.

Therefore, information technologists involved in the business of establishing moral systems, whether they are aware of it or not, and whether they aspire to that responsibility or not.
