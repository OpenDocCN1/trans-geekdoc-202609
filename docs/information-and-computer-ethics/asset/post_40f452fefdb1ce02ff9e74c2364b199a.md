The autonomous agents are categorized into two types based on their architecture. They are:

- Monolithic Agents; and
- Decentralized Agents.

Monolithic agents: Monolithic agents are those autonomous agents that are made from one source of information and the functionalities of the monolithic agents are limited. For example, monolithic agents are any data file or software libraries that have different information.

Decentralized agents or Decentralized Intelligent Agents: Decentralized agents are called as decentralized intelligent agents and as per their name they refer to the systems where the intelligence is distributed among many different elements. Though the intelligence is distributed these elements function in coordination with one another and are called as the “artificial flocks” by Craig Reynolds because of the demonstrated similarities in the way they live or function.

The decentralized agent is known to be more resistant to the efforts related to deactivating and the impacts of counteroffensives. Therefore, for an example, a botnet is a network of various agents in place of the conventional malware software which would be the central point of control that would be deactivated.

### 7.3.3. The Environment of Softbots

The physical autonomous agents are called as robots, and they work in the regular physical environment where there are a lot of computational agents, and this environment is called as cyberspace.

The cyberspace has various definitions and the White House cyberspace policy review which was published in the year 2011 defines the cyberspace as “the interdependent network of IT infrastructures, and includes the Internet, telecommunications networks, computer systems and embedded processors and controllers in critical industries.”

Cyberspace is a different and specific environment that is created by human beings and hence it’s very exclusive. The cyberspace is made, preserved, and used by a number of people from different private and public sectors, the diversity of the audience would need the cyberspace to be dynamic.

The cyberspace and its surroundings have different characteristics, and according to the Russell and Norvig’s classification the different peculiarities of cyberspace are listed as follows:

- Cyberspace does not have completely available surroundings.
- The different aspects of cyberspace are not available at the same time.
- Any person at a given time would have knowledge of only one small aspect of cyberspace.
- The cyberspace is a very intricate and difficult environment.
- Cyberspace is very dynamic, diverse, and unique space as it is constantly changing with respect to its utility and different requirements.
- Cyberspace is the area which is prone to different sort of warfare and sometimes highly susceptible, despite the presence of cybersecurity software like the firewalls, Intrusion Detection Systems (IDSs) and Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPSs).

### 7.4.2. Embodied Artificial Agents

Embodied artificial agents have been structured or designed for the purpose of operation within physical environments. Cyborgs have been considered as one of the main genera belonging to the embodied artificial agents, the other major genus being the robots that are entirely inorganic in their respective nature.

In the end, Pistorius has not been victorious in beating the qualifying time for entrance to the 400 m event, though his specified time surely would be putting him well beyond the average running speed of human beings. Perhaps, the definition of dictionary generally tends to exclude Oscar Pistorius from the category related to cyborgs on the grounds, which is Cheetah blades that was supposed to get strapped on, not ‘built into’ the provided body.

Setting up with the definitional matters separately, the types of cyborgs that possess most of the interest for the purposes of this chapter are usually those which provide an offer regarding the cognitive enhancements to the humans with the help of embedded computational devices.

The ‘cyborg divide’ could be only as considerate if all these improvements provide with core keys to those who tend to adopt them. Specified advancements also raise certain issues related to ethics.

### 7.4.3. Virtual Artificial Agents

Virtual artificial agents (aka ‘bots’) are already into existence. They operate inside computationally generated environments. The range of a general virtual artificial agent starts with internet as whole till the electronic market. The electronic market is generally referred to the e-commerce market which has started gaining momentum at a global level. Some of the famous examples for electronic markets are; NASDAQ and eBay. The range of virtual artificial agent from electronic market gets extended to computer games, and they exist within networked virtual worlds such as Second Life that are designed primarily for entertainment, but are also increasingly being used for communication and education.

In the electronic markets, there persists automated trading along with the bidding programs, which helps in carrying out all the transactions that are entirely based on the provided formulas that in some cases are a far from the comprehension of those who tend to depend or rely upon them.

The fact that most of the people possess the tendency to overestimate the intellectuality along with the criticality of computer programs, which is now known to the AI researchers in the form of ‘ELIZA effect.’ It is just a demonstration of the formerly mentioned tendency of human beings to anthropomorphize the pertaining things.

The perspective for ‘persuasion’ by non-verbal means is just the starting to get probed, but the probable for ethical abuse in situations like these is already quite tacit.
