### 2.6.1. Acceleration of Change

An IT has a very interesting pattern of growth that has been seen since the establishment of industries. Gordon E. Moore, an Intel engineer found that the number of components that could be mounted on an IC, i.e., integrated circuit almost doubled every year for a slight economic cost, and further he thought it might last that way for next ten years or so from the time he found it in the year 1965.

This process is not restricted to the chips of computer only. Various forms of information technologies can be found in it. The latent power of the accelerating change has taken the thoughts of Ray Kurzweil, the futurist, and the inventor.

There has also been supporting for the thesis from the community of technology with institutes like the Acceleration Studies Foundation, the Singularity Institute, Future of Humanity Institute, and H+. The reaction of philosophers to this hypothesis has been really mixed but very crucial.

Similarly, Sullins (2006) contends that there is generally a quasi-religious facet to the transhumanism acceptance that is devoted to few results like uploading of consciousness of humans into the computer as a method to attain immortality, and that the receiving of transhumanist hypothesis impacts the values implanted in the computer technology, which can be hostile or dismissive to the body of human being.

Not every philosopher is critical of transhumanism, as an instance Nick Bostrom (2008) of the Future of Humanity Institute at Oxford University claims that putting the feasibility argument aside, we must accomplish that there are distinct posthumanism forms that would eventually lead to long as well as valuable lives and that it would be a very good thing for humans to become posthuman if and only if it is possible at all.

AI as well as ALife are wide research projects that challenge the simple explanation. Here, the emphasis is on the moral values that these technologies influence and the way these technologies are programmed so as to affect morale and emotional concern.

- **Artificial Life (ALife):** is known as the outgrowth of AI. ALife refers to the utilization of IT to synthesize or simulate the functions of life. The issue of describing life has been an interest in philosophy after its founding.

Mark Bedau has been outlining the philosophical inferences of ALife for some time and contends that there are two different forms of ALife and each would have a distinct moral impact if and when the separate research agendas are realized.

One kind of ALife is entirely computational, and in fact, it is the initial form of ALife. ALife is basically encouraged by the work of John von Neumann, a mathematician on self-replicating cellular automata which he believed would definitely lead to a computational considerate of biology as well as the life sciences (1966).

Christopher Langton (1989), the computer scientist simplified the model of von Neumann greatly and generated a simple and cellular automaton known as loops during the early eighties. He helped in getting the field off to the ground by managing the few conferences based on ALife.

ALife programs are very much unlike from AI programs. Where AI is committed on producing or improving intelligence, ALife is gratified with very simple-minded programs that show life functions instead of intelligence.

The main moral concern here is that these programs are framed to self-reproduce and in that way look like computer viruses and certainly successful ALife programs could become as malware vectors.

The second form of ALife is very much morally charged. This form of ALife is built on deploying definite biochemical and biological processes in such a way as to yield novel life forms not seen in nature.

Craig Venter introduced a public relation campaign and tried to direct the conversation regarding the issues concerned with the creation of life. In the synthesis of life, this first episode provides us a taste of controversy as well as excitement. The controversy will be created when more robust and viable artificial protocells are made.
