## 6.5 USE OF FORCE AND CYBERATTACK

Cyber-attacks around the world are not identical. The impact of cyber-attack that cause electricity blackout for one hour is less severe when compared to one that causes planes to crash by scrambling air traffic signals. Thus there is a necessity to have a framework for assessing a particular attack or class of attacks to conclude whether the use of armed force is justified.

In 1999, Michael Schmitt, professor of international law and Director of the Program in Advanced Security Studies at the George G. Marshall European Center for Security Studies in Germany offered seven principles which differentiated the operations which use the forces from diplomatic, economic, and other soft measures.

The high end resembling the use of force and the low end resembling soft measures are the key spectrum of consequences when those seven criteria are segregated into one criterion. The description of seven criteria is based on the work of both the authors of international law conflict, Thomas Wingfield and Schmitt.

- **Severity:** It refers to the damage to life and property. It denotes that wide-spread calamity, loss of life and damage to property is caused due to armed attacks, but soft measures do not have such consequences.

- **Immediacy:** It refers to the time that it takes for the effect of the attacks to be felt. Generally, it is seen that the effects of use of armed forces are immediate, often within seconds or minutes, while in case of softer measures, like imposing bans, the consequences are felt much later, often after weeks or months.

- **Directness:** The association between an operation and the effects of operation is known as directness. Generally, the effects for an armed attack are caused by and attributable to the application of force, whereas for softer measures there could be multiple explanations.

- **Invasiveness:** Those armed attacks which comprises of physical cross borders into the target country is known as invasiveness. Concurrently, softer measures are implemented from within the borders of a sponsoring country.

- **Measurability:** This refers to the ability to quantify the effects of an operation. The basis is that the impact of armed attacks can be more readily measured like the number of deaths, the value of impairment to wealth, etc. than softer measures, for example, trade restrictions.

- **Presumptive Legitimacy:** Presumptive legitimacy is the validation of an operation to be genuine in the global community. Whereas the use of armed force is generally unlawful absent some justifiable reason such as self-defense, the use of soft measures is generally lawful absent some prohibition.

- **Responsibility:** Responsibility refers to the degree to which the consequence of an action can be attributed to a state as opposed to other actors. In case of use of armed force, the state can be directly held responsible as it is the exclusive province of the state hence can be attributed directly to it, whereas non-state actors are capable of getting themselves involved in such soft activity as propaganda and boycotts.

Let us consider an example to understand how these criteria would apply to a cyber-attack. A cyber-intrusion on air traffic control has resulted in two large aircraft to enter the same airspace and collide. The effects of crash have been devastating leading to the loss of 500 lives on board of these two aircraft.

Responsibility comes out moderate to high. In theory, the crime could be committed by anyone. But most hackers would not have the required skill and information. Only a state could possess such resources. In summary, five criteria such as immediacy, directness, presumptive legitimacy, measurability, and legitimacy are ranked high, whereas two criteria such as responsibility and invasiveness are ranked at least moderate. Thus, apparently, the attack is not a soft measure or a legitimate form of coercion but a more severe application of force.

## 6.6 NON-STATE ACTORS AND ITS INCREASE IN CYBERWARFARE

Armed forces and intelligence around the world have recognized the urgency to rapidly achieve a military cyberspace operation capability since the cyberwarfare has started to spread to more and more countries. During the year 2013, the Department of Defense of the U.S. sanctioned a major expansion of its Cybercommand.

Consequently, its size multiplied fivefold to almost 5000 troops and civilians. For well-developed nations the priority would be to build defensive capability, securing susceptible digital resources, such as command and control systems. Whereas developing countries may identify cyberspace operations as a striking technique of warfare, owing to the fact that is relatively low-cost method, and low risk from a political perspective to engage in a war against an opponent with kinetic battlefield dominance.

Cyberwarfare is expected to involve a plethora of methods. It could include destruction of vital infrastructures, cause critical injuries and loss to human lives, psychological techniques that could be disruptive. Other methods include creating confusion which would lead to information warfare and create uncertainty among their opposition. The character of cyber-attack is such that it can strike both strategic and tactical targets from great distances. Additionally, the technology involved is also less expensive.

However, the drawbacks of using cyber-attacks are substantial. The downside to it is the lack of control over the extent of collateral damage and calculation of value of the same in the targeting process, whereas the data is more readily available in case of conventional warfare. The cyberspace is a highly sophisticated and interdependent in nature.

This makes it difficult to assess whether an attack in this space will not only cause disruptions in the military network but also cause collateral damage to civilians, non-combatant or possibly the person who is carrying out the attacks himself. Hence the political risk needs to be taken into consideration, and this is often high. The unintended consequences may cause conflict escalation, carries the risk of making the cause look less legitimate in front of the international community, receive negative responses from their own people and augment resistance in the nation that was attacked. These shortcomings will deter nation-states from using cyber-attacks.

### 6.6.2 Employment of Non-State Actors in Cyberworld

In traditional methods of warfare can be attributed to a particular state. In case of cyberspace, anonymity can be maintained, and to a large extent, perpetrators can carry out attacks without the risk of being exposed. While employing a non-state actor in a cyber-world, the nation-states inclined to have very little or no backup in binding definition of cyberwar. This employment of non-state actors also tends to restrict the liberty to take any action or to formally take responsibility for executed cyber attacks.

Additionally, in cyber-attacks, there is less drainage of resources and is believed that it can cause severe disruptions or at least cause disturbances to ICT-based services.
