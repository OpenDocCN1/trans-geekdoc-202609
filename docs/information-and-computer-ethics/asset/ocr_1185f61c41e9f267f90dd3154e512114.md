The course offered by Maner in combination with the Starter Kit and
the conferences he conducted influenced the students of America in the field
of computer ethics. Several scholars began to concentrate on this new filed,
and there was also an increase in the number of university courses that were
offered on this subject.

Maner also generated a Starter Kit on Teaching Computer Ethics, basing
it on his teaching experiences and research in the planned new field. He
circulated copies of the kit to the attendees of the workshop he ran and
speeches that he gave at philosophy conferences and computing conferences
in America. The starter kit was published as a monograph in 1980, by
the Helvetia Press and the National Information and Resource Center on
Teaching Philosophy, headed by the present author.

# 1.6. PROFESSIONAL AND COMPUTER ETHICS

Donald Gotterbarn, in the early 1990s, supported a different emphasis
within computer ethics. He was of the view that computer ethics should
be perceived as professional ethics. It should be dedicated to the progress
and improvement of standards of good practice and codes of conduct for
computing professionals. In 1991 Gotterbarn published an article “Computer
Ethics: Responsibility Regained,” in which he quoted:

> “There is little attention paid to the domain of professional ethics – the
> values that guide the day-to-day activities of computing professionals in their
> role as professionals. By computing professional, I mean anyone involved
> in the design and development of computer artifacts. The ethical decisions
> made during the development of these artifacts have a direct relationship to
> many of the issues discussed under the broader concept of computer ethics.”

Gotterbarn wanted to contribute to the field of professional ethics,
hence collaborated with other supporters of professional ethics like Simon
Rogerson, Keith Miller, Dianne Martin and Chuck Huff in a large number of
projects to enforce professional ethics in computer practitioners. Gottenbarn
was already a part of ACM to generate the next version of its “Code of
Ethics and Professional Conduct.” This was adopted by ACM in 1992.
