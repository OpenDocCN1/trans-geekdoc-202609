+   - The commander’s first duty is to “do everything feasible to verify that the objectives to be attacked are neither civilians nor civilian objects.”
+   - The commander also has the duty to “take all feasible precautions in the choice of means and methods of attack with a view to avoiding, and in any event to minimizing, incidental loss of civilian life, injury to civilians and damage to civilian objects.”
+   - The commander should not instigate any attack “which may be expected to cause incidental loss of civilian life, injury to civilians, damage to civilian objects, or a combination thereof, which would be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated.”

It is important to note that, the concept of proportionality does not in any way place a constraint on commanders from causing any civilian death or injuries or destruction to civilian property. Proportionality states that loss to life and property should not exceed when compared to the “concrete and direct military advantage contemplated.”

The principle of proportionality prohibits an attack if it is anticipated that expected damage to civilian lives and destruction of civilian property would outweigh the military gain contemplated from such an attack. In order to analyze proportionality, the direct military advantage should be evaluated against the destruction of civilian life and property.
