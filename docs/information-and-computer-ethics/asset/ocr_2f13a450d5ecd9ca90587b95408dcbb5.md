# ## 3.9.1. Will Computer Ethics ‘Disappear’ in the Future?

Johnson recommended that the area of computer ethics may or should disappear in the coming future. She assumes that whatever we are considering as issues in computer ethics may become well integrated into the “ordinary ethics” issues.

Johnson has been considered as very cautious while pointing out or indicating that the issues or the problems are not going to get mitigated on their own, instead they are not even going to get manipulated or posed similar to the of the problems or issues related to the ethics belonging to the information along with the communication technologies.

He argued that the computer technology tends to mend or affect the actions performed by the human beings in varied ways in which it has never been implemented or executed. Nevertheless, in case a novel execution or the application has been established within the process of moral thinking, it generally tends to become the potential conditions based on the various contexts.

And, as soon as the various researchers were about to have an assumption regarding the computer technology, being one of the major parts of the world that they are living in, he presented with the prediction that the ethics or the morality of the information and communication technology is most likely to get vanished.

Then, the major question arouses as if the predictions made by Johnson, worth the laud or not. When considered from the vantage point of the cluster of computer ethics issues involving privacy, security, property, and free speech, her speculation about the future of the field would seem fairly cogent.

Nevertheless, as computing becomes a part of presumed background condition, privacy issues comprising computing would not observe to be significantly distinct in nature from the privacy issues in general.
