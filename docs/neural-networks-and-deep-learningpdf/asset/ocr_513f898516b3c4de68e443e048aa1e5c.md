So on the basis of these experiments it looks as though the improved weight initialization only speeds up learning, it doesn't change the final performance of our networks. However, in Chapter 4 we'll see examples of neural networks where the long-run behaviour is significantly better with the 1/√n_in weight initialization. Thus it's not only the speed of learning which is improved, it's sometimes also the final performance.

The 1/√n_in approach to weight initialization helps improve the way our neural nets learn. Other techniques for weight initialization have also been proposed, many building on this basic idea. I won't review the other approaches here, since 1/√n_in works well enough for our purposes.
