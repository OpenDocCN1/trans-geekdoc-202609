According to some clinical criteria, these subjects are divided into three categories: AD, MCI, and CN. To the focused balanced problem, we balanced the data of each class and independently select 158 multi-modal data from the MCI and CN subjects. A total of 467 AD and FDG scans and 364 subjects constitute the experimental dataset in this study. Then, we split the experimental data using a five-fold cross-validation strategy to evaluate the performance, choosing the hyper-parameters and model training while referring to paper survey [47], which works with future data due to the limited sample size. We also used transfer learning method demonstrated affinity to improve diagnosis performance [44, 45]. In real-world scenarios, the scans of a new subject will be seen by the model before it has been seen before.

Several learning-based studies for AD diagnosis using multi-modal data have attempted to extract and complement information. Despite their great overall performance, they ignore a key issue of multi-modal learning-cross-modal interaction. Existing methods combine multimodal information in a straightforward way, which is limited from different ways to combine the information from modalities, which is conducive to improving the diagnostic performance. Moreover, AD diagnosis using multi-modal biomarkers suffers from the large discrepancy across modalities, which is missed in most previous studies.

# 3.1. Data acquisition

This paper uses multi-modal data from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) [43], which is publicly available to study AD diagnosis. In this work, subjects are selected from ADNI-1 and ADNI-2 when their sMRI scans, CSF and PET measures are used. # 3.1.2. Processing data

For data analysis, we use the software platform to register sMRI and FDG-PET scans to a DARTel template and perform normalization using standard Montreal Neuroimaging Institute (MRI) space, as reported in Fig. 1. The inputs are the sMRI and FDG-PET scans. To avoid the effect of irrelevant information, the non-brain tissues between sMRI and FDG-PET scans are removed. Additionally, sMRI and FDG-PET scans are resized into 128 x 128 voxels to decrease computational expense.
