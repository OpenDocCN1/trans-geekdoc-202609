AD vs CN: This classification is considered to be the most important in AD-related tasks for several reasons. Firstly, the MRI scans of AD patients show lower values, significant range, and higher contrast, compared with healthy groups. AD patients have abnormal glucose metabolism in glutamate that can be detected by PET scans. Secondly, the extracellular space of the brain, so it could reveal the biochemical changes that exist in the brain of AD patients with healthy people. This facilitates the deep learning network to learn the features of AD more effectively. As seen from Table 2 for AD vs CN, the classification of our proposed method outperforms competing methods in all tasks. As shown in Table 3, our MCAD, Concation, DC, and Gated methods against competing methods (MCAN, MCCN, SA, and BBFNN) show significant differences in terms of p-value (p < 0.05). The difference between MCAD and competing methods could learn the fine-grained interactions between different modules and explore the potential concordance and complementarity between regions and CSF biomarkers, providing critical information for AD diagnosis.

As shown in the results of AD vs CN and MCI vs CN, we show that the diagnostic performance of the model is generally worse than that of other methods. Similar results are found in other relevant studies. A possible explanation for this is that the brain changes are less pronounced in early stage patients. The neuroimaging features of AD are mainly manifested in two aspects: brain atrophy and deoxy glucose metabolism. These two imaging changes are much more apparent for AD vs CN.
