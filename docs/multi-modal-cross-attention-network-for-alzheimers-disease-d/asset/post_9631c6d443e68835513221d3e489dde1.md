## 5.4. Discussion of cross-attention extending to imaging modules

In the proposed MCAD, multi-modal interaction based on cross-attention learns to learn the interactions between imaging features and CSF. To investigate the performance of MCAD using cross-attention, we perform experiments by comparing MCAD with a variant where cross-attention is extended to modules, design "MCAD-extended". Specifically, after obtaining features of two modalities, the outputs are concatenated for classification. The experimental results are shown in Table 5.

## 5.5. Visualization analysis

In this section, we demonstrate the behavior of the proposed framework by visualizing the attention maps in multi-modal interaction, as shown in Fig. 12. Cross-attention attention represents the CSF-attention attention extraction branch, whereas in CSF-attention it is the feature attention. Fig. 12 shows the visualization of attention maps corresponding to each feature. We use yellow color in the attention map to represent high attention weights. The corresponding results show that the attention mechanism differs between imaging patches and CSF features. Due to space limitations, we only show one example from each disease status is reported. From the visualization results, we have the following observations. In AD category, P- and T-tau may show significant attention in brain region with atrophy, particularly in the temporal lobe and hippocampus. This finding is consistent with previous work [78], which shows a relationship between CSF biomarkers and T-tau and A42, in different brain regions. Previous researches [77-81] also finds the correlation between CSF biomarkers and imaging features in AD.
