Tribune Publishing, a highly reputed media organization and owner of the newspaper Chicago Tribune, went through a cultural transformation following a 1985 strike by its production unions. The crisis created an opportunity for the organization to reflect on its current state and operating style and craft a new path forward. The management team made a commitment to a customer-focused operating philosophy, to establishing stronger vendor relationships, and to meeting its printing goals. They embraced a more disciplined approach to performance measurement by aligning the system with corporate mission, values, and goals. The cultural transformation results were quite impressive—productivity improved by 25% and the Chicago Tribune also realized tens of millions of dollars in annualized advertising revenue savings.35

To examine the validity of high-performance cultural traits from the standpoint of cybersecurity governance, data was gathered from primary and secondary sources. A multimethod approach of literature review, focus groups, and expert interviews was used to collect data. Representatives from for-profit and non-profit organizations participated in this study. To ensure a rich and diverse perspective, information security experts from technology user and technology provider firms were interviewed. Industries ranging from healthcare and public health to supply-chain management, higher education, security and information management solutions, and financial technology were in the surveyed sample. Qualitative analysis was used to validate the information security relevancy of the three high-performance cultural traits and also to identify the associated cybersecurity success factors and best practices. These traits and success factors are discussed in the following chapters.
