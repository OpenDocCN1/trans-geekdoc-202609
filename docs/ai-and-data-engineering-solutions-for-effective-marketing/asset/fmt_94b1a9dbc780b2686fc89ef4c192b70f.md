
# Expected Effort

Expected Effort refers to the perception of ease or difficulty of using the technology. A technology perceived as complex and difficult to use is less likely to be adopted. This concept is similar to that of ease of use in the TAM and TAM2 models. According to [Davis (1989)](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor370), perceived ease of use is the degree to which a person believes that a particular system is user-friendly. It is the second main concept of TAM, which has been used in previous studies on technology adoption and the adoption of online administration. Researchers ([Almuraqab & Jasimuddin, 2017](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor349); [Alonazi et al., 2020](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor351)) have found that end-users may prefer to use a service because it is convenient, simple, easy to access, less complicated, and easy to use. Other studies have indicated that perceived ease of use has a significant impact on the adoption of m-government services([Choi & Song, 2020](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor367)). Therefore, the following hypothesis was developed.

*   H2: Expected Effort will have a positive influence on behavioral intention to use smart services.

# Social Influence

Social Influence reflects the pressure exerted by an individual's social circle on their choice to use or not use technology. If a person perceives that the use of technology is valued by their social circle, they will be more inclined to adopt it([Ranaweera, 2016](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor394)). This corresponds to the notion of subjective norm in the TAM2 model. According to ([Venkatesh et al., 2003](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor403)), it is the extent to which an individual believes it is important that others (for example, their family and friends) think they should use the new system. Social influence emerges as a factor in the intention to use government services. These three factors directly influence a person's intention to use a technology, which in turn determines their actual use. So, based on these findings, the following hypothesis was developed:

*   H3: Social influence will have a positive influence on the behavioral intention to use smart services.

# Facilitating Conditions

The fourth factor, Facilitating Conditions, though it does not directly affect the intention to use, plays a crucial role in the actual use of technology, such as the availability of documentation or technical support. According to [Venkatesh et al. (2003)](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor403), facilitating conditions are related to the extent to which an individual believes that there is an organizational and technical infrastructure to support the use of the system([Bouhtati, Alla, et al., 2023](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor362)). Facilitating conditions are also defined as a person's belief in the extent to which a governmental and technical infrastructure is available to facilitate the system([Almuraqab & Jasimuddin, 2017](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor349); [Babullah et al., 2015](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor355)). Based on the above discussion, the following hypothesis can be formulated:

*   H4: Facilitating conditions will affect individual behavior of using smart services.

# Perceived Risk

Perceived risk is defined as a citizen's subjective expectation of suffering a loss in pursuit of a favored outcome([Carter & Bélanger, 2005](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor365)). Citizens' behavior is strongly influenced by risk perception. End-users are often uncertain about the consequences of a decision or action. Moreover, it has been revealed that end-users tend to minimize risk rather than maximize utility. The end-user's subjective perception of risk can largely explain their behavio([Eid et al., 2021](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor373); [Zhou et al., 2023](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor405)). Therefore, we hypothesise:

*   H5: The perceived risk will have a negative impact on users' intentions to use smart services.

# Awareness

Awareness is the understanding people have of technology and the availability of e-services([Venkatesh et al., 2003](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor403)). ([Abdelghaffar & Magdy, 2012](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor343)) feel that fostering awareness is the initial stage for users to become cognisant of the government's provision of services over the internet. Several studies have indicated that individuals' knowledge of smart government services and the accessibility of electronic resources is a significant issue. Undoubtedly, a deficiency in understanding has a detrimental effect on citizens' willingness to embrace e-government and m-government services([Almuraqab & Jasimuddin, 2017](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor349); [Alryalat et al., 2023](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor352)).

*   H6: Perceived awareness has a positive and significant influence on citizens’ intention to use smart services.

# Perceived Cost

Perceived cost is a crucial consideration. Access devices must be affordable, and the cost of accessing online public services must be low. The cost of mobile services is one of the main factors influencing end-users' intentions to use m-government services([Almuraqab & Jasimuddin, 2017](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor349); [Eid et al., 2021](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor373)). Perceived cost has a negative effect on users' intentions to adopt m-government services. In the context of citizen behavior, price is considered a very important factor ([Oumlil & Aderkaoui, 2020](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor390)). This work posits the following hypothesis:

*   H7: The perceived cost will have a negative impact on users' intentions to use smart services.

# Perceived Trust in Technology

Perceived trust in technology is consistently recognized as a key factor in the adoption of m-services. This type of trust is often referred to as institutional trust. According to([McKnight et al., 2002](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor385)), it refers to an individual's perceptions of the institutional environment, which contains the structures and principles that make the environment safe. In this regard, ([Carter & Bélanger, 2005](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor365)) emphasize that citizens must have trust in enabling technologies. Furthermore, ([Pavlou & Gefen, 2004](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor392))suggest that institution-based trust is a very critical form of trust in objective financial environments where the sense of a community with shared values is important([Chen & Aklikokou, 2020](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor366); [Tavitiyaman et al., 2021](#979-8-3693-3172-9.ch006.bits-pdf.xhtml#_idTextAnchor402)). As a result, rooted in UTAUT, this study hypothesizes:

*   H8: Perceived trust in technology will have a positive impact on users' intents to use smart services.
