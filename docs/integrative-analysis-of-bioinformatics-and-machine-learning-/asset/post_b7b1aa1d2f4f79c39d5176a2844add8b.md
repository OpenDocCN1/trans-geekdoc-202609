Costimulatory molecules are an indispensable signal for activating immune cells. However, the features of many costimulatory molecules (CMGs) in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) are poorly understood. This study systematically explored expression patterns of CMGs in the tumor microenvironment (TIME) of patients with LUAD. Their expression profiles were downloaded from the GEO database. Two subsets 'hot' and 'cold' were identified by K-clustering and estimated immune cells in malignant tumors using tissue expression. The 'hot' subset suggested higher activation in immune cells and enrichment in the immune cell receptor signaling pathway and adaptive response. Three CMGs (CD80, LTB, and TNFSF8) were screened as diagnostic markers by means of Absolute Signature Selection Operator and Survival Maximum Regression algorithms. The developed nomogram for predicting LUAD tissue status satisfied diagnostic accuracy in the Gene Atlas training cohort as well as GSE62110 and GSE13847. Immunohistochemistry staining of 16 specimens revealed an apparently positive correlation between expression of CMGs and pathologic response to immunotherapy. Thus, the independent prediction of status of LUAD patients with good predictive accuracy, which could serve as a test for identifying immunotherapy responders. In recent years, revolutionary breakthroughs in immunotherapy have led to advances in lung cancer, owing to the increasing recognition and focus of tumor microenvironment (TIME), which contains tumor cells, mesenchymal cells, and others. These diverse components and their interactions through multiple ligand and molecular mechanisms further lead to their vital and multifaceted impacts in different phases of tumor, including tumorigenesis, development, progression, metastasis, and antitumor immunity.

Development of immunotherapy in biology has been hindered by the heterogeneity and unique characteristics of TIME or multicomic genetic expression profiles. This has facilitated precision medicine and identifies candidates that properly respond to immunotherapy, parameters are extracted from the entire data source or limited to small-sample spatial relationships among lung cell types.

Some of the aforementioned studies are those that evaluate spatial data, as they learned from information about treatment response and functional mechanisms. Thus, they are rarely applied to clinical practice.

Immunotherapy, specifically the use of checkpoint inhibitors (ICs) that block programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-L1) or programmed cell death protein 2 (PD-1) and T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4), has improved the survival rates of patients with locally advanced or metastatic, non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

As the first-line treatment in the advanced/metastatic setting, clinical trials have shown that ICs may generate a sustained immune response and clinical efficacy. Patients in situ have high immune infiltration and relatively mechanisms for the activation of T cells to target tumor cells and metastases. Unfortunately, only few subsets of patients with LUAD benefit from ICs, due to de novo or acquired resistance.

The complex intratumoral TIME promotes high heterogeneity and clonal diversity of strategies that enable tumors to evade surveillance.

The counts of tumor cells, PD-L1, and score for Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion have been identified as potential indicators for predicting response to immunotherapy for patients with LUAD.

However, their practical application in the clinic are limited by lack of sensitivity and detection, the predictive accuracy in different clinical studies and therapeutic strategies are low, and thus, novel biomarkers for predicting immunotherapy ideal candidates for immunotherapy and design effective strategies.

Costimulatory molecules are made of B7-CD8 and tumor necrosis receptor family (TNF). The B7-CD8 family (CD80, CD86, PD-L1, PD-2, inducible T cell ligand (H-B17, H-B3a, and HERV-LR antigen 2) belonging to the B7 family (CD74, CTLA-4, inducible T cell checkpoint, PD-1, and TIMD2) belonging to the TNF family.

The family contains 19 molecules from the TNF ligand superfamily (TNSF) and 29 members from the TNF receptor superfamily. These costimulatory molecules provide signal for T cells, and participate in cell proliferation, differentiation, activation, and function. Hence, they serve as potential targets for current immunotherapy, which have been explored in prostate cancer and head neck squamous carcinoma.

However, PD-1, PD-L1, and CTLA-4, the majority of molecules are still poorly understood in LUAD. The exploration of costimulatory genes in patients with LUAD is presented here.

We utilized the transcriptional profiles of patients with LUAD obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Gene Expression Omnibus database to classify tumor subtypes ('cold' and 'hot'). A series of bioinformatics and machine learning algorithms were used to establish a novel signature based on CMGs in patients with LUAD.

This performance was validated with internal and external cohorts. Moreover, a small-sample immunohistochemistry (IHC) explored the correlation between the expression level of identified CMG biomarkers and the response to immunotherapy.

### Data Extraction and Processing

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Sun-Sen University Cancer Center (IRB No. B2019-116-1) and was conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent for the retrospective study was waived by the dean of this respective study.

Count data of transcriptome were acquired in the LUAD project from TCGA (TCGA, nci.cancer.nih.gov), last November 4, 2021.
