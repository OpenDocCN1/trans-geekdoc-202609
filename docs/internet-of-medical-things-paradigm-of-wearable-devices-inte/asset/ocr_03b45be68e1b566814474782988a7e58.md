# 4.4.2 Promoting Virtual Clinical Trials

Clinical trials, a critical issue in the drug industry, are burdensome and costly seeks in developing novel medicines. Rising expenses are because of the challenges in ending and enrolling competent individuals, organizing and controlling personalized procedures for many subjects over a long time, and dealing with the rising levels of loss-to-follow-up. Organizing procedures is costly for the demand to enroll individuals from definite areas, having access to special clinical trials, and to recruit dedicated and competent clinical personnel. The regional limits restrict the patterns and numbers of the trial sample [30].

Virtual clinical trials could simplify the sample, deliver a personalized experience, and minimize dedicated clinical personnel. The drug industry has adopted virtual techniques, especially with non-interventional clinical trials, and achieved variable outcomes for many factors influencing accuracy, protection, and confidentiality of data and problems of credibility, regulated adherence, and managing consents. The blockchain technique combined with the IoT-integrated sensors enables adequate credibility, protection, and confidentiality of data as demanded by regulating bodies [31].

Blockchain technologies enable an intelligent technique of contracting to guarantee that the participants' consent information is recorded, electronically signed, and sufficiently accountable. The incentives offered to the trial individuals would be token-based, instead of the present-day money-based ones, for growing participation, enhancing adherence, and developing more personalized experience. Finding and enrolling the trial individuals could be considerably simplified if the archived clinical data were accessible via ensuring data would be safeguarded jeopardizing privacy and security of confidential medical data of the participants. Widely implementing blockchain-derived PHRs enables pharmaceutical companies to search the global populations to rapidly spot the proper sample of varied participants worldwide. Ultimately, blockchain techniques could secure patient data, vastly growing data worldwide, and minimize clinical trials' load, complexity, and expenses [30].
