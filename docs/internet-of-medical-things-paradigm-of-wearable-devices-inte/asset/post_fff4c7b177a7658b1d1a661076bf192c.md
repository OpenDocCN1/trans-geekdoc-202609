In all of the above studies, different types of CAs are used to facilitate symptom monitoring, treatment adherence, question-solving and the promotion of health habits. These strategies were designed to provide better and increased care to those who need it, due to the increasing number of patients with chronic diseases. In qualitative results, through an interview, all the studies evaluated how participants felt after their experience with the respective CA. The participants appreciated the level of personalisation [13], considering it an attractive and convenient option to facilitate self-management [50]. In the same way, in some cases, the study participants demonstrated improvements in their condition by decreasing the frequency with which symptoms occurred [9, 50].

Subsequently, with regard to education and counseling about a specific issue, the existing literature is recent because this area emerged a short while ago to complete the efforts of health professionals to educate and support on certain issues. As is the case of Crutzen et al. [16], who in 2011 presented a chatbot that answered questions about sex, and alcohol. In 2013, Edwards et al. [19] presented an interactive agent that provided breastfeeding information and support to mothers interested in breastfeeding. In the same year, Gardner et al. [24] developed an animated character to promote preconception care, improving the health of women and mitigating risk factors prior to pregnancy. Lastly, in 2018, Zhou et al. [66] presented a virtual agent to advise people with alcohol problems about their situation.

Although the above-mentioned stories were the same, the purpose is to help patients resolve their questions by which they were denied, allowing them to develop their own understanding of the problem and what can be done about it. As the qualitative results of these four studies, just three of them measure the level of satisfaction with the CA. Two of these used a 7-point satisfaction survey [19, 24] and one uses 5-point Likert Scale [16]. Despite this difference in evaluation, additionally, mean overall satisfaction with the CA was high. The patients were very satisfied and additionally, the CAs were perceived as a faster and more anonymous tool to seek reliable information [16].

Last but not, there were four documents in the literature related to health behavior change. All of these aimed at encouraging patients to change their health behavior de bad habits that can diminish the quality and duration of patients' lives. For these researches, researchers have developed some technology strategies based on evidence, which are described below. 2015, Bickmore et al. [8] proposed a relational agent for older adults who would play the role of physical activity advisor. In 2021, Schulman et al. [55] presented a virtual agent to promote physical exercise, improving people's motivation and confidence through dialogue. In 2013, Lisset et al. [34] proposed a customized virtual to encourage the reduction of alcohol consumption in problem drinkers through motivational interventions. And finally, in the same year, Bickmore et al. [11] proposed an automated agent to promote both physical activity and fruit and vegetable consumption through a series of conversations.

By conducting a 7-point satisfaction survey [55] and a Godspeed questionnaire [6, 34], it could be concluded that the CAs were accepted and liked. ## 7.3.2 REHABILITATION

According to the WHO, rehabilitation is a set of interventions needed when a person is experiencing limitations in everyday functioning due to aging or a health condition, chronic diseases or disorders, injuries or traumas [45]. Examples of limitations in function includes difficulties in relationships, communicating, and moving around, among others [5]. Based on the forego, the area of rehabilitation is a very broad domain that includes several specialties, (i) communication skills rehabilitation, (ii) speech and language therapy, (iii) physical rehabilitation and (iv) psychological rehabilitation.

At present, the need for rehabilitation is largely unmet due to, in many-low and middle-income countries, a lack of trained professionals to provide rehabilitation services, with less than 10 skilled practitioners per million population [45]. That is why researchers and developers support the use of CAs to supplement the rehabilitation processes of people with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) [59, 64], neurological conditions [27, 52, 62], mobility-impairment [17, 57] and psychological trauma [53], which represent a large percentage of the total number of people with disabilities.
