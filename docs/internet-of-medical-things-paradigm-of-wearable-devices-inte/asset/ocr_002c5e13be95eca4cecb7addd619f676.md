# 6.2 PHYSICAL EXERCISE IN REHABILATION

Physical Exercise (PE) is understood as any activity that requires contracting muscles and more energy than a resting state [67]. Performing physical activity provides numerous benefits to people's health, such as the improvement of the cardiovascular respiratory system and the development of muscular groups, which are essential for daily life tasks [61]. The World Health Organization (WHO) highly recommends PE to prevent and treat many chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular diseases, cancer, stroke, and diabetes [78].

Several rehabilitation programs have incorporated PE in their therapies; for example, in (i) cardiac rehabilitation, to increase the cardiovascular performance [19, 32, 54]; (ii) oncology rehabilitation, to ease the effects of pathological fatigue [18, 65], which is a symptom normally presented in patients with cancer [46]; (iii) neuromuscular rehabilitation, to recover mobility of limbs, and by regaining the affected neural paths [15, 38, 68]; (iv) pulmonary rehabilitation, to raise the pulmonary capacity of the patients [26, 66]; and (v) musculoskeletal rehabilitation, to recover joint mobility and muscle strength after surgery [22, 23]. The inadequate use of PE can lead to extreme physical or physiological conditions that can result in health complications, especially if heart or pulmonary disease is present [41]. Therefore, some considerations need to be taken into account when implementing PE in rehabilitation and designing exercises according to each individual's needs and conditions [53].
