In this sense, physical activity is a key factor in prevention of chronic diseases like overweight [44] and, in some cases, illness such as Parkinson's disease [20]. Performing physical activity has significant benefits, consequently the development of technological alternatives that promote regular, planned and structured exercise could improve the quality of life of the global population. To this end, CAs are a good way to promote physical activity through a series of simulated conversations and accompaniment during training sessions.

Based on the existing literature, the main purpose of CAs in this field is to motivate and support users to perform regular exercise, especially in a population that is overweight and has a sedentary lifestyle and motor limitations in Table 7.5. Actually, there are two domains where the intervention of CAs can increase adherence to physical exercise; as a consequence, people can lose weight [33, 63] and manage their physical and physiological conditions [12].

First, in relation to weight, the fight against obesity and excessive weight in adolescents and adults requires the development of effective interventions. For example, most guidelines recommend that people who are overweight or obese aim to lose 5-10% of their weight to achieve improvements in health. For these reasons, researchers have proposed strategies to lose weight by increasing physical activity levels. As is the case for Watson et al. [63] who in 2012 proposed a virtual chat that promotes the performance of physical exercise on a regular basis. In this case, Le Rouge et al. [33] presented a study to address how avatars can help obese adolescents commit to weight loss.

In the first study [63], the participants reported beneficial changes in their lifestyle, such as exercising more and improving diet and eating habits. In the same way, they asked specific questions regarding their interactions with the virtual chat: 58% (of total participants) agreed that the chat motivated them to become more active, and 87% felt guilty if they skipped an appointment. In the second study [33], the adolescents felt great interest in including avatars in their overweight management programs to make it more fun and entertaining. Also, they felt the avatars could reinforce the guidance and support provided by lifestyle modification programs. As a conclusion of both studies, the use of CAs may be a means to increase the engagement of obese adolescents participating in a weight management program.

On the other hand, concerning the management of physical and physiological conditions, the strategies that currently exist are aimed at taking advantage of the potential role of exercise in reducing disabilities in persons with Parkinson's disease [20] and creating adherence to exercise in sedentary older adults [12]. Based on the above, Ellis et al. in 2013 promoted virtual exercise to reduce the physical limitations presented by their condition. In that year, Bickmore et al. [12] presented a CA for older adults who are in need of greater mobility, to promote and improve physical activity.

As a result of the first study [20], the level of satisfaction and adherence to the program was assessed. Overall, the participants had a mean satisfaction score of 5.6 over 7 (with 7 indicating maximal satisfaction). They successfully used the virtual coach to adhere to a walking program for 1 month, demonstrating the initial feasibility of this approach. In such a study [12], the level of satisfaction was assessed through interviews, where participants expressed a high level of satisfaction and acceptability towards the goal. In quantitative results, the number of steps was measured throughout the study, showing significant increases in walking from the first intervention.

All findings suggest the use of CAs in physical training may be useful adjusting automated applications to promote physical activity. Furthermore, given the current situation with a global pandemic, the promotion of programs that allow people to exercise at home will make it possible to combat the mental and physical consequences of the COVID-19 quarantine [28].

## 7.3.5 Palliative Care

The WHO estimates that more than 20 million people need palliative care or about one of them have cancer, and the rest suffer from degenerative diseases or life-threatening illnesses [40]. That is, the purpose of palliative care is not only to relieve pain, but also to alleviate the physical, psychosocial and emotional suffering of patients with serious illnesses in their advanced stages and to help the families of these people care for their loved ones [42].

In view of the above, palliative care is an emerging field in which CAs could intervene to relieve the suffering and worries of those with terminal diseases who do not respond to curative treatments. The current evidence is supported by two studies, which are shown in Table 7.6.

Based on the literature, there are two domains in the area of palliative care, where CAs can intervene: crisis support and end-of-life planning. Concerning the last one, in 2017, Utami et al. [61] presented a virtual agent that helps people manage emotions during their last year of life. The study was conducted in a single session, and a questionnaire was constructed on a scale of 1 (unsatisfied) to 7 (satisfied); older adults are comfortable discussing these topics with the agent. On the other hand, 2018, Easton et al. [18] presented a CA for supporting people with Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) by creating a therapeutic relationship with the agent. As a result, this study indicated that the benefits of this low-cost system increased self-confidence, less social isolation, and better patient awareness of the disease.

As shown above, due to a growing research interest in creating CAs that can attend to people, it is important that researchers and developers of CAs for healthcare delivery understand their limitations in order to improve and explore new features. In the first study, the participants reported a significant reduction in their anxiety state.

### 7.4.1 LIMITATIONS

In Section 7.3 it was proved that CAs are being tested and used to provide and collect information related to people's health and, in some cases, provide treatment and counseling services. In the majority of cases, CAs are based on mood and have limited conversations [25]; some of the others work in the background providing information when and needed [51]. Nevertheless, the scope and adaptability of these strategies is limited by the variability of user preferences, context and noise in the user input. Most CA applications do not integrate wearable devices, being a great limitation because the feedback, advice or assistance provided by the agent is not based on the state of the organism.
