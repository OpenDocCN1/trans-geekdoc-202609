Regarding the integration of wearable devices with CAs, few studies are found in the literature, only two recent studies clearly show an integrated system between wearable devices and CAs. Both studies were presented in 2018 by Samekhi et al. [56] and Bickmore et al. [9]. Firstly, Samekhi et al. presented a CA that acts as a virtual meditation coach for alleviating symptoms related to a variety of chronic health conditions, including pain, anxiety, and depression. Meditation takes practice and requires proper training that most people cannot perform. For these reasons, the designed CA can be used at any time, if a computer is available. Additionally, the CA is interactive and adaptive to a user’s breathing behavior, based on inputs from a respiration sensor as shown in Figure 7.3.

The respiration sensor is used to calculate the user’s breathing rate that the coach can provide feedback on at key points during the meditation. As a result of this study, participants felt that the virtual meditation CA was significantly more aware of their status when it incorporated information from a respiration sensor in its customized training. In the same way, participants also reported that their meditation was significantly more relaxing and helpful when the coach responded to their breathing. Regarding the level of acceptance, to evaluate the users’ reactions ranged from 1 (not at all satisfied) to 7 (very satisfied), scoring with an average of 6.11. In view of the above, these results indicate that users were highly receptive to the CA and appreciated the interactivity afforded by the respiration sensor.
