The current health model needs to be enhanced, to optimize personalized medicine, in all aspects from finding drug products to communicating with the consumer (patient). Delivering individualized care necessitates a breakthrough in the domain and a drastic modification in designing, producing, and distributing drug products. The existing fixed-dose pattern of mass-production for medical products does not offer adjustment or personalization of medicine to personal genes, metabolism, or the level of activity. Hence, the concepts of pharmaceutical product design should be shifted towards mass adaptation of information-based medical products [5]. The process of pharmaceutical product discovery and development needs improvement via enrolling patients in patient-centered clinical trials and applying the principle of the digitized patient into the needed handling framework, adhering to the existing data safeguards with no breaching of patent. Such a principle covers the domain of Big Data and machine learning-driven diagnosis [6–8].

Nowadays, investing in the pharmaceutical industry is greatly devoted to finding drugs and, using the result produced is designed applying leeway; for example, the ancient composition technique for designing solid dosage forms (tablets) [9]. Ultimately, the production process is universally conventional and unwilling to change, primarily for the legislative and regulating workload [10, 11]. Yet, the medication prices have risen drastically, adding to the fiscal burden of the community and having unsatisfactory levels. Here, a novel technique is highly needed to minimize the medication cost via reconstructing the drug product design guidelines, associated production interventions, and distribution patterns. Such a technique would secure access to affordable, safe, and effective medicines by switching from the batch manufacturing of fixed-dose products to producing-demand individualized products. Such products could be designed to include information models; for instance, rapid responding codes, facilitating monitoring of a single product from the production unit to the patient side [12].

## 4.2 BLOCKCHAIN TECHNOLOGY IN HEALTHCARE

Blockchain stands for a computational system of recording transactions conducted in cryptocurrency and maintaining them across several servers interconnected in a peer-to-peer network [13]. Although its origin can be traced back to finance because of its inherent features and the transaction-based model of operation that these fields have incorporated. As far as its features are concerned, blockchain comprises various features including distributed ledger, decentralized storage, authentication, security, and immutability. As far as the spread of a transaction-based operational model across disciplines is concerned, healthcare services are considered providers delivering care as a product. These transactions include an impressive deal of personal, confidential, and technical data with potential implications on individuals' well-being and professional development, among others [14]. Violations of such data have led many voices to support a blockchain healthcare vision system, where practitioners gain more autonomy, and patients will not be afraid of having their data communicated with third parties [15].

### Blockchain-Based IoT for Personalized Pharmaceuticals

Except for doctor–patient communication and individual patient records, other elements of healthcare need similar technical infrastructure. The pharmaceutical sector is probably one of the most crucial. A popular saying goes like, "All drugs can be a poison; the dosage makes them remedy or poison." Amid the 4th Industrial Revolution, quote could be recited in terms of pharmaceutical data [16]. There is a huge data flow concerning medicines, not only those that are already in use, but those that are currently developed and tested. Clinical trials, the process of testing novel medicines on humans, is based on the quality and the quantity of data testing for the efficacy and the safety of an agent compared to a placebo. Any information related to the participants is remain confidential by individual outcomes for the sake of randomization [17]. Emerging branches of pharmaceutical science, particularly pharmacovigilance and pharmacogenomics, require a significant amount of data to be safely stored and protected. Unauthorized access to such information can lead to falsified approval and allegation on medicines not only doctor–patient communication, but also the life and well-being of patients [18].

Why can blockchain contribute to this? The biomedical applications of blockchain require stronger authentication, interoperability, and record sharing modalities. These require standing on ethical and legal grounds, taking into account the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) and the General Data Protection Regulation of 2018 (GDPR) [14, 19]. Adapting blockchain modalities to the context of healthcare has already led to considerable advantages and ever more. Such modalities include smart contracts, fraud detection, and sound systems of identification. In pharmaceuticals, the aforementioned can work as additional valves safeguarding integrity and confidentiality subsequently producing reliable data. There are still concerns regarding the inherent vulnerabilities of blockchain on the quality and the inadequate digital literacy of the users, let alone mining incentives, attacks, and key management [20].

## 4.3 THE INTERNET OF THINGS IN HEALTHCARE

The Internet of Things (IoT) is described as a network of physical devices that uses connectivity to enable the exchange of data. These devices can either be contemporary or cutting-edge technology. What is remarkable about the IoT is fast streamline processes that enable workers to complete tasks in a timely way. The IoT is expected to be supported by the 5G network infrastructure, which guarantees the safety of large datasets' transfer and analysis [21]. In the last years, public or private entities specialized in healthcare or technology have developed their interest and investment in the IoT. Now, from the existing devices, smartphones with wearable-related apps and imaging machines, are manufactured with connectivity modalities such as infrared, Bluetooth, or Wireless Fidelity (Wi-Fi). This is a remarkable asset to form data-exchange networks where the provision of healthcare relies on the exchange of data, such as imaging and laboratory tests, patient records, and research results [21, 22].

IoT-enabled medical devices provide critical data transfer and analysis services that assist healthcare practitioners and researchers in a wide range of tasks, from emergency to regular procedures.

The benefit of incorporating IoT modalities in healthcare is twofold. Not only it facilitates a large load of work to improve and speed up biomedical research and clinical practice, but it also relieves burden of health practitioners allowing them to be more creative and more empathetic [14, 23]. The potential of the IoT infrastructure in remote patient monitoring, treatment and outcome, vaccines, and medicines has become obvious. In terms of cost, any similar IoT health platform can be owned and operated by a specialized center, providing services to patients all over a region or even a country. So far have been few IoT applications in healthcare finding their way to clinical practice [24].

Beyond the collection and analysis of data, IoT data platforms such as Kaa (KaaIoT Technologies), MindSphere (Siemens) and Azure (Microsoft) have IoT-based data processing features leading to meaningful actionable. Such modalities are expected to be used widely as far as commercial benefit and concerns. Physical activity levels in combination with caloric burn and intake, nutritional medicines compliance data report, heart rate, and blood glucose monitoring have already valuable at an individual level and can make a difference if extrapolated in population research by IoT modalities. Human control is important, but if the collection and processing are semi-automated, or even automated, it can speed up the production of the results and their translation in clinical practice [25, 26].

## 4.4 APPLICATIONS OF THE IOT AND BLOCKCHAIN IN THE PHARMACEUTICAL INDUSTRY

As previously mentioned, blockchain is a developing set of ledgers (chunks) where the ledgers are linked by the hashing cryptographic technology. The highly famous application of this technology is the cryptocurrency bitcoin. An effort of large-scale blockchain integration in healthcare is currently taking place in countries like the United Kingdom. Starting from January 2016, the authorities have communicated relevant information. This stated the National Health Service as a potential beneficiary of blockchain modalities, to improve delivery of services and communicating patients' records and sensitive information in a confidential and secure manner, abiding by the existing regulations [27]. Philips with the Philips Blockchain Lab has partnered with blockchain record-keeping-start Tier to examine how blockchain technology could be used in the healthcare sector since 2018 [27]. Regarding the pharmaceutical setting, blockchain implies interesting potential such as investigating a lifelong medication history.
