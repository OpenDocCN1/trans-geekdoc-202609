# 7.1 INTRODUCTION

In the 1950s, research in Artificial Intelligence (AI) was supported by a group of scientists and researchers who predicted that machines would be capable, in a few decades, of carrying out any task that humans could. Nevertheless, it was not until 60 years later that the Conversational Agents (CAs) were started with Siri (Apple, 2011) [51], which provided a speech interface for question answering. Following Apple, some companies, including Google, T&T, Nuance, have successfully participated in the market of smart CAS [25] creating awareness in millions of consumers that indeed smartphones could answer limited set of questions, execute simple tasks and, in some cases, be friendly.

Since the creation of the first CAS, their common functionality was their capacity to interpret natural language via spoken interaction and to provide answers in the form of a running software program (e.g., making calls, sending text messages, setting reminders, etc.) or a spoken response (“What is the capital of Colombia?”, Which is the nearest Colombian restaurant?” [25]. Over time, interest in offering better services increased, and in response, the idea of personifying CAs was revolutionary [14] giving rise to the Embodied Conversational (ECAs).

An example of every ECA is SimCoach (US Institute for Creative Technologies, 2013) [22], which was created to respond to healthcare challenges caused by the conflicts in Iraq and Afghanistan in the U.S. population. The SimProject developed different architectures of support agents to serve as online guides for assisting the military and their families initiate the healthcare process [53]. Despite its potential use, it was not considered to deliver diagnosis or treatment, or as a replacement for human providers and experts.

Currently, ECAs are not the only name given to CAs, according to their characteristics they can also be called chatbot [32], avatar [64], virtual human [29], among others [25]. Despite the different names they receive, the definitions are rather similar with variations of character that demonstrates many of the same properties as humans or in a face-to-face situation.

As CAs evolved, so wearable devices; advances as heart rate monitors, smartwatches, activity monitors, and other wearables, have mobile devices ideal for healthcare delivery [48,51] and with working together with CAs opened up a new world of possibilities. For example, there are currently a large number of applications that can track user activity, eating habits and analyze signals as blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, skin temperature, etc., by interacting with various wearable devices as shown in Figure 7.1.

The use of wearable devices has been emerging due to their potential to improve the level of healthcare delivery and reducing costs. For this reason, the possibility of innovation by means of using wearable devices with the intervention of CAs could allow in some cases immediate feedback, supplementing the shortage of staff in primary healthcare centers and thereby reducing the burden on providers.
