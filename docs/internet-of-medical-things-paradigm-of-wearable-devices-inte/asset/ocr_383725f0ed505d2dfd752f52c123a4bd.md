# 5.1 INTRODUCTION

Microwaves are electromagnetic radiations with frequency ranging from 300 MHz and 300 GHz. Due to their basic properties like penetration, absorption, reflection, high-frequency and short wavelength, they find applications in disease diagnosis (imaging process), therapy (treatment of cancer using hyperthermia) and prevention [1, 2]. Wireless transmission of signals over a distance allows patient health to be remotely monitored. This eliminates the need to keep the patient in the hospital for many days, which reduces the total cost incurred by the patient. This is already being used in glucose monitoring, endoscopy, insulin pumps and for testing other body fluids.

Keeping in this view, the research on antennas and the signal propagation for body communication, an IEEE 802.15 standardization group was set up for standardizing applications focusing on off-, in- and on-body communication. The in-body equipment are used to communicate between the medical devices implanted in the body (for example, WCE) and the on-body nodes. Off-body communication is the link connecting body-worn devices and the receiver unit in the surrounding area [3]. Body-Centric Communication (BCC) occupies an important place in Body Area Networks (BANs) and Personal Area Networks (PANs).

# 5.2 BIOMEDICAL TELEMETRY

The use of implantable devices such as pacemakers, antennas, swallowable pills for monitoring different internal parameters of the body is increasing day-by-day. Such devices usually consist of an antenna system and power supply unit [4], so that after recording the signals, the information can be transmitted to the receiver unit outside the human body.
