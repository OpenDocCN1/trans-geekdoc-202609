# 1.5 COMMUNICATION PROTOCOLS

Communication protocols are considered the backbone of IoT-based systems, as they enable network connectivity and data exchange over the network. They determine data encoding, data exchange formats, and package routing from source to destination. They are also used in sequence control, flow control, and retransmission of lost packets [30].

The following protocols are most commonly used in IoT:

- 1. IEEE 802.11 – WiFi: WiFi is a collection of Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) communication standards: 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11ac, and 802.11ad operating in the band of 2.4 to 60 GHz. They provide data exchange rates from 1 Mb/s to 6.5 Gb/s and a communication range in order of 20 m (meter) to 100 m (meter).

- 2. IEEE 802.16 – WiMAX: WiMAX (Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access) provides data exchange rates from 1.5 Mb/s to 1 Gb/s. It provides an average data rate of 1 Gb/s.

- 3. IEEE 802.15.1 – Bluetooth: Bluetooth is a low-power technology.

- 4. LoRaWAN – LoRa: LoRaWAN is a long-range protocol.

The IoT has undoubtedly affected the healthcare industry. Its corporation has brought benefits that have improved the efficiency and quality of therapies, treatments, and follow-ups that have positively impacted the health and lives of patients.

# 1.7.1 BENEFITS

Among the palpable benefits of the IoT in healthcare we can identify:

- 1. Remote medical assistance: IoT solutions allow physicians to assist patients who can be remotely monitored. Physicians can talk to the patients, check their vital signals using specialized Apps and Internet-connected devices, diagnose them, and prescribe the corresponding medicines. Besides the benefits to the patient, remote medical assistance impacts hospital costs as well; is moving medical appointments from hospitals (hospital-centric) to the patients' homes (home-centric) optimizing infrastructure resources.
