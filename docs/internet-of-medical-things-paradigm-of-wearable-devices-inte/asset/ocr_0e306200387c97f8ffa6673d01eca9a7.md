# 4.5 DISCUSSIONS

According to the Office for Science suggestions, patients' records can be operated via a decentralized blockchain rather than being safeguarded in a central database. Blockchain decreases the risk of unauthorized access through protocols governing the methods and the people or entities allowed to access such data. For example, a secretary of a primary care facility might only access essential information about a patient, such as their name and address. A doctor would have complete access to all medical information [14, 27]. Patients could access their own records as well and interact with them in a moderated manner. Suggestions include patients to specify preferences of treatment or a list of members who could participate in decisions concerning their health. Consent can be moderated and documented easily in this manner. This applies to keeping track of their records regularly and also to people with a complicated medical history who need to decide on emergency conditions [38].

The communication between the public and private healthcare facilities would be easier. So these facilities use their own owning systems. Access to each other is complicated and most requires arduous bureaucratic procedures and hand of data. Providing patients with the option to access to their records contained on a blockchain system to all healthcare providers, including private healthcare entities, has been regarded as a significant aspect [16]. This has already been implemented in Estonia, where a data security start-up, Guardian, was established. This collaboration deploys a blockchain-based safeguarding over one million healthcare records. With Guardian's key signature infrastructure integrated into the Estonian eHealth Foundation's database, patients and practitioners have real-time access to the records. Similarly, IBM Watson Health announced in 2017 a collaboration with the US FDA in defining a secure, efficient, and scalable exchange of health data via blockchain modalities [27].

George Bernard Shaw has stated, “the single biggest problem with communication is the illusion that it is happening”. Similarly, the single biggest problem with blockchain modalities can be the fact that people who are not trained and familiarized with this technology expect and are expected to implement in their working conditions formidable results rapidly [39]. Blockchain literacy is an important obstacle. Not all governments have released policies regarding cryptocurrency in countries such as the United States (US), Russia, and France have probably cultivated such mistrust [40]. In terms of policymaking, the involvement of international institutions or political schemes such as the United Nations (UN), the World Health Organization (WHO), or the European Union (EU) can increase interest among national governments and healthcare systems.
