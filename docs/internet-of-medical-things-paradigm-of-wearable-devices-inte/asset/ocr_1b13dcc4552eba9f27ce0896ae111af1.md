# Internet of Medical Things

Wearable devices for medical applications, orable Health Devices (WHDs), such as sensing and actuating have become the basis of lots of revolutionary digital health fields. Examples are wearable electrocardiography (ECG) monitors, blood pressure sensors, They consist of, such as motion sensors, which include accelerometers to collect huge amounts of data for medical and fitness applications, record step rate, and motion acceleration and speed. Also, these sensors can help in collecting information on post-surgery, motor degradation measurement of patients, regulating sleep activity, REM sleep reduction, and post-traumatic stress disorders information [9–11].

There are many technologies for power available for WHDs such as resonance-based charging, inductive coupling and microwave. They are all different in terms of working frequency, power level, transfer distance, forming factors and size. They are all preferred in different applications, as microwave systems are used over longer distances at low power, inductive coupling systems are used limited distances at tens of kilometers, and resonance systems in the megahertz band at tens of centimeters. The implantable devices are used over shorter distances; these wireless systems have many misalignment. With wearable devices, resonance-based charging systems are most beneficial compared to others due to their charging flexibility and misalignments of sender and receiver coils [12–14].

In this chapter, after presenting an introduction in Section 3.1, Section 3.2 gives a comprehensive literature review. wearable devices and their parameters for medical applications. Section 3.4 and 5 present the applications and key challenges respectively in wearable devices. Conclusion is drawn in Section 3.6.

# 3.2 LITERATURE REVIEW

The compact and precise antenna design having a novel Electromagnetic Bandgap (EBG) structure for medical application is presented at 2.4 GHz. It demonstrates a compact, robust and low-profile solution for wearable applications requirements. It is shown that the EBG structure minimizes the effect of frequency detuning and back-radiation of a large loss of human body. Also, the proposed design antenna 46 × 46 × 2.4 mm³ dimension leads to 27(2.17–28.3) GHz bandwidth with 7.8 dBi gain. It is concluded that antenna is a potential solution for wearable devices in various domains [2].

A human monitoring system is designed and analyze human body ailments. It is shown that smart WHDs may provide health data and alerts with various types of sensors, as well as notifying them using smartphones. This reduces the load of IoT-connected network and the overall cost of users. It is also shown that this proposed monitoring system can provide identical communication to various IoT devices [5].

A U-networking framework based on ultrasonic communications for WHDs is presented here. The bit-error-rate achieved (BER) is less than 10⁻⁵ at 2 kHz bandwidth, 20 mW power and 2.76 kbit/s rate. It is concluded that this proposed design can be implemented for reconfigurable data processing applications. It offers higher performance, higher data rates and lower energy consumption [9].
