# 7.3.2 REHABILITATION

According to the WHO, rehabilitation is a set of interventions needed when a person is experiencing limitations in everyday functioning due to aging or a health condition, chronic diseases or disorders, injuries or traumas [45]. Examples of limitations in function includes difficulties in relationships, communicating, and moving around, among others [5]. Based on the forego, the area of rehabilitation is a very broad domain that includes several specialties, (i) communication skills rehabilitation, (ii) speech and language therapy, (iii) physical rehabilitation and (iv) psychological rehabilitation.

At present, the need for rehabilitation is largely unmet due to, in many-low and middle-income countries, a lack of trained professionals to provide rehabilitation services, with less than 10 skilled practitioners per million population [45]. That is why researchers and developers support the use of CAs to supplement the rehabilitation processes of people with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) [59, 64, 65], neurological conditions [27, 52, 62], mobility-impairment [17, 57] and psychological trauma [53], which represent a large percentage of the total number of people with disabilities.
