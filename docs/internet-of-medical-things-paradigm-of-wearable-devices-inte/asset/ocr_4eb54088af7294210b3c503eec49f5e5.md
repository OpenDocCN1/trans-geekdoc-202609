Both studies evaluated the effect of the agent intervention using different strategies. In the first study [64], the impact was evaluated through feedback given by students. Each student interacted with the games for 10 to 15 minutes, and at the end of the interaction, students were requested to fill up a feedback form (unspecified). All of them expressed acceptance and appreciation for the virtual agent. On the other hand, in the second study [59], the system's effectiveness was evaluated by comparing pre- and post-training scores predicted by the system. Result of this study showed significant improvement in social skills pre- and post-training, as well as the relationships between overall social skills and non-verbal behaviors.

Secondly, with regard to speech, there are several types of interest, of which there are many reasons why speech can be affected. For example, as a result of suffering from neurological diseases [27], aphasia [62] and ASD [65], children and adults experience speaking and communication problems. This is why researchers developed rehabilitation strategies to improve the quality of life of people suffering these limitations. It is the case of Wik et al. [66], who in 2009 described an embodied conversational agent for language learning in children and younger. The agent takes the role of a teacher who guides and encourages feedback. In 2014 vanBuren et al. [62] proposed a virtual therapist for delivering speech and language therapy to people with aphasia by visibly specifying speech. And finally, in Ireland et al. [27] tested an artificial agent to converse with the user, and in turn measure voice and communication outcomes in the daily life of the user with Parkinson's disease and dementia, who have difficulties in language and communication.

Three different strategies were used by each of the students to measure their effect. For example, in the first study [65], a request was sent to all the people who had the agent to fill out a questionnaire to get some qualitative feedback. All questions were multiple choice with the range from 1 (I do not agree at all) to 5 (I totally agree). On average, the scores were over 3.29, which led to the conclusion that the agent has been well received by the people. In the second study, no assessment was made regarding perception or acceptability. As a conclusion, the study indicated that for persons with aphasia, receiving treatment supported by a virtual therapist can lead to faster learning. Finally, to the third study [27], participants were given time to speak with an agent and subsequently asked to share their experiences. And participants identified identified problems such as processing speed.
