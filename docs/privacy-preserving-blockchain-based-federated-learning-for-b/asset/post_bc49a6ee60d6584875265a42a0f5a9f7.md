## 5.4. Comparison with other methods

Many studies have been conducted to detect brain tumors, including [44,52,53,55,58–60]. These studies, however, often overlook the importance of data sharing in enhancing global model performance, particularly in medical imaging where data scarcity, due to privacy concerns, is a significant hurdle. Our proposed framework addresses this gap by incorporating a privacy-preserving mechanism that enables collaborative training while maintaining data confidentiality. Unlike traditional methods, our approach ensures robust performance even in data-limited scenarios. We provide a detailed comparison of our federated permissioned blockchain algorithm with both contemporary deep learning models and classical approaches in brain tumor detection (refer to Table 6). Our results demonstrate that our method maintains high accuracy, comparable to scenarios where the entire dataset is used, by effectively distributing data across multiple entities and securely aggregating model weights through our innovative blockchain-based federated learning system. The proposed method outperforms these methods across all metrics, which includes the Dice score and Hausdorff distance for enhancing tumor (ET), whole tumor (WT), and tumor core (TC) on both validation and test datasets of BraTS 2020.

These findings can be attributed to the effective aggregation of local model updates within the blockchain-based federated learning (FL) framework, which appears to enhance the model’s generalization capabilities across diverse data distributions from various institutions. The consistent performance across different tumor regions indicates that the proposed method can adeptly handle the variability and complexity inherent in brain tumor segmentation tasks.

By examining the convergence graphs, it is clear that the proposed method achieves robustness and generalization more rapidly than baseline methods. This is likely due to its proficiency in managing statistical heterogeneity among the data from different clients. This underscores the potential of blockchain-based FL not only to preserve privacy but also to bolster collaborative learning in a decentralized and secure environment.

Additionally, these outcomes not only underscore the feasibility of our approach in real-world medical applications but also highlight its potential to transform collaborative medical data analytics.

## 7. Conclusion

In this article, we focused on the problem of secure data sharing and trained a federated learning model in a decentralized network. The proposed framework, composed of federated learning and blockchain, provides secure data sharing and protects the data. Moreover, the blockchain-based algorithm aggregates local model updates while ensuring data privacy. Furthermore, we integrate the blockchain-based federated learning technique to develop an intelligent model that preserves the privacy of data providers and fosters trust in the data training process. The experimental results showed that our proposed method achieved a 1.99% improvement in the Dice similarity coefficient for enhancing tumors and a 19.08% reduction in Hausdorff distance for whole tumors compared to the baseline methods. These improvements are significant as they demonstrate enhanced accuracy and robustness in brain tumor segmentation, leading to better diagnostic capabilities and treatment planning. By addressing privacy concerns and improving segmentation performance, our framework represents a substantial step forward in the application of federated learning in medical image analysis. Future research will focus on optimizing the computational efficiency and latency of blockchain operations, exploring more efficient consensus algorithms, implementing parallel processing strategies, and addressing data heterogeneity challenges. These efforts aim to develop more robust, scalable, and practical solutions for secure data sharing and collaborative learning in the healthcare domain.

## 6. Limitation and future works

The blockchain-based federated learning framework we propose shows promise in enhancing privacy and security for collaborative brain tumor segmentation. However, it is important to recognize the limitations of our study and suggest potential areas for future research.

A significant limitation of the current framework is the increased computational overhead and latency caused by the blockchain process. Unlike traditional federated learning methods, our approach requires additional computations to maintain the blockchain and achieve consensus among nodes, resulting in longer training times and decreased efficiency. To address this issue and improve practically for real-world applications, further optimizations are necessary. Future research should investigate more efficient consensus algorithms and parallel processing strategies to streamline blockchain operations and reduce computational demands.

Another limitation lies in the framework’s ability to handle extreme data heterogeneity among participating clients. In practical scenarios, data distributions and characteristics can differ significantly across various hospitals or medical institutions. The performance and robustness of our framework under these heterogeneous conditions necessitate extensive evaluation. Future research should broaden the scope of experiments to include a larger number of clients with diverse data distributions, simulating varying levels of heterogeneity to assess the framework’s generalizability and identify areas for improvement. Moreover, exploring techniques such as transfer learning, domain adaptation, or personalized federated learning could help tackle the challenges posed by non-IID data distributions.

Ultimately, while the proposed blockchain-based federated learning framework represents a significant step towards enabling secure and privacy-preserving collaborative medical image analysis, there is still room for improvement and further investigation. By addressing the limitations related to computational efficiency and data heterogeneity, future research can build upon the foundation laid by this study and contribute to developing more robust, scalable, and practical solutions for secure data sharing and collaborative learning in the healthcare domain.
