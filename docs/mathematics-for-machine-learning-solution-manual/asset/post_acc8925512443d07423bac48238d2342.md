
E3 has dimension 1 (it could not be anything else anyway, as justified above) and

$E_1 = \text{span}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\-1\\-1\end{bmatrix}.$

(iv) Therefore, we can find a new basis $P$ as the concatenation of the spanning vectors of the eigenspaces. If we identify the matrix

$\boldsymbol{P} = \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 1 & 1\\-1 & -1 & 0\\-1 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix},$

whose columns are composed of the basis vectors of basis $P$, then our endomorphism will have the diagonal form: $\boldsymbol{D} = \text{diag}[3, 0, 0]$ with respect to this new basis. As a reminder, $\text{diag}[3, 0, 0]$ refers to the $3 \times 3$ diagonal matrix with $3, 0, 0$ as values on the diagonal. Note that the diagonal form is not unique and depends on the order of the eigenvectors in the new basis. For example, we can define another matrix $\boldsymbol{Q}$ composed of the same vectors as $\boldsymbol{P}$ but in a different order:

$\boldsymbol{Q} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 1\\-1 & -1 & 0\\0 & -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}.$

If we use this matrix, our endomorphism would have another diagonal form: $\boldsymbol{D}' = \text{diag}[0, 3, 0]$. Sceptical students can check that $\boldsymbol{Q}^{-1} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{Q} = \boldsymbol{D}'$ and $\boldsymbol{P}^{-1} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{P} = \boldsymbol{D}$.

### c.

$\boldsymbol{A} = \begin{bmatrix}5 & 4 & 2 & 1\\0 & 1 & -1 & -1\\-1 & -1 & 3 & 0\\1 & 1 & -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

$p(\lambda) = (\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 4)^2$

$E_1 = \text{span}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad E_2 = \text{span}\begin{bmatrix}1\\-1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix},\quad E_4 = \text{span}\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix}.$

Here, we see that $\dim(E_4) = 1 \neq 2$ (which is the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue 4). Therefore, $\boldsymbol{A}$ cannot be diagonalized.

### d.

$\boldsymbol{A} = \begin{bmatrix}5 & -6 & -6\\-1 & 4 & 2\\3 & -6 & -4\end{bmatrix}$

(i) We compute the characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda)=\det(A-\lambda I)$ as

$p(\lambda)=\begin{vmatrix}5-\lambda & -6 & -6\\ -1 & 4-\lambda & 2\\ 3 & -6 & -4-\lambda\end{vmatrix}$
$= (5-\lambda)(4-\lambda)(-4-\lambda)-36-36+18(4-\lambda)+12(5-\lambda)-6(-4-\lambda)$
$= -\lambda^3+5\lambda^2-8\lambda+4=(1-\lambda)(2-\lambda)^2,$

where we used Sarrus rule. The characteristic polynomial decomposes into linear factors, and the eigenvalues are $\lambda_1=1,\lambda_2=2$ with (algebraic) multiplicity 1 and 2, respectively.

(ii) If the dimension of the eigenspaces is identical to multiplicity of the corresponding eigenvalues, the matrix is diagonalizable. The eigenspace dimension is the dimension of $\ker(A-\lambda_i I)$, where $\lambda_i$ are the eigenvalues (here: 1,2). For a simple check whether the matrices are diagonalizable, it is sufficient to compute the rank $r_i$ of $A-\lambda_i I$ since the eigenspace dimension is $n-r_i$ (rank-nullity theorem).

Let us study $E_2$ and apply Gaussian elimination on $A-2I$:

$\begin{bmatrix}3 & -6 & -6\\ -1 & 2 & 2\\ 3 & -6 & -6\end{bmatrix}.$ (4.1)

(iii) We can immediately see that the rank of this matrix is 1 since the first and third row are three times the second. Therefore, the eigenspace dimension is $\dim(E_2)=3-1=2$, which corresponds to the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue $\lambda=2$ in $p(\lambda)$. Moreover, we know that the dimension of $E_1$ is 1 since it cannot exceed its algebraic multiplicity, and the dimension of an eigenspace is at least 1. Hence, $A$ is diagonalizable.

(iv) The diagonal matrix is easy to determine since it just contains the eigenvalues (with corresponding multiplicities) on its diagonal:

$D=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 2 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}.$

(v) We need to determine a basis with respect to which the transformation matrix is diagonal. We know that the basis that consists of the eigenvectors has exactly this property. Therefore, we need to determine the eigenvectors for all eigenvalues. Remember that $x$ is an eigenvector for an eigenvalue $\lambda$ if they satisfy $Ax=\lambda x \iff (A-\lambda I)x=0$. Therefore, we need to find the basis vectors of the eigenspaces $E_1,E_2$.

For $E_1=\ker(A-I)$ we obtain:

$\begin{bmatrix}4 & -6 & -6\\ -1 & 3 & 2\\ 3 & -6 & -5\end{bmatrix}+4R_2\quad\sim\quad\begin{bmatrix}0 & 6 & 2\\ -1 & 3 & 2\\ 0 & 3 & 1\end{bmatrix}\cdot(1/6)$
$\cdot(-1)\text{swap with }R_1\quad -\tfrac12 R_1$
~→ $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 & -2\\0 & 1 & \frac13\\0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ +3R₂ ~→ $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & -1\\0 & 1 & \frac13\\0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

The rank of this matrix is 2. Since 3 − 2 = 1 it follows that dim(E₁) = 1, which corresponds to the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue λ = 1 in the characteristic polynomial.

(vi) From the reduced row echelon form we see that $E_1 = \text{span}\left[\begin{matrix}3\\-1\\3\end{matrix}\right]$, and our first eigenvector is $[3, -1, 3]^{\top}$.

(vii) We proceed with determining a basis of $E_2$, which will give us the other two basis vectors that we need (remember that dim$(E_2)=2$). From (4.1), we (almost) immediately obtain the reduced row echelon form $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & -2\\0 & 0 & 0\\0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$, and the corresponding eigenspace $E_2 = \text{span}\left[\begin{matrix}2\\1\\0\end{matrix}, \begin{matrix}2\\0\\1\end{matrix}\right]$.

(viii) Overall, an ordered basis with respect to which $A$ has diagonal form $D$ consists of all eigenvectors is $B = \left( \begin{bmatrix}3\\-1\\3\end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix}2\\1\\0\end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix}2\\0\\1\end{bmatrix} \right)$.

## 4.8 Find the SVD of the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix}3 & 2 & 2\\2 & 3 & -2\end{bmatrix}$. $A = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2} & \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}}_{=U} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}5 & 0 & 0\\0 & 3 & 0\end{bmatrix}}_{=\Sigma} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2} & -\frac{3}{\sqrt2} & -\frac{2}{3}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2} & \frac{3}{\sqrt2} & \frac{2}{3}\\0 & -\frac{2\sqrt2}{3} & \frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}}_{=V}$

## 4.9 Find the singular value decomposition of $A = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 2\\-1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$.

(i) Compute the symmetrized matrix \(A^{\mathsf T}A\). We first compute the symmetrized matrix

\[
A^{\mathsf T}A=
\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\2&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}2&2\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}5&3\\3&5\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

(ii) Find the right-singular vectors and singular values from \(A^{\mathsf T}A\). The characteristic polynomial of \(A^{\mathsf T}A\) is

\[
(5-\lambda)^2-9=\lambda^2-10\lambda+16=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This yields eigenvalues, sorted from largest absolute first, \(\lambda_1=8\) and \(\lambda_2=2\). The associated normalized eigenvectors are respectively

\[
v_1=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
v_2=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

We have thus obtained the right-singular orthogonal matrix

\[
V=[v_1,v_2]=
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

(iii) Determine the singular values. We obtain the two singular values from the square root of the eigenvalues \(\sigma_1=\sqrt{\lambda_1}=\sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}\) and \(\sigma_2=\sqrt{\lambda_2}=\sqrt{2}\). We construct the singular value diagonal matrix as

\[
\Sigma=
\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_1&0\\0&\sigma_2\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}2\sqrt{2}&0\\0&\sqrt{2}\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

(iv) Find the left-singular eigenvectors. We have to map the two eigenvectors \(v_1,v_2\) using \(A\). This yields two self-consistent equations that enable us to find orthogonal eigenvectors \(u_1,u_2\):

\[
Av_1=(\sigma_1u_1v_1^{\mathsf T})v_1=\sigma_1u_1(v_1^{\mathsf T}v_1)=\sigma_1u_1=
\begin{bmatrix}2\sqrt{2}\\0\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\[
Av_2=(\sigma_2u_2v_2^{\mathsf T})v_2=\sigma_2u_2(v_2^{\mathsf T}v_2)=\sigma_2u_2=
\begin{bmatrix}0\\\sqrt{2}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

We normalize the left-singular vectors by dividing them by their respective singular values and obtain \(u_1=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(u_2=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}\), which yields

\[
U=[u_1,u_2]=
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

(v) Assemble the left-/right-singular vectors and singular values. The SVD of \(A\) is

\[
A=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}=
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{8}&0\\0&\sqrt{2}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
