### 12.1 Introduction

**Blockchain** is the emerging technology in the fields of secured sharing of medical data, cross-border payments, logistics and food selling, etc. Simply put, it is a decentralized, shared network computing that demonstrates the overall origin of the digital item. Blockchain is trying to extend its features in numerous new industries, given its high potential. With its expertise proven in the supply chain, banking, digital authentication, investment management, and the arts and music sector, it is now expanding into the fast-growing market of EV. The foremost barrier to EV adoption is range anxiety. The majority concern is about power capacity running out on long road journeys. This is due to the fact that electric chargers are not readily available everywhere and, as a result, of the absence of charging infrastructure, potential consumers remain hesitant to switch to EVs. As already said before, blockchain is a peer-to-peer (P2P) technology that enables users to share their chargers when not in use. Moreover, in turn, charger owners will be benefited by getting paid for using the chargers. It also allows people and organizations to exchange, purchase, and transfer extra energy between themselves without the intrusion of a central authority or general public having control over how and when transactions can happen.

Experts think that by allowing P2P charging, this new blockchain enterprise will minimize drivers’ range anxiety and boost the desirability of EVs in the future. This chapter shows the relationship between the broad field of machine learning (ML) and blockchain. Since ML is a venture of artificial intelligence (AI) where systems can analyze information processed from data, identify patterns, and make decisions with little or no human intervention, ML with Blockchain in Fig. 12.1 can create a deadly combination if utilized properly. Both are more or less like the two sides of a coin if used together. While blockchain helps to store correct data that is unaltered and permanent, ML can utilize this data to analyze data patterns and give accurate predictions.

In blockchain’s P2P network, mobile apps are used by users to access participating homeowners’ locations to charge their EV. It always keeps track of the time taken by each vehicle to fully charge and the amount of energy consumed for the same. Further, a ledger account helps in the implementation of payment in digital mode from the driver to the charger owner linked in the network. With the data collected from the blockchain, the data can be used by the ML algorithm to predict the availability of fast charging ports nearby. This will reduce the waiting time and charging time of the vehicle and will also lead to hassle-free transportation even as drivers can get rid of anxiety rising from driving range issues.

## 12.2 Types of blockchain technologies

### 12.2.1 Public blockchain network

Anyone can join public blockchains, and all the information is accessible by them. This makes blockchains suitable for firms that value transparency and trust concerns in data storage. As a result, NGOs and social support organizations can benefit from the public blockchain. The public blockchain is depicted in Fig. 12.2.

Because of its public nature, the data stored by a public blockchain cannot be used for private-sector operations since the information is not secured for the use of just one entity or organization but can be accessed by anyone. Furthermore, public blockchains can be costly to maintain since they require nodes to function as miners and run either proof-of-work or proof-of-stake (PoS) algorithms (PoS).

### 12.2.2 Private blockchain network

Because it provides a private network, the private blockchain is the right opposite of the public blockchain. It’s ideal for companies who desire a private network while still reaping the advantages of blockchain. They’re also centralized, which means a firm may govern the network without having to keep it accessible to the public.

They provide all of the key benefits of a blockchain, as well as a method for corporate members to develop trust through immutability and security.

The company can develop its own set of rules and operate the network according to its own needs, secure in private networks.

### 12.2.3 Consortium blockchain network

Next, rather than a centralized node (private) or a decentralized (public) network, a consortium blockchain network is functioned by a group of organizations or nodes.

A consortium blockchain is advantageous since it includes nodes that have been pre-selected.

It’s great for a solution that necessitates cross-departmental cooperation. Supply chain, food, and medicine, for example, would all necessitate cross-brand collaboration.

### 12.2.4 Hybrid blockchain network

Finally, we have the hybrid blockchain, which is the most recent blockchain technology type. This blockchain combines all of the advantages of both private and public blockchains with the least number of drawbacks.

In actuality, the hybrid blockchain, like other blockchain technology types, has drawbacks though fairly minor ones.

Hybrid blockchains appear to be ideal for a variety of prospective business models.

## 12.3 Blockchain applications

### 12.3.1 Peer-to-peer based energy exchange between renewable microgrids

Grid-connected microgrids, as demonstrated by TERI, will be used for access to energy, P2P energy markets, and utility network management. The blockchain verifies the transactions and ensures security, safety, and while maintaining efficiency in each step of transaction processing with the help of digital ledger basics. The blockchain is a chain of several digital units that collects and shares replicas of a transaction such that each node recognizes what transaction actually happened at a specific point in time.

A “transaction” is a method of exchanging data between two physically located nodes, the digital version of which is saved as a record on the blockchain, as shown in Fig. 12.3. The notion of cryptography, which falls under the domain of information security, is one of the bases on which blockchain technology is developed. Each physical node’s information is encrypted before being disseminated on a blockchain network using a private and public address key (Ping et al., 2020).

### 12.3.2 Peer-to-peer based vehicle charging

With this strategy, the blockchain has been proposed as a supporting tool for accelerating the adoption of EV in India. Several vehicles commuting through their itineraries might be charged at privately created EV charging stations, and also the transaction of charging site owners’ bids, energy flow, accounting, and legitimate transaction closure might all be handled by the blockchain.

The blockchain-enabled application could be used to increase the utilization of privately owned EV chargers through the proper linkages between EV customers and EV private owners in an attempt to compensate for a shortage of charging stations and to make greater use of existing facilities. EV owners can use the P2P mode of communication for accessing nearby charging stations to charge their vehicles’ batteries.
