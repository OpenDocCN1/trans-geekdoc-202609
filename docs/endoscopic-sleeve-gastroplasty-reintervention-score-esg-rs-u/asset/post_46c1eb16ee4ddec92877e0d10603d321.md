While the adverse effect profile of ESG is favorable compared to laparoscopic gastrectomy and Roux-en-Y bypass, patients with high ESGRS should be considered for closer monitoring. [23, 24] The variables from our study that are associated with increased risk of reintervention have previously been identified as risk factors for post-surgical complications. [25, 26, 27, 28, 29] Stratification and education of patients using a scoring system before ESG based on these variables can help predict and thereby prevent future complications, including reintervention.

There are certain limitations to this study. While we used pre-op predictors to derive the risk scoring system, additional predictors, like hospital-level information, were omitted that could have improved the AUC. The AUC between 0.7-0.8 has good predictability and internal validity; however, there is a need for external validity to optimize its precision in making clinical judgments. Since the study is limited to patients from North America, it may have limited generalizability for the world. It is unclear which procedures were conducted by a gastroenterologist and which were performed by a surgeon to determine if this could contribute to the probability of requiring further intervention. Certain variables may be significantly associated with reintervention but may lack predictive power and hence be excluded after LASSO regression. Moreover, a database does not have information on MBSQIP center designation therefore the factor pertaining to ‘volume of ESG performs’ is not feasible to be considered in the present study. Despite the limitations above, we utilized a vigorous 10-fold cross-validation for internal validation, which delivers a lower bias of estimates than split-sample validation, resulting in more predictions. Despite being a retrospective database, the longitudinal structure of the data confirmed that on the overall volume of bariatric cases, ESGRS score may improve knowledge about the patient-level risk of reintervention within 30 days after longitudinal studies are needed to see whether addressing the identified factors prospectively will mitigate the need for endoscopic reintervention within 30 days.
