
## Materials and Methods:

The present study utilized the Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery Accreditation Quality Improvement Program (MBSAQIP) database, the largest bariatric-specific database worldwide with procedure dates between January 2016 and December 2021. [10] All 88 accredited bariatric centers report data from all bariatric interventions performed at respective centers to MBSAQIP. The MBSAQIP database includes pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative variables, including patient comorbidities and 30-day postoperative outcomes. Due to privacy concerns, the MBSAQIP database does not report hospital locations, patient identifiers, or physician data. Only complete cases are included in the database. MBSAQIP registry performs regular audits and excludes centers with poor 30-day follow-up rates (below 80%) or greater than a 5% disagreement rate. [11]

## Study Population

This study utilized the MBSAQIP database 2016-2021 to identify patients who underwent Endoscopic sleeve gastrostomy (ESG). A intervention was defined as an endoscopic, surgical, or radiologic intervention procedure completed following ESG within 30 days of the primary procedure. [12] These also included any interventional radiology or surgical interventions as previously defined. [12] Cases were excluded if age was less than 18, underweight (BMI < 30), incomplete data, miscoded, and those undergoing revision or conversion procedures. [13] Potential clinical variables in the database were assessed a priori based on scientific knowledge and predictors identified in recent literature. [2] Variables with missing data were excluded. A correlation matrix was used to evaluate all explanatory variables for collinearity (Supplementary File 1). Variables with a correlation coefficient > 0.7 were excluded if present. The candidate risk factors include demographics (i.e., age, body mass index, gender), clinical variables, laboratory data (i.e., albumin, hematocrit, and creatinine), and comorbidity data (i.e., history of thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, etc.). ## Statistical analysis:

We utilized the "pmsample" command in STATA to compute the minimum sample size to develop a new multivariable prediction model with a binary outcome; the expected C-statistic was 0.95, and the number of potential inclusions in the new prediction model was 26. The minimum sample size required for the ESGRS was 2032. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) penalized regression was utilized to determine the adequate predictors of 30-day reintervention. LASSO selects a subset of predictors by shrinking the coefficients of the minor contributive variables to zero, thereby excluding them from the model. The tuning parameter lambda, which determines the coefficient shrinkage, was selected by cross-validation. [14] Independent odds ratios (ORs) of LASSO-selected variables were assessed, and the predictors identified by LASSO regression analysis were entered into multivariable logistic regression models. We utilized backward stepwise selection to select variables with a P-value below 0.05, for the final multivariable logistic regression model. Any variables with P-values < 0.05 were excluded from the final risk score model, as done previously. [15] A 10-fold cross-validation generated a pragmatic estimation of the model's predictive performance (area under the curve (AUC)) in new cases since it prevents excessively optimistic estimates of predictive performance in overlooked cases. [14] The AUC's value indicated the model's ability to predict 30-day reintervention. An AUC of 0.5 to 0.6 indicates unsatisfactory discrimination, 0.6-0.7 indicates satisfactory, 0.7 to 0.8 is good, 0.8 to 0.9 is very good, and more than 0.9 is excellent. [16] We also generated Bias-corrected bootstrapped (BC) 95% confidence intervals for the AUC.

The model-predicted and actual 30-day reintervention agreement was evaluated via calibration belt plots. This allows a visual inspection of any difference of observed frequencies significantly to expected probabilities and the direction of miscalibration. [17] A calibration test indicating if any deviations from the bisector [45° line of perfect fit] were significant was also included in the calibration belt plot. [18, 19] The Brier score, a variance measurement between the predicted and the observed outcome, was used to assess overall performance. [16] Its value range from 0 (no disagreement) to 1 (or complete disagreement), and lower is better.

## Results

A total of 3583 patients were included in the present analysis. The demographic and clinical characteristics are shown in Table 1. The 30-day endoscopic reintervention rate for ESG cases was 3.3%, even out of the 26 predictors were retained by LASSO regression for predicting 30-day reintervention, with a mean lambda of 0.003, including eleven variables were selected by LASSO, including the history of foregut surgery, preoperative anticoagulation (AC) use, female gender, long term steroid use, history of percutaneous coronary intervention, ASA score >=2, hypertension, preoperative GERD diagnosis, history of deep venous thrombosis, preoperative albumin, and hematocrit (table 2)

After multivariable logistic regression, five variables were statistically significant, including the history of foregut surgery (OR 4.98, 95% CI 3.08–8.06, P < 0.001), preoperative AC use (OR 2.22, 95% CI 1.10–4.51, P = 0.011), female gender (OR 1.48, 95% CI 1.05–2.01, P = 0.012), long-term steroid use (OR 1.85, 95% CI 1.02–3.35, P = 0.025), history of percutaneous coronary intervention (OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.15–3.90, P = 0.005), ASA score >=2 (OR 2.45, 95% CI 1.45–4.12, P = 0.001), hypertension (OR 1.68, 95% CI 1.12–2.51, P = 0.002), preoperative GERD diagnosis (OR 1.32, 95% CI 0.85–2.01, P = 0.125), history of deep venous thrombosis (OR 1.55, 95% CI 0.98–2.45, P = 0.045), preoperative albumin (OR 1.42, 95% CI 0.95–2.12, P = 0.065), and hematocrit (OR 1.28, 95% CI 0.82–1.98, P = 0.110).

## Discussion

The ESGRS was generated using variables selected using supervised machine learning, showing no miscalibration and good performance (AUC >0.7). Our results indicate that predicting the individualized risk of 30-day reintervention using five pre-operative predictors: history of foregut surgery, preoperative AC use, female gender, ASA score >=2, and hypertension. We suggest a helpful method to determine the individualized risk of reintervention after ESG, and to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to do.
