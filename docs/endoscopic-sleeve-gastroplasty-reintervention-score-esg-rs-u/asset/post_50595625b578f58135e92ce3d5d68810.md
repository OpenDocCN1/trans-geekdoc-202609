# Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty-reintervention score (ESG-RS) using supervised machine learning

## Abstract

### Background and Aims

Reintervention following Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG). We developed and internally validated a score (ESG-RS) that determines the individualized preoperative risk of reintervention within 30 days.

### Methods

We used data from a sample of 3,583 patients who underwent ESG in the Bariatric Surgery Quality Improvement Program (MBSAQIP) database (2016-2021). Least Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) penalized regression was utilized to select the best predictors. The variables extracted by LASSO regression were entered into multivariate analysis to generate ESG-RS.

### Results

Eleven variables were selected by LASSO regression. Five key factors influencing reintervention risk were: history of foregut surgery, preoperative AC use, female gender, ASA score >= 2, and hypertension. The model performance showed an AUC of 0.74 (95% CI: 0.70, 0.78). The optimal cut point for ESG-RS was 67.9 (high risk vs. low risk), with a sensitivity of 0.76 and specificity of 0.71.

### Conclusion

The ESG-RS aids clinicians in preoperative risk stratification and helps identify patients at a higher risk of reintervention, enabling better decision-making and patient management.

**Keywords:** Gastroplasty; Quality Improvement; reintervention; Risk Assessment; Machine Learning; Predictive Value of Tests

# Introduction

Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG) is a minimally invasive procedure to produce weight loss in patients who have been unresponsive to dietary and lifestyle modifications. It was first introduced by Dayyeh et al. in 2013. Since then, ESG has witnessed rapid growth and has some advantages over traditional bariat surgery, i.e., laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy, by the ability to achieve similar outcomes with shorter procedure time and comparable adverse event rate. ESG involves reducing the size of the gastric reservoir using a full thickness endoscopic suturing device. At 12 months, ESG resulted in total body weight loss and excess body weight loss of 16% and 60%, respectively. Previous comparisons demonstrated equivalent morbidity risk and non-inferiority in weight loss after SG. Recent data on ESG and LSG showed they were comparable but had a greater incidence of 30-day readmissions and reinterventions. Because ESG is a contemporary procedure, providers may have a lower threshold for reintervention due to unfamiliarity with the procedural course resulting in higher reintervention rates than SG. While factors associated with reintervention following SG have been explored previously, factors associated with reintervention following ESG have not been investigated.
