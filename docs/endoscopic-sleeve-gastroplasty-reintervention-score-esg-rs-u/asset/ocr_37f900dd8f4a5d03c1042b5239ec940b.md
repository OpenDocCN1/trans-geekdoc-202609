# Materials and Methods:

The present study utilized the Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery Accreditation Quality Improvement Program (MBSAQIP) database, the largest bariatric-specific database worldwide with procedure dates between January 2016 and December 2021. [10] All 88 accredited bariatric centers report data from all bariatric interventions performed at respective centers to MBSAQIP. The MBSAQIP database includes pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative variables, including patient comorbidities and 30-day postoperative outcomes. Due to privacy concerns, the MBSAQIP database does not report hospital locations, patient identifiers, or physician data. Only complete cases are included in the database. MBSAQIP registry performs regular audits and excludes centers with poor 30-day follow-up rates (below 80%) or greater than a 5% disagreement rate. [11]

# Study Population

This study utilized the MBSAQIP database 2016-2021 to identify patients who underwent Endoscopic sleeve gastrostomy (ESG). A intervention was defined as an endoscopic, surgical, or radiologic intervention procedure completed following ESG within 30 days of the primary procedure. [12] These also included any interventional radiology or surgical interventions as previously defined. [12] Cases were excluded if age was less than 18, underweight (BMI < 30), incomplete data, miscoded, and those undergoing revision or conversion procedures. [13] Potential clinical variables in the database were assessed a priori based on scientific knowledge and predictors identified in recent literature. [2] Variables with missing data were excluded. A correlation matrix was used to evaluate all explanatory variables for collinearity (Supplementary File 1). Variables with a correlation coefficient > 0.7 were excluded if present. The candidate risk factors include demographics (i.e., age, body mass index, gender), clinical variables, laboratory data (i.e., albumin, hematocrit, and creatinine), and comorbidity data (i.e., history of thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, etc.).
