# Introduction

Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG) is a minimally invasive endoscopic procedure to produce weight loss in patients who have been unresponsive to dietary and lifestyle modifications. It was first introduced by Abu Dayyeh et al. in 2013. [1] Since then, ESG has witnessed rapid growth and has some advantages over traditional bariat surgery, i.e., laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy, by the ability to achieve similar outcomes with a shorter procedure time and comparable major adverse affect rate with SG as defined by previous literature. [2, 3] ESG involves reducing the size of the gastric reservoir using a fullthickness endoscopic suturing device. [4] At 12 months, ESG resulted in total body weight loss and excess body weight loss of 16% and 60%, respectively. [5] Previous comparisons demonstrated an equivalent morbidity risk and non-inferiority in weight loss after 12 months between ESG and SG. [6] Recent data on ESG and LSG showed they were comparable regarding major adverse effects: However, they had a greater incidence of 30-day readmissions and reinterventions. [2]

Because ESG is a contemporary procedure, providers may have a lower threshold for reintervention due to unfamiliarity with the postcedural course resulting in higher reintervention rates than SG. [2] While factors associated with reintervention following SG have been explored previously, factors associated with reintervention following ESG have not been investigated. [2, 7] In a meta-analysis by Mohan et al., the pooled rate of adverse events after ESG was 2.9%. [8] The most common AEs include pain, nausea, upper gastrointestinal bleeding, leak or fluid collection, pulmonary embolism, and pneumoperitoneum. [9] Although most AEs can be managed conservatively, severe AEs, including gastric or peptic leak or fluid collections, may require additional intervention.

In this study, we aimed to develop a risk stratification score that helps determine the individualized preoperative risk of reintervention within 30 days following ESG.
