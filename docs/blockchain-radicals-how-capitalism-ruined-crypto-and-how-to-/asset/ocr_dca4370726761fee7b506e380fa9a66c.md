# The Merge to Proof of Stake

Much of what people have said about PoW energy use on Ethereum has become obsolete at this point. While it's unlikely that Bitcoin will ever move to a different consensus mechanism, and the implications of that for electricity use should certainly be studied and scrutin, the second largest cryptocurrency by market cap has moved away from PoW, as was planned since its launch, to Proof of Stake (PoS).

PoS is where a blockchain network is secured by stakeholders who "stake", or lock, some of the native cryptocurrency, in batches of 32 other in the case of Ethereum. The more someone stakes, the more likely they are to be randomly chosen by an algorithm to validate the next block on the blockchain. If a chosen stake is found to be attempting to check the system, they can be "slashed" or lose the cryptocurrency that they have staked, enforcing the rules laid out by the protocol. The hardware needs of a staking node are a fraction of what's needed for a mining node and can be as little as a Raspberry Pi or any other miniature computer. All that's needed is sufficient memory to hold the full state of the blockchain and maybe at least eight gigabytes of RAM, depending on the requirements.
