Furtherfield was also one of the first groups to begin exploring blockchains for art and boasts many of the first artists to use the technology that preceded NFTs as fellow collaborators. One example was Rhea Myers, who created several art pieces using smart contracts on Ethereum, even in its test network stage before it went live in 2015⁶ Her work was an early exploration of many of the properties that NFTs have today. Furtherfield also published a book entitled Re:Thinking the Blockchain that includes the works and writings of multiple artists and thinkers who were well ahead of their time.⁷ In a crypto world dominated by right-wing libertarianism, it was clear to that Furtherfield that this was not the end of the story.

In order to put the DIWO framework to the test in regard to blockchain, one of the projects group undertook was a new application they called CultureScale.⁸ One of these tests took place at Finsbury Park in London in 2021 as a project they called People’s Park Plinth⁹ During the event, throughout May, June and July, local residents were allowed to come to the park to look at three “taster” digital public works that incorporated local stories and spoke about the park’s heritage. In August the residents were then allowed to take part in a quadratic vote on the Ethereum blockchain to choose which piece of work they would like to be commissioned so that a larger version of it could be made for the park.
