Herndon has been experimenting with these technologies for long enough that she has developed her own AI model that can replicate the sound of her own voice in real time, no matter who is singing into the microphone. One of her latest projects is called Holly+, a custom voice instrument that allows anyone to upload polyphonic audio and receive a download of that music sung back in her voice. 18 In essence, Holly+ is a digital twin of her real voice which is open access for anyone to use.

As an artist who relies on her voice to make art, there is obviously a tension at first. Why give your voice away for free when that’s how you make money? However this approach is actually consistent with McKenzie Wark’s “My Collectible Art” principle, which states that the more prominent a work of art, the more valuable the original.

This development of AI-with-voice still raises novel questions about voice ownership that Herndon thinks can be addressed by DAO governance. Based on her model, open sourcing her voice leaves the possibility for its noncommercial use, however only those who are part of the Holly+ DAO are able to certify and profit from derivative works created from it. Membership of the DAO is determined by who owns VOICE tokens on Ethereum, which were airdropped to previous collectors of her work as well as others who supported the project. Artists can submit their works to be minted as an NFT and VOICE holders vote to approve or reject the work to be certified. All revenue from the sales of the NFTs is then given to the DAO. Thus, token holders are incentivised to certify works that contribute to the value of the voice, and not dilute that value through the production of bad art or negative associations. The tokens also allow for decisions to be made about what happens to the voice model and, potentially, decisions made off-chain if DAOs can become proper legal entities.
