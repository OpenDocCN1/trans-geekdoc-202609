
Maximally separated input vectors represented by $H$. Then,

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_j\| = \frac{1}{n - f} \left\| \sum_{k \in N_i \setminus N_j} \mathbf{v}_k - \sum_{k \in N_j \setminus N_i} \mathbf{v}_k \right\|
$$

Note that $|N_i \setminus N_j| = |N_j \setminus N_i| \le f$. Therefore, we can write

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_j\| = \frac{1}{n - f} \times \left\| \sum_{k \in N_i \setminus N_j} (\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_i) - \sum_{k \in N_j \setminus N_i} (\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_j) + |N_i \setminus N_j| (\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j) \right\|
$$

Due to the triangle inequality, we obtain that

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_j\| \le \frac{1}{n - f} \left( \sum_{k \in N_i \setminus N_j} \|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_i\| + \sum_{k \in N_j \setminus N_i} \|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_j\| + f \|\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j\| \right)
$$

As $N_i$ represents $n - f$ input vectors closest to $\mathbf{v}_i$ and $|H| = n - f$, for all $k \in N_i$, we have $\|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_i\| \le \max_{k' \in H} \|\mathbf{v}_{k'} - \mathbf{v}_i\| = \|\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i\|$. Similarly, for all $k \in N_j$, we have $\|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_j\| \le \|\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i\|$. Using this above, we get

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_j\| \le \frac{3f}{n - f} \|\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j\|
$$

This suggests that when $f$ is small enough, specifically when $\frac{3f}{n - f} < 1$, the separation between the set of vectors $\{\mathbf{z}_i, i \in H\}$ might be smaller than that of the original vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_i, i \in H\}$. The above intuition is further refined below, in Sect. 5.1.2, to prove that NNM reduces the robustness coefficient, hence amplifies the robustness, of a robust averaging.

## 5.1.2 Robustness Amplification

In this section, we analyze the robustness amplification property of NNM. We show that the composition of NNM with an $(f, \kappa)$-robust rule $F$ results in a new aggregation rule $F' := F \circ \operatorname{NNM}$ that has an improved robustness coefficient compared to $F$. The analysis proceeds in two steps. First, we obtain a generic result, in Lemma 5.1, that relates the empirical variance of the vectors NNM outputs to the variance of the input vectors. Then, we build upon this result, in Theorem 5.1, to precisely characterize the robustness amplification property of NNM.

### Variance Reduction

Lemma 5.1, presented below, allows us to demonstrate the variance-reduction property of NNM. Specifically, we consider arbitrarily a vector $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, a set of $n$ vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$ from $\mathbb{R}^d$, and a subset $S \subseteq [n]$ comprising $n - f$ elements. We then obtain an upper bound on the (Euclidean) distance between the average $\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S$ of vectors $\mathbf{v}_i$, $i \in S$ and the average of $n - f$ vectors in $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$ that are nearest neighbors of $\mathbf{u}$. The bound suggests that NNM leads to a reduction in the (empirical) variance, as we elaborate immediately after presenting Lemma 5.1 (and its proof).

**Lemma 5.1** Consider an arbitrary vector $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and an arbitrary collection of $n$ vectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Let $N_u \subseteq [n]$ be a set of indices representing $n - f$ nearest neighbors of $\mathbf{u}$ in the set $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$. Then, for any $S \subseteq [n]$ such that $|S| = n - f$, the following holds true:

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_u - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \le \frac{4f}{(n - f)^2} \sum_{i \in S} \|\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{u}\|^2,
$$

where $\mathbf{z}_u := \frac{1}{n-f} \sum_{i \in N_u} \mathbf{v}_i$ and $\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S := \frac{1}{n-f} \sum_{i \in S} \mathbf{v}_i$.

**Proof** Consider an arbitrary $S \subseteq [n]$ such that $|S| = n - f$. Recall that $N_u \subseteq [n]$ is a set of indices of $n - f$ nearest neighbors of $\mathbf{u}$ in $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$. Then, by definition of $\mathbf{z}_u$ and $\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S$, we obtain that

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_u - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 = \left\| \frac{1}{n - f} \sum_{i \in N_u} \mathbf{v}_i - \frac{1}{n - f} \sum_{i \in S} \mathbf{v}_i \right\|^2 = \frac{1}{(n - f)^2} \left\| \sum_{i \in N_u} \mathbf{v}_i - \sum_{i \in S} \mathbf{v}_i \right\|^2.
$$

Further simplifying the difference in the right-hand side, we get

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_u - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 = \frac{1}{(n - f)^2} \left\| \sum_{i \in N_u \setminus S} \mathbf{v}_i - \sum_{i \in S \setminus N_u} \mathbf{v}_i \right\|^2.
$$

The bound presented in Lemma 5.1 implies variance reduction by NNM. Specifically, consider an arbitrary $S \subseteq [n]$ of size $n-f$ and an arbitrary $i \in S$. Then, upon substituting $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{v}_i$ in Lemma 5.1, we get

$$
\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \leq \frac{4f}{(n-f)^2}\sum_{j\in S}\|\mathbf{v}_j-\mathbf{v}_i\|^2,
$$

where recall that $\mathbf{z}_i$ is as defined in Sect. 5.1.1. As the above holds true for any $i \in S$, we obtain that

$$
\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \leq \frac{4f}{(n-f)^2}\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{j\in S}\|\mathbf{v}_j-\mathbf{v}_i\|^2,
$$

where recall that $(\mathbf{z}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{z}_n) := \operatorname{NNM}(\mathbf{v}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{v}_n)$. Note, as explained earlier in Remark 4.5, that $\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{j\in S}\|\mathbf{v}_j-\mathbf{v}_i\|^2 = 2|S|\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{v}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2$. Therefore, from above we obtain that

$$
\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \leq \frac{8f}{n-f}\cdot\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{v}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2. \tag{5.4}
$$

Let $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S := \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{z}_i$. Then, we obtain that

$$
\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 = \sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S-(\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S)\|^2 = \sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 + |S|\|\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2.
$$

Substituting from above in (5.4), we obtain that

$$
\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 + \|\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 \leq \frac{8f}{n-f}\cdot\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|\mathbf{v}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2. \tag{5.5}
$$

This implies that the variance of the vectors that NNM outputs corresponding to the honest nodes, i.e., when $S = H$, is smaller than the original variance, provided that the fraction of adversarial nodes $\frac{f}{n}$ is sufficiently small (such that $\frac{8f}{n-f} < 1$). Specifically, due to (5.5), we have

$$
\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{i\in H}\|\mathbf{z}_i-\overline{\mathbf{z}}_H\|^2 \leq \frac{8f}{n-f}\cdot\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{i\in H}\|\mathbf{v}_i-\overline{\mathbf{v}}_H\|^2.
$$

In the following, we use the above analysis, specifically (5.5), to derive the amplification in the robustness of a robust aggregation rule imparted by NNM.

### Provably Tighter Robustness Coefficient

The aforementioned variance-reduction property of NNM enables us to show how this scheme enhances the robustness coefficient of an $(f, \kappa)$-robust averaging. Specifically, the following result holds true.

**Theorem 5.1** *Consider an aggregation rule $F : \mathbb{R}^{d \times n} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$. If $F$ is an $(f, \kappa)$-robust averaging, then $F \circ \operatorname{NNM}$ is an $(f, \kappa')$-robust averaging where*

$$
\kappa' \leq \frac{8f}{n - f}(\kappa + 1).
$$

*Proof* Consider $n$ arbitrary vectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n \in \mathbb{R}^d$, and an arbitrary $S \subseteq [n]$ such that $|S| = n - f$. Denote $(\mathbf{z}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{z}_n) := \operatorname{NNM}(\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n)$ and $\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S := \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} \mathbf{z}_i$. Since $F$ is assumed to be an $(f, \kappa)$-robust averaging, the following holds true:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|F \circ \operatorname{NNM}(\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n) - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 &= \|F(\mathbf{z}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{z}_n) - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 \\
&\leq \frac{\kappa}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} \|\mathbf{z}_i - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2. \tag{5.6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that for any real values $a_1, a_2$ and $a_3 > 0$, due to Young's inequality, we have $(a_1 + a_2)^2 \leq (1 + a_3)(a_1)^2 + (1 + \frac{1}{a_3})(a_2)^2$. Therefore, using the triangle inequality, we obtain that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|F(\mathbf{z}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{z}_n) - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 &= \|F(\mathbf{z}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{z}_n) - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S + \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \\
&\leq \left(1 + \frac{1}{\kappa}\right) \|F(\mathbf{z}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{z}_n) - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 \\
&\quad + (1 + \kappa) \|\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting from (5.6) in the above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|F(\mathbf{z}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{z}_n) - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 &\leq \left(1 + \frac{1}{\kappa}\right) \frac{\kappa}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} \|\mathbf{z}_i - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 \\
&\quad + (1 + \kappa) \|\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \\
&= (1 + \kappa) \left( \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} \|\mathbf{z}_i - \overline{\mathbf{z}}_S\|^2 + \|\overline{\mathbf{z}}_S - \overline{\mathbf{v}}_S\|^2 \right). \tag{5.7}
\end{aligned}
$$
