Substituting from the above in (6.19), we obtain that

$$\mathbb{E}_t[\|\xi_t\|^2] \leq \beta_t^2(1+\gamma_{t-1}L)(1+4\gamma_{t-1}L)\|\xi_{t-1}\|^2 + (1-\beta_t)^2\frac{\sigma^2}{b(n-f)} + 2\gamma_{t-1}L(1+\gamma_{t-1}L)\beta_t^2\|\delta_{t-1}\|^2 + 4\gamma_{t-1}L(1+\gamma_{t-1}L)\beta_t^2\|\nabla L(\theta_{t-1})\|^2.$$

This concludes the proof.

# Large Momentum Coefficient Increases Deviation

For simplicity, suppose that we have a constant learning rate γ and a constant momentum coefficient β. Then, observe that the rate of growth of the deviation analyzed in Lemma 6.3 is effectively given by the factor (1 + γL)(1 + 4γL)β². For the deviation to be stable, i.e., to be bounded eventually by a finite value, this factor needs to be less than (or equal to) 1. Specifically, if β² is larger than (1 + γL), the honest nodes’ average momentum can deviate arbitrarily from the average of their local gradients, which could prevent the learning algorithm from converging to a stationary point. This observation is also valid in the case when there are no adversarial nodes. This rate of growth of the deviation ξₜ, with respect to β, is contrary to that of the aggregation error δₜ. Although controlling the former is important to the convergence of the algorithm, the latter is critical to the robustness against adversarial nodes. In the following section, we present a candidate for the momentum coefficient that favorably controls the opposing consequences of local momentum on deviation and aggregation error.
