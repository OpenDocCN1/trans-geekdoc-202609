where $v_{(1)},\ldots,v_{(n)}$ denote the order statistics of $v_1,\ldots,v_n$, i.e., the sorting of the values in non-decreasing order (with ties broken arbitrarily). In essence, $\mathrm{TM}_q$ computes the average of the $n-2q$ values that remain after eliminating the $q$ largest and $q$ smallest values. Similar to the median, if we change an input $v_i$ to $v_i'$, the change in the final value of the aggregation is also bounded by the maximum absolute difference between any two unchanged input values. The change in trimmed mean is further reduced (compared to median) by a factor of $n-2q$, thanks to the averaging operation after the elimination of extreme values. A trimmed mean is thus clearly less vulnerable to manipulation than standard averaging. We illustrate this phenomenon in Fig. 4.2.
