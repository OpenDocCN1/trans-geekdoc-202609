Identifying the minimal set of nodes whose removal breaks a complex network apart, also referred to as network dismantling, is a highly non-trivial task with applications in multiple domains. Whereas network dismantling has been extensively studied over the past decade, research has primarily focused on the optimization problem for single-layer networks, neglecting that if not all, real-world networks display multiple layers of interdependent interactions. In such networks, the optimization problem is fundamentally different as the effect of removing nodes propagates across layers in a way that can not be predicted using a single-layer perspective. We propose a dismantling algorithm MultiDismantler, which leverages multiple representation and deep reinforcement learning to optimally dismantle interdependent networks. MultiDismantler is trained on synthetic graphs; and when applied to either real or synthetic networks, it displays exceptional dismantling performance, outperforming all existing benchmark algorithms. We show that MultiDismantler is effective in guiding strategies for the control of diseases in social networks characterized by multiple layers of social interactions. Also, we show MultiDismantler is useful in the design of protocols aimed at preventing the onset of cascading failures in critical infrastructures.

Networks are ubiquitous in both natural (for example, climate networks and protein-protein interaction networks) and man-made (for example, social networks and infrastructure networks)¹. Connectivity is essential for the overall network function, as being part of any connected component is a necessary condition for interaction. Due to its heterogeneous structure, finding the minimal set of nodes whose removal would destroy any connected components is known as the network dismantling problem². Network dismantling has numerous applications: inhibiting specific species or proteins for drug design is network dismantling; enhancing the tolerance of networks such as traffic or electric systems, to maximize the efficiency of infrastructural networks which can be thought of network dismantling³; finding the smallest set of nodes that should be vaccinated to prevent a disease outbreak is well approximated by the solution of a network dismantling problem.

Dismantling is the most fundamental optimization problem for networks, but it is notoriously, NP-hard (non-polynomial time)⁴⁵. Such exact solutions, for large networks with only medium and large layers, can only be obtained. Many optimization methods provide approximate solutions that are based on node-centric heuristics, such as high degree adaptive (HDA)⁶ collective influence (CI) and Minsur.
