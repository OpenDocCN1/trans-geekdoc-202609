This classifier is based on the assumption that every feature contributes independently and equally to the outcome. Some of the popular Naive Bayes Classifiers include Multinomial and Bernoulli Classifiers. In case of multinomial classifiers, frequencies are represented by feature vectors that are generated by multinomial distribution, whereas in Bernoulli, features are usually independent binary variables. Inputs in a Naive Bayes algorithm usually undergo of the following steps:

+   Step 1: Convert the entire data set into a table of frequencies.
+   Step 2: Create a likelihood table on the basis of certain probabilities.
+   Step 3: Use the Bayesian Equation for calculating the posterior probability for each and every class; the one with the highest posterior probability is taken to be the predicted outcome.

Navie Bayes Classifiers usually require a small number of training data to find out the required parameters and they are extremely fast. They also eliminate certain dimensional problems and fewer amounts of training data is enough for better prediction. This runs well when categorized inputs are given rather than numerical variables. In case of numerical variables, bell curves are usually used [23].
