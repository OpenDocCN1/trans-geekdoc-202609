Texts frequently labeled as modernist novels are El novela and Cinelandia by Sera, Nomada by Miró, La quimera by Bazán, La cópula by Rueda, Paz en la guerra by Unamuno, Romance del fantasma y doña Juanita by Pemán, Historia vulgar por Lorca, Escenas by Jarnés, and Locuras de Carnaval by Baroja.

The most similar category to this one is the bucolic novel (42% shared texts and features), followed at a distance by autobiographic (15%), biographic, and psychological novel (14%).

## 11.14 Autobiographical Novel

This category shows an eigenvector of 0.10 and a rather low specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.59). The category is populated by 26 instances in CoNSSA, 11 in CoNSSA-canon. The novels are relatively canonized, with 1.28 pages on average in the manual MDL (1.68 for CoNSSA-canon). The results of the classification are relatively high: 0.93 F1-score.

Two literary features are notably distinctive for this category: The first is, surprisingly, a strong relation between the author's life and the plot of the text (3.08 z-score). However, the second one is less obvious: the mix of several kinds to types of places (2.19 z-score), meaning a change of settings from a town to a city, for example. The protagonist tends to be a child at the beginning of the text from the middle classes of society and the ends tends to be either partially or clear positive.

The overrepresented vocabulary in this category is about childhood (niño, joven, pequeño), personal descriptions (carácter, inseguro, dócil, brusco, benévolo), food (estómago, digerir), time (seguida), cognitive process and education (resum, encuadernar, colegio), and others (tipo, buscar, util, apartar).

Texts frequently assigned with this label are by Valdés (Papeles del doctor Angelico y Sinfonia pastoral), Francisco de Ayala Cazador en el alha y Erika ante el invierno), Serna (El novelista), Miró (Amores de Antón Hernando), and Pérez de Ayala (La pata de la raposa).

Similar subgenres are the biographic (60%), memoir (20%), philosophical (18%), and psychological novel (19%).

## 11.15 Comedy Novel

The specific features are linguistic, especially vocabulary about communication and sounds (secreto, silencio, ruido, sonar), natural elements (weather marked by WordNet, hámedo, frío, quemar), descriptions (observar, cavidad), emotions (timbler, insensible, arrepentirse), food (azúcar, recolección, cosecha), and others (sostener, caballar, pintar).

The most distinctive literary metadata are plots in several geographical settings: other continents (specifically America and Asia, this last case only in CoNSSA and not in CoNSSA-canon), rural places, trips, Italy.

Some frequently assigned texts are by Valdés (La alegría del capitán Ribot), Salaverría (La Virgen de Aranzazu), Bazán (Morriña, la primera), Ganimet (Los trabajos del infatigable creador Pio Cid), Barojo (Camino de perfección, El cura de Monleón, Susana), and Azaña (El jardín de los frailes).

The most similar category to this one is the bucolic novel (42% shared texts and features), followed at a distance by autobiographic (15%), biographic, and psychological novel (14%).

## 11.16 Psychological Novel

The psychological novel shows an eigenvector of 0.10, more specifically 0.09, with a rather low specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.60). The category is populated by 20 instances in CoNSSA. The novels are relatively canonized, with 1.58 pages on average in the manual MdLE (2.29 for the cases in CoNSSA-canon). The results of the classification are relatively low (0.81 F1-score).

The most specific features are linguistic, especially vocabulary about communication and sounds (secreto, silencio, ruido, sonar), natural elements (weather marked by WordNet, hámedo, frío, quemar), descriptions (observar, cavidad), emotions (timbler, insensible, arrepentirse), food (azúcar, recolección, cosecha), and others (sostener, caballar, pintar).

One literary feature has a very high z-score (3.71): a mixed narrator. This means that the change of narrator (for example from a heterodiegetic to an autodigetic one) would be a very distinctive feature of this subgenre. Other literary top features are settings in Europe, Spain, and rural areas, a partially negative ending, and a male protagonist from the middle class.

Texts frequently labeled as modernist novels are El novela and Cinelandia by Sera, Nomada by Miró, El de novelista, and Miró (Amores de Antón Hernando), or Bazán (Morriña, la primera), Ganimet (Los trabajos del infatigable creador Pio Cid), Barojo (Camino de perfección, El cura de Monleón, Susana), and Azaña (El jardín de los frailes).

This category is populated by 34 novels (in CoNSSA-canon) with values about canonicity in the middle range (1.36 pages on average in the manual).
