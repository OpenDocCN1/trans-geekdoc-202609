### 3.1.4 Statistical Population of Prose

But as I will clarify, some texts do not show a main genre: it is unclear whether they are novels, essays, or texts without any general genre (genreless texts in Derrida’s terminology). That is why, I will first define a population of prose written by the authors defined in the last section. These texts will be analyzed in the following section with the purpose of obtaining a population of novels.

This population is constituted by all prose texts mentioned in the MdLE published between 1880 and 1930 by the authors of authors. From these works, I gather the following information: name of the text, year of publication, main genre (i.e., novela, cuentas...), subgenre and dedicated number of pages in the MdLE. The next step is to observe whether this manual assigns a further label to them, a subgenre, like erotic novel, adventure novel, or naturalist novel.¹³ Out of 727, a subgenre label can be identified for 349. In total, this manual uses 88 labels, the most frequent being novel erótica (erotic novel) with 38 works (5% of the population). This label is followed by five labels between thirty and eight works: *episodio nacional, histórica, naturalista, social, realista, de aventuras, y humorista*. The first of this group has been already discussed in the 2.¹ as one of the one-author-labels, similar to historical novels, coined and employed by Galdós. The rest of the labels can be accepted as unproblematic types of novels. After this, there is a long tail of dozens labels with few texts explaining them, and many of them are disputable: *de carne* ('about the flesh'), de la vida ('about life'), *sevillanas* ('from Seville'), sentimental, valenciana ('from Valencia'), *lírica, de transición* ('of transition'),

At the end of this long tail of the frequency of labels in the MdLe, 65 of the 88 labels are used for up to three texts. In fact, there are 36 labels that are assigned to a single novel: Either all these are extraordinary single works that functioned and ended a subgenre, or it is a too differentiated use of labels to categorize them. That means, the MdLE has a strong tendency to use very specific labels for few texts with a hand-full of exceptions like erotic, historical, or naturalist novel.
