The main question of this chapter is to observe whether literary fiction can be understood as a subgenre comparable to erotic, comedy, education novel, or memoir. If the answer is positive, we expect it to behave similarly to the other types of novels. To find an answer to this question, I will run several tests observing whether this specific label stands out from the group or not. If it does, I will have to suppose it represents a different type of categorization of texts, such as an operationalization of literaryness or a group of genres. There will be no reason to think that literary fiction is anything but a subgenre, If it does show similar characteristics to the other labels, there will be no the publishers are treating it.

To be more specific, I will first describe the use of this label applied by the publishers, observing its frequency in sources, its correlation with other approximations of the canon, and general correlations with other metadata. Second, I will apply classifiers on linguistic information to predict this particular label and compare the results with the rest of the labels. Third, several types of metadata (especially regarding the author, but also metadata internal and external to the text) will be employed to discover whether literary fiction depends more heavily on non-linguistic information than the other subgenre labels. Finally, I will argue for adopting literary fiction as a hypothetical subgenre of the novel since the data of the publishers and the tests do not suggest otherwise.
