The same central position has literary fiction, a special case of the subgenre palette which has been found in a single source: the publishers on Amazon (see Chapter 5.3). This subgenre has the lowest specificity of features of all subgenres (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.28). It contains 190 novels in CoSSA, 94 in CoNSSA-canon. The novels are, surprisingly, highly canonized, with 1.64 pages in the manual on average. This explains a low classification result of 0.75 F1-score. The strongest feature of this subgenre would be a protagonist of lower classes of the society, followed at a distance by a setting in France, and surprisingly, that the story does not reflect the author's life. From the linguistic perspective: communication (criticar, discurso, oratoria, char), money and work (ajretar, bien, propiedad, encargar, función, pagar), royalty (soberano, rey, linaje), and other fields (familia and negations) are distinctive.

Publishers use this several texts by authors like Baroja (Mala hierba, la busca), Valle-Inclán (Baza de espadas, la media noche), Galdós (El doctor Centeno, lo prohibido), Azorín (Tomás Rueda), or Unamuno (Paz en la guerra). The most similar neighbors are the two most central ones: the realistic (59% of shared features and texts) and the social novel (56%).

The historical novel is in the middle range of centrality of the network (eigenvector of 0.18), with a relatively low specificity of features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.48). It contains 113 novels in CoNSSA, 59 in CoSSA. Its novels are relatively highly canonized, with 14.6 pages of manual average. The most distinctive feature, unsurprisingly, is a setting in the antiquity, followed by geographical settings in Italy or America, and a protagonist of the higher classes of society. An interesting aspect is that, even though the setting in Spain is not very distinct for this subgenre, the references to Spain are. In others, historical novels do not take place often in Spain than the rest, but they do mention Spain by more frequently. These vocabulary about groups (genre, asoci, población, and nouns about groups marked by WordNet), military and war (milicia, soldado, luchar, luchado, guerra, delito, aprior), politics (politero, politica, empleo, autoridad, mandar), and places (municipio, territorio, and nouns referring places marked by WordNet are specific for this subgenre. Some novels frequently associated with this category by several sources were written by Baroja (Los últimos románticos, La feria de los desiertos, La ciudad de la niebla), Coloma (Jerónimo), Costa (Último día del paganismo), Valle-Inclán, Viña mío, la corte de los milagros), Pere (Pedro Sánchez), or Sender (Mr. Witt en el Cantón). The most similar subgenres are literary fiction (33% of shared elements), followed by adventure (29), social (22), and realist novel (both with 21%).

In the periphery of the network is the erotic subgenre, with an eigenvector centrality of 0.03 and a relatively low specificity of features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.81). This subgenre comprehends 17 novels in CoSSA, and only four novels in CoSSA-canon. Its texts are normally not strongly canonized (0.5 pages in the manual average). This explains a higher classification score of 0.96 F1-score. This category has very distinctive features (with values over two-z scores) like type of relationships (novio, matrimonio), sentimental verbs or actions, mainly positive (admirar, caricia, enamorar, encantar, apasionar, abandonar), personal qualities (vergüenza, sincero, extravagante, feo, antipatia), gender and sexual orientation (mujer, afinado, which was a historical manner of referring to homosexuality), (convertir, suelto, sal, espina). It is interesting that only the semantic field in the top 20 about physical relations is caricia (‘caress’), and none of them is related to sexual practices or body parts.
