# 2.1.3 Literary Genre and Novel in this Period

In this section, I use the information and previous research from the area of Literary Studies to create an overview of the novel as well as the genre, which has been constantly constituted and revised. In Mainer's opinion, "the conventional literary genres were resolutely iconoclast" (Mainer, Alvar, and Navarro 1997, 557, my translation), and Buckley refer to an overflow of the generic boundaries of poetry and prose, and novel and story, drama and cinematic image, [which] create what are in new modes of expression" (2008, 45). Longhurst describes the modernist novel in a broader frame as "incontvertibly and self-consciously different" (1999, 2). Ródenas de Moya translates the opinion of the Spanish poet Guillermo de la Torre: "we all agree that: the <literary genres> hardly exist now" (2000, 9, my translation). The best-known defender of the absence of genres in this time was Benedetto Croce, the Italian intellectual who published the *Estetica scienza dell'espressione e linguistica generale* in 1992 with great impact across Europe and America, a text I will come back to in Section 2.3.2.1.

Opinions about the specific genre of the novel during this period are similar. Ródenas de Moya (2000, 89–90), Altisent (2008, 2–3), and Longhurst (2008, 41) hypothesize a hybridization of the novel, mixing with other genres like the essay, autobiography, lyrical poetry, cinema, or philosophy.
