The concept of buolic is frequently associated with one of the authors that represent this subgenre: Miró (Alisent 1988; Fernández Palmer 2019; Benyeto Pérez 1980; Lina Corona 2009; Baquero Goyanes 1973). If the label is accepted, we could consider that a buolic novel existed and practised mainly between 1908 and 1927, with many examples such as Miró, Valle-Inclán, Azorin, or Jarnés, i.e. the buolic novel.The concept reflect many characteristics of these novels, such as perception, description of nature, and a rural setting. However, the label buolic novel is really satisfying as a secondary matter. The important finding is the identification of these groups of texts.

In this chapter, I propose these clusters can be understood as subgenres from a cross-sectional perspective. Nevertheless, they do not have the same status as the rest of the labels by human sources. They continue to be hypothetical subgenres that have arisen from a clustering process that has been evaluated in several stages. In this way, they are integrated into the genre palette, now containing 26 labels, and will be analyzed in the following chapters, with a special interest in whether they show any abnormality in comparison to the rest. In the future, it would be necessary to discuss both the proposed subgenres in the circles of research on Spanish literature and the methodology in DH conferences. In addition, this innovative method can be applied in the field of Digital Literary Studies to other languages and periods to observe its full capacity.
