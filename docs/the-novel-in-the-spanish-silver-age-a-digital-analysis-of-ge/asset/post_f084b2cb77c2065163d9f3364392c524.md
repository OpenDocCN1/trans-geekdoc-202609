
The most similar subgenre is the dialogue novel (43%), followed by poetic (7%), naturalist, realist, and social novel (this last three with 5%).

## 11.22 Costumbrist Novel

The costumbrist novel also has an eigenvector of 0.04, with a relatively low specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.82). The genre is populated by 16 texts (six when only CoNSSA-canon is considered) of low canononization status (1.06 pages in the manual MDLe, and, 1.95 for CoNSSA-canon). The results of the classification are relatively high, with a 0.90 F1-score.

In this subgenre, the top features are a mix of linguistic and literary characteristics. Specific vocabulary for this category are about relations (computer, reserv, suelto, comedir, admirador), descriptions (solución, breve, remote, encontrar, ocasion, propio, ceñir, and a larger proportion of adjectives), positive (regocajo, risa) but also negative vocabulary (pinchar, chocar, desvergüenza), and others (muchacho, café).

The literary metadata that are overrepresented in this group are female and young protagonists, who, when only looking at CoNSSA, tend to be higher classes of society. The ending is usually partially clearly positive, the narrator is heterodiegetic, and the authors do not try to represent their life in the text. The settings are normally rural areas or towns in Spain.

Texts who receive this label frequently are works by mainly two authors: Valdés (Cádiz, los majos de Sinfonía pastoral, Marta y María, Tristán o el pesimismo) and Pereda (Sotileza, la puchera, De tal palo, ta astilla, El sabor de la tierruca), but else also other authors such as Rueda (La reja) or Serna (La Nardo).

The most similar category is the naturalist novel (15% of shared texts and features followed by the realist (6%), erotic, comedy, and spiritual novel (5).

## 11.23 Nivola

The nivola, a one-author-label coined by Unamuno and introduced in Section 2.1.10, also has an eigenvector of 0.04. This category has a high specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 1.79) and only five texts which are highly canonized (4.8 pages on average in the manual MDLe). This very small category associated with only one author can be classified with perfect results (1.0 F1-score).

Although both the literary and linguistic features show high z-scores (over two), the strongest ones are linguistic. They can be mainly organized in negative vocabulary (*hindi*, annular, *excluir*), life and childbirth (*retorno*, *destino*, crear, *reproducción*, *útero*, aprender, *vida, origen*), poverty (*mendigo*, *limosna*), and objects (*canal*, *tallo*, *cuerno*). Beside, several typographical signs are overrepresented, with such question, explanation, and quotation marks.

Nonetheless, the literary features also show some high values, such as a long span of the text, a homodietic narrator, a high identification of the author in the text, a partially or clearly negative ending, and a setting in unspecified towns of Spain.

The works by Unamuno assigned with this label are *Niebla*, Abel Sánchez, San Manuel Bueno, mártir, La Tía, and *Amor y pedagogía*.

The most similar categories are the spiritual (12%), the educational (11%), and the philosophical novel (8%).

## 11.24 Erotic Novel

In the periphery of the network is the erotic subgenre, with an eigenvector centrality of 0.03 and a relatively low specificity of features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.81). This subgenre represents 17 novels in CoNSSA, and only four novels in the manual on CoNSSA-canon. Its texts are normally not strongly canonized (0.5 pages in the manual on average). That explains a rather high classification score of 0.96 F1-score.

This category has very distinctive features (values over two z-scores) like type of relationships (*novio*, *matrimonio*), sentimental verbs or actions, mainly positive (*admirar*, enamorar, encantar, apasionar), personal qualities (*vergüenza, sincero, extravagante, fe*), gender and sexual orientation (*mujer*, *affado*), and others (*convertir*, *suelto*, *espina*). It is interesting that the only semantic field in the 20th century about physical relations is 'caress', and none of them is related to sexual practices or body parts. The protagonist is typically an adult woman of the higher classes of society. Even more distinctive is the fact that stories do not represent the author’s life. Other distinctive metadata are urban settings, traveling through different countries, in a contemporary world, with a heterodiegetic narrator. Many of these features are not explicitly mentioned by the erotic novels that I summarized in Section 2.1.8. However, some of the characteristics are confirmed in this analysis, like a middle-class protagonist, urban settings, or trips.

## 11.26 Poetic Novel

Close to the limits of the network is the poetic novel, with an eigenvector centrality of 0.02 and a relatively high specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 1.56). The category is populated by only five texts, a number that is reduced to two when CoNSSA is considered (that is why the following description is based only on CoNSSA), but the texts have a high status (1.88 pages on average in the native MDLe). For a category with few instances, it yields a low classification result of 0.80 F1-score.
