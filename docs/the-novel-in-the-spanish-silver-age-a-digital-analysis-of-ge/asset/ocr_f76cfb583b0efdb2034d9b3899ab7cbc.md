The concept of Bag of Words is a useful metaphor, but it gives the false idea that each text is considered in isolation and that each text is understood as a bag containing tokens which are counted. Actually, all tokens are counted for each bag, even though many of them do not appear. This is what one of the most important and problematic features of linguistic data, i.e., its sparse nature. The majority of the words do not appear in the texts; in other words, the majority of the items are counted zero times in many texts. It also implies the sparseness and the size of a corpus should correlate with each other: The larger the corpus is, the more types it will contain, and therefore the sparser the distribution will be.
