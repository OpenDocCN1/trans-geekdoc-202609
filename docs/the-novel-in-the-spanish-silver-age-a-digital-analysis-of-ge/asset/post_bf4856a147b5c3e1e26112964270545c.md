## 3.2.8 Place and Period of the Action

Many subgenre-labels contain information about the place of the action of the plot. There are numerous types of geographical references: novela americana (reference to the continent), novela regional (to a rural setting), novela madrileista (to the city or town), novela cosmita (to the size of the place), novels del mar (to the sea, in contrast to the land). Multiple facets of the setting have been encoded using different categories of metadata. Many of these are implied: If the action takes places in Madrid, it means that it takes place in Spain, which is in Europe, and it means that the sea is far away. Nevertheless, I have explicitly encoded all the fields to use this information in various scenarios.

To assign a value to each metadata, at least half of the novel has to correspond with the value. Otherwise, it was assigned as mixed. This specific value is especially interesting because it indirectly represents travel; depending on the geographical level of this value, it can point out within Spain (for example La vuelta by Azorin), Europe (*El mundo es ansi* by Baroja) or the world (*Genio y figura...* by Valera). These will be observed closely in the descriptions of the subgenres of the Appendix.

Along with the place, information about the period (point and total span) has been given. Here is the complete list of the categories:

- Setting settlement: Name of the place (city, town, or village) where the action takes place, represented exactly the way it is referred to in the text (Madrid, Paris, Valencia, Marineda..).

- Setting territory: Name of the region to which the place belongs, especially interesting for the novels in the rural places, encoded in Spanish (Castilla, Valencia, Andalusia, Isla de Paris).

Commonly the reader cannot be sure about how time passes considering the references in many cases are vague (after some weeks, time passed and...). ## 3.2.9 Protagonist Metadata

Another type of literary information with a strong resemblance in the labels is the protagonist. Some subgenres are related to different types of information about the most important character in the novel, like her or his social level (picaresca, social), profession (de guerra, policía, taurina), or the age at the beginning of the action (picaresca, de educación). Two other basic pieces of information about protagonists have been gathered: their gender and their name (to document which character has been chosen, particularly pertinent in novels in which this decision is unclear). The specific fields are:

- Gender: With two basic values (male and female), mixed if there is no clear protagonist but instead a group with mixed genders, and for non-human protagonists.

- Name: The longest version of her or his name (surname, if present), with or his pseudonym in parentheses.

- Profession: Categorical values about the protagonist from a list of 30 general values (lord, artist, business owner, military personnel, religious...).

- Social-level: The social level of the protagonist as three possible ordinal values (for characters whose basic need like nutrition or education are at risk), medium (for characters who need to work, default value), and high.

- Age: Age of the protagonist from a list of four ordinal values: child, young, adult, and mature.

## 3.2.10 Other Metadata

Finally, several literary fields related to genre have also been collected:

- Narrator: With five possible values: heterodiegetic, autodiegetic, homodiegetic, and epistolary.

- Representation: Information about whether the action takes place in a realistic world or not, expressed as three ordinal values: realistic, neutral, and non-realistic.

- Type of ending: Information about how the text ends, with five ordinal values: negative, partially negative, neutral, partially positive, and positive.

- Quality in literature: Along with the number of pages, any reference to the quality of the novel has also been gathered, as three possible values: low, medium, and high.

## 3.2.11 Description of the Metadata of CoSSA

In this final section, my goal is to describe the corpus in terms of metadata. By doing so, I want to achieve three different goals: Firstly, I show how existing knowledge in digital metadata (coming from summaries or personal readings) can uncover broader pictures about literature. In other words, metadata is the first step to run analyses that are not explorations of uncertain tombs, but inductive descriptions of literary features. Second, I describe the general tendencies of novels of this period. These general patterns of the entire genre can be used to compare specific characteristics of some subgenres: Are urban or contemporary settings of the erotic novel, or are they the most common values in all novels of that time, regardless of the subgenre? Finally, I explore the most noticeable changes of specific literature phenomena over time: For example, are sad more common at the beginning of this period and therefore more typical in subgenres of the 19th century like in the realistic or naturalist novels?
