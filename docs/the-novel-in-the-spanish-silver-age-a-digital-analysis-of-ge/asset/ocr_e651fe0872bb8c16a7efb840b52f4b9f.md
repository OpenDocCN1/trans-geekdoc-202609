These values are calculated independently from each other, and actually in both versions of the corpus there one single novel which fulfils all its criteria: Tristán o el pesimismo by Valdés. This novel can be seen as the one literary characteristics are most similar to the rest; in other words, the least surprising work in terms of its metadata. Deleting the two most strict criteria (the duration of the action and the specific place), 17 other novels fulfil the rest of the criteria, with Alarcón, Baroja, Blasco, Francisco de Ayala, Jamnés, Lanza, León, Miró, Munilla, Pemán, Unamuno, and Valle-Inclán (details in the Jupyter Notebook). As a summary, a short empirical definition of the novel of this person can affirm that the story typically takes places in a realistic contemporary Spain, in third person, written and starred by men, with a sad ending.

After looking at the general picture of the novel, I continue investigating the distribution and development of specific literary metadata during this sixty-year period. As I have shown in Section 3.1.5, some subgenres were published in the last decades of the 20th century (naturalist, realist novels), in the 1900s and 1910s (erotic novel), and a third group in the 1920s and 1930s (social, historical, and comedy novels). That is why the development of literary patterns are not only interesting for se, they can shed light on internal characteristics of subgenres.

One field that shows a clear chronological pattern is the information of whether the text resembles the author's life. As described above, the majority of works are somewhat autobiographical increases over the analyzed period (Figure 27): While 88% of the texts in the decade of 1880 were clearly not autobiographical, this amount drops to 88% by the 1930s. Similar tendencies are observable in both versions of the corpora, with an even stronger tendency in the canonized one, reaching high statistical significance when analyzed through linear regression (p-values < 0.001 in both versions).
