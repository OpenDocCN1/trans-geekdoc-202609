In addition, the graph gives rise to further mathematical possibilities that can be applied to answer new questions about genres. For example, the question of whether some categories constitute intermediate groups of genres (macro-genres) can be answered through community detection in the network. A first attempt shows that, apart from the case of the *greguerías*, the subgenres of this period do not show any genres of genres. There is no evidence that canonic subgenres (educational, realist, naturalist novels, literary fiction) cluster more tightly together, nor are there other groups, like the adventure-historical macro-genre. Even though these subgenres share similarities, they are not separated from the rest to constitute their category at a higher level. However, other periods or languages could show these intermediate groups of genres, as the classical French drama or the genres of the Bible (see Section 8.6 and Calvo Tello 2020). In other words, the tripartite graph model is able to find macro-genres, but it does not impose them as the scholastic taxonomy does genres. In this way, the researcher can maintain a descriptive attitude towards genres, rejecting prescriptive axioms about how they should be structured.

The results have led me to be very critical with taxonomies as a model for genres. However, it is only fair to admit that, at the beginning of this project, I implicitly accepted the taxonomy of genres and this skewed the gathering of the data and the research questions. My goal was to analyze subgenres of the novel, such as the adventure novel, connected to a more general category, the novel. On the one hand, this helped me to structure the phases of my research. In any case, the taxonomies as a complete model for genre are not able to fit the texts in them: As observed again and again in the different chapters, texts can belong to any number of categories and this relation is better described in ordinal terms than categorical ones. These two characteristics are hard to model in a taxonomy. However, taxonomies can only be useful as macro-models for genres, i.e., they can only be used to describe the relations between different categories.
