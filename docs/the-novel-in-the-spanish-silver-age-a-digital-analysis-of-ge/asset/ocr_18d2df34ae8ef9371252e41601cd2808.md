# The Novel in the Spanish Silver Age

The model of Figure 106 and the subsequent visualization connects the three main components of the analysis of genres. This visualization illustrates the lack of necessary and sufficient conditions, and instead shows a cross of shared features. For example, vocabulary about language is a specific feature of adventure novels. However, it is also a distinctive feature of the text La media, even though it is not part of the adventure genre. It belongs, among other categories, to war novels, sharing with its many semantic features, as vocabulary about soldiers, peace, or fighting. In a sense, this is a visualization of the cross-crossed characteristics described by Wittgenstein in the family resemblance model or Wellek and Warren's social institutions. In addition, the model describes the participation of the instances in the subgenre not in categorical terms, but rather in ordinal terms, in the prototype theory. Through this, the model unites elements of the family resemblance and prototype theory into a single formal model.

Until this point, the several steps of this section have assigned and delivered new data to the model. The four observations that will follow are an essential part of the scope of this model (following Weisberg's terminology). From now, I do not deliver new data to the model, I add more examples of categories, features, and texts. The model is now expected to reproduce the result of the observations, or allow responding aspects that have not found a proper quantification.

The seventh observation states that some genres are more similar to one another than other genres. Similarity can be understood as the number of shared features and texts across genres. Therefore, two genres that are more similar would share more connections to features and texts and can be visualized as closed nodes in the illustration.³ To observe this more clearly, in Figure 107 Add a subgenre to the network, the dialogue novel, which is very dissimilar to the categories analyzed until now in this chapter.
