# 6.2.7 Exploration Meta-Cluster 1: Cluster 217

In section I explore the best representative of second meta-clusters parallel to the analysis in the previous section: the cluster 217, which contains 87 novels. The calculation of this cluster regarding authorship, subgenres, and decades that show the highest value for a subgenre, the modernist novel (0.20), followed closely by the author Miró (0.20), a modernist author. After this, the ARI drops by half, with the authors Valle-Inclán (0.10), Azorin (0.09), the bibliographic subgenre (0.08), the decade of 1900 (0.06), and the philosophical and poetic novel (0.06). The modernist subgenre, modernist authors, and the modernist decade point clearly towards the direction of this cluster.

When the 87 texts in this cluster are contrasted with the rest of the novels, 129 out of 210 textual and metadata fields show a statistical difference. The novels of this cluster tend to be much shorter (22,892 tokens on average versus 68,722), and one of the fields with the strongest association is the author Miró, who wrote 19 of the 87, and only his very first novel is placed outside the cluster, a novel (*La madre de Ojeda*) that the author himself rejected during his life. In the negative group, the author that best fits the group is Baroja, who is mainly found outside the cluster (35 works), except for his three dialogue novels. This could give the impression that this cluster tends to select the entire production of several authors. To a certain degree, many authors appear mainly in one cluster, but many in both such as Gáldos, Valle-Inclán, Bazán, Blasco Ibáñez, Serna, and even Miró and Baroja. Two authors are found merely in one cluster are Azorin (inside the cluster of Miró) and Valdés (outside the cluster). In general, the authors of the cluster are more likely to be male and tend to be young, born around 1879 (specific details in the Jupiter Notebook).

Besides the groups of authors, there is a surprisingly long list of data and literary phenomena that differentiate the cluster from the rest of the novels. The 87 works of the group tend to be more autobiographical, more prone to be vague in their representation of the world, and show a tendency to favor autodiegetic narrators. Furthermore, the protagonists of both groups are different. In the cluster, there is a preference for male protagonists, old or mature, typically working as artists. Differences in the setting can also be found. Although contemporary times are in general more frequent, the action of texts in cluster 217 are slightly more likely to place in the past (antiquity, middle ages).
