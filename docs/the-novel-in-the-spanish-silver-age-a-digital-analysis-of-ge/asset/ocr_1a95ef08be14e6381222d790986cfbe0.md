They compare the combination of features with the 30 and 50 most frequent words and use Multiple Regression for analyzing the corpus. Their results show that their combination of features had fewer errors than both for authorship and genre. From the current per- perspective, this work shows a clear cleavat: The frontier of dozens of most frequent words has been largely overcome and raises the question whether the chosen features would still yield better results than thousands of lexical features.

The first article considering Spanish texts for genre classification, to my knowledge is Cerviño Beresi et al. (2004), who apply classification algorithms for journalistic speeches in a big corpus of Argentinian newspapers. They evaluate various algorithms and the annotation from several lexical stemming tools and report an accuracy between 0.82 and 0.96 for the different binary genres. Nevertheless, they select the 10 (out of 19) genres that achieve higher results in the first sections of the evaluation, a step that can be criticized as cherry picking genres.
