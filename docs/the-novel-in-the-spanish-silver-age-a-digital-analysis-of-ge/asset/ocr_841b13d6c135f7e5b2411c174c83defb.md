# 11.20 Dialogue Novel

The dialogue novel shows an eigenvector centrality of 0.05, with a relatively high specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.95). This subgenre is populated by 14 texts (when considering only CoNSA- canon), with a middle range of canonicity on average (0.95 pages of the man- ual MdLe, 1.14 when considering only CoNSA-canon). The classifiers yield perfect results (1.0 F-score).

Although the top feature is a literary one, in general the linguistic and textual characteristics are more specific for this genre, especially the TEL- tag sp, typically used for encoding plays, but also for these texts (see Sections 2.1.4 and 3.1.8). However, other linguistic information is less obvious and still very specific, as the forms of pronouns and verbs in first person (annotated by Freeling), the short length of the sentences, many divisions, or the frequency of the typographical sign of person. Beside, semantic features are overrepresented, as social interaction (conceder, simpatia), negative vocabulary (impertente, initil, insensato, vengar, disparate, desortient, insustancial), cognitive verbs (suponer, aprender, attenuar, and cognitive verbs marked by WordNet), and others (óptica, ser).
