Even more distinctive is the fact that stories do not represent the author's life. Other distinctive literary metadata are urban settings, in some cases traveling through different countries, a contemporary realistic world, with a heterodiegetic narrator. Many of these features are not explicitly mentioned by the two monographs about the erotic novels that I have summarized in Section 2.1.8. However, some of the characteristics that they are mentioned in this analysis, like a middle-class female protagonist, urban settings, or trips. Other references to interior spaces, underwear, female body parts do not stand out in my analysis. The texts most frequently assigned with this label are by Trigo (Del frío al fuego, Mi media naranja, En la carrera), but also from others like Bacarisse (Los terribles amores de Aglibtero y Ciudad), Picón (Del culto y sabrosa), or Serna (El gran hotel, la vida blanca y negra), followed by others subgenres at around 5% and 6% (costumbrist, poetic, naturalist novel).

Are these four empirical definitions of subgenres adequate? They provide an overview of several internal and external aspects, as well as their in the computational analysis and its reception in the history of literature. However, it can be argued that there is important information missing, as the development of the protagonist, the authors that wrote in the category, or the decade in which it was mainly produced (1880 for naturalist novel, 1900 for erotic novel, 1920 for historical novel, see Section 3.1.5). This is partially a result of the cross-sectional interest of this research, which does not try to observe the chronological development of the genres. However, some of these aspects could be fixed by using this information as features if they are considered important characteristics for descriptions of genres.
