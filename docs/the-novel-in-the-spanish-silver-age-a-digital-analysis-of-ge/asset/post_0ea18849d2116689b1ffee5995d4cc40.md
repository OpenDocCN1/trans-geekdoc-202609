## 11. Empirical Description through the Tripartite Graph Model

From the point of view of linguistic information, one semantic area is clearly overrepresented: magic (adivinar, hechicería, predecir, encantar). Besides, other areas that appear frequently are qualifications (excelente, falso), danger and movement (refugio, imprevisión, destreza, adelantar, imposible), social interactions (preguntar, confessar, chisme), very general words (ser, cosa), and others (soberano, empleo, óptica).

Some works associated with this category are La jirafa sagrada de Madariaga, works by Fernández Flores (El secreto de Baba Azul, las siete columnas, el hombre que compró un automóvil), El profesor iniciado by Jarnés, Morsa by Valeria, La torre de los siete jurados by Carrere, el secreto del Acueducto by Serna, La tournée de Dios by Jardiel Poncela, La última llamada by Bazán.

The most similar subgenre is the adventure novel (15% of shared features and texts), followed by the historical, comedy (12%), and philosophical novel (10%).

A step further away from the center is the novel, with an eigenvector centrality of 0.05, and relatively high specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.94). The subgenre is populated by 21 novels (only six when considering CoSSA-canon), and these are works that have a relatively low canonization status (0.77 pages in average in the manual MdLE, 1.58 when considering only CoSSA-canon). The classification results are relatively high, with a 0.93 F1-score.

The most specific features of this subgenre are linked and closely related, unsurprisingly, to the topic of war (enemigo, luchar, guerra, paz), army (tropa, milicia, soldado, grado, artillería), fire and weapons (llama, explosión, fuego, proyectil), also to people and social interactions (gente, sustituir) and space (municipio, patria).

The literary features distinctive for this category are settings in towns, in Africa, or several countries (these last only in CoSSA). The ending tends to be either partially positive or partially negative (only in CoSSA). Also only in CoSSA, the information about the protagonist tends to be blurred (not single protagonist, but a group of soldiers). An interesting aspect is that novels tend to take place in contemporary times in a realistic world. This is noteworthy, as the enormous majority of plots in the corpus share these characteristics.

Texts assigned frequently with this label are works by Concha Espina (Luna roja), Unamuno (Paz en la guerra), Blasco Ibáñez (Los cuatro apocalipsis, Los enigmas de la mujer), Maeter, Díaz Fernández (Acero de Madrid).

The closest subgenre is the historical novel, but with a lower percentage: 21%. This is followed by historical (18%), bucolic (10%), literary fiction (6%), and social novel (5%).

## 11.2 Dialogue Novel

The dialogue novel shows an eigenvector centrality of 0.05, with a relatively high specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.95). This subgenre is populated by 14 texts (when considering only CoNSA-canon), with a middle range of canonicity on average (1.05 pages in the manual MdLE, 1.14 when considering only CoNSA-canon). The classifiers yield perfect results (1.0 F1-score).

Although the top feature is a literary one, in general the linguistic and textual characteristics are more specific for this genre, especially the TEI-tag, typically used for plays, but also for these texts (see Sections 2.1 and 3.1). However, other linguistic information is less obvious and still very specific, as the forms of pronouns and verbs in the first person (annotated by Freeling), the short length of the sentences, many divisions, or the typographical sign. Beside, semantic features are overrepresented, as social interaction (conceder, simpatía), negative vocabulary (insútil, insensato, disparate, desorientar, insustancial), cognitive verbs (suponer, aprender, conceder), and others (ser).

However, the most predominant feature is the lack of a narrator in the first or third person. The geographical settings show several possibilities such as Africa, non-existing cities, or rural areas. The plots to end happily and the texts do not be autobiographical. Many protagonists as adults, female characters are slightly overrepresented, commonly from the middle class.

Novels frequently assigned with this label are works by Azaña (La velada en Benicarló), Baroja (La casa de Aizgorri, Rey, la leyenda de Juan de Alzate), Galdós (Realidad, el abuelo, la razón de la sinrazón), or Benavente (Cartas de mujeres).

The clearly similar subgenre is the mono-dialogue novel (43% of shared features and texts), followed at a distance by the philosophical, fantasy (9%), comedy (7), and bucolic novel (6%).

This sense is the third exceptional case analyzed in this research study, obtained in Chapter 6.2 through unsupervised methods (along with literary bucolic novel). This category has an eigenvector centrality of 0.04 and a relatively high specificity of the features (standard deviation of z-scores of 1.11). It is composed by only 11 texts (when considering CoSSA-canon). The works in this category show a middle range of canonicity (1.05 pages in average, 1.17 for CoSSA-canon). As in the previous case, the classifiers yield perfect classification.

Also, as in the dialogue novel, the most distinctive features are forms that do not have a narrator in the first or second person, but are written in epistolary or dialogue form. After these, the most specific features are the frequency of the TEI-tag sp, negative vocabulary (insútil, desorientar, prescindir, excluir, insensato, disforme, insustancial, tímido, sentido, impertinent, disparate), cognitive verbs (precaer, suponer, aprender, conceder, marked by WordNet), and others (otro). Beside, pronouns and verbs in the first person and a large proportion of divisions are overrepresented.

In addition to the narrator, other literary features are overrepresented in this category, as a positive ending, several possible settings (non-existingents, a trip through several countries, rural areas, and cities), and female protagonists are overrepresented in comparison to the rest of the corpus.

Texts assigned with this label are works by Benavente (Cartas de mujeres), Chacel (Estación), Aub (Luis Álvarez Peña), Azaña (La velada en Benicarló), Lorca (Fray Antonio), or Galdós (Realidad, la razón de la sinrazón).
