However, this methodology also has an underlying hypothesis that no single specific source (covers, publishers, literary scholars, annotators) can completely understand literary subgenre. Rather, several perspectives show clear differences, which some seem closely related (social-nationalist, naturalist-realist, social-realist).

In the final, subgenres show clear differences to be reconciled. Only one group (autobiographic, biographic, memoir, educational novel) throws doubts on whether one would be able to distinguish between all of them. The palette still contains some subgenres that are clearly on the frontier of the novel and other genres (dialogue, poetic, autobiographical novel, *gregueria*). Contrary to my expectations, three-author-labels have been confirmed as consistent semantic-labels: *nivola, gregueria* and * nacional*, while many others have proved to be inconsistent across the sources. This does not mean that they are truly a phenomenon comparable to subgenres as dialogue or adventure novel, but it does point out that different authors consider them to be useful categories and that some remaining labels in the palette are closely related to specific decades, such as the realist, naturalist or modernist novel.

One of the goals of this chapter was not only obtain a set for the rest of this research, but also to try to express it in such a way that other researchers can use it for other languages, periods or corpora. For this reason, I have mapped the semantic-labels with The Thema schema whose codes could be used as multilingual bridges. In a perfect scenario, the entire palette could be expressed using the codes of Thema. Sadly, this was not the case for nine of the remaining subgenres, although it was for 14,$^{10}$ which accounts for 61% of the palette.

Are the sources applying *wrong subgenres* or is *Thema* broad enough? I do not think the role of *Thema* is to cover one-author-labels as *nivola* or *gregueria*, because that would speak against the very basic purpose of such schema. On the other hand, it is surprising that subgenres that are very well established in studies of literature, such as realist or naturalist, are not covered.
