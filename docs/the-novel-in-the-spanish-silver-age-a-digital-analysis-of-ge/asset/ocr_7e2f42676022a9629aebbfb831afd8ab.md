The distribution of Figure 10 resembles the previous one, with two unexpected high values in the 1930s and 1940s. The median is the year 1945, while the mode⁷ of the decades is 1930, with an IQR of 31.5 years, meaning half of the authors died between 1913 and 1977. Because the average span of life is about 70 years, the decades around 1950s would have been expected to be the ones with the highest values. The reason for this abnormality is arguably explain why the Civil War and its repercussions during the Francoist dictatorship (for example, Miguel Hernández died in prison in 1942). This could explain why no young writer published novels during the decade of the 1930s: after 1936, the cultural branch could have been affected by the war, a hypothesis that will be tackled later. The span of deaths is even longer than the span of births, covering around 120 years. One interesting aspect is the fact that some authors died shortly after 1880: Selgas Carrasco died in 1882 at 60 years, Rosalía de Castro died in 1885 at 48. Therefore, some writers are markedly older than the rest, which means that this population, the corpus, contains authors related to previous literary periods, and it can be the explanation for some abnormalities observed in Section 8.6.⁸
