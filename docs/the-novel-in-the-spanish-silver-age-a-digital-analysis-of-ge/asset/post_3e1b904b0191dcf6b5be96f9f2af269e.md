# 11. Appendix

Finally, this Appendix aims at offering empirical descriptions (or real definitions following Pawlowski) of each subgenre using the model presented in Chapter 8. The aim is to provide several short paragraphs summarizing the most important information about each subgenre, such as the results on classification, its canonicity, its main distinctive semantic, textual, and literary features (intention), the number of texts in the corpus (extension) and its best representatives (the ones that are most frequently asked to it), and its similarity to other subgenres in terms of shared features and texts. The intention of every subgenre is defined by the top specific features, both linguistic and literary, mentioning every which of these two types is more specific for the subgenre (see Section 7.1.5). The semantic features are grouped to give a more coherent overview, but the specific data from the dictionary by Maria Moliner or Wordnet are listed in parenthesis in their original language. Furthermore, I do not pick only the information that fits my concept of the genre correctly, but all the top features, in many cases indicating that their interpretation is unclear.
