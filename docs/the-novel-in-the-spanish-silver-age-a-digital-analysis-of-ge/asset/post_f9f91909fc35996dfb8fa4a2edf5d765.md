I understand that romantic stories starring either vampires (Thema CFR) or protagonists with uniforms (Thema FRP) currently constitute a larger section of the editorial market than picararesque novels or collections of sonnets, yet a fraction of the catalogs of specific publishers could be described with more accurate historical terms and, moreover, other fields to adopt this schema. Thema is a valuable initiative towards a standardization of textual prose and subgenres, but its goal is book trade and furthermore important concepts in literary history are ignored.

What is the result for the corpus? How much of the corpus can be described through the analyzed palette? It covers almost the entire corpus, or 98% of the texts. To be more specific, only five novels have lost their labels not consistent enough: El primer loco de Castro, Contraque by Sender, Doña Isabel by Azorín, and by Serna (El chalet de las rosas y La mujer de ámbar). It is interesting that de Castro and Sender are authors who belong to other periods (de to Romanticism, Sender to the post-war period), which is probably the reason why their texts fit better in subgenres that are not (anymore or yet) present in the corpus.
