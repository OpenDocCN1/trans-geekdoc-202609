What exactly comprises a token is still open for discussion. The particular
case of punctuation is usually treated in three different ways: One can keep
it as a token, delete it, or maintain it together with the previous or following
lexical token. Normally, these decisions are not explicitly explained and in the
section on feature evaluation the reader only has a mere glimpse into it. In
test tests, I have observed that some punctuation characters (like excla-
marks) can be some of the best markers for genre distinction, which
is the reason why I decide to include all punctuation characters as separate
tokens.

A more complex question is whether tokens are the best way of modeling
t lexical information that current linguistic tools can give us. Lexology
and lexicography consider units like memos ('miss') or example ('for
example') as lexical units or idioms whose meaning is not (or at least
entirely) derived from its components. The tool FreeLing offers two differ-
base units of analysis: tokens or multiwords (i.e. idioms). As previously
explained, both of these have been annotated in the corpus with the goal of
testing whether a more sophisticated understanding of lexical information
im improve the results, or if tokens remain the best type of features.



Grammatical features are usually information added to the classification of
gene that improve the accuracy of results (Kessler, Numberberg, and Schütze
1997; Stamatatos, Faktotakis, and Kokkinakis 2001; Santini 2011; Allison et al.
2011). For this kind of annotation, I have applied the tool FreeLing which is
developed and maintained by Luis Padró at the Universitat Politècnica de
Catalunya (Padró and Stanislavsky 2002). Besides typical steps like tokeniza-
t, sentence splitting, PoS (Part of Speech) tagging and lemmatization, the
resource has several functions for Spanish texts, such as Named Entity Recog-
ition, WordNet-based sense annotation, or syntactical parsing. The tool uti-
lizes the EAGLES tag set and offers several output formats (CSV, JSON). Theesearch group wrote scripts that take the XML output of FreeLing and con-
verted it into TEI XML.
