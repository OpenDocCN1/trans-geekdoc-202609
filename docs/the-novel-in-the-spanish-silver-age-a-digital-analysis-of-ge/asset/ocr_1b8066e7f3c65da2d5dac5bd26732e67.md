But these errors are not randomly distributed, they show specific tendencies and it is be interesting to observe the associations among them. Realist, naturalist and social novels tend to be confused with one another, and other subgenres like educational or erotic novels are grouped together. Adventure novels and memoirs tend to be labeled as historical novels, while philosophical and partially erotic novels tend to be recognized as comedy novels. In summary, the classification groups the text into three macro-subgenres: historical, comedy and realist-naturalist-social.

Following this, a further multi-class classification test can be performed using only three subgenres that would include the rest: historical, naturalist. Using logarithmic transformation and the 5,000 MFW again, the F-score (micro) of the classification now increases to 0.70 (over the baseline of 0.56 with statistical difference, p-value < 0.001) and the fusion matrix in Figure 85 is generated. When using only these three major subgenres, the perpendicular lines have the greatest values of the matrix, although many texts are still mismatched. Roughly a third of the texts are misclassified, even when the algorithm has only three different classes.

The multi-class classification approach would be the most fitting task for researchers with a single and highly qualitative label for each text, which can be a better fit for a longitudinal historical perspective. The confusion matrices produced allow one to observe which subgenres tend to be mismatched. These errors can be understood as similarities between the categories in gradual macro-models such as the one by Petersen (1944, Section 2.2.3.3). There are two main disadvantages of the multi-class task. The first is the necessary reduction of information when the sources apply more than one label per text, which is the normal situation for subgenres. The second is the low results, which can be understood as proof that the subgenres of this period cannot be correctly classified, as many authors and literary scholars of the period have revealed (see Chapter 2.1).
