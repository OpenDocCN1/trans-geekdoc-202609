# 6.2.2 Clustering in Stylometry

In Chapter 2.2, the concept of clustering was briefly explained. This is one type of unsupervised task in Machine Learning, meaning that the researcher does not have labels for the instances (in my case, texts), and the algorithm uses the similarities of the features (e.g. word frequencies) to group the instances (Müller and Guido 2016, 133–34). Clustering is the most applied technique in stylometry, partially due to its visual impact and because it is the default option for a very popular tool:lo (Eder, Kestont, and Rybicki 2016). In a typical stylistic analysis with sty, either for exploratory purposes or for attribution, a number of tokens are transformed into a matrix using a distance measure such as Delta (Burrows 2002) in any of its variants (Ever et al. 2018). This distance matrix is clustered in a hierarchical structure (a dendrogram) and uses the labels of the authors for coloring the names of the texts. The Figure 74 shows the turnout of clustering 42 randomly selected texts from ConSSA.

As be observed, some clusters group together texts of specific authors, such as Bazán, Gáldos, Fernández Pérez, Valle-Inclán, or Azorín, while clusters mix works from various authors.

In my opinion, there are several bad practices and misunderstandings in the process described above. The most important error is the fact that the two types of tasks in Machine Learning are being confused. Instead of using supervised methods, unsupervised methods are applied for data sets that contain specific labels (in this case, about authors). In addition, the clustering algorithm as default in stylo is Ward, an agglomerative method that first links the instances to a new instance or to a new cluster, composing a hierarchical tree-like structure. Due to this, a structure without a fixed number of groups is created, which leads the researcher to pick the clusters that fit their hypothesis. In my case, I can consider that the two texts of Gáldos (lower section of the cluster) are separated from Baroja, while the three texts of Azorín (upper section) belong to the same cluster.
