## 5.1.7 Consistent Labels across Sources

After analyzing how the sources classified the texts, the focus now changes to the labels themselves, and the question of which labels are assigned with a minimal consistency by several institutions is asked. This will also establish which terms are applied without any human agreement, being randomly assigned.

To observe the consistency of the labels, I calculate the Kappa-value for each label in all 15 possible combinations of sources. This gives a table with 109 columns for the semantic-labels and 15 rows for the pairwise combinations of sources. If a column contains only zeros, no pair of sources uses the label in a minimally consistent manner. In other words, there is no evidence that they were not assigned randomly. This is the case for 77 semantic-labels of all 109 labels: The different institutions treat the labels without any shared understanding on which texts should be included. Even when the inventory is large, it is worthy including in its full extent to better comprehend the importance of this step:

almacenamiento, americana, animales, antiguo, antireligiosa, antología, autoayuda, biblioteca, borrador, catalán, **ciencia-ficción**, ciudad, clásico, completa, consulta, cortesana, crimen, crítica-social, romano, cuando, cuento, cómic, **de tesis**, de acción, diario, ensayo, **epistolar**, epopeya, escena, **español**, **esperento**, estrategia, etopeya, etope, europea, ficción, familia, ficción-política, folletín, fábula, híbrido, idiomas, inglés, intriga, irrever, juvenil-infantil, larga, leyenda, libre de viajes, literatura, marítimo, **moral**, nebulosa, normativa, novela, original, otro, pacifista, periodismo, picaresca, **policía**, **regeneracion**, religiosa, saga, sentencias, siglo-xix, suelta, sátira, teatro, teoría literaria, terror

## Figure 55: Agreement between sources for semantic-label

![](img/98c86d70e9e43286af05120dbff29113_240_0.png)

As can be observed, the agreement is very low. The median value (thick line in the box, the thin one represents mean) shows values of zero in almost all medians with only four exceptions: war, historical, cedy and adventure. If the mean value of all 32 labels is calculated, it gives a Kappa-value of 0.02 (with a standard deviation of 0.03). In general, the sources do not agree on the assignment of the subgenres. Almost no pair of sources shows strong agreement for any label (as mentioned before, Artstein 2017, 302). For the majority of cases, agreement is an exceptional phenomenon, shown as a flier in the box plot on top of gregaria. Some labels are very close to being randomly assigned since one pair of sources agrees, and in many cases with a very weak agreement. The labels on the right show agree for only two pairs of sources with a value lower than 0.1. Even when two pairs of sources have agreed on their assignment, should such low values be accepted, and should they be considered consistent? The sentimental novel be treated as a consistent subgenre only because two of the six sources agree on it with a Kappa-value of 0.02. Speaking against this is the fact that there are labels for which two sources agree, but the value of these are extremely high: gregaria (perfect agreement in one pair) or episodio nacional (0.95). In these cases, only two sources agree on their use, but their assignment is almost identical, which is a very rare outcome.

To achieve a more robust consistency and yet be sensitive to the extremely high cases, I define two criteria for a label to be considered consistent:

- Either two pairs of sources or more have to agree on its assignment,
- Or the Kappa-value of the agreeing pair has to be greater than the accepted threshold of 0.8.

These two criteria filter out those labels that are assigned by only two sources with a very weak agreement: drama, historia, política, regionalista, romántica, sentimental, simbólica, taurina, vanguardista. These labels are not completely randomly assigned, but the agreement of only two sources is too weak. This step yields 23 consistent semantic-labels:
