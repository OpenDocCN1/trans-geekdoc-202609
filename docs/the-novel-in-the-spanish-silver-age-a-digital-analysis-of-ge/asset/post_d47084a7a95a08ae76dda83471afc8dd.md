## 11.5 Educational Novel

The next subgenre is the educational novel, with an eigenvector centrality of 0.22. It contains 70 novels in CoNSSA, 39 in CoNSSA-canon. These are highly canonized, with an average of 1.6 pages in the manual. The specificity of features is the lowest (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.36), which in general explains a poor classification result, 0.79 F1-score.

The most distinctive features are a child or young protagonist at the beginning, and a rather long span of the action. The metadata features are also noticeable: a change between rural and urban spaces, different narrators. As far as linguistic features are concerned, voluntary (school, university, study, protest, imagine, describe), social processes (school, university, study, protest, imagine, describe), family, family (father, lineage), relatively general concepts (world, literature, mechanics, category, list), or qualitative adjectives (impositive, exact, extravagant), besides the quotation mark and a large number of sections (divisions inside a chapter).

Some instances with larger proportions of sources assigning them to this subgenre are in Italy or Africa, either with the plot taking place completely in towns or the continuation in rural places. The narrator tends to be homodie, the protagonist male, and the ending tends to be either uncertain or partially negative.

The clearest examples are El jardín de los frailes by Azaña, Genio y figura... by Valera, El doctor Centeno by Galós, and Camino de perfección or piedra angular by Baroja.

## 11.6 Philosophical Novel

The philosophical novel has an eigenvector centrality of 0.19. It contains 88 novels in CoNSSA, 45 in CoNSSA-canon. These are relatively highly canonized, with 1.35 pages in the manual. The specificity of features is relatively low (standard deviation of z-scores of 0.34), which explains the low classification result of 0.77 F1-score.

The features that are most specific for this subgenre are semantic features about cognitive (vac, deschar) and social processes (study, teach, protest, imagine, describe) school (college, university), relatively general concepts (world, literature, mechanics, category, list), or qualitative adjectives (impositive, exact, extravagant), besides the quotation mark and a large number of sections (divisions inside a chapter).

Some of the texts identified with high frequency are La pata de la raposa and Troteras y zaraderas by Pérez de Ayala, Locura y muerte de Nadie by Jarnés, Cinematógrafo by Carrananque, or La piedra angular by Bazán.

The subgenres that share a larger proportion of features and texts with the philosophical novels are the educational novel (29%), and a clear step behind the social novel (21%), the comedy novel (20%), literary fiction, and memoir (both with 20%).
