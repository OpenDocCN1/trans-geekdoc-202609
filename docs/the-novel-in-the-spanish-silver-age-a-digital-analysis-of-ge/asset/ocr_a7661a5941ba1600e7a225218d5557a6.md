As with the squares in Rosch's paper of 1973 shown in Figure 3, the birds should have been arranged along an axis (horizontal or vertical), with the robin as the positive (the best exemplar) and the penguin and ostrich at the negative pole (the worst ones). The central ap- appears in Rosch's 1973 paper up to 58 times, many times as a synonym for focal or prototypical, but was dropped in her paper of 1975. The concept of central has its purpose in the example of the colors, where the wavelength creates a natural arrangement, but is not in the other categories.

In summary, Rosch's prototype model, each item does not belong to the concept in conceptual terms (i.e. it does or it does not), but rather in terms of degree (i.e. it belongs to the category or it belongs vastly to the category).

One of the most comprehensive papers on the application of the family re- semblable and prototype theories to genres was written by Hempelfer (2014) initially rejected re as an accurate model for genres (405–6) and the nominalistic critique against genre as a tautology (409). Hempelfer proposed the model of family resemblance to explain the instances (texts) where there is no agreement on a basic set of features in order to belong to a genre (409). To operationalize the ahorical invariants (archi-texts or modes in Genette), he proposed the model of the prototype (414), remarking that:

> [...] the fundamental difference between the 'classical' concept of class as means for categorization and the prototype concept consists in the fact that a class tolerates only membership or non-membership, while mem-ship in a prototype category is ‘a matter of gradience’ (Hempelfer 2014, 411).
