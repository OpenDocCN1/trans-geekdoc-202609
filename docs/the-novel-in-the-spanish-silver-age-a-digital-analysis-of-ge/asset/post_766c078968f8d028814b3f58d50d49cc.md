In a recent paper, the researcher Juana María González has studied the Index literario, which published reviews of literary works between 1932 and 1936 (González 2021). One of the aspects that she analyzes is the distribution of the authors' gender, arguing that this journal was a progressive publication with women among the publishing board. However, in her study González finds that the number of texts written by women is surprisingly low: around 6% in total, in the case of the novels. As it can be observed, the distribution in the MdLE and in CoNSSA are similar to these results.

Achieving a more balanced scenario of past periods will profoundly modify our concept of literature for researchers and Academia in general. ## 3.1 Publication of Data

The publication of the data was important for my project and for the entire CoNSSA. A section of it was made available in early phases of the project as part of the textbook, a series of corpora in French, Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese. See Schöch et al. (2019) for a description of the corpora and Calvo Tello, Henny-Krahmer, and Schöch (2018) for several comparative analyses.

Similarly to the textbook, the CoNSSA is made available as a GitHub repository. In contrast to the textbox, a section of the texts in CoNSSA is still under copyright. For this reason, CoNSSA has several folders depending on the legal status of the texts. Besides the GitHub repository, the corpus has also been archived in the European platform Zenodo (Nielsen 2013). As the repository states, the texts themselves are in the public domain and are provided here with the Public Domain Declaration and can be reused without restrictions. The TEI XML markup and the metadata are published with a Creative Commons License CC-BY. The repository also contains a suggestion as to how other researchers can reference the corpus.

For those whose authors died 80 years ago, three versions of the texts are available: the master format in TEI XML, the annotated format in TEI XML, and a simple file that only contains the body as plain text. Around 61% of CoNSSA (217 novels) is published in this manner.

A recent interdisciplinary article with a focus in Germany has proposed a series of extracted features that can be published even when the texts are still protected by copyright (Schöch et al. 2020). Following this article, I have decided to publish not the entire text but a selection of extracted features for all the texts that can either be published without restriction or that I digitized myself. These files are saved as tables (CSV files). In these files, each column represents a different feature. Among other features, the researchers can find the frequency in the text of tokens (tokenized in different manners), semantic annotation, grammatical annotation, entity information, or TEI tags (see Chapter 4.1 for more details). By this, the most relevant features are available, covering almost 78% of the entire CoNSSA (279 novels).

One part of CoNSSA that I am able to publish for the entire corpus is the metadata. Given that the metadata consists of formalized information about the texts annotated by myself, it is not copyright protected. The metadata available also contains the information about the source of the digital text. This means that even in the cases where I am not able to publish neither the entire text nor extracted features, I am providing the source of the digital text. Reconstructing the remaining 22% of the texts is a feasible task for other researchers.

Besides, as time passes, I plan to publish more and more texts when the moving window of 80 years after the author's date of death allows it. For example, the novels of the following authors will be made available freely as part of CoNSSA in the coming years: José Díaz Fernández (2022), Miguel Hernández (2023), Ricardo León, and Félix Urabayen (2024). On one hand, this will slowly increase the proportion of the available CoNSSA as years pass by. On the other hand, this means that, with the current strategy, the entire corpus will be in theory only fully available by the end of the 21st century (Francisco de Ayala died in 2009).

### 3.2.2 Metadata, Distant Reading, and Hypotheses Testing

Metadata is at the very core of many studies. As Burnard points out "without metadata, the investigator has nothing but unknowable provenance" (2004). When many researchers or areas of research do not adopt the word metadata, they employ it as the very basic index. For example, the standard use case for stylometry, author attribution, needs to assign every text to an author. A subfield of stylometry, stylochrony, investigates the date of publication of the texts, which can be seen as another type of metadata. Library catalogs can be understood as collections of metadata about books (NISO 2004) with a special kind of information: the reference to the place in the library where the book is supervised. Machine Learning would use metadata, calling labels or only representing its classes, would apply the available metadata as ground-truth to color and display the results. As the repository states, the texts themselves are in the public domain. The TEI XML markup and the metadata are published with a Creative Commons License CC-BY. The repository also contains a suggestion as to how other researchers can reference the corpus.
