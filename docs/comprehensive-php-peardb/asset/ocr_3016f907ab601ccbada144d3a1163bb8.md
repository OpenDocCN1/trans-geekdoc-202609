# TransAlchemy: A Novel AI-Driven Framework for Personalized Recommendation in the Digital Era

Alexander T. Maxwell, Sophia R. Chen, Daniel L. Wright, Emily R. Carter

Department of Computer Science, Stanford University, Stanford, CA 94305, USA

# **Abstract**

The rapid proliferation of digital platforms has created an overwhelming amount of user-generated content, making personalized recommendation essential. TransAlchemy is a novel AI-driven framework that integrates large language models with collaborative filtering and knowledge graphs to capture nuanced user preferences. By jointly modeling semantic and behavioral signals, the framework produces accurate, diverse, and interpretable recommendations. Extensive experiments on public benchmarks show consistent improvements over state-of-the-art baselines.

Index Terms—Recommendation Systems, Large Language Models, Knowledge Graphs, Explainable AI, Personalization

# 1 Introduction

The digital era has fundamentally changed the way users interact with information and services. However, the sheer volume of available options often leads to information overload, making it difficult for users to discover relevant content. Traditional recommendation models rely primarily on historical user-item interactions and thus struggle to understand semantic preferences or cold-start users. In this paper, we propose TransAlchemy, a framework that combines the reasoning power of large language models with structured knowledge to perform personalized and explainable recommendation.

# 2 Related Work

Prior research has explored recommender systems through collaborative filtering, content-based filtering, and hybrid approaches. More recently, large language models have been used to enhance recommendation quality by encoding item descriptions and user preferences. Knowledge graphs have also been adopted to introduce semantic relationships among users and items. TransAlchemy builds on these directions by unifying large language model representations with graph-based reasoning in a single end-to-end framework.
