These data come from patients who were admitted to a tertiary hospital due to acute stroke. They were treated in the ward and the status (dead or alive) were recorded. The variables are:

- doa: date of admission
- dod: date of discharge
- status: event at discharge (alive or dead)
- sex: male or female
- dm: diabetes (yes or no)
- gcs: Glasgow Coma Scale (value from 3 to 15)
- sbp: Systolic blood pressure (mmHg)
- dbp: Diastolic blood pressure (mmHg)
- wbc: White cell count
- time2: days in ward
- stroke_type: stroke (Ischemic stroke or Hemorrhagic stroke)
- referral_from: patient was referred from a hospital or from a hospital

The outcome of interest is from admission to death. The time variable is time2 (in days) and the event variable is status. The event of interest is dead. We also note variables dates and other categorical variables are in character format. The rest are in numerical format.

# 11.7 Kaplan–Meier Survival Estimates

Kaplan–Meier survival estimates are non-parametric survival estimates. Using `survfit()`, we can estimate the survival probability based on Kaplan–Meier (KM). Let's estimate the survival probabilities for all patients:

```r
KM <- survfit(Surv(time = time2, event == "dead"), data = stroke)
```
