# 9.1 Introduction

Cellular materials are found extensively in nature [1,2] and have been widely used
for high-performance devices and systems as sandwich panels and protective
damping systems. In the past decade, a research frontier of cellular materials known
as instability-based architected materials has created unusual properties such as
negative Poisson' ratio [3–6]. Specifically, architected materials are rationally
designed materials that enable complex geometry and assembly of unit
cells. Such programmability has been implemented at multiple scales
[7–11]. However, most studies do only focused on the uniform and periodic
terns. Recent advances in digital fabrication have made it possible to design and
manufacture materials with arbitrary architectures that enable nonuniform properties
and tunable responses can be achieved. Asymmetry of assembly, architecture, and nonuniform cells to enable target
function at the structural level is trivial to design and such structural complexity
will be a continuous challenge in the fields of architected materials [12].
