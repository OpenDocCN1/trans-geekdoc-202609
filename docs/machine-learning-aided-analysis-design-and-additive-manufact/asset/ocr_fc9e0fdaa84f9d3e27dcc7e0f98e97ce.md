# 15.2.3 Additive manufacturing

A commercial 3D printer called “Raise3D N2 PLUS” is used in the additive manufacturing process. First, we generate the computer-aided design of Voronoi foams. Secondly, the models are converted into stereolithography formats and transferred to a slicing software “IdeaMaker”. Finally, the models are sliced into sequentially layers and imported into the 3D printer for manufacturing. The base material of all specimens is a commercial polyacrylic acid called PolyMax. The density and melting point of printing material is 1200 kg/m³ and 170°C, respectively. The printing parameters are optimized to eliminate the influence on the mechanical properties of specimen. The layer height, extruder temperature, and speed are determined as 0.1 mm, 220°C, and 30 mm/s, respectively. The nozzle of this printer has a diameter of 0.4 mm and therefore the cell thickness of all specimens is set to 0.4 mm. Three specimens for each configuration are produced to check for the reproducibility of experimental results.

The relative density $\rho$ is defined as the ratio between foam density $\rho^*$ and the density of base material $\rho_$, is usually applied to describe the mechanical properties of foams. The average densities of all Voronoi foams are calculated and listed in Tables 15.1 and 15.2. It can be seen that the relative densities of uniform foams increase with decreasing cell size while all graded foams share a similar relative density.
