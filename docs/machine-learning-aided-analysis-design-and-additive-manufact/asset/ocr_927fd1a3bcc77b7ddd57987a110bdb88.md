Apart from the simulation, IDD shock theory [29] has been developed to study the shock wave propagation and impact resistance of graded foams under high-velocity impact [30–35]. Shen et al. [30] found the single shock mode was observed in foams with a positive gradient while there was a double shock mode in negatively graded foams. Yang et al. [33] and Ding et al. [34] focused on the crashworthiness of graded foams. However, some conclusions from these numerical and theoretical studies have not been verified by experiments.

In terms of experimental studies, only limited works over the compressive behaviors of graded cellular solids such as hollow sphere agglomerates [37]. Furthermore, these works are mainly restricted to the graded foams with discrete gradients (Fig. 14.3). In Zeng's work [36], the strongest and weakest layers were suggested to be the first and last layers, to enhance the energy absorption capacity of graded foams. Hangai et al. [37] performed the drop weight impact tests on two-layer foams with two different base materials. They found that the stress–strain curves of graded foams showed two plateau stages according to the plateau stresses of two layers. Nevertheless to the authors' knowledge, studies on the experiments of continuously graded foams with predetermined gradients remain lacking, due to the difficulty of fabrication.
