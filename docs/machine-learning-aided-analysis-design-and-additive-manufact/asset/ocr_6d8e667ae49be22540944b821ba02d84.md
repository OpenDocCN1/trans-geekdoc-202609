Utilizing advanced additive manufacturing (AM) technology, multiscale porous structures with unprecedented geometries can be readily produced and exhibit better performance than traditional stochastic metal foams. For example, the AM-manufactured AIS100Mg foams achieve a 60% higher heat transfer coefficient compared to the traditional metal foams with stochastic porosities [10]. New applications (see Fig. 17.3) for porous structures are emerging across civil engineering for protective layers, space engineering for lightweight aircraft, biomedical engineering for implants and scaffolds, etc., which attracts billions of dollars in investment from industry and academia.

#  17.2.1 Aerospace engineering applications

Aircraft are widely used nowadays as the heyday of globalization, and it has always been a challenge to find materials which possess lightweight, temperature resistant, and high strength at the same time. Porous structures provide us with a good choice, and many attempts have been made to apply porous structures in aerospace field. Rosseau et al. investigated the noise reduction potential of porous trailing-edge noise devices at an unconventional extreme high-lift air configuration (Fig. 17.4). It is found that the flow resistivity is coupled with the average pore size of a porous material, and the reduction-frequency excess noise is always accompanied with a growth in high-frequency excess noise. Hoe et al. [20] proposed a combined experimental and numerical design of graded cellular structures for multifunctional aerospace application. A bird strike experiment was conducted by numerical simulation to compare the performance of the optimized material to that of a nonnegative sandwich structure [21] developed four cellular structure topologies to act as a vibration damper in small electric aircraft motor (Fig. 17.5).
