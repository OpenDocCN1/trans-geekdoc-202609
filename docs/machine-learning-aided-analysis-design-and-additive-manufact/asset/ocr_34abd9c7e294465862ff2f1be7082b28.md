Selective laser sintering (SLS) and selective laser melting (SLM) are two typical manufacturing techniques of PBF. Take SLS as an example, as shown in Fig. 14.5, the system consists of a powder bed, powder supply platform, roller, laser source, and scanner system. Powers then, high-energy laser source is used as the heat source to melt the powder of the current layer. Afterward, the next layer of powder is covered on the previous layer by a roller. Based on the 2D plane of the scanner system is applied to melt a new layer of powder according to the preset processing path. Finally, the melting process is repeated until the entire 3D model is manufactured. SLM is a variant of SLS, whose processing orders are almost the same. SLS ensures densification through laser sources with higher energy density to produce almost dense products without postprocessing. SLS can better control the crystal structure of resulting materials and make them a higher degree of densification. SLS applies to a wider range of materials including polymer, metal, and alloy. However, SLM can only be used for specific metals and alloys, such as aluminum, and steel.
