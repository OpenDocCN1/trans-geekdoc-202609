## 17.1 Introduction

Foams or cellular solids are materials that obtain the desired properties by utilizing voids [1] that can be categorized into stochastic structure and nonstochastic structure, as shown in Fig. 17.1.

Cellular structures consisting of foams, honeycombs and lattices are widely used in industries such as aerospace engineering, civil engineering, automotive engineering, and biomedical engineering due to their excellent impact energy absorption, high specific strength, low thermal conductivity, and other special characteristics. Take metal foam as an example, the applications can vary from automotive engineering to biomedical engineering, which attracts billions of dollars investment from industry and academia (see Fig: 17.2).

While most industrial applications need specific characteristics in both structural and functional purposes, that is a gradual change in composition or structure in a certain direction. Inspired by this idea, a novel composite that combines the advantages of functionally graded materials with the properties of porous materials, called functionally graded porous (FGP) materials [3], is proposed by controlling density/or the size of the unit cells. FGP materials are utilized in many specifically engineered areas with its excellent energy absorption and significant strength properties, which comes from special adjustments to its structural shape and compositional modification, especially in the form of porosity distribution, to achieve the form that meets engineering requirements [4,5]. Therefore FGP materials have been commonly selected for a wide range of engineering applications such as absorbing systems, porous elastic sound absorbers, heat exchangers, construction materials, and electromagnetic shielding (6–9).

This chapter aims to review the applications of FGP structures in various field and the organization. ### 17.2.1 Aerospace engineering applications

Aircraft are widely used nowadays as the heyday of globalization, and it has always been a challenge to find materials which possess lightweight, temperature resistant, and high strength at the same time. Porous structures provide us with a good choice, and many attempts have been made to apply porous structures in aerospace field. Rosseau et al. investigated the noise reduction potential of porous trailing-edge noise devices at an unconventional extreme high-lift air configuration (Fig. 17.4). It is found that the flow resistivity is coupled with the average pore size of a porous material, and the reduction-frequency excess noise is always accompanied with a growth in high-frequency excess noise. Hoe et al. [20] proposed a combined experimental and numerical design of graded cellular structures for multifunctional aerospace application. A bird strike experiment was conducted by numerical simulation to compare the performance of the optimized material to that of a nonnegative sandwich structure [21] developed four cellular structure topologies to act as a vibration damper in small electric aircraft motor (Fig. 17.5). The topologies are found to have lower natural resonance and yield strength to weight ratio. Kumar et al. [22] successfully fabricated continuous fiber-reinforced graded composites, which have been found to possess good-structural capability and high-temperature reusable feature. The air–oil separator of an aircraft turbine engine employs the high porosity open-cell foam as the porous medium. Carvalho et al. [23] established numerical model to study the oil flow into the porous medium.

## 17.2.2 Civil engineering application

Porous structures are widely adopted in civil engineering fields due to their outstanding impact absorption, high-strength-weight ratio, advantageous damping, and acoust absorption properties. Porous materials are a new type of high damping material, whose damping performance are 3–10 times higher than that of dense materials [24]. Yao et al. [25] utilized the foam material in a magnetological (MR) damper and found that the MR foam is a critical factor influencing the magnetic field and leading to the adaption of dynamic response. The working mechanism of the foam MR damper is shown in Fig. 17.6. Fig. 17.6(a) is the schematic of the damper; (B) is 3D model of damper and (C) is the physical detail. Besides, porous materials possess excellent mechanical and physical characteristics, such as damping, sound isolation, light weight with high performance in dissipating energy, which is widely used as sound-absorbing and sound-insulating material in the construction industry [28]. Xu et al. [29] established a sound insulation prediction model of the aluminum foam sandwich panel using the statistical analysis method and verified by experiments to discuss the effect of the density and thickness of the aluminum foam on the sound insulation property of the sandwich panel. In Fig. 17.7, the sandwich panel, (B) is sound source room and (C) is receiving room. They also optimized the sound insulation property of the sandwich panel by installation of damping layer.
