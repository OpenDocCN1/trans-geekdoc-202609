# ## 16.2.1 Experimental set-up

Quasi-static compression tests are conducted at an initial strain rate of $10^{-3}^{-1}$. The tests are carried out according to ASTM D1621-16 using a universal testing machine. The specimen is compressed between two cellular plates. The applied load $F$ and the deformation $\Delta$ of the specimen are measured by the load cell and optical extensometer, respectively. A digital camera is employed to capture the global deformation during the tests. The image resolution and acquisition frequency is $1280 \times 800$ pixels and 50 fps, respectively.

# ## 16.2.2 Construction of the mesoscopic geometric model

Foam materials have pores inside a solid base material, which is similar to the generation process of the Voronoi model. Hence, the Voronoi model [50] is employed in this chapter to construct the mesoscopic geometric model for the numerical simulation of foams. The generation process of Voronoi foams can be divided into three steps. First, a three-dimensional space is created. Second, a set of nuclei is inserted into this space according to Voronoi tessellation. Specifically, a subsequent nucleus is accepted only if its distance to existing nuclei is greater than $\delta$. Finally, all nuclei grow isotropically until they touch each other to form a Voronoi foam. Foams with different cell sizes can be controlled by giving different $\delta$, resulting in different densities. Foams with gradient density configuration can be achieved by employing cell-distribution of gradient along the axial direction. For comparison, uniform foams (UF) with uniformly distributed density are also considered. The wall thicknesses of all foams and the wall thickness of the tube are both around 0.4 mm (0.4 mm of the 3D printer). It is noted that when the cell wall thickness is to a multiple of the print nozzle diameter, the 3D printer can produce more accurate foams.
