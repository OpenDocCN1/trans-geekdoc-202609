In terms of the postyielding behaviors of solid/network phase TPMS lattices, the property of base material plays a dominant role in it compared to the unit cell topology. For example, diagram structures show plateau stress when fabricated with a polymeric material in the study by Maskery et al. [95]. In contrast, a significant drop in the stress after yielding is observed and related to the fracture of the struts for metallic TPMS lattices in study by Al-Ketan [3] and Yan et al. [89]. Fig. 13.13 presents the deformation of solid/network phase TPMS lattices manufactured by different materials. The differences in their postyielding behaviors and deformation mechanism could be explained by the fact that the base material has distinct ductility. When the deformation reaches a certain level, the struts in the lattice may be subjected to a higher local strain. As a result, local fractures may be observed and lead to propagating of cracks, causing catastrophic failure of lattices eventually if the base material is brittle. However, struts deform plastically when the base material is ductile. Meanwhile, there are other factors which may affect the deformation mechanism: (1) defects introduced by the fabrication process such as the microacks or void in the lattice and (2) the set-up of the experiments as creating lateral strain due to friction between sample and loading planes.
