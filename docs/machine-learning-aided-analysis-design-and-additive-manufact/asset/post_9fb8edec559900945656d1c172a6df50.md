
Free and freed vibrations of functionally graded porous cylindrical shells

The strain vector $\varepsilon$ is expressed as:

$$\varepsilon = [\varepsilon_x, \varepsilon_{\theta}, \varepsilon_{x\theta}, \kappa_x, \kappa_{\theta}, \kappa_{x\theta}]^T \quad (4.9)$$

where $\varepsilon_x, \varepsilon_{\theta}$ denote the middle surface strains and $\kappa_x, \kappa_{\theta}$ denote the middle curvature surfaces. According to Sanders' theory, the strain–displacement relation and the curvature changes of the middle surface can be expressed as:

$$\varepsilon_x = \frac{\dot{u}}{L\eta}$$
$$\varepsilon_{\theta} = \frac{1}{R}\frac{dw}{d\theta} - \frac{w}{R}$$
$$\varepsilon_{x\theta} = \frac{1}{R}\frac{du}{d\theta} + \frac{\dot{v}}{L\eta} \quad (4.10)$$

$$\kappa_x = -\frac{\ddot{w}}{L^2\eta^2}$$
$$\kappa_{\theta} = -\frac{1}{R^2}\frac{\partial^2w}{d\theta^2} - \frac{1}{R}\frac{\dot{w}}{R}$$
$$\kappa_{x\theta} = -\frac{1}{R^2}\frac{\partial^2u}{d\theta^2} + \frac{2}{RL}\frac{\partial v}{d\theta} - \frac{2}{RL}\frac{\dot{\eta}}{R\eta} \quad (4.11)$$

The stress matrix $S$ in Eq. (4.8) is given by:

$$\begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} & 0 & B_{11} & B_{12} & 0 \\ A_{12} & A_{22} & 0 & B_{12} & B_{22} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & A_{66} & 0 & 0 & B_{66} \\ B_{11} & B_{12} & 0 & D_{11} & D_{12} & 0 \\ B_{12} & B_{22} & 0 & D_{12} & D_{22} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & D_{66} & 0 & D_{66} \end{bmatrix} \quad (4.12)$$

where $A_{ij}, B_{ij}$ and $D_{ij}$ ($i, j=1, 2, 6$) denote the elements of extensensional, extensional-coupling, and bending matrices, respectively. They are defined as:

$$A_{ij} = \int_{-h/2}^{h/2} Q_{ij}dz, B_{ij} = \int_{-h/2}^{h/2} zQ_{ij}dz, D_{ij} = \int_{-h/2}^{h/2} z^2Q_{ij}dz \quad (4.13)$$

$Q_{ij}$ in Eq. (4.13) is the element of stress-strain matrix, which can be determined by:

$$Q_{11} = Q_{22} = \frac{E(z)}{1-\mu(z)^2}, Q_{12} = \frac{\mu(z)E(z)}{1-\mu(z)^2}, Q_{66} = \frac{E(z)}{2(1+\mu(z))} \quad (4.14)$$

Artificial springs implemented at the two ends of the shell are used to simulate arbitrary boundary conditions. The potential energy of the boundary artificial springs is expressed as:

$$U_b = \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi} \left\{ k_u^2[u(0, \theta, t)]^2 + k_v^2[v(0, \theta, t)]^2 + k_w^2[w(0, \theta, t)]^2 + k_{\rho}^2\left[\frac{\Delta w(0,t)}{L_z}\right]^2 \right\} d\theta$$
+ \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi} \left\{ k_{\theta}^2[u(1, \theta, t)]^2 + k_{\phi}^2[v(1, \theta, t)]^2 + k_w^2[w(1, \theta, t)]^2 + k_{\rho}^2\left[\frac{\Delta w(1,t)}{L_z}\right]^2 \right\} d\theta \quad (4.15)$$

## 4.2.2 Solution procedure

The mode of FG cylindrical shell in the circumferential direction is defined as a harmonic function, and the axial mode is selected as a polynomial. Then displacement functions can be expressed as:

$$u(\eta, \theta, t) = \cos(n\theta+\omega t)U(\eta)$$
$$v(\eta, \theta, t) = \sin(n\theta+\omega t)V(\eta)$$
$$w(\eta, \theta, t) = \cos(n\theta+\omega t)W(\eta) \quad (4.16)$$

where $\omega$ is circular frequency and $n$ is circumferential wave number. axial mode functions $U(\eta)$, $V(\eta)$, and $W(\eta)$ expressed by Chebyshev polynomials can be written as:

$$U(\eta) = \sum_{g=0}^{NT} a_g \varphi_g(\eta)$$
$$V(\eta) = \sum_{g=0}^{NT} b_g \varphi_g(\eta)$$
$$W(\eta) = \sum_{g=0}^{NT} c_g \varphi_g(\eta) \quad (4.17)$$

in which $NT$ is the truncation order; $a_g, b_g$, and $c_g$ are unknown coefficients; $\varphi_g(\eta)$, $\varphi_g(\eta)$, and $\varphi_g(\eta)$ are the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind, which the recurrence relations are given by:

$$\varphi_0(\eta) = 1, \varphi_1(\eta) = \eta, \varphi_{g+1}(\eta) = 2\eta\varphi_g(\eta) - \varphi_{g-1}(\eta), g \ge 1 \quad (4.18)$$

The transformation of coordinates from $\eta$ to $2\eta-1$ is necessary for applying Chebyshev polynomials since Chebyshev polynomials are defined in the interval $[-1, 1]$. Once the energy expressions and admissible functions are obtained, the Ritz method is employed to derive the eigenvalue equation of FG porous shell.

Substituting Eq. (4.16) into Eq. (4.7), the kinetic energy $T$ of FG porous shell can be expressed as:

$$T = \frac{\pi LR}{2} \rho_0^2 \int_{0}^{L} \omega^2 (U^2 + V^2 + W^2) d\eta \quad (4.19)$$

where
$$\rho_0^2 = \int_{-L}^{L} \rho(z) dz \quad (4.20)$$

Similarly, substituting Eq. (4.16) into Eq. (4.8), the strain energy of FG porous shell is calculated by:

$$U_\epsilon = \frac{\pi L}{2} \int_{-L}^{L} \left( n^2 A_{66} U^2 + \frac{R^2 A_{11}}{L^2} V^2 + 2n \left(\frac{RA_{12}}{L} + \frac{B_{12}}{L}\right) U' V' \right)$$
$- 2n \left(\frac{RA_{66}}{L} + \frac{B_{06}}{L}\right) U' V' + 2n \left(\frac{n^2 B_{12}}{L} + \frac{RA_{12}}{L}\right) U' W'$
$+ \frac{4n^2 B_{66}}{L} UW' - \frac{R^2 B_{66}}{L^3} U' V' + n^2 \left(\frac{A_{22}}{R} + \frac{D_{22}}{R^2}\right) V^2$
$+ \left(\frac{D_{66}}{L} + \frac{2RB_{66}}{L^2}\right) V' W' + 4n \left(\frac{RB_{66}}{L^2} + \frac{B_{06}}{L}\right) V' W''$
$- 2n \left(\frac{n^2 D_{12}}{L^2} + \frac{RB_{12}}{L}\right) VW' W'' + 2n \left(\frac{n^2 B_{22}}{R} + \frac{B_{22}}{R^2} + \frac{n^2 D_{22}}{R^2}\right) W' W''$
$+ A_{22} \frac{2n^2 B_{22}}{R} + \frac{n^2 D_{06}}{R^2} W^2 + 4n^2 \frac{D_{06}}{L^2} W^2 - \frac{R^2 D_{11}}{L^4} W''$
$- 2 \left(\frac{n^2 D_{12}}{L^2} + \frac{RB_{12}}{L^2}\right) WW'' d\eta \quad (4.21)$$

Then, substituting Eq. (4.16) into Eq. (4.15), the potential energy of the boundaries can be written as:

$$U_b = \frac{\pi R}{2} \left\{ k_u^2[U(0)]^2 + k_v^2[V(0)]^2 + k_w^2[W(0)]^2 + k_{\theta}^2\left[\frac{W(0)}{L_{\eta}}\right]^2 \right\}$$
$+ \frac{\pi R}{2} \left\{ k_u^2[U(1)]^2 + k_v^2[V(1)]^2 + k_w^2[W(1)]^2 + k_{\theta}^2\left[\frac{W(1)}{L_{\eta}}\right]^2 \right\} \quad (4.22)$$

The Lagrange function of the FG porous cylindrical shell with arbitrary boundary conditions is defined as:

$$\Pi = T - U_c - U_s \quad (4.23)$$

Applying a standard Ritz procedure, minimize $\Pi$ with respect to the unknown coefficients, the expression (4.24) can be obtained:

$$\frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial a_g}=0, \frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial b_g}=0, \frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial c_g}=0, \dots, NT \quad (4.24)$$

So the following eigenvalue equation is generated:

$$\omega^2 \mathbf{M} \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{K} \mathbf{q} = \mathbf{K}_s \quad (4.25)$$

where $\mathbf{q} = [\mathbf{a}_g, \mathbf{b}_g, \mathbf{c}_g]^T$ is the Ritz vector given $\mathbf{a}_g = [a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{NT}]$, $\mathbf{b}_g = [b_1, b_2, \dots, b_{NT}]$, and $\mathbf{c}_g = [c_1, c_2, \dots, c_{NT}]$.

The expression for the mass matrix $\mathbf{M}$ is:

$$\mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} M_{aa} & M_{ab} \\ M_{ac} & M_{bc} \end{bmatrix} \quad (4.26)$$

where
$$M_{aa}(p, q) = \rho_L^{LR} \int_{0}^{1} \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.27)$$
$$M_{ab}(p, q) = \rho_L^{LR} \int_{0}^{1} \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.28)$$
$$M_{bc}(p, q) = \rho_L^{LR} \int_{0}^{1} \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.29)$$

The expression for the stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}$ is:

$$\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} K_{aa} & K_{ab} & K_{ac} \\ K_{ba} & K_{bb} & K_{bc} \\ K_{ca} & K_{cb} & K_{cc} \end{bmatrix} \quad (4.30)$$

where
$$K_{aa}(p, q) = \frac{L}{R} \int_{0}^{L} \left[ n^2 A_{66} - \frac{n^2 B_{66}}{R} + \frac{n^2 D_{66}}{4R^2} \right] \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) + \frac{R^2 A_{11}}{L^2} \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.31)$$
$$K_{ab}(p, q) = \frac{L}{R} \int_{0}^{L} \left( \frac{nRA_{12}}{L} - \frac{nB_{12}}{L} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) + \frac{3nD_{66}}{L} \left( \frac{nRA_{66}}{L} - \frac{nB_{66}}{L} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.32)$$
$$K_{ac}(p, q) = \frac{L}{R} \int_{0}^{L} \left[ \left( \frac{RA_{12}}{L} + \frac{nB_{12}}{L} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) + \left( \frac{n^2 D_{66}}{L} - \frac{2n^2 B_{66}}{L} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) \right] - \frac{R^2 B_{11}}{L^3} \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.33)$$
$$K_{ba}(p, q) = \frac{L}{R} \int_{0}^{L} \left[ \left( \frac{nRA_{12}}{L} + \frac{nB_{12}}{L} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) + \frac{3nD_{66}}{4L} \left( \frac{nRA_{66}}{L} - \frac{nB_{66}}{L} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) \right] d\eta \quad (4.34)$$
$$K_{bb}(p, q) = \frac{L}{R} \int_{0}^{L} \left[ n^2 A_{22} - \frac{2n^2 B_{22}}{R} + \frac{n^2 D_{22}}{R^2} \right] \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) + \frac{R^2 A_{66}}{L^2} + \frac{3RB_{66}}{L} + \frac{9D_{66}}{4L^2} \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.35)$$
$$K_{bc}(p, q) = \frac{L}{R} \int_{0}^{L} \left[ \left( \frac{2nRB_{66}}{L^2} + \frac{3nD_{66}}{L^2} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) - \left( \frac{nRB_{12}}{L^2} + \frac{nD_{12}}{L^2} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) \right] + \left( nA_{22} + \frac{n^2 B_{22}}{R} + \frac{n^2 D_{22}}{R^2} \right) \varphi_p(\eta)\varphi_q(\eta) d\eta \quad (4.36)$$
