The unit normal vector has a zero divergence, and the Gaussian curvature of the surface is defined as:

If the minimal surface is repeated infinitely and periodically in the three independent directions in three-dimensional space, it is then known TPMS.

Several methods were explored to define the minimal surface [35,36]. For instance, the nodal coordinates of TPMS can be generated by integrating the Weierstrass function based on Enneper–Weierstrass representation [37]. Bonatti and Mohr designed shell-based lattice structures with TPMS topology by minimizing the principal curvatures of the fundamental unit as a minimal function [38]. However, the most adopted and simple method to generate the topology of TPMS is the level-approximation equation based on a summation of the Fourier series,

$\Psi(r) = \sum_{k} F(k)\cos[2\pi r - \alpha(k)] = 0. \quad (13.3)$

where $k$ is the reciprocal vector, $\alpha(k)$ is a phase shift, and $F(k)$ is the amplitude for a given vector, $k$ [35]. The series can be truncated to a function $\phi$ which is a combination of trigonometric functions and satisfies $\phi(x,y,z) = c$. If the function $\phi(x,y,z)$ is evaluated at the isosovale $c$, a surface can be generated with a similar topology to minimal surfaces. Many different types of TPMS have been discovered, and a few level-approximation equations defining here:

Primitive:

$\Phi_{Primitive}(x,y,z) \equiv \cos(w_x x) + \cos(w_y y) + \cos(w_z z) = c. \quad (13.4)$

Gyroid:

$\Phi_{Gyroid} \equiv \sin(w_x x)\cos(w_y y) + \sin(w_y y)\cos(w_z z) \n + \sin(w_z z)\cos(w_x x) = c. \quad (13.5)$

IWP:

$\Phi_{IWP}(x,y,z) \equiv \cos(w_x x)\cos(w_y y) + \cos(w_y y)\cos(w_z z) + \cos(w_z z)\cos(w_x x) \n - \cos(2w_x x) + \cos(2w_y y) + \cos(2w_z z) = c. \quad (13.6)$
