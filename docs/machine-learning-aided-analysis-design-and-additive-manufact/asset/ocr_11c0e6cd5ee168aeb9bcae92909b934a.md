After the formwork is printed, the concrete material with high flowability is fed into the formwork for casting purposes. In some cases, prior to the concrete casting reinforcements are placed into the printed formwork to enhance the structural durability of the concrete. The concrete structure is considered digitally fabricated once the concrete material in the printed formwork gets hardened. In some cases, the printed formwork can be removed (demolished) from the cast structure (inert temporary formwork), while in other cases the formwork is not taken away from the cast structure but as part of the integral concrete structure (integrated in-place formwork).

Here the *Eggshell* method developed by ETH Zurich is briefly introduced as an example to demonstrate the ideas behind the Formwork-based Concrete Fabrication. The Eggshell primarily comprises two stages: (1) 3D printing of shell with thermoplastic materials (such as polyethylene terephthalate glycol) to generate the formwork with complex geometry; (2) simultaneously filling the formwork with fast-setting concrete during the formwork process to promote the structure fabrication efficiency (simultaneous process), or feeding the finished plastic shell with set-on-demand concrete for casting (consecutive fabrication process) [74].
