Table 8.2 reveals that the present theory is with good agreement compared to the results obtained by Li et al. [44] and confirm the validation of the proposed method. It can also be seen that the CBT model, which omits shear deformation underestimates and stresses of the thick FG beams.
The impact of the material index (p) and slenderness ratio (L/h) on transverse $ar{W}$ and axial $ar{U}$ displacements, axial $\sigma_{z}$ and $\sigma_{	heta}$, stresses of S-FG beam under uniform load are identical in Tables 8.2 and 8.3. In general, shear deformation models give identical results, for the case of transverse shear $\sigma_{rc}$. It can be explained by the different shear shape functions $g(z)$ used in each model. It can be seen that both displacements and axial stress of S-FG beams increase with increasing the power-law exponent (p). This is due to the fact that higher value of (p) correspond to high proportion of metal part, thus makes FG beams more flexible. In fact, when $p=0$, beam is made from fully ceramic and has the smallest displacements.
