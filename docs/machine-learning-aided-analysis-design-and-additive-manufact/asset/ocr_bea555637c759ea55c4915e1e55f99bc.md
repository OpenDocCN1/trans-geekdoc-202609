# 10.2.1 Representative volume elements for finite element
homogenization

Homogenization is a commonly adopted approach for multiscale modeling, assum-
ing that the overall mechanical constants of a small representative material region,
that, RVEs, are equivalent to those of anywhere else [28–30]. Thus regardless of
the irregularity in microscopic/mesoscopic views, the general material form may be
regarded as homogenized and shares the features of RVEs. In this study, multiscale
modeling is employed to investigate the Young’s modulus of closed-cell metal
foams that is adopted in the porous structural analysis. Using Voronoi tessellation
where the seed positions are generated using the standard uniform distribution, 2D
square RVEs can be constructed by stochastic porosities via commercial soft-
ware MATLAB (version: R2021a) and ANSYS/APDL (version: 2020 R2), illustrated in Fig. 10.1 for a typical example. Note that the edge is designed
for the implementation of periodic boundary conditions (PBCs) requiring the corre-
snode pairs on the opposite edges to move equivalently in both horizontal
vertical directions, the internal cell wall morphologies and thicknesses jointly
determine the RVE stiffness, while the influence of regularity in edges may be
ignored.
