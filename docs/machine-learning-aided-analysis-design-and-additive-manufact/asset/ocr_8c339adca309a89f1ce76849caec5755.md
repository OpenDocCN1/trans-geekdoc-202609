After that, the numerical investigation was carried out on the performance of porous concrete structures with various hollow shapes (square, circle, triangle, and rhombus) up to order of two. The results demonstrated in the case of the first-order fractal the maximum compressive strength went to the square-shape structure, in contrast to the triangle-shape structure with the lowest. Regarding the similar structure in the second order, although the square-shaped structure still had the maximum strength, its load-bearing capacity did not show great discrepancy with the fractal structure with other hollow structures. To sum up, Wang et al. [11] concluded that the square-shaped MS hollow concrete structure at the second order would be an ideal option for practical engineering applications due to its reasonably low fraction and adequate load-bearing capacity against compressive load.

# 13.5 Potential applications

This section discusses and explores the potential applications of lightweight concrete structures digitally fabricated in the context of 3D printing.

The first section is the protective concrete armor units deployed in coastal engineering. For the protection of coastal structures such as breakwater, these concrete armor units are normally fabricated in precast form with plain concrete, and a wide variety of armor units with various geometries have been designed and invented over the years, including solid units like a tripod, hexapod, tetrahedron, and dolos, [102]. The previous study shows that concrete armor units can be subject to three types of loading conditions [103], including (1) static load originating from the self-weight of armor units and the surrounding armor units resting on them; (2) dynamic pulsating load causing erosion on the marine structure; and (3) dynamic impact due to insensitive rigid body rotation of armor units leading to unit–unit impact. Notably, the dolos armor unit has been extensively applied for marine protection, and its mechanical performance under both static and dynamic loading has been well investigated [104–106]. In this case, the framework-based 3D-printed lightweight cellular structures were proposed by Nguyen-Van et al. [6], namely TPS structure (primitive and gyroid), could be a potential candidate for the application of protective concrete armor units, due to its adequate mechanical performance in terms of static compressive loading and impulse resistance.
