The thickness-stretching impact was introduced in analyzing the vibrational buckling of thick FG beams [38]. Osofero et al. [39] introduced an analysis method of buckling in FG sandwich beams, considering thickness-stretching and shear effects. Merazdeh et al. [40] also introduced the thickness-stretching effects in a new shear theory to analyze the bending vibration of FG beams.

From the literature review, it is evident that there is no published work considering the impact of thickness stretching on the mechanical vibration of thick FG beams. This problem is not well investigated and there is a need for further studies. In this work, a refined theory is presented to analyze the bending vibration of the thick FG beam, with supported ends and under loading condition. This although provides a constant transverse displacement and avoids axial displacement through the depth of the beam so that there is no need for any shear correction factors. By supervising the deflection on the bending, shear and thickness-stretching factors, the governing equations are derived from Hamilton's principle. The equations system obtained is presumed to change through the thick beam following the law of dosing. Detailed mathematical formulations are provided, and example results are proposed to show the relevance of the present theory and to verify its accuracy. Our approach presents the advantage of using less variables in comparison to other theories as well as the effects of thickness stretching and the influences of many parameters such as material index and slenderness ratio on frequency response and stresses. The proposed higher-order normal deformation and shear theory is not only accurate, but provides an elegant and easy-to-implement approach to simulating the bending and vibration behaviors of thick FG beams.
