B1 is presented, since it is observed in Fig. 6.6 that porosity type B1 has better vibration response in comparison to porosity types B3 and U1. As it can be seen from Fig. 6.7, the higher order porosity distributions can enforce forced vibration of porous beams in comparison to the conventional porosity distribution. For instance, at constant amplitude of vibration ($A_{max}$), the excitation frequency for the third-order porosity distribution ($n=3$) is around 50% higher than the conventional porosity distribution ($n=1$). Therefore to decrease the amplitude of vibration, higher order porosity distributions are more beneficial than the conventional porosity distribution.

Fig. 6.8 illustrates the effect of the total porosity volume fraction ($\alpha$) on the frequency response curve of the porous beams with porosity B1. As it is assumed the porous beam is free of axial load. Based on the presented figure, at constant excitation frequency, amplitude of vibration of the beam is decreased by increasing the total porosity volume fraction ($\alpha$). In other words, by changing the amount of porosity in the porous beam, vibration amplitude can be decreased. The improvement can be considerable, for instance, to reach constant amplitude of vibration ($A_{max}=2$); the required excitation frequency for beam with 60% porosity is 1.2 times the excitation frequency for beam with 40% porosity.
