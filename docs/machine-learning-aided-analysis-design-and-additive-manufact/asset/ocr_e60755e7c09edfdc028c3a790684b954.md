# 16.1 Introduction

Despite researchers have made significant progress in investigating energy-absorbing, impact loading is inevitable in transportation engineering, causing accidents that severely endanger human life. Generally, reducing the damage of impact load can be achieved in two ways, including reducing the peak impact load [1–4], and prolonging the duration, by using the energy-absorbing component the thin-walled tube [5–9]. For example, as a typical energy-absorbing component while absorbing the impact energy below a lower load threshold. This is accomplished by the plastic deformation of the thin-walled tube: collapsing, folding and buckling; Consequently, thin-walled tubes have been widely applied to highway traffic safety, anti-bird impact on aircraft, astronaut recovery, and penetration of projectiles [10–16].

In the past decades, the crashing behaviors of thin-walled tubes have been extensively investigated [17–23]. Andrews et al. [17] proposed a classification chart to predict the collapse of thin-walled circular tubes using geometry and material parameters. Different deformation methods including concertina, diamond, and and Euler buckling have been reported, respectively. Furthermore, the deformation of min-walled tubes can also be affected by the infiltrated material.
