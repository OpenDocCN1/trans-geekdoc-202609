However, the aforementioned methods achieve the continuous variation of foam
density in a monotonic or uncontrollable way.

The graded foams with nonmonotonic and controllable gradients can be obtained
by finite element simulation [22,23] and theoretical models [24,25]. Cui et al. [26]
studied the energy absorption of graded foams using the finite element method. Chen et al. [27] investigated the gradient effect on the compressive
property, deformation pattern as well as energy absorption of graded foams
Fo own et al. [28] found that the single shock mode was observed in foams
with a positive gradient while there was a double shock mode in negatively graded
foams. Yang et al. [29] and Ding et al. [30] optimized the crashworthiness of
graded foams. Due to the difficulty in fabricating graded foams with continuous and controllable gradients, these numerical models can hardly be verified.
Furthermore, in general, accurate models from experimental data need to be established

It is worth noting that graded foams have great potential in energy absorption.
to the best of the authors’ knowledge, there is a limited experimental study
of continuously graded foams with controllable gradients. The objective of
this chapter is to conduct testing and investigate the mechanisms of
continuously graded foams using recently emerging fabrication methods, such as
additive manufacturing techniques [31–35]. Among many additive manufacturing
with desired complex structures, the
fused filament fabrication technique enables the construction of enclosed
space models [36,37] and is chosen to produce foam specimens. Uniform foams and
g foams are both produced and tested. A systematic comparison of the mechani-
biors between graded foams and uniform foams is performed. We
stablish an elastic, plastic-hardening model for graded foams based on the experi-
mental data and stress-strain relationship of analogous uniform foams. The predictive
models proposed in this work can be used to predict the stress-strain curves of vari-
graded foams in engineering applications.

The outline of this chapter is organized as follows. Section 15.2 introduces the
production of Voronoi foams as well as the experimental settings. Then, quasi-static dynamic experimental results are presented in Sections 15.3 and 15.4,
respectively, the compressive response and deformation mechanism of graded
foams are both discussed in these sections. In Section 15.5, predictive models
of stress–strain relations of graded foams are proposed and validated. Finally, the
chapter is concluded in Section 15.6.
