**Figure 11.1** Functionally graded porous plate with porosity distribution 1: (A) and distribution 2: (B) [5].

```
$$\begin{aligned} E(z) &= E_m - e_0\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right) + \frac{\pi}{4}\\ G(z) &= G_m - e_0\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right) + \frac{\pi}{4}\\ \rho(z) &= \rho_m - e_m\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right) + \frac{\pi}{4}\end{aligned}$ \quad (11.2)$
```

in $E_m, \rho_m,$ and $E_c, G_c, \rho_c$ are the values of Young's modulus, shear modulus, and mass density of ceramic material; $e_0$ is the porosity coefficient which is related as expressed as $e_0 = 1 - E_m/E_c = 1 - G_m/G_c, 0 < e_0 < 1,$ $e_m$ is the porosity coefficient which is associated with the mass density as $e_m = 1 - \rho_m/\rho_c, 0 < e_m < 1.$ The relationship between Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio can be expressed as $G = E/2(1+\nu)$

According to Eqs. (11.1) and (11.2), it can be easily seen that the maximum value of material properties for distribution 1 is the top and bottom surfaces of the plate, and the mid-plane surface contains the minimum material properties. For distribution 2, it is noticeable that the maximum and minimum value of material properties can be found at the top and bottom surfaces of the plate. Considering the general connection of mechanical properties, the relationship can be shown as:

```
$E_c = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_c}\right)^2$ \quad (11.3)$
```
