## 16.2 Materials and methods

### 16.2.1 Experimental set-up

Quasi-static compression tests are conducted at an initial strain rate of $10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$. The tests are carried out according to ASTM D1621-16 using a universal testing machine. The specimen is compressed between two cellular plates. The applied load $F$ and the deformation $\Delta$ of the specimen are measured by the load cell and optical extensometer, respectively. A digital camera is employed to capture the global deformation during the tests. The image resolution and acquisition frequency is $1280 \times 800$ pixels and 50 fps, respectively.

### 16.2.2 Construction of the mesoscopic geometric model

Foam materials have pores inside a solid base material, which is similar to the generation process of the Voronoi model. Hence, the Voronoi model [50] is employed in this chapter to construct the mesoscopic geometric model for the numerical simulation of foams. The generation process of Voronoi foams can be divided into three steps. First, a three-dimensional space is created. Second, a set of nuclei is inserted into this space according to Voronoi tessellation. Specifically, a subsequent nucleus is accepted only if its distance to existing nuclei is greater than $\delta$. Finally, all nuclei grow isotropically until they touch each other to form a Voronoi foam. Foams with different cell sizes can be controlled by giving different $\delta$, resulting in different densities. The relative density $\tilde{\rho}$ is defined as the ratio between foam density $\rho$ and the density of material $\rho_{m}$. Foams with gradient density configuration can be achieved by employing cell-distribution of gradient along the axial direction. For comparison, uniform foams (UF) with uniformly distributed density are also considered. The wall thicknesses of all foams and the wall thickness of the tube are both around 0.4 mm (0.4 mm of the 3D printer). It is noted that when the cell wall thickness is to a multiple of the print nozzle diameter, the 3D printer can produce more accurate foams. The relative densities of all specimens are calculated and listed in Table 16.1. The number in the first column represents the cell size of foams. For example, "UF-12" means the specimen with a uniform density distribution and "GF-612" represents the specimen with a gradient density distribution and its cell size grows up linearly from 6 to 12 mm along the axial direction. The diameter and length of all foams are 60 and 90 mm, respectively. It also corresponds to the size of a thin-walled tube, where the internal diameter and length are 60 and 90 mm, respectively. It indicates that no gap exists between the foam and the inner wall of the thin-walled tube. The external diameter of the tube is 60.8 mm. To make the interaction between the foam and the thin-walled more obvious, this chapter takes the FFT as an integrated identity (instead of two parts assembled) for additive manufacturing. For sake of convenience, uniform foam-filled tubes and graded-foam-filled tubes are abbreviated in the next sections as UFTs and FFTs, respectively. The mesoscopic geometric orders of foams are shown in Fig. 16.1.

### 16.2.3 Specimen preparation

The constructed foams or FFTs are sliced and imported into a 3D printer for manufacturing. One of AM techniques, the fused filament fabrication, is used because of its capability to produce structures with enclosed space. All specimens are manufactured by an industrial-scale 3D printer (Pro3 Plus, Raise 3D Innovations, Shanghai) with dual brass nozzles. For sake of reproducibility, the print speed and the layer height are optimized to be 30 mm and 0.1 mm, respectively. The bed temperature and nozzle temperature are 30°C and 220°C, respectively. The printing pattern is set to rectilinear and the filament has a diameter of 1.75 mm. The base material of the specimen is poly lactic acid (PLA) named “PolyMax™” which has a density of about 1200 kg/m³.

### 16.2.4 Set-up of the finite element simulation

The numerical study is conducted to simulate the interaction between the cell-wall structure of the foam and the thin-walled section of the tube, to reveal the internal deformation mechanism of FFTs that cannot be monitored in experimental studies. To calculate the large deformation and elastic-plastic material behavior of the foam, Abaqus package and explicit time integration are employed to simulate the compressive behavior of foam and FFTs. The mesoscopic models are sandwiched between two rigid plates. The bottom plate is fixed in all degrees of freedom, while a constant velocity along the y-direction is imposed on the top plate with the rest of the degrees of freedom fixed. A velocity of 0.1 m/s is employed to approximately provide a quasi-static loading and balance the computational efficiency. The cell wall material of numerical models possesses the elastic modulus, Poisson's ratio, and density of 1511 MPa, 0.3 and 1130 kg/m³, respectively.

### 16.2.5 Reproducibility of experiment and validation of simulation

UFFT-9 is chosen here to check the reproducibility. As shown in Fig. 16.3B, a mesh sensitivity analysis on UFFT-9 is conducted with increasing resolution 2.0, 1.0, 0.75, and 0.5 mm. The appropriate element size is set to 0.5 mm to achieve good mesh density.

To verify the reliability of numerical models, the force–displacement curves calculated from the numerical model are compared with experimental results in Fig. 16.4A. The simulation results match well with the experimental data. However, the numerical strengths are slightly higher than the experimental strengths because of two reasons. Firstly, no material failure is considered in simulations. Secondly, the relative density of relatively manufactured specimens used in experiments is slightly lower than the predetermined value. The numerical deformation distributions (ε = 0.4) are compared with experimental results in Fig. 16.4B, where good agreement is observed.
