## 7.1 Introduction

Functionally graded porous nanocomposites are a new class of composites by mixing carbon nanofillers, such as carbon nanotubes, fibers, and graphene platelets (GPLs), with open-cell or closed-cell foams in a specific distribution [1]. Compared to traditional materials, the nano-reinforced porous composites present high strength to weight ratio, outstanding energy absorbing capacity, and advantageous physical properties, especially for GPL-based nanocomposites with higher surface area and good dispersion ability [2]. The great potential of functionally graded graphene reinforced (FGGR) polymers in aerospace, civil, biomedical, defense, and automotive has attracted large numbers of research efforts from both academic and industrial sectors.

The mechanical characteristics and structural behavior of functionally graded (FG) porous structures (such as beams [3], plates [4], shells [4], and arches [5]) have been comprehensively investigated on bending, buckling, vibration, and dynamic analysis. Chen et al. [6] studied the free and forced vibration of FG porous beams and found symmetric FG porosity distribution obtains best mechanical performance. The nonlinear dynamic analysis of imperfect FG metal beams was conducted by Gao et al. [7]. Chen et al. [8] also presented and bending analysis of FG porous plate using Chebyshev-Ritz method. Zhao et al. [9] analyzed free vibrations of FG porous rectangular plates with uniform elastic boundary conditions. Gao et al. [10] proposed an analytical method for the primary resonance analysis of cylindrical shells of FG porous materials subjected to a uniformly distributed harmonic load. The free vibration of FG cylinders was performed [11] using finite element methodology. As the pioneers, Kitipornchai and his colleagues [12] proposed the FGGR polymers, which inject new energies into porous materials and boost the development of nano-reinforced materials. Chen et al. [13] applied the Ritz method in the analysis of nonlinear vibration and postbuckling of FG porous nanocomposite beams with different porosity and reinforcement distributions, Yang et al. [14] examined the uniaxial, biaial, and shear buckling loads of FG GPLs reinforced plates within the framework of first-order shear deformation.

## 7.2 Material properties of functionally graded reinforced beams

As shown in Fig. 7.1, the beam is made of FGGR material with a length L, width h, and thickness h, respectively. Four types of porosity distributions T1, T2, T3, and T4 are reinforced by the other four GPL patterns, named P1, P2, P3, and P4, and 16 scenarios in total.

The distribution functions of FGGR beams are given in Eq. (7.1) for Young’s modulus $E(z)$, shear modulus $G(z)$, and mass density $\rho(z)$:

$E(z) = E_{max}[1 - e_E\psi(z)]$
$G(z) = G_{max}[1 - e_G\psi(z)]$
$\rho(z) = \rho_{max}[1 - e_\rho\psi(z)]$ (7.1)

where

$\varphi(z) = \begin{array}{cc}\cos^2\frac{\pi z}{h} & T1\\cos^2\frac{\pi z}{2h}-\frac{\pi}{4} & T2\\sin^2\frac{\pi z}{h} & T3\\ & T4\end{array}$ (7.2)

$\alpha$ is the random power-law factor that considers the source of uncertainties in porosity distributions and GPLs reinforcement patterns, as shown in Fig. 7.2. When $\alpha=1$, the model reduces to the traditional ones. The shear modulus of the beam is $G(z)=E(z)[2(1+\nu(z))]$, and $\nu(z)$ is the Poisson’s ratio from closed-cell Gaussian Random field theory, which is expressed as:

$\nu(z) = 0.221[1-\frac{\rho(z)}{\rho_{max}}] + \nu_{max}[(1-\frac{\rho(z)}{\rho_{max}})^2+1.21\frac{\rho(z)}{\rho_{max}}-0.21]$ (7.3)

and $v_{max}$ is effective Poisson’s ratio of the porous nanocomposite beams.
