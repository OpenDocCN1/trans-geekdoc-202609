#  14.3.1 Open-cell foams

The relative density of open-cell foams [44–46] is generally lower than that of closed-cell foams. Due to the lack of support from cell-wall, the ability of impact resistance and energy absorption of open-cell foams is generally weaker than that of closed-cell foams. However, open-cell foam has unique advantages in noise reduction and biological applicability, this has been widely considered by different research groups [47–52].

Nava et al. [44] filled spheres in a cylindrical container to produce open-cell foams, represented by the area spheres. They reversely engineered the 3D model by inverting X-ray computed tomography. By adjusting the size of the gate, open-cell foams with different porosities could be produced. Finally, metal additive manufacturing was used to prepare the open-cell foam according to the predictions to obtain the final specimens with different porosities (as shown in Fig. 14.9). The authors carried out quasi-static compression tests on these specimens. The relationships between elastic modulus and yield strength with relative density were achieved, and the corresponding Gibson-Ashby relations were fitted.
