# 5.1 Introduction

In fields such as aerospace and mechanical engineering, some structures are composed of thin-walled open cylindrical shells, spherical shells, hyperbolic parabolic shells, etc. These structures are characterized by a small radius of curvature relative to the size of the other parameters. It is customary to call the above structures shells reign supreme because of their excellent load-carrying capacity. As a result of the extremely harsh working conditions of these structures, it is of great significance to carry out comprehensive and profound analysis for structural design and failure assessment. In the last years, functionally graded porous (FGP) materials have been widely used in various industrial fields because they not only possess the excellent properties of functionally graded materials but also have superior mechanical properties, which are achieved through appropriate boundary distribution [1]. The aim of this chapter is to develop a unified analytical model for the functionally graded porous opening shell constrained by arbitrary boundary conditions and on an elastic foundation

In the past years, a considerable effort was directed towards the dynamic characteristic analysis of FGM structures. While the investigation of FGM structures, complex structures situated on elastic foundations such as shells, was relatively rare [2–5]. Most of the objects are simple structures such as beams, plates, and cylindrical shells. The research of FG shells was mostly limited to the free vibration of homogeneous isotropic or FGM structures. Najafizadeh and Isvandaei [5] studied the vibration of FG cylindrical shells with support rings arranged at arbitrary positions on the shell's axial direction. Shah et al. [6] investigated the vibration characteristics of thin-walled FGM cylindrical shells under the framework of Love's shell theory, in which the displacement was exponentially distributed along the radial direction. Ng et al. [7] proposed the motion equations for FG shells subjected to the harmonic loading and analyzed the dynamic stability by Bolin's method. Yan et al. [8] studied the effects of boundary conditions, geometric parameters, and porosity distribution of the response of FGP shells grounded on Donnell's shell theory in connection with the Galerkin method. Santos et al. [9] investigated the vibration characteristics of FG cylindrical shells by a semianytical method. Gao et al. [10] employed the analytical method to analyze the resonance of the FG cylindrical shell subjected to simple harmonic loads. Wan and Wu [11] studied the effects of pore coefficient, on the free vibration of FG cylindrical shells on a sinusoidal shell deformation theory. Li et al. [12] proposed a new semianytical method to analyze the vibration of FG cylindrical shells constrained by arbitrary boundary conditions using Jacobi polynomials, Fourier series as well as the Rayleigh–Ritz method. Zhao et al. [13] also modeled the dynamics by an energy approach and then analyzed the vibration of the FGP cylindrical shell. Artificial springs was used to model arbitrary boundary conditions and Fourier series with auxiliary functions was employed to express the displacement field. A semianality method was employed by them to analyze the vibration behavior of FGP doubly curved panels.
