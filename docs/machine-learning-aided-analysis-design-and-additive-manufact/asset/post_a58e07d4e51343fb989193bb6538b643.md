> $T_0(\eta)=1, T_1(\eta)=\eta, T_n(\eta)=2\eta T_{n-1}(\eta)-T_{n-2}(\eta), g \ge 1$

The expressions of generalized coordinates and modal vectors are:

> $q_w = [a_{10}, a_{11}, ..., a_{1G}, a_{N0}, a_{N1}, ..., a_{NG1}]^T \times e^{i\omega_wt}$

> $q_v = [b_{10}, b_{11}, ..., b_{1G}, b_{N0}, b_{N1}, ..., b_{NG1}]^T \times e^{i\omega_vt}$

> $q_u = [c_{10}, c_{11}, ..., c_{1G}, c_{N0}, c_{N1}, ..., c_{NG1}]^T \times e^{i\omega_ut}$

> $U = [T_0(\eta)\cos(\theta), ..., T_G(\eta)\cos(\theta), T_1(\eta)\cos(\theta), ..., T_G(\eta)\cos(\theta)]^T$

$V = [T_0(\eta)\sin(\theta)]$

$W = [T_0(\eta)\cos(\theta), \dots, T_G(\eta)\cos(\theta), \dots, T_1(\eta)\cos(N), \dots, T_G(\eta)\cos(N)]^T \quad (4.55)$

$J = N(G+1)$.
The Lagrange equations of the FG porous cylindrical shell can be expressed as:

$\frac{d}{dr}\frac{\partial \tilde{T}}{\partial \tilde{q}_u} +\frac{\partial (U_z + U_n)}{\partial \tilde{q}_u}}=f_u$

$\frac{d}{dr}\frac{\partial \tilde{T}}{\partial \tilde{q}_v} +\frac{\partial (U_z + U_n)}{\partial \tilde{q}_v}}=f_v \quad (4.56)$

$\frac{d}{dr}\frac{\partial \tilde{T}}{\partial \tilde{q}_w} +\frac{\partial (U_z + U_n)}{\partial \tilde{q}_w}}=f_w$

$f_u, f_v$, and $f_w$ are the vectors of external force in the axial, circumferential, and radial directions, respectively.
Substituting Eqs. (4.7) and (4.15) into Eq. (4.56), the governing equation of the FG porous cylindrical shell can be derived as:

$M\dot{q} + C\dot{q} + (K+K_s)q=F(t) \quad (4.57)$

with

$q = [q_u, q_v, q_w]^T \quad (4.58)$

in which $M, C, K$, and $K_s$ are the mass matrix, the damping matrix, the structure stiffness matrix, and the springs stiffness matrix, respectively.
The mass matrix $M$ is expressed as:

$M = \begin{bmatrix} M_1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & M_2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & M_3 \end{bmatrix} \quad (4.59)$

with

$M_1 = \rho_z^2 LR \int_0^{2\pi} UU^T d\eta \quad (4.60)$

$M_2 = \rho^b_{LR} \int_0^{2\pi} \mathbf{VV}^T d\theta\eta \quad (4.61)$

$M_3 = \rho^b_{LR} \int_0^{2\pi} \mathbf{WW}^T d\theta\eta \quad (4.62)$

The stiffness matrix $K$ is expressed as:

$K = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2}K_{11} & \frac{1}{2}K_{12} & \frac{1}{2}K_{13} \\ \frac{1}{2}K_{21} & \frac{1}{2}K_{22} & \frac{1}{2}K_{23} \\ \frac{1}{2}K_{31} & \frac{1}{2}K_{32} & \frac{1}{2}K_{33} \end{bmatrix} \quad (4.63)$

where

$K_{11} = \frac{L}{R} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( \frac{L}{A_{66}}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} + \frac{R^2 A_{11}}{L^2}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} + \frac{D_{66}}{R^2}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} \right) d\theta\eta \quad (4.64)$

$K_{12} = \frac{L}{R} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( \frac{2RA_{12}}{L}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} + \frac{2RA_{66}}{L}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} - \frac{3D_{66}}{2R}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} \right) d\theta\eta \quad (4.65)$

$K_{13} = \frac{L}{R} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( \frac{2RA_{12}}{L}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} + \frac{2D_{66}}{LR}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta} \right) d\theta\eta \quad (4.66)$

$K_{22} = \frac{L}{R} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( A_{22} + \frac{D_{22}}{R^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} + \frac{R^2 A_{66}}{L^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} + \frac{D_{66}}{4L^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} \right) d\theta\eta \quad (4.67)$

$K_{23} = \frac{L}{R} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( \frac{2A_{22}}{L}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\theta} - \frac{2D_{22}}{R^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} + \frac{6D_{66}}{L^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} - \frac{2D_{12}}{L^2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\theta} \right) d\theta\eta \quad (4.68)$

$K_{33} = \frac{L}{R} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( A_{22}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^T + \frac{D_{22}}{R^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} + \frac{R^2 A_{11}}{L^5}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} + \frac{4D_{60}}{L^2}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta} \right) d\theta\eta \quad (4.69)$

Free and forced vibrations of functionally graded porous circular cylindrical shells

The springs stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_s$ is expressed as:

$\mathbf{K}_s = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{K}_{s11} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \mathbf{K}_{s22} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \mathbf{K}_{s33} \end{bmatrix} \quad (4.70)$

where

$\mathbf{K}_{s11} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} (k_1^0U(0)T(0) + k_1^1U(1)T(1))R d\theta \quad (4.71)$

$\mathbf{K}_{s22} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} (k_2^0V(0)V^T(0) + k_2^1V(1)V^T(1))R d\theta \quad (4.72)$

$\mathbf{K}_{s33} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} [k_3^0W(0)W^T(0) + k_3^1W(1)W^T(1) + \frac{k_3^0}{L_2^2}\frac{\partial W(0)}{\partial\eta}\frac{\partial W(0)}{\partial\eta} + \frac{k_3^1}{L_2^2}\frac{\partial W(1)}{\partial\eta}\frac{\partial W(1)}{\partial\eta}] R d\theta \quad (4.73)$

$\mathbf{C} = \alpha\mathbf{M} + \beta\mathbf{K} \quad (4.74)$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ represent damping coefficients and are given by:

$\alpha = 2 \left( \frac{\xi_u}{\omega_u} - \frac{\xi_v}{\omega_v} \right) \left( \frac{1}{\omega_u^2} - \frac{1}{\omega_v^2} \right) \quad (4.75)$

$\beta = 2 \left( \frac{\xi_u}{\omega_u} - \frac{\xi_v}{\omega_v} \right) / \left( \frac{1}{\omega_u^2} - \frac{1}{\omega_v^2} \right)$

in which $\omega_u, \omega_v$ are the first- and second-order frequencies; $\xi_u, \xi_v$ are the corresponding damping ratios. The single-point harmonic excitation acting on $(\eta_0, \theta_0)$, is considered in this section, which can be expressed as:

$F(t) = [f_u, f_v, f_w] = [0, 0, f_{ex}\cos(\omega_{ex}t)]^T_{\eta_0, \theta_0} \quad (4.76)$

where $f_{ex}$ is the excitation amplitude and $\omega_{ex}$ is the excitation frequency.
By Solving Eq. (4.57), the dynamic response of FG porous shell can be obtained. Compared with the transient response, which tends to dampen quickly due to the existence of damping, the steady-state response analysis is more important, especially in fatigue design. In this section, the steady-state frequency response is conducted

### 4.3.2 Solution procedure

The frequency response function of the FG cylindrical shell is given by:

$\mathbf{H} = [(\mathbf{K} + \mathbf{K}_b) + i\omega\mathbf{C} - \omega^2\mathbf{M}]^{-1} \quad (4.77)$

According to the orthogonality of modes, Eq. (4.78) can be obtained:

$\mathbf{Q}^T\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{I}_{3(G+1) \times 3(G+1)}$

$\mathbf{Q}^T(\mathbf{K} + \mathbf{K}_b)\mathbf{Q} = \text{diag}(\omega_1^2, \omega_2^2, \dots, \omega_{3N(G+1)}^2) \quad (4.78)$

$\mathbf{Q}^T\mathbf{C} = \text{diag}(\alpha_1 + \beta_1\omega_1^2, \dots, \alpha_{3N(G+1)} + \beta_{3N(G+1)}\omega_{3N(G+1)}^2)$

where $I$ is the identity diagonal matrix, $\omega_1, \omega_2, \dots, \omega_{3N(G+1)}$ represent the circular frequencies of different orders, $Q$ is the mode vector of the system is expressed as:

$\mathbf{Q} = [q_1; \dots; q_{3N(G+1)}] \quad (4.79)$

Eq. (4.77) can be decoupled using Eq. (4.78), so the frequency response matrix of displacement is obtained:

$\mathbf{H}_{ij}(\omega) = \sum_{n=1}^{NT} \frac{q_{in}q_{jn}}{\omega_n^2 + (\alpha_n + \beta_n\omega_n^2)\omega^2} \quad (4.80)$

where $NT$ represents the truncated number of modes in practical calculation.
fact, the mode vectors $q_i$ are unknown coefficients of the displacement functions [see Eq. (4.48)], which can be expressed as follows:

$\mathbf{q}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \alpha_{i1}^a & \alpha_{i2}^a & \dots & \alpha_{iNG}^a \\ \alpha_{i1}^b & \alpha_{i2}^b & \dots & \alpha_{iNG}^b \\ \alpha_{i1}^c & \alpha_{i2}^c & \dots & \alpha_{iNG}^c \\ b_{i1}^a & b_{i2}^a & \dots & b_{iNG}^a \\ b_{i1}^b & b_{i2}^b & \dots & b_{iNG}^b \\ b_{i1}^c & b_{i2}^c & \dots & b_{iNG}^c \end{bmatrix}^T \quad (4.81)$

It is significant to note that the frequency response function between the excitation $p$ and the response $q$ of an arbitrary point, which is an element of the frequency response matrix in Eq. (4.80). Generally, the radial response of the FG cylindrical shell is of interest with the other directions. The mode vectors constructing the excitation point and the response point are based on radial displacement functions, which are expressed as follows:

$\q_i^a = \sum_{e=0}^G T_{ie}^a(r_p)\cos(\theta_e), \sum_{e=0}^G T_{ie}^a(r_p)\cos(2\theta_e), \dots, \sum_{e=0}^G T_{ie}^a(r_p)\cos(G\theta_e)$

$\q_i^b = \sum_{e=0}^G T_{ie}^b(r_p)\cos(\theta_e), \sum_{e=0}^G T_{ie}^b(r_p)\cos(2\theta_e), \dots, \sum_{e=0}^G T_{ie}^b(r_p)\cos(G\theta_e) \quad (4.82)$
