# 14.3.3 Honeycomb architectures

Honeycomb [59,60] has low density, low cost, and a large deformation range. It is usually used as a sandwich material and has been widely employed in light energy-absorbing structures [61,62].

Duan et al. [59] conducted a compression-shear test on honeycombs and Kelvin foams to compare their yielded elbows. The honeycomb and foam specimens with a similar relative density were fabricated using the additive manufacturing technique. Results revealed that the normal strengths of both honeycomb and Kelvin foam decreased when the shear strengths increased with the loading angle increasing. Fig. 14.15, honeycombs changed the deformation mode from the progressive folding mode to the global rotation mode, while Kelvin foams maintained the progressive folding mode as the loading angle increased.
