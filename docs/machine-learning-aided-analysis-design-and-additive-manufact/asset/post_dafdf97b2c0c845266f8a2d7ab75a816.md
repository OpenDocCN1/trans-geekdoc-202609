## 14.2 Additive manufacturing

### 14.2.1 Powder bed fusion

The powder bed fusion (PBF) uses a laser beam to melt the powder. There are two typical technologies of PBF: selective laser sintering (SLS) and selective laser melting (SLM). As shown in Fig. 14.5, the system consists of powder bed, powder supply, roller, laser source, and scanner. The energy source is used to melt the current layer. Afterward, the roller covers the next layer of powder. The entire process is repeated until the 3D model is manufactured. SLS is a variant of SLS, which ensures higher density. SLS applies to wider range of materials including polymer, metal. However, SLM can only be used for specific metals, such as aluminum and steel. The wider range of materials for PBF is discussed in Section 14.3.

### 14.2.2 Material extrusion

Material extrusion firstly squeezes base material from a nozzle and forms the finished specimen. Fused filament fabrication (FFF) is the most widely used manufacturing technique in material extrusion. As shown in Fig. 14.6, the system consists of filament, drive wheel, extrusion nozzle, print bed, and fabrication platform. Through driving filament based on the material spool, the filament first passes through the drive wheel. It is then heated into a molten fluid and extruded by the extrusion nozzle. Furthermore, the extension nozzle moves in 2D space to complete the manufacturing of the current layer. After that, the fabrication platform keeps moving down to the next layer, and the extension nozzle continues to manufacture the structure in this layer. The entire process is repeated until the specimen is manufactured. FFF has many advantages including high material utilization, simple process, low cost, and a wide range of materials including thermoplastics and low melting point metals. However, due to the shortcomings like surface roughness and poor manufacturing accuracy, FFF is mostly used for the verification of conceptual models or prototype models.

Direct ink writing (DIW) is another technique of material extrusion. From Fig. 14.7, materials such as liquid, paste, and hydrogel are contained in the syringe. The syringe is pressurized by pneumatic pressure or a stepper motor. The extruded material is heated or induced by ultraviolet light, and the curing reaction occurs. The syringe can move in 3D space to manufacture 3D models. DIW is fast, efficient, and flexible, which allows the design and manufacture of complex structures. This technique is used in the additive manufacturing of ceramic materials.
