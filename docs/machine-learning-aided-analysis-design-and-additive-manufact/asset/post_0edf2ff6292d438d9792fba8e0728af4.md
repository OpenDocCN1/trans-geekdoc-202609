### 3.3.3 Steady-state response of FGP plates

#### 3.3.3.1 Verification examples

In this part, validation examples of CDQM for steady-state response of FGP plates are conducted first. Fig. 3.17 shows the comparison between the steady-state response of FGP plates obtained with CDQM and FEM, all the structural edges are clamped. As shown in Fig. 3.17, excellent consistency of displacement curves with CDQM and FEM, demonstrates the correctness and universality of CDQM to FGP structural steady-state response.

#### 3.3.3.2 Parametric analysis for steady-state response

Based on the previous verification in subsection 3.2.1, the next works are concerned with the behavior of FGP structural steady-state response with diverse porosity coefficients and distributions. Fig. 3.18–3.20 are carried out to evaluate the qualitative regularity of effects of porosity parameters on steady-state response of FGP plates. For one reason, we conclude that the different porosity coefficients $e_0$ makes a little impact on the displacement amplitudes regardless the distributions and geometric shapes, while displacement amplitudes with Type 2 are generally larger than those with Type 1. For another, the displacements move to higher frequency with increasing the porosity coefficient $e_0$ in case of Type 1 porosity distribution, nevertheless, it converses with Type 2 porosity distribution. Moreover, the impact degree of porosity coefficient $e_0$ is sensitive to porosity distributions, the influence caused by $e_0$ with Type 2 porosity distribution is much more remarkable than that with Type 2 porosity distribution.

### 3.4 Conclusion

A general theoretical model of arbitrarily shaped FGP plates is established for investigation of free vibration, transient, and steady-state response. The technique of simulate virtual springs with the spectral Chebyshev method is employed to simulate various constraints of arbitrary curved edges. According to the FSDT and Lagrangian–Ritz approach, constitutive relation and energy equation are obtained. With coordinate transformation and two-dimensional Chebyshev polynomials, the displacement components of FGP plates are expressed in a square area named computational domains. The solutions to vibration of FGP plates can be easily obtained by Gauss–Lucas iteration technique. In order to confirm the universality and correctness, sets of examples are carried out. As a consequence, the contributions of porosity coefficient and distributions are analyzed in detail, including free vibration, transient response, and steady-state response.
