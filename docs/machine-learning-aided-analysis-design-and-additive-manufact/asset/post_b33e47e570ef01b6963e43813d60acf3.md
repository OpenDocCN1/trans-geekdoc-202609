## 15.2 Specimen preparation

### 15.2.1 Modeling of uniform foams

The Voronoi model is well suited for real foams because its generation process is similar to the physical foaming process. Therefore it is used to build uniform foams [43]. Uniform foams with different cell sizes, ranging from 5 to 10 mm, are shown in Fig. 15.2. Specimens are marked by U5–U10, where U represents uniform foams followed by their average cell size. For instance, “U5” represents uniform foams with a cell size of 5 mm. All foams used in the quasi-static test are cubic with the side length of 50 mm. In the dynamic test, the specimen adhered to the impact bar is accelerated in the air gun, which is difficult for the high-speed camera to capture a specific plane of foams. Therefore in the dynamic test, the cross-section of the specimen is set as a cell with a diameter of 50 mm, and the height is the same as that used in the quasi-static test. At least five cells are included in all directions to avoid the size effect [45]. For each cell size, we build three specimens with different mesostructural geometries to support the reproducibility of experimental results.

### 15.2.2.2 Modeling of graded foams

The Voronoi model is also employed to model graded foams. A continuous gradient is introduced by changing the cell size distribution along the loading direction while the cell wall thickness is kept constant. Based on the coordinate system (defined in Fig. 15.3A), we can customize the cell size distribution along the h-axis. To get a density gradient, the cell size distribution is given by:

$$s(\bar{h}) = \frac{s_{max} + s_{min}}{2} + \frac{s_{max} - s_{min}}{2} \cdot (1-2\bar{h})}{\frac{\dot{h}}{2}} \quad (0 \le \bar{h} \le \frac{1}{2})$$
$$s(\bar{h}) = s_{min} + \frac{s_{max} - s_{min}}{2} \cdot (2-2\bar{h})^M \quad (\frac{1}{2} \le \bar{h} \le 1) \quad (15.1)$$

where $M$ is the gradient parameter. $h$ is the normalized height and is defined as $h/H$, where $h$ is the coordinate along the height direction with an origin at the loading end of the uniformed specimen, and $H$ is the initial height of specimens (Fig. 15.3A). $s_{min}$ and $s_{max}$ are the minimum and maximum cell sizes of graded foams and are 5 and 10 mm, respectively. According to Eq. (15.1), the number of internal cells gradually increases along the crushing density, causing a gradually increasing density. Moreover, to get a negative gradient density, the cell size distribution is defined by:

$$s(\bar{h}) = s_{min} + \frac{s_{max} - s_{min}}{2} \cdot (2-\bar{h})^M \quad (0 \le \bar{h} \le \frac{1}{2})$$
$$s(\bar{h}) = s_{min} + \frac{s_{max} - s_{min}}{2} \cdot (2-\bar{h})^{\frac{1}{M}} \quad (\frac{1}{2} \le \bar{h} \le 1) \quad (15.2)$$

The number of internal cells gradually decreases along the loading direction, causing a gradually decreasing density. The variation of cell size for the normalized height of different graded foams is depicted in Fig. 15.3B. The first character “M” in the legend represents the gradient parameter given by Eqs. (15.1) and (15.2). Values of M including 1 and 10 are chosen for quasi-static tests. The second symbol “NG” or “PG” represents a negative or positive density, respectively. Apart from the monotonic density described above, six nonmonotonic distributions are also considered in Fig. 15.4. The number “H” or “L” denotes the highest or lowest density, respectively. The first character in the legend represents the gradient parameter given by Eqs. (15.1) and (15.2). Set to 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, and 10 to make more configurations of graded foams. In addition, others are the same as those of quasi-static test and thus are omitted.

### 15.2.3 Additive manufacturing

A commercial 3D printer called “Raise3D N2 PLUS” is used in the additive manufacturing process. First, we generate the computer-aided design of Voronoi foams. Secondly, the models are converted into stereography formats and transferred to a slicing software “IdeaMaker”. Finally, the models are sliced into sequentially layers and imported into the 3D printer for manufacturing. The base material of all specimens is a commercial polyacrylic acid called PolyMax. The density and melting point of printing material is 1200 kg/m³ and 170°C, respectively. The printing parameters are optimized to eliminate the influence on the mechanical properties of specimen. The layer height, extruder temperature, and speed are determined as 0.1 mm, 220°C, and 30 mm/s, respectively. The nozzle of this printer has a diameter of 0.4 mm and therefore the cell thickness of all specimens is set to 0.4 mm. Three specimens for each configuration are produced to check for the reproducibility of experimental results.

The relative density $\rho$ is defined as the ratio between foam density $\rho^*$ and the density of base material $\rho_$, is usually applied to describe the mechanical properties of foams. The average densities of all Voronoi foams are calculated and listed in Tables 15.1 and 15.2. It can be seen that the relative densities of uniform foams increase with decreasing cell size while all graded foams share a similar relative density.
