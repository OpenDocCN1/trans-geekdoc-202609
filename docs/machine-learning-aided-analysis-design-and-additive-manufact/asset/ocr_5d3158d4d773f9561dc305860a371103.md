# ## 10.1 Introduction

The development of lightweight structures is an active research field providing
to solve the long-term dilemma between weight and stiffness. The recent
innovations, as carbon fiber reinforcing systems [1] and new lightweight
hy materials [2], are increasingly appreciated in the industrial practice, where
t light yet stiff components are critically applied in the production of vehicle
frames [3], aircraft skeletons [4,5], spatial building designs [6,7], etc.
The porous-matrix structures are among the most adopted lightweight
forms in structural and mechanical engineering, and involve honeycombs, lattices,
and foams in line with the geometrical characteristics. Compared to honeycombs
[8] and lattices [9] which contain ordered (2D) or three-dimensional (3D) reg-
lular materials, foams are formed by random distributions of
internal pore sizes, densities, and consequently relatively low manufacturing
costs. In addition to the reduced weight (occasionally over 90% light-
er), they also offer under multifunctional properties, including
definite density, good energy absorption, and useful thermal and acoustic traits
[10]. Integrating metal foams into one single structural component gives
to the FG porous structures (metal based), which have been demonstrated
to possess even more outstanding mechanical attributes [11–16].
