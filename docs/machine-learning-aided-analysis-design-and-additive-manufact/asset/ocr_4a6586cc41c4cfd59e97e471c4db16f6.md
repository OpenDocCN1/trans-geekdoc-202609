However, foams of FFT with uniform density distribution cannot adapt the mechanical response of the loading end and supporting end to the specific load condition [30–34]. This made foams-filled tubes (FFFTs) popular in recent research in energy absorption.

Since it is difficult to achieve quantitative and controllable preparation of foams, there are few reports on the specimen preparation and experimental study of GFFTs [35–43] can obtain a priori conclusions with a small cost in time and resources, guiding the specific experimental design and engineering applications. For example, Sun et al. [38] show that the structural crashworthiness of FFTs can be optimized by employing gradient distribution of foam density. Nighi et al. [43] simulated various gradient functions on FFTs and investigated the energy absorption diagram. Energy absorption capacities of different kinds of GFFTs have been compared to the optimal crashworthiness of GFFTs. It is important to note that the solid element is employed to simulate the internal structure and mesoscopic mechanism of foam and cannot accurately capture the interaction between the cell-wall structure of the foam and the thin-walled structure of the tube. Furthermore it is difficult to obtain the deformation mechanism of GFFTs and may exhibit incorrect physical phenomena. This indicates that mesoscopic finite element analysis is needed to overcome the shortcomings of the solid element modeling method.

Furthermore, additive manufacturing (AM) enables the accurate fabrication of cellular structures with complex mes geometries. Many researchers have been working on actively manufactured cellular materials, such as metallic microlattices [44], cellular composites [45], auxetic cellular structures [46], polymer-derived ceramics [47], octet truss lattice materials [48], and smooth-lattices [49]. Using the mesoscopic method, AM can produce the corresponding specimen, making the mesoscopic geometric structure of specimens in simulation and experiment. Using mesoscopic finite element analysis and AM technique, this chapter serves two purposes. First, this work employs the AM technique according to the predetermined geometric FFTs, tubes with different cell sizes and foams with gradient configurations are all fabricated and tested under quasi-static compression. The compressive behavior is then reported on the experimental results. Exact geometric models used for the experiment are applied to the mesoscopic finite element analysis. This numerical work is carried out to simulate the interaction between the cell-wall structure of the foam and the thin-walled structure of the tube of FFTs. The obtained results will effectively guide the material and structural combination of energyabsorbing components in future engineering applications.
