#  14.3.2 Closed-cell foams

Closed-cell foams [53–55] have been widely used in various applications including protective structures, packaging, and structural function, due to their good energy absorption capacity, high strength/weight ratio, and outstanding tailorability [56–58].

Virat et al. [53] studied the relative density and loading rate on the compressive stress, deformation pattern, and energy absorption of Kelvin foams. As shown in Fig. 14.2, the 3D computer-aided drawing (CAD) data of cellular structures with Kelvin cells were first generated. Then, CAD models of specimens with different-cell thicknesses were converted into STL formats for additive manufacturing. Kelvin foams were tested by a universal testing machine for quasi-static loading and a direct-impact Hopkins Pressure Bar for dynamic loading. Four deformation modes were observed in experiments for the specimens with different relative densities and at different loading rates. Further numerical results indicated the presence of a critical relative density, below which the Kelvin foams deformed primarily by cell edges bending and beyond which the cells membranes deformed.
