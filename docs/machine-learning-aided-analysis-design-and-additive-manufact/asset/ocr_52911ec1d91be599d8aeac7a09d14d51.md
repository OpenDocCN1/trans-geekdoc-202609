# 1.1 Porous materials

Natural porous media, such as rocks, timbers, and biological bones, have inspired researchers and engineers to design lightweight composite forms by introducing cells into the originally homogeneous solid substances, leading to the development of artificial porous materials [1,2]. Not all the composites with internal pores should be called porous materials, as the porosity here does not refer to undesired defects during fabrication or usage. Instead, evenly or unevenly distributed pores are manually generated inside the solid matrix to achieve superior material performance for space applications, including reduced weight, improved absorption capacity and mechanical, thermal, chemical, electrical, biological, and optical behaviors. Note that generally speaking, cellular material is another term for porous material.

The development of artificial porous materials has been a hot research topic and experienced a rapid advance in recent decades. Various porous materials were proposed and designed in terms of their cell frame morphologies (regular or irregular, open or closed, specific cell sizes, distributions, and shapes), to form cell walls (metal, polymer, concrete, ceramic, or glass), and the fluid phases of pores (gas or liquid), as illustrated in Fig. 1.1.
The size of interval pores may vary across microscopic, mesoscopic, and macroscopic scales. These changeable parameters lead to the vast variety of property tailoring and the great potential of porous materials in achieving multifunctionalities.
