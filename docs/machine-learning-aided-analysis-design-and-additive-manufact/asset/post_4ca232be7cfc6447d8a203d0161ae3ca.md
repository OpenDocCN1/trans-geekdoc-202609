In recent years, functionally graded porous (FGP) has been increasingly applied in aerospace, medical devices, and other fields due to its low weight, high strength, excellent impact resistance, and so on. Compared with conventional materials, FGP material has the advantages of thermal resistance, acoustic insulation, and durability. At present, the free and forced vibration of FGP beam is a popular topic.

The mainstream methods for modeling and solving the FGP beam include the finite element method, differential quadrature method (DQM), and the Ritz method. Researchers have verified the reliability and accuracy of these methods, but there is still room for improvement in their computational efficiency.

FGP beam is one of the structures that has been extensively and deeply studied. Based on the premise that porosity is related to the thickness, Jamshid and Arghovani [10] found that gradient porosity affects the critical buckling load and frequency. Ebrahim and Zia [11] took the FGP Timoshenko beams as the research object to study its vibration characteristics, and discovered that material uncertainty affects the buckling behavior.

The analysis of bending [13–15], buckling [16,17], and dynamic vibration characteristics are important research results of FGP beams. Pennya [20] proposed a method that can solve the bending response of FGP beams in hot and humid environments. Chen et al. [21] came to the conclusion that both the pore distribution and graphene platelets have a huge influence on the nonlinear free vibration and postbuckling characteristics of F F graphene-reinforced composite beams. A parametric analysis of the elastic buckling of FGP beam was carried out by Harsha et al. [22], where the porosity parameters of the material were the focus of the study. Nguyen et al. [23] proposed a bivariate shear deformation theory for the prediction of buckling and vibration characteristics of symmetric porous beams and carried out a parametric analysis of the porosity coefficient and density type.

In addition, researchers have provided numerous discussions and analyses on the nonlinear behavior of FGP beam. Chen et al. [25] took the theoretical analysis of its low-velocity impact response, and found that small incursion impact loads would cause larger deflection of the beam. Chen et al. [26] applied a DQN-based Galerkin program to establish a theoretical analysis of vibration for FGP beams with imperfections, and classified the nonlinear resonance behavior. According to the method, Fouadi et al. [27] developed a solution for analyzing nonlinear bending characteristics of FGP beams. Based on the statistical linearization method and Euler beam theory, Raskheni and Ghadiri [28] studied the nonlinear random vibration of FGP nanobeam under elastic boundary conditions, and verified the reliability of their method by comparing the results with the perturbation method. Chen et al. [29] took the FGP sandwich beam as the research object and analyzed the influence of material and geometric parameters on the nonlinear free vibration under the clamped and simply supported boundary conditions.
