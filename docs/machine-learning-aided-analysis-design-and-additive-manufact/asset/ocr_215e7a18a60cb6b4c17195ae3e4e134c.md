FG porous composite structures

Despite the speedy advance in the research of FG porous structures, predictions of their properties have been continuously intricate, induced from the random nature of metal foams. Although the relative density is widely regarded as a dominant factor, the contribution from cell arrangements can be important and changing stochastic porosity distributions may result in evident variances when determining the overall foam mechanical features. For example, Young's modulus of closed-cell aluminium foams, discussed by Chen et al. [17], have a distinct variation up to 14.38% over the same relative density, matrix material, wavy edge, and representative volume element (RVE) size. This inherent difference is difficult to be identified from the foam morphologies.

On the other hand, the rapid development of AI-enhanced engineering provides a powerful tool for resolving problems [18–20], and is prone to opening up new horizons for cracking the aforementioned issue in the evaluation of porous structures made of random foams. Among various AI approaches, deep learning [21–23] has gained much attention of research scientists in the last decade and has been widely used in numerous applications. One of the factors that helped this is the massive amount of data available in each field. With the high computation power and advances in stochastic modeling and simulation of the physical phenomenon, researchers have used synthetic data to train models, which was not possible earlier due to lack of data or severe costs and resources required for acquiring such data. Deep learning models could be considered as a universal solution for data-driven problems meaning these can handle all types of data. Deep learning models such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) [24], convolutional neural networks (CNNs) [25], and others can be incorporated into various applications based on the nature of data and desired outputs.

This work adopts deep learning to measure how the stochastic distributions impact the metal foam behavior with a focus on the influence of random cell wall morphologies, exploring a new way to calculate the foam properties directly from cellular structures simply without building up the corresponding mechanics models. Specifically, CNNs are employed to investigate Young's modulus as efficiently as possible. CNN provide significant insights into the correlation between images and the features they represent been widely adopted in various fields, that is, image segmentation, investigating the microstructure–property relations, and prediction of fault and failure [26,27]. Following the deep learning, the structural uncertainty analysis is performed the static bending response of an FG porous three-layer beam, in order to identify its deflection ranges considering the indeterminate CNN-estimated properties of different layers.
