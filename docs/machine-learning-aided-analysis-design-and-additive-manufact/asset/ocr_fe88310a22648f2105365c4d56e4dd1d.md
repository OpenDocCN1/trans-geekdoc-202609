# I. Concrete digital fabrication methods

There are in general two categories of digital fabrication techniques for concrete structures depending on the usage of framework [60]. The first technique is called 3DCP. To begin, the freshly mixed cementitious materials are extruded out of a nozzle to form one layer. After that, the nozzle moves up by a height and repeats the extrusion process that allows the subsequent filament on the upper layer to deposit on the existing filament on the lower layer. Ultimately, the concrete structure is fabricated through the layer-by-layer deposition process, and therefore 3DCP is known as the No Formwork (free) fabrication method. A typical-set of 3DCP consists of the following components: (1) a concrete mixing system where raw materials (binder, aggregate, water, additive, etc.) are mixed to produce the freshly mixed concrete; (2) a pumping system where the freshly flowable concrete is transported by the pump to move through the hose that is linked to the printhead (nozzle); (3) robotic system with the controller and multiple-axis robotic manipulator, and the nozzle is mounted on the robotic arm that moves based on the designed tooling during the extrusion process [61]. Since 3DCP adopts a de-free printing process, the quality finishes the target concrete structure primarily depend on the material properties of fresh concrete. Here three important material properties are introduced, which are key to achieving the 3D printed concrete structure with adequate quality. The first property is pumpability, meaning before extrusion the concrete needs to be flowable enough to be transported to the nozzle without experiencing any blockage. The second property is extrudability, the concrete needs to be extruded as a continuous filament with an unaltered material. The third property is buildability, meaning after the extruded concrete needs to develop green strength to resist the self-weight of the upper layers (achieving the minimum deformation of the overall structure). Generally there have been ongoing research on the relationships between concrete properties and material properties [62]. Besides, there is also a growing interest in promoting the sustainable aspect of 3DCP by replacing the raw materials (cement and river) with recycled materials [33,63], including (1) supplementary cementitious materials (such as fly ash and silica fume), substituting cement [64,65]; (2) waste glass particles and rubber crumb subrutting agent [66–68]; (3) cement-ferrite alkali-activated materials/geopolymer [69–71]; (4) limestone called clay cement (LC) [72,73]. The fresh properties (pumpability, extrudability, buildability) together with the mechanical properties of 3DCP have also been extensively investigated over the past few years.
