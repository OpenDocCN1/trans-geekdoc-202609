## 8.1 Introduction

Functionally graded materials (FGMs) are new types of composites obtained by mixing ceramic and metallic constituents [1–4]. Material properties vary continuously through the beam thickness in function of the mixing process. This avoids the stress consideration observed in laminate materials. FGMs are reserved for specific uses, for example, coatings of thermal barriers for turbine blades, shielding for military applications, automotive, and aerospace industries, biomedical materials.

In the past decades, studies on FGM beams [5–10], plates [11–19] and shells [20–22] have received substantial attention, and an extensive spectrum of beam and plate theories has been introduced, based on the classical and the shear deformation-theory of beams.

FGMs are currently in great demand by industries, requiring specific models to analyze their behavior and predict their responses. Many researchers have been interested in different FGM structures because of their wide application areas. Both beam models, the Euler-Bernoulli model (CBT) for thin beams and the Timoshenko model (SDT) for thick beams, were introduced. The CBT model ignores the transverse deformation. It was later proposed to account for the shear deformation into consideration, resulting in the SDT. Nevertheless, this second model requires a shear correction to satisfy the top and bottom beams' boundary conditions, influences the results. The higher-order SDT aims to eliminate the faulty of CBT and for transverse deformation through beam thickness without providing any shear correction.

Multiple models with various shear stress have been proposed, for example, the Reddy model [23]. Thai and Vo [24] have presented various higher-order beam theories for FGMs. They have shown that these models are effective in the static and dynamic analysis of beams. Ebrahemi Jafari [25] analyzed thermomechanical vibration of temperature-dependent FGM beams with porosities by using an higher-order shear deformation theory (HSDT). Aydoglu and Taskin [26] used the Euler–Bernoulli model and parabolic and exponential shear functions to examine the bending responses of a simply supported beam. The higher-order SDT aims to eliminate the faulty of CBT and for transverse deformation through beam thickness without providing any shear correction.
