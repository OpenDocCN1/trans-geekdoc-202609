
$\mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} I_0C^T V_{R_0}C & I_1C^T V_{R_1}C & 0 \\ I_1C^T V_{R_0}C & I_0C^T V_{R_1}C & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_2C^T V_{R_2}C \end{bmatrix} \quad (2.33)$

## 2.3 Result and discussion

In this section a unified model of FGP straight and curved beams is established, the main purpose of which is to study the free and forced vibration characteristics of FGP beams with various material and geometric parameters. A series of numerical examples are provided to test the convergence, reliability, and accuracy of the formulations in the previous section. Three classical boundary conditions are considered, including simply supported, and free support, which are denoted by symbols of C, S, F, respectively. For case of understanding, the boundary conditions the FGP beam at $x=0$ and $x=L$ are connected together to each boundary condition which is referred to by values of boundary stiffness corresponding to Ref. [30]. To facilitate subsequent analysis and reduce uncertainties, the geometric parameters of straight and curved beams are uniformly given as: (a) straight beam: $L=1$ m, $h=0.1$ m, and $b=0.05$ m; (b) curved beam: $L=2$ m, $\varphi_0=30$ degrees, $\varphi_1=150$ degrees, $h=0.1$ m, and $b=0.05$ m; (c) elliptical beam: $a=1.5$ m, $b_v=1$ m, $\varphi_0=0$, $\varphi_1=\pi$, and $b=0.05$ m. Unless otherwise indicated, the dimensionless frequency is calculated by $\Omega = \omega L^2\sqrt{\rho_{max}/E_{max}}$, and the material of FGP beams is assumed to be open-cell aluminum foam with $E_{max}=70$ GPa, $\rho_{max}=2702$ kg/m$^3$, $v=0.3$.
