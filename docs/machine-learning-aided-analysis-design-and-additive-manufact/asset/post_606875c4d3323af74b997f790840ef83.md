Meanwhile, the brittleness of MS cubes was observed to rise with the increase of order. In general, for MS cubes with hollow square patterns, the second-order MS cube was considered the most suitable fractal-like structure due to its lower volumetric fraction (40%) than that of its first-order counterparts (75%), as well as its lower brittleness compared with the third-order counterparts resulted from the early-age stress concentration around the sharp edges.

## 13.5 Potential applications

This section discusses and explores the potential applications of lightweight concrete structures digitally fabricated in the context of 3D printing.

The first section is the protective concrete armor units deployed in coastal engineering. For the protection of coastal structures such as breakwater, these concrete armor units are normally fabricated in precast form with plain concrete, and a wide variety of armor units with various geometries have been designed and invented over the years, including solid units like a tripod, hexapod, tetrahedron, and dolos, [102]. The previous study shows that concrete armor units can be subject to three types of loading conditions [103], including (1) static load originating from the self-weight of armor units and the surrounding armor units resting on them; (2) dynamic pulsating load causing erosion on the marine structure; and (3) dynamic impact due to insensitive rigid body rotation of armor units leading to unit–unit impact. Notably, the dolos armor unit has been extensively applied for marine protection, and its mechanical performance under both static and dynamic loading has been well investigated [104–106]. In this case, the framework-based 3D-printed lightweight cellular structures were proposed by Nguyen-Van et al. [6], namely TPS structure (primitive and gyroid), could be a potential candidate for the application of protective concrete armor units, due to its adequate mechanical performance in terms of static compressive loading and impulse resistance.

The second potential application is the noise barrier wall structure. Previous studies have demonstrated the feasibility of manufacturing porous concrete slabs or wall panels with the adequate capability of sound absorption [107,108]. It should be noted here the term "porous concrete" specifically refers to the materials with relatively moderate porosity made of course aggregates and cementitious binding materials. Several studies have also shown that depending on the type and size of course aggregates used for producing such porous concrete slabs or panels the recommended porosity to achieve an optimized sound-absorbing capability could be around 25% [109]. Meanwhile, while the porosity of porous concrete increased from 14% to 23%, the mean sound absorption coefficient was observed to show an increasing trend whereas porous concrete has served as a typical example inspiring the application of 3D-printed lightweight cellular concrete in the field. For instance, the lightweight porous concrete blocks consisting of either TPMS structure (primitive and gyroid) [6] or fractal-like MS structure, which were both conceptualized and experimentally investigated by Nguyen-Van et al., [7, 8], had the solid volumetric fraction of 40%–75%. These porous concrete structures fabricated based on 3D printing technology, have the great potential to be applied to the next-generation lightweight sound-absorbing structure.

The third recommended application is the large-scale concrete structure for either structural or nonstructural purposes. Here two realized examples are proposed and described. The first sample is the bicycle bridge in Gertmert, the Netherlands which consists of six individual extrusion-based 3D-printed structural segments. Each segment had two straight edges that acted as the top and bottom of the bridge. For the interior of the segment, the bottle-shape pattern was designed as to form a hollow-structure concrete part, whose function was mainly to transfer the shear force (see Fig. 13.17). The density of the infill pattern (bottle-like shape) for each segment was carefully designed by the structural engineer to ensure the printed bridge had adequate structural performance while achieving the lightweight structure construction at the same time. ## 13.6 Conclusions

This chapter reviewed recent research on the mechanical performance of functionally graded structures (FPGSs) and their current design and manufacturing techniques. These lightweight cellular structures are preferred for the construction industry because of their superior attributes such as energy absorption properties, high strength-weight ratio, enhanced heat dissipation, and acoustic insulation characteristics. The following specific conclusions are drawn:

- Design of FPGSs was described in detail, in which the topology of TPMS to lattice structures with functionally relative density was clearly.
- Fabrication methods of these FPGSs were investigated on both composite and concrete materials. Here, the AM of composite materials and digital fabrication of lightweight concrete structures were critically reviewed.
- For the results and discussion, the mechanical performance of the TPMS composite-based and concrete-based structures under different types of loads were reported. Besides, some potential applications of such FPGSs were proposed. By using the advantage of AM technology, these intricate designs have been made easier than ever.
