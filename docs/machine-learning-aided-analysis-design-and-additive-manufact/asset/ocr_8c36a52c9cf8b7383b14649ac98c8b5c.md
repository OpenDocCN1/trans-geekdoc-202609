patterns and reproduced that 48,960 counterparts share identical responses, which we called "reproduced patterns." It is noted that the training data contains both the unique patterns and their corresponding reproduced patterns because the DNN does not naturally "know" that unique patterns and their counterparts should have an identical response.
After the preparation, we feed the training data (pattern-response) for the DNN and used the test data to verify the performance of the training. The more the number of layers, the deeper the network. Training the DNN, remains a challenging problem. The network architecture has become more applicable in recent years due to (1) the invention of new training/learning methods for faster and less computational cost, (2) training 61-GT and (3) the advantage of faster computer hardware such as GPUs. In this study, the DNN has five layers, and the number of neurons in each layer is given in Fig.9.2B. We built the DNN in MATLAB£ using the stochastic gradient backpropagation as the learning algorithm. Using the hyperbolic tangent function, the output (uniformly sampled forces) are scaled to $[-0.9, 0.9]$ for better performance. The proposed model does not depend on the learning method, and one may use more or fewer layers to fast-train the proposed model and increase its accuracy. In Fig. 9.1C, we applied the trained DNN as a predictor to observe the response of the test patterns. For the response prediction, we compared the performance of four different batch numbers/sizes and five training ratios is fixed at 250. We also identified an appropriate batch number (30) and the training ratio (20%) for the validation and accuracy of response prediction.
