Quasi-static compressive and four-point bending tests were applied to cubic blocks and prisms, respectively. For the compression test, the experiment results showed the hollow cube specimen with the lattice pattern had the maximum compressive strength, which was around 17%, 59%, and 99% higher than with cellular, triangular, and truss patterns, respectively. Compared with the primitive solid cubes, the compressive strengths of hollow cubic specimens with different hollow patterns had a relative strength range between 45% and 90%. Results of compressive strengths for different hollow specimens indicated the mechanical performance of lightweight porous structure could be significantly influenced by the tool path design that correlated with the stress distribution and deformation within the printed structure. Furthermore, the bending test results showed the hollow beam with a lattice pattern having the minimum tensile strength at the peak load, suggesting the highest capacity against the flexural deformation. Due to different designs in hollow beams, the crack was initiated and propagated from the bottom of the beam was found from each other, which could be another reason for the different deformation behaviors under the bending test. Overall, the study of Wang et al. [77] demonstrated the feasibility of optimizing the quasi-static mechanical performance of lightweight hollow concrete structures fabricated via the 3DCP method.

When it comes to the framework-based lightweight concrete, the mechanical performance at a quasi-static state has also been evaluated through both experiments and a simulation. Nguyen-Van et al. [66] fabricated the porous concrete (100 × 100 × 100 mm) with the 3D-printed PLA mold as the outer shell, and the structure consisted of lightweight cells with three different patterns, including TPMS (such as primitive and gyroid) and lattice. Compression tests at the loading rate of 70 kN/min were executed on those three types of porous structures, and the stress-strain curves and failure patterns were recorded and analyzed. The results showed the lattice structure had the minimum compressive strength (~ 3 MPa), and primitive structure (~ 10 MPa). The damage pattern analysis through numerical simulation has confirmed the possible reason for the weak mechanical response of lattice structure in compressive loads, which could be the included cell walls in the lattice structure that induced bending-dominated effects and accelerated the fracture damage. In contrast, concrete structures with TPMS structures generally had the spatial arrangement of intertwined and interconnected curvatures, providing supporting structures for the structure and leading to the minimum stress concentration. Moreover, among the two types of TPMS structures, the primitive structure was related to have a higher strength than the gyroid, which could be related to the location of different extents of stress distribution.
