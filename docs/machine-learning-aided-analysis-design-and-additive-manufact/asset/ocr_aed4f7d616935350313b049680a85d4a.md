FG porous composite structures

In fact, pioneer works regarding the uncertain structural behavior of FG structures have been investigated thoroughly in the past years, but limited concentrations are focused on the FGP structures with the consideration of nondeterministic system properties. For instance, Yang et al. [6] used the classical perturbation technique to calculate the uncertain response of FG plates with temperature loading conditions. Shaker et al. [7] considered the parametric sensitivity analysis upon natural frequency of functionally graded plates with the aid of first and second order reliability techniques. Subsequently, Sheokkar and Lal [8] adopted the first order perturbation method to evaluate the uncertain nonlinear behavior of advanced composites with the consideration of stochastic material properties. Considering the unique composition of FGP structures, the nondeterministic mechanical test against such material matters and needs to be investigated thoroughly.

In this chapter, the stochastic free vibration of the FGP plates are considered. To obtain a robust and efficient quantification framework, the machine learning aided free vibration analysis (MLA-SFV) technique has been introduced for the FG plates through the stochastic finite element method (SSEM). Systematic properties of the composites can be considered and implemented into subsequently, the inherent relation between the input and structural vibration responses can be assessed through the two machine learning algorithms, namely the Gaussian process regression and the extended support vector regression (X-SVR). Moreover, polynomial kernels are used for the machine learning algorithms to enhance robustness and stability. By establishing the regression model, the fundamental frequencies of the FGP plate can be predicted with detailed statistical moments of the outputs. Reliability profiles [i.e., probability density function (PDF) and cumulative distribution function (CDF)] of the outputs can be acquired for effectively evaluating the structural safety for FGP plates through the theory of reliability. Different from the traditional uncertainty quantification methods, the MLA-SFV framework is capable of self-optimizing and self-updating with the varied syntactic inputs, also the computational efforts by using the framework can be greatly eliminated. Such advantageous features of the machine learning aided stochastic analysis framework could indeed provide some reference to the real-life composite structure applications.
