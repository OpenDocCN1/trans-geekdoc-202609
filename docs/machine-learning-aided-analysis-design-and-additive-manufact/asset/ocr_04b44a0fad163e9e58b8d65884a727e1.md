Thereafter, at a nominal strain of 0.03 (Fig. 15.9B), the initial deformation concentration appears in the weakest region and influences neighboring cells, resulting in a decrease in the load-bearing capacity. As the nominal strain continues to increase, the collapsed cells are packed together and the undamaged cells begin to bear the load, the stress rises again. The final figure shows the densification stage with a significant rise. At a nominal strain of 0.65 (Fig. 15.9B), the cell walls touches each other and the foam will be collapsed into a solid material.

It must be emphasized that the nominal strain only reflects the deformation but cannot represent the localized deformation of specimens. To investigate the effects of cell size and gradient distribution on the deformation modes of foams, the DIC method is introduced to measure the strain contours of uniform and graded foams. These contours are calculated at the nominal strain of 0.08 and 0.04, respectively. These strain values correspond to the initial deformation forming of uniform and graded foams, respectively. The strain contours of uniform foams with different cell sizes under quasi-static compression are demonstrated in Fig. 15.10, where black arrows indicate the loading direction. It can be observed that the deformation bands are randomly distributed. In addition, the foams with larger cell sizes exhibit much more serious deformation localization than those with smaller cell sizes. To summarize, foams with a lower relative density deform more locally while uniform foams with a higher relative density deform more.
