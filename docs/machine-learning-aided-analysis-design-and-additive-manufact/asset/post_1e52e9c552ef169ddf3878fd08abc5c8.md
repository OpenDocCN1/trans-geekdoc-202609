## 12.1 Introduction

Functionally graded materials (FGMs) are typical kind of effective composites in recent decades, which has been popular due to its continuous gradation of material phases along certain axis [1]. Such arrangement was proposed by Japanese scientists in 1980s to enhance the high temperature protection of their spacecraft under extreme conditions [2]. The FGMs are heterogeneous composites containing two or more constituents, by which the overall properties of the structure can be rigorously designed with diverse purposes [3]. Another type of advanced composite, the lightweight porous materials (i.g., metal foams) are also attracting attentions considering its excellent absorbing abilities in aerospace, civil engineering fields [4]. Inspired by these two advanced materials, researchers review the potential of combining them as a functionally graded porous (FGP) type materials toward more promising properties, by implementing graded phase of pores into the microstructure level.

Due to the exceptional nature of FGP structures, numbers of studies have been published to investigate the behavior of FGP structures under various environmental conditions [5]. However, there exist some critical problems that affect the appropriate design of FGP structures in practical engineering applications. Among which, there is always a discrepancy between the real phase distribution of produced FGP structures and procedure owing to the inherent factors of uncertainty. Subsequently, the inevitable discrepancy would then reflect on their actual material properties, eventually the mechanical performance (i.e. natural frequency) of FGP structures in realtime applications. Thus it is vital to investigate the stochastic free vibration analysis for FGP structures and deliver appropriate safety assessment for the advanced composite against various aspects.

In fact, pioneer works regarding the uncertain structural behavior of FG structures have been investigated thoroughly in the past years, but limited concentrations are focused on FGP structures with the consideration of deterministic properties. For instance, Yang et al. [6] used the classical perturbation technique to calculate the uncertain response of FG plates with temperature loading. Shaker et al. [7] considered the parametric sensitivity analysis upon natural frequency of functionally graded plates with the aid of first and second order techniques. Subsequently, Sheokkar and Lal [8] adopted the first order perturbation method to evaluate the uncertain behavior of advanced composites with the consideration of material properties. Considering the unique composition of FGP structures, the deterministic mechanical test against such matters needs to be investigated.

In this chapter, stochastic free vibration of FGP plates are considered. To obtain a robust and efficient quantification framework, the machine learning aided free vibration analysis (MLA-SFV) technique has been introduced for FG plates. Systematic properties of composites can be considered and implemented subsequently, the inherent relation between input and structural responses can be assessed through two machine learning algorithms, namely Gaussian process regression and the extended support vector regression (SVR). Moreover, polynomial kernels are used for machine learning algorithms to enhance robustness and stability. By establishing the regression model, the fundamental frequencies of FGP plate can be predicted with detailed statistical moments of the outputs [i.e., probability density function (PDF) and cumulative distribution function (CDF)] of the outputs can be acquired effectively for evaluating the safety for FGP plates through the theory of reliability. Different from traditional uncertainty quantification methods, the MLA-SFV framework is capable of self-optimizing and self-updating with varied inputs, also the computational efforts can be greatly eliminated. Such advantageous features of machine learning aided stochastic analysis could indeed provide some reference to real-life composite structure.

## 12.2 Material models of the functionally graded porous plates

The FGP structure has been established in a Cartesian coordinate system with point (x, y, z). The plate is designed with thickness h, width b, and length l. Two types of porosity distributions are considered along the thickness direction as shown in Fig. 12.1, the porosity varies nonuniformly. The nonuniform distributions are presented with the Young's modulus, where E1, E2 are the maximum and minimum values of the FGP plate and other kinds of material properties (i.e., shear modulus, mass density) also satisfy these distributions. The two nonuniform distribution formulations of the FGP can be presented by Eqs. (12.1) and (12.2).

$$E(z) = E_1 - e_0 \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right)$$
$$G(z) = G_1 - e_0 \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right)$$
$$\rho(z) = \rho_1 - e_0 \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right) \qquad (12.1)$$

$$E(z) = E_2 - e_0 \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right)$$
$$G(z) = G_2 - e_0 \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right)$$
$$\rho(z) = \rho_2 - e_0 \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right) \qquad (12.2)$$

where $E_1, G_1, \rho_1$ and $E_2, G_2, \rho_2$ are the maximum and minimum values of Young's modulus, shear modulus, and mass density; $e_0$ is the porosity coefficient for Young's modulus and shear modulus, which can be acquired through
$e_0 = 1 - E_2/E_1 = 1 - G_2/G_1, 0 < e_0 < 1$; $e_m$ is the porosity coefficient for mass density, which can be acquired through $e_m = 1 - \rho_2/\rho_1; 0 < e_m < 1$. The shear modulus is related with Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ through
$G = E/2(1+\nu).$

From the material schematics, for the distribution type 1, the maximum values of Young's modulus and mass density are located at top and bottom surfaces of the FGP plate, while the minimum value of the properties is on the midplane. For the distribution type 2, the maximum and minimum values of Young's modulus and mass density are distributed on the top and bottom surfaces, respectively. Based on the general connection of mechanical properties [9], the above coefficients of the FGP plate can be related with:

$$\frac{E_2}{E_1} = \left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^2 \qquad (12.3)$$

$$e_m = 1 - \sqrt{e_0} \qquad (12.4)$$

## 12.3 Stochastic free vibration analysis

### 12.3.1 Free vibration analysis of functionally graded plate

For the free vibration analysis, typically as a multideree-of-freedom system, the governing equation for the free vibration analysis of FGP plate is presented as:

$$\mathbf{M} + \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{C} = 0, \quad \ddot{\mathbf{u}}(t) = d^2\mathbf{u}(t)/dt^2 \qquad (12.5)$$

where $\ddot{\mathbf{u}}(t)$ is the acceleration vector; $\mathbf{u}$ is the deflection vector; $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ are the global mass and stiffness matrices of the FGP plate, which is assembled from individual element mass matrices $\mathbf{M}^e$ and stiffness matrices $\mathbf{K}^e$ through

$$\mathbf{M}^e = \int_{V_e} \rho N dV, \quad \mathbf{K}^e = \int_{V_e} \mathbf{B}^T\mathbf{DB} dV \qquad (12.6)$$

where $e$ is the individual element within the FGP plate; $\rho$ is the mass density of the FGP plate; $\mathbf{N}$ is the deflection interpolation matrix for an element; $\mathbf{B}$ is the strain deflection compatibility matrix for an element; $\mathbf{D}$ is the elasticity stiffness matrix; $V_e$ is the volume of an element.
