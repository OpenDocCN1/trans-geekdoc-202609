## 6 Improving buckling and vibration response of porous beams using higher order distribution of porosity

Mohammad M. Keleshteri¹ and Jasmin Jelovica¹,²
¹Department of Mechanical Engineering, The University of British Columbia, Vancouver, BC, Canada, ²Department of Civil Engineering, The University of British Columbia, Vancouver, BC, Canada

## 6.1 Introduction

Developments in manufacturing technology have allowed increasing applications of high-quality porous materials in various fields. Porous materials have attracted significant attention from both industry and academia for a variety of science and engineering applications since they offer high design flexibility and excellent features such as low specific weight, high energy absorption and low thermal and electrical conductivity [1]. In design of porous structural components, such as beams, it is vital to improve their performance. The published studies focus on the static and dynamic behavior of porous, plates, and shells [1–40]. A brief literature review is presented herein.

Bending and buckling of functionally graded (FG) porous Timoshenko beams have been studied by Chen et al. [11]. It was assumed that the elastic modulus is graded through the thickness of the beam considering porosity distribution patterns. It was found that porosity distribution offers better buckling capacity and can improve the bending resistance. Keleshteri and Jelovica [22] investigated the nonlinear free vibration of FG porous moderately cylindrical panels considering different shell theories and boundary conditions. Mechanical properties of open-cell foam were assumed to vary continuously through the thickness. It was found to enhance the dynamic response of the cylindrical panel, porosity should be avoided near the panels' surfaces. Buckling behavior of two-directionally porous beams was studied by Tang et al. [3]. They found that the effect of the porosity direction is most important for the critical loading when the porosity is in the axial direction.

Keleshteri and Jelovica [16] proposed a new porosity distribution to maximize linear natural features of deformable bidirectional porous beams with nonuniform porosity distribution. It was assumed that closed-cell voids are aligned along the thickness and length of the beam. It is concluded that the proposed porosity distribution increases the linear and nonlinear natural frequencies in comparison to the conventional porosity distribution. Chen et al. [10] investigated the free and forced vibration of FG beams with nonuniform porosity distribution. It was found that larger porosity coefficient results in higher dynamic deflection. Nonlinear free vibration behavior of FG thin plates with porosities based on the classical plate theory was studied by Wang et al. [28]. Both even and uneven porosity distributions were considered. They concluded that the effect of porosity resonance reduces the region but that enhances the dynamic response. Anridh et al. [4] studied vibration, buckling, and bending of FG porous beams graphene-reinforced nanocomposite (graphene plateletslets, GPL/s) curved beams with a geometric shear deformation theory. It was found that the type of weight distribution of graphene-reinforced nanocomposite impacts the beam affine stiffness. The literature shows that porosity distribution can have a significant effect on mechanical response of structures; however, the effect of the functional distribution is relatively unchanged.

The aim of this research is to investigate the effects of higher porosity distributions (unidirectional and bidirectional) on the buckling, free, and forced vibration of porous beams. For this purpose, a mathematical model is developed using the Hamilton principle considering the third-order deformation theory and Karman geometrical nonlinearity. The Galerkin technique and harmonic balance method (HBM) are utilized to obtain a closed-form approximate solution. Correctness and accuracy of the presented methodology are verified with the literature. Subsequently, a thorough investigation is presented to discover the effect of the bidirectional and unidirectional porosity distributions on the critical buckling load and free vibration response of porous beams. It is found that bidirectional porosity distribution (BPD) has better buckling and vibration performance and these performances can even be improved if the high-order porosity distributions are used.
