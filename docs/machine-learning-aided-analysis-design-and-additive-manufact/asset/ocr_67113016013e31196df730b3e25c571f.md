Besides, the structures can be optimized during the design process to save time and construction cost, while minimizing material waste, as well as satisfying safety and aesthetic criteria [23].

In recent years, the additive manufacturing of concrete (AMoC) has been employed to fabricate FGPS, such as TPMS cementous cellular structures [6,24,25], fractal-like structures [1], and Bologna structures [26,27]. It is not easy to practically implement the FGPS on a large scale, as the AMoC is still in its infancy [28]. Depending on the availability of framework in concrete fabrication, the AMoC method is divided into two categories: (1) AMoC with no 3D concrete printing (3DCP) that is obtained through layer-by-layer deposition [29], shot [30], powder-based technique [31]; (2) FGPS, including 3D printing framework [32]. However, the FGPS normally contain many parts, which can be difficult to achieve by using the 3DCP technique without framework. To overcome this limitation, 3D printing framework becomes the mechanism of fabricating complex and optimized structures like the FGPS [33]. By using the 3D printing framework technique, the mechanical performance of the TPMS cementous cellular structures was first conducted by Vuong et al. [6] using 3D-printed polyacrylic acid (PLA) molds. Besides, mechanical behaviors of different types of fractal-like cementous cellular blocks under uniaxial compressive loading were numerically studied, in which the 3D printing method was supposed to be employed [1]. The 3DCP process without framework was also used to print some FGPS like honeycomb, cellular panel, and Bologna structures to study their failure and inelastic deflection

With the super mechanical properties of the TPMS, these structures can be promising for a wide range of engineering applications such as noise barrier wall, decorative walls, coastal protection blocks, and so forth. The aim of this chapter is to present a comprehensive review for evaluating the behaviors of these TPMS subjected to various types of loads including static and dynamic loading. A description of the design process and manufacturing techniques for these porous structures is provided in Sections 13.2 and 13.3, followed by the discussion in Section 13.1 in which performance evaluations of the TPMS structures under static and dynamic loading are investigated. The last section of this chapter is the Conclusions, regarding the results obtained, and potential applications of the TPMS structures.
