#  15.5 Predictive model

Despite empirical and numerical studies have been published on the predictive model of graded foams. However, a predictive model that can predict the mechanical properties of graded foams will be very useful in material designing for engineering applications. In this section, elastic-plastic-hardening model for graded foams is established using the stress—strain relations of uniform foams. Based on the proposed models, the stress—strain curves of graded and uniform foams are compared to figure out their relationship.

#  15.5.1 Comparison of graded foams with uniform foams

In this chapter, specimens are loaded under quasi-static (20 m/s) compression where no shock wave occur. Therefore the nonmonotonic gradient distribution will not influence the stress—strain curves, which can be treated similarly to monotonic foams. Therefore only graded foams with the monotonic gradient are discussed here.

For quasi-static tests, as shown in Fig. 15.20, since specimen “U8” has a similar overall relative density to graded foams, its compressive curve is similar to that of specimen “M1-PG.” For the same relative density, uniform and graded foams have similar elastic modulus and densification stages. Besides, the plateau stress of “M1-PG” (1.41 MPa) and “U8” (1.39 MPa) is close to each other, though “M1-PG” exhibits a hardening plateau stage rather than a constant one (like “U8”).
