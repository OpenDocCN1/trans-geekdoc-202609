# ## 6.1 Introduction

Developments in manufacturing technology have allowed increasing applications of high-quality porous materials in various fields. Porous materials have attracted significant attention from both industry and academia for a variety of science and engineering applications since they offer high design flexibility and excellent features such as low specific weight, high energy absorption and low thermal and electrical conductivity [1]. In design of porous structural components, such as beams, it is vital to improve their performance. The published studies focus on the static and dynamic behavior of porous, plates, and shells [1–40]. A brief literature review is presented herein.

Bending and buckling of functionally graded (FG) porous Timoshenko beams have been studied by Chen et al. [11]. It was assumed that the elastic modulus is graded through the thickness of the beam considering porosity distribution patterns. It was found that porosity distribution offers better buckling capacity and can improve the bending resistance. Keleshteri and Jelovica [22] investigated the nonlinear free vibration of FG porous moderately cylindrical panels considering different shell theories and boundary conditions. Mechanical properties of open-cell foam were assumed to vary continuously through the thickness. It was found to enhance the dynamic response of the cylindrical panel, porosity should be avoided near the panels' surfaces. Buckling behavior of two-directionally porous beams was studied by Tang et al. [3]. They found that the effect of the porosity direction is most important for the critical loading when the porosity is in the axial direction.
