Instead, following the classic work of Gibson and Ashby [8], both of them are called cellular solids, referring to materials with a large number of holes.

As shown in Fig.14.2, cellular solids can be classified into disordered foams and ordered lattice structures. Materials can be open-cell and closed foams. Open foams are connected by edges, and there is no cell wall-filling between the edges [12]. Closed-cell foam refers to cellular solids with holes separated by cell walls [13]. A lattice structure is a kind of structural material composed of one or more structural units. According to its macro dimension, it can be divided into one-dimensional (1D) lattice (like architecture [14]), two-dimensional (2D) lattice (like honeycomb architecture [15]), and three-dimensional (3D) lattice (like oct-truss architecture [16]).

The above discussions are limited to uniform cellular solids, which physical properties are uniformly distributed. However, uniform cellular solids lack design flexibility. To improve the mechanical properties of uniform cellular solids, many studies have focused on graded solids. Graded cellular solids lie at the intersection between gradient materials [17,18] and cellular solids [19,20]. It not only has design of gradient materials but also more complete deformation and excellent energy absorption capability than uniform cellular solids.
