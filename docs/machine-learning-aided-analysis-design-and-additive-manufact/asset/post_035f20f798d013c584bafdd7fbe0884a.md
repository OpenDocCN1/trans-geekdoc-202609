Duan et al. [65] employed SLM to produce steel 316 L plate lattices. It should be noted that since plates have closed-cell, it is difficult to directly produce such lattices with powder bed or liquid-based additive manufacturing technologies. Therefore in Fig. 14.19, the authors made a few small holes in the plate faces to allow removing the closed cell after manufacturing. Further experiments show that for any novel class of plate lattice, elastically isotropic could be achieved for a wide range of relative densities. Moreover, the proposed plate lattices exhibit superior mechanical performance over shell lattices and truss lattices.

Andrew et al. [66] fabricated three elementary plate lattices including SC, BCC, and FCC as well as hybrid plate lattices (SC-BCC, SC-FCC, and SC-BCC-FCC) using FFF technique. They found that the SC-BCC-FCC plate lattice had the best response as each constituent in the lattice contributed to the load-carrying capacity. The combination of additive manufacturing and micro-architected cellular solids promotes the development of metamaterials with unprecedented properties (Fig. 14.20).

Maskery et al. [83] manufactured periodic lattice structures using SLM. Lattice structures with uniform density and graded density were both produced to compare the quasi-mechanical property (Fig. 14.21). Graded lattices exhibited deformation mode and increasing strength while uniform lattices showed lowest plateau stage. Moreover, graded and uniform structures absorbed similar energy until specimens were compressed into densification. This is one of the early articles to combine additive manufacturing technology and graded cellular solids. It provides new concepts for the subsequent research on the design of graded cellular solids.

Xiao et al. [84] fabricated graded Ti-6Al-4V lattice structures using SLM (Fig. 14.22). They focused on the dynamic mechanical performance of graded lattice and thus the Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar system was used on specimens for strain rate up to 1000s. The deformation mechanism and strain distribution of graded lattice were analyzed. Results demonstrated that the designed graded modes and load directions in the work showed no influence on the compressive response of graded lattices within the tested strain ranges.
