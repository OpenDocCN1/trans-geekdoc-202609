The FG beam is composed of a combination of metal and ceramics whose combination changes from the top surface purely ceramic to the bottom surface metallic. The beam material effective characteristic $\wp$ is assumed to change through the FG beam thickness through volume ratio and the characteristics of the constituent materials. It is formulated by the law of mixing as follows:

```
$$\wp = \wp_m\theta_m + \wp_z\theta_z = (E_m, \nu_m, \dots)$
```

$E$, $ho$, $
u$ are Young's modulus, mass density, and Poisson's coefficient, respectively. Variation of $
u$ is generally small, so it remains constant $	heta_z$ and $	heta_m$ are ceramic and metal volume proportions, respectively, defined by [41]:

```
$$\theta_m + \theta_z = \theta_z = (0.5+z/h)^p, \p \ge 0$
```

The gradient index ($p$), with $p \ge 0$ determines the profile of the material in the FG beam thickness direction. It can be modified to obtain the optimum component materials distribution. In Fig. 8.2 shows the distribution of ceramic volume proportion across the FG beam thickness for various material indexes. Each effective characteristic of the FG beam can be expressed as follows:

```
$$\wp(z) = (\wp_p - \wp_m)(0.5+z/h)^p + \wp_m = E_m, \p$
```
