Duvayd et al. Several theories, including Love’s shell theory and Donnell—Mushtari theory, have been used for the vibration of shallow shells [22–25]. Discrepancies exist between the results obtained by these theories because of different assumptions. Most are limited to thin shells and classical boundary conditions. The material properties are usually isotropic or anisotropic. Qian et al. [26] studied the moist-buckling and post-buckling of double-shallow shells which was confined by an elastic foundation and in a thermal field. Liew and Lim [27] investigated the vibration of double curved shells with superelliptical cutout inside the shells. Qatu [28] obtained the natural frequencies of shallow shells with different curvatures and different boundaries. Shou [29] introduced a temperature change, shape distribution, and other parameters on vibration characteristics of composite curved shells composed of FG platelets (FGPLs) with classical boundary conditions. Ajit et al. [30] adopted the Galerkin method to conduct vibration analysis of FGM double-shallow shells under the constraints of classical boundary conditions. Articles on the structure of FGP materials can be found in many other monographs and papers, as refs to [33–41].

Although efforts have been made in the above studies, there is still a gap in gaining insight into the vibration behaviors of FGP plates and on elastic foundation. The main focus of this paper is to establish a unified dynamic analysis model for shallow shells on elastic foundations. In the following subsections, a unified theoretical formula model will be established. Then, the convergence analysis and validity analysis will be carried out. Finally, the effects of factors on free vibration and forced vibration induced steady-state response and transient-state response will be investigated.

## 5.2 Theoretical formulations

### 5.2.1 Functionally graded porous material properties

FGP materials have similar material characteristics with FGM. The elastic modulus (E) of the material are also nonlinear functions of the direction along the material thickness coordinate (assuming the thickness coordinate is z). Mass density $\rho(z)$ and shear modulus $G(z)$ also have such a relationship. FGP material typically has various types of porosity, such as uniform distribution, symmetric distribution, and non-symmetric distribution. In the chapter, two representative porosity distributions along the thickness are namely Type 1: symmetric distribution, Type 2: asymmetric. The biggest difference between the two types is that the porosity distribution function along the thickness coordinate direction is different. The material property function of Type 1 can be expressed as follows:

$E(z) = E_1 - e_{e_0}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right)$
$G(z) = G_1 - e_{g_0}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right) \quad (5.1)$
$\rho(z) = \rho_1 - e_{\rho_0}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right)$

The material expression of Type 2 is defined as Eq. (5.2). It is obvious that the Young's modulus, the mass density, and shear modulus show a monotonic trend along the thickness direction.

$E(z) = E_1 - e_{e_0}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right)$
$G(z) = G_1 - e_{g_0}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right) \quad (5.2)$
$\rho(z) = \rho_1 - e_{\rho_0}\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2h}\right)$

In the above formula, $h$ indicates the thickness of the shell, and $e_m$ are porosity coefficients [42], which is given by:

$e_0 = 1 - \frac{E_0}{E_1} = 1 - \frac{G_0}{G_1} (0 < e_0 < 1)$
$e_m = 1 - \sqrt{1 - e_0} \quad (5.3)$

where $E$ is elastic modulus, $\rho$ is mass density, $G$ is shear modulus. In addition, subscripts 0 and 1 represent the minimum and maximum values of material parameters. It is worth noting that $e_0 = e_m = 0$ indicates there are no pores in the material, while $e_0=e_m=1$ is obviously an impossible form because the material property above degenerates to zero.
