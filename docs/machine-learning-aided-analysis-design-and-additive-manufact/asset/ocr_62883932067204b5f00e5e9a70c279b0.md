Similarly, the specimens 1/4-H and 3/4-H show gradually increasing plateau stages (Fig. 15.1B). In practice, the tendency of the stress-strain curve (grade fluctuation) is mainly dependent on the local density near the end. For example, the support end of specimen M1-PG possesses a fluctuation while the end of M1-NG possesses a gradually increasing plateau stage. In the experiment, the strain gauges noted the support end obtain the stress–strain curve of the support end of specimens. If the lowest-density region is close to the support end, this will end with a gradually increasing plateau stage. In contrast, if the highest-density region is placed here, this will exhibit a fracture fluctuation before the gradually increasing plateau. The deformation process of graded foams can explain these phenomena. For instance, for a positive graded foam M1-PG (Fig. 15.17D), the initial plastic deformation is dominated by the lowest-density region where the specimen collapses in a layer-by-layer manner.
