A wide range of materials has been utilized to fabricate TPMS structures based on PBF techniques. For instance, maraging steel and stainless steel were used to produce both network/solid phase and matrix phase TPMS lattices [40–44,46]. From apart, biocompatable materials and commercial-pure titanium (CP–Ti) Titanium alloy (Ti6Al4) were explored to manufacture TPMS lattices for tissue engineering applications [47–49]. Aluminium alloy (AlSi10Mg) were also used to manufacture lightweight TPMS structures [50,51]. Meanwhile, polymeric powders such as nylon were considered for the fabrication of TPMS lattices [52].

The stereolithography (SLA) also employed by researchers to fabricate TPMS lattices. In SLA, the liquid polymer is cured by a UV laser on a moving tray. Initially, the layer is placed in a tank filled with liquid polymer. A digital miller is used to manipulate the UV laser to produce the desired shape through a transparent bottom. Similar, after a layer is completed, the building tray moves up, and another layer is cured upon the previous layer [53]. It is noticeable that the SLA technique is limited to polymers, but it is possible to fabricate thicker walls and struts with currently available PBF materials. Studies have produced polymeric TPMS lattices based on this technique [54–56].

Material extrusion is also employed by researchers to produce TPMS lattices. The most commonly used extrusion AM technique is fused filament deposition modeling (FDM), in which 3D objects are fabricated by the heating filament of the nozzle to a semiliquid state and extruded from a nozzle to a building platform where the object is. Researchers have employed FDM techniques to fabricate polymeric TPMS lattices [17,57,58]. Material jeting is also employed by some studies to fabricate TPMS lattices [43,59]. In the material jetting process, droplets of photo-sensitive polymers are jeted into a building platform by inkjet heads and cured by UV lights. It is worth mentioning that in the material jetting, wax support may be required and need to be removed by postprocessing with thermal treatment, while high-pressure water jets may be required to remove the support materials in some systems.
