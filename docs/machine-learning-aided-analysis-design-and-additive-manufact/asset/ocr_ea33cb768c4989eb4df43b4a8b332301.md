# 3.4 Conclusion

A general theoretical model of arbitrarily shaped FGP plates is established for investigation of free vibration, transient, and steady-state response. The technique of simulate virtual springs with the spectral Chebyshev method is employed to simulate various constraints of arbitrary curved edges. According to the FSDT and Lagrangian–Ritz approach, constitutive relation and energy equation are obtained. With coordinate transformation and two-dimensional Chebyshev polynomials, the displacement components of FGP plates are expressed in a square area named computational domains. The solutions to vibration of FGP plates can be easily obtained by Gauss–Lucas iteration technique. In order to confirm the universality and correctness, sets of examples are carried out. As a consequence, the contributions of porosity coefficient and distributions are analyzed in detail, including free vibration, transient response, and steady-state response.
