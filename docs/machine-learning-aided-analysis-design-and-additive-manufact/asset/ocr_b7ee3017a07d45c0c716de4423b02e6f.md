**Fig. 4.23** Excitation amplitude response of the functionally generated porous cylindrical shell with different porosity distributions (---, stable solution; -- unstable solution; $e_1=0.5; \omega^*=1$).

Excitation amplitude responses of the FG porous cylindrical shell with different porosity distributions are shown in Fig. 4.23. There is only one stable solution for the driven mode when the excitation amplitude is small. With the increase of the excitation amplitude, the stable solution bifurcates and another branch appears near 0.01. Then branch 1 of the driven mode becomes unstable. For the companion mode, branch 1 is always zero. At the same bifurcation point, branch 2 appears. The branch 2 increases with the excitation amplitude. Above phenomenon shows that the emergence of the additional branch 2 to 1:1 internal resonance requires a sufficiently large excitation amplitude. It is seen that the PD-A shell requires the lower excitation amplitude to excite the nonzero response of the companion mode because branch 2 of the PD-A shell appears first.

Fig. 4.24 illustrates excitation amplitude response of the FG porous cylindrical shell with various porosity coefficients. As the porosity coefficient increases, the excitation threshold for the 1:1 internal resonance of the PD-A shell increases, while that of the PD-B shell increases. It is seen that the largest pores of the PD-A shell are located at the middle surface of the shell, which enhances the stiffness of the shell. However, the case of the PD-B shell is the opposite. The effect of the porosity coefficient on the excitation amplitude response of the PD-U shell is insignificant.

Fig. 4.25 and 4.26 show the time responses of the FG porous cylindrical shell with different porosity distributions and coefficients for $\omega^*=1$ (branch 2 in Fig. 4.21), respectively. It is obvious that the phase difference between the companion and driven modes is $\pi/2$, which is a typical characteristic of traveling wave vibration. Porosity distribution and porosity coefficient mainly affect the peak value of the time response, while they have insignificant effect on the vibration period and phase of the FG porous circular cylindrical shell.
