# 10.2.2 Database construction

Various RVEs with different internal porosity distributions and relative densities are generated and calculated to obtain their Young’s modulus through FE compression simulations. The combination of RVE images (see Fig. 10.1 as an example) and their compressive modulus forms denote for AI training. All the RVEs adopt the same settings: size 30 × 30 mm²; BEAM188 element with the uniform thickness as 0.142 mm; aluminum cell walls (aligned with the validation case): Young’s modulus is 61.7 GPa, mass density 2700 kg/m³; Poisson’s ratio 0.3. Their relative densities change from 6.1% to 20.1%, leading to a wide value range of Young’s modulus: 282.5–487.3 MPa. During the calculation, the beam thickness is not considered as a variable when creating the datasets, in fully center on the impact of internal morphologies. Most of the RVEs are featured with convex cells, others contain limited numbers (usually just one) of concave cells, as illustrated in Fig. 10.3. In terms of the high-porosity metal foam, convex cells represent the internal pores, while concave cells denote those with bent or missing walls, leading to a significant drop in the compressive stiffness. In addition, this research also includes special cases handled by regular cell geometries to promote the database diversity. Therefore a mix of usual (convex pores, most of the cases) and internal (concave or regular) RVEs are produced, as illustrated in Fig. 10.3 and 10.4.

This dataset consists of a total of 201 images (each linked to an individual RVE modulus) which are split into training, validation, and testing subsets with 70%, 20%, and 10%, respectively. It will be shown, later on, that this number of images results in an acceptable range of errors in the prediction for this particular problem. Certainly, providing more image samples for the AI algorithm is indeed useful to reduce the error of predictions. The original size of the images is 512 × 512 pixels.
