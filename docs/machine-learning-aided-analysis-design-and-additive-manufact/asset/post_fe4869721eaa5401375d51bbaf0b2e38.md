## 1.3.2 Mechanical analysis

Among many interesting aspects of FG porous composite structures, their superior mechanical behaviors have continuously been the focus of attention. To carry out the corresponding analysis, the effective material properties may serve as a starting point, where various models have been linked to the mesoscopic/microscopic geometric features (relative density mainly) with macroscopic parameters (such as Young’s modulus [49], including Meng–Kipsch model [43], Gibson–Ashley model [6,44], Christensen model [45], Roberts–Garbageci model [46], Chen model [47], and modified rule of mixture [48]. On the basis of these constitutive relations, the variations of material with respect to the graded properties can be identified and employed in the structural assessments within the framework of deformation theories: Tenshenko beam theory [49], first-order shear deformation of plate theory [50]; Domnell shell theory [51]. Consequently, the overall behaviors of FG porous structures are characterized by determining their critical buckling loads [49], static bending deformations [49], natural frequencies [52], forced vibration amplitudes [52]. On the other hand, numerical simulations via finite element (FE) software are often adopted to directly construct the mesoscopic/microscopic features of graded cellular materials which is especially applicable for the energy absorption related to novel porous geometries under impacts [53–55]. Furthermore, various experimental works have been conducted on the manufacturing and mechanical testing of FG porous specimens [32,56,57].

With the aid of these models and methods, the mechanical examination of FG porous composite structures has been an active area with intensive research focusing on the effects from graded properties on the structural performances. Chen et al. [49,52,58–60] systematically studied the bending, buckling, postbuckling, free vibration, forced vibration, and nonlinear vibration of FG porous beams and plates, as well as their sandwich counterparts. Zhao et al. [61] investigated the dynamic instability of graphene-reinforced FG porous arches. Gao et al. [62] assessed the nonlinear free of FG porous nanocomposite plates resting on an elastic foundation. Dong et al. [63] revealed the vibration characteristics of reinforced FG porous cylinders with spinning motion. Wang et al. [64] focused on the free and forced vibration analyses of FG porous containing nonsymmetric or symmetric porosity distributions. Wu et al. [65] constructed an FG framework for the examination of porous structures and determined the dynamic features of a three-story-bay FG porous frame. Chen et al. [67] combined the FG homogenization and structural assessment in the multiscale modeling of FG porous beams and linked the cellular structures to overall mechanical buckling and vibration performances. Mohavedi et al. [66] addressed the quasistatic and dynamicions of FG metal foams and discussed their deformation mechanisms, plate stresses, energy efficiencies, etc. Chen et al. [53] built up FE models for FG porous structures with four graded morphologies and carried out the dynamic simulations to identify their energy absorption capacities under different impacts. Fan et al. [67] fabricated FG porous scaffolds via selective methods and explored their full compression responses numerically and experimentally, providing guidelines for topological porous lattice designs. Teimouri and Asgari [68] developed new FG porous and lattice structures using a topology optimization technique and evaluated the structural stiffness and crashing behavior by performing FE simulations and compression tests on adequately manufactured samples.

Although the aforementioned publications individually presented a number of interesting perspectives on the underlying potential of this new composite form, a thorough summary is desired to unfold its past, present, and future to motivate the further advance, as given in this book.

## 1.4 Chapters in this book

This book covers various topics regarding the FG porous composite structures on the machine learning–aided analysis, design, and additive manufacturing, comprehensively address how to examine, shape, and fabricate with the latest techniques. The machine learning and additive manufacturing providing tremendous changes in the investigation and construction of complex geometries, opening a new window on the study of FG poros. Meanwhile, the traditional approach on the basis of potentially, plate, and shell theories associated with constitutive relations of porous media is commonly adopted for effective performance predictions. All these research strategies are employed here separately or jointly. In addition, the application potential of FG porous composite structures is specified. Therefore, this book endeavors to draw a big picture of the up-to-date research advances in this field by integrating the conventional and new methods, and offering a full-scale overview from analysis and design to manufacturing and application.

The key information of each section and chapter is summarized in Fig. 1-6, in terms of the research subjects and material forms. Section I and Chapter 1 are written to provide an easy and profound understanding of the whole book by elaborating the general concepts of porous materials, FG porosities, and the composite structural forms. The related fabrication and mechanical analysis are briefed as needed.

The following 16 chapters are divided into four sections. Section II contains seven chapters, where the first four aim at the fixed and forced vibrations of FG porous straight and curved beams (Chapter 2), quadrilateral plates and shells (Chapter 3), cylindrical shells (Chapter 4), and elastic foundations. Symmetric and asymmetric material gradations are discussed and compared to those of uniform/random geometries on the vibration of porous beams and shells. Chapter 6 evaluates the improved beam buckling and vibration responses brought by the high-order unidirectional and bidirectional open-cell voids. Chapter 7 investigates the impacts of stochastic material properties on probabilistic stability analysis of FG graphene-reinforced beams. Chapter 8 suggests a bending theory considering the thickness effect when evaluating the bending behavior of FG beams, which can be effective in the examination of porous composite components with graded materials. These seven chapters cover the deterministic and nondeterministic, buckling, vibration, and bending studies of FG (porous) structures based on the beam, plate, and shell theories.

Section III consists of four chapters with a focus on the adoption of machine learning in the calculation of FG porous structural performance. According to the connection between geometric features and mechanical responses, Chapter 9 uses a machine-learning-based framework for accelerated characterization and pattern generation of noncellular architectural materials. Chapter 10 develops a convolutional neural network model to directly predict the metal modulus from the images of FE representative volume elements, and conducts a structural uncertainty analysis on the bending of FG porous to examine the influence of uncertainty predictions. Chapters 11 and 12 carry out the machine learned static and free vibration analyses of FG porous plates, by proposing an interface to solve the uncertainty problem. These four chapters use different strategies incorporating machine learned techniques into the assessment of porous composites, offering innovative solutions to facilitate the traditional mechanical computations.

Following the analysis and design of composite forms with graded porosities, Section IV provides insights into their efficient fabrications via 3D printing and includes four chapters. Chapter 13 reviews the recent advances in the performance evaluation of FG porous structures containing periodic minimal surfaces, and introduces the additive manufacturing techniques of composite materials and the digital fabrication of lightweight concrete structures.
