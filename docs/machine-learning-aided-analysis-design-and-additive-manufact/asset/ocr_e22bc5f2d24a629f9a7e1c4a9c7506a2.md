# 13.4.1 Mechanical\u2019s properties\u00u2019s of triply periodic minimal surface composite-based structures

Early research on lattice structures was based on a simplified analytical approach due to the limitations of fabrication techniques. Calladine and English [85] described two different types of structures. Type I cellular structures present a nearly constant (if not increasing) strength under loading, while type II structures show a softening caused by the buckling of cellular material and are more sensitive to inertial effects. The response type of a lattice structure is determined by the number of nodes and struts according to Maxwell Criteria [9]. To evaluate the mechanical properties of lattice structures (Ls), an analytical model has been described by Ashby and Gibson. They showed that relative density (\rho*/\rho_s) is the most important characteristic in cellular materials [86]. Although the cell shape, size, edge, and face connectivity can affect the mechanical properties, they are neglected in this model, only considering ligament bending.
