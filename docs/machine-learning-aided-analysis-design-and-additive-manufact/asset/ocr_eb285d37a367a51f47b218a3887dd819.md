Since all cells in foams have the same strain at the elastic stage, the elastic modulus of graded foams is the average of all component cells for any gradient distribution.

As seen from Fig.15.21, the graded foam M1-NG and uniform foam U10 possesses a similar collapse stage (the first peak stress after the elastic stage). When the foams begin to collapse, the graded foams always collapse initially in their weakest layer (cell up to 10 MPa). As result, the collapse stress of graded foam depends on the strength of the weakest layer.

For the plateau stage, uniform foams exhibit a constant plateau stage where graded foams M1-NG exhibit a gradually increasing plateau stage (Fig. 15.21). This phenomenon can be explained by the successive deformation mode of graded foams since they always deform from the weakest layer to the strongest one. When a weaker layer is compressed, the mechanical response would stay at the plateau of the current layer. After the weaker layer totally collapses, a stronger layer continues to sustain the applied load, and the mechanical response follows a new plateau stage in the current layer. This behavior behaves continuously from the weakest region to the strongest region, gradually increasing stress with increasing nominal strain.
