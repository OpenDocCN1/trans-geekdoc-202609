## 15.3 Quasi-static results and discussion

### 15.3.1 Repeatability and characteristic mechanical parameters

Typical compressive stress–strain curves of foams are demonstrated in Fig. 15.7. For uniform foams (Fig. 15.7A), the stress–strain curves show a long plateau stage similar to that of conventional foams. Nevertheless, for graded foams in the curves, there is an unusual strain hardening rather than a plateau stage in the curves, which introduces a deformation mechanism which will be discussed in detail Section 15.3. Zeng et al. [18] also found this hardening behavior in their foam materials with discrete gradients (four uniform-foam layers). The stress–strain curves of both uniform and graded foams have little deviation, indicating excellent reproducibility of experimental results and the produced specimens.

The mechanical parameters of foam materials, especially the plateau stress and densification strain, are essential to evaluate compressive properties. Hence, it is necessary to determine them by detailed analysis. As shown in Fig. 15.8, the elastic modulus $E$ is the slope at the elastic stage. The collapse stress $\sigma_{cr}$ is the first peak value after the plateau stage and the corresponding strain is the collapse strain $\varepsilon_{cr}$. The plateau stress $\sigma_{pl}$ is defined as the average value of stress between the collapse and densification strain. The energy absorption method [3] is used to determine the densification strain $\varepsilon_D$. The energy absorption per unit volume $W$ is given by:

$W = \int_{0} \sigma(z)dz.$

The energy absorption efficiency $\eta$ is defined as the energy absorption up to a given nominal strain $\varepsilon_a$ divided by the stress value, which is expressed as:

$\eta = \frac{\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_a}\sigma(z)dz}{\sigma(\varepsilon_a)}$
