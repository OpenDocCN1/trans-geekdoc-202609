Duan et al. [65] employed SLM to produce steel 316 L plate lattices. It should be noted that since plates have closed-cell, it is difficult to directly produce such lattices with powder bed or liquid-based additive manufacturing technologies. Therefore in Fig. 14.19, the authors made a few small holes in the plate faces to allow removing the closed cell after manufacturing. Further experiments show that for any novel class of plate lattice, elastically isotropic could be achieved for a wide range of relative densities. Moreover, the proposed plate lattices exhibit superior mechanical performance over shell lattices and truss lattices.

Andrew et al. [66] fabricated three elementary plate lattices including SC, BCC, and FCC as well as hybrid plate lattices (SC-BCC, SC-FCC, and SC-BCC-FCC) using FFF technique. They found that the SC-BCC-FCC plate lattice had the best response as each constituent in the lattice contributed to the load-carrying capacity. The combination of additive manufacturing and micro-architected cellular solids promotes the development of metamaterials with unprecedented properties (Fig. 14.20).
