As shown in Fig. 4.3D–F, with the decrease of boundary spring stiffness, the influence of porosity on natural frequencies gradually converges. Even the natural frequencies increase with the increase of porosity coefficients when the circumferential wave number is small (see Fig. 4.3E and F). At the same time, for large wave numbers, the influence of porosity on natural frequencies is also slight.

Fig. 4 illustrates the effect of boundary conditions on the variation of natural versus porosity coefficient. Under C-C, C-S, $k_c=10^{10}$ and $k_s=10^8$ boundary conditions, but for C-F and S-F boundary conditions, the natural frequencies have little change. This shows that the reduction of natural frequencies on account of porosity coefficient is closely associated with boundary conditions.
