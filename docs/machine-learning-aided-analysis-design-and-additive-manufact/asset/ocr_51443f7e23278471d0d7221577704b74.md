When the DCPCP method is applied to fabricate the complex lightweight concrete at a large scale, topology optimization is frequently incorporated at the design stage prior to the practical fabrication in order to achieve an optimized material layout within the structure [80,81]. The most representative example is the concrete structure developed by Vanthem et al., who proposed an integrated fabrication strategy consisting of layer-deposition 3DCP process, topology optimization, and postconditioning of concrete structure [82]. The overall structure part was subdivided into several parts. The code of each subdivided part was generated and sent to the robotic arm as the guiding of the toothbrushpath. at the printing speed of 80mm/s, with the printing duration lasting 24 hours for the completion of all parts. Finally, all printed parts were assembled, and joined using posttensioning methods, with the interior filled with grout mortar. When it comes to Fachwerk-based Concrete Fabrication, it has been utilized to fabricate lightweight cellular concrete structures with porous architecture. According to Nguyen-Van et al., cellular concrete structures with the unit of TPMS and lattice, achieving 50% volume of fraction reduction, could be successfully fabricated via the Fachwerk-based method [6]. The fabrication procedures consist of two steps: (1) 3D printing of PLA molds containing different unit cell patterns by FDM, with the wall thickness and overall dimension of 1 mm and 100 ×100 ×100 mm, respectively; (2) filling the printed molds with Flowable cement-based mortar and curing for 28 days.
