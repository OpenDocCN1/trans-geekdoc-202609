
## 14.2.3 Vat photopolymerization

Vat photopolymerization generally aims at base materials of photosensitive resins. Under ultraviolet light, the resin is polymerized rapidly to complete the manufacturing-photopolymerization. Stereolithography (SLA) is the most representative technology in vat photopolymerization. As shown in Fig. 14.8, the system is composed of a liquid photopolymer, fabrication platform, laser source, and scanner system. The manufacturing process is similar to the SLS shown in Fig. 14.5. The difference is that the powder base material is replaced by the liquid resin base. The big-advantage of SLA is that it can produce high-resolution (submicron-level) material. However, two disadvantages of SLA exist, where the strength of the base material is low, and the residual photinitiator and uncured resin are of cytotoxicity.

### 14.3.1 Open-cell foams

The relative density of open-cell foams [44–46] is generally lower than that of closed-cell foams. Due to the lack of support from cell-wall, the ability of impact resistance and energy absorption of open-cell foams is generally weaker than that of closed-cell foams. However, open-cell foam has unique advantages in noise reduction and biological applicability, this has been widely considered by different research groups [47–52].

Nava et al. [44] filled spheres in a cylindrical container to produce open-cell foams, represented by the area spheres. They reversely engineered the 3D model by inverting X-ray computed tomography. By adjusting the size of the gate, open-cell foams with different porosities could be produced. Finally, metal additive manufacturing was used to prepare the open-cell foam according to the predictions to obtain the final specimens with different porosities (as shown in Fig. 14.9). The authors carried out quasi-static compression tests on these specimens. The relationships between elastic modulus and yield strength with relative density were achieved, and the corresponding Gibson-Ashby relations were fitted.

With Ray CT, the distribution of collapse and fracture of open cell foams was obtained. We found that the traditional foam had more ligament collapse without rupture. The foams produced by PBF were more brittle than the traditional foam.

Wang et al [46] used different base materials including polyurethane, polyamideide 12, and polyamide to produce addively manufactured open-cell foams. They studied the macro mechanical properties and deformation mechanism of species under quasi-static loading. The Voronoi diagram is a typical method to generate random cells, whose process is similar to the physical growth of traditional foams. In the Voronoi diagram, a 3D finite space with $V_0$ volume is first established, the set of $N$ nuclei is created in this space by one. If $N$ nuclei distribute in the $V_0$ space uniformly, a fully regular Kelvin foam (also tetrakaidecahedral foam) will be created, and the distance between adjacent nuclei in a Kelvin foam is given by:

$$d_0 = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2} \sqrt[3]{\frac{V_0}{N}}$$

### 14.3.2 Closed-cell foams

Closed-cell foams [53–55] have been widely used in various applications including protective structures, packaging, and structural function, due to their good energy absorption capacity, high strength/weight ratio, and outstanding tailorability [56–58].

Virat et al. [53] studied the relative density and loading rate on the compressive stress, deformation pattern, and energy absorption of Kelvin foams. As shown in Fig. 14.2, the 3D computer-aided drawing (CAD) data of cellular structures with Kelvin cells were first generated. Then, CAD models of specimens with different-cell thicknesses were converted into STL formats for additive manufacturing. Kelvin foams were tested by a universal testing machine for quasi-static loading and a direct-impact Hopkins Pressure Bar for dynamic loading. Four deformation modes were observed in experiments for the specimens with different relative densities and at different loading rates. Further numerical results indicated the presence of a critical relative density, below which the Kelvin foams deformed primarily by cell edges bending and beyond which the cells membranes deformed.
