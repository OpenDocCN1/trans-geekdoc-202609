However, most research works focus on foam materials with linear or monotonic gradient distribution, while the nonlinear or nonmonotonic graded foams remain to be explored. This motivates the present work to investigate two graded foams with two kinds of gradient distribution.

On the hand, for graded foams with a monotonic gradient, the gradient parameter M plays a crucial role at the plateau stage. Compressive stress-strain curves of graded foams with different gradient parameters are shown in Fig. 15.11A, and the zoom-in plots of curves $\epsilon=0.1–0.4$ and $\epsilon=0.4–0.7$ are shown in Fig. 15.11B and C. Specimen with higher gradient parameters such as 'M5' and 'M10' have more cells with maximum and minimum sizes. These graded foams collapse initially in the region with maximum size (the weakest region) with lower strengths. The deformation extends to the region with maximum size (the strongest region) presenting higher strengths at the end of the plateau stage. In addition, although the specimens with positive and negative gradients are tested, there is a slight difference in their corresponding stress–strain curves (Fig. 15.11A). This can be explained as a balance in the force between the loading and speeds under quasi-static compression.
