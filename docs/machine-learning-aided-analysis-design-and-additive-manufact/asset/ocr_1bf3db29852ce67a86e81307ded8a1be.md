In the second examination and analysis example, effects of shear and thickness stretching on the nondimensional axial and transverse displacements of the S-S FG beam are evaluated in Fig. 8.6, for (L/H=5) and (p=5). It is obvious from these that the shear effect is more important than the two displacements, and it is greater than the effect of thickness stretching for the transverse displacement. It means that the inclusion of shear deformation effect leads to an increase in the deformations and more for short beams. So, the shear on the displacements cannot be neglected, especially for the thick beams.

In Fig. 8.7A and B, the transverse displacement W(L/2) at mid-span through the FG beam under uniform load is presented for (L/H=5) and (p=5). A slight difference appears for this shortest. It is seen that the maximum displacement is at the bottom of the beam for axial displacement and the median plane for transverse displacement. This is due to the consideration of the thickness stretching (ε≠0).

Fig. 8.8 illustrates the evaluation of the nondimensional transverse shear stress and Fig. 8.8B shows the stresses due to the thickness stretching versus the power-law index for (L/H=5). All curves display the material index dependence of the stresses. It is because stress due to the thickness stretching exhibits high but not negligible values compared with those of the transverse shear stress.
