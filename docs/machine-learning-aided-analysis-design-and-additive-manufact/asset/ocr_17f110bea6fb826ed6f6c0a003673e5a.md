As an emerging material manufacturing technique, additive manufacturing has a wide range of applications, which complements the shortcomings of the traditional manufacturing technique. The combination of cellular solids and additive manufacturing provides a new research direction for key scientific fields such as aerospace, air transport, biomedicine, and so on. To this end, authors summarize potential future research topics as follows.

First of all, additive manufacturing is not an omnotent technology and cannot substitute the traditional manufacturing technique. Furthermore, the additive manufacturing still has several disadvantages, including high cost, low densification, and poor strength and toughness of the produced materials. Hence, combining cellular and traditional manufacturing, understanding cost, material properties, and design flexibility, is an important problem to be solved.

Secondly, cellular solids are used as an energy-absorbing material in impact protection. Although cellular solids have higher specific stiffness and strength, their absolute stiffness is lower than that of dense materials. In engineering applications with energy absorption, cellular solids are generally combined with dense metals or ceramics to form energy-absorbing components, such as thin-walled and sand plates. The cellular solid takes advantage of large plastic deformation and energy absorption, and the dense metal or ceramic materials the characteristics of elastic resistance and structural support. This energy-absorbing component can achieve optimal impact protection. So, the research on additively manufactured cellular solids mostly focuses on the material level, whereas research on additively manufactured cellular solids on the component or structure level remains limited.

Finally, the application of combining cellular solids and additive manufacturing needs to be further explored. For example, 4D printing [101,102] and intelligent manufacturing [103,104] can be integrated into the current research. Recently, a class of robotic metamables capable of moving with multiple degrees of freedom was designed and manufactured [105]. This work integrated piezoceramic, metallic, and structural materials into a 3D architecture and the robotic metamaterials could actively sense and move. By combining smart metamaterials and topology optimization design, the application of additively manufactured cellular solids can be further expanded. Nevertheless, extensive investigations are needed in this area.

# 14.5 Conclusions

In summary, starting from the state-of-art applications and research limitations of cellular solids, this chapter clarifies the necessity of research on cellular solids combined with additive manufacturing. Currently, traditional manufacturing technique fails to meet the design flexibility of cellular solids, making additive manufacturing technology urgently needed to break the shackles of material preparation.
