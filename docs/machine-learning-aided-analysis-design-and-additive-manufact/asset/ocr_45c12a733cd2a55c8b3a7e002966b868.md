# 11.4.4 The extended support vector regression

The conventional support machine vector (SVM) was proposed by Vapnik [7] and has been implemented into various forms with characteristics along the newly developed X-SVR approach. For input and output vectors of **x** and **y**, the governing equation for determining the hyperplanes separating two distinguish types of objects is described as:

$$\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{wx}^T - \gamma$

(11.35)

where $\mathbf{w}=[w_1, w_2, ..., w_n]^T \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the perpendicular vector against the hyper plane; $\gammagamma is the bias.

Based on the research of Feng et al. [8-10], the X-SVR approach was proposed based on the doubly regularized support vector regression and the regression method. For input and output vectors of **x** and **y**, the X-SVR creates a hyperface($\mathbf{x}$) to describe the relationship between the datapoints:

$$\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})=k(\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{q}_j)^T - \tilde{e}_i^T G_{ij} \tilde{u}_j$

(11.36)

where $k(\)$ is the kernel-induced vector for the inputs, $\mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{q}_j \in \mathbb{R}^n$ are the two positive kernelized coefficients, $\tilde{u}_j$ is the concerned optimal solution, matrix $G_{ij}$ and vector $\tilde{e}_i$ are defined by:
