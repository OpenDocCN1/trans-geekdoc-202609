## 3.1 Introduction

Functionally graded porous (FGP) plates are regarded as new types of lightweight structural elements, and have superior mechanical properties, of which the material properties can vary in a continuous gradient along desired directions. FGP plates for and forced vibrations of functionally graded porous quadrilateral plates with curved edges are generally made of materials with porosity, and reasonable material materials are obtained by regulating the porosity parameters and distributions. As a result, FGP plates have been widely utilized in engineering areas requiring lightweight, extreme temperature, high speed and strong impact resistance, as bullet trains, space equipment, and advanced industries [1–3]. In order to promote the mechanical dynamic characteristics, the foundation and key are exploring the vibration mechanism of FGP plates. Since the shapes of FGP plates are always irregular, it is meaningful in theoretical and engineering fields to develop a unified approach to analyzing the vibration behaviors of FGP plates with complex edges.

A number of institutions have developed methods to predict the vibration characteristics of FGP structures. Barati et al. [9] studied electro-mechanical vibrational behavior of functionally graded piezoelectric plates via a fixed four-variable plate theory for the first time. Cuong Le et al. [10] performed a three-dimensional (3D) numerical solution to the free vibration and buckling of FGP structures, showed that porosity of the FGP structures has influence on free vibration and buckling response. With the work of the first deformation theory (FSDT), Ebrahim et al. [11] analyzed the vibration wave of FGP cylinders with regard to parameters containing porosity coefficient, length-thickness ratio, radius-to-thickness ratio, circumferential wave number, and properties of elastic foundation. FSDT analyzed by Parsani et al. [12] for prediction of the free vibrations of FGP plates in contact with fluid, side-to-thickness ratio, length-to-width (aspect) ratio were analyzed. Foroutan et al. [13] developed a formulation of a fully nonlinear refined plate theory in a total Lagrangian approach to estimate the postbuckling and deflection of a sandwich FGP plate. Gao et al. [14] studied the characteristics of FGP sandwich plates with viscoelastic square-celled core. Gong [15] developed a cell-vertex finite method for analyzing FGP structures with consideration of thermal environment. Results show that the distribution of the porosity has a weak influence on the temperature and the deformation, but obviously affects the thermal stress. Hung et al. [16] presented an analytical approach for nonlinear buckling and postbuckling of FGP plate shells of which thickness varies. Taking three kinds of porosity distributions are investigated, and the corresponding results show that the parameters of porosity distribution, geometric, and foundation make remarkable effects on the mechanical behavior of the FGP toroidal shell. Kumar et al. [17] investigated the static and dynamic behavior of the exponential FGP material under the thermal-electromechanical coupled loads. The motion equations are deated by means of FSDT and Hamilton's principle. The accuracy of their method is validated compared to the existing literature. As a consequence, the contribution of materials parameters, geometric porosities, and boundary conditions to structural dynamic performance are accounted for. Liu et al. [18] analyzed the dynamic behaviors of FGP plate reinforced by graphene platelets and surrounded by elastic foundation with elastic boundary constraints. Four types of materials along with the thickness are taken into account the work of FSDT and the misegment partition technique. Jacobi polynomials are employed to express the displacement fields of any segment. Numerical results for the dynamics of the FGP plate is obtained by the Rayleigh-Ritz method, and the convergence and robustness are tested by comparing the results to literature and FEM. Finally, sets of parametric analyses are carried out to observe the structural vibrations, including the free vibration, transient, and steadystate response. [19] were the first to use the displacement-based and stress-based approaches to the nonlinear bending response of FGP plates based on FSDT. Three configurations of porosity distributions including nonuniform, symmetric, and nonuniform asymmetric are selected. The Galerkin method and Fourier series are applied to solve the nonlinear algebraic governing equations, and the solutions are obtained by means of Newton-Raphson method. Results obtained provide a validation for correctness and accuracy of the method. In the end, parametric analyses are conducted to assess the impacts of the porosity coefficient and distribution, geometric parameters foundation parameters, and boundary conditions on the mechanical performance. Lists, lots of papers about FGP structures have been published [20–28].

The aforementioned papers are almost concerned with certain structure; however, in order to develop adequate approaches for vibration solution of structures with various shapes, some researchers conducted a series of theoretical explorations and published comprehensive achievements. Liu et al. [29] utilized the FSDT and Chebyshev differential method (CDQM) to model plates with curved edges. By introducing the one-to-one mapping technology into CDQM, the structural displacement fields can be expressed in terms of Chebyshev polynomials nowadays. Abundant comparisons among the results with CDQM and results obtained by experiment, FEM, and existing methods show the accuracy and efficiency of CDQM. Zhong et al. [26] applied to clarify the free vibration mechanism for plates with curved edges considering irregular hole and curved corner. Tornabebe et al. [30–36] utilized and developed isogeometric and Generalized Differential Quadrature methods to investigate structures of complex edges and corners. In order to overcome the difficulty of isogeometric method on with Dirichlet boundary conditions, Liu et al. [37] developed new functions for an isogeometric approach to predict the vibration of thin plates. By presenting a method that transforms the tensor product on the basis of triangular plates to area coordinates, the singularity problem at edges eliminated. Based on the above, Xue et al. [22] investigated the free vibration of FGP structures including square plates, circular plates, and rectangular plates with a hole by employing the isogeometric analysis (IGA) and FSDT.
