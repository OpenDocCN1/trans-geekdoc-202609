## Free and forced vibrations of functionally graded porous cylindrical shells

### ### 4.1 Introduction

Metal foams, as a kind of low density materials, are getting more and more attention on account of their low density, prominent energy absorption, and high thermal conductivity [1,2]. Because the size of the inner pores can be tailored, it is possible to form functionally graded (FG) porous structures, which have excellent mechanical properties with homogeneous structures [3-5].

An important kind of engineering structures, circular cylindrical shells have excellent mechanical performance as lightweight and high strength. Thus they have been widely used in the shipbuilding industry, aerospace industry, machinery manufacturing industry, construction, etc. There are many detailed and comprehensive [6-9] on basic theories and solution methods of circular cylindrical shells.

Studies on vibration of circular cylindrical shells have been extensively extended. Soldiers and Hadidegroud [10] solved the free vibration of isotropic cylindrical shells and under singly supported boundary conditions. Chung [11] investigated the free vibration of circular cylindrical shells with classical boundary conditions by introducing Fourier series. Paliwal et al. [12] analyzed the free vibration characteristics of circular cylindrical shells on Winkler and Paternak foundations. By using the Hamilton's variational principle, Salhah and Morehab [13] proposed a technique for vibration analysis of cylindrical shells under harmonic forces. By the domain decomposition technique, Qu et al. [14] analyzed the vibration of uniform and stepped cylindrical shells with boundary conditions. Go et al. [15] developed the simplistic wave-based method for steady-state forced vibration of cylindrical shells with different boundary conditions.

Fakuwa et al. [16] studied the induced vibration response of spinning cylindrical shells with radial bearing supports and the vibration characteristics of the shells were verified by frequency response testing. The above studies focus on the linear free vibration of cylindrical shells mostly focus on the classical boundary conditions.

It is known that circular cylindrical shells are often subjected to external forces and vibrate with large amplitudes, which needs to be described using nonlinear shell theories. Therefore many studies concerning the nonlinear vibration of circular cylindrical shells are conducted [17–24]. Chai et al. [25] studied the nonlinear vibration characteristics of spanning cylindrical shells with arbitrary boundary conditions. The effect of viscosity on the internal resonance of cylindrical shells was analyzed by Rosikhin and Shitnikova [26]. Under clamped boundary condition and uniform temperature change, Zhang et al. [27] carried out the analysis on the nonlinear dynamics of FG circular cylindrical shells. The 1:1:2 and 1:1:2 internal behaviors of cylindrical shells were analyzed by Amabili [28] and Ye and Wang [29], respectively.

In this chapter, free and forced vibrations of FG circular cylindrical shells are presented, including linear and nonlinear analyses. Arbitrary boundary conditions are realized by introducing the artificial spring technology. The Donnell's shell theory is employed, and the Lagrange equation is adopted to derive the equations of FG circular cylindrical shells. Chebyshev polynomials are utilized to approximate the displacement of the shell. The Ritz method and the modal superposition method are used to solve the natural frequency and frequency response, respectively. Finally, nonlinear free and forced vibrations are solved using the method of multiple scales and the pseudo-arclength continuation technique, respectively.
