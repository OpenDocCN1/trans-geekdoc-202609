Fully graded lattice structures produced by additive manufacturing are promising bone tissue engineering, which can mimic the native bone in terms of its structure and mechanical properties. Onal et al. [39] introduced a concept of generating continuous gradient structures and found that scaffold can be tailored to fulfill the mechanical properties and simultaneously improve biological response. Surmeneva et al. [40], based on the ideas of bio-inspired metallic bone to treat the segmental defects, developed the regular lattices and optimized the geometrical parameters of open-porous Ti6Al4V scaffold to match the porosity of the natural human bone (Fig. 17.13). In addition, Hindly et al. [41] proposed a 3D-printed FGP titanium alloy for the application of implantable materials and studied the influence of porosity distribution through comparing the characteristics of fully dense sample and two gradient lattice structures with a dense core and a different diameter porous lattice structure surrounding (Fig. 17.14). The results demonstrated that the Young’s modulus of graded sample matched very well with with that of bone.
