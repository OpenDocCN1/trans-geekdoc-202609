## 9.1 Introduction

Cellular materials are found extensively in nature [1,2] and have been widely used for high-performance devices and systems as sandwich panels and protective damping systems. In the past decade, a research frontier of cellular materials known as instability-based architected materials has created unusual properties such as negative Poisson's ratio [3–6]. Specifically, architected materials are rationally designed materials that enable complex geometry and assembly of unit cells. Such programmability has been implemented at multiple scales [7–11]. However, most studies have only focused on the uniform and periodic terms. Recent advances in digital fabrication have made it possible to design and manufacture materials with arbitrary architectures that enable nonuniform properties and tunable responses can be achieved. Asymmetry of assembly, architecture, and nonuniform cells to enable target function at the structural level is trivial to design and such structural complexity will be a continuous challenge in the fields of architected materials [12].

As the design of geometric patterns becomes complex, it is impossible for the conventional intuition-based method to find feasible patterns of desired properties across the entire domain. Motivated by these recent developments, we take initiative to apply machine learning (ML) techniques to accelerate the design and characterization of cellular materials. Emerging ML have revolutionized the way of doing research across all fields of study [13,14]. In material science, computation-based approaches help to gain insights into material behaviors and properties. Thanks to recent advances in computational power and robust algorithms, we have witnessed the rise of an undiscovered field in computational science [15,16]. Specifically, researchers have already shown success in using ML on two major categories: (1) to accelerate the prediction of material properties for specific applications [17–21], and (2) to accelerate the on-demand design and the optimization of material microstructure and composition for targeted properties [22–26]. These promising studies have shown superior effectiveness of ML techniques compared to traditional computational modeling or experimental tests on a variety of materials.

In this chapter, we investigate the spatial heterogeneity and resulting mechanical properties of nonuniform unit squared cells. Our baseline geometry includes $3 \times 3$ and $4 \times 4$ square-shaped cellular materials with cross infills as reinforcement architected material. We discuss the complexity of geometric design with $3 \times 3$ units and then used $4 \times 4$ units as a demonstration for ML. An ML-based framework is developed to predict mechanical responses to nonuniform geometries.

### 9.2.3 Neural network parameters and architecture

In this work, we use machine learning to solve problems. Once properly trained, the model is capable of predicting results with less computational cost. The network architecture has become more applicable in recent years due to (1) the invention of new training/learning methods, (2) training with large datasets, and (3) the advantage of faster computer hardware such as GPUs. In this study, the DNN has five layers, and the number of neurons in each layer is given in Fig.9.2B. We built the DNN in MATLAB using the stochastic gradient backpropagation. Using the hyperbolic tangent function, the output (uniformly sampled forces) are scaled to $[-0.9, 0.9]$ for better performance.

## 9.3 Analysis and prediction results

### 9.3.1 Response classification of $3 \times 3$ units

As mentioned in Section 9.2.2, a total of 168 simulations were run. To classify the response within the domain, we observed that 48,960 counterparts share identical responses, which we called "reproduced patterns." It is noted that the training data contains both the unique patterns and their reproduced patterns because the DNN does not naturally "know" that unique patterns and their counterparts should have an identical response. After the preparation, we feed the training data (pattern-response) to the DNN and used the test data to verify the performance of the training.
