## 16.5 A predictive model of graded foam-filled tubes

### 16.5.1 The existing predictive model of foam-filled tubes

As mentioned above, the bearing capacity of FFT is supported by three components: thin-walled tube, foam, and the interaction between the two. Therefore a model is developed to predict the compressive force of FFTs, where the thin-walled tube part is related to its configuration, the foam part is related to its relative density, and the interaction part is related to the bearing capacity of both the thin-walled tube, foam. Hansen et al. [25] have proposed a widely predictive model for the compressive force of FFTs as follows:

$$F_{avg} = F_{avg}^0 + \sigma_t \pi \left(\frac{b_n}{2}\right)^2 + C_{avg}\sqrt{\sigma_t\sigma_b}bm \quad (16.1)$$

$F_{avg}^0$ is the contribution of the empty-walled tube, the middle term and the last term describe the resistance of individual foam and the interaction effect, respectively. $b$ and $h$ are the outer cross-section diameter and the wall thickness of the tube, respectively. $b_m=b-h$ and $h_m=b-2h$ are the middle cross-section diameter and the diameter of the foam, respectively. $\sigma_t$ is the plateau stress of foam. $\sigma_b$ is the characteristic stress of tube material and is defined as the average of yield and ultimate stress of tube material.

### 16.5.2 The new predictive model of graded foam-filled tubes

Although Eq. (16.1) offers a reliable method to predict the average compressive force of FFTs, it is difficult to apply this model directly to GFFT. As shown in Eq. 16.8, GF has a gradually increasing compressive strength as pressure proceeds. The strength of foam in Eq. (16.1) varies with increasing strain. For GFFT, this chapter proposes the following predictive model:

$$F_{GFFT} = F_{avg}^0 + \sigma^* \pi \left(\frac{b_n}{2}\right)^2 + C_{avg}\sqrt{\sigma^*\sigma_b}bm \quad (16.2)$$

The first term of Eq. (16.2) can be written as [25]:

$$F_{avg}^0 = \kappa_D \sigma_b bm^{1/3}h^{5/3} \quad (16.3)$$

$\kappa_D$ is a dimensionless constant for an empty-walled tube showing deformation method. In this chapter, $\sigma_0, b_m,$ and $h$ are identified as 52.86 MPa, 60.4 mm, and 0.4 mm, respectively. $\kappa_D$ can be determined as 2.572 by fitting data. In addition, it is found that the plateau stress obeys a power law with the density of foams [34,51]. By fitting the experimental data into power-law equations, Eq. (16.4) can be obtained:

$$\frac{\sigma_{ys}^*}{\sigma_{ys}} = 0.903 \left(\frac{\rho^*}{\rho_l}\right)^{1.787} \quad (16.4)$$

$\sigma_{ys}^*$ and $\sigma_{ys}$ are respectively the plateau stress of foams and the base materials. The yield stress $\sigma_{ys}$ is 52.86 MPa. Here, the energy absorption direction method is used to determine the plateau stress of UFs. The energy absorption efficiency is defined as the absorbed energy up to a nominal strain $\varepsilon_a$ divided by the corresponding stress $\sigma(\varepsilon_a)$ as follows:

$$\eta(\varepsilon_a) = \frac{\int_0^{\varepsilon_a}\sigma(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon}{\sigma(\varepsilon_a)} \quad (16.5)$$

As shown in Fig. 16.13, as the energy absorption efficiency reaches a maximum, the corresponding strain value is defined as the densification strain $\varepsilon_D$. Then the plateau stress $\sigma_{pl}$ is determined by:

$$\sigma_{pl} = \frac{\int_0^{\varepsilon_{cr}}\sigma(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_D - \varepsilon_{cr}} \quad (16.6)$$

Eq. (16.4) gives the relationship between the stress and the relative density of uniform foams. According to Section 15.5.2 of Chapter 15, the relationship between the nominal strain and the relative density gradient of graded foams can be determined by $\rho(\rho^{-1}(\varepsilon))$. Hence, for GF, the compressive stress is defined as:

$$\sigma^*(\varepsilon) = \sigma_{pl}(\rho(\rho^{-1}(\varepsilon))) \quad (16.7)$$

Combining Eqs. (16.2), (16.3), and (16.7), the compressive force of GFFT can be calculated as follows:

$$F_{GFT} = k_0\sigma_{0m}b_m^{3/h/3} + \sigma_p^f(\rho^{f-1}(c))\left(\frac{b_h}{2}\right)^2 + C_{avg}\sqrt{\sigma_p^f(\rho^{f-1}(c))\sigma_q^q}b_h \quad (16.8)$$

The interaction coefficient $C_{avg}$ is related to the degree of interface bonding between foam and tube. In this chapter, the FFT is regarded as an ideal identity and the interaction coefficient $C_{avg}$ is found to obey a power law with the relative density of foam as $C_{avg} = 251.991^{1.925}$. To verify the accuracy of the predictive model, the specimen GFFT-6-12 is validated. As shown in Fig. 16.14, the predictive model proposed herein captures the increasing strength.

## 16.6 Conclusions

This chapter carries out experimental and numerical studies on both UFFTs with different densities and GFFTs with different gradient configurations. The internal deformation mechanism of interaction between foam and tube is elaborated. Results show FFT has a higher load-bearing capacity compared with the sum of foam and foam used alone to the interaction between the two. The interaction includes the extrusion of the buckled tube to foam and the support of densified foam to the tube. Since more foam material is involved in the interaction, FFTs with higher-density infiltrated foams possess higher compressive force and more obvious hardening. In addition, although GF and UF possess similar densities, FFT shows an increasing strength rather than a plateau. A predictive model including three terms (tube, foam, and their interaction) is proposed for Application of actively manufactured functionally graded porous structures
