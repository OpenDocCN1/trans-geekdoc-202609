By introducing the coordinate transformations, the plate with complex curved edges can be transformed into a computational domain with both varieties along two axial directions limited in the interval of $[-1, 1]$. According to the chain rule of differentiation, relationships between the first partial derivatives of function $f$ in the Cartesian $(x, y)$ coordinate system and $f$ in the natural coordinate system $(\xi, \eta)$ it can be given as:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = |J| \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial \xi} \frac{\partial \xi}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial \eta} \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} \right)$$ $(3.17)$$

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = |J| \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial \xi} \frac{\partial \xi}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial \eta} \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial y} \right)$$ $(3.18)$$

$$|J| = \det \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial \xi}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial \xi}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial y} \end{pmatrix}$$

$$J_{11} = \frac{\partial \xi}{\partial \eta} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} N_{ij}(\xi, \eta) x_{ij,\eta}, J_{12} = \frac{\partial \xi}{\partial \xi} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} N_{ij}(\xi, \eta) \eta_{ij}$$ $(3.19)$$

$$J_{21} = \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial \eta} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} N_{ij}(\xi, \eta) x_{ij}, J_{22} = \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial \xi} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} N_{ij}(\xi, \eta) \eta_{ij}$$

In the natural coordinate system, the function $q(\xi, \eta)$ is expressed by Chebyshev polynomials of first order:

$$q(\xi, \eta) \equiv \sum C_k \mathbf{a}_k$$ $(3.20)$$

$$C_k = C_{M-1}(\xi) \otimes C_{N-1}(\eta)$$ $(3.21)$$

$$C_k = \cos{[0 \cdot \cos^{-1}(\xi)], [1 \cdot \cos^{-1}(\xi)] \cdots, \cos[(M-1) \cdot \cos^{-1}(\xi)]}$$ $(3.22)$$

$$C_{N-1}(\eta) = \cos{[0 \cdot \cos^{-1}(\eta)], [1 \cdot \cos^{-1}(\eta)] \cdots, \cos[(N-1) \cdot \cos^{-1}(\eta)]}$$ $(3.23)$$

$$\mathbf{a} = (a_{0,0}, \dots, a_{0,N-1}, a_{M-1,0}, \dots, a_{M-1,N-1})^T$$ $(3.24)$$

where symbol $\otimes$ is the Kronecker product; $\mathbf{a}$ is the coefficient vector of expansion. $M$ and $N$ are the truncation number of polynomials, which separately represent the total terms of polynomials in $\xi$ and $\eta$ directions, in which the superscript of the vector indicates that it is the unknown coefficient of the corresponding displacement.

## 3.2.4 Solving procedure

According to Eqs. (3.10–3.13), the overall energy equation $L$ of the FGP plate can be expressed as

$$L = T - U_i - U_{wp} + W_f \quad (3.33)$$

Substituting Eq. (3.32) in Eq. (3.33), set the partial derivatives of the Lagrangian with respect to the unknown coefficients, one can be obtained as:

$$(\mathbf{K} - \omega^2\mathbf{M})\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{F}$$
$$\mathbf{Q} = [q_1, q_2, q_3, q_4, q_5]^T$$

where $F$ represents the external loading vector, $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{M}$ are the stiffness and mass matrices; $\omega$ and $\mathbf{Q}$ denote the circular frequencies and generalized displacement vector. Combined with the mapping algorithm herein, the stiffness and mass matrices are given as:

$$\mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} M_{11} & & M_{14} \\ M_{12} & M_{25} \\ M_{41} & M_{13} & M_{14} \end{bmatrix} \quad (3.35)$$
