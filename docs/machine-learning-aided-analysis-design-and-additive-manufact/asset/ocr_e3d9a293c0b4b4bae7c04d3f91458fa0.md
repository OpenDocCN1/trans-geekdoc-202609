# # 15.6 Conclusions

This work has presented systematic investigations on the quasi-static and dynamic compressive behaviors of additively manufactured uniform and graded foams. The Voronoi-graded foams with predetermined density gradients, as well as uniform foams with different relative densities, are successfully fabricated by the additive manufacturing technique. The quasi-static and dynamic crushing behaviors of graded and uniform foams are investigated experimentally. Results indicate that under similar densities, uniform and graded foams have similar mechanical properties at the elastic and densification stages. However, graded foams all exhibit an increasing plateau, rather than a constant one of uniform foams. This is because the density gradient brings deformation mechanisms with a tendency to collapse from the lower-density region and gradually to higher-density regions. In other words, graded foams control stresses of each component layer. Furthermore, this also proposes an elastic-hardening model for predicting the mechanical behaviors of graded foams. Firstly, the relationship between mechanical properties and relative densities of uniform foams is determined. Secondly, the local relative density corresponding to each nominal strain is obtained through the compression process of a graded foam. Finally, this model is verified by our experimental results. Based on the model derived in this study, the quasi-static and dynamic (20 m/s impact) stress–strain curves of continuously graded foams can be determined without additional experimental work. This avoids a large number of experimental studies on graded foams for different engineering applications.
