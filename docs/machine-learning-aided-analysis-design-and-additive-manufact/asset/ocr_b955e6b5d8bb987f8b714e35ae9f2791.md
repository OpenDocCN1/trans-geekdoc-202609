# 1.3.2 Mechanical analysis

Among many interesting aspects of FG porous composite structures, their superior mechanical behaviors have continuously been the focus of attention. To carry out the corresponding analysis, the effective material properties may serve as a starting point, where various models have been linked to the mesoscopic/microscopic geometric features (relative density mainly) with macroscopic parameters (such as Young’s modulus [49], including Meng–Kipsch model [43], Gibson–Ashley model [6,44], Christensen model [45], Roberts–Garbageci model [46], Chen model [47], and modified rule of mixture [48]. On the basis of these constitutive relations, the variations of material with respect to the graded properties can be identified and employed in the structural assessments within the framework of deformation theories: Tenshenko beam theory [49], first-order shear deformation of plate theory [50]; Domnell shell theory [51]. Consequently, the overall behaviors of FG porous structures are characterized by determining their critical buckling loads [49], static bending deformations [49], natural frequencies [52], forced vibration amplitudes [52]. On the other hand, numerical simulations via finite element (FE) software are often adopted to directly construct the mesoscopic/microscopic features of graded cellular materials which is especially applicable for the energy absorption related to novel porous geometries under impacts [53–55]. Furthermore, various experimental works have been conducted on the manufacturing and mechanical testing of FG porous specimens [32,56,57].

With the aid of these models and methods, the mechanical examination of FG porous composite structures has been an active area with intensive research focusing on the effects from graded properties on the structural performances.
