Garra et al. [60] analyzed the effect of cell patterns on the mechanical properties of honeycomb materials. As shown in Fig. 14,16, the analysis encompassed the 2D cell shapes, including Anti-tracheal, Circular, Hexachiral, Hexagon, Re-entrant Hexagon type I and II, Lozenge, Rotachiral, Sinusoidal Layers, Square Grids, ScroBO and Tetrahedral. The experimental data provided in this paper could further guide the design-for-additive manufacturing strategies with cellular solids.

#  14.3.4 Lattice architectures

Lattice architectures [63–66] have high designability. By optimizing the structural elements, improved mechanical properties, including specific strength and specific stiffness, can be achieved. This makes lattice architectures popular in structural lightweight design [67–75]. Moreover, the mechanical properties of lattice materials are also known as geometric configuration rather than chemical components, lattice materials are also mechanical metamaterials. While traditional manufacturing techniques fail to satisfy the requirement of designing lattice materials with complex configurations, this gap is posed to be filled by additive manufacturing technique [76–82].

Abucciella et al. [63] focused on the cellular solids with tri periodic minimal surfaces (TPMS). They designed three TPMS architectures including Schwarz Primitive, Schoen I-wrapped cubic (IWP), and Neovius as shown in Fig. 14.17. They produced these models using SLS with polyamide 12 as the base material. They reported that IWP and Neovius architectures outperformed others in mechanical properties and both of them had higher stiffness and strength than the Primitive architecture.

TPMS architectures were also investigated by Bonatti et al. [64]. They employed a parameterized surface-smoothing technique to derive three families of lattices including simple cubic (SC), face-centered cubic (FCC), and body-centered cubic (BCC) unit cells. They pointed out that the lattices can provide higher average stiffness, strength, and energy absorption capabilities than truss and lattices. In addition, shell lattices had geometric smoothness and the stress concentrations can be avoided. This better fatigue life and strength were expected to be obtained for shell lattices.
