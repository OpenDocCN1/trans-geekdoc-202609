From 15.16A and B, the Voronoi foams exhibit three stages including the elastic, plastic, and densification stages in their curves. The difference is that the uniform foams have a constant plateau stage while the plateau of graded foams shows an obvious increasing with increasing nominal strain. This finding is consistent with quasi-static results in Section 15.3. The loading process of two impact scenarios follows. In the direct impact test, the impact loading starts at the impact bar-specimen interface and then a stress wave propagates from the impact end to the support of specimens. Since the wave impedance of the support bar is much higher than that of specimens, the stress propagation to the support bar is akin to that of a fixed boundary. According to the stress wave theory, the displacement is zero and the stress is fixed at the boundary. As a result, the initial deformation appears at the specimen-bar interface as shown in Fig. 15.16C (U-8 and U10-D). On the contrary, the impact loading starts at the specimen-bar interface in the Taylor test, for which the direction of stress propagation is the opposite.
