# 9.4 Conclusion

In this chapter, we investigate the response complexity of complex materials caused by the nonuniform geometric design. Through a combined FE modeling and clustering analysis, we discovered the hidden connection between a geometric feature (such as the layout and number of infills) and their mechanical responses for $3 \times 3$ cellular patterns. Guided by learned geometric rules, we further characterized the responses of $4 \times 4$ cellular patterns with varied geometric infills and experimentally verified the linking between geometric features and their mechanical performance. As the design domain becomes larger and failure mechanisms become more complex, we introduce a ML method to accelerate the response of patterns from the testing data using a 20% training ratio. The framework is not limited to certain material architecture of the number of configurations, although we only validated the framework using $4 \times 4$ cells. We believe that Our ML-based framework should be capable of accelerating the material design with properties at the local level and improving the performance and/or functionality at the global level.
