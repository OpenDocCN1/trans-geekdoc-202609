# 7.1 Introduction

Functionally graded porous nanocomposites are a new class of composites by mixing carbon nanofillers, such as carbon nanotubes, fibers, and graphene platelets (GPLs), with open-cell or closed-cell foams in a specific distribution [1]. Compared to traditional materials, the nano-reinforced porous composites present high strength to weight ratio, outstanding energy absorbing capacity, and advantageous physical properties, especially for GPL-based nanocomposites with higher surface area and good dispersion ability [2]. The great potential of functionally graded graphene reinforced (FGGR) polymers in aerospace, civil, biomedical, defense, and automotive has attracted large numbers of research efforts from both academic and industrial sectors.

The mechanical characteristics and structural behavior of functionally graded (FG) porous structures (such as beams [3], plates [4], shells [4], and arches [5]) have been comprehensively investigated on bending, buckling, vibration, and dynamic analysis. Chen et al. [6] studied the free and forced vibration of FG porous beams and found symmetric FG porosity distribution obtains best mechanical performance. The nonlinear dynamic analysis of imperfect FG metal beams was conducted by Gao et al. [7]. Chen et al. [8] also presented and bending analysis of FG porous plate using Chebyshev-Ritz method. Zhao et al. [9] analyzed free vibrations of FG porous rectangular plates with uniform elastic boundary conditions. Gao et al. [10] proposed an analytical method for the primary resonance analysis of cylindrical shells of FG porous materials subjected to a uniformly distributed harmonic load. The free vibration of FG cylinders was performed [11] using finite element methodology.
