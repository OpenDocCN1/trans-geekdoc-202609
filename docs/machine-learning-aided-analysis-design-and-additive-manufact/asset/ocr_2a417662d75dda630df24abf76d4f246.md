Figure 16.6 Deformation evolutions of specimens: (A) empty thin-walled tube, (B) thin-walled tube supported by infilled foam, and (C) individual foam of UF-9. (The red-dotted box indicates the local area of typical deformation.)

Fig. 16.6B, the thin-walled tube supported by infilled foam exhibits a distinct transformation in the deformation pattern from diamond mode to concertina mode. This is because the inner-filled foam restricts the inward displacement of the thin-walled tube, making it difficult to destabilize during the deformation process. Fig. 16.6C shows the deformation evolution of individual foam. As the loading proceeds, due to the irregular distribution of cell structure, the local stresses reach the maximum as compression progresses, the adjacent cell wall begins to buckle. The contact between the walls of buckled cells arrests this thus stabilizing the local microstructure. This leads to the collapse of cells in the neighboring local collapse zone. The process repeats until local collapse spreads to the whole specimen. This causes a nearly constant force level of the plateau region in the force–displacement curves (Fig. 16.5A). Finally, all cells are compacted together and original pores are filled, resulting in the densification stage with steeply rising strength.
