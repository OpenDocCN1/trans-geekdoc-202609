## 17.1 Introduction

Let `z = σ + i t`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si2.png) be a complex number: `σ` is the real part, and `t` is the imaginary part. Let `P` be a set of numbers, called primes: an element of `P` cannot be factored into a product of elements of `P`. In some sense, numbers are to molecules what primes are to atoms. Typically, but not always, `P` is the standard set of all prime numbers, or a subset of it, either finite or infinite. Finally, `p` represents an element of `P` and `χ(p)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si3.png) is any function taking on two possible values: +1 or −1 depending on `p`. The function `χ(·)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si4.png) is extended outside `P` using the formula `χ(ab) = χ(a)χ(b)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si5.png), with `χ(1) = 1`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si6.png).

This chapter summarizes known properties and conjectures about various functions of `z` that can be represented by the following product, called Euler product:

```
\prod_{p \in P} \frac{1}{1 - \chi(p) p^{-z}}.
```

![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si7.png)

The most well-known example is when the function `χ(·)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si4.png) is constant (thus equal to 1) and `P` is the full set of prime integers: this corresponds to the Riemann zeta function `ζ(z)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si8.png). When expanded into a series, the Euler product becomes what is called a Dirichlet series. The series and product may not convergence on the same domain; when the series is conditionally but not **absolutely convergent** [[Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_convergence)] the product can diverge. This typically happens if `σ < 1`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si9.png). It is the source of considerable difficulties, and the reason why the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) is unproven to this day. Also, this explains why all the action takes place when `1/2 ≤ σ < 1`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si10.png).

I will not discuss complex analysis in details here. It is sufficient to know that if `z = σ + i t`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si2.png), then `p^{-z} = exp(-z log p) = p^{-σ} cos(t log p) - i p^{-σ} sin(t log p)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000217/si11.png). Also, the factors in the Euler product are ordered by increasing values of `p`. Without this specification, the product may be subject to multiple interpretations with different values, when convergence is conditional but not absolute. An introduction to the Riemann zeta and Dirichlet functions can be found in [[4]](#Chp017_329-360_B9780443218576000217.xhtml#br0020) and [[18]](#Chp017_329-360_B9780443218576000217.xhtml#br0090). Finally, I occasionally use the term conditionally or absolutely convergent product. This intuitive concept is defined [here](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Infinite_product).
