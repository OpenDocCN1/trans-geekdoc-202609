
The neutral choice `β[0]` = 0![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si13.png) as the starting vector of regression coefficients, for the iterative fixed point algorithm, works well. Another option consists of choosing regression coefficients that preserve the correlation sign between the response `Y`, and each feature `X[1]`, …, `X[m]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si23.png). Here, `X[i]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si48.png) is the `i`th column of the matrix `X`. Let

$c_i = \frac{Cov[Y,X_i]}{Var[X_i]},\quad c = {(c_1,\ldots,c_m)}^{T},\quad Q = \sum_{i=1}^{m}c_i X_i = Xc,$

![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si49.png)

with `ω` a real number chosen to minimize the error `ϵ`^(*T*)*`ϵ` = (`Y` − `ω``Q`)^(*T*)(`Y` − `ω``Q`) = `Y`^(*T*)*`Y` − 2`Q`^(*T*)*`Y``ω` + `Q`^(*T*)*`Q``ω`²![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si50.png). We have

$w = \frac{Y^{T}Q}{Q^{T}Q} = \frac{Y^{T}Xc}{c^{T}X^{T}Xc}\quad\text{and}\quad{Var}[c_i X_i] = {Var}[Y] \cdot \rho^{2}[X_i,Y] = \sigma_{Y}^{2} \cdot \rho^{2}[X_i,Y],$

![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si51.png)

where `ρ` denotes the correlation function. Now, `β[0]` = `ω``c`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si52.png) is a starting point that makes sense, easy to interpret, and better then `β[0]` = 0![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si13.png). It significantly reduces the residual error, over the base model `β` = 0![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si53.png). In many cases, it yields a residual error almost comparable to that of the best predictors.

As an illustration, let us say that `X[2]` = `X[3]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si54.png). You should avoid highly correlated features in your data set, but in some cases the interdependencies among several features are strong but much harder to detect, and result in the same problem. In my example, assuming both `X[2]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si55.png) and `X[3]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si56.png) are positively correlated to the response `Y`, a model with +4 and −2 for the regression coefficients attached to `X[2]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si55.png) and `X[3]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si56.png), performs just as well as −2, +4 or +1, +1. The `β[0]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si7.png) proposed here addresses this issue: it guarantees that the regression coefficients attached to `X[2]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si55.png) and `X[3]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si56.png) are both positive in this example, and identical if `X[2]` = `X[3]`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si54.png). It makes the regression coefficients much easier to interpret. In addition, this technique is numerically stable and more robust.

## 7.3 Damping schedule and convergence acceleration

The `m` × `m`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si5.png) diagonal matrix `Λ^(-1)`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si62.png) in Eq. [(7.2)](#Chp007_113-129_B9780443218576000114.xhtml#deq3) is called the damping parameter, or **preconditioner** [[Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preconditioner)] of the fixed point iteration. It governs the rate of convergence. The fixed point algorithm converges if |`φ`| < 1![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si63.png), where `φ` = `φ`(`S`)[Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si64.png) is the largest eigenvalue of `S` = `I` − `Λ^(-1)``X^T X`![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si65.png), in absolute value. The smaller the |`φ`|![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si66.png), the faster the convergence. The convergence speed is eventually determined by how fast `S^k` → 0![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si10.png) as `k` → ∞![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si11.png), itself being a function of |`φ`|![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si66.png). Thus, it makes sense to choose `Λ` so that `S` is close to zero. One way to do it is to choose `Λ` that minimizes the Frobenius norm of `S` or, in other words, `Λ` that minimizes the sum of the square of the coefficients of `S`. This leads to

$\lambda_i^{- 1} = \frac{1}{m_{ii}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}m_{ij}^{2},\quad i = 1,\ldots,m,$

![Image](images/B9780443218576000114/si67.png)(7.4)
