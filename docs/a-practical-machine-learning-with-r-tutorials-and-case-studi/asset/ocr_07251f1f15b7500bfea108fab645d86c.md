Afterwards, between the hidden and the output layer (see Figure 9.1), these 0 or 1 activities will be multiplied by the weights which do not change for the different observations (they stay 0 or 1), the output of the Neural Network will remain constant regardless of observation.

If this is not bad enough, the optimization process used in Neural Networks (most of the time it varies when the slope of the Gradient Descent algorithm) has difficulties when the slope of activation functions is 0. Consequently, the beta that multiplies the inputs would not get (smaller) during the iterations.

To make a numerical example, you can process two different diamonds through the Neural Network (Clarity₁ = 0.1, Clarity₂ = 5 and Clarity₃ = 0.4, Clarity₄ = 6). Both diamonds will generate $A_1^1=A_2^1=1$ and, despite being different, will have exactly the same predicted output.

> As a rule of thumb: When you get a zero or similar values for predictions for all of your training data, it is often to have a lack of scaling or inappropriate scaling.
