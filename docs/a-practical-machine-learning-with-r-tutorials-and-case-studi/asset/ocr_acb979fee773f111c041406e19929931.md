The output of the str() command shows that the data frame Diamonds contains 53,940 observations and five different variables (columns) that describe properties of the diamonds together with their prices (in U.S.-).
The variables *Clarity, Cut, and Color* chosen as predictors are also known as the four Cs of diamond appraisal:4

+   1. Carat: A measurement used to describe a diamond's weight. It is measured in metric grams (1 carat equal to 0.2 g) and is the most apparent factor when comparing diamonds.
2. Clarity: A measurement for the visibility of natural microscopic inclusions and imperfections within a diamond. Diamonds with little to no inclusions are considered rare and highly valued. The clarity of the diamond is rated in eight categories measuring how clear the diamond is (II worst, SI, SSI, VS1, VS2, VVS1, IF (best)).5
3. Cut: For a diamond's facets, symmetry, and reflective qualities. The cut of a diamond is directly related to its overall sparkle and beauty. The cut of a diamond is rated in five categories as *Fair, Good, Ideal, and Super Ideal.
4. Color: For the natural color or light of color visible in a diamond. The closer the diamond is to "colorless", the more expensive the diamond. Color is rated in seven categories from *D* (best) to *J* (worst).

From the str() command output above, you can see that *Clarity, Cut, and Color* are stored as *R factor-type with 8, 7, and factor-levels-*
