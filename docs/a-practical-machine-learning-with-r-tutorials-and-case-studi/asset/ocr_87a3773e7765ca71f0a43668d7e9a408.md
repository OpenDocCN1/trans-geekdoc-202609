# Predictor Variable Contribution

Question 3: What is a predictor variable's contribution to the predicted outcome of an observation?

We will introduce two techniques quantifying the contribution of predictor variables to a specific prediction: SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) values and the LIME (Local Model-agnostic Explanations) approach.

Section 11.7 introduces SHAP values. SHAP values measure how much predictor variable contributes to the result for an observation.

Section 11.8 introduces LIME. In contrast to SHAP values, LIME does not interpret machine learning models directly. Instead, LIME approximates a machine learning model with a simpler interpretable model (e.g., a linear OLS) in the neighborhood of an observation. The simpler model is then used for interpretation.

In the case of an applicant with the loan, SHAP values and LIME values could provide the following kind of information: Because of the applicant's age, the risk score is higher by 17 units; because of the applicant's low income, the risk score is higher by 5 units, and because the applicant never defaulted on a loan, their risk score is lower by 12 units.
