In the following sections, we will interpret the workflow WfModelRF as a whole (global interpretation), and we will interpret a specific observation of interest (local interpretation). The observation of interest is the wage of a woman (Female = 1) with 17 years of education (Educ = 17) and 18 years of tenure in her current job (Tenure = 18). She has an hourly wage of $88.90.

```
ObsHelga<<ttible(Wage=8.9, Educ=17, Tenure=18,
print(ObsHelga, levels=c(0,1)))
```

To make it address this observation of interest later, we call the women Helga and the related observation ObsHelga.

It is essential to distinguish Helga's actual hourly wage ($88.90) and her predicted hourly wage — the predicted by the Random Forest model. In the following sections, we can only explain Helga's predicted hourly wage (.pred) but not her true wage. Consequently, we have to use the data frame with Helga's predictor variable values (ObsHelga) and augment it with the predicted wage:

```
ObsHelga<-augment(WfModelWageRF, new_data=ObsHelga)
print(ObsHelga)
```

```
## A tible: 1 x 5
## Wage Educ Tenure Female .pred
## dbl dbl dbl <fct>
## 1 8.9 17 18 9.24
```

You can see that Helga's true hourly wage is $88.90, while the Random Forest model predicted a wage of $9.24 (.pred = 9.24).

> This wage seems to be very low, but consider that the wage data are from 1976 (see Shea (2023)).
