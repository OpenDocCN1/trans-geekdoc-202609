### Why Neural Networks Need Large values

When some or all predictor variables contain large values, the effective inputs can also become large. Suppose one of the inputs is the U.S. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) measured in millions of U.S. Dollars ($20,600,000 million for 2022). Such high values multiplied by the weights $\beta$ adds part of the effective inputs for the hidden neurons. Consequently, these effective inputs will also become large even if the absolute $\beta$ values that multiply the GDP are small (e.g., 0.004–0.0002).

You can see the resulting problem in Figure 9.3. Effective inputs smaller than -5 or greater than 5 will impact the activation function at its (almost) horizontal sections, leading to activity values of 0 or 1.

Consequently, for predictor variables with large input values such as the GDP, a Neural Network will likely create activities of either 0 or 1 for all observations. Afterwards, between the hidden and the output layer (see Figure 9.1), these 0 or 1 activities will be multiplied by the weights which do not change for the different observations (they stay 0 or 1), the output of the Neural Network will remain constant regardless of observation.

If this is not bad enough, the optimization process used in Neural Networks (most of the time it varies when the slope of the Gradient Descent algorithm) has difficulties when the slope of activation functions is 0. Consequently, the beta that multiplies the GDP is problematic.
