Google server centers have even achieved a 40% savings in energy consumption by the use of AI to optimize energy consumption [21, 22]. Areas as smart cities and mining have also benefited considerably from the application of AI.

Due to its exponential adoption rate and wide scope, AI has become an enabler for several disruptive technologies related to Industry 4.0. It could lead to unprecedented developments we witnessed before and is considered the brain of industry 4.0. Similar to the benefits which other technologies have obtained from the incorporation of AI, the potential benefits of the integration of AI into Industry 4.0 promises to be immense. Operational costs have reduced significantly and better accuracy and quality have been added to the manufacturing process. The downtime has been minimized thanks the application of predictive maintenance in the supply chain. With enhanced and better tuned equipment, the risk of injuries and other hazards have been significantly reduced on factory floors. In addition to in-factory benefits, businesses which require predictive analytics can benefit from a network of sensors to capture large volumes of data in real-time and analyse the data with ML algorithms [23].

In general, the impact of AI in Industry 4.0 can be categorized into the following four main parts:

+   a) Predictive quality and yield – It uses Artificial Intelligence to reveal the hidden causes of many of the perennial production losses manufacturers face on a daily basis. This is done via continuous, multivariate analysis, using Machine Learning algorithms that are uniquely trained to intimately understand each individual production process. The specific AI/Machine Learning technique used here is termed “supervised learning,” where the algorithm is trained to identify trends and patterns in the data. Automated recommendations and alerts can then be generated to inform production teams and engineers of an imminent problem, and seamlessly share important knowledge on how to prevent the losses before they happen [25].

+   b) Predictive maintenance – One of the most common and fundamental applications of Industrial AI is predictive maintenance. In predictive maintenance, the next breakdown of a machine or system is predicted using algorithms. Maintenance can then be carefully planned and scheduled in advance, based on these predictions, to prevent system failure and at the same time system availability. Essentially, unsupervised machine learning algorithms such as Generalised Additive Models (GAM) and Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM) are used in predictive maintenance.
