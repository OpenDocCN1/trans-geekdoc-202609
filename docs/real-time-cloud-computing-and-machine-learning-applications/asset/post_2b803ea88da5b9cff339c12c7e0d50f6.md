## 1.2.2. Applications of Machine Learning and AI

AI and machine learning have a wide spectrum of applications which include speech and image recognition, traffic prediction, weather forecasting, spam detection, network traffic classification, etc. Some of the most popular ones are summarized as follows:

- **Big Data Analytics** – Big data and AI indeed have a positive synergy in the sense that the more data that are input to a machine learning model, the better the training and hence the output. The main reason behind the close relationship between AI and Big Data is the possibility of integrating AI seamlessly with data analytics. New rules for future business analytics are being formulated by machine learning which is obtaining valuable inputs from each data input. Complex descriptive and predictive analytical tasks can be easily automated and enhanced by users of big data who employ AI. Such tasks would consume a lot of labour and time if performed in the conventional way. For example, it requires the testing of 450 million mathematical models by Google’s search correlation while performing a search. Moreover, billions of pages of translations have to be incorporated into the engine of Google Translate to translate simple English words to French with the highest possible accuracy. Obviously it is impossible for any human to produce the accurate reasoning behind the choices AI software makes by performing these complex computations on such quantities of data [20].

- Industry 4.0 – The Fourth Industrial Revolution and the artificial intelligence at its core are fundamentally changing the way people live, work and interact as citizens. There are several major contributions which AI has made in the industry, educational sector and society in general. Among the most significant contributions in the industry there is diagnostic, system planning and design such as task scheduling on machines. Other prominent industrial applications that have been made possible with the evolution of AI include financial forecasting, airplane landing and optimization of vehicle routes for minimizing delivery costs. Google server centers have even achieved a 40% savings in energy consumption by the use of AI to optimize energy consumption [21, 22]. Areas as smart cities and mining have also benefited considerably from the application of AI.

Due to its exponential adoption rate and wide scope, AI has become an enabler for several disruptive technologies related to Industry 4.0. It could lead to unprecedented developments we witnessed before and is considered the brain of industry 4.0. Similar to the benefits which other technologies have obtained from the incorporation of AI, the potential benefits of the integration of AI into Industry 4.0 promises to be immense. Operational costs have reduced significantly and better accuracy and quality have been added to the manufacturing process. The downtime has been minimized thanks the application of predictive maintenance in the supply chain. With enhanced and better tuned equipment, the risk of injuries and other hazards have been significantly reduced on factory floors. In addition to in-factory benefits, businesses which require predictive analytics can benefit from a network of sensors to capture large volumes of data in real-time and analyse the data with ML algorithms [23].

In general, the impact of AI in Industry 4.0 can be categorized into the following four main parts:

- a) Predictive quality and yield – It uses Artificial Intelligence to reveal the hidden causes of many of the perennial production losses manufacturers face on a daily basis. This is done via continuous, multivariate analysis, using Machine Learning algorithms that are uniquely trained to intimately understand each individual production process. The specific AI/Machine Learning technique used here is termed “supervised learning,” where the algorithm is trained to identify trends and patterns in the data. Automated recommendations and alerts can then be generated to inform production teams and engineers of an imminent problem, and seamlessly share important knowledge on how to prevent the losses before they happen [25].

- b) Predictive maintenance – One of the most common and fundamental applications of Industrial AI is predictive maintenance. In predictive maintenance, the next breakdown of a machine or system is predicted using algorithms. Maintenance can then be carefully planned and scheduled in advance, based on these predictions, to prevent system failure and at the same time system availability. Essentially, unsupervised machine learning algorithms such as Generalised Additive Models (GAM) and Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM) are used in predictive maintenance. Several economic benefits are accrued with predictive maintenance as in the first case it prevents downtimes during critical periods of peak demand and seriously leads to major cost reductions when machines are maintained before breakdown [25].

- Generative design- AI can assist manufacturers in the product design phase. An AI algorithm, commonly referred to as generative design software, can be used to analyze all feasible schemas of a solution, from a precise design brief which is fed to the algorithm as input. The brief can define limitations and specificities of the material types, manufacturing techniques, timeliness factors and financial constraints. Machine learning can be used to test the solution set generated by the algorithm. More information on the designs which worked best and those which failed can be obtained during the testing phase, to subsequently come up with an optimal solution [15].

- Market adaptation/supply chain – The application of AI to Industry 4.0 is not restricted to only the manufacturing processes. Deep AI can be employed across the full spectrum of the Industry 4.0 ecosystem. One example of this is the use of AI in optimizing the supply chain of manufacturing operations. In this way, AI can allow manufacturers to provide a better response to market fluctuations. Several factors such as demand trends sorted according date, location, socio-economic characteristics, macroeconomic performance, political stability, weather forecasts etc., can be used by an algorithm to generate predictions of the market demand. This information can be used in a groundbreaking way by manufacturers to make improved financial decisions with regards to the organisation's goals as well as optimization of inventory control, staff deployment, energy efficiency and processing of raw materials [26].

- Mobile communications – The diversity of service requirements, applications, devices, and networks have led to the evolution of Next-generation wireless networks such as 5G and beyond into very complex systems. Available resources such as power, spectrum, and infrastructures have to be used in an optimum way by Mobile Network Operators (MNOs). Major challenges have been created in wireless communications by the rising demands for capacity, coverage, latency, efficiency flexibility, compatibility, quality of experience and silence convergence. One of the most effective ways of dealing with these challenges is to make use of AI and deep neural algorithms. Deep ML algorithms can be applied to several components of a mobile communication system such as optimization of system architecture, physical and cross-layer signal processing, radio air interface and protocol design, beam-forming and antenna setup, network coding and slicing, cell acquisition and handover, scheduling and rate adaptation, access control, smart proactive caching, modulation and coding as well as adaptive resource allocations.
