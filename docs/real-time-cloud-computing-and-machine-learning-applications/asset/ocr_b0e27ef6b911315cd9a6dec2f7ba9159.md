# Short-hand Example 7

In this example, six cars are grouped into two sets based on their weights and mileages. Then k-NN is used to assign a new car to the most appropriate group. The value of k in this example is set to three. The data about the six cars are in Table 3.13:

# Table 3.13. K-Nearest Neighbour – Car details

| Car | Weight (Pounds) | Mileage (Per Gallon) | Group |
|---|---|---|--|
| A | 1835 | 26 | X |
| B | 1970 | 31 | X |
| C | 2372 | 24 | X |
| D | 3086 | 14 | Y |
| E | 3504 | 18 | Y |
| F | 4341 | 15 | Y |

The new car which needs to be assigned to a group has a weight of 3000 pounds and a mileage of 22 MPG. The Euclidean distances between the new car and the six grouped cars are calculated as shown in Table 3.14:

# Table 3.14. K-Nearest Neighbour – Euclidean distances calculation

| Car | Weight (Pounds) | Mileage (Miles per Gallon) | Group | Euclidean Distance |
|---|---|---|---|--|
| A | 1835 | 26 | X | $\sqrt{(1835-3000)^2 + (26-22)^2} = 1165.0$ |
| B | 1970 | 31 | X | $\sqrt{(1970-3000)^2 + (31-22)^2} = 1030.0$ |
| C | 2372 | 24 | X | $\sqrt{(2372-3000)^2 + (24-22)^2} = 628.0$ |
| D | 3086 | 14 | Y | $\sqrt{(3086-3000)^2 + (14-22)^2} = 86.4$ |
| E | 3504 | 18 | Y | $\sqrt{(3504-3000)^2 + (18-22)^2} = 504.0$ |
| F | 4341 | 15 | Y | $\sqrt{(4341-3000)^2 + (15-22)^2} = 1341.0$ |

Based on the Euclidean distances, the 3 nearest neighbours are cars C, D and E. As the majority of the 3 nearest neighbours are group Y, the new car is assigned to group Y.
