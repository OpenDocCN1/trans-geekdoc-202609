### 3.4.2.2. Convolutional Neural Network

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are advanced neural networks that can learn without the need for manual feature extraction and are widely used in object recognition applications [44]. A CNN is composed of an input layer, multiple hidden layers and an output layer (similar structure to MLPs). The neurons in a CNN layer are high-dimensional filters that are connected with the input of the layer. The CNN perform data classification based on multiple abstract feature representations obtained from the input [45]. This concept is illustrated in Figure 3.24.

#### Short-hand Example 9

In this example, the mechanism of CNNs is demonstrated. An image is fed to the input layer of the CNN which makes use of a filter in its hidden layer which allows vertical edges in the image to be detected [47].

For this example, a single channel 6x6 grayscale image is used. The image in this example is represented by the following image matrix:

```
$M = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & 0 & 5 & 5 \\ 2 & 4 & 7 & 6 & 5 \\ 5 & 5 & 4 & 5 & 3 \\ 7 & 6 & 3 & 5 & 2 \\ 8 & 1 & 2 & 6 & 7 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$
```

The filter in the hidden layer to be applied to the image matrix for detecting vertical edges as follows:

```
$F = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
```

The image matrix is convolved with the filter matrix at the hidden layer:

```
$O = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & 0 & 5 & 5 \\ 2 & 4 & 7 & 6 & 5 \\ 5 & 5 & 4 & 5 & 3 \\ 7 & 6 & 3 & 5 & 2 \\ 8 & 1 & 2 & 6 & 7 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix} * \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
```

The value in the first row and first column of matrix $O$ is equal to the sum of the element-wise product of the elements from first 3 rows and 3 columns from matrix $M$ and matrix $F$ as follows:

```
$$\begin{bmatrix} \color{red}{3\times1 + 1\times0 + 0\times(-1)} & 5 & \color{red}{2\times1 + 4\times0 + 7\times(-1)} & 5 & \color{red}{5\times1 + 5\times0 + 4\times(-1)} & 3 \\ 7 & 6 & 3 & 5 & 2 \\ 8 & 1 & 2 & 6 & 7 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$
```

$O_{1,1} = (3 \times 1) + (1 \times 0) + (0 \times (-1)) + (2 \times 1) + (4 \times 0) + (7 \times (-1)) = 0$

$O_{1,2}$ is calculated by shifting matrix $F$ towards the right over matrix $O$ and the sum of element-wise product of the elements is:

| | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 | 0 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 4 | 7 | 6 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| 7 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| 8 | 1 | 2 | 6 | 7 |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 |

$O_{1,2} = (1 \times 1) + (0 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) + (4 \times 1) + (7 \times 0) + (6 \times (-1)) = -6$

Using the same principle, the remaining values in matrix $O$ are:

$O_{1,3} = (0 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) + (5 \times 1) + (6 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) = -3$

$O_{1,4} = (5 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) + (3 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) = 3$

$O_{2,1} = (2 \times 1) + (4 \times 0) + (7 \times (-1)) + (5 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (4 \times (-1)) = -4$

$O_{2,2} = (4 \times 1) + (7 \times 0) + (6 \times (-1)) + (4 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) = -3$

$O_{2,3} = (7 \times 1) + (6 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) + (5 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) = 5$

$O_{2,4} = (6 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) + (3 \times 1) + (3 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) = 2$

$O_{3,1} = (5 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (4 \times (-1)) + (7 \times 1) + (6 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) = 5$

$O_{3,2} = (5 \times 1) + (4 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) + (6 \times 1) + (3 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) = 1$

$O_{3,3} = (4 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) + (3 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) = 0$

$O_{3,4} = (5 \times 1) + (3 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) + (5 \times 1) + (2 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) = 5$

$O_{4,1} = (7 \times 1) + (6 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) + (8 \times 1) + (1 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) = 10$

$O_{4,2} = (6 \times 1) + (3 \times 0) + (5 \times (-1)) + (1 \times 1) + (2 \times 0) + (6 \times (-1)) = -4$

$O_{4,3} = (3 \times 1) + (5 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) + (2 \times 1) + (6 \times 0) + (7 \times (-1)) = -4$

$O_{4,4} = (5 \times 1) + (2 \times 0) + (2 \times (-1)) + (2 \times 1) + (7 \times 0) + (3 \times (-1)) = 2$

Hence,

```
O = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -6 & -3 & 3 \\ -4 & -3 & 5 & 2 \\ 5 & 1 & 0 & 5 \\ 10 & -4 & -4 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
```

In MATLAB, CNNs can be trained using the trainNetwork function which is available from the Deep Learning Toolbox. The syntax of the function for training a CNN is:

```
net= trainNetwork(images,layers,options)
```

The input to the function is described below:

+   * images: Image data in a datastore format.
"* layers: Layers to be used in CNN.
"* options: Training parameters.

The function outputs net which is the CNN in a SeriesNetwork object format.

The CNN can then classify new data using MATLAB's classify function from the Deep Learning Toolbox. The syntax of the function is:

```
YPred= classify(net,imds)
```
