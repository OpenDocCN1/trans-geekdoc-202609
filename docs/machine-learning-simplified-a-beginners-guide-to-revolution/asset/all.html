Introduction to AI
Artificial Intelligence is a hot topic all around the world. Most of the big companies claim to have used Artificial Intelligence in their products and services. Artificial Intelligence is actually an ideology. It is an ideology in which a computer must have human-like cognitive abilities. By cognitive abilities, I mean Human-like understanding, vision and speech.
Artificial Intelligence can be categorized into 3 types on the basis of its abilities.
Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI):
Often referred to as weak AI, this is the kind of AI that is less capable than human beings. This is the AI we all are seeing now-a-days. They are often limited to a certain task. For example, a face recognition program cannot perform speech recognition. On a quick glance, they may seem more capable than humans, but our vast brain and experiences make us far better than these.

This kind of AI’s capabilities is on-par with human’s. They are also referred to as strong AI. It can mimic humans but exists only on concepts and theories. Not only on a single task, but it should be able to excel on various tasks. With current computing technologies, we cannot expect AGI anytime soon, unless there is a revolution. Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI):
Above human’s capabilities, they are the most intelligent beings on the planet. They have exceeded humans and may enslave humans just like humans enslave animals. They can have emotions and become self-aware. Robots shown in movies like Ex-Machina, Terminator, Enthiran, etc. fall under the category of ASI.
Fortunately, they are just hypothetical and most probably we may not see them in our lifetime (unless there is a major breakthrough in technologies.)
So, to answer our first question of whether history is going to repeat, it all depends upon the expectations of the general population. If they are expecting AGI and ASI, then, it might repeat.

What is Machine Learning?
Machine Learning is the use of mathematical and statistical tools to recognize a pattern in a dataset. Mostly, it is used to perform tasks that are repetitive in nature and exhausting for humans. By far, it is the best method to approach Artificial Intelligence.
Machine Learning process can be divided into 4 steps :
●       Collect and refine the dataset (labelled or unlabeled)
●       Choose a Machine Learning Algorithm
●       Train a Machine Learning model
●       Make predictions out of trained model
There are three types of Machine Learning Algorithms:
Supervised Machine Learning Algorithms:
Those kinds of Machine Learning Algorithms where you use a collection of input-output pairs to make machines understand the relation between them. This collection of input-output pairs is called a labelled dataset. Applying that relation, the machine can then predict the output for a given unknown input.  (Don’t worry if you haven’t understood, just stay till the end, you’ll understand all.)
Some of the examples of supervised Machine Learning Algorithms are:
●       Linear Regression
●       Artificial Neural Networks
●       Logistic Regression
●       Support Vector Machine
●       Naive Bayes Classifier, etc.
Unsupervised Machine Learning Algorithms:
Those kind of machine Learning Algorithms where you have to use unlabeled dataset to train a machine learning model are unsupervised machine learning algorithms. They are mostly used for classification and clustering tasks.
Some of the examples of Unsupervised Machine Learning algorithms are:
●       K-means clustering
●       Autoencoders
●       Principal Component Analysis
●       Generative Adversarial Networks , etc.
Reinforcement Learning Algorithms:
This is a relatively new approach which works on a hit-and-trial method. There is a system of reward and punishment. The model performs random activities at initial stage and for each activity that didn’t provide a good outcome, it’ll get a negative point and vice versa. The model works to increase its points. After the process, the result is an optimal model.
For example, let’s say you are learning cycling. Then, every time you fall, you realize that particular step used when you fell should not be used again i.e. punishment and every time you cycle without falling, you realize you should use that particular steps i.e. reward. After a few days of similar repetition, you will learn cycling. This is how reinforcement learning works.
What is Deep Learning?
Neural Network is the network of artificial neurons that best tries to mimic the biological neuron. Let's talk about biological neurons.
Biological neurons
Fig 1-1. Structure of a neuron (source: Click Here )
Human body contains around 100 billion neurons that transmits messages throughout the body. A neuron consists of Dendrites, Soma and Axon. Soma processes the information passed by Dendrites and axon. A neuron is connected to another through axon and dendrites.
Fig 1-2. Artificial Neural Network (Source: Click Here )
Similarly, Artificial Neural Network consists following parts:
Input Layer: Identical to Dendrites of Biological neurons, it receives the input signal that is to be processed. Then it passes the input to Hidden Layer.
Hidden Layer(s): It receives and processes the input provided by the input layer similar to Soma of Biological Neuron. Depending upon the complexity of the problem, there can be more than 1 hidden layer in a neural network.
Output Layer: After Processing the input, Hidden layer passes the information to the output layer which provides the output to the user. It is similar to the axon of a biological neuron.
If we use more than two Hidden layers, then our network becomes DEEP. so, we call them Deep Neural Networks . And the process of training a Deep Neural Network is called DEEP LEARNING.

Let’s say you are a chocolate lover and you always go to Frodo’s Retail Shop to buy chocolates. On day 1, you bought 6 packs of KitKat and they asked you to pay $12. No. of chocolates = money / 2

This is Linear Regression.
Linear Regression is the stepping stone into the world of Machine Learning. It is the process of mapping the (linear) relation between input and output and using that to predict the output.
A linear function looks like this,
Y = mX + c
Where X is the input and Y is the output. Using the data the algorithm is provided, it tries to estimate the value of m and c which would be used to find Y i.e. Output.
Let’s say you have plotted some points on the graph.

Now, what Linear Regression does is try to fit the line that best suits these data points.

To do this, the program generates a random value for m and c. Then, it takes input-output pairs to validate m and c. To validate, it uses mean squared error.

Logistic Regression
Introduction:
Don’t let the name fool you. It is not a Regression algorithm. It doesn’t predict continuous (numerical) values but it predicts CATEGORICAL values like dog/cat classifier like this:
Fig 4-1. Logistic Regression (source: Click Here )
So, Basically It creates a boundary for categories. The data outside that boundary would be one category and inside would be another.
Everything in the working mechanism is almost similar to Linear Regression with a few tweaks. Let’s say for this example, you have more that 1 input variable(X). So, the equation will be:
Z = m1 X1 + m2 X2 + m3 X3 +...+ mn Xn + c
Where X­­­1 , X­2­ , X­3­ …. are input variables and m­­­1 , m2­ , m­3­ ­ …. are the slopes and c is the intercept and Z is the output variable.
We used to do up to this and it used to be our output in Linear Regression but in Logistic Regression, there is one more step.
The value of Z is passed to a logistic function.
P(Y=1) = 1 / (1+ e-Z )
This function converts any given numerical value into categorical value i.e. 0 or 1. So, here it returns the probability of our output being 1. This function can be visualized as:
This function is commonly called Sigmoid Function.

Confusion Matrix:
After you’ve trained a model, you need check its performance. There are many metrics to do it but the best and the most popular is confusion matrix. It is used to check the performance of a classification model.
It is a matrix in the form of:
Fig 4-2. Confusion Matrix (source: Click Here )
Terms used:
  • True Positive (TP): The number of examples where both Actual Value and Predicted value are positive.
  • True Negative (TN): The number of examples where both Actual Value and Predicted value are negative.
  • False Positive (FP): The number of examples where Actual Value is Negative but predicted value is Positive.
  • False Negative (FN): The number of examples where Actual Value is Positive but predicted value is Negative.
Let’s see one random meme to understand this better:
Fig 4-3. Confusion matrix real-life example
From these values, you can calculate different values like precision, recall, accuracy, etc. to check the performance of your model.
  • Recall:
This value is for each category. High Recall means the category is predicted correctly. It gives us an idea about when an example is positive, how often does the model predicts positive.
  • Precision:
This value is for each category. High Precision means the positive example is predicted to be positive. It gives us an idea about when the model predicts positive, how often is it correct.
  • F1 Score:
It is a combined representational score of both Recall and Precision.
  • Accuracy:
Before this, you might have thought good accuracy means better performance. It’s not true. Let’s say you have a dataset containing 90% of class A and 10% of class B. Then, if you predict every example to be of class A, then you have an accuracy of 90%. But is it really accurate? Accuracy works in an ideal condition where all the classes have equal number of data.

Introduction:
“You are the average of 5 people you spend the most time with.” – Jim Rohn
This statement by one of the most popular American Entrepreneur, Jim Rohn, comes very close to the concept of K-Nearest Neighbors.
It is a classification algorithm just like Logistic Regression. This is better than Logistic Regression because it introduces non-linearity in the boundary instead of just using a straight line.
Steps in KNN Algorithm:
1. Plot the given training labeled data. The below image has 2 class i.e. Red and Green. There is no training process in KNN. The calculations are done in real-time.
Fig 5-1. Labeled Data plotted (source: Click Here )
2. Now, introduce the input that you want to predict. Then, the machine looks at K (any integer number) nearest datapoints. The category with highest datapoints is assigned as the class of the new input.
Fig 5-2. New data point (source: Click Here )
If we take K = 5 in the above figure, Just by our guess, 3 will belong to the red category and 2 will belong to green category. So, the new data belongs to red category.
Remember to use K as an odd number because you might get equal number of data points from each category if you use even number.
The distance between datapoints can be calculated by many ways but the common one is Euclidean method. By finding distance, the machine can know which data points are nearest.

Neural Networks
In Chapter 1, we touched the surface of Deep learning and Neural Networks. Now, we have to dive a bit deeper.
There’s one question that might arise in your mind. We have Linear Regression; then why do we need other algorithms?
The simple answer is NON-LINEARITY.
Linear Regression can map only linear relation but real life data rarely have linear relation and neural networks is the best algorithm for most of the cases if provided enough data.
Fig. 6-1. Structure of a Neural Network
Don’t get panicked by seeing this figure. I will explain every bit of it. From the previous knowledge, you might have understood this neural network takes two inputs and gives one output. Each circle in the figure is called a node and as you can see in the figure, each node is connected with every other node of neighboring layers. You might be wondering what are those w1, w2, w3, …. Stuffs. They are weights of the network. Simply, each of them are the strength of the relationship between two nodes.
In the figure, w1 determines the strength of the relationship between x1 and h1. Similarly, w2 determines that for x1 and h2 and so on. For example, if x1 is the amount of rainfall and h1 is the amount of crops grown, then w1 will be high because these two things are strongly related.
Same applies to your brain as well. You remember some moments of your life clearly whereas you forget most of them. This is because you’ve been thinking about that moment and it made the relationship strong. So, you remember those moments. Whereas, you don’t think about other moments and gradually the relationship gets weaker and will be difficult for you to remember later on.
Consider it as the fundamental thing that defines what decisions the neural network makes.
Now, what are those b1, b2, b3? These are biases. Let’s say you have a factory. You get expenses from electricity, raw materials, etc. while your industry is running. But there are some fixed expenses that will occur even if you stop production for sometime. Those costs include land rent, employee salary, etc.
From now on, Whenever I use W and B, W represents all the weights of the model i.e. w1, w2, w3, .... ,wn and B represents all the biases of the model i.e. b1, b2, b3, ..., bn.
B and W changes throughout the training process to make the prediction better. So, B and W are called Parameters .
Also, consider B as the c in the linear equation Y = mX + c. They are constant values which helps a line to deviate from the origin to fit the data better. (And of course, W is similar to m in the linear equation.)
Now, let’s see what happens inside each node in a hidden layer (In this case, h1,h2 and h3).
h1 = x1 * w1 + x2 * w4 + b1
h2 = x1 * w2 + x2 * w5 + b2
h3 = x1 * w3 + x2 * w6 + b3
And
ŷ = h1 * w7 + h2 * w8 + h3 * w9
As you can see, each input node is multiplied with the respective weight connecting it to a specific hidden layer node and added together with the bias. After that, those hidden layer nodes are multiplied with the weights connecting them with the output layer and added together.
(Note: Output Layer doesn’t contain any bias. We’ll talk about it later.)
By doing this, the data is moving in the forward direction i.e. from input node to hidden layer(s) to output layer. So, during training the neural network model, this process is called Forward Propagation .
After it reaches the output layer, an output is generated i.e. ŷ. At first, W and B are all randomly generated. So, ŷ is also arbitrary. Now, The error/loss in the prediction is calculated by,
Loss = (y - ŷ) ²
For a better prediction, This loss has to be minimized to its least. For this, we have to change the values of the parameters (W and B). We do it by the process called Backward Propagation .
Fig 6-2. Backward Propagation algorithm
The goal of taking derivatives of Loss (L) with each respective weight is to check how a small change in each weight affects the Loss. Let’s take w8 and see how it is updated.
w8 ” = w8 - η * dL/dw 8
Where, η = Learning Rate (LR). They are introduced to prevent a big change in weights. Let’s say dL/dw 8 = 20. This is a huge number and will change w 8 drastically. So, to minimize this, we can multiply it by some minimal value, let’s say 0.01. Then, it would only change the value of w 8 by 0.2. This is the function of Learning Rate.
Similarly, every other weights and biases are updated. As you can see, the process moves backwards i.e. from output layer to hidden layer to input layer. So, this process is called Backward Propagation .

Activation Function
There’s one thing I haven’t told you yet. The actual non-linearity in the model is given by activation function. Previously, we wrote that,

h2 = x1 * w2 + x2 * w5 + b2
h3 = x1 * w3 + x2 * w6 + b3

Did you know that we could train our tongue to help us see if we are blind? Yes, it’s true. There’s a company called Brainport doing this. Now you might question, how does CNN provide better results in image processing? The simple answer is ‘With the process called Convolution ’. CNN is a simple neural network with a convolution at the beginning.
Let’s say you want to recognize the face of a person in an image. But there are some items like cars, roads, etc. in the background. With a simple Neural Network, it would assign weights to every pixel in the image. But, with the help of convolution, the network would assign weights to only the face which will significantly increase computational efficiency and also increase the accuracy. So, it is used by big techies like Google, Facebook, Instagram, etc.

Convolution Process
Let’s say this is an image.
Now, to extract the useful information (features) from this image, element-wise multiplication is performed with it and a relatively smaller matrix (usually of size 3x3).
That smaller matrix is called kernel or filter.
Now, this kernel is applied to the image matrix to extract features. The resulting matrix after the operation is called a feature map.
Fig 7-2. Feature Map Extraction (source: Click Here )
The feature map is always smaller than the input image. If you want the feature map to be of the same size as input, then you can add zeroes around the feature map matrix. This process is called zero padding .
Check out this image for better understanding of convolutions.
Fig 7-3. Feature map images (source: Click Here )
Some of the terms in CNN are:
Depth:
It is the number of filters to be applied in the image. We used only one filter in the above example. So, its depth is 1. If there are 3 filters used then its depth is 3 and so on. Usually, 3 filters are used for a colored image as there are 3 matrices.
Stride:
It is the number of pixels jumps in an input matrix per step. If stride is 1, then the kernel moves one pixel forward per step and if it is 2, then the kernel moves 2 pixels forward per step and so on.

Example of Stride 2

Non-Linearity
After the feature map is extracted, you need to apply non-linearity to the feature map. The best one for CNN is ReLU.
Pooling / Subsampling
We have extracted features and applied non-linearity to it. Now, we must get it ready for processing. Pooling is a way to reduce the size of feature maps. The reduced size means a smaller number of weights which means the model will less likely be overfitted and also increased computational efficiency. There are 2 types of pooling.
Fig 7-5. Types of pooling(source: Click Here )
As you can see, Max pooling is a technique to take the pixel with max value out of the pool size in a matrix and Average pooling is the technique of averaging the pixels into one pixel out of the pool size in a matrix. In the figure, the pool size is 2x2, so each color covers 2x2 of the matrix and performs pooling operations on them.
Neural Network

Quick Recap:
CNN consists of following steps:
  1. Creating a feature map from the input image using kernel/filter
  2. Providing Non-linearity to the feature map using an activation function (mostly ReLU)
  3. The Non-linear feature map is then subjected to pooling which reduces the size of the feature map.
  4. The matrix obtained after pooling is given to the simple neural network for prediction.

You all know Siri, Alexa, Bixby, and many other voice assistants. But how do they understand what you said? It turns out that they use Recurrent Neural Network. They are specialized in time series and sequential data.
From the learnings we’ve had till now, you might know that the output of a simple neural network is not affected by previous input. But there are some cases where we need to take care of the previous input.
Let’s say you want to do a stock market forecasting. But you can’t do that by treating the market price of each day as an independent input. Today’s market price is affected by yesterday’s and yesterday's is affected by a day before’s and so on. That’s where RNN comes handy. See it’s architecture.
Fig. 9-1. RNN architecture (source: Click Here )
As you can see, The model has only one hidden neuron. The same network is recurring for every input. Hence, it is called recurrent neural network. For the input x t , the output from  x t-1 is used as a part of input and so on. This way, the sequence of the data is kept. Each and every output is influenced by previous input.
The number of input passed recurrently is a time step. In the above figure, there are 3 time steps.
Problems in RNN
RNN seems very good. It’s applicable as well. But there’s one problem with it. During training it is prone to suffer from Vanishing/Exploding Gradient Problem . As we know that the update in weights during backpropagation is done by,
Fig 9-2. Weights update (source: Click Here )
This is done for every timestep in RNN. So, subtracting the derivative from last time step to first time step, The first few layers are going to have either tiny weights or huge weights. This will result in the stopping of training.
So, to overcome this problem, few variants of RNN were introduced. The most popular ones are LSTM and GRU.
Long Short Term Memory (LSTM)
To solve the Vanishing/Exploding Gradient problem in RNN, a modified architecture of RNN i.e. LSTM was introduced. The major idea of this architecture is the use of a thing called cell state . It travels throughout the network. It carries information along with it. To change the information in the cell state, LSTM uses the structure called gates . They are controlled by sigmoid function which gives output from 0 to 1. 0 means don’t let information flow and 1 means let every information flow .
There are 2 types of gates. Forget gate decides what and how much information to erase from the cell state. Input gate decides what and how much new information to add to the cell state.
That’s it for LSTM. We won’t go deeper into LSTM in this book. If you want to learn more, you can check this beautiful blog:

Language Fundamentals:

Cleaning:
