
Molinari `et al` \[[122](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib122)\] have already briefly detailed the first and second-generation approaches. Table \[[9.3](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9t3)\] presents the benchmarking table employing first-, second-, and third-generation methodologies utilized throughout the last two decades in this area. The techniques of the first generation are addressed first. Liang `et al` \[[123](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib123)\] employed DP to quantify cIMT from 50 photos in the year 2000. Stein `et al` \[[124](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib124)\] and Faita `et al` \[[125](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib125)\] employed edge detection approaches to calculate cIMT from 50 and 150 CCA images with cIMT errors of 0.012 ± 0.006 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn89.gif"/ and 0.010 ± 0.038 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn90.gif"/, respectively. Ikeda `et al` \[[48](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib48)\] employed a bulb edge point detection methodology to estimate cIMT from 649 images, with a bias of 0.0106 ± 0.00310 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn91.gif"/ between the anticipated and ground truth. Following that, a combination of first- and second-generation approaches will be discussed. Molinari `et al` \[[72](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib72)\] estimated cIMT from 200 photos using a mix of level set and morphological image analysis. The cIMT error was found to be 0.144 ± 0.179inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn92.gif"/ mm. Molinari `et al` developed CARES \[[126](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib126)\], CAMES \[[24](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib24)\], CAUDLES \[[127](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib127)\], and FOAM \[[128](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib128)\], which are fusion-based patented methodologies for cIMT estimation. From 647, 657, 630, and 665 photos, the cIMT error for each of them was 0.172 ± 0.222 mminline-graphic style="height:1em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn93.gif"/, 0.154 ± 0.227 mminline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn94.gif"/, 0.224 ± 0.252 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn95.gif"/, and 0.150 ± 0.169 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn96.gif"/, respectively. Some of the second-generation techniques are now discussed. Gutierrez `et al` \[[129](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib129)\] created an active contour model for measuring cIMT from 180 images in 2002. Molinari `et al` \[[130](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib130)\] created a snake-based model to estimate cIMT from 200 images. Molinari `et al` \[[131](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib131)\] created a new CALEX 1.0 model for calculating cIMT from 665 images in 2014. The active contour, snakes, and CALEX 1.0 had cIMT errors of 0.090 ± 0.060 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn97.gif"/, 0.01 ± 0.01 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn98.gif"/, and 0.191 ± 0.217 *m**m*inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn99.gif"/, respectively. Rosa `et al` \[[92](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib92)\] employed an ANN as the ML model for cIMT error from 60 ultrasonography CCA images using three metrics: mean, Suri’s polyline, and centerline, with average values of 0.03763 ± 0.02518inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn100.gif"/ mm, 0.03670 ± 0.02429inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn101.gif"/ mm, and 0.03683 ± 0.02450inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn102.gif"/ mm, respectively. Rosa `et al` \[[93](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib93)\] also utilized RBNN to compute the cIMT error, which was 0.065 ± 0.046inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn103.gif"/ mm, using a database of 25 CCA images. Molinari `et al` \[[23](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib23)\] computed cIMT from 200 ultrasound CCA scans using an unsupervised FKMC methodology. The obtained cIMT error was 0.054 ± 0.035inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn104.gif"/ mm. Rosa `et al` \[[110](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib110)\] presented the computation of cIMT from 55 ultrasound pictures with a mean cIMT error of 0.0499 ± 0.0498inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn105.gif"/ mm using a mixture of ML (ANN) and a DL autoencoder. Biswas `et al` \[[111](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib111)\] used a database of 396 carotid images to demonstrate the DL paradigm for cIMT calculation utilizing an FCN by combining the ground truth from two separate observers. When evaluating the two ground truths, the cIMT error was 0.126 ± 0.134inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn106.gif"/ mm and 0.124 ± 0.100inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn107.gif"/ mm, respectively. Utilizing the same FCN methodology, Elisa `et al` \[[103](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib103)\] obtained a TPA of 20.52inline-graphic style="height:0.9375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn108.gif"/ mm² and 19.44inline-graphic style="height:0.9375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn109.gif"/ mm², respectively, using the two sets of ground facts. Biswas `et al` \[[104](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib104)\] went on to show how to compute cIMT from 250 ultrasound CCA pictures using a two-stage (CNN + FCN) DL method. In the second stage, the authors employed a patching-based strategy to extract the ROI from the first stage and outline the LI and MA borders. The cIMT error was 0.0935 ± 0.0637inline-graphic style="height:1.0625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn110.gif"/ mm. In the sense that both stages were DL phases, the patch-based method was a one-of-a-kind approach. FCN was used on 331 carotid ultrasound scans with a cumulative cIMT error of 0.02 mm in another investigation by Del Mar `et al` \[[112](#Chapter_9.xhtml#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib112)\].
