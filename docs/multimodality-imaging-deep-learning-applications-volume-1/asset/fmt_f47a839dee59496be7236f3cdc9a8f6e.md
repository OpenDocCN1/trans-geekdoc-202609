
where `η` inline-graphic style="height:0.875em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn37.gif"/ is the learning parameter. The pixel intensities generated by a kernel process are the input pattern for each ANN. Three kernels of sizes `3 × 3`, `7 × 7`, and `11 × 11` were applied pixel by pixel, by shifting to collect contextual information on nearby pixel intensities for each image. Convolution is another name for the process, and figure [9.4](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9fig4) (iii) shows how it works with the `3 × 3` kernel. The ground truth was pixel class information gathered by manually segmenting CCA images and annotating whether or not each pixel represented a cIMT border. As a result, the inputs were transmitted through three ANNs for training and testing, respectively. Each of the three ANNs produces another reconstructed binary image as the output. Following that, the three output binary images are merged, and a third kernel of size `3 × 3` is applied to the merged picture before being fed into the fourth ANN to produce the final binary mask. Figure [9.4](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9fig4) (iv) depicts a representative model of the complete process. The final stage involves identifying the LI and MA borders (as shown in figure [9.4](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9fig4) (v)) and computing the cIMT.
