###### Appendix A Mathematical representations of the ML and DL paradigms

The term learnability evolved from the general tendency of humans and machines to improve performance based on experience. In recent times, computational models such as AI have shown the ability to discover patterns from unstructured data and infer logic and make decisions. AI models such as `ML` and `DL` have been used in the area of medical imaging to make life saving diagnostic decisions \[[82](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib82), [150](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib150)–[152](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib152)\]. Here we give a brief mathematical discourse on the `ML` and `DL` models for better understanding.

*Machine learning: a mathematical representation*

The mathematical approach is given as follows \[[153](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib153), [154](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib154)\]: let us say that the instance space is denoted as **`X`** = {*x*[1],*x*[2],…,*x*[*i*]}inline-graphic style="height:1.1875em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn120.gif"/ and label space as **`L`** = {*l*[1],*l*[2],…,*l*[*j*]}inline-graphic style="height:1.625em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn121.gif"/. The characterization job is therefore denoted as *f̂_`X`_:**X**→**L**inline-graphic style="height:1.4375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn122.gif"/, where *f̂_`X`_(*x*[*i*])inline-graphic style="height:1.4375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn123.gif"/ denotes the estimate of the desired function *f_`X`_(*x*[*i*])inline-graphic style="height:1.4375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn124.gif"/, that represents the true label of *x*[*i*]inline-graphic style="height:0.875em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn125.gif"/. In general, the difference between *f_`X`_inline-graphic style="height:1.1875em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn126.gif"/ and *f̂_`X`_inline-graphic style="height:1.4375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn127.gif"/ is used as a feedback to fine-tune the `ML` model. The least square model is the most common loss model to compute the difference between the desired and actual output and given by

$$\varepsilon = \frac{1}{n}\parallel f(\mathbf{X}) - \hat{f}(\mathbf{X}) \parallel^{2},$$

where *f_`X`_(_`X`_)inline-graphic style="height:1.1875em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn129.gif"/ is the input to the `ML` model and *f̂_`X`_(_`X`_)inline-graphic style="height:1.4375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn130.gif"/ is the predicted output. Different loss models are applied by different `ML` models. Although label estimation forms a major part of supervised learning, there are other paradigms of learning such as regression, where function approximation is performed. If *R* ∈ r_e_a_l_n_u_m_b_e_r_s, **`X`**=*R_,**`X`**=*R_, and **`Y`**=*R_, then *f̂_`X`_:**X**→**Y**_ is an approximation function mapping from **`X`** to **`Y`**. In another form of learning, instead of label outputs, probability is generated for each label for a test instance, i.e. *f̂_`X`_:**X**→[0,1]^(|**Y**|)inline-graphic style="height:1.4375em;" xlink:href="bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9ieqn137.gif"/.

Any instance is represented by the feature or independent variables, where the instance automatically becomes the dependent variable. The instance space is therefore defined as _g_[_k_]:_`X`_→_`I`_[_k_] where, _g_[_k_] is defined as the mapping from instance space **`X`** to feature space _`I`_[_k_]. Therefore, the instance space **`X`** can be defined as the Cartesian product of _k_ domains **`X`**=**`I`**[1]×**`I`**[2]×…×**`I`**[`k`].

As stated previously, `ML` models are two-stage systems where the features are extracted which are generally statistical information. As per the nature of features, they are categorized into quantitative, ordinal, and categorical. For images, the features are generally quantitative in nature, which basically deals in orientation, direction, scale, texture etc. `ML` models on the other hand can be categorized into probabilistic, logical, or geometric with the latter most commonly used with respect to medical images. The geometric models use lines, distances, curves, arcs etc. These models work by drawing a hyper-plane between two labeled clusters. The most common geometric `ML` models are _K_-nearest neighbors \[[155](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib155)\], support vector machines (SVMs) \[[156](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib156)\], artificial neural networks (ANN) \[[157](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib157)\], etc. The probabilistic models consider the most probable class of an instance by computing the maximum posterior probability, i.e. using a Bayesian classifier \[[158](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib158)\]. Logical models follow rule based constructs to build models such as decision trees \[[159](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-2244-7ch9bib159)\]. The training and testing paradigms of the `ML` and `DL` models are the same, although the feature extraction part is done earlier for the former. The training and testing data are different for both cases. The models are trained to learn the mapping from instances to labels from labeled instances in the training phase. After learning, the performance of training is judged from testing the trained mode on unknown test instances.
