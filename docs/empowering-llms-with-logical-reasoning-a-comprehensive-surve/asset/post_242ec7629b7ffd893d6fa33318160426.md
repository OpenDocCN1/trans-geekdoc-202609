## 3.4 Factuality Consistency

Factuality consistency refers to the degree of alignment between model's generated responses or reasoning outcomes and real-world knowledge. In fact-checking, the system evaluates the actual factuality of model outputs by providing reliable knowledge sources, detecting potential logical errors or factual inaccuracies. When to factuality consistency, there are some inconsistency contradicting statements, constraints or a knowledge base (KB), factuality agrees on all facts contained in a KB [Calzano et al., 2025].

## 3.2 Implication Consistency

The implication consistency is based on the logic rule $p \rightarrow q, p$. It means that if a constraint “p $\rightarrow$ q” and the premise, p, it can be that “p $\rightarrow$ q” is true. If the model predicts “q” is False, then the answer violates implication consistency. For example, given the physical fact that All Iron (p) is metal (q), LLMs are not to denote True to this statement if Iron (p) and Metal (q) is false.

Unlike, implication consistency is closely related to the CoT method, where each chain can be regarded as a logical implication. Failure to satisfy implication consistency will lead to inaccurate outputs from these explanation methods. For instance, Automatic Prompting (Jun et al., 2022) corrects the correct answer from unreliable explanations. Multiple Prompting produces a single explanation, Maieie produces abductive implications both “true” and “false” are converted into logical constraints and the optimal aspects using a MAX-SAT solver to avoid implication inconsistency.

## 3.3 Transitivity Consistency

To address the issue of logical consistency in extending models under complex queries, a logical consistency and improvement framework for LLMs with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) was produced [Ghosh et al., 2025]. Specifically, a logical-based evaluation framework is firstly designed to handle logical consistency through de-junction, and conjunction. Secondly, by constructing tree benchmark datasets based on knowledge graph (KG), it is demonstrated that LLMs exhibit poor logical consistency in complex queries. To address this issue, they use in-contextualized prompts and few-shot approach to learn factuality consistency of LLMs in complex factoring tasks, leveraging parameter-efficient methods such as QLoRA to improve performance.

Transitivity represents the logical relationships among order or propositions. When two premises $p \rightarrow q$ and $q \rightarrow r$ it can state that $p \rightarrow r$, which is regarded as transitivity consistency. It has shown that LLMs lack transitivity consistency. For state-of-the-art question answering (QA) model answers to “Is a bird does it have feathers?, or are birds? No to Sparrow have feathers?” [Mitchell et al., 2022]. Then the model provides an affirmative answer when crow has the head according to the transitivity rule, which is incompatible with the other output.

## 3.5 Compositional Consistency

Compositional consistency refers to the maintenance of logical consistency when combining multiple facts. When a model needs to combine into a complex chain by logical operators (e.g., implication, conjunction), it must ensure that each step of the derivation conforms to the rules, and make final conclusions self-consistent under conditions. This also requires the model to not only understand the meaning of actual fact, but also to capture the logical relationships when they are connected, avoiding inferences. Loco-LLMS [Calzano et al., 2025] is proposed to maintain factuality consistency through de-integration, which makes factuality and self-consistent by fine-tuning a LLM according to a knowledge base of facts and rules. The logical formulas that can be arbitrary propositions are compiled into a circuit and then be allocated to generate the answer. [Liu et al., 2024b] provides a universal framework to quantify the positional consistency via three universal proxies: transitivity, commutativity, and negation inference.
