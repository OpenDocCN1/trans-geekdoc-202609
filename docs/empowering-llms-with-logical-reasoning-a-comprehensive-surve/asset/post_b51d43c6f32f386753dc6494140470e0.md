# Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable successes over various natural language tasks. However, recent studies have found that there are significant challenges to the reasoning abilities of LLMs. This paper aims to categorize these challenges into two aspects: (**Logical reasoning**, LLMs often fail to get the correct answer within complex problems which requires sophisticated strategies); (**Logical consistency**, LLMs are prone to producing inconsistent responses across different questions. For example, in the Macaw question-answering task LLMs answer Yes to both questions: "Does a bird have wings? Does a dog have wings?" To address this direction, we investigate various reasoning methods and provide detailed tactics of these methods. Specifically, to accurately answer complex logical questions, methods can be categorized based on relation to external solvers, prompting, and inference. We also discuss concepts of various locations of logical consistency, including implication, negation, truthfulness, consistency and composition. In addition, we review benchmark evaluation metrics and discuss research directions, such as extensions to model logic to account for uncertainty, and efficient algorithms satisfying multiple-logical consistency simultaneously.

# 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated performance in a broad range of natural language tasks-including generation, classification and translation. However, recent studies have found that there are significant challenges to the reasoning abilities of LLMs. On one hand, handling syntax, semantics, and word tasks such as next-word prediction or masked language modeling does not ensure the logical ability of LLMs [Liu et al., 2023]. On the other hand, the primacy of LLMs in processing human-written texts, lacks high-quality logical reasoning such as logical deduction and proofs [Moshita et al., 2024]. These challenges significantly limit the ability of LLMs due to the following summarized aspects.

LLMs fail to get the correct logical answer in questions which require deductive, inductive or abstract reasoning from given premises and constraints. # 2 Logical Question Answering

Leveraging language models (LLMs) for complex reasoning problems is a key focus of recent researches [Lao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024]. Numerous studies have explored methods to enhance the logical reasoning capabilities of LLMs, which can be categorized into several branches, including reliance on external solvers, prompting techniques without further training and fine-tuning. Many benchmarks are developed to evaluate the logical reasoning performance of models.

# 2.1 Solver-Aided Approach: Translation via LLM and Solver

The solver-aided approach refers to parsing natural language (NL) problems into symbolic (SL) expressions, and then solving them via external solvers.
