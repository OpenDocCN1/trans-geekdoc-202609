Attacks against data integrity: Attacks targeting data integrity in cloud pose significant risks by tampering with or altering stored information, compromising its accuracy and reliability [77]. Malicious actors may attempt through unauthorized modification, deletion, or insertion of data to manipulate the integrity of information stored within the cloud [77]. Such attacks can occur through various vectors, including vulnerabilities in cloud infrastructure, unauthorized access to credentials, or leveraging malware to data in transit or at rest. The repercussions of compromised data integrity in cloud services can be severe, leading to misinformation, financial loss, legal ramifications, and erosion of trust among users and customers [78]. Protecting data integrity in the cloud involves implementing robust encryption, access controls, regular integrity checks, and maintaining stringent security practices to prevent unauthorized alterations and maintaining the trustworthiness of the stored data.

Attacks against virtualization technology: Attacks against virtualization technology in cloud services exploit vulnerabilities in the hypervisor or the virtualization layer, aiming to compromise the isolation between virtual machines (VMs) or gaining unauthorized access to the underlying infrastructure [79]. Malicious actors may attempt various techniques like VM escape, where they break out of the confines of a VM to access the hypervisor and subsequently other VMs, leading to potential data theft or manipulation [79, 80]. Other attacks include resource exhaustion, where an attacker overwhelms the virtualization layer to disrupt service availability or DoS attacks. Furthermore, vulnerabilities in the hypervisor or misconfigurations can allow privilege escalation or control over the virtualized environment [80]. Securing virtualization technology in cloud services demands regular updates and patches, strict access controls, segregation of duties, implementing IDSs, and employing security measures like sandboxing and isolation to mitigate these potential threats and ensure the integrity and security of cloud infrastructure [80].

Confidentiality threats: Confidentiality threats in IoT systems are pervasive due to the extensive data exchange among interconnected devices, often without robust security mechanisms [81]. These threats stem from vulnerabilities such as weak authentication mechanisms, inadequate encryption protocols, and insufficient data protection within IoT ecosystems. Archers exploit these weaknesses to eavesdrop on communications between IoT devices, intercept sensitive data transmitted across networks, or gain unauthorized access to confidential information stored within the devices or the cloud infrastructure they interact with [81].
