IoT: Research Challenges and Future Applications was the idea out by Abdel Rahman H. Hussein [35]. Understanding the many IoT application areas that could be used and the research difficulties associated with them is crucial as the IoT moves progressively towards the next stage of development of the Internet. Nearly every aspect of daily life, including smart cities, smart environments, living and smart, dorms, as well as smart agriculture, smart logistics, and smart retail, is projected to be impacted by the IoT. IoT-enabled technologies have recently made considerable strides, there are still many of problems that need to be resolved. Because the IoT is a product of numerous technologies, there will undoubtedly be many research obstacles. IoT is an extremely important topic for studies in many fields, including information technology as well as computer science, is creating new opportunities for research due to its tremendous scope and influence on every aspect of our life. This task examines the current development of IoT technology in addition to covering potential applications and research issues.

Given now the number of IoT devices is increasing, IoT cybersecurity is essential for the future of the software industry. Because of this, it's critical that experts consider threat modelling and vulnerability assessment when creating software. The goal of this investigation is to investigate how modelling and evaluation of vulnerabilities are in the business environment. The study is carried out with a single case study with an IoT company to analyse the aforementioned aspects from the practitioner's perspective by conducting interviews, evaluating the documentation of an IoT solution, and reviewing current literature. Threat modelling is a well-researched issue, but the findings demonstrate that there is evidence that is not entirely included in the process of designing software as a whole. Nevertheless, even when they are not employing a particular approach, practitioners conduct threat identification analysis in their work. Additionally, it was clear that there was no systematic or structured process in place for carrying out vulnerability assessment tasks.

There is a need for integrating security and penetration testing into the software development lifecycle (SDLC), even though practitioners appear to review their design and architecture. Our study's outcomes have been utilised to distill our suggestions for practitioners, which is consistent with a list of open-source security breach detection tools, best practices, and deis risk modelling and evaluations of vulnerabilities. Additionally, our scientific contributions shed light on the industry's established work procedures for threat modelling and vulnerability assessment.
