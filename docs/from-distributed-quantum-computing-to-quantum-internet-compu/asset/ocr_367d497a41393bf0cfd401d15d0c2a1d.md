Let's see how this works in detail. Extending from the single target case, let's consider another machine C that also has a qubit \(|s\rangle\) to be controlled by the qubit \(|c\rangle\) at A, as shown in Figure 4.3. Note that the three nodes share a (distributed) GHZ state (one qubit each) marked by the wavy lines across the three nodes.$^1$

Now consider the state of the system at slice 1 in Figure 4.3, where we let \(|c\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle\), and reordering the qubits in our expressions, i.e. writing the target qubit of B just before the target qubit of C:

```
at slice 1 : (\( \alpha |0\rangle + \beta |1\rangle \)) \(\otimes\) (\( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|000\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|111\rangle \)) \(\otimes |t\rangle \otimes |s\rangle\)

= (\( \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}|0000\rangle + \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}|0111\rangle + \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}|1000\rangle + \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}|1111\rangle \))

\(\otimes |t\rangle \otimes |s\rangle\)
```

then, applying the CNOT between the top two qubits, we have, at slice 2:

```
at slice 2 : (\( \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}|0000\rangle + \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}|0111\rangle + \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}|1100\rangle + \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}|1011\rangle \))

\(\otimes |t\rangle \otimes |s\rangle\)
```
