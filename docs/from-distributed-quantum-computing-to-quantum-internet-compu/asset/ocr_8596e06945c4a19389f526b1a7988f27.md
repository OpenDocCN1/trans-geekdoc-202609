If the control qubit was any one of the two qubits in the cat-like state \(\alpha|00\rangle + \beta|11\rangle\), the target will be affected the same way, since these two qubits will have effectively the same state, both are 0 with probability \(|\alpha|^2\) or both are 1 with probability \(|\beta|^2\). This means that if the cat-like state \(|c\rangle\) has its two qubits distributed on different machines, then one can control on either of them. Note that the purpose of applying the \(H\) gate (i.e. the steps from slide 4 onward in Figure 4.2) and measuring and sending the measurement to control on \(Z\) is to disentangle the first qubit (at \(A\)) from the third qubit (i.e. the first qubit of \(B\)), now that the distributed CNOT has been done.
