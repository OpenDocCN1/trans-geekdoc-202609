Note that as we continue the training of the model (and this includes extra layers), there is no guarantee that $P(\mathbf{x})$ (in averaging over the training set) will monotonically increase. As our lower bound continues to increase, actual log-likelihood could start decreasing. Let us examine more how this could happen. It would require $KL(Q(h^1|\mathbf{x})||P(h^1|\mathbf{x}))$ to decrease as the second RBM continues to be trained. However, this is unlikely in general: the DBN's $P(h^1)$ deviates more and more from the first RBM's marginal $Q(h^1)$ on $h^1$, it is likely that the posteriors $P(h^1|\mathbf{x})$ (from the DBN) and $Q(h^1|\mathbf{x})$ (from the RBM) deviate more and more (since $P(h^1|\mathbf{x})Q(\mathbf{x})P(h^1)$ and $Q(h^1|\mathbf{x})Q(\mathbf{x})Q(h^1)$), making the KL term in equation (8.1) increase. As the likelihood for the second RBM increases, $P(h^1)$ moves smoothly from $Q(h^1)$ towards $P^*$. Consequently, it seems plausible that continued training of the second RBM is going to increase the DBN's likelihood (not just initially) and by transitivity, adding layers will also increase the DBN's likelihood.
