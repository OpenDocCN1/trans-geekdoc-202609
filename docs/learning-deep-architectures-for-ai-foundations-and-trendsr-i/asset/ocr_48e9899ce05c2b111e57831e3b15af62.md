If we have a procedure to sample from $P(\mathbf{h,x})$ and one to sample from $P(\mathbf{x,h})$, we can obtain an unbiased stochastic estimator of the log-likelihood gradient. [1, 76, 77] introduced the following terminology: in the *positive phase*, **x** is clamped to the input vector, and we sample **h**: in the *negative phase* both **x** and **h** are sampled, ideally from the model itself. In general, only approximate sampling is easily tractable, e.g., using an iterative procedure that constructs an MCMC. The MCMC sampling approach introduced in [1, 76, 77] is based on *Gibbs sampling* [4, 57]. Gibbs sampling is of the joint of $N$ variables $S = (S_1, \dots, S_N)$ through a sequence of $N$ sampling sub-steps of the form

$s_i \sim P(S_i|S_{i-1} = s_i)$ \qquad (5.17)

where $S_i$ contains the $N-1$ random variables in $S$, excluding $S_i$. After these $N$ samples have been obtained, a step of the chain is completed, yielding a sample of $S$ whose distribution converges to $P(S)$ as the number of steps goes to $\infty$, under some conditions. A sufficient condition for convergence of a finite-state Markov Chain is that it isperiodic$^2$ and irreducible$^3$

How can we perform Gibbs sampling in a Boltzmann machine? **s = (x, h)** denote the units in the Boltzmann machine, and **s$_i$** the set of values associated with all units except the ith one. The Boltzmann-machine energy function can be rewritten by putting all the parameters in a vector **d** and a symmetric matrix $A$,

$Energy(s) = -d^T s - s^T As.$ \qquad (5.18)

Let **d$_i$** denote the vector **d** without the element **d$_i$**, $A_{-i}$ the matrix $A$ without the ith row and column, and **a$_i$** the vector that is the ith row (or column) of $A$.
