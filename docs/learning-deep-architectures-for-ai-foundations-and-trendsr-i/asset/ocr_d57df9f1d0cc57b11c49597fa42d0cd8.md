A deep architecture is a composition of many operations, and it could in any case be represented by a very large depth-2 architecture. The composition of computational units in a small deep circuit can be seen as an efficient "factorization" of a large shallow circuit. Reorganizing the way in which units composed can have a drastic effect on the efficiency of representation size. For example, imagine a deep $k$ representation of polynomials where odd layers implement products and even layers implement sums. This architecture can be seen as a particularly efficient factorization, when expanded into a depth 2 architecture such as a sum of products, might require a huge number of terms in the sum: consider a level 1 product (like $x_2x_4$) from the depth $2k$ architecture. It could occur many times as a factor in terms of the depth 2 architecture. One can see in this example deep architectures can be advantageous if some computations (e.g., at one level) can be shared (considering the expanded depth 2 expression): in this case, the overall expression to be represented can be factored out, i.e., represented more compactly with a deep architecture.
