# 9.1.1 Greedy layer-wise Training of DBNs as a Continuation Method

The greedy layer-wise training algorithm for DBNs described in Section 6.1 can be viewed as an approximate continuation method, and First of recall (Section 8.1) that the top-level RBM of a DBN can be unfolded into an infinite directed graphical model with tied parameters. At each step of the greedy layer-wise procedure, we unite the parameters of the top-level RBM from the parameters of the penultimate layer. So one can view the layer-wise procedure as follows. The model structure remains the same, an infinite chain of said layers, but we change the constraint on the parameters at each step of the layer-wise procedure. Initially all the layers are tied. After training the first RBM (i.e., optimizing under this constraint), we unite the first level parameters from the rest. After training the second RBM (i.e., optimizing under this slightly relaxed constraint), we unite the second level parameters from the rest, etc. Instead of a continuum of training criteria, we have a discrete sequence of (presumably) gradually more difficult optimization problems. By making the process greedy we fix the parameters of the first k layers after they have been trained and only optimize the (k+1)th, i.e., train an RBM. For this we would need to initialize the weights of the newly added RBM with the transpose of the previous one. Note also that instead of optimizing all the parameters, the greedy layer-wise approach only optimizes the new ones. But even with these approximations, this analysis suggests an explanation for the good performance of the layer-wise training approach in terms of reaching better solutions.
