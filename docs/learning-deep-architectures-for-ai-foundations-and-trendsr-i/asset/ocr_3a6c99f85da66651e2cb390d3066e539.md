Another alternative to Contrastive Divergence [84, 85, 86], a general approach to train energy-based models in which the energy can be computed tractably, but not the normalization constant Z. The score function of the density $p(x)=q(x)/Z$ is $\psi=\log p(x)/\partial\mathbf{x}$, and we exploit the fact that the score function of our model does not depend on its normalization constant, i.e., $\psi=\log q(x)/\partial\mathbf{x}$. The basic idea is to match the score function of the model with the score function of the empirical density. The average (under the empirical density) of the square norm of the difference between the two functions can be written in squares of the model score function and second derivatives $(\partial^2 q(\mathbf{x}))/\partial\mathbf{x}^2$. Score matching has been shown to be locally consistent [84], converging if the model family matches the data generating process, and it has been used for unsupervised models of image and audio data [94].
