A cyber attack might not trigger as many physical casualties as offline attacks. Yet, they can still have a potentially catastrophic impact, such as shutting down a large portion of power plants, cause economies to crash, or populations deprived of essential services (Munk, 2015, p. 176). According to Conway (2014), the definition of cyberterrorism links the convergence of cyberspace and terrorism. This is not the convergence of cyberspace and something big or something bad. If a cyber attack is classified as cyberterrorism, it is likely to be both ‘big’ and ‘bad’ (Conway, 2014). However, the critical element is that the attack has a political motive, but it is less important than the attack being conducted through cyberspace or have been linked to the online environment during the process.
