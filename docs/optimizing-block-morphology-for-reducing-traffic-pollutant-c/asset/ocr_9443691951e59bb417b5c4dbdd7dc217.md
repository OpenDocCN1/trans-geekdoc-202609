# 4.3. Results of optimizations

Both the single-objective and multi-objective optimizations converged within 100 iterations (Fig. 9). The optimal result for the single-objective optimization has a Cped_AES of 0.080, while the multi-

objective optimization has a lower result with a Cped_AES of 0.035, and all three concentration values in the other regions are between 0.03 and 0.05. Whether single or multi-objective, the optimization framework incorporating ML surrogate models generated block configurations with lower polluting concentrations than the 1000 sampling cases. The traditional CFD simulation optimization process would take more than two months to meet such a large number of iterations. In contrast, the optimization process incorporating the ML surrogate model takes only 3 h to accomplish this requirement (Table 6).

The multi-objective optimization revealed a synergistic relationship between the two global indices and similarly between the two local indices. However, there is a trade-off relationship between global and local indices. The global and local metrics cannot be optimal simultaneously. Although there was a certain linear correlation between the four concentrations (Fig. 10a), the solution space still formed a Pareto front surface in the last generation of the solution set (Fig. 10b). In particular, there is no absolute optimal solution between the two targets Cpod_Block and Cped_AES. The solution space formed by these two objectives can be seen as a clear trade-off which indicates that all regions’ pollutant concentrations decreased during the optimization. However, when the optimization results converge to vary in a smaller interval, there is some conflict between the global and local objectives.
