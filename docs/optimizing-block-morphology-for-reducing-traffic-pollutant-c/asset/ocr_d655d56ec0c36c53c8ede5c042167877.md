# 3.1. Parametric model development

The complexity and a large number of morphological properties of actual urban blocks make it difficult to comprehensively analyze the morphological effects on pollutant diffusion [53]. Therefore, a block type with more pronounced building height standard deviation characteristics needs to be selected for discussion based on the diversity of block morphology. The case study utilized the block basic morphological settings based on one of the clustering results of Wuhan residential block morphology from previous studies [28]. These blocks have a relatively large BH_std ranging from 20 to 35 m. To examine how BH_std and block layout affect traffic pollutant concentrations, the model fixed the FAR and Rb_c at 2.3 and 0.22, respectively, while varying the BH_std of the block from 10 to 35 m. The formula for calculating the standard deviation of building height in this paper is shown in Eq. (1) [54]:

```
BH_{std} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (h_i - \bar{h})^2} (1)
```

where n is the number of buildings in the target block which is 21 in this paper. h_i is the height of each building. \bar{h} is the average height of the buildings in the block, which is 31.43 m. The block scale was also fixed according to this type of block: 530 m for the long side and 270 m for the wide side. The target block had 35 sites for building placement (Fig. 3a), but only 21 sites were occupied (Fig. 3b), and the total building area was 330,000 m^2 to maintain consistent FAR and Rb_c. These building placements were numbered along the Y-axis by A-E and the X-axis by 1-7 (Fig. 3a). The study only considered the height and location of 21 buildings in the target block.
