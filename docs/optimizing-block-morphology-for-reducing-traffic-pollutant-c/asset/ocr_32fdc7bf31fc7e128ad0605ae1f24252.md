The distribution of AQI in the sampling cases shows that AES is vulnerable to light and moderate pollution. Although pollutant concentrations within the AES are lower than along the street canyon area, the AES is also an area where residents often engage in outdoor activities in the neighborhood [72]. Prolonged outdoor activities in light and moderate pollution areas still pose a greater risk of exposure to traffic-related air pollutants for residents. Long-term exposure to lightly polluted areas may irritate healthy people, so it is recommended that children, the elderly, and people with heart and respiratory diseases should reduce prolonged, high-intensity outdoor exercise [61]. Therefore, more attention needs to be paid to the air environment in the AES, and design strategies that can improve the air quality in the area need to be proposed.

# 5.2. Effect of block configurations on pollutant concentrations

The ML models can be applied to generate partial dependence plots to examine the nonlinear effects of individual variables on pollutant concentrations. Partial dependence plots (PDPs) are popular for interpreting ML models. They show how the target prediction changes when a feature varies while considering the average effect of all other features [73]. The relationships between BH_std and the four regional pollutant concentration indicators were analyzed separately. It was found that the effects of BH_std on the global and local indicators were not the same (Fig. 13a). Consistent with the results in Section 1 of Chapter 4, BH_std is almost linearly related to the global metrics, Cpod_Block and Cped_Block. The larger the BH_std, the lower the average pollutant concentration in the block. This indicates that the degree of staggered building heights within the block positively affects reducing pollutant concentrations [30,74]. However, It is also mentioned in many empirical studies that BH_std does not have a single linear relationship with pollutant concentration [25,28]. There is also literature suggesting that the range of positive effects of BH_std needs to be discussed [54]. In this study, BH_std below 25 m instead leads to an increase in pollutant concentration for local metrics. Only when the BH_std exceeds 25 m does the pollutant concentration decrease with increasing it (Fig. 13a). This illustrates that the mitigation of traffic pollutant concentration impacts in the AES region does not rely on increasing BH_std alone. There is spatial heterogeneity in the effect of BH_std on pollutant concentrations.

The reason for the difference in the impact mechanism of BH_std in global and local metrics can be analyzed by the relationship between building height and pollutant concentration at various sites within the block. Fig. 13b and c shows how the building heights at different locations within the block affect Cpod_Block and Cped_AES, respectively. Overall, the building heights have less effect on Cpod_Block than on Cped_AES. The effect of building height on pollutant concentrations in these two areas diminishes as the location is deeper into the neighborhood. In particular, the regression curves of building heights in row C with Cpod_Block converge to a horizontal line. This suggests that a block-wide building configuration still needs to be examined when focusing on pollutant concentrations in the AES area. The effect trend of buildings in rows A and B is not the same, but the increase in height led to a slight rise in Cpod_Block. C-row buildings had little effect on Cpod_Block but a more significant positive correlation with Cped_AES. Buildings in rows A, B, and C positively correlate with Cped_AES. An increase in building height leads to significant growth in Cped_AES, especially when the building height in the area is greater than 66 m. It can be observed that the building heights in rows E and D are negatively correlated with the pollutant concentrations in both regions. The effect trend of building height in row E on the global and local indicators is also very consistent. Both pollutant concentration indicators remain constant at a high value, then drop sharply with the increase in building height until a critical point, and remain constant. The only difference may be the location where these two inflection points occur. # 5.3. CFD simulation analysis of optimization results

The top five solutions of the single-objective optimization were simulated separately by CFD. The obtained Cped_AES ranking slightly deviated from that predicted by the ML model. As shown in Table 7a, the actual simulated concentration of S1, predicted initially to have the lowest Cped_AES ranking, was ranked fourth among the five cases. S4, predicted initially to have a relatively high Cped_AES, performed the best in the simulation results, with the lowest concentration of 0.056. Comparing the optimized results with Control group A, all results were found to reduce the Cped_AES by more than 83% and the Cpod_Block by at least 50%. To determine the role of BH_std in the dilution of pollutant concentrations in the block, a control group B with a homogenous height corresponding to five sets of optimized results was established. Compared with control group B, S3 performed the best, with 59%, 74%, 62%, and 84% reductions in global and local metrics, respectively. This indicates that BH_std is more critical in reducing pollutants concentration in this block configuration.
