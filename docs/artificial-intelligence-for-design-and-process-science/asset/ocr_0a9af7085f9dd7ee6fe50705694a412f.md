# 3.2 Telopolarity

Telopolarity includes the possible effects of utilizing both human and artificial intelligence. It addresses both the positive and negative effects of these intelligence interactions, as well as the difficulties in determining the consequences for personal and social life that come along with the greater use of AI in the equation.

Heidegger's perspective highlights how technology drives us to view things through the lens of predictability and attainability by putting human-technology interaction in the spotlight. It fosters an endless thirst for advancement by making the world flexible to our planning, arrangements, and manipulations [27]. However, when the human mind struggles with the pervasiveness of technology, such a worldview may obscure the necessity for introspection and other strategies. ANT breeds this discussion to take into account how non-human actors, may influence the potential results of technological advancement. All participants in a system are interrelated, according to ANT, which presents telolarity as a complex interaction between human and non-human components. However, we may neglect the behavior or autonomy of sophisticated AI systems because its main attention is on network connections.

The entire impact of technological breakthroughs, particularly those involving AI-based devices, is frequently not until after they have gained popular use. At that point, technology has typically been thoroughly ingrained in our society norms, making its removal or correction extremely difficult. This emphasizes the necessity of thoughtful, anticipatory reflection on the possible effects of new technologies before they are incorporated into society. Heidegger suggests the concept of Besinnung, which translates as “sensibility,” to counteract the pervasive impact of technology [28]. We are better prepared to handle the intricacies of technological breakthroughs, thanks to our increased understanding of their ramifications and means. We may call the meditative that technology encourages us by encouraging sensitivity, which keeps us up to the world around us.

Although Heidegger's method and ANT have virtues of their own, they are insufficient for foretelling the effects of artificial intelligence, particularly in circumstances when AI becomes self-sustaining and acts independently of human considerations. Heidegger's theory, which mainly concentrates on how people interact with technology, not fully account for the autonomous behavior of artificial intelligence. Similar to ANT, sophisticated AI systems' emergent behavior or potential autonomy may be overlooked by ANT's focused on networked interactions. Currently, self-creating capabilities are not present in AI systems. The current status of AI is a product of human design, reliance on human oversight, adherence to human legal systems, and human-based ethical considerations. The code, data, and algorithms that humans used to create the AI systems ultimately dictate how they behave, even in a hypothetical situation in AI develops autonomy.
