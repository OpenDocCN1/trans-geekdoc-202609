# 3.3 Virtodynamica

Virtodynamica is an additional philosophical school having strong roots in the larger Intelligenticism movement. It provides a profound lens through which to consider the moral implications and foundations that are inextricably linked to the development of machines, particularly strengthened AI models. This idea emphasizes the critical necessity for ongoing reflection on the ethical bounds of AI and the necessary safeguards to ensure adherence to these limits. Virtodynamica emphasizes the inherent risks connected with allowing untrained human feedback loops. This is due to the constant chance that potential moral hazards be incorporated into these models or created with dubious morality by malevolent actors [29]. Virtodynamica must therefore be seen as a complicated interaction between human and non-human actors within a complex ethical network that is closely related to the development of machines.

The AI models themselves are a part of this complex web, are the data that power their learning, the architects who create their capabilities, the users who interact with them, and the social norms and laws that influence how they operate. Regardless of whether it is considered to be human or non-human, each entity makes a contribution to the network's ethical architecture, which influences elements including accountability, transparency, fairness, and bias. In conclusion, virtodynamica offers a framework for ethical considerations that is necessary for the ethical creation and use of intelligent machines, and it establishes a new fundamental tenet of modern philosophical thought toward the comprehension of AI-based intelligent machines.

An uneasy worry about intelligent machines displaying AI models amplifying biases inherent in their training data occurs within this network [30]. Virtodynamica works to address this problem by promoting thorough testing, ongoing observation, iterative improvements, and network interest alignment to give justice and inclusivity priority when implementing intelligent systems.
