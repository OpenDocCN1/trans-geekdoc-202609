These scientists were determined, as Stephen Hawking put it, to “know the mind of God.” That inspired them to work prodigiously hard.

Though Albert Einstein was not religious in any conventional sense, he, too, driven by a passion to know. In and elsewhere he wrote movingly of his Autobiographical Notes, he recounts his fascination as a child when his father showed him a compass: “This experience made a deep and lasting impression upon me. Something deeply had to be behind things.” And elsewhere of his struggle to reach the general theory of relativity: “The years of searching in the dark for a truth that one feels but cannot express, the desire and the alternations of confidence and misgiving until one breaks to clarity and understanding.”

Although I am no longer a believer, I am grateful for the training I received in Roman Catholicism, which taught me to see the world as having grandeur and meaning in the physical world, which became habitual. That vision helped inspire my wishful searching for grandeur and meaning

Finally, let me add that in deciding what to wish for, a feeling for beauty is an invaluable asset. Exposure to beautiful objects and sounds art and music as well as beautiful ideas ideas can develop that sense.

It’s a wonderful thing and not, I think, an accident that we humans naturally take delight in imagination and wishful thinking. They help us to solve problems and thrive. Teachers and managers should encourage them, and even the most practical, hard-headed engineers and scientists should cultivate them.
