By the end of the course, students are able to discuss the limits of AI, including various ethical dilemmas that AI technology may present in the future as further developments are made. One student brings up the fact that “the people are actually developing the technology will have their biases,” while another student states that the potential for future developers working in AI have “a lot of power and with lot of responsibility.” Many students agree that AI technology needs to be monitored because it can “definitely be used for good, but it can also be used for very bad things.” Through the process of completing the project, including the group work (see finding #2) and the emphasis on community (see finding #3), students now understand there are both scientific implications and humanistic implications when developing AI technology.

# Appreciation for Project-Based Learning Initial students find that the modules used in the courses at the three institutions are type of learning experiences students desire, as expressed in the interviews. For example, when asked what advice they would give instructors on how to make course content more relevant to their lived experiences, at the beginning of the course, the majority of focus group students begin the semester wanting as many opportunities as possible to apply the course in contextualized situations.

During focus groups at the end of the semester, students across all courses refer to the “AI for Social Good” project in the present course as an example of what other STEM-related courses should use as a summative assessment.

> “Project-based learning (PBL) is a type of inquiry-based learning students with opportunities to construct knowledge through real-world questions, problems, and practices [35] to create meaningful learning experiences [36] that include peer social interactions and the sharing of knowledge and understanding with others. With a PBL assessment, the students discuss aspects of the “AI for Social Good” project they enjoyed, including collaborating with their peers, applying their personal knowledge and expertise, and demonstrating their content through an authentic context. When talking about the advantages of collaboration, one student explains that “a huge advantage of working in a group is that you’re able to get perspectives from many different places, demonstrating their understanding of how effective group-based learning is compared to the traditional, passive learning found in lecture-based courses. Another student even calls the present course “an outlier” STEM courses tend to focus more on a technical understanding, or it’s very just ‘get it done,’ instead of having human factor to it,” which, they feel, their course did take into consideration in relation to the course content, completing the PBL assessment with their peers, and designing the final product with other communities in mind.
