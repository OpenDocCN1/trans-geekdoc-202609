# Introduction

Deep neural networks have become a versatile tool for a range of problems [1] with successes in many challenging applications, such as speech recognition [2], image recognition [3, 4], and machine translation [5–7]. Along with this success is a paradigm shift from feature designing to architecture designing, e.g., from SIFT [8] and HOG [9] to AlexNet [4], VGGNet [10], GoogleNet [11], and ResNet [12]. However, the performance of these systems tends to be highly sensitive to the specific network architecture and the available dataset. Although the effort of many tools requires expertise and time. Even simple choices as the architecture hyperparameters for multilayer perceptrons (MLP), i.e., the number of hidden layers and units, are complex decisions with significant impact on performance on specific dataset [13]. This difficulty of designing architecture emphasizes the need for Automatic Neural Architecture (ANA) design [14].

Despite the fact ANA design has been a topic of research for many years, current approaches tend to suffer from limitations in terms of their ability to address larger network architecture spaces [15, 16] or to transfer problems within a domain without requiring complete re-training on the new target problem [17–19]. The former is usually due to the use of a discrete,specific set of network transformations that can be used during optimization, while the latter stems from the use of a local architecture search procedure that relies on frequent re-evaluation and retraining during the local optimization process.

The goal in this chapter is to develop a more general framework that uses reinforcement learning with a flexible architecture modification action space that allows to represent a broader domain through architecture embedding and that facilitates planning through a performance prediction [20] component to learn a transferable policy for architecture optimization. Initial experiments in this chapter show that the framework, trained on a set of classification problems, can successfully learn a policy that results in an optimized architecture on a new problem without need for retraining.
