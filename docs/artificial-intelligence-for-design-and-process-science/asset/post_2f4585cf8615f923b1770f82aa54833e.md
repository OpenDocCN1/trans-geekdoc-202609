## Concept of Communication Dynamics (CD)

The groundbreaking communication dynamics theory is built on four foundational assumptions:

**Assumption 1** The universe (nature) displays anisotropy.

**Assumption 2** The universe adheres to the second law of thermodynamics (arrow of time), implying that time is an evolving metric of the universe's entropy. The continual increase in the total entropy of the universe denotes that time is irreversible.

**Assumption 3** A shift from a low entropy state to a higher one is perceived as acceleration. This apparent acceleration is consistent with the general theory of relativity, meaning as the universe accelerates, the temporal speed (measured in clock ticks) varies across microsystems. Such variances might be perceived as different colors by observers (Wilczek, 2015).

**Assumption 4** The universe's essence is encapsulated as a one-dimensional communication system in reality. All observations, including those having multi-dimensionality, stem from this singular, accelerating dimension termed space-time.

To craft a model founded on these assumptions, certain concepts warrant clarification:

**Definition 1**: Computing. A universal state of existence, equally applicable to both natural and artificial systems across every accuracy scale. In the realm of communication, we substitute traditional physics terms such as interactions, conduction, and entanglement with “computing.” Here, a consistent rule governs the exchange of information, with computations taking place in what we term as space-time.

**Definition 2**: Space-Time Point. In the intricate tapestry of communication dynamics, a space-time point, represented as *x + it*, belongs to the complex domain. Time is symbolized by “it,” while space by “x.”

**Definition 3**: T-Vectors and U-Matrices. Introducing the timed vector (T-vector), a novel mathematical object in communication dynamics is designed to seamlessly incorporate space-time. Expanding the typical vector definition, the T-vector is characterized by magnitude, direction, and uniquely, frequency. Fourier series aptly capture the frequency attributes of T-vectors. A distinct matrix made of T-vectors is denominated as the U-matrix.

**Definition 4**: Eigenvalue. In the lexicon of communication dynamics, the eigenvalue associated with T-vectors and U-matrices symbolizes energy, providing an insightful metric to understand the energy attributes of systems.

# Approach

Mathematical models of atomic and molecular systems, grounded in both classical and quantum physics, utilize three-dimensional spatial portrayals of atoms. Such models emerge from the classical mechanics assumption that space and time are distinct dimensions. However, this premise, questioned by Einstein's theory of general relativity, limits these models in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. We propose that the constructs of 3D space and time are predominantly human-derived perceptions, suggesting that mathematics can depict the universe beyond these confines. To enhance computational capabilities and represent physical reality across scales, it's essential to rethink the 3D space-time paradigm.

We developed the communication dynamics model that challenges the conventional space-time belief, offering a unified mathematical formulation that encapsulates physical significance. In this model, one-dimensional frequency space evolving hyperbolically over a clock time (or T-vector). Our model permits representation precision across varying system scales, introducing an adaptive precision mechanism. With this model, precision can range from the minimum quantum time, represented by Planck's constant (h), to complex entities like atoms, molecules, and even biomolecules like proteins and DNA. This model facilitates seamless cross-scale integration using a uniform formula, presenting an avenue to refine foundational physics theories by shifting from a 3D space-time representation to a frequency-vector model.

Communication dynamics systematically analyze and compare chemical structures at any chosen scale. It holds the potential to address data-heavy and computationally demanding challenges in chemical systems. In prior work [1], we introduce unique communication channel representations for every element in the periodic table, conceptualizing electron orbitals as distinct communication channels. This innovative approach allowed for the depiction of intrinsic orbital properties, including energy and radius. The approach is translatable to modeling complex chemical systems.

Our approach to chemical systems starts with the building of a communication channel representation of electron shell structure. We approach this concept by taking inspiration from Shannon's capacity theory, which defines the maximum amount of information, or data capacity, that can be sent over any channel of medium. From a first principle, we assume that space-time is fundamentally dynamic and at a smallest level, can be most efficiently described as a periodic evolving (Eq. 4). Evolution is conceptualized as occurring in quantum "jump steps," represented in a complex number plane. Subsequently, we develop this approach to accommodate four quantum numbers (Table 1). ## Compounded Error Graphs in Communication Dynamics

Our mathematical models are developed based on the concept that communication theory can represent various scales of biological units including electrons, atoms, molecules, cells, and organisms. Compounded error channels (error contained, compounded channels) represent the interactions and assemblies between signal generation and moments in space-time where signal is modeled to be. Figure 1 shows a sample communication and a related error (or information) content graph.

The symbols on the left side correspond to a signal, and the symbols on the right side show the mapping of the sent and received signals. On the left in Fig. 1, we show Shannon's representation of an error content graph. In this representation, the information content of the lower and uppercase symbols is the same. If lower-case letter corresponds to the same letter (uppercase) on the other side, no error is encountered in the communication.

In the polygon formulation (Fig. 1, right), each of these symbols may be considered to be a point on a circle and the error pairs as lines joining these points. Inherently, in the polygon, we understand $a \rightarrow a, b \rightarrow b$, etc. can represent an error-free transmission over space-time, whereas $a \rightarrow b \rightarrow c \rightarrow d \rightarrow e \rightarrow A$ (implied cycle) efficiently represents the dynamic error content of the graph [2], or equivalently, we can call this information entropy [1].
