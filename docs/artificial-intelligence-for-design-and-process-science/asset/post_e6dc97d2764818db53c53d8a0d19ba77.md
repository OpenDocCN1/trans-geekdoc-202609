# Shortcuts: On the Misleading Promise of Language Models

**Abstract** Geetali Bihani and Julia Ray

The advent of large language models (LLMs) has enabled significant gains in natural processing. However, recent studies have found that LLMs often resort to shortcuts, lacking generalizability. This chapter provides a concise survey of relevant research and puts forth a perspective on shortcut learning, contributing to our comprehension of language models.

**Keywords** Large language models · Natural language understanding · Shortcut learning · Generalization

## Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have become the convention in the field of natural processing in recent years, showing performance gains in a variety of NLU tasks, including question answering, sentiment analysis, and commonsense reasoning. Whether such gains are meaningful depends on the quality of evaluation.

Unfortunately, LLMs have been shown to exploit spurious associations and dataset biases as "shortcuts," achieving inflated scores on benchmarks while lacking generalization on out-of-distribution samples. Recent works have shown that LLMs tend to learn shortcuts based on cues such as "not," keywords, and language variations.

## 2 What Is Shortcut Learning?

Shortcut learning refers to the phenomenon where models rely on superficial cues in the training data to make predictions instead of learning the semantics to perform an NLU task. This overreliance on specific features or biases results in poor generalization in out-of-distribution settings. Identifying shortcut learning in language models is an ongoing research area, with recent works utilizing model attention, dataset statistics, and human-annotated samples to identify spurious correlations.
