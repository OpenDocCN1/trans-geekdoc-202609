A key aspect of an effective chatbot's functionality lies in its ability to understand and interpret user intent from its classes a task commonly known as intent classification. Intent classification involves user utterances into predefined categories, each representing a distinct goal [5]. Successful intent classification provides the basis for generating relevant, contextualized responses, enhancing the user experience. Traditionally, intent classification was achieved using rule-based systems or feature engineering approaches, which often struggled to capture the complexities of natural language and are also time-consuming.

The emergence of pre-trained models, such as all_MiniLM_L6_v2, which is one of the models has revolutionized NLP tasks, including intent classification. These models pre-trained on large quantities of text data that have an inherent understanding of linguistic structures and contextual indexes. Their application to intent classification offers the potential to improve accuracy, adapt to diverse domains, and reduce reliance on hand-crafted features.
