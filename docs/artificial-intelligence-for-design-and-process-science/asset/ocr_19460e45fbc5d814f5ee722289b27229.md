MDM is an approach that moves away from drawing and other definition tools, such as natural language descriptions [28] and 3D models, and moves to interactive 3D models. MDM converts the 3D models through model transformation into modular manufacturing operations, digital manufacturing processes, and digital threads that are ultimately realized using additive manufacturing techniques or on the factory floor. Model transformation takes as input the model corresponding to the previous level in the MDM hierarchy and transforms it into the equivalent model of the level immediately below in the MDM hierarchy.

# Product Ideation and Design (PID) MDM enables product engineers and customers to imagine, think, and act on (complex) product development projects. The PID layer of the MDM architecture is essentially a series of sessions that represent a formalized part of the product design phase. Different stakeholders, including designers, developers, engineers, and customers, come to share their visions, insights, and ideas.

Product ideas take this information and apply it to the design and customization of specific products at the product-agnostic manufacturing model level. At this level, 3D-CAD models interactively capture and visualize all relevant information in real time for production and quality planning, controlling production processes, work orders generation, and coordination of related processes. In previous R&D efforts, we have designed and implemented a novel approach to reach this level that is formally grounded in the DT blueprints detailed in Sec. 5, through the 3D interactive product-oriented configuration management (PoCL), [3, 29].

# Platform-Independent Manufacturing Model (PIM) At this level, DT controls digital activities, processes, and recipes to create the first product of interest. PIM is rooted in the ISA98 Manufacturing Operations Management level, which provides resources, equipment, personnel, and energy to transform digitized raw material and component descriptions, quality checks, production flows, and production schedules into products. It carries the activities to adapt to a product.

Therefore, it includes the management of information about schedules, uses, skills, definitions, history, and the current status of all resources (suppliers, equipment, and materials) and related to the manufacturing facility. The data collected with the product design at the PIM level can be used to drive the manufacturing flow and provide PIM with an abundance of information that will drive production innovation. PIM combines this data collected from resources and other processes to provide a more complete real-time view of all manufacturing plants, production lines, and increasingly supply chains. Combined with the analytical power of digital tools, these increasingly rich datasets offer opportunities for innovation that brought advantages and benefits.

The digital threads derived in this layer are then used to create a fully functional prototype using additive manufacturing (AM) techniques or to instruct machines and robots on the shop floor to carry out product manufacturing functions.
