In the study [43], machine learning, in the form of a convolutional neural network (CNN), was applied to automate the scoring of the Test of Creative Thinking-Drawing Production (TCT-DP). The TCT-DP is a figure of creativity designed to assess creativity through a more gestalt-oriented perspective compared to traditional, quantitatively oriented divergent tasks. The TCT-DP utilizes incomplete and irregular figured fragments (such as a semicircle, a right angle, or a dot) within a large square frame to elicit responses. The study utilized a training dataset comprising 414 samples, with all images scored by at least two trainers.

Various models were employed to explore the capabilities of the proposed method, utilizing different numbers of classes ranging from two (representing low and high creativity) to seven (corresponding to the classes defined in the TCT-DP test manual). The study considered factors such as model accuracy (percentage of correctly classified images), sensitivity to the size of the training dataset, sensitivity to model parameters (e.g., number of epochs, learning rate), and utility (ability to provide useful information). Overall, the models achieved high accuracies (above 85% in all cases) in assessing creativity.

## 3.4 Design Creativity and AI

In the previous sections, we covered the AI applications related to creativity. Here, we would like to highlight the differences between creativity and design creativity and report on some of the studies employing AI in design creativity research.

To the best of our knowledge, there exist some studies such as [36, 51–53] targeting the AI applications in design creativity and how AI can help design/design creativity. In contrast, the authors studied the effects of design thinking on the cognition of AI learning, attitudes toward AI, idea creativity, and the product creativity of AI applications [54]. This study found that design thinking significantly enhances conceptual cognition of AI learning, particularly in relational contexts and classes of AI concepts, though it also had no significant effect on cross-linking and examples. Design thinking also effectively fosters positive views over AI input and processing due to its real-life applicability. Additionally, design thinking improves the creativity of AI-generated works, notably in their novelty, value, functionality, and elaboration, though not in material, style, and structure.

There also exist comprehensive reviews [23, 55, 56] investigating the effects of AI, specifically generative adversarial networks as toolbox for design practitioners [55], (in the last 15 years) are reported which indicates the wide usage of AI in design [57]. However, according to our investigation, in the field of design creativity, fewer AI-related studies can be found. It should be noted that other applications of AI or machine learning, design creativity research powered by AI has been increasing by leaps and bounds. In other reviews [58], shorter communications, and editorial letters [58–60], AI has been reported as a pivotal part of design creativity research indicating the importance of exploring opportunities to employ AI in design creativity processes.

## 4 Discussion

Creativity spans various disciplines and intersects with numerous fields. With the advancement of AI and computational machines, AI has found applications in various creativity studies. While much of the research has focused on creativity assessment, human-AI co-creativity, and the comparison between artificial and human creativity, there is a notable gap in exploring other aspects, such as simulating creativity using AI based on neurophysiological findings.

Existing literature predominantly enhances generic or artistic forms of creativity. However, there is a pressing need for further investigation into the challenges and limitations of machine learning and deep generative models, particularly in domains like design creativity, constraint handling, and simultaneous modeling of function and functional performance.

While many studies have primarily targeted tasks like AUT and TTCT, there is a lack of exploration in other creativity tasks. Although most studies suggest that AI outperforms human creativity levels in AUT and TTCT, it is crucial to consider additional factors like validity, appropriateness, and usefulness to comprehensively compare human and AI creativity.

In this domain, various AI methods and models have been employed, yet there is room for exploration and comparison with other AI methods. Choosing the appropriate AI tailored to specific creativity applications is essential, considering each method's unique capabilities and potential limitations. Future researchers should prioritize understanding AI methods, with workshops on prompt engineering being particularly beneficial.

Moreover, the social and psychological implications of AI application in creativity warrant attention. Future studies should investigate how AI impacts students' problem-identification capacity, convergent thinking, and long-term creativity when integrated into educational settings.

We would like to shed light on the aspect that has been less discussed in the literature. While several studies have applied AI or explored it in the realm of creativity, there are just a few studies that addressed the considerations of AI applications in design creativity. Investigating design creativity presents significant challenges due to its multifaceted nature, involving nonlinear cognitive processes and various tasks such as divergent and convergent thinking, perception, and memory retrieval.

Considering the points mentioned above, AI can be employed to investigate or model the intricate processes of design creativity. However, there is a necessity to understand the differences between creativity, design, and design creativity provide the foundation for future AI-based research in this field. To explore these intricate considerations, we shall well begin with the definitions to understand the connections between creativity and design to better understand design creativity. As mentioned before, creativity is defined as the ability to develop useful, novel, and surprising ideas [10-12, 61]. Design aims at changing existing situations into desired ones [62]. Design is nonlinear and complex [63] and lies at the heart of creativity [64-67]. On the one hand, by definition, creativity is a measure of the process of design, either intentional or unconscious. On the other hand, it is important to note that not all existing ones are inherently creative; differing only in their specific context. Design creativity typically denotes a scenario where the resulting product implies creativity. Design creativity is inherently unpredictable; yet it may seem—yet it happens. It is noteworthy that different designers may provide various solutions to the same design problem, and even the same designer generate diverse solutions for the same problem over time [10]. Creativity may spontaneously emerge even if one does not intend to create a creative design, whereas creative design just may not come out matter how hard one tries. Moreover, it is conceivable that a designer may lack creativity, while the product demonstrates creativity attributes, and conversely, a designer may exhibit creativity, while the resulting product does not [16]. Concerning what was covered in this chapter, future studies are recommended to focus on AI-powered design to creativity and design creativity. Although there exist several papers on “AI and creativity,” deep explorations are required to associate complex and abstract concepts of design creativity with AI-related ones. In addition, for the differences between creativity and design creativity, researchers should focus on AI-based design creativity studies as there are a relatively limited number of studies in the field.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the exploration of AI in creativity is still in its early stages, and there is a need to adopt advanced and updated AI methods to further enrich this area of research. Although the literature indicates some applications of AI in creativity, other types of AI-powered research, such as machine-machine creativity, AI in teams, and machine-machine creativity, will soon emerge due to the advancement of AI methods. Generative neural networks, for example, have the potential to be employed in creativity and design creativity applications. In this chapter, we aimed to cover the most pivotal topics in the realm of AI applications in creativity and design creativity. The intricate considerations must be taken into account when applying AI to design creativity, as there are several nuanced differences between design, creativity, and design creativity.
