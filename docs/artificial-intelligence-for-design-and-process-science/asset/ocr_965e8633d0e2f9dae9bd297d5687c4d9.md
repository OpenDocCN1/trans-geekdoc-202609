# 2 Background

# 2.1 Multi-word Expressions

English compound nouns exemplify some potential challenges faced by non-large
language model NLP systems employed to process MWEs. Such constructions are
diff to interpret as there may be multiple meaning relations between the nouns
involved [7]. For instance, the compound IMB Lecture could imply a lecture about,
by, or expressed by IBM [7]. This ambiguity requires contextual knowledge for
correct interpretation, as no syntactic analysis can differentiate between the
various possible semantic relations in a basic noun compound. This complexity
makes compound nouns excellent test case for assessing the semantic capabilities
of current transformer encoder NLP systems.

Recent research has dealt how pretrained transformer models grasp the
semantics of multi-word expressions (MWEs), even without targeted optimization
for these complex expressions. These studies have scrutinized the models’ ability
to comprehend MWEs and their semantic attributes, including their level of
compositionality [6], highlighting the models’ inherent capacity to decode the
nanced meanings of such phrases. Our findings seem to support a similar conclu-\sion, as they success in transformer encoder models accurately observing
semantic distance between simple compound nouns.

# 2.2 Language Models

The choice of a language model is crucial for the success of any NLP task, especially
in complex areas as multi-word expression (MWE) analysis. Large language
models, particularly transformer-based models like GPT-4, have given attention to
ther generative capabilities. Pioneered by Vaswani et al. [8], different transformer
models excel in different tasks as retrieving semantically similar text and
producing convincing, contextually relevant text.
