```
$u'_{n}(t) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1} \sum_{m_{\ell}}=-\ell}^{\ell} \frac{e^{\ell(q+Im_{\ell})t}}{(q/n + Im_{\ell})^2} \quad (10a)$

In order to incorporate the effect of nuclei on electrons, Eq. (8) is further modified by introducing atomic number (A) (number of protons in the nuclei) and **q = 1 for nonradio isotopes**, and as a period number:

```
$u'_{\ell}(t) = \sum_{m_{\ell}}=-\ell}^{\ell} \frac{2 * n * n * y * e^{\ell(A+Im_{\ell})t}}{(\A + I m_{\ell})^2} \quad (10b)$

y is the row of the periodic table, A is the atomic number, and $n$ is the principal quantum number.

Eigenvalues play a pivotal role in linear algebra, particularly in the context of matrices and linear transformations. Within this theory, the eigenvalue of the U-matrix $u'_{\ell}(t)$ intrinsically symbolizes the energy of the communication channel, seamlessly with traditional physics interpretations.
