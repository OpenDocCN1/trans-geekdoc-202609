## 3.1 Generating Compound Noun Phrases and Analysis of Uncommon Phrases

Our research methodology was designed to focus on generating and analyzing compound noun phrases, delving into the nuances of semantic relationships within these phrases. We began by selecting a set of base nouns related to culinary items, specifically drink, salad, smoothie, chicken, and duck. These were chosen to cover a diverse range of food categories, ensuring a comprehensive examination of semantic interactions. To modify these nouns, we chose a variety of modifier nouns, starting with those that were closely related (e.g., edible fruits such as kiwi, strawberry, orange, apple) and then expanding to include modifiers with a broader semantic scope to explore a wide semantic field, from modifiers with direct relevance (e.g., orange with apple) to systematically varying the semantic scope between the base nouns and their modifiers, we enabled a detailed exploration and interpretation of how semantic proximity and diversity influence the formation of compound noun phrases.

In the analysis phase, we excluded noun phrases deemed extremely uncommon, as apple chicken, apple duck, chocolate salad, green chicken, green duck, kiwi chicken, kiwi duck, strawberry chicken, and strawberry duck, to maintain a focus on more commonly encountered combinations. However, we chose to include chocolate chicken and chocolate duck to evaluate their capabilities. In order to evaluate the models' capability to grasp nuanced semantic changes from modifier substitution, we measured the cosine similarities between compound noun phrases and their semantic similarity within compound noun phrases, enhancing our insights into the modified versions. Through this methodology, we aim to offer an exploration of semantic similarity, the cognitive processing of language and the potential of transformer encoder models in capturing complex semantic relationships.

Notably, a remarkable observation emerged when comparing noun phrases involving strawberry and chocolate, with these combinations showing a surprisingly high level of similarity, so more than when strawberry was paired with other fruits. This observation suggests a unique semantic interaction between strawberry and chocolate, potentially due to their common association in culinary contexts and popular culture, a fascinating aspect of language where cultural and contextual factors significantly influence semantic relationships.

Furthermore, an anomaly was observed in the context involving the modifier drink, as depicted in Fig. 2. Unlike other categories, drink combinations deviated from expected similarity patterns, suggesting an unexpected complexity in how beverages are semantically processed compared to solid food items. This deviation warrants further exploration, as it may reveal insights into the distinct cognitive categorizations of food and beverages in language.

## 5 Conclusion

This research delve into the intricate realm of semantic similarity within compound noun phrases, employing the advanced capabilities of bidirectional transformer language models. Our systematic investigation yielded findings that not only corroborate the efficacy of transformer encoder models, such as BERT and sBERT, in processing compound nouns despite their challenging linguistic features but enrich our understanding of semantic dynamics in natural language processing (NLP). Our we studied the hypothesis that alterations to the first noun in compound phrases generally produce higher semantic scores than modifications to the second noun, emphasizing the pivotal role of the head noun in determining the semantic essence of the phrase. This observation underscores the fundamental principle of head-driven semantic interpretation within compound noun constructs.
