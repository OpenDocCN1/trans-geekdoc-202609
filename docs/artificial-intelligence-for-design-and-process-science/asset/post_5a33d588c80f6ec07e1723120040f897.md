
## A Reinforcement Learning Framework for Transferable Neural Architecture Optimization

Abstract
Historically, deep learning has proven increasingly useful and has become a frequently used tool for a range of applications. However, the sensitivity of deep learning systems to the specific architecture, in terms of learning ability and performance, have made them difficult to utilize for non-experts and emphasized the need for automatic architecture optimization. Previous work largely optimizes a network for one problem using architecture search, requiring significant amounts of time training. To alleviate this, the proposed approach learns a network optimization policy on architecture embedding that transfers across problems and permits the optimization of networks for new problems without excessive training. Siamese networks learn an effective embedding of the architecture space together with feature vectors to capture the complexity of the problem, serving as a base for reinforcement learning agents to acquire a transferable policy to derive high-performing architectures. To demonstrate the feasibility and potential of the approach, initial experiments with a set of classification networks are performed, showing the ability of the approach to learn a policy that optimizes the architecture even for new problems. In summary, we employed a novel RL approach to successfully learn a policy for network optimization in a derived architecture space.

**Keywords** RL, TD3, Actor, Critic

## Introduction

Deep neural networks have become a versatile tool for a range of problems with successes in many challenging applications, such as speech recognition [1], image recognition [3, 4], and machine translation [5–7]. Along with this success is a paradigm shift from feature designing to architecture designing, e.g., from SIFT [8] and HOG [9] to AlexNet [4], VGGNet [10], and ResNet [12]. However, the performance of these systems tends to be highly sensitive to the specific architecture and available dataset. The effort of many tools requires expertise and time. Even simple choices as the architecture hyperparameters for multilayer perceptrons (MLP), the number of hidden layers and units, are complex decisions with significant impact on performance on specific datasets [13]. This difficulty of designing architecture emphasizes the need for Automatic Neural Architecture (ANA) design [14].

Despite the fact ANA design has been a topic of research for many years, current approaches tend to suffer from limitations in terms of their ability to address larger architecture spaces [15, 16] or to transfer problems within a domain without requiring complete re-training on the target problem [17–19]. The former is usually due to the use of a discrete set of network transformations that can be used during optimization, while the latter stems from the use of a local architecture search procedure that relies on frequent re-evaluation and retraining during the optimization process.

The goal in this chapter is to develop a more general framework that uses reinforcement learning with a flexible architecture modification space that allows to represent a broader domain through architecture embedding and facilitates planning through a performance prediction [20] component to learn a transferable policy for architecture optimization. Initial experiments in this chapter show that the framework, trained on a set of classification problems, can successfully learn a policy that results in an optimized architecture on a new problem without need for retraining.

## Related Work

Different aspects of the Automatic Neural Architecture (ANA) modelling procedure have been studied, except for the practice of repetitive trial and error, which is time-consuming and produces uncertain results [17]. Reinforcement learning (RL) has been effectively used to learn a policy to incrementally build neural network classifiers for a specific problem [17, 18]. In reinforcement learning [21]-based methods, the choice of a component of the architecture is regarded as an action. A sequence of architectures defines an architecture of a neural network whose performance is used as the reward. Many of these techniques use a small, pre-defined set of architecture modifications [15, 17], which limits their applicability to specific architecture types. One approach to attempt to address this is the use of an architecture embedding space to permit a general, continuous action space [18, 19]. However, target performance over this embedding space tends to be highly non-convex and leads to many local extrema, limiting the optimization in this approach to several problems, requiring retraining for each target problem and extensive evaluation training for each individual architecture optimization.

Generalization over target problems has been the focus of [15], where a generic policy for network optimization is learned. This approach, however, is limited by its simple action space with ensembles of fully connected networks and cannot be applied to the wider range of problems.

In this chapter, an approach is proposed that combines the concepts of a network embedding space with the learning of a transferable policy to allow for more general architecture search and to limit the need for extensive retraining for new problems. To address the challenges of complex dynamics of network performance under architecture transformations in an embedding space, the approach condenses and organizes network embeddings using Siamese networks and utilizes a reinforcement learning framework for complex policy formation that permits the system to more reliably achieve high-performing final architectures in the presence of significant local extrema for task performance. Taking inspiration from previous work using network embeddings [15, 18, 19, 22] and various approaches to network architecture search [14, 17, 23, 24], we propose an architecture that provides the promise to extend to various network types as well as to transfer optimization strategies across problems.

Future avenues of research should extend beyond the current scope of multi-word expressions (MWEs) analyzed to encompass a broader spectrum of linguistic structures and delve into additional languages. Moreover, integrating external knowledge bases or ontologies could offer substantial enhancements in models’ grasp of complex semantic relationships, paving the way for more nuanced and contextually aware language processing capabilities.

In synthesizing our findings, it becomes evident that transformer models are not only proficient in identifying and interpreting compound nouns but also offer pivotal insights into the dynamic nature of linguistic semantics. This capability signifies a considerable advancement in search technologies, allowing for more sophisticated and semantically aware query processing.
