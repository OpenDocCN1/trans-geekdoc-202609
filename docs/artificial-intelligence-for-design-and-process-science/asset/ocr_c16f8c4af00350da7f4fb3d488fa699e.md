# Introduction

Large Industry 4.0 or smart manufacturing is signaled by a transition toward a physical-to-digital-to-physical connection. Smart manufacturing represents the next industrial revolution, realized through the application of digital technologies in industrial processes. A smart manufacturing ecosystem will interweave all aspects of manufacturing, from plant operations to the supply chain, exhibiting a set of connected machines, process automation, and manufacturing units, enabling virtual tracking of capital assets, processes, and resources. The aim is to create an integrated, automated, and optimized production flow that produces increasingly individualized customer products [1].

The manufacturing paradigm advocated by smart manufacturing allows systems (machines, sensors, devices, actuators, valves, and networking technologies) to have both a digital counterpart and a physical interaction with the real world. This results in a digital twin model that mirrors the physical and the virtual worlds [2]. This digital-twin approach enables manufacturers to build an intelligent (sensor-equipped) virtual, digital counterpart capturing any product at any stage of production. The digital twin approach can analyze the behavior of a digital product and provide actionable insights that guide informed deisions about processes and materials using advanced visualization tools, such as 3D CAD/CAM tools, and simulations that observe the impact on the shape and properties of the physical counterpart of the product.

The digital twin representation shows the evolution of a product and production process in every stage of the product life cycle [3]. This helps designers and engineers to optimize performance, predict behavior, and gain insights from past design and production experience. Data from the field can be fed back into the digital twin and used as the basis for further innovation and improvement, enabling manufacturers to virtually recreate a product and its production and even simulate its performance in the real world without taking any physical action.

The vision of smart manufacturing and digital twins requires new languages for modeling, correlating smart digital product aspects (product, process, supplier, and machine knowledge) while supporting the distributed nature of industrial processes.

To address these challenges, this article proposes (i) a novel digital twin knowledge model (a semantic model that captures relevant direct and indirect product production data) and (ii) a digital twin programming paradigm that operates on top of the twin knowledge model and serves to correlate and manage readily available product/manufacturing information and processes. The work presented in this chapter focuses on smart manufacturing enablers and building blocks in [3] by integrating and synergizing an advanced model-driven product engineering approach that leverages the power of meta programming languages. Model-driven engineering has the potential to subsum the complexity of the manufacturing ecosystem to simplify the product design and development processes
