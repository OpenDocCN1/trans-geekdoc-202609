A periodic function $f(t)$ with $w_0 = 1$ can be represented by complex (exponential) form of the Fourier series where:

```
$f(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_k e^{ikt} \quad (4)$
```

with $|c_k e^{ikt}| = |c_k|$. The Fourier series of a uniform polygon is given as:

```
$f_n(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{e^{ikt}}{k^2} \quad (5)$
```

where $k = q + n\ell$ with $\ell = -p \dots p$ and $\ell \in [-p, p]$.nThe following parameters are defined to represent the polygon with the Fourier series: $q \in Z^*$ corresponds to the jump step value of uniform polygon; $n \in Z^*$ corresponds to the number of vertices of the uniform polygon; $\ell \in Z^*$ determines the summation interval $[-p, p]$, in which the Fourier series will be calculated; $N \in Z^*$ is the sampling factor for the numerical algorithms and it determines the number of samples $t \in [t_0, 2\pi + t]$.
Including all of the parameters of the algorithm, the new equation is:

```
$u(\ell, t) = \frac{e^{i(q+n\ell)t}}{(q+n\ell)^2} \quad (6)$
```

where $t_r = t_0, t_1, \dots, t_{N-1} = (2\pi + t_0)$ and $\ell = -p, \dots -p+1, \dots,1,0, \dots, p-1, p$. Since the resulting Fourier series calculation is the sum of each Fourier term at time $t_0$, we can rewrite the equation for $u(\ell, t)$ as:

```
$u(\ell, t) = \sum_{\ell=-p}^{p} \frac{e^{i(q+n\ell)t}}{(q+n\ell)^2} \quad (7)$
```

By observing the quantum numbers in Table 1 and the parameters of our equation, we produced our new equation (Eq. 8).

```
$u'_l(t) = \sum_{m_l=-\ell}^{\ell} \frac{e^{i(q+lm_l)t}}{(q/n+lm_l)^2} \quad (8)$
```
