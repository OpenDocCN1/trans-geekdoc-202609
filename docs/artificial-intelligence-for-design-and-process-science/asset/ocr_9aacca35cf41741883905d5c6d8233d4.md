Past examples of CRC demonstrate the connection it has to CRSP. Examples include girls using digital technology to educate their local community about relevant, but stigmatized, social issues (e.g. pregnancy) [23], using “social GIS” to explore social justice issues with science and mapping tools [24], using computational skills to share Indigenous knowledge and language [20], and with young women to develop a Zambian Women’ Rights mobile app [25]. Past research has established that when students are encouraged to embody their identities, cultures, and communities into STEM learning and computing, students’ motivation is enhanced, which has the power to increase student agency and STEM participation [10, 26]. Teaching through a CRC lens creates opportunities to align the goals of students and community members through a collaboration that leverages “community assets” that are relevant to the students and their lived realities [22] (p. 5) while promoting equity in CS [27].

# 3 AI4SG Curriculum Design

We designed AI learning modules called “AI for Social Good (AI4SG)” to educate undergraduates from STEM and non-STEM backgrounds. These instructional modules are a result of interdisciplinary work from the research team and are based on CRC and targeting sustainability as an application case [20]. CRC is particularly suitable for interdisciplinary, community-based, and inclusive AI due to its five tenets: (1) all students are capable of digital innovation, with varying levels of learning support and application; (2) the learning contexts support transformational use of technology, modules will be designed to address relevant social issues in students’ communities; (3) learning should be about one’s self along intersecting sociocultural lines that allow for technical innovation, modules are interdisciplinary and the exchange of cultural values will be incentivized; (4) technology should be vehicle in which students reflect and demonstrate understanding of their intersectional identities, modules will be designed for students to express and bring their full self into the practice and community; (5) barometers for technological progress should consider who creates, for whom, and to what rather than endures culturally and irrelevant curriculum, modules will emphasize the importance of social good, community, and ethics in AI, allowing students to contribute to their communities as they learn AI. In addition to CRC, the project also incorporated sustainability, particularly the Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), into the AI4SG modules. This integration aims to educate students about the role of AI in achieving these global goals and encourage them to consider sustainability in their AI projects as a case for social good.
