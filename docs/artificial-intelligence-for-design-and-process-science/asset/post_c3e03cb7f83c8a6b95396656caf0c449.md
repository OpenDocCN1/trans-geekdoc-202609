At first glance, each may be straightforward compositions, a leaf from a tree and juice from one, respectively. However, the former also come to symbolize a covering for embarrassing or shameful. While the meaning of orange juice can be determined from its individual parts, the meaning of fig leaf cannot for all cases. It should be noted that orange juice is also ambiguous, but the ambiguity stems from orange being a fruit or a juice, while thus ambiguous, orange juice is still compositional.

Moreover, the ability to identify and understand compositional compound nouns has profound implications for improving search functionalities. ## 2 Background

### 2.1 Multi-word Expressions

English compound nouns exemplify some potential challenges faced by non-large language model NLP systems employed to process MWEs. Such constructions are diff to interpret as there may be multiple meaning relations between the nouns involved [7]. For instance, the compound IMB Lecture could imply a lecture about, by, or expressed by IBM [7]. This ambiguity requires contextual knowledge for correct interpretation, as no syntactic analysis can differentiate between the various possible semantic relations in a basic noun compound. This complexity makes compound nouns excellent test case for assessing the semantic capabilities of current transformer encoder NLP systems.

Recent research has dealt how pretrained transformer models grasp the semantics of multi-word expressions (MWEs), even without targeted optimization for these complex expressions. These studies have scrutinized the models’ ability to comprehend MWEs and their semantic attributes, including their level of compositionality [6], highlighting the models’ inherent capacity to decode the nuanced meanings of such phrases. Our findings seem to support a similar conclusion, as they success in transformer encoder models accurately observing semantic distance between simple compound nouns.

### 2.2 Language Models

The choice of a language model is crucial for the success of any NLP task, especially in complex areas as multi-word expression (MWE) analysis. Large language models, particularly transformer-based models like GPT-4, have given attention to ther generative capabilities. Pioneered by Vaswani et al. [8], different transformer models excel in different tasks as retrieving semantically similar text and producing convincing, contextually relevant text.

Encoder-only models, such as BERT and sBERT, have shown capability in our specific focus of semantic similarity of phrases. Unlike GPT-based models that process text unidirectionally, BERT processes text in a bidirectional manner, allowing for a richer understanding of context [9]. sBERT, an adaptation of BERT, refines this approach by producing single-vector sentence embeddings [10]. These embeddings are critical for our objective: comparing semantic similarities in phrases (that can be treated as short sentences), particularly in the context of noun phrase modifications within MWEs.
