The maximum of $P(r)$ is obtained by setting $dP/dr=0$ and solving for $r$. For 1s orbital, the maximum of $P(r)$ is when $r=a_0$. For Hydrogen (Z = 1), the expectation value of 1s radius $<r>$ is 1.5 times the maximum probability radius $r$ that is the Bohr radius. For 2s, $r=2a_0$ and for 3s $r=\frac{3(3+\sqrt{3})a_0}{2Z}, \frac{3(3-\sqrt{3})a_0}{2Z}$ (two nodes).

On the other hand, the H atom spectrum can be described by the Rydberg equation. This implies a series of discrete energy levels as:

$E_n = -hc_H\frac{1}{n^2}$

The eigen equation for the radial function $R_n$, can be expressed as follows and the eigenvalue of which is:

$$\frac{\h^2}{2me}\frac{\partial^2R}{\partial r^2} + \frac{2\hbar R}{r} + \frac{\h^2}{2\mu r^2}(l(l+1) - \frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0})R=ER$

where $\mu = \frac{m_{electron}m_{Nuclees}}{m_{electron} + m_{Nuclees}}$. Therefore, when $l=0$

$E = -\frac{Z^2\mu e^4}{32\epsilon_0^2\pi^2\hbar^2}\frac{1}{n^2}=-13.6\frac{Z^2}{n^2}$

which is the same as Eq. (20) when $Z=1$, indicating that both theories are in agreement at H atom.

When $n_2 \to \infty$, the electron is free from nucleus and this process is called ionization. The ionization energy for electrons in the valence shell $Ei = -E(26)$; when $n$ is a constant, the ionization energy increases as $Z$ increases. Because the amount of energy required for ionization (ionization energy) is the same for a given quantum number $n$, the atom orbital exhibited energy levels of $2n^2$ degeneracy (e.g., second shell $2s+2p$ has eight degenerate states).
