## The Philosophy of Intelligentism and Its Implications on Future Directions for Research and Practice

Francis Joseph Costello and Kung Chang Lee

In light of recent impressive developments in AI and the proliferation of large language models, this chapter proposes the novel philosophical concept of “Intelligentism” as a response to these developments. With a new lens through which to observe and navigate the ever-shifting landscape of AI, it aims to reevaluate our proposed novel notion named “intelligentism” in light of these revolutionary advancements. In this sense, we propose and define Intelligentism consisting of four philosophical components such as anthroposia, telolarity, virtuedynamics, and temporal fusion. Then, this study delves into the effects of AI’s widespread adoption, specifically as they pertain to IS/IT study. It provides the IS field with a novel philosophical perspective on the topic. A conceptual framework that accounts for the unique challenges and issues of deploying and supervising intelligent devices that communicate with humans is also addressed in the study. Intelligentism will serve as the jumping-off point for our philosophical exploration.

**Keywords** Intelligentism · Anthroposia · Telolarity · Virtuedynamics · Temporal fusion

## 1 Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) has advanced in ways that are nothing of astounding [1, 2], especially with the rise of large language model (LLM) AI. Technology and society have undergone a dramatic change as a result of these innovations, posing complex problems with unique solutions that call into question preexisting philosophical paradigms [3]. We suggest an innovative philosophy known as Intelligentism in order to understand and effectively respond to the complexity brought about by this technological revolution. With a new lens to view and negotiate the quickly changing environment of AI, this chapter seeks to rethink intellectualism in the light of these profound breakthroughs in intelligent machines.

Intlectualism has traditionally been a human-centered ideology that places a strong emphasis on human intelligence, reason, and information acquisition. Intelligentism's foundation was laid by influential intellectuals like Socrates and Plato, who emphasized the importance of human reason and knowledge in understanding and reshaping the world [4]. The importance of rational comprehension and the categorical imperative in making ethical decisions was highlighted by Kant, who strengthened this philosophy [5]. These individuals' ideologies have provided important insights into human cognition and decision-making, advancing our knowledge of the complex web of human.

Heidegger's view of technology as "enframing" [6] underlines both the possible problems that may arise with the development of intelligent robots while providing insightful perspectives on how technology affects society. We must be aware of the ethical and societal ramifications of these technologies as we continue to create and use AI-powered intelligent devices, making sure they are used to improve human welfare and defend our core values and rights [7]. This criticism challenges us to reconsider and broaden the traditionally human-intellectual boundaries between humans and machines. With this in mind, we suggest examining Intelligentism using the ANT (actor-network theory) guiding principles. The unique and insightful perspective offered by ANT recognizes the importance of non-human entities in influencing our socio-technical environment. ANT can assist us in comprehending how AI changes our understanding of the world by taking into account non-human entities as important participants in the socio-technical network [8].
