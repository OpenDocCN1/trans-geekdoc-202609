# 3.3 Creativity Assessment Through AI

Assessing creativity has long been a practice in various fields of study, often relying
on expert evaluation. Researchers administer tests of creative, akin to the Consensual Assessment
Technique (CAT). While subjective scoring can be reliable and valid, it is also time-
consuming and resource-intensive, slowing down research progress and posing a
barier for researchers and practitioners lacking the necessary human resources to
support subjective scoring methods like the CAT [44, 45].

Despite the effectiveness of subjective scoring, its resource-intensive nature can
hinder the pace of research, making it challenging for those without adequate human
resources to employ such methods [46]. Recently, the field of creativity has achieved
a notable milestone with the emergence of advancements in AI. AI holds the
potential to transform creativity assessment methods, offering cost-effectiveness,
automation capabilities, and enhanced reliability compared to human raters [21, 45].

# 3.3.1 Creativity Measures

Participants' responses in creativity tests are typically assessed across several
dimens, including fluency, originality, and flexibility [47, 48]. Some creativity
tests may also incorporate additional indices such as usefulness and elaboration
[47, 48]. Fluency refers to the total number of ideas generated for each object.
Fluency-valid, on the other hand, considers only unique and non-repeated ideas,
providing a valid count of ideas. Flexibility measures the diversity of idea categories
produced for each object. Originality evaluates the rarity or novelty of the ideas
