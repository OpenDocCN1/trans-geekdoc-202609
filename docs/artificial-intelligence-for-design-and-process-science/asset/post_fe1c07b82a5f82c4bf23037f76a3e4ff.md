## 6.1 Product Customization

Customization begins with the customer, in collaboration with a product designer searching and querying the blueprint repository to find a base product or variant that is close enough to the customer’s requirements. For this purpose, the stakeholders in product design invoke the retrieve operator presented in Sect. 5.2. For example, a customer may be looking for a laser processing machine that incorporates high-accuracy rotary table motion combined with a new generation beam director capability to support new manufacturing processes and improve existing processes. The beam director needs to provide a rotary axis motion of 800°, tilt axis motion of 400°, and an axis speed of 800 rpm. This is expressed using the retrieve operator of the blueprint meta-programming language and textually as:

```
S = retrieve(LaserProcessingMachine,
hasHighAccuracyRotaryTable,hasBeamDirector(800,400,80))
```

We assume that retrieve returns the three products S = {p1, p2, p3}. Assume further after inspecting the set of returned products and comparing them using the compare operator, the stakeholders decide to choose p1 as the base product for customization.

Scenario 1: Parameterized customization when the customer selects a product, say p1, a new variant, say p11, is created that inherits all the characteristics of its root p1, as components, parts, assemblies, and them. In its simplest form, consider a scenario where the customer might require that the work area of p11 should be X 650 mm, Y 650 mm, and Z 650 mm. Now, assume that p1 has its working area as X 750 mm, Y 550 mm, and Z 650 mm. This results in a branching mechanism that creates new products/variants from base products or existing variants by means of parameterization, after performing consistency checks (enabled by the ontology reasoner) to ensure that values of product p11 are valid. This is expressed as:

```
p11 ≡ p1
p11(workingArea(650,650,650))
insertIndividual(p11)
```
