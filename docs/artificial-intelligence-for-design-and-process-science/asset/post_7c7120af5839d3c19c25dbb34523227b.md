## 3.2 AI-Human co-creativity

Computational co-creative systems represent a burgeoning field where human users collaborate with AI agents to produce creative works [36]. These systems, particularly in design, allow users to move beyond their initial ideas and overcome design fixing by working with an AI agent on open-ended creative tasks throughout the design process. While some research in computational creativity focuses on generative, co-creative systems emphasize how systems implementing creativity can collaborate with humans on tasks. For instance, sketching is a common technique used in creativity tasks where designers generate and refine ideas. In the realm of design creativity, AI has proven valuable in assisting designers with ideation and overcoming issues.

Co-creative systems hold significant promise for enhancing human creativity across various domains by fostering and encouraging creatives in a human-AI co-creative sketching system, reporting the outcomes of a user study that analyzed user responses to the Creative Sketching Partner’s (CSP) sketches.

The authors developed an AI model to facilitate the mapping process between visual and conceptual spaces, aiding users in making connections between these spaces to generate fresh ideas for their design tasks. By utilizing deep learning models trained on a large dataset of sketches, users could adjust the level of creativity and conceptual similarity in the AI-generated responses when seeking inspiration from the CSP.

The tool translates the user's sketch into another category that shares structural and semantic characteristics, with users able to control the degree of similarity in both visual and conceptual aspects to inspire their current designs.

Fifty design students participated in the study, and their interactions and self-reports were analyzed. Participant behavior clustered under three types of design creativity: combinatorial, exploratory, and transformational. The results indicate that the tool effectively facilitates ideation and helps overcome fixation. Moreover, the analysis suggests that inspiration linked to conceptual similarity is more associated with transformational creativity, while inspiration based on visual similarity is more prevalent during the detailed design stages and is closely tied to combinatorial creativity.

## 3.3 Creativity Assessment Through AI

Assessing creativity has long been a practice in various fields of study, often relying on expert evaluation. Researchers administer tests of creative, akin to the Consensual Assessment Technique (CAT). While subjective scoring can be reliable and valid, it is also time-consuming and resource-intensive, slowing down research progress and posing a barrier for researchers and practitioners lacking the necessary human resources to support subjective scoring methods like the CAT [44, 45].

Despite the effectiveness of subjective scoring, its resource-intensive nature can hinder the pace of research, making it challenging for those without adequate human resources to employ such methods [46]. Recently, the field of creativity has achieved a notable milestone with the emergence of advancements in AI. AI holds the potential to transform creativity assessment methods, offering cost-effectiveness, automation capabilities, and enhanced reliability compared to human raters [21, 45].

### 3.3.1 Creativity Measures

Participants' responses in creativity tests are typically assessed across several dimensions, including fluency, originality, and flexibility [47, 48]. Some creativity tests may also incorporate additional indices such as usefulness and elaboration [47, 48]. Fluency refers to the total number of ideas generated for each object. Fluency-valid, on the other hand, considers only unique and non-repeated ideas, providing a valid count of ideas. Flexibility measures the diversity of ideas produced. Originality evaluates the rarity or novelty of the ideas relative to those generated by all. Elaboration assesses the level of detail in the responses, while usefulness gauges the functional utility of the idea.

### 3.3.2 Creativity Assessment Studies

The evolution of computational approaches to creativity assessment, by Guilford’s (1950) prediction to today's AI-based methods, can be delineated into four distinct phases.

In the first phase, the focus was on text-mining statistics derived from responses, including word length, count, and punctuation usage. The second phase was the application of latent semantic analysis to originality through semantic distance. The third phase witnessed a transition from latent semantic analysis to Global Vectors for Word Representation. This shift capitalized on superior corpus and neural network capabilities, offering a better approximation of human thought processes. Research indicated that this approach surpassed latent semantic analyses in predicting human ratings. In the fourth phase, there was a shift from unsupervised to supervised learning approaches. This leveraged large language modeling techniques to significantly enhance scoring accuracy [44].

In a study [46], a creativity assessment system called AuDRA—an Automated Drawing Assessment format—was employed to extract visual creativity ratings, trained AuDRA and tested its generalizability to untrained drawing sets, raters, and tasks. Using a collection of line drawings and drawings from simple drawing productions. This work employed four distinct datasets of rated drawings—totaling over 13,000 drawing-rating pairs. While training the model to perform the abstract drawing task to optimize its predictive capacity for specific task. They opted to use ResNet [50] as the computer vision backbone for AuDRA.

The model successfully learned to predict the creativity scores of human drawings. It was not trained on remarkably high accuracy. The model showed evidence of learning visual features of drawings, simply predicting their level of elaboration. Critically, AuDRA generalized to predict the creativity of sketches from entirely unpublished datasets of human ratings that were not used in the model. They found AuDRA scores to be highly correlated with human creativity ratings on the same drawing task.
