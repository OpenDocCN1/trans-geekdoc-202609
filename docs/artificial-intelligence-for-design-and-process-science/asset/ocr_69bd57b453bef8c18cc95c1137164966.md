# 5.2 Training Actor and Critic Models

Actor and critic networks were trained in multiple stages as indicated before to evaluate whether reinforcement learning [33] can produce a policy that will optimize the network architecture for the UCI datasets.

The networks were trained on nine UCI target problems with alternate individual pre-training of critic and actor the goal of evaluating the sufficiency of the used network structures and of stabilizing the initial value function estimate and consistent policy in order to avoid excessive trajectory generation.

Once critic and actor have been pre-trained, they are trained together using the TD3 actor-critic approach [34]. Here trajectory data is constructed using the perturbed critic policy and used to augment a temporally decaying buffer. This buffer stores states, actions, rewards, and random sampling on the buffer is used to obtain sufficient training examples to allow the networks to converge. Rewards are predicted by passing next state to the accuracy model and computing the need for further target problem solving. The two critic networks are trained using a loss obtained from the Bellman Eq. (1) that provides separate losses for V(s) and As(a), and the critic network is updated by updating its weights in the better of the two critics (i.e., the one with the larger value). The actor is trained using policy gradient by propagating the gradient of the negative advantage as a loss back through the critic network.

The actor-critic network was trained for 5000 episodes with a new step being generated and 300 samples trained per episode. Actions generated by the actor are displacement vectors in the two-dimensional embedding space represented as the sine and cosine of the direction and a length between 0 and 1 and are multiplied by a scaling factor of δ=0.005 to result in steps in the embedding space:

```
next_state = state + δ * length * (cos(dir) / sin(dir))
```
