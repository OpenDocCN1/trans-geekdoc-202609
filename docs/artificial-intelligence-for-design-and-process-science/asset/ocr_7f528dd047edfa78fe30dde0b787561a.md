In the study [43], machine learning, in the form of a convolutional neural network (CNN), was applied to automate the scoring of the Test of Creative Thinking-Drawing Production (TCT-DP). The TCT-DP is a figure of creativity designed to assess creativity through a more gestalt-oriented perspective compared to traditional, quantitatively oriented divergent tasks. The TCT-DP utilizes incomplete and irregular figured fragments (such as a semicircle, a right angle, or a dot) within a large square frame to elicit responses. The study utilized a training dataset comprising 414 samples, with all images scored by at least two trainers.

Various models were employed to explore the capabilities of the proposed method, utilizing different numbers of classes ranging from two (representing low and high creativity) to seven (corresponding to the classes defined in the TCT-DP test manual). The study considered factors such as model accuracy (percentage of correctly classified images), sensitivity to the size of the training dataset, sensitivity to model parameters (e.g., number of epochs, learning rate), and utility (ability to provide useful information). Overall, the models achieved high accuracies (above 85% in all cases) in assessing creativity.

# 3.4 Design Creativity and AI

In the previous sections, we covered the AI applications related to creativity. Here, we would like to highlight the differences between creativity and design creativity and report on some of the studies employing AI in design creativity research.

To the best of our knowledge, there exist some studies such as [36, 51–53] targeting the AI applications in design creativity and how AI can help design/design creativity. In contrast, the authors studied the effects of design thinking on the cognition of AI learning, attitudes toward AI, idea creativity, and the product creativity of AI applications [54]. This study found that design thinking significantly enhances conceptual cognition of AI learning, particularly in relational contexts and classes of AI concepts, though it also had no significant effect on cross-linking and examples. Design thinking also effectively fosters positive views over AI input and processing due to its real-life applicability. Additionally, design thinking improves the creativity of AI-generated works, notably in their novelty, value, functionality, and elaboration, though not in material, style, and structure.

There also exist comprehensive reviews [23, 55, 56] investigating the effects of AI, specifically generative adversarial networks as toolbox for design practitioners [55], (in the last 15 years) are reported which indicates the wide usage of AI in design [57]. However, according to our investigation, in the field of design creativity, fewer AI-related studies can be found.
