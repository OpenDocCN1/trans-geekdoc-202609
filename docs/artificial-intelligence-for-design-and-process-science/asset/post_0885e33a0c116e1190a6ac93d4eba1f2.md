By the end of the course, students are able to discuss the limits of AI, including various ethical dilemmas that AI technology may present in the future as further developments are made. ## Appreciation for Project-Based Learning

Initial students find that the modules used in the courses at the three institutions are the type of learning experiences students desire, as expressed in the interviews. For example, when asked what advice they would give instructors on how to make course content more relevant to their lived experiences, at the beginning of the course, the majority of focus group students begin the semester wanting as many opportunities as possible to apply the course in contextualized situations.

During focus groups at the end of the semester, students across all courses refer to the “AI for Social Good” project in the present course as an example of what other STEM-related courses should use as a summative assessment.

> “Project-based learning (PBL) is a type of inquiry-based learning students with opportunities to construct knowledge through real-world questions, problems, and practices [35] to create meaningful learning experiences [36] that include peer social interactions and the sharing of knowledge and understanding with others. With a PBL assessment, the students discuss aspects of the “AI for Social Good” project they enjoyed, including collaborating with their peers, applying their personal knowledge and expertise through an authentic context. When talking about the advantages of collaboration, one student explains that a huge advantage of working in a group is you are able to get perspectives from many places, demonstrating their understanding of how effective group-based learning is compared to traditional, passive learning found in lecture-based courses. Another student even calls STEM courses tend to focus more on technical understanding, which is an outlier, instead of having human factor to it, which they feel their course did take into consideration in relation to designing the final product with other communities in mind.”

## AI for Social Good Education Through Culturally Responsive Pedagogy

### Developing Community Inside and Outside the Classroom

A final theme is how students discuss the importance of “community” both inside and outside the classroom. At the beginning of the course, when discussing the scenario of a student of color who is struggling to feel included in STEM courses, the topic of “community” is constantly brought up. Some students speak about experiences on campus; others mention their family and friends from home who support them emotionally and help them persist through difficulties.

During focus groups at the end of the semester, students still hold the same views on the importance of community when unpacking the scenario with classmates. Several students expressed positive experiences during the “AI for Social Good” project; students characterize their learning environments as “welcoming,” with one student feeling “very lucky and fortunate to have such a diverse group of people to work over the semester.” Another student talked about how this project created a strong bond within the class, revealing the community that was created through the efforts of instructors, the learning that linked course content to community, and collaborating with peers.

Worth noting is that students do not mention the role of local communities during focus groups at the start of the course; but by the end of the course (Spring 2023 focus groups), students include the role of local communities in their learning and future careers. For example, when asked what advice they would give instructors to make courses more relevant to their lived experiences, students suggest instructors integrating more opportunities to integrate the local community, such as interviewing community members and working with local organizations to understand their needs and to improve social good more directly.

Additionally, when asked to rank the importance of the course content to their future careers, daily lives, or improving the lives of communities, by the end of the semester, students prioritize themselves and improving the lives of communities. One student uses the class project as an example of improving the social good for others or themselves, stating: “a great example of this was just in the class. A lot of projects were based on learning about communities, whether it is gaining access to knowledge or bridging a gap between education… I think it could be utilized in helping communities of color.” By leveraging their experiences within their own communities alongside content knowledge and skills, initial findings indicate that through the project, students begin building connections with self, others, and the greater good—something lacking in their previous STEM experiences. Any evidence of students developing an interest in local or marginalized communities by the end of the semester will be further examined in future semesters.

## 6 Dissemination

To maximize the impact of the AI4SG project, we devised a strategic dissemination plan aimed at broadening the project's scope beyond our immediate academic and geographic confines. Central to this strategy was a project website, designed to showcase the "AI for Social Good" projects undertaken by students across CSU campuses, available at https://www.aifsocialgood.org/. This site not only highlights the innovative solutions that students have developed to address societal challenges through AI but also serves to inspire educators, stakeholders, and investors about the potential of AI for social improvement.

Further amplifying our outreach, we organized a symposium in collaboration with the CSU Chancellor's Office and STEM-NET, designed to foster disciplinary and cross-institutional dialogues about AI's role in societal transformation. The First Annual AI for Social Good Innovation Symposium brought students, faculty, industry partners, and community stakeholders, facilitating a rich exchange of ideas and highlighting career opportunities in the field of AI. It attracted about 150 attendances in May 2023. Through these initiatives, our dissemination effort not only showcased the practical outcomes of the AI4SG project but also established a foundation for ongoing collaboration and innovation in the application of AI for Social Good.

## 7 Conclusion

In this study, we introduced the AI for Social Good (AI4SG) project, which aims to offer an innovative educational pedagogy to impart AI knowledge to undergraduate students from diverse academic disciplines, with a focus on societal betterment. Students were instructed to generate a multifaceted understanding of AI applications through case studies, practical hands-on labs, and the prototyping of AI-driven solutions for local societal challenges. The AI4SG curriculum was implemented among three universities between Fall 2022 and Spring 2023 semesters, encompassing 514 students from three distinct disciplines.
