# 3 Compounded Error Content Graphs in Communication Dynamics

Our mathematical models are developed based on the concept that communication theory can represent various scales of biological units including electrons, atoms, molecules, cells, and organisms. Compounded error communication channels (error contained, compounded channels) represent the interactions and assemblies between signal generation and moments in space-time where signal is modeled to be received. Figure 1 shows a sample communication and a related error (or information) content graph.

The symbols on the left side correspond to a signal, and the symbols on the right side show the mapping of the sent and received signals. On the left in Fig. 1, we show Shannon's representation of an error content graph. In this representation, the information content of the lower and uppercase symbols is the same. If lower-case letter corresponds to the same letter (uppercase) on the other side, no error is encountered in the communication. In the formulation of Shannon, (left) $a ightarrow b, b ightarrow c, c ightarrow d, d ightarrow E$, and $e ightarrow A$ represent errors that occur.

In the polygon formulation (Fig. 1, right), each of these symbols may considered to be a point on a circle and the error pairs as lines joining these points. Inherently, in the polygon, we understand $a ightarrow a, b ightarrow b$, etc. can represent an error-free transmission over space-time, whereas $a ightarrow b ightarrow c ightarrow d ightarrow e$ a efficiently represents the dynamic error content of the graph [2], or equivalently, we can call this information entropy [1]. This polygon can be considered an error content graph that represents the transmission error in the communication channel.
