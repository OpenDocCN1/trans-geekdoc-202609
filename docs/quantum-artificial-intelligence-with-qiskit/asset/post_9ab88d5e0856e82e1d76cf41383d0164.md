## 15.2 MONTE CARLO LEARNMATRIX

The suggested probabilistic retrieval rule for the determination of the answer vector $\mathbf{y}$ for the query vector $\mathbf{x}$ is

$p(y_i=1|\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{net_i}{\sum_{v=1}^n net_v}\right)$

and

$p(y_i=0|\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{n}\left(1 - \frac{net_i}{\sum_{v=1}^n net_v}\right)$

describing the probability of firing or not firing of one unit with

$\sum_{i=1}^n p(y_i=1|\mathbf{x}) + p(y_i=0|\mathbf{x})$

During the query operation unit is randomly sampled and either it fires or not according to the probability distribution. To determine the answer vector, we must sample the Monte Carlo Lernmatrix several times. For the reconstructed vector, three states will be present: 1 for fired units, 0 for not fired units, and *unknown* for silent units. The Monte Carlo Lernmatrix is a close description of the quantum Lernmatrix. In quantummatrix units are represented by quantum states, sampling corresponds to the measurement.

## 15.3 QUANTUM COUNTING ONES

In a binary string of the length $N$ we can represent the fraction of $k$ ones by the simple formula $k/N$ and the zeros as $(N - k)/N$ resulting in a linear relation. We can interpret these as probability values. We can map these linear relations into the sigmoid-like probability functions for the presence of ones using Euler's formula [111] in relation to trigonometry

$$\left(\sin\frac{\pi \cdot k}{2 \cdot N}\right)^2 = \frac{e^{\frac{\pi \cdot k}{N}} - e^{-\frac{\pi \cdot k}{N}}}{2} \in [0, 1] \quad (15.9)$$

and of zeros with

$$\left(\cos\frac{\pi \cdot k}{2 \cdot N}\right)^2 = \frac{e^{\frac{\pi \cdot k}{N}} + e^{-\frac{\pi \cdot k}{N}}}{2} \in [0, 1] \quad (15.10)$$

together with

$$\left(\sin\frac{\pi \cdot k}{2 \cdot N}\right)^2 + \left(\cos\frac{\pi \cdot k}{2 \cdot N}\right)^2 = 1$$

In Figure 15.5 the sigmoid-like probability functions for $N=8$ are indicated. To count the number of ones we introduced the control qubit in $1/\sqrt{2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$. For the superposition part represented by the control qubit $|0\rangle$, the phase $e^{i\pi/3}$ is applied for each one. For the superposition part represented by the control qubit $|1\rangle$, the phase $e^{-i\pi/3}$ is applied for each one.

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e^{i\pi/3} \cdot |0\rangle \otimes e^{-i\pi/3} \cdot |1\rangle)$$

$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e^{i\pi/3} \cdot |0\rangle \otimes e^{-i\pi/3} \cdot |1\rangle) = (15.11)$

If we apply a Hadamard gate to the control qubit we get

$(H \otimes I \otimes I \otimes I) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e^{i\pi/3} \cdot |0\rangle + e^{-i\pi/3} \cdot |1\rangle) = \frac{e^{i\pi/3} + e^{-i\pi/3}}{2}|001\rangle + i \frac{e^{i\pi/3} - e^{-i\pi/3}}{2}|101\rangle = \cos\left(\frac{\pi \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 3}\right)|001\rangle + i \sin\left(\frac{\pi \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 3}\right)|101\rangle (15.12)$

The probability of measuring the control qubit $|0\rangle$ is

and the probability of measuring the control qubit $|1\rangle$ is
