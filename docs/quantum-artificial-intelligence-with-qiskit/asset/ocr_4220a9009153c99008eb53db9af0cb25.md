# 8 Puzzle - Chapter 10

The n-puzzle is a classical problem for modeling algorithms. For n-puzzle there are n+1 objects: n cells and empty cell. Each object can be coded by $\rho = [log n+1]$ qubits and a configuration of $n+1$ objects can be represented by a register of $z := \rho \cdot (n+1)$. The function $g(x)$ is represented unitary operator $T$ acts on the $n+1$ cells $x \in B^2$ and $c \in B^1$.

# Blocks World - Chapter 11

The blocks world is a popular domain in AI another [73]. The blocks can be placed on the table and picked up and set down on another table and the goal is to build one or more vertical stacks of blocks. Only one block may be moved at a time and any blocks that are under block cannot be moved. There are three types of blocks. They differ by attributes such as color (red, green, and blue) or marks, not by them. In AI, they are traditionally called A, B, C [61]. The simplicity of this problem allows to compare classical approach with quantum computing. We indicate how the binding of the stats is achieved through the entanglement. We indicate how to represent the states with a class descriptor and position descriptor (adjective) and perform a search of depth one. The A, B, C blocks planning problem results in a non-constant branching factor. We show that the branching factor is reduced by Grover's amplification to the square root of the average branching factor and not to the maximum branching factor.
