# Quantum Gates – Chapter 4

Quantum gates are the building blocks of quantum circuits, like classical logic gates are for conventional digital circuits. Unlike classical logic gates, quantum logic gates are reversible and can be described by unitary matrices. Then, we introduce Boolean quantum gates that allow us to map any Boolean circuit in such a way that the circuit becomes reversible. Such a circuit represents a permutation in Hilbert space and can be represented by a permutation matrix composed of the unitary matrices representing the quantum gates. We describe Clifford gates as elements of the Clifford group and can efficiently simulated with a classical computer. Parameterized gate is a parameterized gate with the parameter being the amount of rotation to be performed over the three axes. We introduce the controlled-U gates and introduce the unitary decomposition and formulate the process of translation. Translation is a complex problem of finding an optimal decomposition into the present quantum gates for a quantum computer.

# Grover’s Amplification – Chapter 5

We describe the Grover’s amplification algorithm. We represent n state by superposition; each state has the same real positive amplitude. A parallel computation is applied to all n states, and the state is solved by a minus sign, and the amplitude is negative. We then apply a linear operation based on the Householder reflection, and by the reflection, the value of the amplitude grows linearly. For dimensions higher than four, the operations of marking and the Householder reflection must be repeated √n times, since at each step the amplitude grows linearly. After √n steps we measure the solution. The algorithm guarantees us a quadratic speed over a classical computer that would require n steps. The Grover’s amplification algorithm is optimal and one can prove that a better algorithm cannot exist. We will demonstrate the primitives of Grover’s amplification using the matrix notation NumPy.
