## 2.2.2 Representation

A quantum mechanical description of a physical system is related to a probabilistic representation; it is described by a vector in Hilbert space. This description extends the two- or three-dimensional Euclidean space into spaces that have finite or infinite number of dimensions. In such a space, the Euclidean norm is induced by the inner product

```
$|x| = \sqrt{\langle x|x\rangle} (2.1)
```

Without a scalar product there is no orthogonality. In a Hilbert space, two vectors are orthogonal if and only if the scalar product is zero. A basis of $n$ dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_n$ is chosen. A 2-state system is described by a two-dimensional space $\mathcal{H}_2$. For the basis

```
$e_1 = \left(\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}\right), e_2 = \left(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}\right) (2.2)
```

the system is described by a vector $x$ with complex numbers $\omega_1, \omega_2$ that represent the amplitude of each dimension

```
$x = \omega_1 \cdot e_1 + \omega_2 \cdot e_2 = \left(\begin{pmatrix} \omega_1 \\ \omega_2\end{pmatrix}\right) (2.3)
```

The probabilities are real numbers between 0 and 1. The probability that the system is in $e_1$ and $e_2$ is $|\omega_1|^2$ and $|\omega_2|^2$. This is because the product of complex number with conjugate is always a real number

```
$$\omega^* \cdot \omega = (x-y \cdot i) \cdot (x+y \cdot i) = x^2 + |\omega|^2 (2.4)
```

The vector representing a state is normalized. Its length is one. The amplitudes correspond to the probability with

```
$|\omega_1|^2 + |\omega_2|^2 = 1.
```

Paul Dirac introduced the following notation for a vector $x$ describing a state

```
$|x\rangle = |\omega_1 \cdot e_1\rangle + |\omega_2 \cdot e_2\rangle = |\omega_1 |x\rangle + |\omega_2 |x\rangle = \left(\begin{pmatrix} \omega_1 \\ \omega_2\end{pmatrix}\right) (2.5)
```

with

```
$|e_1\rangle = |x\rangle
```

It is a shorthand notation for a column vector. Related to the scalar product $\langle x|x\rangle$ row vector are $\langle x|$ "bra" and column vectors are $|x\rangle$ "ket" from bra-ket. A state vector is just a particular instance of a ket vector. It is specified by a particular choice of basis and refers to observatle that can have some system properties.

## 2.2.3 Linear Operators

The computation on the coherent qubits (before the measurement) is described by linear operators that change the distribution of the quantum probabilities represented by amplitudes in a linear way [119]. The operators are represented by orthogonal matrices for real amplitudes (inverse of the matrix is the transpose of the matrix), for complex amplitudes by unitary matrices (inverse matrix is the conjugate transpose of the matrix)

Operators represented by a square matrix give mathematical description how something changes in the quantum world. For a 2-state quantum system, an operator that acts on the memory register would be a $2 \times 2$ dimensional unitary matrix. In unitary matrices, its conjugate transpose is equal to its inverse.

```
$U^* = U^{-1}$
```

For a example coin is a system with two basis states 0 and 1 with

```
$|0\rangle = \left( \begin{matrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{matrix} \right), |1\rangle = \left( \begin{matrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{matrix} \right)$
```

The mapping is represented as

```
$|0\rangle \rightarrow \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle$
```

and

```
$|1\rangle \rightarrow \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle$
```

The corresponding operator is indicated by the unitary matrix,

```
$H = \left( \begin{matrix} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \end{matrix} \right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( \begin{matrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{matrix} \right)$
```

called a Hadamard or Walsh matrix. If the system starts in state $|0\rangle$ and undergoes the evolution, the probability of observing 0 or 1 is $|1/\sqrt{2}|^2 = 1/2$. If we do not perform a measurement and repeat the mapping, the probability of observing 0 becomes 1 and observing 1 becomes zero. This is due to the fact, that the amplitudes $|1|$ cancel each other. This effect is called destructive interference and cannot occur in the probability distribution since all its coefficients are non-negative numbers.

## 2.3Compound SYSTEMS

A 2-state quantum system (qubit) is described by a two-dimensional Hilbert space

```
$|0\rangle = \left( \begin{matrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{matrix} \right), |1\rangle = \left( \begin{matrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{matrix} \right)$
```

is described by a vector $|x\rangle$ with complex numbers $\omega_1, \omega_2$ that represent the amplitude of each dimension

```
$|x\rangle = \omega_1 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_2 \cdot |1\rangle = \left(\begin{pmatrix} \omega_1 \\ \omega_2 \end{pmatrix}\right).
```

The vector has length

```
$|\omega_0|^2 + |\omega_1|^2 = 1 \rightarrow ||x||^2 = 1.
```

The unitary matrix $H$ (Hadamard matrix) performs the mapping in the ket notation

```
$H \cdot |0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle$
```

with the vector notation

```
$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( \begin{matrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{matrix} \right) \cdot \left( \begin{matrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{matrix} \right) = \left( \begin{matrix} 1/\sqrt{2} \\ 1/\sqrt{2} \end{matrix} \right).
```

Applying $H$ again results in

```
$H \cdot \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle \right) = H \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle + H \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle = |0\rangle$
```

with the vector notation

```
$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( \begin{matrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{matrix} \right) \cdot \left( \begin{matrix} 1/\sqrt{2} \\ 1/\sqrt{2} \end{matrix} \right) = \left( \begin{matrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{matrix} \right).
```

How can we represent a register composed of two qubits? Such a register would represent $2^2$ possible states and would be represented in a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_4$. The first qubit is represented by a two-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_2$,

```
$|x\rangle = \omega_0 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |1\rangle = \left(\begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \\ \omega_1 \end{pmatrix}\right).
```

and the second as

```
$|y\rangle = \omega_0 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |1\rangle = \left(\begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \\ \omega_1 \end{pmatrix}\right):
```

The register of two qubits is represented as a direct product of $|x\rangle$ and $|y\rangle$.

```
$|x\rangle \otimes |y\rangle = |x\rangle|y\rangle = |xy\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \\ \omega_1 \end{pmatrix} \otimes \begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \cdot \omega_0 & \omega_0 \cdot \omega_1 \\ \omega_1 \cdot \omega_0 & \omega_1 \cdot \omega_1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \cdot \omega_0 & \omega_0 \cdot \omega_1 \\ \omega_1 \cdot \omega_0 & \omega_1 \cdot \omega_1 \end{pmatrix} \quad (2.14)
```

or

```
$|xy\rangle = |\omega_0 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |1\rangle| \otimes |\omega_0 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |1\rangle$
```

```
$|xy\rangle = \omega_0 \cdot |00\rangle + \omega_0 \cdot |01\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |10\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |11\rangle \quad (2.15)
```

with the new basis

```
$|00\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, |01\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, |10\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, |11\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad (2.16)
```

A register of three qubits represents $2^3$ states represented in a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_s$.

```
$|xyz\rangle = |x\rangle \otimes |y\rangle \otimes |z\rangle = \omega_0 \cdot |000\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |001\rangle + \omega_2 \cdot |010\rangle + \omega_3 \cdot |011\rangle + \omega_4 \cdot |100\rangle + \omega_5 \cdot |101\rangle + \omega_6 \cdot |110\rangle + \omega_7 \cdot |111\rangle \quad (2.17)
```

A quantum register of length $m$ represents $m$ qubits in a Hilbert space of dimension $n=2^m$.

The compound system of the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_n$ and a $w$-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_w$ defined by an orthonormal basis $|y_1\rangle, |y_2\rangle, \dots, |y_w\rangle$ is defined by the tensor product

```
$\mathcal{H}_{n-w} = \mathcal{H}_n \otimes \mathcal{H}_w \quad (2.18)$
```

According to this definition we can define operator on two qubits

```
$H \cdot (\omega_0 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |1\rangle) \otimes H \cdot (\omega_0 \cdot |0\rangle + \omega_1 \cdot |1\rangle) = \\
```

```
$$\left(H \cdot \begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \\ \omega_1 \end{pmatrix}\right) \otimes \left(H \cdot \begin{pmatrix} \omega_0 \\ \omega_1 \end{pmatrix}\right) \quad (2.19)$$
```
