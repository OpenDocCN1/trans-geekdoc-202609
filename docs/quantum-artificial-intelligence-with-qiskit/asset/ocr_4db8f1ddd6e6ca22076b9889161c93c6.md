# 2.1.1 Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics

During observation (measurement by the observer, us), the wave collapses into one definite state with a certain probability. A quantum system that is perfectly maintains its wave representation, its coherence. If it is not isolated, during interaction with the environment the wave representation of an event is lost, this is called the quantum decoherence. Quantum decoherence happens during the environment. Quantum decoherence is universally viewed as the loss of information from a system into the environment. Quantum decoherence provides us only with a framework for apparent wave-function collapse and it does not explain the collapse. Since quantum systems are not isolated in the large scale (macro scale), the effects are mostly present at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles (micro scale). During the measurement true randomness is present, but after the measurement the physical system is in a definite state. Measurement does something to the wave function.
