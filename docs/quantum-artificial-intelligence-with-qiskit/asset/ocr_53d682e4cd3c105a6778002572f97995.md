# 1.14 Deep Learning

GPUs (Graphics Processing Units) were originally designed to accelerate the rendering of 3D graphics. Over time, they became more flexible and programmable, enhancing their capabilities. The first GPU in history was known as the GeForce 256.

With more power by GPUs, it is possible to train huge data by deep networks (Deep Learning) that use many layers to increase the model's statistical power. The methods achieved results in handling vision, speech recognition, synthesis, image generation, and machine translation. It is now common to identify AI with deep learning and not with symbolic systems, assuming that the symbolic approach is no longer relevant. However, the downside of the deep learning (DL) approach is requirement of computing power, large data sets, and the lack of comprehensibility and explanation contrary to the symbolic approach.

# 1.15 Quantum Artificial Intelligence

With the appearance of first small quantum computers (QAI) emerged by IBM, Google, Microsoft and developing quantum artificial intelligence the quantum computers execute some quantum circuits with certain error, their capacity varies from 5 to qubit, but the race is set up. One of the most popular software development kits (SDK) for working with quantum computers is Qiskit [21], IBM Quantum, https://quantum-computing.ibm.com/. It provides tools for creating and manipulating quantum programs running on prototype devices or simulators on a local computer, like your simple laptop. It follows the quantum circuit model which can be used for any quantum hardware that follows this model. This allows us to investigate the possibilities for the realization of AI by means of quantum computation through computer experiments [118]. If we relate qubits to bits, it becomes clear that the symbolic approach is an ideal candidate for quantum computation since the algorithms require much lower capacity compared to the ML algorithms.
