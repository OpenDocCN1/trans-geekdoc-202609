
The previous researchers have introduced different data security schemas to enhance the security of EMI. However, the previous studies have not effectively enhanced data security. Most of them fail to mention encryption and decryption time ([Adnan and Ariffin, 2019](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_2); [Singh et al., 2020](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_22); [Ali et al., 2022](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_6); [Prabha et al., 2022](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_25); [Parmar and Shah, 2023](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_24)) which helps prove the efficiency of the proposed algorithm. However, computational time needs to be addressed while storing EMI as they are used for real-time applications. The computational time is the time involved in accessing our EMI which involves de-steganography and decryption process for the proposed hybrid method. The previously used research works implemented conventional techniques such as Advanced Encryption Standard which is susceptible to brute force attacks ([Adnan and Ariffin, 2019](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_2)), RSA, least significant bit (LSB) steganography (Adee and Mouratidis, 2022), Two-Fish algorithm ([Maata, Cordova, and Halibas, 2020](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_21)). Thus, leading to proposed hybrid method (PHM) which combines steganography with cryptography on X-ray images. The X-ray images are hidden one at a time into a normalized cover image Lena. Then three-layers of cryptography are applied to stego-image which will further enhance the security of EMI. This hybrid method is a light-weight combination and is found suitable for real-time applications where EMI can be stored, or before transmitting them over a network, and accessed in real-time ([Sharma et al., 2021](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_28)) through real time applications.

### Research motivation

The current study focuses on reducing the size of the EMI which renders them useless for future referencing by medical practitioners, researcher, and insurance agencies, etc. Recently, there has been an increase in the number of EMI users. The security and privacy of EMI have become increasingly challenging as no EMI is the same, having varying properties, thus, they vary in size and have unique features. Due to varying sizes (such as dimensions, storage space, etc.) and unique features (therefore each image belongs to a unique person at a unique point in time) for the EMI images. Previous studies have focused on normalizing the EMI to dimensions 256 × 256 ([El-Shafai et al., 2022](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_9)) and 512 × 512 ([Akkasaligar and Biradar, 2020](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_5); [Brar et al., 2022](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_11)) which renders them useless for future medical referencing. As medical data needs to be detailed, clear, and accurate for correct diagnosis. Thus, such methods have harmed the key feature of the EMI. Thus, EMI of varying properties such as number of pixels, size in bytes, dimensions, and belonging to different patient’s security is an important aspect as it needs to be enhanced further while maintaining the original EMI properties ([Shukla et al., 2021](#ch37.xhtml#ref37_30)). The easy to use, hassle free, anytime access to EMI is provided, faster access, and device scalable access to patients, medical practitioners, etc. EMI are used in case of medical emergencies thus they need to be reliable, accurate, and accessible in real time. Therefore, EMI security and privacy need to be enhanced without affecting its features.
