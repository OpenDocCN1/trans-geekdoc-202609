The real reason for such a situation was the Gramm-Rudman Act. The act permitted banks to engage in trading lucrative products which were sold to stockholders. Such mortgage-backed securities required home loans as surety. The products formed a voracious demand aimed at increasing loans. Hedge funds and a few more financial institutions around the globe possessed the mortgage-backed securities. The securities included cutting-edge mutual funds, corporate assets, and pension funds. The banks bundled up the mortgages and further sold their property in tranches. That led to a reduction in the value of property.

One more symptom of the financial emergency was in 2007 when banks freaked and realized they had to absorb the losses. Banks were not giving financial support to the buyers, respectively.
