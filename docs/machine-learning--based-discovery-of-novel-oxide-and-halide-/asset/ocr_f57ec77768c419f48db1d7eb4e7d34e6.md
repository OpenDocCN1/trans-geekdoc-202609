alkaline-earth, alkalines, and lanthanides, the B cations are the metals with 3d, 4d or 5d configuration and X is an oxygen and halogens (Cl, Br and I) etc, [25]. The perovskite crystal structures with the highest symmetry (aristotype) belong to \(Pm\bar{3}m\) cubic space group [26]. Several variations in the aristotype perovskites of \(ABX_3\) stoichiometry are formed when A and B cations are replaced by combinations of other cations, or while forming double perovskites or quadruple perovskite [27]. Further, the electronic and ionic conductivities and ion diffusion of halide perovskites along with their low cost production make them a suitable candidate for storage applications. The fluoride perovskites exhibit high redox potential and high power density [20,28]. Fluoride perovskites have the highest electronegative fluorine element along with impressive capacitive control. Hence the \(ABF_3\) perovskite is very popular in the research of new electrode materials for energy storage applications. The monometallic fluoride perovskite exhibits very good stability [20] in energy storage applications. Therefore it is important to explore new fluoride perovskites with improved crystallinity and higher electronic conductivity [29–31]. The existing oxide perovskites and halide perovskites are not good candidates for the next generation of electrode materials. This gap can be filled with novel perovskite structures with enhanced charge capacity and superconducting behavior. A few recently proposed novel perovskite structures can cover up to 90 % of periodic table elements because of their physiochemical properties [20].

Formability indicates the ease with which a material can be formed or characterized [24]. Formation of a material typically refers to the process by which a material is created or synthesized, either naturally or artificially. An \(ABX_3\) compound synthesized with a perovskite structure is considered “formable” while if it is synthesized with a non–perovskite structure, it is termed as “non-formable”. Formability of perovskite crystal structure can be rapidly estimated using the approach of descriptor based methodology that allows high-throughput screening of the materials. The tolerance factor t is found reasonably accurate to find the formability of perovskite cubic structure. The tolerance factor for a perovskite structure can be estimated using the ionic radii of each ion (coordination-dependent Shannon’s ionic radii) as given by

\(t = (r_A + r_X) / \sqrt{2}(r_B + r_X),\) \newline (1)

where \(r_A\), \(r_B\) and \(r_X\) are Shanon’s ionic radii of A cation, B cation and X anion, respectively. Based on recent empirical evidence, it is found that cubic perovskite compositions are formable if t is in the range of 0.9–1.0 [24]. Further, the octahedral factor (\(\mu\)) is another remarkable descriptor for classifying perovskite and non-perovskite using the basic motif (octahedron \(BX_6\)) of perovskite. It is defined as

\(\mu = r_B/r_X.\) \newline (2)

With the combination of \(t\) and \(\mu\), Li et al. [33] successfully classified perovskites and non-perovskites structures out of 197 known \(ABO_3\) compounds. Zhang et al. [34] also demarcated perovskites and non-perovskites structures from 397 known \(ABO_3\) compounds. Sun et al. [35] give the relative stability prediction among any two perovskites with an accuracy of ~ 90 % using a stability descriptor (\(\mu + t)^n\). Further Filip and Giustino [36] suggest a new combination with addition of octahedral mismatch factor \(\mu_B\) for \(A_2BB'X_6\)-type perovskites and introduce a 3D map using (\(t\), \(\mu\), \(\bar{\mu}_B\)). The octahedral mismatch factor is given by

\(\bar{\mu}_B = |r_B - r_{B'}| / 2r_X,\) \newline (3)

where \(r_B\), \(r_{B'}\) and \(r_X\) are the Shanon’s ionic radii of B-cation, \(B'\) cation and X anion, respectively. The octahedral mismatch factor helps map \(A_2BB'X_6\) type perovskites. Filip and Giustino et al. [36] have generated a library of new hitherto unknown 94,232 formable perovskites with 80 % fidelity. Talapatra et al. [24] predicted 414 oxide perovskites using 28 elemental features along with (\(t\), \(\mu\), \(\bar{\mu}_B\)). Their predicted compositions are

most promising for experimental synthesis as they are predicted from both formability and stability prediction models, simultaneously. The stability of perovskites refers to the thermodynamic stability of cubic perovskites [18].

The formability and stability are the key features of new compounds. The formability of perovskites relies on various geometric features. On the other hand, stability is another essential feature that helps to understand the thermodynamic stability concerning the crystal structure of perovskites. With the study of the stability of perovskites, we can predict whether the crystal structure will decompose into some other phases or remain unchanged. The stability analysis can be performed using an energy convex hull. The energy convex hull is a linear combination of phases that are lower in energy than any other phase [18]. The phases of a compound that lie on the energy convex hull will be treated as thermodynamically stable and phases above the convex hull will be either meta-stable or unstable. The energy above the convex hull (\(\Delta E_h^{ABX_3}\)) of a compound \(ABX_3\) can be calculated with the help formation energy of the compound (\(E_f^{ABX_3}\)) and the convex hull energy of \(ABX_3\) composition (\(E_{ch}\)), given by

\(\Delta E_h^{ABX_3} = E_f^{ABX_3} - E_{ch},\) \newline (4)

where the formation energy of \(ABX_3\) can be calculated by the total energy of the compound and chemical potentials of individual elements A, B, and X (i.e. \(\mu_A\), \(\mu_B\) and \(3\mu_X\)) which is given by

\(E_f^{ABX_3} = E^{ABX_3} - \mu_A - \mu_B - 3\mu_X,\) \newline (5)

This approach is also applicable for double perovskites.

Traditional approach to discovering new materials has been been based on tedious testing, characterization, and prior art. Density functional theory (DFT) can be used for predicting stability, electronic, and optical properties. However, DFT-based calculations are known to require expensive computation resources and are time-consuming. The literature about already synthesized materials, predicted but not synthesized materials and the DFT-based database of stable and unstable materials can help extract valuable information using machine learning (ML). In this manuscript, ML-driven discovery of oxide and halide perovskites is carried out considering single and double perovskite structures. The formability prediction of novel perovskites is performed based on the experimentally known perovskites and non-perovskites. Further, the stability prediction is performed using energy above the hull information of the perovskites using a DFT-based data available in the open literature. Overall, the databases contain the atom-specific and geometric properties of perovskites. Only those compounds are selected that are common in the formability and stability dataset. Finally, the novel perovskites are predicted that are formable as well as stable with remarkably high threshold probability [24,37]. Further, the characteristics of novel compounds are investigated to examine their potential in energy storage applications. A workflow followed in this work is shown graphically in Figure 1.
