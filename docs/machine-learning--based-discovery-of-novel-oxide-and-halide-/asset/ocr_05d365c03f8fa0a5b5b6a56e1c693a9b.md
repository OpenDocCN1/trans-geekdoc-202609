# 4.2.1. Feature Importance towards formability

The feature set for analyzing the formability of single and double types oxide and halide perovskites is analyzed. In Figure 6a., the TFI and PFI are shown before the hierarchical clustering. The hierarchical clustering dendrogram for Spearman rank-order correlations is shown in B. Only the feature sets that remain after hierarchical clustering are included in the PFI as shown in Figure 6b. The top contributing features to the formability of perovskites are the two geometric features, t and \(\mu\). The other contributing features are IX_B, IE_B and mismatch factor of atom at B-site along with EA_A and LUMO_A of atom at A-site. The PFI after the hierarchical clustering shows that that the HOMO_A and HOMO_B, shown in Figure 6b, are important for formability for perovskites. An appropriately positioned HOMO_A can enhance the A-O bonding, making it easier for the perovskite structure to form and stabilize during synthesis. A well-aligned HOMO_B, ensures effective overlap with oxygen orbitals, facilitating the formation of a stable and well-ordered perovskite lattice [59]. Appropriate energy matching between these orbitals ensures that the A-O and B-O bonds are strong and consistent, aiding the material's formability.

The partial dependence plot (PDP) of important features before the hierarchical clustering is given in B. In all partial dependencies, the most contributing feature t is the common horizontal axis and other important features are at the vertical axis. In order to get insights into the relationship between predictions and features, PDPs are very useful. One can understand, the influence of a specific feature's values on the prediction of the model. We have shown the impact of the most important feature on other dominating features.
The Pearson correlation in the form of a heat map is shown in Figures C before and after hierarchy, respectively. The amount of collinearity among the feature set is indicated by the coefficient of Pearson correlation in the heat map. The reduction in correlation scores and number of features can be seen in Figure C.1b after hierarchical clustering. The elimination of multicolinear features is based on the optimum cut-off chosen in the dendrogram shown in Figure B.1.
