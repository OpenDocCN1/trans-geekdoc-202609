### 5.2. Potential candidate for battery applications

A total of 591 novel oxide perovskite and halide perovskite compounds are found using the ML model. The final list of compounds comprises 247 oxide perovskite, 19 fluoride perovskite, 132 chloride perovskite, and 190 bromide perovskite. For battery application, the charge capacity [60] is estimated using the formula given by

$Q = nF/(3600 * Mw)mAhg^{-1}$

where $F$ is the *Faraday constant*, $n$ is the number of charge carriers and $Mw$ is the molecular weight of perovskite materials to be used in electrodes. Consider the following chemical equation:

$LiTaO_3 \longrightarrow Li^+ + TaO_3 + 1e^-$

The value of n will be 1 and molecular weight of LiTaO3 is 235.89 g/mol. Hence using the above equation, the charge capacity will be 113.62 mAhg⁻¹.

We prepared a separate data frame to estimate the charge capacity of final compounds. The highest charge capacity is found in a fluoride perovskite in all halide perovskite. The charge capacity is 215.63 mAhg⁻¹ found in TlBTeGeF₆ in double halide perovskites. In other halide perovskite, e.g. in chloride perovskites TlBTeGeCl₆ results in highest charge capacity around 170 mAhg⁻¹ and in bromide perovskites ReVTeGeBr₆ results in 116 mAhg⁻¹. While in oxide perovskites RbTe-GeO₆ shows the highest charge capacity among all which is around 380 mAhg⁻¹. ## 6. Conclusions

A systematic analysis is presented to explore novel perovskite materials using advanced classification techniques of machine learning by preparing feature sets and a new database. The formability classification model is trained with the existing experimental database of known materials and by preparing feature sets of large chemical space of single and double oxide perovskites and halide perovskites. Similarly, the stability classification model is trained using the DFT-based database and feature sets of large chemical space of single and double perovskites. Only cubic symmetries of DFT datasets are considered in stability classifications and an energy threshold of 50 meV is selected in energy convex hull to define stable and unstable compounds in the DFT dataset. By increasing the threshold, the range of stability can be tuned and this increases the overlap with the formability database. However, this may increase the risk of adding unstable compounds to the final list of compounds. The cubic (Pm-3m) symmetry is a prototype phase of perovskites even though they can belong to other space groups like tetragonal (P4/mbm) and orthorhombic (Pmbn), but considering multiple space groups presents a complex challenge that demands the development of more sophisticated machine learning models for formability and stability predictions. The stability database uses the convex hull estimation techniques of all the compounds to predict the shape, unstable, and metastable nature of compounds using the Material Project database.

The databases comprising several atom-specific features and geometric feature sets have been prepared. The formability and stability classification models are built based on the random forest (RF) technique. These ML models are built to maximize the accuracy and reduce the variance. Another classifier, Gaussian Process Classifier (GPC) has also been employed for formability and stability models to verify the RF classifier model. It can be seen that the proposed RFC-based models are more robust and reliable. Further, the predictive values of features are measured to understand the relationship between the feature and the true outcome. The hierarchical clustering technique is used to analyze the effect of multi-collinearity among selected features and their effect on perovskite formability and stability, respectively. The formability classification model predicts 475024 new formable compounds, whereas the stability classification model predicts 437828 new stable compounds. Finally, 591 compounds are shortlisted as they are formable and stable compounds, simultaneously with great confidence. These 591 compounds are the most promising novel oxide and halide perovskites for future experimental realization. Further, these shortlisted compounds are tested theoretically for energy storage applications. The final list of compounds consists of 247 oxide perovskites, 19 fluoride perovskites, 132 chloride perovskites, and 190 bromide perovskites. The highest charge capacity is found in a fluoride perovskite but the overall highest charge capacity is found in oxide perovskites. With this ML approach, one can also study the properties of novel compounds of other crystal structures for distinct applications. The ML approach can set the path to examine the functionality of novel compounds in an extensive approach.
