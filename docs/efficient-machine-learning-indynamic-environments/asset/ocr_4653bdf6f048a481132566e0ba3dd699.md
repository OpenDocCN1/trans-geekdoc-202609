To address this problem, recent approaches to online optimization have extended this notion of regret to *dynamic regret*; where we compare our allocations to a sequence of minimizers instead of a fixed minimizer—in our case, the sequence of optimal allocations. With the notion of dynamic regret the algorithmic goal in online learning now becomes providing dynamic regret guarantees that scale favourably with the variation in optimum allocations across time; that is, if the change in solutions across time is small then our algorithm should also provide solutions with small dynamic regret [146, 150, 163]. However, even this modification is unsuitable for round-based resource allocation, especially in the context of slowly evolving systems that is the subject of this chapter, as the run-time of the algorithms is not typically a concern in this setting. In contrast, in continual optimization, we aim to provide algorithms that have both low dynamic regret as well as run-times that depend on the variation in optimum across rounds.
