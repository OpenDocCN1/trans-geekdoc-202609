# Part II – Continual Optimization in Gradually Evolving Environments

+   • Chapter 4 – Continually Adapting to Gradually Evolving Environments. Formalizing “slow evolution” in large-scale resource allocation problems, this chapter introduces the continual optimization paradigm and instantiates it with the COPTER algorithm for GPU cluster scheduling. By reusing prior solver work and warm-starting on each new round, COPTER achieves up to 100× reductions in solver time while maintaining near-optimal job completion times.

+   • Chapter 5 – Gradual Evolution Beyond Machine Learning. To show generality beyond ML training, we apply continual optimization to two further domains—shard load balancing and WAN traffic engineering. Using COPTER, both MILP and LP formulations solve 10–100× faster than state-of-the-art baselines, with negligible loss in allocation quality.

# 1.5 Broader Impact and Future Outlook

The evidence we collect across Part I and Part II shows that adapting computation to its deployment environment can unlock order-of-magnitude resource efficiency savings while remaining competitive. The gains translate into longer battery life on edge devices, lower carbon footprints for data center workloads, and wider access to high-quality machine learning in resource-constrained settings. Looking forward, the adaptive lens opens several research frontiers:

+   • Edge-to-Cloud Continuum. Current elastic models end at the device boundary, while continual optimizers start inside the data center. An end-to-end pipeline that jointly tunes on-device inference and backend scheduling will minimize total cost for hybrid applications such as real-time video analytics.

+   • Privacy-Aware Adaptivity. Benign workloads often arise in federated or privacy-constrained settings, where data cannot leave the device. Extending continual optimization to respect differential privacy or secure aggregation constraints would enable low-cost personalization without compromising data.

+   • Theory of Continual Optimization. Chapters 4 and 5 provide empirical evidence and formalizations that show how continual optimization can accelerate benign workloads. Extending these to other notions of gradual changes, considering cases where even the warm-started solver gets only a few iterations, and providing tighter regret/runtime bounds are interesting open problems.

By treating environmental variability—whether wild or mild—as an opportunity rather than a threat, this dissertation charts a path toward machine learning systems that are not only more efficient, but also more sustainable, privacy-preserving, and universally deployable.
