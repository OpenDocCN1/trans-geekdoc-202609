However, the use of ensemble agreement as a deferral mechanism for more general, modern ML workloads has not received enough attention. A few trends in ML make ABC a particularly attractive approach for model cascading. While using an ensemble of models at each level may initially appear to increase overall inference costs, this simple baseline could excel in real-world applications due to: (i) the growing ease of obtaining pretrained models of various sizes and accuracy levels due to the rise of model registries [98] as well as flexible compression schemes [166, 184]; (ii) scaling laws that predict a large increase in inference cost for every marginal increase in accuracy [64] (Figure 3.1); and (iii) an increased ability to execute ensembles of relatively small models in parallel, with minimal additional costs [16, 173, 178] (Figure 3.3).

In this chapter we (i) propose ABC, a training-free, deferral-based cascade that leverages agreement within an ensemble to decide when to escalate, removing the need for dedicated routing networks or specialized architectures; (ii) theoretically characterize when ABC can replace an existing deployment without sacrificing accuracy, introduce *safe-deferral* conditions, and derive bounds on expected accuracy and inference cost; and (iii) empirically demonstrate that ABC improves both efficiency and accuracy across diverse vision and language benchmarks. In practical settings, ABC cuts communication cost by up to 14× in edge-to-cloud inference, reduces GPU rental cost by up to 3× in heterogeneous model-serving scenarios, and lowers average API cost per token by up to 25× when models are accessed as black-box services.

# 3.2.1 Overview of ABC Approach

As outlined in Section 3.1, the goal of adaptive inference is to identify data samples that can be evaluated accurately by a less expensive model. This way, we can reduce the inference cost based on whether a sample is ‘easy’ or ‘hard’. A common approach to this problem is deferral-based cascading, where we cascade over a sequence of models, starting from the least expensive and using a ‘deferral rule’ to determine if a higher tier model must be used.
