# ## 3.4 Experiments

This section evaluates our training-free Agreement-Based Cascading (ABC) approach across a variety of language and vision tasks, focusing on accuracy and inference cost. First, in Section 3.4.1, we examine ABC’s accuracy-cost tradeoff against state-of-the-art models under full parallelization, setting the _parallelization factor_ ($ho$) to $ho = 1.0$ for optimal accuracy-FLOPs trade-off. Next, in Section 3.4.1, we show that using ensembles at lower tiers has minimal impact on inference cost when lower-tier models are at least 50$	imes$ cheaper than the largest model. In such cases, the _relative cost_ ($\gamma$) satisfies $\gamma \le \frac{1}{50}$, making ABC effective without parallelization. Finally, Section 3.4.2 explores practical scenarios where low relative costs make ABC naturally suited for real-world deployments.

**Estimating Voting Threshold:** ABC’s deferral rule uses a configurable voting threshold, $	heta$ (see Equations 3.3 and 3.4) at each cascading tier. We estimate $	heta$ empirically on a small set of unseen data; see Appendix B.2 for details.

**Models:** We select diverse models for both image and language tasks, summarized in Table B.2, Appendix. For BERT-based models, we use the **BASE** and **LARGE**, and for image models, we tier by FLOPs count.
