Gradually evolving environments: Many real systems are more benign and evolve gradually. The job queue on a GPU cluster at minute t resembles the queue at minute t−1; network throughput in minute five is close to that in minute four. Such slow drift makes yesterday’s solution a valuable head start on today’s problem. Algorithms for this regime are therefore stateful: they cache intermediate results, apply differential updates, and amortize effort across rounds instead of recomputing from scratch.

This dissertation is organized into two parts based on the type of environment they focus on: Part I designs elastic models for ML inference in arbitrary environments, and Part II discusses continual optimization for gradually evolving environments.

# 1.4 Contributions and Roadmap

By fusing ideas from machine learning, systems, and optimization, this work delivers practical contributions to support our thesis, organized across two parts:

# Part I – Adaptive Inference in Arbitrary Environments

+   Chapter 2 – Constructing Elastic ML Models. We introduce progressive ensemble distillation, a principled way to decompose a large pretrained model into an elastic ensemble whose inference cost can be dialed up or down at runtime. The resulting B-DISTIL algorithm uses a boosting-like training procedure to build the ensemble and delivers competitive accuracy under tight resource budgets. B-DISTIL shows improvements in resource utilization across tasks in vision, speech, and sensor benchmarks and admits a O(1/√T) convergence guarantee for the distillation procedure.

+   Chapter 3 – Dynamically Determining Elasticity. Shifting focus from fluctuating budgets to fluctuating query difficulty, ABC leverages ensemble agreement as a difficulty signal to construct elastic models that adapt to query difficulty changes. Because it relies solely on forward passes for the agreement checks, ABC is completely training-free and can be deployed on black-box APIs. Across four language, vision, and multimodal tasks, ABC cuts communication cost by up to 14× in edge-to-cloud inference, reduces GPU rental cost by up to 3× in heterogeneous model-serving scenarios, and lowers average API cost per token by 25× when accessed as black-box services.
