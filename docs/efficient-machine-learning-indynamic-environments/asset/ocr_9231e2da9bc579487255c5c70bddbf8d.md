Finally, if the ensemble $h_1(x)$ outperforms the SoTA model on the ‘easy’ samples by some strictly positive $\xi > 0$, such that

$P(h_1(x) \neq y \mid r(x) = 0) \leq P(h_2(x) \neq y \mid r(x) = 0) - \xi$

Then, from Equation (B.2)

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{excess}}(\mathcal{M}_r, h_2) = \Big(P(h_1(x) \neq y \mid r(x) = 0) - P(h_2(x) \neq y \mid r(x) = 0)\Big)P(r(x) = 0) = -\xi P(r(x) = 0) < 0,$$

where the selection rate satisfies $P(r(x) = 0) > 0$. Negative excess risk implies an improvement over the SoTA classifier we were using with the overall amount of improvement depending on $P(r(x) = 0)$.
