Let's take a look at only the dep_delay, arr_delay, and the resulting gain variables in
t first 5 rows in our updated flights data frame in Table 3.1.

**TABLE 3.1:** First rows of departure/arrival delay and gain variables

| dep_delay | arr_delay | gain |

| 47 | 34 | 13 |

The flight in the first row departed 203 minutes 32 minutes late, so its
“gained time in the air” is a gain of -2 minutes, hence its gain is $203-205 = -2$,
is a loss of 2 minutes. On the other hand, the flight in the third row departed
late (dep_delay of 47) but arrived 34 minutes late (arr_delay of 34), so its “gained time
in the air” is $47-34 = 13$ minutes, hence its gain is 13.
