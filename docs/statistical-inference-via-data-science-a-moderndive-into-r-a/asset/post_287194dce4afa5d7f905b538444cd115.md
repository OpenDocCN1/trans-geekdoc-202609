**FIGURE 5.8:** Life expectancy in 2022 by continent (boxplot).

Some people prefer comparing the distributions of a numerical variable between different levels of a categorical variable using a boxplot instead of a faceted histogram. This is because we can make quick comparisons between the categorical variable's levels with imaginary horizontal lines. For example, observe in Figure 5.8 that we can quickly convince ourselves that Europe has the highest median life expectancies by drawing an imaginary line near y = 81. Furthermore, as we observed in the faceted histogram Figure 5.7, Africa and Asia have the largest variation in life expectancy as evidenced by their high interquartile ranges (the size of the boxes).

It's important to remember, that the solid lines in the middle of the boxes correspond to the medians (the middle value) rather than the mean (the average). So, for example, if you look at Asia, the solid line denotes the median life expectancy of around 75 years. This tells us that half of all countries in Asia have a life expectancy below 75 years, whereas half have a life expectancy above 75 years. We compute the median and mean life expectancy for each continent with a little data wrangling and display the results in Table 5.6.

life_exp_by_continent <- gapminder2022 |>

## 5.2.2 Linear regression

In Subsection 5.1.2 we introduced simple linear regression, which involves modeling the relationship between a numerical outcome variable y and a numerical explanatory variable x. In our life expectancy example, we instead have a categorical explanatory variable. Our model will not yield a “best-fitting” regression line like in Figure 5.3, but rather offsets relative to a baseline for comparison.

As we did in Subsection 5.1.2 when studying the relationship between fertility rates and life expectancy, we output the regression coefficients for this model. Recall that this is done in two steps:

- 1. We first “fit” the linear regression model using the lm(y ~ x, data) function and save it in life_exp_model.
- 2. We get the regression coefficients by applying the coef() function to life_exp_model.

```r
life_exp_model <- lm(life_exp ~ continent, data = gapminder2022)
coef(life_exp_model)
```

| (Intercept) | continentAsia | continentEurope |
|---|---|---|
| 66.31 | 8.64 | 13.60 |
| continentNorth America | continentOceania | continentSouthAmerica |
| 9.99 | 8.11 | 8.92 |

We once again focus on the values in this coefficient values. Why are there now 6 entries? We break them down one by:

- 1. intercept corresponds to the mean life expectancy of countries in Africa of 66.31.
- 2. continent corresponds to countries in Asia and the value +8.64 is the same difference in mean expectancy relative to Africa displayed in Table 5.7. In other words, the mean life expectancy of countries in Asia is 66.31 + 8.64 = 74.95.
- 3. continent corresponds to countries in Europe and the value +13.6 is the same difference in mean expectancy relative to Africa displayed in Table 5.7. In other words, the mean life expectancy of countries in Europe is 66.31 + 13.6 = 79.91.
- 4. continent corresponds to countries in North America and the value +9.98 is the same difference in mean expectancy relative to Africa displayed in Table 5.7. In other words, the mean life expectancy of countries in North America is 66.31 + 9.98 = 76.29.
- 5. continent corresponds to countries in Oceania and the value +8.11 is the same difference in mean expectancy relative to Africa displayed in Table 5.7. In other words, the mean life expectancy of countries in Oceania is 66.31 + 8.11 = 74.42.
- 6. continent corresponds to countries in South America and the value +8.92 is the same difference in mean expectancy relative to Africa displayed in Table 5.7. In other words, the mean life expectancy of countries in South America is 66.31 + 8.92 = 75.23.

To summarize, the 6 values for the regression coefficients correspond to the “baseline for comparison” Africa (the intercept) as well as five “offsets” from this baseline for Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, and South America. You might be asking why Africa was chosen as the “baseline for comparison” group for no reason it comes first alphabetically; you can change this baseline to another if you manipulate the continent factor “levels”. See Chapter 5 of R for Data Science (Grolem and Wickham, 2017). We now write the equation for our fitted values $\hat{y}$ = life_exp.

$$\hat{y} = b_0 + b_{Asia} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Asia}(x) + b_{Europe} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Europe}(x) + b_{NorthAmerica} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{NorthAmerica}(x) + b_{Oceania} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Oceania}(x) + b_{SouthAmerica} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{SouthAmerica}(x)$$

Whoa! That looks daunting! Don't fret, however, as you understand what all the elements simplify greatly. First, $\mathbb{1}_{A}(x)$ is what is known in mathematics as an "indicator function." It returns one of two values, 0 and 1, where

$$\mathbb{1}_{A}(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x \text{ is in } A \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

In a statistical modeling context, this is also known as a dummy variable. In our case, we consider the first such indicator variable $\mathbb{1}_{Amer}(x)$. This indicator returns 1 if a country is in Asia, 0 otherwise:

$$\mathbb{1}_{Amer}(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x \text{ is in Asia} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Second, $b_0$ corresponds to the intercept as before; in this case, it is the mean expectancy of all countries in Africa. Third, $b_{Asia}, b_{Europe}, b_{NorthAmerica}, b_{Oceania},$ and $b_{SouthAmerica}$ represent the five "offsets relative to the baseline for comparison" in the regression coefficients.

We put this all together and compute the fitted value $\hat{y}$ = life_exp for a country in Africa. Since the country is in Africa, all five indicator functions $\mathbb{1}_{Asia}(x), \mathbb{1}_{Europe}(x), \mathbb{1}_{NorthAmerica}(x), \mathbb{1}_{Oceania}(x),$ and $\mathbb{1}_{SouthAmerica}(x)$ will equal 0, and thus:

$$\hat{y} = b_0 + b_{Asia} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Asia}(x) + b_{Europe} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Europe}(x) + b_{NorthAmerica} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{NorthAmerica}(x) + b_{Oceania} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Oceania}(x) + b_{SouthAmerica} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{SouthAmerica}(x)$$
$$= 66.31 + 8.64 \cdot 0 + 13.6 \cdot 0 + 9.98 \cdot 0 + 8.11 \cdot 0 + 8.92 \cdot 0$$
$$= 66.31$$

In other words, all that left is the intercept $b_0$, corresponding to the life expectancy of African countries of 66.31 years. Next, say we are considering a country in Asia. In this case, only the indicator $\mathbb{1}_{Asia}(x)$ for Asia will equal 1, while all the others will equal 0, and thus:
