TABLE 9.6 Population Notation Point estimate Symbol(s)

| Scenario | Population parameter | Notation | Point estimate | Symbol(s) |

So, whereas the sampling bowl exercise in Section 7.1 concerned proportions, the almost exercise in Section 8.2.1 concerned means, the case study on utterly yawning is contagious in Section 8.4 and the music genre activity in Section 9.2 concerned differences in proportions, we are now concerned with differences in means.

In other words, the population parameter of interest is the difference in population mean ratings $\mu_a - \mu_r$, where $\mu_a$ is the mean rating of all movies on IMDb and similarly $\mu_r$ is the mean rating of romance movies. Additionally the point estimate/sample statistic of interest is the difference in sample means $ar{x}_a - ar{x}_r$, where $ar{x}_a$ is the mean rating of the $n_a=32$ movies in action movies and $ar{x}_r$ is the mean rating of the $n_r=36$ in our sample. Based on our earlier exploratory data analysis, our estimate $ar{x}_a - ar{x}_r = 5.275 - 1.047$.

So appears to be a slight difference of -1.047 in favor of romance movies. The question is, however, could this difference of -1.047 be merely to chance and sampling variation? Are these results indicative of a true difference in mean ratings for all romance and action movies on IMDb? To answer this question, we will use hypothesis testing.

# 9.6.3 Conducting the hypothesis test

We will be testing:

$H_0 : \mu_a - \mu_r = 0$
vs $H_A : \mu_a - \mu_r 
eq 0$

In other words, the null hypothesis $H_0$ suggests that both romance and action movies have the same mean rating. This is the “hypothesized universe” we will *assume* is to be true. On the other hand, the alternative hypothesis $H_A$ suggests that there is a difference.
