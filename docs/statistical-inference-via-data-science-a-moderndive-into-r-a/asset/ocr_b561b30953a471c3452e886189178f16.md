# 9.7.3 Problems with p-values

On top of the many misunderstandings about hypothesis testing and p-values we listed in Section 9.5, another unfortunate consequence of the expanded use of p-values and hypothesis testing is a phenomenon known as “p-hacking.” phacking is the act of “cherry-picking” only results that are “statistically significant” while dismissing those that are not, even at the expense of the scientific ideas. There are lots of articles written regarding misunderstandings and the problems with p-values. We encourage you to check some of them:

Such issues are so problematic that the American Statistical Association (ASA) put out a statement in 2016 titled, “The ASA Statement on Statistical Significance and P-Values,”⁹ with six principles governing the proper use and interpretation of p-values. The ASA released guidance on p-values to improve the conduct and interpretation of quantitative science and to inform the growing emphasis on reproducibility of science.
