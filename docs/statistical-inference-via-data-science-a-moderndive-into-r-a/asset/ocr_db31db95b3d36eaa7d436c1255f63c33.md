# 11 Tell Your Story with Data

Next, see in the histogram for price (the top-left plot) that a majority of houses is less than two million dollars. Observe also that the x-axis stretches out to 8 million dollars, even though there does not appear to be any houses close to that price. This is because there are a very small number of houses prices closer to 8 million as noted in the tidy_summary(). These are the outlier prices we mentioned earlier. We say that the variable is right-skewed as exhibited by the long right tail.

Further, the histogram of sqft_living in the middle plot shows that most houses appear to have less than 5000 square feet of living space. For comparison, an American football field in the US is about 57,600 square feet, whereas a standard soccer/association football field is about 64,000 square feet. Observe also that this variable is also right-skewed, not as drastically as the price variable.

For both price and sqft variables, the right-skew makes distinguishing houses at the lower end of the x-axis hard. This is because the scale of the x-axis is compressed by the small number of quite expensive and immensely-sized houses.

So what can we do about this skew? Let's apply a log10 transformation to these variables.

In summary, log transformations allow us to alter the scale of a variable to focus on multiplicative changes instead of additive changes. In other words, we shift the view to be relative changes instead of absolute changes. Such multiplicative/relative changes are also changed in orders of magnitude.

Let's create new log10 versions of the right-skewed price variable and sqft_living using the mutate() function from Section 3.5, but we'll give the latter name log10_size, which is shorter and easier to understand than the log10_sqft_living.

```
house_prices <- house_prices |>
mmutate(
  log10_price = log10(price),
  log10_size = log10(sqft_living)
)
```

Let's display the before and after effects of this transformation on these variables for the first 10 rows of house_prices:

```
house_prices |>
select(price, log10_price, sqft_living, log10_size)
```
