In other words, if we gathered 100 samples of $n = 50$ participants from a similar pool of people and constructed 100 confidence intervals each based on each of the 100 samples, about 95 of them will contain the true value of $p_{seed}-p_{control}$ while not given. Given that this is a little winded, we use the shorthand interpretation: we are 95% “confident” that the true difference in proportions $p_{seed}-p_{control}$ is between (-0.238, 0.302).

There is one value of particular interest that this 95% confidence interval contains: zero. If $p_{seed}-p_{control}$ were equal to 0, then there would be no difference in proportion yawning between the two. This would suggest that there is no associated effect of being exposed to a yawning recruiter on whether you yawn yourself.

In our case, since the 95% confidence interval includes 0, we cannot conclusively say either proportion is larger. Of our 1000 bootstrap samples with replacement, sometimes $p_{seed}$ was higher and those exposed to yawning yawned themselves more often. At other times, the reverse happened.

Say, on the other hand, the 95% confidence interval was entirely above zero. This would suggest that $p_{seed}-p_{control} > 0$, or, in other words $p_{seed} > p_{control}$, and thus we'd have evidence suggesting those exposed to yawning yawn more often.
