
$\tilde{y} = b_0 + b_{02}D_2 + b_{03}D_3 + b_{04}D_4 + b_1x + b_{12}xD_2 + b_{13}xD_3 + b_{14}xD_4$

The linear regression shows how the average life expectancy is affected by the categorical variables, the numerical variable, and the interaction effects. There are eight coefficients in our model and we have separated their coefficients into three lines to highlight their roles. The first line shows the intercept and the effects of the categorical explanatory variables. Recall that $D_2, D_3,$ and $D_4$ are the dummy variables in the model and each is equal to one or zero depending on the category of the country and at hand; correspondingly, the coefficients $b_{02}, b_{03},$ and $b_{04}$ are the offsets with respect to the baseline level of the intercept, $b_0$. Recall that the first dummy variable has been dropped and the intercept captures this effect. The second line in the equation represents the numerical variable, $x$. In our example $x$ is the value of life expectancy. The coefficient $b_1$ is the slope of the line and represents the change in fertility rate due to one change in life expectancy. The third line in the equation represents the interaction effects on the slopes. Observe that they are a combination of life expectancy, $x$, and income level, $D_2, D_3,$ and $D_4$. What interaction effects does is to modify the slope for different levels of income. For a low income member state, the dummy variables are $D_1=1, D_2=D_3=D_4=0$ and our linear regression is:

```
$\tilde{y} = b_0 + b_{02} \cdot 0 + b_{03} \cdot 0 + b_{04} \cdot 0 + b_1x + b_{12} \cdot 0 + b_{13} \cdot 0 + b_{14} \cdot 0$
$= b_0 + b_1x$
```

Similarly, for a high income member state, the dummy variables are $D_1=D_2=D_3=0,$ and $D_4=1$. We take into account the offsets for the intercept, $b_{04},$ and the slope, $b_{14}$; and the linear regression becomes:

```
$\tilde{y} = b_0 + b_{02} \cdot 0 + b_{03} \cdot 0 + b_{04} \cdot 1 + b_1x + b_{12} \cdot 0 + b_{13} \cdot 0 + b_{14} \cdot 1$
$= b_0 + b_{04} + b_1x + b_{14}x$
$= (b_0 + b_{04}) + (b_1 + b_{14}) \cdot x$
```

Observe how the intercept and the slope are different for a high income member state compared to the baseline low income member state. As an illustration, we construct this multiple linear regression for the UN member dataset in R. We first “fit” the model using the `linear model` function and then find the coefficients using the function `coef()`. In R, the formula is used $y = x1 + x2$ where $x1$ and $x2$ are the reality variables in the dataset and represent the main effects while $x1:x2$ is the interaction term. For simplicity, we can also write $y \sim x1 * x2$ for both, main effects and interaction effects. R would let both x1 and x2 be either explanatory or numerical, and to make sure the dataset is appropriate for the regression we want to run. Here is the code for our example:

```r
# Fit regression model and get the coefficients of the model
```

> $fert \ rate = b_0 + b_1 \cdot x = 11.92 + (-0.12) \cdot x$,

which is the equation of the regression line in Figure 6.1 for low income countries.
The regression has an equation of 11.92 and a slope of -0.12. Since life expectancy is greater than zero for all countries, the intercept has no practical interpretation we only need to produce the most appropriate line. The interpretation of the slope is: for Low income countries, every additional year of life expectancy reduces the average fertility rate by 0.12 units.

As discussed earlier, the intercept and slope for all the other income groups are determined by taking into account the appropriate offsets. For, High income countries $D_4 = 1$ and all other dummy variables are equal to zero. The regression line becomes

> $y = fert rate = b_0 + b_1x + b_{04} + b_{14}x = (b_0 + b_{04}) + (b_1 + b_{14})x$

where $x$ is life expectancy, $life\_exp$. The intercept is (Intercept) + incomeHigh income

> $b_0 + b_{04} = 11.92 + (-6.58) = 5.34$,

and the slope for these High income countries is $life\_exp + life\_exp:incomeHigh income corresponding to

> $b_1 + b_{14} = -0.12 + 0.07 = -0.05$.

For High income countries, every additional year of life expectancy reduces the average fertility rate by 0.05 units. The intercepts and slopes for other income levels are calculated similarly.

Since the life expectancy for Low income countries has a steeper slope than High income countries, one additional year of life expectancy will decrease fertility rates more for the low-income group than the high-income group. This is consistent with our observation from Figure 6.1. When the effect of one variable depends on the value of another variable we say that there is an interaction effect. This is the reason why the regression slopes are different for different income groups.
