# 7.3.5 Summary

Let's look at what we have learned about the sampling distribution of the sample proportion:

+   1. The mean of all the sample proportions will be exactly the same as the population proportion.
2. The standard deviation of the sample proportions, also called the standard error, is inversely proportional to the square root of the sample size: the larger the size used to calculate sample proportions, the closer the sample proportions will be from the population proportion, on average.
3. As long as the random samples are large enough, the sampling distribution of the sample proportion, or simply the distribution of $X$, will approximate the normal distribution. This is true for sample proportions regardless of the structure of the underlying population distribution; regardless of how many red and white balls are in the bowl, or whether you are performing any other experiment that deals with sample proportions.
