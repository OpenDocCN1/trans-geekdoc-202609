which is the mean life expectancy for countries in Asia of 74.9 years in Table 5.7. Note the "offset from the baseline for comparison" is +86.4 years.

 do one more. We are considering a country in South America. In this case, the only indicator $\mathbb{1}_{South America}(x)$ for South America will be one, while the others will be zero, and thus:

```
$life \exp = b_0 + b_{Asia} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Asia}(x) + b_{Europe} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Europe}(x)\n+ b_{North America} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{North America}(x) + b_{Oceania} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Oceania}(x)\n+ b_{South America} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{South America}(x)\n= 66.31 + 8.64 \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Asia}(x) + 13.6 \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Europe}(x)\n+ 9.98 \cdot \mathbb{1}_{North America}(x) + 8.11 \cdot \mathbb{1}_{Oceania}(x) + 8.92 \cdot \mathbb{1}_{South America}(x)\n= 66.31 + 8.64 \cdot 0 + 13.6 \cdot 0 + 9.98 \cdot 0 \ 0 + 8.92 \cdot 1\n= 66.31 + 8.92\n= 75.23$
```

which is the mean life expectancy for South American countries of 75.23 years in Table 5.7. The "offset from the baseline for comparison" is +86.4 years.

We generalize this idea. If we fit a regression model using a categorical explanatory variable $x$ with $k$ possible countries, the regression will return an intercept and $k-1$ "offsets." In our case, since there are $k=6$ continents, the regression model returns an intercept corresponding to the baseline for comparison group of Africa and $k-1=5$ offsets corresponding to Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, and South America.

Understanding a regression table output when you are using a categorical explanatory variable is a topic new to regression often struggle with. The real remedy for these struggles is practice, practice, practice. However, once once once yourself yourself with how to of regression models using categorical explanatory variables, you'll be able to incorporate many new variables into your models, given the large amount of world's data that is categorical.
