### 9.6.6.`Analysis of the results`

In relation to ‘utility’, this component of the Evaluation activity was intended to ensure that the proposed model had ‘serviceability’, ‘applicability’ and ‘appropriateness’ to the law enforcement, commerce, incident response and legal domains as stated in the research problem. The model was therefore subjected to an external, independent evaluation by the leading experts in its intended user community to ensure that it had been built right and that the right model had been built. The comments regarding the SDFIPM were very positive, and the feedback was both constructive and insightful. There were a total number of 31 detailed pieces of feedback in relation to the larger model, the CDFIPM, 11 of which related to the SDFIPM, including suggestions for new additions, amendments and deletions. Out of the 11 feedbacks for the SDFIPM, 2 new additions, 4 amendments and 1 deletion were made in and from the SDFIPM. Having made the new model subject to the experts’ scrutiny, it became clear that there did not exist any aspects of the SDFIPM that were not representative of the processes as carried out in the fields covered by the research scope. Similarly, there were not any aspects of the processes as performed in the stated fields that were not covered in the SDFIPM. In response to the question of utility (see the questionnaire in Appendix B), “Please rate the model on the scale of 1 to 10, with 1 being not useful at all and 10 being the most useful.”, experts provided the mean score of ‘9’. This suggests that the SDFIPM has achieved the intended component of utility required of a useful artefact. The following are some comments made by the experts in relation to the utility of the model:

•*“Much of your model covers most aspects in good detail of what is required but the order would change or be adopted to operational needs. But, all principles and processes are there in good details.”* (`Law enforcement DFP and manager`)

•*“You have identified the relevant problems with the current models and have provided detailed analysis. I believe that your model has made a significantly novel contribution in its current length and presentation. Congratulations on accomplishing such a daunting task!”* (`University professor`)

•*“This model allows a smooth transition from one phase to another during a digital investigation. If I were to use it, it would certainly give me a detailed advice about what to do with the evidence.”* (`Commercial DFP`)

•*“You have covered all avenues in different elements of digital investigations.”* (`Law enforcement DFP`)

•*“It is absolutely practicable and structured. You would not go wrong.”* (`Commercial DFP`)

•*“This model is very comprehensive; I can’t see any loopholes. Some issues are raised about detaining a suspect, where there could be multiple persons in a property with none saying who the suspect is.”* (`Law enforcement DFP`)

•*“I can say that your model provides transparency in acquiring produced result for each given process.”* (`Legal practitioner, Judge`)

•*“Because of its level of details, your model will facilitate collecting high quality evidence in a fast manner.”* (`Law enforcement DFI`)

With regards to the ‘usability’, this component of the Evaluation activity was intended to determine the ease with which experts in the law enforcement, commerce, incident response and legal domains can employ the SDFIPM to carry out those activities described by the model that relate to their practice. In response to the question of usability (see the questionnaire in Appendix B), “Please rate the model on the scale of 1 to 10, with 1 being very difficult to use and 10 being very easy to use”, experts provided the mean score of ‘8.7’. This also indicates that the SDFIPM has achieved the intended component of usability required of a useful artefact. The following are some comments made by the experts in relation to the usability of the SDFIPM:

•*“This model is clear in its layout and the majority of its terms, which largely enables the user to understand the steps taken during the investigation without assistance. Where some of the terms are unclear or technical, the assistance of the explanation of a digital forensic investigator would overcome any difficulty in comprehension.”* (`Legal practitioner, barrister`)

•*“It is important to have robust process behind the presentation of digital evidence because you have an adversarial system. Each seeks to undermine the evidence of the other if they can. Your model presents the forensic process very clearly; it is readily understandable to someone without technical expertise especially for jury.”* (`Legal practitioner, judge`)

•*“Your model has a very clear layout that covers all aspects succinctly.”* (`Law enforcement DFI`)

•*“I like the workflows as they are simple but effective.”* (`Law enforcement DFI`)

The aim of this subsection was to evaluate the SDFIPM in order to determine whether the model had been built right and whether the right model had been built and consequently to establish whether the SDFIPM had achieved both the ‘utility’ and ‘usability’ components. To this end, the model was subjected to an independent, external evaluation by its intended user community. From the positive feedback received and its analysis, it has become clear that the model has been built right and the right model has been built. It has also become obvious that the SDFIPM has achieved both the utility and usability required from a forensic artefact.

### 9.7.`Conclusion`

### 9.7.1.`Research problem addressed`

Following the identification of three major issues with the previous DFIPMs, namely the lack of a model that has all three characteristics of being “comprehensive”, “formal” and “generic”, we formulated the following research question that we set out to address:

There does not exist a comprehensive model encompassing the entire digital investigative process that is formal, such that it can assist a court of law in determining the reliability of the investigative process followed, and that is generic, in that it can be applied in the different fields of law enforcement, commerce and incident response.
