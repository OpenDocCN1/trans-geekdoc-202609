
If we consider `blockchain` a mechanism which works in harmony with other technologies then it has the potential to offer an additional layer of security to protect mission-critical data. This could be deployed in harmony with the practices of rolling out policies/compliance, training, keeping patching up to date, IDPS and so on. These are to further protect the privacy of an individual’s data and enhance the security through methods of encryption which provide that added layer of security should traditional methods break down due to sophisticated cyber attacks and targeted methods used to obtain data and commit identity theft. Also, with the recent emergence of IoT and the value-add it brings to many organisations, it is important to consider how the huge increase in data should be handled and kept secure. The billions of additional smart wearable devices in use today may contain sensitive data on individuals which should be protected by a combination of traditional security methods and blockchain.

Blockchain security is itself interesting. More research in machine learning and algorithms may also help in protecting blockchain as machine learning and algorithms can detect intrusions from and recognise patterns in data collected through behavioural monitoring and profiling (Xu, 2016). This will help strengthen the defences against malicious attackers and prevent them from succeeding if the method is recognised by AI. The wider adoption of blockchain will also help protect users against malicious activity as more effort will be expanded on developing prevention systems. Certainly, blockchain has many applications, and can be made to work in synergy with traditional cyber security mechanisms. For the time being, blockchain is a formidable and strong security option. It is a useful mechanism which can provide support in many industries which collect sensitive and personal data, particularly in industry sectors which are most vulnerable to cyber attack.
