### 8.5.Results and Analysis

The written and audio recordings were transcribed verbatim, after researchers continuously listened to the recording and made relevant notes.

Kuada (2012) emphasizes that focus group analysis differs from individual interviews, in that the data gathered will also come from observing participants’ reaction patterns and the overall interpersonal dynamics. The data gathered from the focus was analysed using a structured approach to present a range of evidence on the design, usability and efficacy of the DSS.

### 8.5.1.*Results*

The applicability of the system design was assessed by subject experts with working knowledge of GDPR who provided feedback on the topic. Over the last few years, organisations have seen an increased trend in data breach incidents and sophisticated attack vectors (P.1, P.2, P.3, P.4, P.5, Q.1, Q.2). Some of the attacks became more targeted. Such attacks are probably motivated by financial gain.

### 8.5.2.*Usability of DSS for addressing privacy by design and default*

The design of the system employing the UML process flow provides an overview of processes that can aid decisions to address security incidents as well as breach notification and communication requirements (P.39, P.40, P.41, P.42, P.43, P.44, Q.12, Q.13). When assessing the usability of the system with reference to the incorporation of privacy by design and default, the participants noted the relevance of the change request diagram and the distinct colour scheme of the UML process flow to developing and integrating security safeguards for new hardware and software systems (P.45, P.46, P.47, P.48, P.49, Q.14, Q.15). Further discussions on the mapping of articles against security controls noted the usability and relevance of the DSS mapping document for the interpretation and integration of the GDPR into the incident response plans (P.50, P.51, P.52, Q.16, Q.17).

### 8.5.3.*Feedback and recommendations for improving design*

The discussions and feedback provided by the focus group interviewees presented valuable professional insight into the GDPR implementation challenges and the use of a decision support system for addressing mandatory requirements. The feedback on the overall design described the system as having the relevant features for formulating GDPR incident response plans, however further improvements were recommended so that the system would have response procedures for incidents occurring outside of normal working hours. It was also recommended that the contacts of key personnel in the response teams be listed (P.53, P.54, P.55, Q.17, Q.18). Other recommendations for improvement in design included suggestions for the next phase iteration of the coding of the UML design into a software plugin.

### 8.5.4.*Feasibility of DSS for implementation*

After providing feedback and recommendations for future improvements of the DSS, the participants discussed the scope of the system design and its suitability for implementation within an organisation. Several implementation solutions were discussed and the DSS was assessed as appropriate for deployment along with existing systems as a guide for creating GDPR incident response plans. In addition, participants noted that business areas such as legal departments and change management departments would need to sanction the deployment of such systems in their organisation.

On balance, the recommendation for system implementation highlighted key functionalities of the system design that are appropriate for incident response and breach notification, however it was suggested that the DSS was suitable for implementation only as an annex to existing frameworks. In addition, further suggestions for improvements to and the refinement of the next phase of design included the development and testing of the DSS to automate some of the incident response phases (P.58, P.59, P.60, Q.20).

### 8.5.5.*Summary of results and analysis*

The feedback on the usability of the DSS design provided information on the relevance of the functionality of each component to addressing the challenges in implementing the GDPR. Discussions on the merits of the overall design of the DSS can facilitate the decision to establish a GDPR incident response and breach notification program within an organisation. Nevertheless, there were also recommendations for further improvements to the next phase of the DSS design, including recommendations to incorporate interactive response phases and to automate some of the functionalities of dashboards.

The development of the Decision Support System and the feedback provided by industry practitioners provided valuable insights into the viability of the design and its ability to meet incident response requirements. The participants suggested that the current design of the system could be implemented as an annex to existing systems integrating the GDPR. The feedback also confirmed some of the challenges found in the literature such as the need to have comprehensive incident response strategies and the issues associated with the ad-hoc and generic response approach for security incidents. The findings are further strengthened by the triangulation of the literature review, the analysis and the interpretation of qualitative data gathered from the feedback. Further insights into the context of the outcome can be obtained by evaluating the overall research approach and this is covered in the next chapter.
