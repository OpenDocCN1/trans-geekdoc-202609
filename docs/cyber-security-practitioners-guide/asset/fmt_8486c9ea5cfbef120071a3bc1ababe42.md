
Various researchers both within and outside the IS domain have provided guidance to define the DSR and have described what goals should be followed in its production (`Hevner` and `Chatterjee`, 2010; `Peffers` *et al*., 2006; `Hevner` *et al*., 2004; `Vaishnavi` and `Kuechler`, 2004; `Adams` and `Courtney`, 2004; `Rossi` and `Sein`, 2003; `March` and `Smith`, 1995; `Walls` *et al*., 1992; `Eekels` and `Roozenburg`, 1991; `Nunamaker` *et al*., 1990; `Takeda` *et al*., 1990; `Archer`, 1984). These researchers have often proposed various methods, processes or theoretical frameworks to rationalise the DSR studies. However, the design science research process (DSRP) model proposed by `Peffers` *et al*. (2006) was selected as the appropriate DSR methodology to conduct the research in this study. The rationale for doing so is due to the fact that it provides a graphical representation of the conceptual process for both carrying out and presenting the DSR. Such a mental model facilitates the application of the DSR and can also assist the authors in producing and presenting a high-quality DSR that would be accepted as valuable, rigorous and publishable within the field of digital forensics science (`Montasari`, 2018; `Montasari`, 2016a).

`Peffers` *et al*.’s (2006) DSRP consists of six components as shown in [Fig. 9.1](#chapter9.xhtml#fig9.1). The remainder of this section aims briefly to discuss the way in which we applied these six components to our study. For more details regarding the application of the DSRP to our research study, see Montasari’s study (`Montasari`, 2016a).

**Activity 1 — Problem Identification and Motivation**

A profound knowledge of the problem addressed by this research was acquired through a detailed analysis and assessment of the literature related to previous DFIPMs. Having drawn conclusions from the literature review, we were able to formulate the research problem that this study intends to address. Interactions with other experts and our own experience in the field of DF also provided insight into the stated problem.

**Activity 2 — Objectives of a Solution**

The research aim was formulated according to the definition of the problem. This approach is supported by `Peffers` *et al*. (2006), who state that objectives should be deduced rationally from the problem specification. Therefore, in order to formulate the stated aim, a deep knowledge was acquired of the state of the problem and its current solution in the form of previously proposed DFIPMs and their efficacy.

**Activity 3 — Design and Development**

Prior to designing and developing the SDFIPM, all previous DFIPMs were critically reviewed and assessed against three sets of assessment criteria to establish which could contribute to the new model. Law enforcement, commerce and incident response were the three environments on which this research focussed. Therefore, previous models within those three domains were considered for their possible contributions to the new model. Once the most reliable models were identified, their specific key contributions were determined for inclusion in the new model. Following this, the essential components necessary for the new model were identified from the specific key contributions. These formed the basic structure of the SDFIPM. The prevailing models were then built upon by the construction of a new set of domain-specific components. The components identified in the previous models contributed to the development of the new models, as did the new set of constructed components. The SDFIPM was graphically represented in the form of UML Activity Diagrams.

Having already applied the first three components of the DSRP to our research (`Montasari`, 2016a), in this chapter we focus on the remaining three components, namely Demonstration, Evaluation and Communication activities. The following sections describe how we intend to apply these elements to this study.

**Activity 4 — Demonstration**

Following the design and development of the SDFIPM, its implementation needs to be demonstrated within an appropriate environment based on the Demonstration Activity of the DSRP. `Peffers` *et al*. (2006) suggest the use of case studies as such an appropriate environment. Other researchers in the field (`Valjarevic` and `Venter`, 2015; `Casey`, 2011; `Beebe` and `Clark`, 2005; `Carrier` and `Spafford`, 2003; `Ciardhuáin`, 2004) have also used case studies as an appropriate environment to determine the applicability of their models. A case study pertaining to the different fields of law enforcement, commerce and incident response has been selected as an appropriate environment for testing the proposed model. Therefore, the SDFIPM will be applied to the selected case study in [Section 9.5](#chapter9.xhtml#sec9.5) in order to demonstrate its application and effectiveness within the three stated domains. We considered this activity important as it would enable us to address any shortcomings prior to the submission of the model to the external, independent experts for formal evaluation. Such an activity would also enable us to avoid impinging on the time of the external reviewers. The results of the Demonstration activity of the research will then determine whether the Design and Development stage needs to be repeated in order for certain alterations to be made.

**Activity 5 — Evaluation**

Based on the DSRP’s requirements, the SDFIPM also needs to be evaluated to determine how well it supports the solution to the stated problem. This activity will compare the SDFIPM’s application and effectiveness with the characteristics set out in the research problem. The Evaluation activity of this study, presented in [Section 9.6](#chapter9.xhtml#sec9.6), has involved the submission of the SDFIPM to DFPs within the three domains that are the focus of this research, namely law enforcement, commerce and incident response, and also to judges, barristers and researchers in academia. The aim of such an approach was to enable us to acquire insightful and reliable feedback as to the effectiveness of the SDFIPM from authoritative external reviewers. As with the Demonstration phase, once the evaluation has been carried out, we will be able to judge whether to repeat the Design and Development Phase of the SDFIPM in order to make improvements. Any such amendments will subsequently be introduced to the Design and Development Phase of the SDFIPM.

**Activity 6 — Communication**

Again, in accordance with the DSRP’s requirements, the problem addressed by this research and its importance, its solution (the SDFIPM), its utility and novelty, the rigor of its design and implementation, and its effectiveness will all need to be communicated to the intended user community. The Communication activity of the DSRP in relation to this research has already been covered through publications in well-known and peer-reviewed journals and conferences, and also via our attendance at various conferences, Digital Forensic Expositions and Cyber Security Summits. In addition, we aim to continue with further publications and also to initiate direct interactions with a wide variety of experts, including DFPs, legal practitioners and experts in academia, to communicate the importance of our proposed model.
