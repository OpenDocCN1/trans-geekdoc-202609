## ABSTRACT

Ensuring continuity of evidence in any investigation is problematic, even more so when multiple agencies are involved. Ensuring there is consistency between paperwork concerning the seizure of artefacts is difficult, and can often be scrutinized in court. The development of this prototype blockchain is for the recording of the transfer of evidence during an investigation, with the aim of providing a guarantee for the continuity of evidence. The implementation of the blockchain is completed using Hyperledger and is referred to in this paper as Blockchain of Custody, or BoC. To test BoC two simple mock investigations are carried out and then evaluated. The evaluation includes querying the evidence, access control, development, security, and auditing. The implementation of BoC is considered a success and the future improvements, disadvantages and advantages are discussed.

*Keywords*: Bitcoin, HyperLedger, blockchain, chain of custody, timestamp, legal assurance

### 10.1.Introduction

Over recent years the hyperbole surrounding Decentralised Applications (DApps) and Blockchain has distracted researchers from their rationales by making them consider new developments. Is the technology pushing change, or is change pushing the technology? Here we have taken the opportunity to investigate how blockchain technology can be implemented for the seizure and transfer of evidence, or, put simply, how a blockchain of custody would work. This proposal considers the development, the implementation, the benefits, the issues and finally the impact on Forensic Science in general.

There are many issues with how a current chain of custody for the collection of evidence is completed. In particular, there is one issue about safeguarding from tampering (ISO, 2017 (Section 7.11)). Chain of custody automation software is almost standard for accredited labs and is recommended in ISO (2017, 2013). However, there is room for improvement when it comes to transferring evidence to third parties and ensuring that the chain of custody is immutable and irreversible. This is the main motivation for this paper, to investigate the blockchain software development lifecycle facilitating the chain of custody procedures.

Blockchain of Custody (BoC) is a decentralisation application that provides an immutable and irreversible record of the chain of custody for evidence collected after any incident. It does not intend to replace evidence management software that provides much of industry standard and structure to generate reports and manage staff and facilities, e.g., Foreman (Lowman, 2018). Instead BoC intends to work with this software and improve the capability, value and quality of evidential-related material regarding the chain of custody.
