
The appropriate number of experts should depend on (1) the scope of the research problem (see below) and (2) the available timing during which the research is expected to be completed (McBride and Burgman, 2012). However, the literature on expert selection strongly suggests the inclusion of as diverse a range of experts as possible (Armstrong, 2006; Clemen and Winkler, 1999) by recruiting multiple experts in order to reduce the impact of individual mistakes and biases and to pave the way for assessments that are representative of the entire expert community (Fisher, 2009; Hokstad `et al`., 1998). In most scientific environments, as Martin `et al`. (2012) and Ludwig `et al`. (2001) point out, the breadth of research problems denotes that no one individual will be expert for all aspects of the problem. Hence, we identified and recruited a diverse group of experts with different skill sets for the Evaluation process. Therefore, since the intended applicability of the SDFIPM was aimed at three domains of law enforcement, commerce and incident response, we recruited experts operating within the stated fields to evaluate the model. This approach enabled us to ensure that the SDFIPM would undergo ‘utility’ and ‘usability’ testing by experts in relation to how ‘useful’ and ‘relevant’ the model would be to each of the experts’ particular domains of practice. Therefore, to acquire the needed expert coverage, we recruited a diverse set of eminent experts who could provide expert review and feedback on the ‘utility’ and ‘usability’ aspects of the proposed model. We based our selection of experts upon the common metrics found in the literature such as those presented in Czembor and Vesk (2009), Drescher `et al`. (2008), and Whitfield `et al`. (2008). The following selection criteria were considered during the recruitment process:

•geographical operation — DFIs and legal practitioners needed to operate within the UK as the model’s intended application would be aimed at the UK jurisdiction.

•period of experience and learning — the participants needed to have extensive knowledge and experience in the domain of DF for a period of at least 10 years.

•the manner in which the experience had been acquired — for instance, the experts’ experience needed to have been attained through practice and theory to ensure that the expert panel would consist of both theorists and practitioners.

•qualifications — the participants needed to have appropriate qualifications in relation to DF, information security (IS) or computer science (CS).

•reputation — the experts needed to have a solid standing in their particular domains of practice.

Therefore, we deemed the use of the following diverse set of experts to be beneficial in increasing what McBride and Burgman (2012) term as “the acceptance or perceived validity of the elicitation process”. This would ensure that different perspectives would be brought into the Evaluation Process. Experts (expert panel) who took part in the evaluation process were then categorized into three groups of (1) DFPs, (2) legal practitioners, and (3) researchers both in academia and industry. Experts from the panel were identified as representatives of their specific domains of practice based on their roles, responsibilities, experience and involvement within pertinent associations in the UK jurisdiction. The recruitment process for the expert panel was considered complete once numerous participants from each of the three fields covered by the research scope had consented to assist. The following are the profiles of the experts constituting the members of the three groups of the expert panel:

•A representative of law enforcement (MSc in Computing & Information Technology and EnCase Certified Examiner) with 25 years of experience as a police officer who manages the entire High-Tech/Cyber Crime team of the North West Regional Organised Crime Unit (TITAN), consisting of six police forces in Cumbria, Cheshire, Lancashire, Greater Manchester, Merseyside and North Wales;

•A representative of commercial practice with 18 years of experience as a Digital Forensic Investigator and a Forensic Triage Specialist who is the director of Digital Forensics for a large company;

•A representative of legal practice (LL.B) with 13 years of experience who is a self-employed barrister and who has worked on over 20 cases involving digital investigations;

•A Detective Sergeant of eForensics (BSc in Computing and Information Technology) who is a certified ENCE, a certified Security Incident Specialist, and a certified Forensic Investigation Analyst with 28 years of experience as a police officer who heads the Cheshire Police Headquarters (Cheshire Constabulary) High-Tech Crime Unit;

•A representative of commercial practice (MPhil in Operating System Anti-Malware/Security and an MSc in Internet Engineering);

•A Detective Constable of Digital Forensics at the South Wales Gwent Police High-Tech Crime Unit representative of law enforcement with 18 years of experience as a DFP;

•A Detective Constable of Digital Forensics at the South Wales Gwent Police High-Tech Crime Unit with 11 years of experience as a DFP;

•A representative of academia (PhD) who is a university professor and a prominent researcher with many publications on information security, biometrics and digital forensics. He has been the keynote speaker at reputable information security and digital forensics conferences;
