
According to Kapersky, as computers evolved from floppy-disk bootable machines into more sophisticated workstation devices such as laptops and smartphones, malicious software technology or `malware` as it is most commonly known evolved along with them (Kaspersky, 2018a). Kaspersky also suggests that malware was existent from information technology’s infancy but that the use of malware only bloomed when personal computers began to be accessible to everyone. The first malware was called the `“Elk Cloner Virus”` (Tasril *et al*., 2017). Examining the features of the Elk Cloner Virus, we see that this malicious technology, which once used simple motifs and techniques, has become more sophisticated and now does more serious damage to computer devices. The Elk Cloner Virus was formed as a “prank” and did not do much harm. Its self-spreading feature relied on social engineering techniques such as floppy-disk insertions (Touchette, 2016). Cyber criminals built upon these foundations until they formed the cyber crime industry as it exists today.

Cyber crime, which has cost industries approximately 100 billion USD, is classified according to the types of malware that the industry produces. Research done by Kapersky has identified the types of malware in a diagram in [Fig. 5.1](#chapter5.xhtml#fig5.1) called the “Malware Classification Tree” (Kaspersky, 2018b).

According to the same report, the classified malwares are considered “specific function” malwares, which means that these malwares are only capable of designated damage and do not produce a complex effect on devices. In contrast, cyber criminals have devised multi-dimensional and multi-functioning malwares. 65.32% of these are `botnets` which affect devices worldwide, and 34.68% of these are a combination of trojans and worms as shown in [Fig. 5.2](#chapter5.xhtml#fig5.2) (Soni, 2018).

Cisco has classified these versatile malwares as “Advanced Malwares” and has identified the most prominent advanced malwares as the following: riskware, spyware, adware and ransomware with `ransomware` being the deadliest due to its capabilities (Cisco, 2018). Esset defines riskware as a type of malware which increases security risk by using encryption methods to identify user procedures and diverting the procedures into digital transactions (e.g. using a credit card) without the user’s permission (Esset, 2018). Spywares are types of malware which, as can be deduced from their name, collect data about a device, encrypt the information and send the data back to the origin of the malware. These data are used illegally to blackmail the people from whom the data is collected (Stavrou *et al*., 2010). Avast, an anti-virus company, adds that spywares are usually accompanied by adwares, which bombard the user with inappropriate and unwanted pop-ups or advertisements which are harmful to the device (Avast, 2018). Finally, when these features are combined and tailored for a bigger purpose, `ransomware` is produced.

An observation can be made that most of the advanced malwares were enhanced by the development of encryption and of new ways to cipher text. The field of `cryptovirology` was developed when researchers realised how these cryptographic techniques could be used to develop malware (Young, 2017).

### 5.2.Ransomware and How It Impacts Different Platforms

Ransomware has affected several companies worldwide by making it necessary for them to pay a large amount of money to their attackers without actually gaining anything beneficial. For instance, Arran Brewery, a brewing company in Scotland, recently paid 2 Bitcoins worth 9,600 GBP to a group of anonymous hackers just to restore the company’s system after all the computers of the company had been locked down (BBC, 2018). A similar incident also happened to the United States government when the Pennsylvania Senate Democratic Caucus had to pay Microsoft to reconstruct the senate’s IT Framework after it was breached on March 3, 2017 (Cimpanu, 2019). The same report states that hackers were demanding 28 Bitcoins as payment, which comes up to 30,000 USD, but instead the government decided to reconstruct for the hefty price of 700,000 USD in order to discourage future attacks to government offices. As these incidents start to increase, attacks are reportedly getting worse.

**Ransomware** is a type of advanced malware where the victim is held at ransom for infected files or documents. The files will not be made available to the victim unless payment is given to the attacker, at which point the attacker will remove the malware from the system (Young, 2017). Ransomware is believed to be one of the most effective malwares for the year 2017, with a total amount of more than 12 million malware counts. The number of malware counts increased by 36% in the last quarter of 2017 (McAfee, 2017). The fact that ransomware accounted for 5 billion US dollars lost globally in the year 2017 led Europol to conclude that ransomware was the most dangerous malware threat, and to say so at their conference entitled “Internet Organised Crime Threat Assessment (IOCTA) 2018” (Walker, 2018).

As ransomware has turned into a booming business, ransomware authors have devised two types of ransomwares: `locker ransomware` and `crypto ransomware` (Deloitte, 2016).

**Locker ransomwares** are the types of ransomwares which do less damage since these ransomwares do not encrypt the files of the victim. Locker ransomware is used to terrorise the victim by prompting the victim to pay for a fine charged for misusing the internet (i.e. watching pornography or downloading illegal programs from torrents). These ransomwares usually come with an interface credible enough for the victim to believe that the warrant is legitimate. Attackers may include the targeted user’s sensitive data, such as their IP address and their last viewed malicious website, to make the warrant more convincing (Deloitte, 2016).

**Crypto ransomwares (crypto-locker ransomwares)** are the types of ransomwares which do more damage to the device and to the financial stability of the victim. Crypto-lockers are a type of ransomware which infects a device and `encrypts` the victim’s files until the ransom has been paid. Some of these crypto-lockers even delete the back-up from the system, making it harder to recover the victim’s files (Richet, 2016).
