
# Future Type of Ransomware Attacks

Future ransomware attacks may come in new forms. For instance, Mansfield-Devine (2016) suggests that ransomware distributed through mobile devices and the cloud will become increasingly common. This can be quite a concern since a user can upload an infected file to the cloud and unknowingly disperse it to the rest of the community (as users might assume it’s a legitimate ‘safe’ service to download). Also, more and more enterprises are seeking cloud- and SaaS-based models, believing them to be safer than on-premise models, even if this may not be the case. This is why a more thorough analysis of cloud security needs to be carried out. Educating humans not to click on unauthorised links or to be vigilant in checking the source of a link before clicking on it will be an ongoing process, and it still will not solve the problem of how to control data access and storage, or, in the event of a cyber breach, how to give the malicious attackers more hurdles to overcome by adding layers of security such as blockchain and encryption. Also, with blockchain there has been further research on using artificial intelligence (AI) to detect malicious outsider attacks and the patterns associated with them. This adds further levels of security so that the blockchain stays protected.

### 6.4.1.*Cyber breach analysis*

Cyber-attack vectors can be categorised in the following way, which helps us define the type of breach and the source of the breach (breachlevelindex.com, 2018):

**Type of Breach:**

•Identity Theft

•Nuisance

•Account Access

•Existential Data

•Financial Access

•Financial Loss

**Source of Breach:**

•Accidental Loss

•Malicious Insider

•Malicious Outsider

•Hacktivist/State Espionage

From the Breachlevelindex analysis shown in [Figs. 6.8](#chapter6.xhtml#fig6.8) and [6.9](#chapter6.xhtml#fig6.9), two factors stand out, that is, that the greatest number of records are lost due to accidental loss and that malicious outsiders committing identity theft have the next biggest impact. From these trends it seems that better training should be given to help mitigate the accidental loss and reduce the number of records lost. Staff members should also be vigilant to prevent malicious outsiders from committing identity theft. The Breachlevelindex analysis also discovered that there were fewer data breaches in which encryption was used.

### 6.4.2.*Deep web impacts to the client*

It is extremely unpleasant for a victim to discover that criminals have access to their identities and can use them to commit crimes because the organisation they have shared their data with has been breached by a malicious attacker. At least with GDPR in place, the authorities must be notified of data breaches within 72 hours or a penalty will be imposed. The consequences are worse when identity theft has occurred. A stolen credit card can be cancelled if the theft is discovered in time by the victim, but one’s identity cannot be changed. It is possible to steal someone’s identity with the aid of medical records accessed through an attack on a healthcare organisation. Medical records hold valuable data points that map a person’s identity. If the data breach goes unannounced or is announced too late, the attacker might have enough time to plan how best to use the stolen data.

The large-scale WannaCry ransomware attack (BBC news, 2017) highlights the dangers of a large-scale data breach. In one of these, no one really knows how far the identity theft will go. On the deep web, the value of medical data is said to be ten times that of stolen credit card numbers (Sulleyman, 2017) and if big data sets are available they can go for large sums of money. As a result, it can be very lucrative to attack the healthcare industry. There likely is a much larger adverse effect on those that suffered from identity theft and criminal victimisation because of the data breach, as identity theft is a deep violation of the patient’s privacy. The loss of data surrounding one’s identity is of course a problem, but so too can be having one’s sensitive medical history (e.g. HIV information) made public. Therefore the impact is far deeper and greater on the victim than a loss of credit card details would have been.

### 6.5.Is Blockchain the Answer Industry is Looking for Regarding Securing Data?

Many worldwide have considered how blockchain may fit into industry environments and into a number of different use cases. It is certain that a new technology such as blockchain has both risks and benefits, but more individuals and organisations are adopting and accepting the use of blockchain. This is probably because this technology provides a much better service than is otherwise available to its users and the organisations using it, whilst also offering higher levels of security. When we look at various industry sectors, it is possible to quickly understand these drivers, and also how blockchain facilitates securing data privacy.

\-**Healthcare:** The healthcare industry suffers the most cyber attacks, and this is reason enough to find a way to better protect the core data. It is a well-known fact that healthcare data is worth 10 times the value of financial credit card data when offered by criminals on the dark net. In 2018, millions of records were breached, and consequently, more people have become aware of the impact of identity theft. Also, although medical records are now kept in electronic rather than in paper form, the main issue is that currently records are still mostly stored in silo environments. This causes major inefficiencies for providers and patients, and makes it difficult for a seamless healthcare data interchange to occur. If blockchain is applied, longitudinal medical histories can be generated from the data that used to be in various silos. Blockchain also puts the patient in control of their data, and also allows them to control who has access to it. Blockchain can also be used to store other types of sensitive data that are increasing in volume e.g. WBAN (wireless body area network) data that collects medical data through sensors and automatically syncs with storage systems. Since these storage systems contain sensitive data they are again of attraction to cyber attackers and so a great deal of attention needs to be paid to security.
