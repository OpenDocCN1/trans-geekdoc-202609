
Autists may be unable to process verbal or non-verbal language such as tone of voice, gestures and facial expressions. They can take as literal anything that is said to them, as some do not understand sarcasm or jokes. This processing of verbal and non-verbal language is a skill that most of us learn as we interact with each other and we can pick up on the different nuances that escape autists.

Some autists have problems with the spoken language but can express themselves very clearly in other ways, for example by using sign language or visual symbology.

The case study relating to JC shows that it is possible to implement communication strategies to enhance and support communication for the autistic person, the employer and their clients. Each individual is different so it is important that time is taken to identify the most appropriate strategy for that person.

### 12.3.2.2.*Social interaction*

Autists can find it difficult to form friendships and often have trouble reading others, that is, recognising feelings, emotions and intentions.

### 12.3.2.3.*Honesty*

The often uncompromising truthfulness of autistic people could be a barrier to developing equitable social relationships.

Internally this situation can be supported by providing coaching and training for all employees although a strategy should be developed to mitigate any issues that may arise with client organisations who are not aware of the relevant circumstances.

### 12.3.2.4.*Routines and repetitive behaviour*

Autists can view the world as a confusing and very unpredictable place. To manage this some may adopt routines such as always arranging belongings in a particular way, or adopting rules to ensure they follow the ‘correct’ way to do things, particularly if they have been introduced to something new. As change can be an issue, repetitive behaviour can be a coping mechanism.

This repetitive behaviour could have advantages in a cyber security role that requires continuous monitoring or tool calibration.

### 12.3.2.5.*Focussed interests*

People on the Spectrum can develop focussed and often intense interests across a wide range of topics — anything from art, computers, history, politics to zoology. Some of these interests will be of interest to potential employers but the narrow focus could prove to be an issue.

Some autists have reported that these activities are fundamental to who they are and to their holistic well-being.

Managing focussed interests carefully could mean a beneficial increase in throughput or production but care will need to be taken to ensure there is no detrimental effect on the autistic employee.

### 12.3.2.6.*Sensory perception*

Autistic people can have a heightened or lowered perception associated with their senses (vision, hearing, smell, taste, touch), temperature and pain. Responses to these stimuli could be anxiety, panic attacks or hyper-focus.

Obviously, any extreme responses will be upsetting to all involved but simple measures may help to control external stimuli.

Heightened sensory perception could manifest as special isolated skills or perception peaks, which in turn may provide advantages in cyber security roles.

### 12.3.3.*Questionnaire response analysis*

### 12.3.3.1.*National Autistic Society questionnaire*

The NAS questionnaire identified autistic traits and strengths that could be useful in a wide range of roles including cyber security. NAS identified a wide range of roles, highlighting industries from the arts, libraries, administration, property management, information technology and horticulture.

Support strategies were identified and these strategies form part of the proposed support strategy proffered here.

From a law and ethics perspective the NAS discussed existing laws and policy requirements. Ethics related more to research ethics when clinical and social researchers were conducting autism studies.

The questionnaire identified a number of employer organisations that had worked with NAS in encouraging and assessing potential in cyber security work. These organisations include GCHQ, NCSC, BAe, NCC Group, Thales, Northrop Grumman, QinetiQ and UK government departments. This list informed which organisations received the Employer Questionnaire.

The National Autistic Society is a charitable institution that works specifically towards supporting autistic people. As such, there may be a natural bias in some of the responses received. As it is a single organisation, their views may not be wholly representative in the autistic environment.

### 12.3.3.2.*Employer questionnaire*

Eight employer organisations, from a total of ten, responded to the questionnaire; as the majority of them requested that available opportunities are not linked to their organisation and as some of the roles are associated with providing security-cleared services it was necessary to complete non-disclosure agreements to access the information. A decision has been made to retain confidentiality for all responding organisations.

The number of employer organisations which participated is small and may not be representative of the wider employment opportunities available. However, all those approached work in the cyber security domain and had previously expressed an interest in employing autistic people. The results could be improved by approaching more organisations for responses.
