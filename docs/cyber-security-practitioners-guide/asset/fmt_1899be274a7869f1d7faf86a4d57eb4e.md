
•Peer-to-peer virtual sharing: This is when a user shares files electronically by using BitTorrent, uTorrent etc. This does not only refer to file sharing between familiar peers but also refers to the `illegal` download of applications via anonymous uploads.

•Removable media: This is the most common method of malware acquisition. Malware is transferred to the victim via electronic devices such as USBs, CDs, External HDDs, etc.

•Network acquisition: This happens when a device connected to the internet comes across a website which has malicious cookies or pop-ups and the victim falls into the trap by exploring the links integrated into these digital platforms.

•General lack of protection: This happens when a device is not kept secure by software which offers protection from malwares.

**Attack Vectors of Ransomware**

Although there are plenty of ways to be infected by malwares, researchers have identified that `fortunately`, there are only a few ways of acquiring ransomware. These vectors could be divided into those which are acquired through virtual means and those which are acquired through physical means.

*Virtual Means of Ransomware Acquisition*:

a)**Emails** are the main source of ransomware attacks. Statistics show that there are approximately 354.5 billion emails delivered daily worldwide and 85.35 percent of these emails are spam, which means only 14.64 percent of emails are legitimate (Cisco Talos, 2018). IBM estimates that there was a 350 percent increase in spam mails from January 2015 to December 2016 and, alarmingly, despite the fact that not all spam mails contain malwares, the total malware content in spam mails by percentage is increasing. Malware-containing spam mails made up approximately 10 percent of 100 percent of spam mails sent in January 2015. By 2016, spam mails had increased by 450 percent, and malware-containing spam mails made up 210 percent of the 450 percent (IBM, 2016) as shown in [Fig. 5.3](#chapter5.xhtml#fig5.3).

Symantec suggests that these malwares are spreading largely due to a botnet named Necrus which was established just to send out spam mails worldwide (O'Brien, 2017). Symantec also states that ransomware, if acquired through email, can be activated through one of the following steps (O'Brien, 2017):

•Malicious attachments being opened

•Malicious attachments initiating third party JavaScript or PowerShell commands

•Malicious attachments initiating the download of `exploit kits`

b)**Exploit Kits** literally exploit the vulnerabilities of a computer. Exploit kits are used to take advantage of a particular device’s weaknesses via web browsers, programs or operating systems. Most exploit kits are integrated into websites, and once a user visits that particular site, the kit is activated in the user’s device. The kit will then scan for vulnerabilities in the device used to navigate the website and, subsequently, it will implement the malware into the device in question. The infected website is not necessarily a malicious website since legitimate websites like BBC or Yahoo could be infested through advertisements as well (Monrose *et al*., 2015). According to a study done by Malwarebytes, as shown in [Fig. 5.4](#chapter5.xhtml#fig5.4), 61 percent of all exploit kits contain exploit payloads for ransomware (Malwarebytes Labs, 2016).

*Physical Means of Ransomware Acquisition*

**Social Engineering** is used as a sure but risky way to spread malware. Social Engineering is the manipulation of human psychology in order to persuade a person to perform a behaviour which may have a devastating effect on that person, another person or a group of individuals. Researchers may refer to it as “hacking” a human for an intended purpose (Mann, 2008).

*Kevin Mitnick’s Proposed Social Engineering Methodology* (Gallegos-Segovia, 2017):

•Information Gathering: This is the step where details of the victim are gathered by certain individuals.

•Trust: This is the step where the attacker establishes a deeper connection with the victim. By doing so, an appropriate attack vector could be identified by the attacker.

•Exploitation: This is where the attacker executes the plan of exploitation and the ransomware infects the device.

•Reach: This is where the system is finally compromised. Usually the attacker is not in contact with the victim at this point.

The *December 2015 Ukraine Power Grid Cyber Attack* is an example of how these methods have been used. Numerous reports suggest that the incident was a well-planned attack on Ukraine’s power company named Ukrenergo (BBC, 2017). Most investigators were amazed by how well the information and trust gathering was executed and further blamed the Russians for devising a way into the Ukraine’s SCADA system then manipulating the system through an infection caused by a USB (Zetter, 2016).

These proposed methods of social engineering can be further used to identify the different types of attacks that can be established. These are as follows (Gallegos-Segovia, 2017):

•Ego Attacks: The attacker tries to create a personal connection with the victim by offering help and proving the reliability of the attacker’s intelligence to the victim. Once the victim falls for a transparent manipulation, the attacker executes the ransomware on the system.

•Sympathy Attacks: The attacker uses emotional means to create a connection with the victim. When the victim starts trusting the attacker, the attacker executes the ransomware on the system.

•Bullying Attacks: The attacker uses force, dominance or black-mailing to gather information. This is the most common and most effective type of social engineering attack.

2.***Second Phase: Consignment and Implementation***

The next step is the process of installing the malware on the system, encrypting the ransomware program inside the device and further establishing a secure platform for the **upcoming** command and control network.

The ransomware uses a simple RSA asymmetric public key (Emsisoft, 2018) that will call a `GET` or `POST` request at some point (Securonix, 2018). The `GET` or `POST` request would consist of the following parameters to be collected (Emsisoft, 2018; Securonix, 2018):

•computer name

•processor information

•volume serial

•operating system

•active drives

•Public IP in a pattern of: `{ ipv4bot[.]whatismyipaddress[.] com}`

•disk space

Researchers (Emsisoft, 2018) have identified the reasons why ransomware developers use RSA public keys in coding ransomwares and these are the following:

•They take up lesser space in memory and can be easily transported.

•Third parties or malware analysts surveying the network would only see plain texts and scrambled encrypted network traffic.

•This gives the malware authors a chance to use their own server during the communication with the deployed ransomware.

After identifying the victim through their unique hashed identity (using the MD5 Algorithm), the ransomware would identify whether the process was running via `root` or `admin` access (Rajpal and Kotov, 2014).
