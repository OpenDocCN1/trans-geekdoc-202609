
Based upon Peffers `et al`.’s DSRP (2006), the acquisition of two resources was sought in order to define the “objectives of the solution”. These included: “… knowledge of the state of the problem” and “current solutions and their efficacy, if any”. The knowledge of the state of the problem for this research was covered in the Introduction section, where the research problem was defined and the value of its solution justified. However, to address the two resources fully, further knowledge of the state of the problem was required and current solutions, if any, had to be identified. In order to acquire the two stated resources, previous models were critically reviewed and assessed against three different sets of assessment criteria in Montasari’s (2016a) research study, with the summary of this analysis being presented in this chapter. Through this assessment, the prevailing models that could contribute to the design and development of the proposed model were identified, and the necessary components that would constitute the basic structure of the new model were determined. Following the Design and Development activity of Peffers `et al`.’s DSRP (2006), the proposed model was designed and implemented based upon the information acquired through the critical analysis.

Once designed and developed, in accordance with the Demonstration activity of Peffers `et al`.’s DSRP (2006), we applied the model to a case study, thereby demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed model to solve the stated research problem. We then covered the Evaluation activity of Peffers `et al`.’s DSRP (2006), through which the model was subjected to a critical review and feedback process by the leading experts in its intended user community to determine how well the new model would support the solution to the stated research problem. To follow up on the feedback obtained from the experts, we returned to the Design and Development stage to make subsequent amendments, including new additions, alterations and deletions as shown in [Figs. 9.4](#chapter9.xhtml#fig9.4) and [9.5](#chapter9.xhtml#fig9.5) in their amended form and in Appendix C in its final version. This was to address the two components of ‘utility’ and ‘usability’ further by finding the answers to the two following questions: ‘has the model been built right?’ and ‘has the right model been built?’. All the acquired feedback was very positive, and there were no negative comments. When there was disagreement, we iterated back to the Design and Development Phase of the research, as stated, to make alterations based on the feedback in order to bring the model as close as possible (within the scope of the research) to its intended application. Having completed the formal evaluation of the model, we contend that ‘the new model has been built right’ and that ‘the right model has been built’ on the basis that its intended application has been observed, accepted and confirmed by the prominent experts in its intended user community. Therefore, the SDFIPM is complete, consistent, and accurate enough to fulfil the solution to the stated research problem, as defined in [Section 9.1](#chapter9.xhtml#sec9.1).

### 9.7.2.*Research contribution*

This research has made a significant and novel contribution to the field of DF science by implementing a process model, the SDFIPM, that would assist DFPs in following a uniform approach. Our study has synthesized, harmonised and significantly built upon the existing models to produce the first process model that has all three characteristics of being comprehensive, formal and generic. No such model previously existed in the literature. The SDFIPM is scientifically rigorous as it assists courts in determining the reliability of the investigative process followed and generic in that it can be applied by DFPs operating in the three fields of law enforcement, commerce and incident response. The proposed model is inclusive of all the advantages of the previous models through their synthesis and harmonisation and contains purified terminology using the terms commonly agreed upon by DFPs. It is therefore argued that the SDFIPM would be a significant step towards the establishment of standards for the digital forensic investigative process. Every component in the model is well defined in terms of scope, functions and order. The model fully and thoroughly describes the flow of information in a digital investigation from the moment digital evidence is captured for examination until the investigation reaches its formal conclusion.

This study has also demonstrated how the digital investigative process can be represented through a proven formal notation, the Unified Modelling Language (UML). The representation of the SDFIPM via the UML Activity Diagrams will assist courts in properly understanding the digital investigative processes followed, a point acknowledged by the legal practitioners and judge who participated in the external evaluation of the model. Due to its level of detail and its step-by-step approach, the SDFIPM provides investigators with concise direction on the processes to be followed, enabling them to speed up the investigative process while minimising errors and negligence. Therefore, in the event of a challenge to the digital investigative process, there would be no doubt concerning the soundness of the processes followed. The proposed model also contains generic methods that DFPs can use to associate technology with non-technical observers. Importantly, the application of the SDFIPM could bring about the improved admissibility of digital evidence in courts, being a formal model with strong overriding principles resulting from rigorous scientific verification.
