
In the first instance, the experts participating in the Evaluation process were requested to assist in this research via email. Once the experts were identified and their confirmation for participation in the Evaluation process was obtained, then in line with the best practices outlined in the literature (Morgan and Henrion, 2010; Rothlisberger `et al`., 2010; Cooke and Goossens, 2000), they were provided with relevant materials associated with the model that had been compiled into an appropriate, accessible form. These consisted of the UML Activity Diagrams of the model, the explanatory notes describing the model’s components, a questionnaire (see Appendix B), background information such as the aim and objectives of the research, and also a description of what they were requested to undertake. These materials had already been carefully piloted to ensure that the documentation had been compiled properly. It was important to ensure that the Evaluation questions had been formulated appropriately to reflect the research problem, identify potential issues with biases or question phrasing, and elicit feedback about any potential ways to enhance the quality of the process and of the knowledge that was to be extracted. The pilot process was conducted with academic colleagues who checked the compiled documents prior to their submissions to the experts for review. Once piloted and compiled, the documents were sent to the participating experts via email so that they could have sufficient time to evaluate them, raise any potential concerns, comment on any aspect of the model, and volunteer relevant information. Having acquired the experts’ feedback via e-mails, formal meetings were then held at the experts’ workplaces at an arranged date and time to discuss their feedback with them and to elicit further expert knowledge that it was not possible to obtain via e-mails. These meetings took place following focus group and interview formats as such approaches are recommended in the literature based on their advantages as follows:

• The interview and focus group formats take full advantage of the experience and skills of those participating by being more open than a narrowly-focussed survey.

• Experts have a stronger understanding of the subject matter as they can ask questions concerning the research rather than simply replying to a fixed set of questions.

• Participants are able to express and discuss views that are not likely to be identified by a simple survey. Such issues can be analysed in some detail during the interview or focus group.

• Experts are left free to express their views in whatever manner they feel is most appropriate.

Once all the data was collected, the participants’ responses were then subjectively analysed through a rigorous verification process and acted upon where necessary. In line with McBride and Burgman’s (2012) and Cooke’s (1991) suggestions, we checked for obvious errors or inconsistencies by comparing the expert’s responses to those of others in the panel. In comparing an individual expert’s responses with those of the rest of the panel, we looked for similarities, irregularities, biases, or strongly conflicting opinions in addition to varying interpretations of the information. When such irregularities or problematic responses were identified, we followed up on the conflicting feedback through further discussions with the experts. We then acted upon the acquired feedback which involved ‘addition to’, ‘alteration to’ and ‘deletion from’ the model. ‘Addition’ refers to a new component being added to the model, ‘alteration’ refers to the change in a component’s naming or order and ‘deletion’ refers to the deletion of a component from the model. The aforementioned three terminologies that have been acted upon are represented on the model’s UML Activity Diagrams using the following references:

1. Reference to Addition: ‘REF: ADD #n’, where ‘n’ represents the number of implemented ‘additions’.

2. Reference to Alteration: ‘REF: ALT #n’, where ‘n’ represents the number of implemented ‘changes’.

3. Reference to Deletion: ‘REF: DEL #n’, where ‘n’ represents the number of implemented ‘deletions’.
