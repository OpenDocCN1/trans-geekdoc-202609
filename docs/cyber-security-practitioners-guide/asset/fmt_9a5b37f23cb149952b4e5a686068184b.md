
Due to the plethora of approaches there is no standard implementation, meaning staff with experience in one organization cannot transfer their knowledge procedures to another company, as the systems, methods and procedures in addressing the security of sensitive data will differ. This poses real challenges for companies that rely on contract staff.

# 1.11.The Future — STIX, TAXII and Cyber Threat Intelligence

To aid the sharing of the security information protocols that have been developed, the computer language known as Structured Threat Information Expression (STIX, 2018) and the protocol known as Trusted Automated Exchange of Intelligence Information (TAXII, 2018) are designed to share CTI (Cyber Threat Intelligence) information. One would expect that a good firewall with DLP would interface with external live CTI databases and that it would automatically update itself using these databases.

Data Classification system vendors need to integrate their systems with gateway firewalls and web proxies to protect against data leakage. With so many solutions available it is important that products adopt the ICAP (ICAP, 2018) standard for transferring information.

# 1.12.Conclusion on How to Guard Against Attacks and Prevent Data Theft

Although the above technical exploits are perhaps emerging threats, an analysis of high profile historic exploits has shown that, generally, less sophisticated methods have caused many of the greatest leaks. It is also apparent that security standards like PCI DSS have yet to address some of the emerging methods that can be used for data exfiltration. This will no doubt happen as the standards continue to evolve. In the breaches reviewed, the auditing of systems has often not been as robust as it should have been, as is seen in the cases where patches and SQL injection were the causes of leaks. Other breaches have involved deception from insiders. Since 2017, however, it is apparent that attacks are getting more sophisticated. Based on the attack vectors discussed, security auditing will need to become more robust, standards will need to evolve faster, and the assessment of emerging risks will need to be a regular item on a corporate board’s agenda.

Packet inspection creates a huge overhead on a firewall’s performance; however, if firewalls are going to be fit for purpose they will need to do more. With so-called next-generation firewalls, manufacturers are rising to the challenge. This is perhaps evident in the recent fast-rising costs of the latest next-generation firewalls, which have increasingly faster processors. Firewalls have sophisticated features that take time to develop and, due to the long testing processes required, this unfortunately means that the detection methods deployed are not necessarily aligned with the detection of emerging threats.

Companies need to assess the risks of storing data on mobiles and desktops. This data can be exfiltrated using apps that may be able to read data on the devices. Security standards will need to become much more adaptive to changes in the usage of technology. Firms that claim compliance should be required to regularly requalify. Banks and other payment providers have required retailers to reassess their PCI DSS compliance on an annual basis, and companies that do not comply have fines applied to their credit card accounts which are automatically paid from a retailer’s bank account. The fines are relatively small but are automatically applied as a way of making sure firms address their need to be compliant. The UK’s Cyber Essentials scheme may need to follow similar strategies.

This reflects how quickly the use of technology changes and how slowly several security standards have evolved to deal with emerging threats. It is notable that the PCI DSS has reacted quickly to change with annual updates, unlike the ISO27001 standard that has only had 3 revisions since 2013. As stated previously, it is surprising that ISO27001:2013 does not mention data loss prevention other than the physical measures taken to prevent data loss which are detailed in Section A.11.2. However, one could argue that ISO27001, COBIT 5 and NIST embrace the need for rapid change in their concept of risk, and that they emphasise an organisation’s responsibility to assess risks, mitigate risks, and remove risks. ISO27001 Section A.12.6.1 states *“Information about technical vulnerabilities of information systems being used shall be obtained in a timely fashion, the organization’s exposure to such vulnerabilities evaluated and appropriate measures taken to address the associated risk.”* ISO 27005:2011 12.1 highlights the responsibility of an organisation to re-assess risks based on *“new threats that could be active both outside and inside the organisation and that have not been assessed.”* Risk managers must therefore research and assess the risks from emerging threats. These threats may not only apply to a company’s systems but may also have adverse effects on the rights and freedom of others.

Sadly, security standards have often failed to remove the threat from insiders. It is important that employees are vetted and understand the motivations for the implementation of systems that monitor their behavior. IT departments must work closely with management and HR departments to not only monitor staff but also to develop a positive culture in an organisation whereby information security is the concern of every employee. Culture, and the ability of companies to implement new policies and get staff to grasp the importance of adhering to them, are essential to improving security and preventing data loss.

Web exfiltration is by far the greatest threat. DNS exfiltration is possible but can be dealt with by not allowing a workstation to resolve external DNS URLs. NTP and ICMP are not required outbound from a workstation so these ports do not need to be enabled though a firewall from a workstation.
