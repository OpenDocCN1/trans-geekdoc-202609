
Part of the objective in developing the system included contrasting different methods to garner feedback on the artefact design. Several distinct methods such as online surveys, one-to-one interviews and focus group interviews were critically assessed before semi-structured focus group interviews were adopted for the research. Focus group interviews are widely used in many disciplines, including the social sciences, to gather knowledge and opinions of a particular phenomenon. Hevner *et al*. (2014) demonstrated the use of a focus group for gathering feedback and the refinement of the artifact.

Although some computer-assisted qualitative data analysis software (CAQDAS) is available to help manage the data collected, due to the learning curve and the researchers’ lack of familiarity with any of the software mentioned, MS Word and Excel were used to process transcripts from the focus group. Although transcribing and coding the data using this method required more time and effort, with the requisite skills and persistence it was accomplished satisfactorily.

### 8.6.1.*Research limitations*

The research is limited to computer security incident response and data breach notification, but there are other important considerations when securing a system, including environmental and physical security. However, this is beyond the remit of this research. During the interview sessions, participants provided personal knowledge acquired from many years of experience. However, they could perceive themselves as representing their respective organisations and may be reluctant to provide information that might portray weakness or failures in their respective organisations.

The recording and transcription of the focus group interviews was limited to a single researcher. Whilst the participants were culturally diverse and heterogeneous in experience and the industry sector, the sample feedback could be considered relatively little in comparison to the views of experts in various and wider industries. Moreover, another limitation of the feedback on DSS is that it did not include the implementation or period of maturation within an organisation. For the purpose of this research only a single session of focus group interviews was carried out. However, the research provides a firm basis for the design to be extended in further research.

### 8.7.Conclusion and Recommendation

The numbers of reported data breach incidents have been increasing over the last few years and this highlights the need for privacy legislation to be strengthened in accordance with changes in technology and the new ways in which data is created, shared and processed. The research started out by posing the following questions.

1. What are the main challenges in interpreting and integrating the GDPR into incident response and breach notification plans?

2. How can the development of a decision support process or system address the challenges identified?

3. What method can be employed to provide feedback on the design and usability of the formulated solution?

To address these questions, the literature review was used to identify existing challenges and to help to inform the design of the Decision Support System using Design Science and UML notation. Focus group interviews were adopted to provide feedback on the usability of the system for meeting GDPR compliance requirements. The feedback from these interviews and the literature showed that a comprehensive response strategy is crucial for managing complex threats as well as the risks associated with third party entities. The research findings also highlight the importance of establishing data privacy by design and default and the potential consequences of failing to notify the relevant supervisory authority and data subjects in the aftermath of a data breach.

The findings further showed that the data breach scenarios and change restriction measures within the DSS were useful for situational analysis and mitigating security risks to personal data. Overall feedback on the usability of DSS indicated it that it was relevant for addressing incident response and breach notification challenges associated with the GDPR. For example, the findings show that the mappings of relevant articles could help facilitate the interpretation and integration of the GDPR into incident response programs. The design of the DSS builds on previous research by assessing the impact of data breaches on data subjects and by incorporating new requirements in Articles 33 and 34 of the GDPR.

The design of the system also provides guidance that can be used by organisations to facilitate incident response decisions and protect personal data. Moreover, further rigor is provided by adopting a hybrid assessment for the development of the DSS by using principles in Design Science and feedback from focus group interview sessions.

### 8.7.1.*Recommendations for future work*

While conducting the research, several noteworthy discoveries were made for viable future work on and improvement of the DSS design. Part of the findings revealed that no formal auditing of security policies or agreement for immediate notice of data breach was established between some organisations and their associated thirdparty partners. Therefore further research is needed to develop an incident notification framework between data controllers or processors based inside of the EU jurisdiction. The recommendations drawn from the findings also suggested that the possible next stage improvements of the DSS design could generate codes from each of the UML process flows to develop a GDPR software system as a plugin for integration with other systems.
