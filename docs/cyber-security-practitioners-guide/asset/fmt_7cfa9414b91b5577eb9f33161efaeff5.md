
3.`SimpleLocker`: a ransomware effective on the Android platform which gathers the IMEI, the model number and the manufacturer details for the command and control centre. Encryption is done via the AES encryption method and the ransomware also accesses the camera to take a picture of the victim. This picture is used to further threaten the victim into paying the ransom (NJCCIC, 2016).

4.`WannaCry`: considered one of the deadliest ransomwares to be developed. This ransomware shut down multiple hospitals across the United Kingdom and Ukraine. The developers of WannaCry were able to detect vulnerabilities in Windows which were first discovered by the United States National Security Agency. Symantec later on began to suspect that the developers originated from a North Korean-linked group called Lazarus (Fruhlinger, 2017). This ransomware is also one of the latest. 6,912 appointments were cancelled and 19,000 affected when WannaCry affected NHS operations in the year 2017. This attack, despite being “unsophisticated” as Kingsley Manning suggests, became so successful due to the fact that the different NHS offices were “intertwined” with each other, thus quickening the spread of the ransomware (BBC, 2017).

5.`Petya` and `NotPetya`: launched a week after the WannaCry outbreak, this ransomware is confirmation that a new age of ransomwares has begun (Fruhlinger, 2017). `Petya` or `NotPetya` was spread by attackers who sent a job application document via email in the PDF file format. When the PDF file was accessed by the company, the ransomware was unleashed. `NotPetya` is the very first ransomware which does not need any spam emails to be sent or social engineering to be carried out in order for infection to occur and administrative access to be gained. As long as there is a network connection in between two devices, `Petya` or `NotPetya` can move from one to the other (Fruhlinger, 2017).

Aside from these famous ransomware attacks, there are a few other ransomwares making their name in the market. One of the most prominent in recent years is `GandCrab` which just released their latest version, Version 4, in July 2018. `GandCrab` acts exactly like `CryptoLocker` and is also considered deadly since the ransomware converts files to `.KRAB` extension files once encrypted (Abram, 2018).

### 5.5.Platforms Affected by Ransomware Attacks

Malwares in general can affect multiple technological platforms, but recent studies have shown that ransomware has only affected three technological platforms so far. During the height of the `WannaCry` ransomware attacks, banks and bank users in India were falsely warned through circulating `Whatsapp` messages that the ransomware had actually breached ATM machines throughout the country. These allegations were proven to be hoaxes upon deeper investigation by one of the leading banks of India, called the Rural Bank of India (NDTV, 2017). Had this been true, ransomware could have been considered a deadly terrorism weapon by governments around the world.

Researchers have identified that ransomware has affected mobile platforms, computing platforms, and IoT and cloud computing platforms. Out of these three types of platform, it is most vital to keep IoT and cloud computing platforms secure.

a)**Computing Platform:** Computing platforms have always been those most affected by ransomwares. As previously discussed, ransomwares act on computers by infecting the system and encrypting the files in the system until a certain amount of ransom is paid. A large number of ransomware attacks on computing platforms — 86.87 percent of total ransomware attacks in 2016 — are directed at home users (Walton, 2017).

b)**Mobile Platform:** Due to people spending more time on their mobile phones, doctors have concluded that most people are suffering from mobile addiction (SC Magazine, 2017). Having this in mind, cyber criminals have taken advantage of people who are “addicted” to mobile phones.

*Android —* This has been the biggest target of ransomware attacks on mobile platforms. This is mainly due to the “openness” of the Android platform. With mobile ransomware increasing by 137.8% from the first to the second quarter of 2017, SC Magazine predicted that there would be a spike in mobile ransomware in 2018 (Snow, 2016). However, in Android ransomware and in mobile ransomware overall, cryptolockers are less common since file encryption would be useless on mobile platforms due to the cloud backup feature that most mobile devices have. This is the reason why most Android ransomwares lock applications with a coded GUI which shows the details of the ransomware once the application is opened (Norton, 2018). The newest mobile ransomware, specifically dedicated for Android, is better implemented. This ransomware, which is tagged as `Android.Lockroid.E`, locks the whole mobile device instead of just locking the specific applications inside the mobile device. This way, the user’s mobile device would be locked and unusable until the ransom had been paid (Savage *et al*., 2015).

*iOS —* According to a report released by Symantec, iOS devices are mostly secure due to Apple’s Technological and Security Ecosystem Structure. The only way that an iPhone can be vulnerable to ransomware is when the mobile device in question has undergone the process called “jailbreaking” (Ismail, 2018). Despite all the protection that Apple offers for iOS phones, researchers suggest that Proton Remote Access Trojan (RAT) has affected plenty of iPhones worldwide (Snow, 2016).

c)**IoT and Cloud Computing Platforms**

As previously discussed, the invention of NotPetya paved the way for a type of ransomware which does not need third party connections like spam mails or social engineering to infect and gain administrative access to any device. This led developers and researchers to think of the next step that ransomware would become capable of. Although there has been no reported ransomware attack on any IoT or cloud computing platform, the implementation of this idea is not too far away, especially now that IoT is being plagued by DDoS attacks such as Mirai (Cobb, 2017). Since ransomware developers can now understand and create ransomwares which can multiply by themselves on a shared network, IoT technologies are not safe any more since all IoTs share a common network, that is, the cloud (Budd, 2016). Although speculation about Microsoft 365’s cloud-based technology being attacked by a ransomware named CERBER or RANSOM_CERBER. CAD has not been fully proven, TrendMicro researchers have stated that the ransomware in question can encrypt 442 file types using AES 265 and RSA encryption techniques. However, it seems like the coding of cloud ransomware is just in its initial stages since the only command that the ransomware can successfully deliver is to play an audio message in a computer-generated (creepy) voice (Sneed, 2016). These incidents may actually hint at a growth of ransomware in the IoT and cloud industries.
