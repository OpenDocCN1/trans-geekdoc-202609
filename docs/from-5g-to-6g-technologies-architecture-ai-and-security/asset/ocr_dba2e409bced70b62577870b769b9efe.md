# 4.2.1 Ethical Governance in Artificial Intelligence

Fresh ideas on building the ethical, legal, and technological governance of AI are required. Ethical governance is centered on the most pressing issues highlighted by AI, including fairness, integrity, privacy, service, and product allocation. Clarity and interpretability are two principles that may be used to improve algorithmic fairness, transparency, and accountability. In Europe, the concept of a "right to explanation" of algorithmic choices is being explored [5]. Individuals would be entitled to an explanation if an algorithm decided something under this rule. Rather than dissecting the switch works, tools for operating algorithms systems could be presented as viewpoints that evaluate the inputs and outputs for biases and harms.

The ownership of AI is a third ethical problem that is crucial for everyone. Who is responsible for the actions, liabilities, and holders of robots? For example, if a robot causes injury to someone due to negligence? Is it more likely to be the robot’s owner or itself? The further ethical question is who is responsible for criminal liability in the eyes of the law. This is still gray area, and it’s unclear if a robot would be considered a real person in the eyes of the law. Balancing the relative sense of values held by many stakeholders in a variety of multicultural contexts, explaining the rationale, and ensuring transparency. The concept that robots could be ethical agents who are accountable for their actions, or "autonomous agents," is a field of some machine ethics discussions today. Machine ethics is making its way into practical robotics, where the assumption that these machines are artificial agents in any meaningful sense is rarely made. It has been highlighted that a robot that has been designed to follow ethical standards may be reproduced for immortal ones [5].
