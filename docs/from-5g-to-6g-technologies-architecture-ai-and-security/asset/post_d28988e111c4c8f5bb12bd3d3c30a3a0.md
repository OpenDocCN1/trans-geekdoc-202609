
## 4.2 Artificial Intelligence: Laws, Regulations, and Ethical Issues

AI is infiltrating every aspect of our society, from the vital to the mundane, such as healthcare and humanitarian assistance. AI may enhance economic, social, and human rights results, as embodied in AI and ML methods. These cutting-edge technologies can help a variety of sectors. At the same time, AI has the potential to be interpreted in unanticipated or dangerous ways. As a result, it is more important than ever to understand the intersection of law, ethics, and technology when it comes to managing AI. AI systems are used in a variety of applications, the bulk of which uses statistical-based learning techniques to find patterns in amounts of data and make predictions based on those [4]. Because AI is increasingly uncommon in its design and control, what frameworks may be utilized to this this, and how can this be done? It is one of the main themes covered by the writers of this special issue. We offer in-depth analysis of the ethical, legal-regulatory, and technological issues when putting up AI governance frameworks.

Complex, high-risk tasks, providing parole, detecting illnesses, and processing financial transactions, are increasingly assigned to AI systems. This raises new difficulties for such autonomous liability, the limits of present legal frameworks in dealing with "Big Data impact" or preventing algorithmic biases, social justice issues relating to law enforcement or social welfare, or online media consumption. Given AI’s extensive reach, these significant challenges can only be fully addressed by a multidisciplinary approach [4].

### 4.2.1 Ethical Governance in Artificial Intelligence

Fresh ideas on building the ethical, legal, and technological governance of AI are required. Ethical governance is centered on the most pressing issues highlighted by AI, including fairness, integrity, privacy, service, and product allocation. Clarity and interpretability are two principles that may be used to improve algorithmic fairness, transparency, and accountability. In Europe, the concept of a "right to explanation" of algorithmic choices is being explored [5]. Individuals would be entitled to an explanation if an algorithm decided something under this rule. Rather than dissecting the switch works, tools for operating algorithms systems could be presented as viewpoints that evaluate the inputs and outputs for biases and harms.

The ownership of AI is a third ethical problem that is crucial for everyone. Who is responsible for the actions, liabilities, and holders of robots? For example, if a robot causes injury to someone due to negligence? Is it more likely to be the robot’s owner or itself? The further ethical question is who is responsible for criminal liability in the eyes of the law. This is still gray area, and it’s unclear if a robot would be considered a real person in the eyes of the law. Balancing the relative sense of values held by many stakeholders in a variety of multicultural contexts, explaining the rationale, and ensuring transparency. The concept that robots could be ethical agents who are accountable for their actions, or "autonomous agents," is a field of some machine ethics discussions today. Machine ethics is making its way into practical robotics, where the assumption that these machines are artificial agents in any meaningful sense is rarely made. It has been highlighted that a robot that has been designed to follow ethical standards may be reproduced for immortal ones [5].

## 4 Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in the Era of 5G and 6G Technology

## 4.3 Potentials of Artificial Intelligence in Wireless 5G and 6G: Benefits and Challenges

### 4.3.1 Artificial Intelligence in Wireless 5G and 6G

The 5G cellular network was launched to enable Internet of Things (IoE) applications by delivering high data rates, low latency, and ultra-reliable connectivity. To facilitate connections between people, machines, and things in 5G, network equipment must be software-driven, intelligent, virtualized, and energy-efficient as the number of connected edge devices grows. The networks will also support various services, such as mobile broadband, ultra-reliable, low-latency communication with connected devices.

At the network level, new technologies and applications like IoE are creating massive volumes of data. Models based on AI are frequently created from acquired data to assist in detecting, classifying, and predicting future events. It is typically impractical to transfer all data to a single place due to time, latency, and storage concerns. Federated learning (FL) has been utilized to meet the growing need for decentralized data. The rollout of 5G communication networks has reached a major milestone in academia and industry, and the industry now aims to advance beyond 5G. The use of AI in the network to collect data with the help of high-performance computing is gaining traction. The typical method entails collecting data in a centralized way, and the heterogeneous data from numerous sources is then aggregated in the server, resulting in communication efficiency. FL based on AI is a bottleneck that improves the workflow of ML with privacy challenges.

### 4.3.2 Benefits and AI in 5G and 6G

- Enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB). The generation of applications that require higher data rates in a constant service system.
- Massive-type communications (mMTC). Scalable connection needed for the number of wireless devices with the effective transmission of tiny amounts of data across broad coverage areas is a major feature of 5G communication services. This traffic of data will be benefited by applications such as body-aware networks, smart homes, IoT, and drones.
- URLLC (ultra-reliable low-latency communications) will be prioritized above data rates in connected healthcare, remote surgery, mission-critical applications, autonomous driving, and smart industry applications.

### 4.3.3 How Can AI Be Used to Enhance 6G Services?

Even as 5G is recognized for its network connectivity and microservices-based design, the next generation of technologies, or the 6G generation, is strongly linked to network orchestration and management. AI will play a huge role in the evolving 6G. However, in many cases, the partnership of 6G and AI may be a double-edged sword due to AI's potential to safeguard or infringe on security and privacy. End-to-end automation of future networks, in particular, necessitates proactive threat detection, intelligent potentially, approaches, and the realization of auto network systems in 6G [6]. Nonetheless, as applications are not real, the AI-driven networks are constrained by the limitations of the conventional architecture that existed at the network's inception. As a result, no assistance is provided for distributed AI or intelligent agent, as these two domains are entirely AI-based.
