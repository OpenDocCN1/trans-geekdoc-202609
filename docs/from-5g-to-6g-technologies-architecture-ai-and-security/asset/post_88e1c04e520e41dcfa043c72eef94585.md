### 2.4.2 Zero-Trust Networking

Zero-trust networking is founded on a security framework that institutes trust via unasing authentication and keeping track of every network access effort [31]. This kind differing from the traditional assumption that everything in a corporate network serves trust. A zero-trust network can bring significant benefits like:

- Improved security. Attacks normally far from the target, and attackers mostly piggyback on the access of sanctioned users before lateral movements within the network to access their targeted assets. Zero-trust networking assists with such attacks.
- Simple security technique. Traditionally, corporations have layered security solutions to keep attackers at bay. Nonetheless, through time, these layered security solutions can generate security breaches for invaders. Zero-trust operating seamlessly with additional integrated networks.
- Management of dispersed infrastructure. Network infrastructure on getting increasingly complex and dispersed with applications, data, and assets spread through various cloud and hybrid settings. Customers from diverse locations, making it increasingly challenging to describe a secure limit. The simple solution of securing a perimeter is out of examples trouble challenges.

The generation of a zero-trust network encompasses a series of well-defined steps. The first thing is to identify the assets. At this stage, the company can take an inventory of assets and evaluate the value and susceptibility of assets like intellectual property and property information. The second step is to verify the users and devices. In many cases, invasions from spoofed devices. To attain zero trust, users and devices need verification of who and what is in them. The verification can be based on user authentication, behavioral analytics for IoT devices, and embedded chips. The third step is to map the workflows. This involves defining who has access to what, what they gain, and how and why access is granted as part of duty. The fourth step is to define and automate policies. At this stage, the organization can use the evaluation outcomes to authenticate policies, using metadata on the device, time, and contextual information to limit recent activity. Provision involves continuous screening for potential threats. The final step is testing, keeping, and preserving. With a zero-trust technique like trust modeling, testing needs to ensure the effect on production is insignificant. Implementation, security teams can observe device trends over time to recognize any irregularities that point to invasions and proactively become accustomed to policies to keep attackers at bay [31].

## 2.4.3 Information-Centric Networking

Information-centric networking (ICN) is a new technique in networking content as opposed to devices that have the connection. It has of warranted significance in network research and communities in change of standardization. National and supported research projects on a global scale. The ITU in the Telecommunication Standardization Project begins the ICN standardization process in 2012. The ICN Research Group of the Internet Research Task Force focuses on standards-oriented research. Global efforts collectively progress the new network structure of ICN. Nonetheless, there are limited research and debate on the comprehensive ICN standardization.

Current users use networks more for content recovery of direct communication between people or a server and a device [6]. This kind in consumer demands needs a network structure suitable for content recovery which results in the discovery of network structures referred to as ICN [32]. The ICN idea emerges in an era with consumers moving their interests to the content instead of the server or the location where it is in storage.
