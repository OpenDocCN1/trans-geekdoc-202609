# 4.4 Cybersecurity Issues in Advanced 5G and 6G

Our essentials network will undergo a physical transformation due to 5G, which will have long-term ramifications. Given that the merging challenge of part of the 5G "race" is rethinking how we safeguard the most important network of the twenty-first century and the ecosystem of devices and apps that spruit from it, from cyber vulnerabilities. The cost of a proactive 5G cybersecurity opportunity will be far higher than the cost of 5G vigilance in the first past. Businesses lost $10 billion in 2017 to the NotPetya ransomware attack. Of course, 5G networks did not exist at the time of the attack, but it does show the expense of such incursions, which pales in comparison to an attack that causes human. In order to be a viable business for all 5G players, we must establish the circumstances for cybersecurity investment and implement cybersecurity policies and reasonably designed to adhere to cybersecurity standards [8].

5G networks will face much more vulnerabilities than previous generations:

+   - The network has from centralized, hardware-based switching to distributed, firmware-based digital routing. Previous networks used hub-based topology, all traffic passing via physical chokepoints where cyber hygiene could be implemented. Meanwhile, in a 5G software-defined network, activity is transferred outside of the network to a web of digital routers, moving the capacity to monitor and manage changes.
- Even if the network's software flaws were caught, the network is still run by vulnerable-early-generation AI. If intruders gain control of the software systems, they can gain control of the network.
- New attack vectors emerge due to the large increase in bandwidth that makes 5G practical. In terms of physical security, low-cost, short-range small-cell antennas across urban areas have become new hotspots. These cell sites will use 5G's Dynamic Feature Sharing, which allows multiple streams of data to share bandwidth in "slices," with each its level of security. Network functions change constantly, the cyber defense must be dynamic rather than relying on a lowest-common-denominator strategy.
- Finally, linking tens of billions of smart gadgets to the IoT poses a security concern. Plans are being developed for a wide range of IoT-enabled devices, including public safety, combat, medical, and transportation applications, which are especially and potentially vulnerable.

As a result, fintech networks are especially vulnerable to a multidimensional cyberattack. This reimagined nature of networks - a new network "ecosystems" also requires a mighty cybersecurity strategy. While many network providers provide dedicated resources to cybersecurity, small- and medium-sized wireless internet service providers (ISPs) serving areas with 5G networks have struggled to justify a justified cybersecurity. Some of these businesses have less than 10 thousand customers. They cannot afford a dedicated cybersecurity officer or a cybersecurity operations center that is 24 hours a day, 7 days a week [8].
