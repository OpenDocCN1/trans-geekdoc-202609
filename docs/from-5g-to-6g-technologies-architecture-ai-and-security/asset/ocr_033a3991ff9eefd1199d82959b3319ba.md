The specialization of networks is also a potential area of concern. 6G networks will allow for subsets of networks in many places. The specialization of smaller-scale specialized networks will require the use of lightweight but well-tested authentication and encryption. The creation and utilization of trusted encryption environments offers potentially more feasible solution for some closed networks. With 5G, the connection and expansion of the open program interfaces (APIs) in 6G should also be recognized. These have been prone to vulnerable to multiple types of attack. The flexibility and computational capabilities of 6G networks will likely have to consider things like distributed denial of service (DDOS) attacks and man-in-the-middle attacks.

And with all technology, the endpoints connecting to the network must be considered a threat to the security of these new and advanced networks. Functions of enhanced Subscriber Identity Module (eSIMs) and Integrated SIMs (iSIMs) for some devices may help secure communication, longer-term moves away from traditional symmetric encryption may be the only way to move forward. It must also be considered necessary to deal with quantum computing and its advantages in decrypting data.

Some uses of 6G technology bring up potential concerns for security attacks. Unvarnished aerial vehicles (UAVs) may be used as they are unnamed and much more susceptible to being jammed and captured to harvest data from their systems. Holographic presence could lead to not only enormous amounts of data to deal with but also help to secure the holographic images being presented and keep them private. When especially used presenting to a remote location. Another potential vector of concern is the world of extended reality. It will lead to an increase in storage and collection of personal data which will require trust in the data collected and the user's perceived quality. However, this is a field that involves a lot of development of this which will require innovative designing solutions.

A field that will grow large in the world of wireless communications networks is connected autonomous vehicles. The interconnectivity will rely on cars and their manufacturers for updates and patching of software and monitoring will only increase with autonomous vehicles. This is a great area for potential attack. The attacks that target these vehicles will also be susceptible to the same kind of attacks meant for UAVs. The networks utilized by these systems will be very heterogeneous and rely on multiple third-party providers service providers (CPS). National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has already begun following out the framework on which they can operate. The security framework will require many considerations to expand to the next generation of smart grid networks as well. Protecting supervisory data and control access (SCA) systems are connected through hundreds and thousands of IoT monitoring that encompass a wide variety of devices and scenarios. Industrial applications are also an area of great potential as well as intelligent healthcare and the use of digital tools.

Blockchain has the potential for all of its applications. The advantages of blockchain as disintermedi, immutability, nonrepudiation of provenance, integrity, and pseudonymity are particularly important to establish trust and secure manner in the 6G networks. This leads to a focus on distributed ledger technology (DLT) to offer more trust to the developing and potential uses of 6G. This could be in the form of protecting the integrity of AI for example. Blockchain is not without its potential problems, however. While not easily attainable, a 51% attack is what has been of concern for current usage scenarios, with many other forms of attack we are already seeing on current iterations of blockchain.
