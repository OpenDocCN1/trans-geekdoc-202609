## 8.3 6G: Key Technologies

Even though 5G is good and all, the human race and its need for internet keeps evolving and needing to adapt that 6G networks are already in the work and are being researched and will for even more powerful networks. Sixth-generation networks are fully expected to support broadband services, global connectivity, and great infrastructure, and promote the development of networking as a whole. We need to improve mobility, networking, and intelligence in almost every aspect of human life and transform societies everywhere. With all of these new network possibilities, a bunch of crazy technologies will emerge. This includes virtual reality, augmented reality, smart cities, ultra-fast smart, holographic communication, IoT, Internet of Everything, and even wireless to computer communication. All of these innovations will require crazy network performance. One of the major innovations that will help bridge the gap from 5G to 6G is the technologies of the sub-THz band. With all the new technologies introduced including holographic communication, virtual reality, internet networking, and many, they are going to need ultra-high speed to keep up. The predicted data rates for these services are supposed to be beyond 100 Gbps, which is hoped Current fourth-generation and fifth-generation technologies cannot keep up. There is going to be as much difference in the frequency range as THz band comes into play. Specifically, the 200-300 GHz (0.2-3 THZ) frequency band has drawn the interest of many researchers due to its ability to be frequency free and low contention. It looks promising that a frequency band transmission with a data rate of ~100 Gbps has already been shown using various techniques. With data rates in the Tbps using the sub-THz band, the sixth-generation technologies actually seem feasible. Generating the signal for the sub-THz band, however, is quite difficult but can be generated using high-tech electronics and photonics-based approaches. Once it is achieved, it will have a myriad of issues. One problem with this is that it is still in the early stages of research and development, and it seems to be short-range. There is a problem with free space path loss in the sub-THz band signals. There is still plenty of research to be done on this 6G technology and its uses.
