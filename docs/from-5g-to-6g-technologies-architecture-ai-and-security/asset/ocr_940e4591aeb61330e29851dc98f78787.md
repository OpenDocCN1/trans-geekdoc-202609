Cybersecurity does not have an identity that everyone in the field can agree on. Throughout my research into the topic, it became that the term had many various and often subjective definitions that truly captured all that industry struggled to capture. When I looked deeper I found a satisfactory answer. Given to me by a research team that set to exactly what I was looking to on, the definition they called "Cybersecurity is the organization and collection of resources, processes, and structures to protect cyberspace and cyberspace-enabled systems from which misalign de [rightful] of de [factual] property rights". Though no definition (not even one) has the backing of all professionals, I will be referring to cybersecurity in this way throughout this chapter.

All the parties involved in cybersecurity make a range of people from individuals, private organizations, and governments of which aim to protect their cyber assets, attacks on others, or even themselves. These attacks find their source in financial, political, or militaristic purposes, and it is in everyone's interest that cybersecurity measures be fully developed [2].

The need for improving cybersecurity cannot be unnoticed, especially when cyber threats themselves as the highest risk on a global scale when we come to economy [3]. As the cybersecurity community evolved, so do the mindset; there is no longer a question of "if we are attacked, but "when" because an attacker wants to and has the capability to do so. In many connected chain of systems, the attack area is as vast and spread out that gaps and vulnerabilities are nearly impossible to defend. There is no such thing as forcing an attack into a network since any attackers will get in way of vulnerabilities in the system. An attacker needs to find one vulnerability to exploit in time to become successful, whereas defenders must always be on guard if they have high bearing. Being prepared to be unrealistic and thinking cyberattacks are completely avoidable, becomes as the inevitable is realized, focused attention elsewhere [4]. Cybersecurity's main concern is to expose these threats and vulnerabilities that can be countered. The defense requires reliable data for interpretation and effective use.

Automation in data processing has been around for a while, largely because humans do not have the sensory capacity to manage enormous amounts of data in and out of their networks, produced by software and hardware that alerts system administrators; automation is the only solution that can make sense. Mostly, we saw this with the introduction of anti-virus programs that use intrusion detection. These programs inspect for known signatures of malware that attaches to machines and causes harm. Over the years, we have seen this increase in malware and defending against worms, Trojans, spyware, ransomware, rootkits, and over-exploiting software. In the past, the emergence of new polymorphic and metamorphic malware, which can change their appearance every second, it is important to recognize these threats if nothing is matching them to the detection database that will continuously be updated.
