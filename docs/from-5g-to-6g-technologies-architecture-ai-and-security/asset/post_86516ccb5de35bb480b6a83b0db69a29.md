### 2.3.1 Wi-Fi

For many people, Wi-Fi plays a very important role in our day-to-day operations. As soon as we arrive at a location of any, we pull out our mobile devices and look for Wi-Fi. Most technical devices are equipped with Wi-Fi-capable devices. These things such as watches, dryers, door locks, refractors, cars, and many more are now equipped with Wi-Fi capabilities. Wi-Fi is for short wireless and as WLAN (wireless LAN). Wi-Fi is based on IEEE 802.11 standards. Wi-Fi uses unlicensed wireless frequencies 2.4 and 5 GHz, devices can connect to frequencies or both, which could be dual-band capability.

There are different revisions of IEEE 802.11 standard. In Wi-Fi, the first one to show its excellence to the world was 802.11a operated using the 5-GHz frequency and was introduced in 1999. 802.11a offers speeds up to 54 Mbps and 802.11b was the next Wi-Fi technology brought to the attention of home users. 802.11b used the 2.4-GHz frequency and was introduced the same year as 802.11a. 802.11b became popular with home users, achieving speeds of 11 Mbps.

When Wi-Fi created, ownership of personal devices such as laptops and data assistants (PDAs) expanded. Consumers could now have the wireless capability in their homes, and they loved it. Although in 1999, many consumers were reaching the high speeds of 11 Mbps, 802.11 technology was introduced. 802.11g was brought into the future of Wi-Fi, and consumers could now enjoy speeds of 54 Mbps using the 2.4-GHz frequency. To the frequencies used by Wi-Fi, there is always consumers prefer 2.4-GHz over 5-GHz as half as much range as 5 GHz, which has greater potential to avoid obstacles like walls, trees, or.

802.11n was introduced in the year 2003. 802.11n choose the range of 802.11b over the speeds of 802.11a. Now, the prices of 5-GHz equipment were drastically more expensive than 2.4-GHz equipment. Wi-Fi turned to grow increasingly popular, and devices to support the growth for this. As in 2003, when we thought we would not ever, in 2009, 802.11n introduced the technology called MIMO (multiple-input multiple-output).
