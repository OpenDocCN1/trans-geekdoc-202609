### 12.7.2 Security and Privacy Concerns

Figure 12.5 shows areas affected by the five security and privacy challenges: authentication (S1), access control (S2), malicious behavior (S3), encryption (S4), and data transmission (S5). Most parts of the diagram display susceptibility to malicious behavior, authentication, and access control [39]. Some technologies display specific sensitivity to some of the mentioned challenges. In case point, VLC with real-time intelligent edge and intelligent radio waves in the face of malicious activities and data transmission procedures [39]. Molecular communication and THz technology both go to intelligent radio. The molecular communication technology links to privacy and security issues regarding encryption, authentication, and communication. On the other hand, THz technology is particularly vulnerable to malicious acts and authentication security challenges [39]. The quantum communication and blockchain technology overlap with AI and intelligent radio. The privacy and security considerations link access control, encryption, authentication, and data transmission.

6G applications might display particular susceptibilities. Robotics and autonomous systems like self-driving cars from Tesla of AI and VLC with encryption, data transmission, and malicious acts can cause challenges. The multisensory XR applications employ molecular communication technology, THz technology, and quantum communication technology, which is vulnerable to malicious acts, control, and data transmission [39]. Wireless brain-computer relations uses the same approaches as the multi-sensory XR application. However, they possess security and privacy challenges.

### 12.7.3 Research Activities and Trends

5G network has already been commercialized. Stakeholders in the initial stages of 6G research need to function in partnerships with the majority of generation momentum in research activities to assist next-generation technology [40]. The government, academia, and industries need to come together at this initial stage. 6G is yet to establish its standards and products. Instead, 6G needs a long runway with adequate room for maturity, discussion and investigation [40]. For the long haul, it is important to pool efforts at the initial stage.

In other instances, it is normal to see stakeholders compete against each other even without thought of sharing it is industry standards. However, with 6G, if stakeholders fail to engage in mutual discussions and efforts, with no public publications, other stakeholders will spend resources, making the same attempts meaningless [40]. Corporations ought to generate momentum and return back 6G research at an initial point. At a later stage, different companies can select specific technologies and products for additional development.

Several countries have already made steps toward 6G research. An example is Europe. In December 2021, Europe wisely founded a Joint Undert on Smart Networks and Services with 6G [41]. This new work took up its initial program from 2021 to 2022 with public funding of approximately €240 million [41]. The Work 2021-2022, intends to financially support all activities of evolving 5G and inclusive of large-scale pilot ventures with vertical sectors to leading research in 6G systems.

The European collaboration on Networks and Services is pioneered by the Commission, and close participation involves the member states. This joint undertakes the R&D course for Europe [41]. This venture capitalizes on the EU’s budget of €7000 million through the years seen, set to be made by the market in an equal figure. The objective is to make it possible for European stakeholders to strengthen their R&I ability for 6G systems and grow lead markets for 5G infrastructure for the green and digital revolution.

Many major factors affecting research toward goal networks and services include the future applications and usages, dynamic changes with time and space, and deep technologies.

Security in 6G systems must explore new paradigms and stages such as virtualization (from basic functions to end-to-end systems using seamless security), the level of flexibility and programmability of orchestrated security closely integrated with orchestrated systems and services, concepts such as moving or target defense, cloudification (delivery of service), define/open service architectures for SNS and integrated into the service-based overall paradigm) and holistic approaches encompassing the whole life cycle, from original/component development to introduction of technologies (e.g. AI Quantum). Security quantification in a 6G context should deal with complexity and fragmentation, and mandates the exploration of mechanisms to identify, evaluate, and monitor the level of security. Based on such quantification, trust can be given to providers and services, enabling the growth of the sector by answering the diversity of demand from the mass market to B2B verticals [3].
