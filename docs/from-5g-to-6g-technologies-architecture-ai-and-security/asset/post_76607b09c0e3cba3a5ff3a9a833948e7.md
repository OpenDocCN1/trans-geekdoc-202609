### 12.6.2 Visible-Light Communications

There is a fast upcoming trend of considering the IoT in smart cities, homes, and next-generation wireless frameworks. It is expected that there will be a connection of the plurality of devices sensors, cameras, machines, tablets, and many others [14]. When this happens, the radio frequency spectrum currently in use proves inefficient to satisfy the needs of data-heavy wireless applications. Operating at the radio frequency spectrum would fail from damage and interference. To eliminate the challenge of spectrum insufficiency and fulfill high-data rate requirements by 5G frameworks, visible light communication (VLC) for Internet of Things applications in indoor settings through effectively routing data in VLC codes will be a promising solution.

Operating at the unrestricted band in the visible light area uses light-emitting diodes (LEDs). LEDs are a friendly-healthy communication technology; VLC comes with the advantage of offering ultra-high bandwidth, intrinsic physical security, and robustness to electromagnetic interference. On the downside, VLC has the shortcomings of suffering from short communication ranges and restrictions line-of-sight [14].

6G networks promise to significantly increase capacity and fulfill the needs of emerging applications. The existing frequency bands may prove inadequate [15]. While 6G will improve coverage through the terrestrial, underwater, space, and air dimensions, it cannot offer high-speed data rates for networks existing outside the terrestrial space. VLC is a high-speed communication approach. VLC has an unrestricted frequency range from 400 to 800 THz. VLC will be taken up as an essential part of 6G considering its high-speed transmission and handling the mentioned challenges. There are prospects that VLC will also work with other communication techniques to improve daily life.

Even though VLC is not so close to the radius of maturity, it is possibly better in many cases, including underwater communication, V2X, and communications with a need for high security and low latency. VLC is considered an extremely promising application technology for short-range connections. This quality of VLC acts as a part of what communication offers. VLC offers enhanced performance in harshest contexts [17].

Aside from providing a huge and unrestricted bandwidth to cover the radio spectrum, the VLC technology also has many advantages, like being easily available without electromagnetic interference and radiation. Free-space optical (FSO) communication systems also function as a viable substitute for optical fibers because they need less deployment [18]. Additionally, FSO can be installed in places with a challenge of deploying a wired network.
