# 8.10 6G Wireless Systems

The architect behind 6G had a vision to meet the demands of what is known as the society 10 years from now, which would significantly go beyond 5G capabilities. The main categories of the 6G vision are intelligent connectivity, deep connectivity, holographic connectivity, and ubiquitous connectivity. The connectivity is said to have to meet the following requirements simultaneously: each of the related devices in a network is considered 'intelligent' as well as its services, and the complex network will need more intelligent management.

# 8.10 6G Wireless Systems

There are three important characteristics that are linked to the next decade of lifestyle changes. They can be defined as a high-fidelity holographic society, connectivity for all things, and time-sensitive/time-engineered applications. High-fidelity holographic society is increasingly becoming the main communication focus and is vastly moving to augmented reality, or AR for it. It has predicted that hologram and multisensory communications are meant to be the next for the virtual world of communication. Hawking gave a lecture in 2017, via hologram in Hong Kong, which showcased the ever-growing power for such a thing.

The system requirements will include data rates, latency, synchronization, security, resilience, and computation. All of them work together in order to ensure that the images and sound flow smoothly, without compromising privacy.

Connectivity for all essentially means that it will include infrastructure essential for the smooth functioning of society. It will bring the necessary needs to operate multiple network types, going beyond the standard territories of today. Connectivity for moving platforms is required for many new applications that are emerging, such as unmanned aerial vehicle-based systems.

Last-sensitive/time-engineered applications consider the timeliness of information delivery. This means it is considered to be a highly interconnected society for the future.

# 8.10.1 Advances

The expected track of 6G is to connect humans with machines within 10-20 years. Current XR and VR/AR devices will not satisfy customers' expectations of a fully immersive experience. Integrating the technology with human bodies in a way that meets expectations would require 10-100-fold improvement to make sure the 'infrastructure' can handle the environment.

Multiple research firms have suggested that 6G will make it possible to merge implants, smart devices, embedded devices, and other forms of interconnected devices with human and interconnected human-mindset. The aggregation of devices connected with 6G will create a more immersive experience. This is a purely helpful but a direction that the technology may be on track to.

The important driving factors for sixth-generation technologies have been discussed by the ITU-T are high-fidelity holographic society, connectivity for everything, and time-sensitive or time-engineered functions. High-fidelity holographic society is implementing or integrating video communications and augmented reality by increasing the speed required for these devices to connect.

Connectivity for everything means integrating within the network to provide connectivity to all sorts of devices. For these tasks, devices will need to be built within the sixth-generation network and meet the range of wireless access to operate on the network. The sixth-generation network will provide a more efficient and uninterrupted connection for these types of devices to continue connecting.

Time-sensitive and engineered functions operate under the premise that sixth-generation networks will provide a faster infrastructure for these types of applications to provide a smooth and seamless process in getting users to connect their applications. As the planning of generation LTE network begins to be replaced or upgraded to fifth-generation, sixth-generation networks provide multiple updates to their predecessors. In the case of the sixth generation, the present network and legacy network architecture will need to be reduced to comply with the sixth-generation. Specifically, focusing on the TCP/IP framework, the sixth-generation network may not provide the necessary QoS to provide all applications with the best service.
