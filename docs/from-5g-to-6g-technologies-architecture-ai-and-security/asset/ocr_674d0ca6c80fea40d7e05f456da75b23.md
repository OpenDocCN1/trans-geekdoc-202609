# 8 Next Generations: Integration, Trustworthiness, Privacy, and Security

AI will play a crucial role in the new network. To address the security demands of 6G networks, SDN (software-defined network) and NFV (network function virtualization) should be complemented with AI techniques for proactive threat detection, intelligent mitigation, and self-securing mechanisms.

6G enables IoT, where security is based on SIM cards, and makes resource-constrained IoT devices vulnerable to attacks. 6G networks are smaller than 5G networks and interconnect with wide-area connectivity when needed. 6G cell sizes will shrink from small cells to “tiny cells,” making a greater number of vulnerable devices be deployed, a new security strategy required, and security for the maximum number of devices within sub-cell-the network area not far from practical. 6G networks will rely on AI to be fully autonomous networks; however, attacks on AI systems, especially ML systems, will affect 6G. Privacy issues are related to attacks on data stored on blockchains and the use of VLC. AI in security is providing systems for security, architectures, technologies, and privacy.

Multilayer intrusion detection and prevention are effective in SDN/AI-enabled networks, but rule-based systems are ineffective against attacks. Machine learning intrusion detection in Industrial IoT (IoT) can identify malicious packets based on their behavior. These systems are more effective than conventional intrusion detection systems for large-scale IoT. 6G sub-networks can be considered an extension of 5G networks and can use ML-based techniques to prevent malicious traffic. 6G will rely on edge intelligence to prevent centralized-cloud AI-based systems. AI at the cell level will use the level-based approach to prevent denial of service (DoS) attacks on cloud servers and the wide-area network. In AI security, we can predict attacks such as DoS attacks on blockchain before they occur. We can also use AI in the 6G networks to manage base stations and allow seamless communication for devices. Edge-based machine learning models could be used to detect privacy-preserving routers, rank them, and allow devices to transfer data via privacy-preserving routers. AI for security, security, and ethics issues. Issues arising from the use of AI in 6G are potentially poisoning attacks, evasion attacks, and API-based attacks. Countermeasures could include adversarial learning, making target defense, input validation, differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, and adversarial training. Solutions AI-based 6G systems have less transparency, and AI may behave differently than human. AI can uncover vulnerabilities, convert vulnerable IoT devices into bots, and launch DoS attacks against critical nodes. Using AI to empower defenses, distributed intelligence can be used to learn the process of the attackers.

There are challenges with integrating the network with AI. This means that there needs to be an AI smart enough to control the network at high speeds without failing. It also means that a lot of time and money is in creating such an AI. It won't be just configuring the network, either—there are going to be many AI controlling devices, smart cities, smart services, etc. This is going to be very costly and some of it may not be possible, so there is going to have to be standardization with it.
