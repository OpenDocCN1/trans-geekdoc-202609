### 1.2.4 Haptic Communication

Haptic communication is also referred to as haptic communication and 3D touch [7]. This is any technology that can create an experience of touch by applying forces, vibrations, or motions to the user. This technology can also be enhanced with VR to create virtual objects in a computer system control objects and enhance the remote control of machines and devices. Haptic technology helps how the human sense of touch works controlling haptic virtual objects. The different ways of implementation are listed here with a brief [8; 9]:

- Vibration. This is created using an eccentric mass (ERM) actuator, which is an unbalanced weight attached to a motor shaft. When the shaft spins, the unbalanced weight causes the device to shake.
- Force feedback. Devices use motors to manipulate the movement of an item that the user holds. This is best known in racing games that use a steering wheel that, when turned, gives the feel of resistance or pull in the opposite direction. This allows the haptic simulation of objects, textures, recoil, momentum, and objects' perceived physical presence in games.
- Air vortices. A donut-shaped air pocket is made of compressed gusts of air. These gusts can blow out or move paper from a few yards away and can be delivered noncontact haptic feedback.
- Ultrasound. A focused beam can be used to create a sense of pressure on a finger without touching the object. The beam can give sensations of vibration and give users the ability to feel virtual 3D objects.

The advancement of ultrasound technology has been integrated into many areas such as personal computers (PCs), mobile devices, virtual reality (VR), teleoperators and robotics, medicine, dentistry, art, aviation, space automobiles, and teledronics. The technology has been used for a while but has gone under the radar for most customers [9].

### 1.2.7 The Internet of Everything

The seventh topic in this chapter is the IoE, which is people, processes, and things. The IoE is the world where billions of devices have sensors to detect and assess the states of the environment. They are all connected over a public or private network. The main characteristics of IoE are data, processes, and things. The IoT is the network of physical objects accessed through the internet. The main difference is that IoE has four distinguishable characteristics, while IoT has one. You will hear the term IoE as or IoT due to the vast range and expanse of high-speed data transfer and accompanying technology.
