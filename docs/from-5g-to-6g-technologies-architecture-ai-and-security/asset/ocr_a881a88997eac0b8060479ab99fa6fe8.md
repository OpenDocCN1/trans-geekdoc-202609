# Next Generations Networks: Integration, Trustworthiness, Privacy, and Security

# 8.1 Introduction

Wireless communication has been developed with significant changes and advancements since the 1980s with each generation. We have recently entered the fifth generation of wireless communication (5G). The adoption and deployment of this newest generation are underway but will likely take three to five more years to see fully realized. 5G has brought about the move to the cloud for software-based networking on-demand, automated learning of networking functions. However, even with these advancements, researchers have already begun to look at the next generation (6G) and how the networking landscape will change with its adoption.

Through a variety of technologies, 6G is expected to bring about a shift to completely intelligent network orchestration and management. Heterogeneous cloud infrastructure of 5G needs to visualize the architectural framework of 6G. Future networks may not disappear with the expectation of 6G as the addition of a new infrastructural component. Where it will begin to diverge from the present is the increased variety and flexibility of specialized networks for personal subnetworks and seamless flexible workload.

With every next generation 6G networks will have their own privacy concerns that need to be addressed. Mobile security continues to be a very significant issue with each generation of wireless communications and seems to only get more difficult with each technological advancement. Past generations have dealt with everything from authentication issues to concerns of attackers, it is expected that 6G networks will see the same types of attacks such as zero-day attacks and physical layer attacks, but also increase in advanced attacks such as quantum-based attacks and artificial intelligence/machine learning-based (AI/ML) intelligent agents. The addition of AI to the networks of 6G will work for its security, in terms of communication.

We must build on security research to gain insight into the challenges that will come with the next generations of communication. Networks may require Terabits per second (Tbps) connection speeds while also facilitating connectivity for high counts of devices like IoT and the flexibility of mobile devices that these networks use. With sensitive latency, end-end attacks of a network will require latency down to milliseconds. These extreme jumps in speed, density, and latency requirements will still require the 6G to be 100 times more energy efficient than current systems which may be achieved through low power communication in some cases.

Latency-sensitive applications will have to consider the impact of increasing demands in the latency of the system, while the extremely high bandwidth of networks will offer many challenges for ingesting and processing the network's data as it flows. Some applications will likely require processing locally to avoid these issues.
