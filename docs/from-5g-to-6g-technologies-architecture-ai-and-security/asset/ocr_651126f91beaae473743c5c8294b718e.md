Every 10 years, wireless (or mobile) communication undergoes undergoes a generation change. The next generation, 6G, has already started. The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) Focus Groups For Networks 2030 has listed several aspects of 6G, including holographic media services, network architecture, and internet protocol (IP). Many researchers and industries have started discussions on use cases, deployments, and performance requirements of 6G. We were compared with previous generations of mobile communication, and new network architectures were discussed. Technology maturity levels vary in 6G, with many technologies in their scientific exploration stage.
Technologies being considered include holographic radio, terahertz communication, large intelligent surface (LIS), orbital angular momentum, atmospheric backhaul, ubiquitous communication, and advanced duplex. The goal of 6G is to provide the information society with services by around 2030. The 6G network will support many challenges, such as intelligence of network services and network architecture, the integration of connected objects (devices), and information support of intelligent services. The 6G network should be able to deliver 10Tb per second and should have lower latency, higher reliability, and higher efficiency.
