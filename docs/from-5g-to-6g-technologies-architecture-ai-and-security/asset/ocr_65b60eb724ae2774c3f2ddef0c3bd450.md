Security is the next thing that will be improved for 6G networks to be rolled out. Mobile networks have relied on SIM cards for identifying devices as part of their security. This method causes issues in IoT from the size of the cards to the symmetric key encryption they use. As the bandwidth increases in 6G networks along with the moving of more data to the cloud, the need for end-to-end security will become even more important. Softwaredefined networking, AI, and NFV need to work together in 6G networks to provide that end-to-end security. Some of the suggested approaches to 6G security include authentication of remote endpoints, certification of the platforms, support of remote authentication, and secure properties.

Privacy in 6G networks is another important concern and many consider the problem. Protecting personal information on the 6G network will be a challenge. This is another global issue that will be viewed differently depending on where in the world you are. Standardizing regulations pertaining to privacy will be difficult due to the difference in governmental views on what should be private.

Liability is just one more potential issue related to the growth of 6G networks that will have to be solved as technologies move forward.

The benefits of 6G are massive. However, it is critical not to overlook the challenges. The enabling technologies of 6G are therefore tasked to develop solutions for these challenges. Some of the issues likely to be faced 6G networks include:

+   - Global coverage
- Limited system capacity
- Challenge in deploying computing and AI capabilities
- Hardware constraints on Edge AI
- The trade-off between high intelligence and privacy
- Security
- Noncomputability of hardware and software nonlinearity

In terms of challenges and hurdles for 6G, multiband and utilization requires advanced front-end hardware, spectrum coexistence creates interference problems, and waveform coexistence also creates new interference problems. Heterogeneous networks face issues with overloading. ISAC systems also suffer from an excess of demand.

Ultra-flexible framework has three key points. First, new tech should be combined into communications through a flexible framework that enables waiting for a second. A key tech network needs to work together to meet various requirements, so a flexible cognitive engine could optimize different aspects. Lastly, flexibility should be quantified while optimizing with some sort of flexibility indicators.

In conclusion, 5G was defined by its variety of requirements and applications, and 6G is expected to continue in its footsteps. Flexibility should be the focus of 6G to better build what 5G has developed by using key enablers.
