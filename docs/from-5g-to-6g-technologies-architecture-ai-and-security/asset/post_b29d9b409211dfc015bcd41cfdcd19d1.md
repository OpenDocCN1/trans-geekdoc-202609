In the 1970s, in their first generation, known as 1G, the telecommunications stands via only voice services to its subscribers. In the 1980s, 2G came with everything and even began roaming data services. 3G also saw the creation of text messages for mobile phones and even brought large data bandwidth and faster internet connectivity. The unfortunate part is that the actual network infrastructure was not rolled out the way it was marketed, but that 3.5G came in the 3G network even more efficient than it was, allowing for fast and better uplink/download speed.

Then the fourth generation, 4G. Its purpose was to allow internet access with speeds of up to 100 Mbps. By the standards of the 4G network, many service providers and users could access it. Based on the standards created by the International Telecommunications Union-Radio communications (ITU-R), the data rate also varying, depending on the frequencies and equipment used. Those who connected with high mobility, such as people in cars and trains, would access data rates ranging from Mbps to higher, while those in low mobility, such as pedestrians, would experience around 1Gbps. These policies were created by the International Mobile Communication Advanced specifications (IMT-Advanced). 4G networks could also be used in network infrastructures like WLANs and WANs.
