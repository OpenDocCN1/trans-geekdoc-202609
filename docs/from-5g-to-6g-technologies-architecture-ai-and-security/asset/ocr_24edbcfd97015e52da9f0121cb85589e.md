# 4.3 Potentials of Artificial Intelligence in Wireless 5G and 6G: Benefits and Challenges

# 4.3.1 Artificial Intelligence in Wireless 5G and 6G

The 5G cellular network was launched to enable Internet of Things (IoE) applications by delivering high data rates, low latency, and ultra-reliable connectivity. To facilitate connections between people, machines, and things in 5G, network equipment must be software-driven, intelligent, virtualized, and energy-efficient as the number of connected edge devices grows. The networks will also support edge computing to dehaul bandwidth consumption. It will be difficult for 5G to provide a single architecture that can support various services, such as mobile broadband, 3D connection, ultra-reliable, low-latency communication with connected devices. As a result, 5G networks must employ AI to improve network services and enable edge computing with low latency and high bandwidth.

At the network level, new technologies and applications like IoE are crowd creating massive volumes of data. Models based on AI are frequently created from acquired data to assist in detecting, classifying, and predicting future events. It is typically impractical to transfer all data to a single place due to time, latency, and storage concerns. Federated learning (FL) has been utilized to meet the growing need for decentralized data training. The rollout of 5G wireless communication networks has reached a major milestone in academia and industry world, and the industry now aims to advance beyond 5G. The use of AI in the network to collect data with the help of high-performance computing networks is gaining traction. The typical method entails collecting data in a centralized way, and the heterogeneous data from numerous sources is then aggregated in the server, resulting in communication efficiency. FL based on AI is a bottleneck that improves the workflow of ML in 5G with privacy challenges.

# 4.3.2 Benefits and AI in 5G and 6G


* Enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB). The generation of applications that require higher data rates in a constant service system.
* Massive-type communications (mMTC). Scalable connection needed for the number of wireless devices with the effective transmission of tiny amounts of data across broad coverage areas is a major feature of 5G communication services. This traffic of data will be benefited by applications such as body-aware networks, smart homes, IoT, and and drones.
* URLLC (ultra-reliable low-latency communications) will be prioritized above data rates in connected healthcare, remote surgery, mission-critical applications, autonomous driving, and smart industry applications.
