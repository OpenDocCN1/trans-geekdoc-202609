### 2.2.1 Approaches

Developers have been working on energy-efficient networking for many years. In the increase of the use of the internet and wired networks, which has driven down the energy efficiency of protocols, networks, and the ones they run on. Different types of energy-related problems have been discovered, covering most topics like media access control (MAC), allocation of spectrum, and routing. This section depicts a few examples of recent progress, especially in increasing the energy efficiency of broadband networks like ethernet and wireless LAN.

Ethernet is one of the most prominent and favored technologies used for local area networks (LANs), which are widely used by 3 billion people. Lumen, AT&T, Spectrum Enterprise, Verizon, Comcast, and Cox Business are the top six companies to qualify for a rank of 4% or more of the US retail services market market. Ethernet is mostly used in commercial, and residential areas. Almost every personal computer, laptop, and server have at least one network adapter. Nowadays, ethernet interfaces are also used in home appliances.

Many relevant technical advancements as gbe and wireless 802.11 contribute to the evolution of ethernet usage in the industry for IoT (Internet of Things). Data speed is rapidly increasing in the past; what was 10 Mbps, and is now increasing to 100 Gbps (Gbe), replacing fast ethernet. Present network adapters that support high data rates utilize the most power, for instance a Gbe Base-T transceiver uses nearly 10 times more than fast.

Researchers and developers have great interest in improving energy efficiency in the last five years. Their hard work resulted in the growth of Standard Energy Efficiency (EEE), which was introduced in 2010. The change made by this EEE introduced the low power idle (LPI). The sender side tells the signals, which is made to maintain the position from the sender to the receiver when no data is transmitted. Using a traditional ideal sign, we can save at least 50% of energy used during this time. Approaches like packet coalescing near the sender before sending the data to the receiver they send in a break are investigated and helped to reduce the energy usage of EEE. At the same time, they keep the data packets in a predicted jump for applications that are based on the internet. It is believed that the use of EEE has resulted in saving energy, becoming almost global.

One more factor to save energy for technology used in present ethernet coding plans and a more energy sensitive encoding that is easy to apply. This suggested technique returns almost 18% and 60% betterment in encoding and the transmission of the circuit.

## 2.3 Wireless Networks

Wireless mobility is a large part of society's today. Cellphones, tablets, and watches are just a few names that connect us to the internet. We need the internet for work, school, entertainment, and sometimes to monitor critical aspects of our lives. Many technologies allow us to interconnect to the world of the internet.
As advancements made daily, cellular access has also become became because of the demands of the customers and users. 2.1 [5] have the rapid growth of global mobile data traffic by ITU (International Telecommunication Union) Mobile data traffic generated in the past decade is estimated to grow at an annual rate of 55% in 2020-2030 to reach 60 Exabytes (EB) in 2025 and 516 EB in 2030.

The journey starts with 1G (first generation), now 5G (fifth generation) is being introduced and in more areas. The second generation was introduced with 2G, 2.5G-, and then 3G (third generation), 4G (fourth generation), were introduced. We have started working on the latest generation, 6G. Although there have been no ratified standards, researcher and scientist have discussed and many applications for this Terahertz technology.
In 1979, the Japanese company introduced the 1G (first generation) with a speed of 10 MHz and frequencies of 800 and 900 MHz. Still, due to analog signaling and technical limitations, it was not very successful. GSM, also as the global mobile communication system, was introduced in the second generation and was a key for further advancement.
Digital systems, elements, and networks were developed to achieve higher quality and include other main features of the second generation. In 2001, 3G was presented in Japan and in the United States. In 2002, Verizon extended the range into the 2.1 GHz band, permitting phones to work at higher rates. MIMO (multiple input, multiple outputs) was the primary lead forward during this time since it supported the network information transmission.
