## 3.6 The Impact of 6G on Other Industries

The healthcare and space industries will not be the only areas of our lives affected by 6G when it is implemented. From the manufacturing industry to the automotive industry and even the agriculture industry, many aspects of our life will be touched by 6G. Likely, we will not perceive at this point how many things will be changed with the advent of 6G since it is still at least a decade away from commercial availability. Figure 3.9 illustrates potential use cases for 6G systems.

The industry is generally slow to adopt and accept technological change. Society often looks down on it as "lesser", but we could not exist without it. With the rapid growth of the world's population, the agriculture industry will likely need to increase output by up to 70% in the coming decades [15]. 6G and the technologies it allows for will take a major role in achieving growth. Wireless sensor networks will allow for remote farm monitoring, and AI will be able to make decisions based on the data read by sensors. Farm vehicles such as tractors and combines may be remotely or autonomously controlled, allowing reality to train new farmers and farmhands.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, kindergartens to university were forced to learn remotely, with poor outcomes. 6G will allow for better options when learning through online classmate. Online learning can be enhanced by augmented reality devices and holograms to improve the experience and even a physical classroom [15]. In nonresident education, 6G can also benefit by allowing for rapid communication and response in any emergency such as earthquakes or an active shooter.

In the manufacturing and transportation/logistics industries, 6G should have a massive impact. 6G will allow for extremely low latency factory automation, and sensors will be able to provide real-time updates on parts as they move through the manufacturing process. Process completion and shipment, 6G will aid the logistics sector by improving inventory management, providing opportunities for logistical feasibility analysis, optimizing communications in warehouses, autonomizing forklifts, and potentially even allowing for nearly autonomous delivery truck driving. Other functions such as shipment tracking and real-time delivery updates will likely be improved with 6G.

## 3.7 Terahertz Wireless Systems and Networks with 6G

In the last decade, global mobile data traffic has been dramatically increasing due to increased demand for service, fast and large data transmission rates in broadcasting, IoT, automobiles, smart cities, energy, communication devices. This of course puts pressure on the existing mobile communications systems to meet the new and evolving data rates. There are several ways to improve capacity and data rates, including increasing the bandwidth, which is directly proportional to the data rate. High-frequency bands, or mmWave bands, the main challenges to support massive MIMO (multi-input, multi-output) and mmWave bands include limited available space, significant path loss, and coverage area. 6G communication, which is in the conceptual and development phase, is envisioned to have high data rate capacities as well as ultra-low latency, extreme capacity, high security, and wider coverage. 6G will likely be a combination of sub-6 GHz, mmWave, and THz bands. Some of the key features of 5G to 6G transition are ultra-high data rate applications, immersive holograms, live streaming, ubiquitous coverage area, and massive data rates. Possibilities for antennas include beam steering, switchable phases, and dual-polarized antennas.
