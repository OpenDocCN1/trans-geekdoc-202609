# 5.3.2 Terahertz Communications

The bandwidth can be increased to improve spectral efficiency. Widening bandwidths and enhancing multiple-input, multiple-output (MIMO) are two ways to do this.
5G uses mmWave frequencies to boost data throughput and to open up new possibilities. 6G, on the other hand, wants to stretch the frequency bands to THz to fulfill an even larger demand. The RF band is nearly full, so it is no longer enough to handle the demands of high-density traffic. For 6G, the THz band will be critical. The THz band is envisioned as the next step toward ultra-dense cells with ubiquitous communication. Sixth generation (6G) networks will be made up of terahertz cells with massive communication capabilities. Wireless transmission in terahertz is a particularly appealing and adaptable approach in this regard. The THz spectrum has the electromagnetic family's real frontier. Not only are they less well-known than radio or microwaves, but there are fewer technologies capable of generating and modulating waves.
The terahertz realm is a technical dilemma, apart from spectrometer instruments in astronomy, and it is often alluded to the "terahertz gap." Terahertz technologies are less established and hence more expensive. However, there are compelling reasons why THz is a better option. The advantages of THz include aiming stability, responsiveness to atmospheric turbulence, and eye protection.
