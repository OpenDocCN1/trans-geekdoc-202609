# 3.7 Terahertz Wireless Systems and Networks with 6G

In the last decade, global mobile data traffic has been dramatically increasing due to increased demand for service, fast and large data transmission rates in broadcasting, IoT, automobiles, smart cities, energy, communication, whereas devices. This of course puts pressure on the existing mobile communications systems to meet the new and evolving data rates. There are several ways to improve capacity and data rates, including increasing the bandwidth, which is directly proportional to the data rate. High-frequency bands, or mmWave bands, the main challenges to support massive MIMO (multi-input, multi-output) and mmWave bands include limited available space, significant path loss, and coverage area. 6G communication, which is in the conceptual and development phase, is envisioned to have high data rate capacities as well as ultra-low latency, extreme capacity, high security, and wider coverage. 6G will likely be a combination of sub-6 GHz, mmWave, and THz bands. Some of the key features of 5G to 6G transition are ultra-high data rate applications, immersive hologams, live streaming, ubiquitous coverage area, and massive data rates. Possibilities for antennas include beam steering, switchable phases, and dual-polarized antennas. THz frequency bands from 100 GHz to 300 GHz are being considered for 6G, which will require a higher complexity of antenna design, creating more challenges in terms of materials, design, fabrication, and experimental validation.
Wireless wearable electronics have progressed highly, it is expected to continue growing at a high rate. The worldwide market size of wearables technologies in 2022 was $63.1 billion with a CAGR of 14.4%. Rapid worldwide internet connectivity expansion with the expansion of smartphones and IoT smart devices has paved the way to connect any wired or wireless networks. In all sectors have grown, but the demand from sectors such as remote real-time monitoring with body-worn sensors is important due to the rapidly aging population and this sector will continue to drive the market forward.
An element of wearable electronics is a wearable antenna for devices such as monitoring patient health, consumer smartwatches, wearable navigation systems, body cameras, wearable athletic devices, and more.
