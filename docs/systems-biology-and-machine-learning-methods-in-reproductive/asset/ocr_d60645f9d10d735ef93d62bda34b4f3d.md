# 3.3.1.2 Whole-Exome Sequencing

The exome refers to the portion of the genome that consists of all the exons of protein-coding genes. It makes up around 1–2% of the genome, with the exact percentage varying depending on the species (Warr et al., 2015). Approximately 85% of mutations that cause diseases are found in the exome (Seaby et al. 2016). As a result, whole-exome sequencing (WES) has gained popularity as a more affordable alternative to WGS. WES offers a balance between cost, coverage of the genome, diagnostic success, and interpretability. WES can help to uncover genetic changes that are linked to fertility, infertility, and reproductive disorders. By prioritizing coding sequences, it provides valuable information on uncommon genetic variations, Mendelian disorders, and prospective targets for therapeutic interventions. This contributes to the progress of tailored strategies in reproductive medicine and genetic counseling.
