## 3.3. Discussion

In our study, we have re-implemented the models proposed by Mak and Düz (2019) and enhanced them by scaling their number of filters resulting in an enhanced version of the models. As discussed in the end Section 2.4, the motivation behind enhancements and each model mechanism are well justified. The same goes for the novel model proposed in this research. The experiments have shown that the enhanced version of the original models performs better than the original models. The enhancement is evident in the box plot diagrams of the MSE and MAE results on the test set in Fig. 15. The figure also shows that the proposed model AT-NN performed better than the original and enhanced models, becoming a new state-of-the-art model for estimating sea state parameters from ship motion responses. The excellence of the proposed model based on the attention mechanism is confirmed even by the NSE metric of NSE(AT − NN) = 976.067 · 10⁻³ on the test set (Table 4). If we compare the results on the test set between the best performing original model (which was CNN-REG-ORG with MSE(MLSTM − CNN − ORG) = 3.533 · 10⁻³) to the proposed model (MSE(AT − NN) = 0.457 · 10⁻³), the error is reduced by 3.076 · 10⁻³ which when put in a ratio is near ≈ 7.73 times lower error rate. Also, upon analyzing the radar diagrams presented in Figs. 12 and 13, it was determined that there is no specific range of angles where the suggested model consistently underperforms in estimating the sea state parameters. Furthermore, the introduced uncertainty estimation of the model's sea state parameters prediction raises the trustworthiness of the proposed model to an even higher level, which is essential for developing applications that utilize machine learning models. The next research step would be testing the proposed method on various real-life datasets. We believe that similar results could be achieved with adequate pre-processing of the input data, but it has to be further investigated for different sea state measurements.

Finally, let us conclude our study by clearly pointing out the achievements and novelty introduced in this manuscript, summarized as follows:

- We have retrained the existing state-of-the-art models proposed in Mak and Düz (2019) and enhanced them by increasing their width. The enhanced models performed better than the originally proposed models.
- We have proven that it is possible to obtain similar or better results by only 3-DOF ship motion responses instead of using 6-DOF ship motion responses (sea state parameters' estimation improved by up to 94% in terms of MSE, by up to 74% in terms of MAE, and by up to 80% in terms of NSE when considering all tested methods and sea parameters). Compared to the best-performing original models, the improvement was up to 83%, 62%, and 8% in terms of MSE, MAE, and NSE, respectively.
- As guidelines for model training, we have dug into data augmentation methods based on shuffling input signal slices. The investigation concludes that all tested models achieve better results with shuffled input signals' slices.
- We have proposed a novel AT-NN model that achieved the best results among all tested models for each estimated sea state parameter. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time the AT-NN to be implemented for sea state estimation.
- Furthermore, in this manuscript, we have proposed a novel way of interpreting the uncertainty estimation of NN outputs based on the Monte-Carlo dropout method. The way we interpret the uncertainty of the model's predictions would raise the model's trustworthiness even if its initial prediction achieved a negligible error rate. Also, this is the first time for the uncertainty estimation of the DL models in the sea state estimation, according to the extensive literature review.

## 4. Conclusion

The paper presents a novel attention-based DL model for estimating important sea state characteristics. By evaluating the performance for each sea state parameter, we confirm that the AT-NN model is fine-tuned and suitable for estimating key sea state parameters. The improvement made by the proposed AT-NN model proved significant as MSE was reduced by up to 83%, MAE by up to 62%, and NSE by up to 8% compared to the best versions of the original, formerly proposed state-of-the-art models considering all estimated sea state parameters. When compared to best versions of modified models, MSE was reduced by up to 13%, MAE by up to 12%, and NSE by up to 0.4%. Despite using 3-DOF input motion data, the proposed model achieved the best results among all tested, original and enhanced models for each estimated sea state parameter. As one of the next steps in future research, the utilization of 6-DOF ship motion responses could potentially improve the precision of parameter estimation. Additionally, in practical applications, entire wave directional spectrum estimation is of great importance. Therefore, future work directions may also include information about wave directionality in numerical simulations that can extend the application of the DL model to short-crested waves and combined wind and swell sea spectrums.
