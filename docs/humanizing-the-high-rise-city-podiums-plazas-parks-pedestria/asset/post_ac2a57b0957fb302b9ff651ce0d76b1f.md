
## 12 Greener-Covered Tall Buildings

People demand reconnecting with nature for multiple reasons, including massive urbanization, health problems, energy crises, artificial digital proliferation and increases in screen time, climate change and resilience, environmental and air quality degradation, the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, and poor aesthetics and urban design. Densely populated cities are often characterized by high stress levels and noise pollution from traffic, construction, and other urban activities. Prolonged exposure to constant noise can lead to irritability and difficulty in relaxation and concentration. The constant presence of large crowds and congestion in urban areas can create a sense of claustrophobia and anxiety. Busy streets, crowded public transportation, and packed public places can contribute to feeling overwhelmed (Al-Kodmany, 2023).

Long periods of electronic device use have further increased the demand for reconnecting with nature. As modern life progressively revolves around electronic devices, such as smartphones, computers, and tablets, finding ways to disconnect from the digital world and reconnect with the natural world has become essential. Nature provides a respite from the constant stimuli of electronic devices and offers an opportunity to relax, unwind, and refresh mentally and physically. Outdoor activities like strolling through a park or relaxing in a green area can aid in lowering stress levels, elevating mood, and advancing general well-being. Nature’s calming effects can counteract the potential negative impacts of prolonged screen time and electronic device use (Boeri & Insuza, 2010).

Urban areas are often plagued by poor air quality, increasing the demand to integrate nature into urban living. The concentration of human activities, industries, transportation, and construction in urban settings leads to the emission of pollutants that can negatively impact air quality. Manufacturing plants and industrial facilities in urban areas release various pollutants into the atmosphere, including sulfur dioxide (SO$_{2}$), carbon dioxide (CO$_{2}$), and other hazardous substances. Poor air quality can harm public health, leading to respiratory, cardiovascular, and other health complications. Buildings are significant sources of CO$_{2}$ emissions in urban environments. The energy used for lighting, cooling, heating, and operating appliances in residential, commercial, and institutional buildings often relies heavily on fossil fuels. The combustion of these fossil fuels, such as coal, natural gas, and oil, contributes to the greenhouse effect and climate change by releasing CO$_{2}$ into the atmosphere (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Yang & Chen, 2020).

Greener-covered towers are a new generation of tall buildings characterized by substantial integration of vegetation. They aim to improve the quality of urban life by reducing air pollution, enhancing biodiversity, creating microclimates, and providing aesthetic and psychological benefits. Vegetation-clad towers are vital in humanizing skyscrapers by providing proximity to nature and its related advantages for the individuals residing or working in these edifices.

Here are some ways in which greener-covered towers contribute to humanizing tall buildings (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Boeri & Insuza, 2010; Guo et al., 2022; Yang & Chen, 2020):

- **Connection to Nature:** By default, tall buildings communicate a feeling of detachment from the natural environment. Consequently, greener-covered towers help bridge this gap by introducing living plants and vegetation, creating a visual and sensory connection to nature. Vegetations provide a calming and refreshing ambience, enhancing well-being and reducing the sense of being disconnected from the natural world.
- **Improved Aesthetics:** Adding greener softens the appearance of tall buildings, making them more visually appealing and harmonious with their surroundings. The vibrant colors, textures, and shapes of plants and foliage create a visually stimulating environment that can enhance the quality of life for building occupants and passersby.
- **Health and Well-being Benefits:** Vegetation in greener-covered towers brings numerous health benefits. Research has shown that exposure to green spaces can reduce stress, improve mood, and enhance overall well-being. The sight of greener and the potential for increased natural light and better air quality can contribute to a healthier and more pleasant indoor environment.
- **Noise Reduction:** Vegetation in greener-covered towers can help mitigate noise pollution from the surrounding urban environment. Plants act as natural sound absorbers, reducing noise levels and creating a quieter and more peaceful atmosphere inside the building. This can improve the quality of life for occupants and contribute to a more comfortable living or working environment.
- **Thermal Comfort:** Greener-covered towers can help regulate indoor temperatures by providing natural insulation. The plants and foliage on the building's exterior help reduce heat gain in the summer and heat loss in the winter, contributing to energy efficiency and creating a more comfortable indoor climate. This can improve thermal comfort and reduce reliance on heating and cooling systems.
