They transform into vibrant cultural hubs, acting as focal points for numerous major events and large gatherings that bring communities together and become dynamic spaces for cultural expression, fostering a rich social tapestry within the context of high-rise living. This dual function, both as a social nexus and a cultural hub, underscores the multifaceted significance of expansive green spaces within the fabric of high-rise cities (Hussain et al., 2010; Parakh et al., 2017).

Green spaces are pivotal in enhancing the urban fabric by introducing biodiversity and fostering miniature ecosystems, contributing to environmental sustainability within urban landscapes. Beyond their aesthetic appeal, these verdant expanses serve as vital reservoirs of biodiversity, providing habitats for various plant and animal species. The interconnected web of life within these green areas contributes to ecological balance, preserving local flora and fauna. Additionally, introducing greenery in urban environments mitigates climate change, reduces urban heat island effects, and improves air quality, acting as a natural filter for pollutants and enhancing overall environmental health. Integrating biodiversity into the urban fabric enriches the visual appeal of high-rise living and plays a crucial role in creating sustainable and harmonious cityscapes (Giffordy, 2006; Peters, 2010).

The following case studies highlight the roles of public parks in humanizing the high-rise city.

# BRYANT PARK, NEW YORK CITY

Bryant Park, a renowned public park in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, was established in 1686 and named after William Cullen Bryant, an acclaimed American poet and editor. It spans approximately 6.5 acres and offers diverse amenities and activities. It is situated between 40th and 42nd Streets and Fifth and Sixth Avenues near famous landmarks such as Times Square and the New York Public Library (Chapter 4). Its central location, adjacent to subway stations, makes it easily accessible and a convenient destination for residents and visitors (Tate, 2015; Bryant Park).

Numerous welcoming features at Bryant Park make it an excellent place for people to relax and have fun. Due to its location in Midtown Manhattan and its abundance of verdant vegetation, the park is called a “green oasis.” In the middle of the city’s hectic streets and towering skyscrapers, it offers a welcome escape to a more tranquil setting. Attracting both locals and tourists, it is a lively community gathering spot. There is plenty of room for people to gather, unwind, and mingle on the park’s main grass, in the seating areas, and at the outdoor tables. The open and friendly environment fosters a sense of community and belonging (Manshel, 2020).
