During the Southern Song Dynasty, Hangzhou peaked, becoming a prosperous and culturally vibrant city known for its beautiful West Lake. The city's West Lake, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, has inspired poets, scholars, and artists for centuries. During the Ming and Qing dynasties, Hangzhou thrived as a significant economic and cultural center in southeastern China. The city has also attracted foreign travelers and traders, with Marco Polo describing it as “the most splendid city in the world” (Liu et al., 2011).

Today, Hangzhou stands as a thriving economic and technological hub, characterized by a striking skyline adorned with significant tall buildings and iconic structures that mirror the city's modern ambitions. Renowned for more than its picturesque landscapes and cultural heritage, Hangzhou has become a symbol of economic prosperity and innovation. The cityscape is punctuated by skyscrapers that house bustling financial districts, embodying the economic vitality and technological prowess that define Hangzhou’s contemporary identity. Amid this urban expanse, the city has successfully preserved its rich cultural heritage, providing a unique juxtaposition of ancient traditions and cutting-edge modernity. The coexistence of historic landmarks, such as the iconic West Lake, with state-of-the-art architectural marvels is a testament to Hangzhou’s ability to seamlessly blend tradition and progress. Additionally, the vibrant sociocultural and sports life in Hangzhou adds another layer to its allure, with a dynamic fusion of local traditions, global influences, and a thriving community that contributes to the city’s multifaceted charm (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Liu et al., 2011).

# HANGZHOU QIANJIANG CBD SCULPTURE PARK

Hangzhou Qianjiang CBD Sculpture Park is a dynamic public park in Hangzhou, China, connecting iconic buildings like InterContinental Hangzhou, Hangzhou Grand Theater, and Hangzhou Civic Center while showcasing sculptural quality on a mega scale, complementing the park’s outstanding aesthetics.

# HANGZHOU CIVIC CENTER

Hangzhou Civic Center in Hangzhou, China, consists of six trapezoidal 26-floor towers connected via five 85-meter (279-foot) sky bridges. They cluster around a central public garden surrounded by four low-rise podiums. Initially, the client wanted to build a single supertall building of 300 meters (984 feet). However, the project architect persuaded the client to replace that with multiple shorter connected towers. This arrangement of shorter buildings has fostered a more human scale while functioning as the visual terminus to a pedestrian axis, making the complex a unique landmark in the city. A pair of sister buildings also flank the Hangzhou Civic Center, the Hangzhou International Convention Center (The Sun), and the Hangzhou Grand Theater (The Moon). The project was designed by Tongii Architectural Design Group and completed in 2012 (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Parker & Wood, 2013).
