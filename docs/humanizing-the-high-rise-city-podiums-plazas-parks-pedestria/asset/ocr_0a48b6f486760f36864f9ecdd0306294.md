# One North Wacker, Chicago [2002]

One North Wacker, formerly UBS Tower, is a 50-story, 199-meter (653-foot)- tall skyscraper in Chicago’s Loop. Completed in 2002, the building was designed when Postmodernist architectural values shifted to contemporary styles. The building provides 163,000 square meters (1,754,000 square feet) of office space and amenities, including a conference center, health club, and a two-level underground garage (Goettsch, 2012). Designed by architectural firm Goettsch Partners, the building was sold for the highest per-square-foot price of any down- town Chicago office building since the late 1980s.

The podium design of One North Wacker showcases a careful considera- tion of transparency, aesthetics, and security, contributing to a dynamic and integrated urban experience. A highly transparent, cable-supported enclosure, constructed with low-iron glass and a non-reflecting coating, is featured at the building’s base. This design creates a seamless visual connection between the exterior and interior spaces, effectively integrating the lobby into the sur- rounding streetscape. The transparency of the base invites natural light into the lobby, fostering an open and inviting atmosphere within (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Goettsch, 2012).

To enhance the overall quality and sophistication of the building, high-quality finishing materials were employed indoors and outdoors. The lobby, elevated to four stories, stands as a grand and welcoming space. Its height contributes to a sense of openness and spatial continuity, further blurring the distinction between the interior and exterior environments.

The building’s entrance is marked by a gesture of hospitality, featuring cir- cular granite seating spaces on the east and south sides of the outdoor plaza. These seating spaces provide a place for visitors to rest and engage with the urban surroundings and serve a dual purpose as planters while fulfilling security requirements mandated for skyscrapers post-9/11. Incorporating these planters seamlessly integrates security measures with landscape design, ensuring a safe and pleasant experience for occupants and visitors alike.

With its circular seating and planters, the outdoor plaza becomes a func- tional and aesthetically pleasing extension of the building’s interior spaces. This intentional blending of interior and exterior elements enhances the overall con- nectivity of the building with its urban context, creating a lively and engaging environment for both occupants and passersby.

In summary, the building’s base features a highly transparent, cable- supported enclosure with low-iron glass and a non-reflecting coating. This transparent base makes exterior spaces blend with interior spaces, allowing the lobby to become part of the streetscape. High-quality finishing materials were used indoors and outdoors, and the lobby’s height was elevated to four stories.
