# **Areas for Improvement**

+   - **Limited Shade:** The plaza lacks substantial shading elements, making it uncomfortable during hot weather and limiting its usability.

+   - **Weather Dependency:** The lack of covered areas or weather protection can restrict the plaza's use during rain or extreme temperatures.

+   - **Intense Urban Activity:** The high foot traffic and frequent events can create a crowded and noisy atmosphere, detracting from its tranquility.

+   - **Maintenance Challenges:** Maintaining the open space and public amenities might be challenging due to heavy use and various activities.

+   - **Retail and Amenities:** Limited options for food, beverages, and amenities might reduce the convenience and comfort of plaza visitors.

# CHASE TOWER PLAZA, CHICAGO

Chase Tower Plaza (the Exelon Plaza) is a public space in the heart of Chicago, surrounded by some of the city's most iconic landmarks. It covers an area of approximately 1.5 acres and features a large fountain, seating areas, and a variety of trees and plants. The plaza is a popular spot for office workers to take a break or have lunch, and it also hosts events throughout the year, such as concerts and farmers' markets. It is a vibrant and inviting public space that adds to the character of downtown Chicago. The plaza is home to Chase Tower, a 60-story skyscraper completed in 1969 and the tallest building inside the Chicago "L" (short for elevated) Loop. Chase Tower has a distinctive curved shape and houses the headquarters of Chase Bank and Exelon, among other tenants (Al-Kodmany and Ali, 2013; Al-Kodmany, 2017; Kamin, 2022; Saliga & Clarke, 1998).

The plaza features a jet fountain, a clock tower, and a colorful ceramic mural by Marc Chagall called Four Seasons. One of the most eye-catching features of the plaza is the fountain, which occupies a central position. The fountain comprises a level basin with nine square sections, each with a water jet that shoots to different heights. The basin is raised above the ground and can be seen from the street even when the water is not running. When the water is turned on, it creates a dynamic and captivating spectacle that attracts the attention of the pedestrians who walk by. The fountain has a wide rim surrounding the basin, holding a thin water layer flowing gently over the edge. This water layer is separated from the deeper basin by a metal lip that prevents the water from spilling over. This design discourages visitors from sitting on the rim or entering the fountain but allows them to interact with water by touching or splashing it lightly. The fountain is a source of delight and relaxation for the visitors and a symbol of the plaza's elegance and vitality. Being inside the Chase Plaza can be a truly special experience, given how the space is separated from the city and how the surrounding skyscrapers fade into the background (Figure 3.11). The wall of the sunken level moves to the forefront, creating a human-scale environment (Pridmore & Larson, 2005).
