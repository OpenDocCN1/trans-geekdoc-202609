The tower’s podium design is characterized by its spacious and inviting layout, creating a welcoming environment for visitors. The architectural design of the entryway and canopy underscores the tower’s significance as both a landmark and a gateway to the complex. The entryway is generously proportioned. Its architectural elements are carefully crafted to convey a sense of grandeur and importance, setting the tone for the tower’s role in the urban landscape (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Binder, 2015).

A noteworthy feature is the striking intricate canopy that extends from the tower’s facade over the entrance. This canopy serves multiple purposes—it adds a distinctive visual element to the tower’s exterior, provides shading for those entering or exiting the building, and acts as a rainwater collector, contributing to the tower’s environmentally conscious design.

In summary, the entrance of the China World Trade Tower is not only architecturally significant but also designed with hospitality in mind. Its spacious, well-designed layout and striking canopy contribute to an overall inviting atmosphere, making it a prominent and welcoming feature within the urban context of Beijing (Figure 2.24).

# *International Commerce Center; Hong Kong [2010]*

Designed by Kohn Pedersen Fox (KPF) Associates and rising to 484 meters (1,588 feet) with 118 floors, the International Commerce Center (ICC) tower is the tallest building in Hong Kong. Completed in 2010, the tower occupies the southwest corner of the Union Square complex and is owned by Hong Kong MTR Corporation Ltd. and Sun Hung Kai Properties. It contains commercial office space and a five-star Ritz-Carlton Hong Kong hotel, which occupies the highest floors of the building (floors 102 to 118).
