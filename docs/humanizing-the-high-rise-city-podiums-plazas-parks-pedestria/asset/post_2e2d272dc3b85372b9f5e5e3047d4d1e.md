
## City-Wide Parks

In summary, Shenzhen Central Park is a vital urban refuge, offering a welcome respite within the confines of a bustling, high-rise cityscape. Its expansive green spaces serve as a sanctuary for relaxation and recreation and cultivate cultural engagement. Residents and visitors find solace in the park’s natural beauty, enjoying tranquility and leisure amid the dynamic urban environment. By seamlessly blending recreational facilities with cultural elements, Central Park has become a versatile haven that significantly contributes to the enhanced well-being and overall quality of life of the diverse community it serves in Shenzhen. Nevertheless, proper pathways, signage, biodiversity preservation, and effective management and governance are crucial for the park’s growth and success, ensuring its sustainability and avoiding negative impacts on local wildlife.

## GARDENS BY THE BAY, SINGAPORE

Gardens by the Bay, an expansive nature park in Singapore, sprawls over 101 hectares (250 acres) of meticulously reclaimed land along the picturesque Marina Reservoir. This iconic destination is distinguished by its triad of waterfront gardens, seamlessly integrating horticulture, architecture, and sustainability to create a truly immersive experience. Bay South, the most extensive garden, showcases a breathtaking array of plants from the Mediterranean and tropical regions, fostering a vibrant and lush landscape. Meanwhile, Bay East provides a serene retreat, offering visitors a peaceful panorama of the city skyline. Bridging the two is Bay Central, which interconnects the gardens through a scenic 3-kilometer promenade, allowing visitors to traverse the breathtaking landscape while enjoying the captivating synthesis of nature and design.

Gardens by the Bay has a dual mission: to highlight exceptional horticulture, sustainability, and architectural design in the context of a tropical city while also serving as an educational platform to underscore the significance of environmental conservation (Rowe et al., 2019). Under the auspices of Singapore’s National Park Board, this visionary project was the collaborative brainchild of two distinguished firms: Grant Associates, renowned for its expertise in landscape architecture, and Wilkinson Eyré Architects, a firm specializing in innovative architectural design. Together, their collective vision has brought forth a botanical oasis that not only captivates with its aesthetic brilliance but also serves as a catalyst for environmental awareness and conservation education. The seamless integration of horticulture, sustainability, and architecture in the Gardens by the Bay is a testament to Singapore’s commitment to pushing the boundaries of urban green spaces (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Flannery & Smith, 2015; Leet, 2013).

The Gardens feature a myriad of attractions that blend nature and innovation seamlessly. The Flower Dome, one of the largest glass greenhouses globally, presents a cool dry biome, showcasing diverse plants. Meanwhile, with its cool moist biome, the Cloud Forest houses an impressive 35-meter-tall indoor waterfall, creating a captivating atmosphere. The Supertree Grove, comprising 18 vertical gardens, transforms into a mesmerizing spectacle illuminated at night, adding a touch of enchantment. The OCBC Skyway, a 128-meter aerial walkway, offers visitors a breathtaking panoramic view of the gardens, providing a unique perspective on this harmonious marriage of nature and cutting-edge design. Together, these attractions embody Singapore's commitment to sustainability, creating an immersive experience that educates and captivates visitors worldwide.

Here are some details of key features and characteristics of Gardens by the Bay (Flannery & Smith, 2015; Leet, 2013):

### SUPERTREES

Gardens by the Bay's Supertrees, soaring 25–50 meters, are a natural and technological marvel, featuring over 200 plant species in a breathtaking vertical garden setting. Their towering structures serve a dual purpose as breathtaking artistic installations and innovative environmental engines. These colossal structures act as rainwater collectors, efficiently harnessing and channeling rainwater to sustain the park's verdant landscapes. Integrating solar photovoltaic cells into their design, the Supertrees contribute to sustainable energy generation, illuminating the gardens with a spectacular display of lights during the evening (Gardens by the Bay).

Beyond their ecological functions, some Supertrees are interconnected by the OCBC Skyway, a 128-meter-long aerial walkway suspended 22 meters above the ground. This skywalk offers visitors an immersive experience, allowing them to traverse amid the towering Supertrees while enjoying panoramic views of the meticulously landscaped gardens below and the dynamic cityscape beyond the horizon. The OCBC Skyway, with its stunning vantage points and structural elegance, offers a unique perspective on the harmonious coexistence of nature and urbanity, making it an integral and enchanting part of the Gardens by the Bay experience. Perched atop the tallest “tree” in the SuperTree Grove, Supertree by IndoChine stands out as the exclusive “SuperTree” featuring dining and bar facilities (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Gardens by the Bay).

### FLOWER DOME

Gardens by the Bay boasts two extraordinary conservatories, with the Flower Dome epitomizing botanical grandeur. As one of the world's largest glass greenhouses, it sprawls across 1.2 hectares, reaching an impressive height of 38 meters. The Flower Dome showcases an array of plants originating from the Mediterranean and semi-arid subtropical regions. Its permanent exhibits provide a consistent display of botanical wonders adapted to these climates. Additionally, the Flower Dome offers an engaging experience by incorporating changing displays in the flower fields. These dynamic presentations are curated to reflect the evolving seasons and various festivals, ensuring that visitors can enjoy a diverse and seasonally appropriate collection of flowers and plants throughout the year. This approach adds a layer of vibrancy and excitement to the botanical experience, allowing patrons to witness the beauty of different floral arrangements and thematic displays as they explore the Flower Dome (Gardens by the Bay).

The Flower Dome is not merely a testament to botanical diversity; it is also a living museum of natural wonders. Within its enchanting confines, visitors encounter a 1,000-year-old olive tree, hailing from the sun-kissed landscapes of Spain (brought to Singapore from Spain in 2011), and a towering 20-meter baobab tree, a majestic representative of Madagascar’s rich flora. As a harmonious fusion of architecture and nature, the Flower Dome at Gardens by the Bay provides an immersive journey through diverse ecosystems, offering a feast for the senses and a testament to the intricate beauty of the world’s botanical tapestry.

### CLOUD FOREST

Nestled within Gardens by the Bay, the Cloud Forest Conservatory stands as a testament to the marvels of nature, offering visitors an immersive journey into a tropical montane environment. At the heart of this botanical wonder lies a breathtaking 35-meter-tall mountain shrouded in verdant vegetation and adorned with an exquisite array of exotic flora. At the pinnacle of this artificial summit is a majestic waterfall (one of the world’s tallest indoor waterfalls) cascading down the mountain slopes, creating a sensory spectacle that captivates the imagination. As visitors enter the conservatory, they find themselves misted, creating an ethereal ambiance reminiscent of a cloud-kissed mountaintop. The Cloud Forest is a visual and educational haven, showcasing plants that thrive without soil, including orchids, ferns, mosses, and carnivorous species (Gardens by the Bay).

Educational exhibits in the Cloud Forest highlight the importance of freshwater resources and the ecological significance of cloud forests, providing visitors with insights into their delicate balance and threats. The Cloud Forest thus serves as a poignant reminder of the urgent need for biodiversity conservation and preserving fragile ecosystems. In alignment with the insights of Davey (2011), this conservatory becomes a platform for raising awareness about environmental challenges and the imperative to protect the rich tapestry of life that thrives within the cloud forests, inspiring a collective commitment to sustainable practices and the stewardship of our planet (Gardens by the Bay).
