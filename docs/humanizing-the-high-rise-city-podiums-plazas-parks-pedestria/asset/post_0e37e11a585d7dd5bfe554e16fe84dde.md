## Salesforce Transit Center, San Francisco

Salesforce Transit Center, formerly the Transbay Terminal, is a central transportation hub in downtown San Francisco, California. Designed by Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects, the modern, architecturally striking building was completed in 2018. It serves as a central hub for various public transportation modes, including buses, regional and intercity buses, trains, and future high-speed rail services.

Located in the South of Market neighborhood, it is primarily designed to accommodate local, regional, and intercity bus lines and serves as a central terminal for several bus agencies. The center offers a range of amenities and facilities, including a spacious bus terminal, retail spaces, restaurants, public art installations, a rooftop park called Salesforce Park, and a pedestrian bridge connecting neighboring areas. The Salesforce Transit Center is about 440 meters long and 165 meters wide (Al-Kodmany, 2022; Salesforce Transit Center, Schorung, 2019).

Based on the transit center's rooftop, Salesforce Park is a 5.4-acre public park with landscaped gardens, walking paths, plazas, and various seating areas spread over four blocks. One of the defining characteristics of Salesforce Park is its lush greenery and extensive landscaping. Situated 70 feet above the grand hall, the park has 16,000 plants arranged in 13 different botanical feature areas (ecosystems), 600 trees, and a linear fountain, creating a serene and inviting atmosphere. The tree canopy provides shade and helps mitigate the urban heat island effect, making the park a refreshing escape from the surrounding cityscape. PVVP landscape architects designed the park (Al-Kodmany, 2018a; Salesforce Transit Center).

Salesforce Park is a multifaceted park, including interconnected gardens and communal areas, providing a range of experiences for visitors. The prominent sections encompass the Main Plaza, Amphitheater, Bus Fountain, Children's Play Area, Walking Trail, and The Gondola, which offers sweeping vistas over the park and the adjacent city, as follows (Al-Kodmany, 2018a, 2018b; Salesforce Transit Center; Schorung, 2019).

- **Main Plaza.** The central square of Salesforce Park has an oculus encircled by a cluster of bamboo trees. This expansive area is an ideal place for individuals to gather and engage in recreational activities. Moreover, visitors have the unique opportunity to stroll on the largest glass floor in the United States. After sunset, the main plaza organizes exclusive activities, including a music series and captivating fairytales. Throughout the day, there are engaging sessions of storytelling led by accomplished literary artists, invigorating exercise courses, and entertaining activities specifically designed for children.

- **Amphitheater.** Situated near the westernmost part of the park, this amphitheater can accommodate up to 1,000 individuals on its verdant lawn. Additionally, it serves as an excellent venue for a diverse range of activities, such as complimentary yoga sessions, live performances, and outdoor film screenings beneath the night sky.

- **Bus Fountain.** An engaging aspect of the park is its interactive permanent public artwork, a fountain designed expressively to synchronize with the arrivals and departures of the buses at the terminal below. This contemporary creation is constructed using almost 1,200 feet of white and gray granite, making it one of the longest water artworks in the world.

- **Children's Play Area.** Situated amid the center lawn and the main plaza lies a designated children's play area, showcasing a climbing rope structure on a cushioned, safe surface. Following their energetic play in the play structure, children can borrow a book from the Children's Reading Room for spontaneous storytelling or select a board game from the game cart.

- **Walking Trail.** The park's perimeter is a walking route extending for 0.5 miles. The path features 13 compact botanical gardens showcasing diverse flora and wildlife from various global areas.

- **The Gondola.** This ascent to a rooftop park, while not the usual elevator or escalator experience, undoubtedly offers a more scenic and picturesque journey.

Salesforce Park hosts cultural and community events, including live music performances, art installations, and wellness activities, attracting residents and visitors. Accessibility is a crucial feature, with multiple entrances and elevators for easy access, including for those with mobility challenges. The park is integrated with the surrounding transportation infrastructure, connecting with the Salesforce Transit Center and nearby public transit options, making it easily accessible to individuals from all over the city.

Salesforce Park incorporates sustainable design elements, such as energy-efficient lighting, eco-friendly technologies, and a rainwater collecting system, aligning with the city's environmental goals. Salesforce Park manages 67% of stormwater runoff annually, providing habitat for 47 bird species and reducing energy costs by 36,100 kWh annually. It attracts an average of 1,067 weekday and 917 weekend visitors and hosts over 30 classes and events annually (Salesforce Transit Center; Dvorak & Drennan, 2021). The park improves mood, supports health and well-being, and enhances educational opportunities.

Visitors report feeling happy and learning something while visiting, with 68% stopping for 33 seconds to read interpretive signs. The park also reduces noise levels by .06 to 7.65 decibels compared to the street level, reducing the sounds of the city. Additionally, the park contributes to an assessed property value of $51,000 higher on average or $40 more per square foot for condos with views of Salesforce Park compared to similarly sized condos overlooking the street (Al-Kodmany, 2022; Dvorak & Drennan, 2021). Overall, Salesforce Park is a significant environmental and social asset to the neighborhood and the city of San Francisco (Figure 11.4).

Nevertheless, Salesforce Park faces some challenges. The park's innovative architecture, lush vegetation, and eco-friendly features come at a high cost, which may burden the public with increased taxes or fees. The park's greenery and landscaping also demand constant maintenance and care to ensure the vitality and beauty of the trees, plants, and grass. The park's small active recreation area, absence of large open spaces, and central location can result in less usability, making it less appealing. Moreover, the park provides little sheltered areas; therefore, inclement weather, such as rain or strong winds, can limit the enjoyment of the park and restrict activities (Dvorak & Drennan, 2021).

## 20 Fenchurch Street, London

20 Fenchurch Street is a skyscraper located in the financial district of London, United Kingdom. It was completed in 2014 and stands at a height of 160 meters (525 feet) with 37 floors. The building was designed by Uruguayan architect Rafael Viñoly and developed by Land Securities and Canary Wharf Group. It is primarily an office building home to various businesses and organizations. The building was nicknamed "the Walkie-Talkie" due to its concave structure and reflective glass exterior, contributing to its distinct appearance on the London skyline. The building offers panoramic city views from its top floors and has become a popular tourist attraction (Viñoly et al., 2015).
