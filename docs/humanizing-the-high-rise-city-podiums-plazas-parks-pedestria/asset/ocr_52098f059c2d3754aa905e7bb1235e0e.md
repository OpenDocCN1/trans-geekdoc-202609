Kupcinet began in 1935 as a sportswriter for the Chicago Daily Times, later known as the Chicago Sun-Times. After eight years, the newspaper introduced “Kup’s Column,” which achieved a remarkable 60-year run. Rick Kogan, a Tribune reporter, emphasized that proving one’s identity in Chicago required having their name in boldface in Kup’s column. The statue portrays him clutching a Sun-Times final edition beneath his right arm while gesturing towards the former site of the newspaper, which is now the Trump Tower. The statue’s installation on Wacker Drive near the Chicago Riverwalk serves as a reminder of the city’s rich history and the individuals who have played a significant role in shaping its cultural fabric. Mayor Richard M. Daley feared that the passing of Irv Kupcinet would create an emptiness in Chicago, as he was strongly associated with the city, like the Picasso sculpture, Hancock Building, and Sears Tower. The bronze material used for the sculpture adds a timeless quality, reflecting the enduring legacy of Irv Kupcinet’s journalistic career (Kupcinet, 2012).

# FLAMINGO SCULPTURE, CHICAGO

The Flamingo Sculpture in Chicago is an engaging and captivating artwork that captures the attention and imagination of viewers. Created by the renowned American artist Alexander Calder (1898–1976), this sculpture stands as a prominent feature in the Federal Plaza, captivating passersby with its striking design and vibrant presence. The Flamingo Sculpture’s engaging nature is evident in its unique form and composition. Calder’s artistic vision comes to life through the sculpture’s dynamic lines, curves, and bright red color (Figure 14.4). The abstract representation of a flamingo evokes a sense of movement and vitality, inviting viewers to interpret and engage with the artwork from different perspectives. Situated in a prominent location in front of the Klucznyski Federal Building, the Flamingo Sculpture serves as a visual anchor in the urban landscape of Chicago. Its towering height (53-ft [16 m] tall) and commanding presence create a sense of awe and curiosity, drawing people in to explore its intricacies and appreciate its artistic craftsmanship. As visitors interact with the Flamingo Sculpture, they are encouraged to contemplate its form, engage with its vibrant colors, and consider the interplay between art and the surrounding architecture. The sculpture introduces a captivating juxtaposition to Mies van der Rohe’s architectural design, known for its disciplined orthogonal lines and dark facades. Mies van der Rohe, a pioneer of modernist architecture, was renowned for his minimalist approach, emphasizing clean lines and a restrained color palette. In contrast, the sculpture injects a dynamic and vibrant palette into the surroundings. This iconic artwork, commissioned by the United States General Services Administration, has been a prominent fixture since its unveiling in 1974 (Marter, 1979).
