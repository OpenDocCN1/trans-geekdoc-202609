# PARKLETS

Parklets, small public spaces born from the transformation of curbside parking spaces or medians, emerge as vibrant urban oases. These green havens, with seating, landscaping, and diverse amenities, invite pedestrians to pause, socialize, and relish the outdoors. As welcoming respites within the bustling urban fabric, parklets contribute to community building and sustainable transportation advocacy. Figure 13.12 captures the essence of these spaces, showcasing their integration into the urban landscape of downtown Chicago. Beyond functionality, parklets boast designs that elevate street aesthetics, enhancing the overall appeal of their surroundings. As testimonials to urban ingenuity, parklets exemplify the urban planners’ commitment to creating inclusive, green, and socially engaging environments, fostering a harmonious coexistence between the urban dweller and their surroundings (Litke, 2016; Koue, 2013).

*Key Takeaways: Planning and Design Principles*
When integrating pedestrian networks, promenades, and streetscapes in a high-rise city, several planning and design principles should be considered to create vibrant, accessible, and people-centric urban environments. Here are fundamental principles to keep in mind:

+   1. **Walkability and Accessibility:**
* Prioritize walkability by designing pedestrian-friendly infrastructure that encourages active transportation.
* Ensure safe, comfortable, and accessible pathways for pedestrians of all ages and abilities.
* Minimize barriers like traffic congestion, road crossings, and physical obstructions to create a seamless and enjoyable walking experience.
