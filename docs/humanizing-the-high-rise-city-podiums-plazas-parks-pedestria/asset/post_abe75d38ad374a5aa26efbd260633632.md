Times Square stands as one of the most accessible and well-connected hubs in New York City. Renowned for its centrality, the square is easily reachable by various modes of transportation. The convergence of multiple subway lines and trains ensures seamless subway access, linking Times Square to different boroughs and neighborhoods. Numerous bus routes further contribute to the area's accessibility. For those preferring above-ground transportation, the square offers ample taxi services and is a popular location for ride-sharing services. Additionally, pedestrians find the square highly walkable, with wide sidewalks and pedestrian plazas providing a leisurely and enjoyable stroll through the heart of this iconic destination. The accessibility of Times Square makes it a focal point for New Yorkers and a welcoming space for visitors from around the world (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Times Square).

## HIGHLIGHTS

### Positive Aspects

- **Pedestrian Emphasis**: The redesign of Times Square prioritizes pedestrians over vehicular traffic. By limiting cars and creating expansive pedestrian zones, the square encourages people to explore on foot. This design choice fosters a sense of connection with the urban environment, allowing individuals to engage with the city on a human scale.

- **Public Gathering Spaces:** Times Square is a social hub where people can gather, linger, and interact. The abundance of public seating, including steps and chairs, provides spaces for relaxation and people-watching. This communal aspect humanizes the urban experience, transforming a busy intersection into a place for shared activities.

- **Cultural and Artistic Expression:** The presence of iconic billboards, digital displays, and public art installations infuses Times Square with creativity and cultural vibrancy. These elements contribute to the visual appeal and create a dynamic environment that reflects the diverse interests and expressions of the city's inhabitants.

- **Accessibility and Inclusivity:** The design of Times Square prioritizes accessibility, making it a space that is welcoming to people of all ages and abilities. Ramps, elevators, and other features ensure that everyone can enjoy and participate in the activities in the square, fostering inclusivity.

- **Nighttime Ambiance:** The strategic use of lighting, including the dazzling electronic billboards, transforms Times Square into a mesmerizing spectacle, especially at night. This illumination enhances the visual appeal and contributes to a sense of safety and energy, making the urban environment more inviting after dark.

- **Celebration of Events:** Times Square is a venue for various events and celebrations, most notably the iconic New Year's Eve ball drop. These events bring people together for shared experiences, fostering a sense of community and connection in the city's heart.

### Areas for Improvement

- **Commercialization Over Community Focus:** The heavy emphasis on commercial advertising and consumerism in Times Square may overshadow its potential as a community-focused space. The pervasive presence of billboards and advertisements might prioritize commercial interests over creating a space that caters to the diverse needs and interactions of the community.

- **Crowded and Hectic Atmosphere:** The bustling nature of Times Square, especially during peak hours, can create a crowded and hectic atmosphere. This may hinder the creation of a relaxed and comfortable environment for people to engage in meaningful social interactions. The constant movement and noise might contribute to chaos rather than community.

- **Lack of Green Spaces:** Times Square is predominantly paved and lacks green spaces or natural elements. The absence of greenery may limit opportunities for relaxation and connection with nature, which can contribute significantly to the humanization of urban spaces.

- **Transient Nature of Visitors:** Many visitors to Times Square are tourists or individuals passing through rather than a consistent local community. This transient nature might limit the development of a robust local community that fosters a sense of belonging and shared identity, crucial for humanizing urban spaces.

- **Limited Local Programming:** The events and programming in Times Square often cater to a broad audience, and there may be a lack of localized and community-specific activities. Increasing emphasis on local programming might bolster community cohesion and foster greater resident engagement in defining the cultural landscape of the region.

In conclusion, Times Square serves as a testament to the idea that even amid towering skyscrapers, urban spaces can be designed to prioritize human experience. It is an example of creating functional and enriching environments, offering residents and visitors a place to connect, celebrate, and embrace the human side of the high-rise cityscape. However, while Times Square is a globally recognized and lively urban space, addressing aspects such as commercial dominance, crowd management, green spaces, community engagement, and local programming could contribute to a more balanced and humanized urban experience. A thoughtful reevaluation of these elements could enhance Times Square’s role as a genuinely inclusive and community-centric space within the high-rise cityscape (Banerjee, 2001; Berman, 2009; Sagaly, 2023b).

## EAST NANJING ROAD, SHANGHAI

East Nanjing Road (also known as Nanjing Road East) is one of Shanghai’s most famous and bustling commercial pedestrian streets. It is in the Huangpu District and stretches for approximately 1 mile (1.6 kilometers) from the Bund in the east to People’s Square in the west (See Chapter 5). East Nanjing Road is renowned for its vibrant atmosphere, diverse shopping options, cultural landmarks, and architectural charm. It is primarily a pedestrian street catering to the needs and preferences of walkers. Vehicular traffic is limited or prohibited, allowing pedestrians to freely explore the street and its numerous attractions (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Falkus, 2001).

East Nanjing Pedestrian Street is easily accessible by public transportation, including metro lines and buses. It is well connected to other parts of the city, allowing visitors to conveniently reach and explore the street (Istrate & Chen, 2022). During the colonial era, this once-dirt route became a major trade center, symbolizing Shanghai’s journey towards modernity and economic prosperity. Various architectural styles, spanning centuries and influenced by many cultures (e.g., Art Deco, Gothic, Neoclassical, and Modern), line the street. These architectural marvels enhance the street’s allure and provide a mesmerizing visual experience (Figure 4.5).

East Nanjing Road is renowned for its extensive shopping options, from luxury brands to local boutiques and department stores. It is a haven for shoppers, offering a wide array of fashion, electronics, jewelry, souvenirs, and traditional Chinese goods.
