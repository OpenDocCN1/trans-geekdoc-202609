On the southern bridgehouses, sculptures commissioned by the B. F. Ferguson Monument Fund and created by Henry Hering depict critical moments in Chicago’s history. “Defense” portrays Ensign George Ronan in a scene from the Battle of Fort Dearborn in 1812, representing the city’s involvement in the conflict. “Regeneration” captures the resilience of Chicagoans as workers rebuild the city after the devastating Great Chicago Fire of 1871, symbolizing the city’s ability to overcome adversity and thrive. These sculptures, created by talented artists of their time, bring Chicago’s history to life through visual storytelling. They serve as permanent reminders of the city’s past, honoring the explorers, pioneers, defenders, and rebuilders who shaped Chicago into the vibrant metropolis it is today. The placement of these sculptures on the bridgehouses along Michigan Avenue ensures their visibility to both residents and visitors, adding cultural and historical significance to the city’s iconic thoroughfare. They invite viewers to engage with the city’s history, sparking curiosity and appreciation for the stories they represent. They enrich the urban landscape, serving as enduring tributes to the city’s past and inspiring a connection to its history (Al-Kodmany, 2021; Chicago Monuments Project).

The central figure of the monument is George Washington (1732–1799), a revered figure in American history.
