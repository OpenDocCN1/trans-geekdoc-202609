## HIGHLIGHTS

### Positive Aspects

- **Urban Oasis:** Citygarden successfully transforms an urban space into a vibrant oasis, offering a refreshing blend of art, nature, and public space.
- **Contemporary Sculptures:** The park boasts a diverse collection of contemporary sculptures, contributing to a dynamic and visually appealing outdoor gallery.
- **Interactive Elements:** Incorporating interactive elements within the sculptures enhances visitor engagement, providing a multisensory experience.
- **Landscape Design:** The park's landscaped design, including gardens, lawns, and water features, creates a serene and aesthetically pleasing environment.
- **Accessibility:** The park's central urban location contributes to accessibility, making it a convenient and inviting space for residents and visitors.

### Areas for Improvement

- **Interactive Informational Displays:** Incorporate interactive displays or digital information kiosks to give visitors more information about the sculptures, artists, and the park's history. This can enhance the educational aspect of the visit.
- **Visitor Amenities:** Consider adding more seating areas, shaded spaces, and/or restrooms to enhance visitor comfort during their park exploration.
- **Artwork Rotation or Expansion:** Periodically rotate or expand the sculpture collection to provide returning visitors with fresh experiences and maintain the park's dynamic and evolving nature.
- **Educational Programming:** Enhance the visitor experience by implementing robust educational programs, guided tours, and workshops, aiming to actively engage and enrich their understanding and appreciation of sculptures and art in a broader context.
- **Community Engagement:** Foster stronger ties with the local community by involving residents in the park's development, hosting community events, and seeking input on potential additions or changes.
- **Digital Presence:** Strengthen the park's online presence through an informative and visually appealing website and social media platforms, ensuring potential visitors are well-informed about the park's offerings.
- **Safety Measures:** Regularly assess and enhance safety measures, including lighting, signage, emergency contact points, and playing in or near water to ensure a secure environment for all visitors.

By addressing these potential areas of improvement, Citygarden Sculpture Park can continue to evolve as a dynamic and enriching cultural space for residents and tourists alike.

# HANGZHOU QIANJIANG CBD SCULPTURE PARK, HANGZHOU

Hangzhou, a city in Zhejiang Province, China, has a rich history dating back to the Qin Dynasty, influenced by its proximity to the Grand Canal and the Yangtze River. Hangzhou, known as “the House of Silk,” was one of China’s seven ancient capitals, with silk fabrics dating back 4,700 years to the Neolithic Liangzhu culture, and its foreign trade in silk, tea, and porcelain dates back thousands of years. It became a vital hub along the southern terminus of the Grand Canal during the Sui and Tang dynasties, connecting it to the Silk Road. During the Southern Song Dynasty, Hangzhou peaked, becoming a prosperous and culturally vibrant city known for its beautiful West Lake. The city's West Lake, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, has inspired poets, scholars, and artists for centuries. During the Ming and Qing dynasties, Hangzhou thrived as a significant economic and cultural center in southeastern China. The city has also attracted foreign travelers and traders, with Marco Polo describing it as “the most splendid city in the world” (Liu et al., 2011).

Today, Hangzhou stands as a thriving economic and technological hub, characterized by a striking skyline adorned with significant tall buildings and iconic structures that mirror the city's modern ambitions. Renowned for more than its picturesque landscapes and cultural heritage, Hangzhou has become a symbol of economic prosperity and innovation. The cityscape is punctuated by skyscrapers that house bustling financial districts, embodying the economic vitality and technological prowess that define Hangzhou’s contemporary identity. Amid this urban expanse, the city has successfully preserved its rich cultural heritage, providing a unique juxtaposition of ancient traditions and cutting-edge modernity. The coexistence of historic landmarks, such as the iconic West Lake, with state-of-the-art architectural marvels is a testament to Hangzhou’s ability to seamlessly blend tradition and progress. Additionally, the vibrant sociocultural and sports life in Hangzhou adds another layer to its allure, with a dynamic fusion of local traditions, global influences, and a thriving community that contributes to the city’s multifaceted charm (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Liu et al., 2011).

## HANGZHOU QIANJIANG CBD SCULPTURE PARK

Hangzhou Qianjiang CBD Sculpture Park is a dynamic public park in Hangzhou, China, connecting iconic buildings like InterContinental Hangzhou, Hangzhou Grand Theater, and Hangzhou Civic Center while showcasing sculptural quality on a mega scale, complementing the park’s outstanding aesthetics.

## HANGZHOU CIVIC CENTER

Hangzhou Civic Center in Hangzhou, China, consists of six trapezoidal 26-floor towers connected via five 85-meter (279-foot) sky bridges. They cluster around a central public garden surrounded by four low-rise podiums. Initially, the client wanted to build a single supertall building of 300 meters (984 feet). However, the project architect persuaded the client to replace that with multiple shorter connected towers. This arrangement of shorter buildings has fostered a more human scale while functioning as the visual terminus to a pedestrian axis, making the complex a unique landmark in the city. A pair of sister buildings also flank the Hangzhou Civic Center, the Hangzhou International Convention Center (The Sun), and the Hangzhou Grand Theater (The Moon). The project was designed by Tongji Architectural Design Group and completed in 2012 (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Parker & Wood, 2013).

## INTERCONTINENTAL HANGZHOU (THE SUN)

One of the most remarkable structures in Hangzhou CBD is the gleaming golden sphere InterContinental Hotel. The Hangzhou International Conference Center incorporates the principles of iconicity, functionality, and innovation. It follows a spherical design concept inspired by the sun, with a dominant color scheme centered around golden hues. The curtain wall of the building combines modern composite panels with golden-tinted glass. The entire edifice exudes grandeur, resembling a colossal golden sun that brilliantly reflects and enhances the significance of Hangzhou, cementing the city's status as a romantic capital. The hotel is a component of a cohesive complex that includes the Hangzhou International Conference Center, Shopping Center, Grand Theater, and parks. The Hangzhou International Conference Centre is a prestigious 5-star Intercontinental Hotel and Conference Centre, recognized for its exceptional quality and service. The conference center's capacity to hold international conferences and offer guests luxurious housing is made possible by its 739 rooms. The Conference Centre comprises a 1,000-seat auditorium with six distinct language interpretation booths. The structure, designed by Canadian architect Carlos Ott, was completed in 2009 (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Binder, 2015).

## HANGZHOU GRAND THEATER (THE MOON)

Hangzhou Grand Theater, built in the newly established Hangzhou Central Business District, is located across from InterContinental Hangzhou and adjacent to the river. The municipality has devised a comprehensive strategy to eventually incorporate a convention center, a new municipal building, and a sizable shopping mall. The city plans to establish a new commercial hub, separate from the downtown district along West Lake, connected by a newly constructed boulevard, offering land for new construction projects.

The Hangzhou Grand Theatre, designed to resemble a sculpture, is the main cultural establishment in the capital city of Zhejiang Province. The moon-shaped building, characterized by intricate double-curved glass facades and a titanium roof, includes the surrounding green space inside its design, resembling a pearl within its oyster shell. The facility includes an Opera Hall with a seating capacity of 1,600 in an inverted conical shape, a Concert Hall with a seating capacity of 600, a Theatre with a seating capacity of 400, and a rooftop Chinese restaurant open 24 hours a day. The restaurant offers views of the outdoor amphitheater and the river. The venue features adaptable flooring, walls, ceilings, and acoustic materials, enabling performances of both Eastern and Western repertoires and special lighting effects to synchronize activities. The project was completed in 2004 (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Liu et al., 2011).
