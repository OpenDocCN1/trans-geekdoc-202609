## HIGHLIGHTS

### Positive Aspects

- **Iconic Landmark**: Rockefeller Center Plaza is a globally recognized and iconic landmark that draws both locals and tourists. The Art Deco architecture and design of the plaza and surrounding buildings add to its aesthetic appeal.
- **Central Location**: Situated in the heart of Manhattan, the plaza benefits from its central location and accessibility.
- **Public Art and Sculptures**: The plaza is adorned with various sculptures and public art installations, enhancing its visual interest.
- **Ice Skating Rink**: The seasonal ice-skating rink is a cherished attraction during the winter months, creating a festive atmosphere.
- **Event Space**: The plaza hosts various events, from concerts to holiday celebrations, making it a vibrant gathering place.
- **Urban Oasis**: Amid the bustling city, the plaza offers a serene and well-maintained green space for relaxation and respite.
- **Cultural Significance**: Rockefeller Center Plaza has historical significance as a venue for major cultural events, including televised broadcasts.
- **Retail and Dining**: The plaza offers a mix of retail shops, restaurants, and cafes, contributing to its vibrancy.
- **Public Seating**: Ample seating options encourage people to gather, socialize, and enjoy the surroundings.
- **Seasonal Decorations**: The plaza is known for its elaborate holiday decorations and installations, attracting visitors year-round.
- **Public Gathering Space**: The open layout and spaciousness of the plaza accommodate various activities and gatherings.
- **Landscaping**: Landscaped areas with trees, plants, and flowers contribute to a pleasing environment.

### Areas for Improvement

- **Crowdedness**: Due to its popularity, the plaza can become overly crowded during peak tourist seasons, leading to congestion and limited seating.
- **Limited Green Space**: While there are landscaped areas, the amount of green space is limited compared to other public plazas.
- **Noise Levels**: The bustling activity and events can lead to high noise levels, impacting the tranquility of the space.
- **Lack of Privacy**: The open layout might lack secluded spots for private conversations or moments of solitude.
- **Accessibility Challenges**: The layout might pose challenges for individuals with mobility issues due to uneven surfaces and crowded areas.
- **Costs**: The cost of activities such as ice skating or visiting certain attractions might be prohibitive for some visitors.
- **Visual Clutter**: The presence of advertisements and commercial signage can create visual clutter and distract from the plaza's aesthetics.
- **Event Impact**: While events add vibrancy, they can disrupt regular space usage and lead to closures.
- **Maintenance Challenges**: Keeping the plaza clean and well-maintained amid heavy foot traffic can be challenging.
- **Overemphasis on Tourists**: The plaza's popularity among tourists might prioritize their needs over those of residents.

## 9/11 MEMORIAL PLAZA, NEW YORK CITY

9/11 Memorial Plaza in New York City is an 8-acre public space that connects the iconic buildings of the complex, including One World Trade Center, the tallest skyscraper in the Western Hemisphere. The plaza features a memorial to the victims of the 9/11 attacks, a museum, a performing arts center, and a transportation hub. It is designed to be a vibrant and welcoming place for visitors, workers, and residents, offering green spaces, art installations, and cultural events throughout the year. The 9/11 Memorial & Museum is the primary institution in the United States dedicated to exploring, documenting, and examining the impact of 9/11, with a focus on honoring those killed in the attacks (9/11 Memorial).

9/11 Memorial Plaza occupies the area between One World Trade Center (the prime tower) and the surrounding buildings within the World Trade Center complex. It spans several city blocks, bounded by West Street to the west, Liberty Street to the south, Church Street to the east, and Vesey Street to the north. The plaza’s design was led by architect Daniel Libeskind, with landscape architect Peter Walker contributing to landscaping. The overall design reflects a spirit of renewal and remembrance, paying homage to the original Twin Towers while creating a modern and resilient space (Parakh et al., 2017).

The plaza incorporates extensive landscaping, including trees, shrubs, and grassy areas. The designers aimed to create a peaceful and inviting environment for visitors, with carefully curated plantings that provide shade, beauty, and a connection to nature amid the urban landscape. The centerpiece of the plaza is the Memorial Reflecting Pools, which are located within the footprints of the original Twin Towers. These vast one-acre, square-shaped pools feature cascading waterfalls along the sides, creating a serene and contemplative atmosphere (9/11 Memorial).

The pools have North America’s most expansive artificial cascades, each dropping 30 feet into a square reservoir. Subsequently, the water in every pool descends a further 20 feet and vanishes into a more compact, central cavity. As per architect Michael Arad, the ponds symbolize the concept of “visible absence.” While water enters the empty spaces, they cannot be filled entirely. The auditory experience of the flowing water creates an atmosphere of serenity and deep thought, distinct from the bustling urban soundscape.

The memorial pools are bordered by bronze parapets that bear the names of the 2,983 individuals who lost their lives in the terrorist attacks of 2001 and 1993. The names are categorized based on the locations and circumstances where the victims were situated during the attacks. The North Pool parapets bear the names of individuals who lost their lives in the North Tower, on hijacked American Airlines Flight 11, and in the 1993 bombing. The South Pool parapets bear the names of both first responders and victims who lost their lives in the South Tower, on hijacked United Airlines Flight 175, at the Pentagon, on hijacked American Airlines Flight 77, and on hijacked United Airlines Flight 93 (9/11 Memorial); (Figure 3.18).

9/11 Memorial Plaza offers various public spaces and amenities for visitors. These include seating areas, walking paths, and open relaxation and social interaction spaces. The plaza also features several retail and dining establishments, providing convenience and options for visitors. Cultural concerts, public art exhibitions, memorial services, and other community events are all held at the plaza. These events contribute to the vibrancy and vitality of the space, fostering a sense of community and engagement among visitors. Moreover, the Memorial Plaza is adorned with almost 400 swamp white oak trees. This resilient tree species is indigenous to the locations of all three 9/11 crash sites: New York City, Arlington, Virginia, and Somerset County, Pennsylvania (Parakh et al., 2017).

9/11 Memorial Plaza is seamlessly connected to the World Trade Center Transportation Hub, known as the Oculus. This architectural marvel serves as a transportation hub for various subway lines and PATH trains and provides underground access to nearby destinations, ensuring convenient connectivity for commuters and visitors. Over a million passengers are served weekly by the twelve subway lines, the World Trade Center PATH station, and dozens of stores in the Oculus. Along with monthly activities, including farmers' markets, art exhibits, musical performances, and retail sales, the main floor is busy with commuters, shoppers, and tourists (Figure 3.19).
