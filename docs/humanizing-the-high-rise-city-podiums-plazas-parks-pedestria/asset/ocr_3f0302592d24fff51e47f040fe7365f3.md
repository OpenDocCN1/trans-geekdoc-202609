+   7. **Cultural and Social Relevance:**
- Ensure public art reflects and celebrates the city's and its communities' diversity, history, and cultural identity.
- Consider themes and narratives that resonate with the local population and address social issues or promote inclusivity. Public art can catalyze dialogue, reflection, and community engagement.

+   8. **Maintenance and Sustainability:**
- Develop a long-term maintenance plan for the public art installations to ensure their preservation and longevity.
- Consider the materials used, their durability, and the required maintenance activities.
- If applicable, incorporate sustainable practices, such as using eco-friendly materials, energy-efficient lighting, or rainwater harvesting for irrigation.

+   9. **Signage and Interpretation:**
- Install clear signage and interpretive elements that offer comprehensive information about the artwork, the artist, and the underlying concept, enhancing public understanding, appreciation, and engagement with the art.

+   10. **Collaboration and Partnerships:**
- Foster collaboration between artists, urban planners, architects, local government, developers, and community organizations.
- Embrace public-private partnerships to fund, implement, and maintain public art projects.
- This collaboration ensures diverse expertise, resources, and a shared commitment to integrating public art in the city.

By adhering to these planning and design principles, integrating public art in a dense, high-rise city can enhance the urban environment, enrich the cultural fabric, and foster a sense of identity, belonging, and civic pride among residents and visitors.
