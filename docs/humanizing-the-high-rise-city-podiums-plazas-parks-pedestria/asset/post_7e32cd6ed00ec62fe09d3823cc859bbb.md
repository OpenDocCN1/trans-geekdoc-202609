The Shanghai World Financial Center podium design is a remarkable and creative feature that enhances the tower's interaction with the surrounding pedestrian environment. The tower's base is conceived as a discrete "sculptural object" delicately attached to the supertall building, hosting entryways, a lobby, retail spaces, and eateries. This design choice contributes to the tower's visually appealing and functional integration with the street-level experience.

A distinctive design aspect is the tapering base, which adds a sculptural quality to the structure and opens up the pedestrian space, creating a more expansive and inviting environment. This tapering effect contributes to the aesthetics and serves as a visual guide, directing the viewer's gaze toward the towering structure in the background (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Binder, 2015).

The base incorporates orthogonal elements that outline the entryway, further enhancing the visual appeal of the sculpted base. The careful detailing and design elements create a sense of precision and sophistication, adding to the overall aesthetic quality of the structure. The human scale of the base is a crucial consideration, fostering delightful street life and providing a relaxed environment for pedestrians to enjoy.

Significantly, the design of Shanghai World Financial Center takes a distinctive approach by decoupling the base from the tower and infusing both elements with sculptural qualities. This marks a practical and innovative design strategy contributing to the broader sculptural podium portfolio discussed earlier. Noteworthy examples within this portfolio include the Lippo Center in Hong Kong, the Concourse in Singapore, designed by Paul Rudolph, and 500 West Madison, conceived by Helmut Jahn. Additionally, One Lujiazui by Tomohiko Yamanashi and The Trump Tower by Adrian Smith, which will be explored later, align with this portfolio, collectively showcasing a commitment to inventive and visually impactful architectural solutions.

It's worth noting that recent renovations have brought changes to the original design, indicating a commitment to adaptability and ensuring that the podium remains a vibrant and engaging part of the urban landscape.

## 155 North Wacker Drive, Chicago [2009]

155 North Wacker Drive is a 45-story, 195-meter-tall skyscraper in Chicago's Loop, designed by Goettsch Partners after One North Wacker and 111 South Wacker, which were discussed and illustrated earlier in this chapter (See Figures 2.16 and 2.17).

The podium design of 155 North Wacker Drive is characterized by a thoughtful combination of cantilevered floors, a striking arcade, and a spacious plaza that collectively exudes an atmosphere of transparency, lightness, and openness. The three-story arcade is a prominent feature, guiding pedestrians toward the building's two entrances and inviting lobby. This arcade is a functional element and an aesthetic delight, adorned with street furniture and greeneries, creating a welcoming space for pedestrians to appreciate the building's charming aesthetics while being shielded from the elements.

The arcade and lobby areas showcase meticulous attention to design, featuring geometric patterns, high-quality finishing, and intricate architectural details. This attention to detail creates a visual continuity that enhances the overall coherence of the building's design (Al-Kodmany, 2020; Goettsch, 2012).

On the east side of the structure, a public plaza further contributes to integrating nature surrounding the tower. Mature trees, lush grass, vibrant flowers, and granite benches combine to form a serene and visually appealing outdoor space. This plaza provides a respite for visitors, offering a harmonious blend of nature and urban architecture.

The podium designs of 155 North Wacker Drive, 111 South Wacker, and One North Wacker share a common thread in their commitment to transparency, seamlessly melding the distinctions between indoor and outdoor spaces and fostering an inviting atmosphere. Employing cutting-edge design principles, these structures exude elegance and sophistication, serving as testaments to material, technological, and structural advancement.

To fully grasp the depth of their innovative contributions, a comparative analysis with the Daley Center becomes illuminating. Notably, the Daley Center, featuring a transparent base dating back to its construction in 1965, serves as a historical benchmark (Figure 2.7). Contrasting these buildings (see Figures 2.16, 2.17, and 2.22) underscores the evolutionary strides in architectural design, showcasing the progressive evolution from earlier transparent base designs to the contemporary masterpieces exemplified by 155 North Wacker Drive, 111 South Wacker, and One North Wacker (Goettsch, 2012).

The completion of the project in 2009 underscores the contemporary design principles and the commitment to creating not just functional office space but a place that enriches the urban experience. Overall, the podium design of 155 North Wacker Drive exemplifies a successful integration of aesthetics, functionality, and green spaces within the urban landscape (Figure 2.22).

## Trump International Hotel & Tower (Trump Tower), Chicago [2009]

The Trump International Hotel & Tower, standing at a height of 423 meters (1,388 feet) and consisting of 98 stories, was constructed in 2009. It now holds the position of the second-highest structure in the city, with the 1974 Willis Tower, more commonly referred to as the Sears Tower, surpassing it in height.

The tower’s podium design is distinguished by its unique integration with the Chicago River. This feature contributes to the building’s iconic presence in the cityscape. The tower’s base is artistically sculpted to reveal the “topographical” outlines of the lower floors, creating an undulating design that emulates the curves of the adjacent river. This architectural choice gives the base a dynamic and visually engaging appearance (Al-Kodmany, 2020; Kamin, 2022).

The recessed floors of the base ingeniously reveal the structural columns of the building, infusing the design with a captivating rhythm and sense of order. This intentional transparency in the structure not only adds an intriguing layer of architectural interest but also offers observers a unique glimpse into the inner workings and foundational elements of the tower. It creates a dynamic interplay between form and function, engaging viewers in an exploration of the building’s structural intricacies.

In addition to the thoughtful design of an articulated base, the tower’s connection with the river is elevated through the incorporation of cascading terraces. These terraces serve not only as a functional component but also as a visual delight, orchestrating a seamless transition between the architectural structure and the picturesque waterfront. Their carefully crafted arrangement enhances the aesthetic appeal of the building while simultaneously fostering a deeper connection with the natural surroundings. These terraces, strategically positioned, provide residents and visitors alike with distinctive vantage points, offering unparalleled opportunities to savor and appreciate the scenic beauty of the river and its environs.

To activate the pedestrian experience, retail stores were strategically placed in the tower’s base. The intention was to foster vibrant pedestrian activity. Still, the effectiveness of this strategy has faced criticism, as some of these retail spaces remain unoccupied, leading to an underutilized pedestrian realm. Despite this challenge, the overall design of the podium reflects a deliberate attempt to harmonize the building with its waterfront setting and engage with the dynamic urban context of Chicago (Figure 2.23).

## China World Trade Tower, Beijing [2010]

The China World Trade Tower is an 81-story skyscraper in Beijing’s Central Business District. It stands at an impressive height of 330 meters and boasts a unique architectural design that combines modernity with traditional Chinese elements. Designed by Skidmore, Owings, and Merrill (SOM) and completed in 2010, the tower contains offices, meeting rooms, restaurants, and the China World Summit Wing hotel, with a lobby on the 64th floor and an observation deck on the 81st floor.
