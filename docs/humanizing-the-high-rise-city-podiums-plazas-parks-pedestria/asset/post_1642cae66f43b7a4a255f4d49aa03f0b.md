## International Commerce Center; Hong Kong [2010]

Designed by Kohn Pedersen Fox (KPF) Associates and rising to 484 meters (1,588 feet) with 118 floors, the International Commerce Center (ICC) tower is the tallest building in Hong Kong. Completed in 2010, the tower occupies the southwest corner of the Union Square complex and is owned by Hong Kong MTR Corporation Ltd. and Sun Hung Kai Properties. It contains commercial office space and a five-star Ritz-Carlton Hong Kong hotel, which occupies the highest floors of the building (floors 102 to 118).

The International Commerce Center (ICC) podium design is distinguished by its thoughtful integration of architectural elements that enhance aesthetics and functionality. At the north side of the building, the façade gracefully descends to form an atrium shaped like a “dragon’s tail.” This unique architectural feature extends from the tower’s base, creating a visually captivating public space that connects the tower with the rail station and mall and adds an artistic touch to the overall design (Figure 2.25).

On the opposite side of the dragon’s tail is the primary entryway of the building. Marked by a large glass canopy spanning the drop-off area, this entryway is designed to provide a grand sense of arrival for visitors and guests. The glass canopy adds a modern and inviting aesthetic to the entrance and functions as a skylight, allowing natural light to permeate the lobby and the mall (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Binder, 2015).

The fountain is a focal point within the plaza, which holds symbolic significance. Representing the tower’s connection to Victoria Harbour and serving as a metaphorical lighthouse for the city, the fountain features a circular basin with dynamic jets that create various water patterns and effects, such as mist, spray, and waves. This central water feature adds a dynamic and interactive element to the public space, contributing to the overall experiential quality of the ICC’s podium.

In summary, the podium design of the International Commerce Center in Hong Kong is characterized by its innovative incorporation of architectural elements, such as the dragon’s tail atrium and the glass canopy entryway, which not only contribute to the building’s aesthetic appeal but also enhance the functional aspects of the space. The symbolic fountain further reinforces the tower’s connection to its surroundings and its role as a prominent landmark in the cityscape.

## Henderson Metropolitan, Shanghai [2011]

Henderson Metropolitan is located at 300 East Nanjing Road in the Huangpu district of Shanghai. It was completed in 2011 and covers an area of 35,450 square meters. It contains a mall featuring a variety of stores, restaurants, and entertainment options, catering to different tastes and budgets. Some of the critical tenants include Apple, Nike, Boy London, P.F. Chang's, and Sephora. The eastern façade features a large LED screen displaying advertisements and interactive contents (Denison & Ren, 2013).

The podium design of Henderson Metropolitan is a remarkable fusion of modern architecture, seamlessly blending functionality and aesthetics to create a dynamic and visually appealing space. The four-story structure serves as the foundation for a towering 24-story building housing offices and apartments while accommodating a diverse range of retail and entertainment spaces, such as a cinema, a supermarket, a food court, and a fitness center.

The podium's façade is composed of glass, metal, and wood panels, meticulously arranged to produce a dynamic and elegant appearance. This architectural approach not only contributes to the overall aesthetics of the building but also reflects a contemporary design ethos. The materials used in the façade add layers of texture and visual interest, creating a visually compelling exterior.

The large atrium that spans its interior is a vital feature of the podium. This design element serves a dual purpose by allowing natural light to permeate the space, creating a bright and inviting atmosphere, and providing natural ventilation. The integration of curved canopies undulating surfaces further enhances the sense of openness and connection within the interior, creating a visually stimulating environment for visitors.

In summary, the podium design of Henderson Metropolitan skillfully combines modern aesthetics, functionality, and a thoughtful spatial layout to create a vibrant and engaging urban space.

## KK100, Shenzhen [2011]

KK100 is a supertall skyscraper in Shenzhen, China, standing at 442 meters (1,450 feet) and housing 98 floors. TFP Farrelis, a British firm specializing in high-rise buildings, designed the tower. Completed in 2011, the building features a curved glass facade that tapers toward the top, creating a sleek and elegant profile. The building also has a distinctive crown that houses an observation deck, a garden, and several restaurants on the upper floors (Binder, 2015).

The podium design of KK100 is a masterful composition that adds to the iconic stature of one of the city's tallest buildings. The main entrance stands out with a distinctive feature—a large circular opening that serves as a focal point, framing the entrance visually strikingly. This circular design enhances the building's visibility from various angles and contributes to a sense of grandeur, elegance, and arrival for visitors.
