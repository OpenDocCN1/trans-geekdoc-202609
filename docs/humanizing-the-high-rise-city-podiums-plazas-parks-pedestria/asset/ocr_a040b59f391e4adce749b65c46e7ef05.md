After the railway ceased operation, the elevated structure faced an uncertain future. There were discussions about demolishing the structure. However, residents and community activists recognized its potential as a unique public space and advocated for its preservation and repurposing. In 1999, Friends of the High Line advocated converting the abandoned railway into a public park. They envisioned transforming the elevated structure into a green space providing recreational opportunities, promoting urban renewal, and preserving the neighborhood’s historic character (Park, 2019).

Through years of advocacy, fundraising, and collaboration with city officials, the first section of the High Line was opened to the public in 2009. The design of the High Line is a joint effort involving James Corner Field Operations as the project lead, together with Diller Scofidio + Renfro and Piet Oudolf. They designed the park to retain the industrial aesthetic of the railway while incorporating natural elements, landscaping, seating areas, and unique design features. The success of the initial section led to the development of subsequent phases, with the final section of the High Line completed in 2023. Overall, the project involved a multidisciplinary team that combined the expertise of architects, landscape architects, urban designers, and horticulturists. The design team worked closely with the City of New York and community stakeholders to create a unique and transformative elevated park experience (Littke et al., 2016).

# KEY DATES

The development of the High Line, an innovative urban park in New York City, unfolded through key dates that marked its transformation from a disused rail track into a celebrated green space, reshaping the city’s landscape (The High Line; Lindner & Rosa, 2017):

+   - **1934.** The initial railway was established to transport a substantial quantity of meat, dairy, and produce, amounting to millions of tons.
- **1980.** The cessation of train operations resulted in the abandonment of the railway tracks.
- **1999.** Until then, the structure was at risk of being demolished. However, Joshua David and Robert Hammond started advocating for preservation and conversion to a public open area.
- **2003.** The High Line organized an “ideas competition” and received 720 submissions from individuals in 36 different countries. These submissions proposed various ways in which the park could be utilized. Some ideas, such as a rollercoaster or a mile-long lap pool, were neither feasible nor practical.
- **2009.** Following the establishment of Friends of the High Line a decade ago, the initial portion of the High Line, spanning from Gansevoort to 20th Streets, became accessible to the public.
- **2011.** Section 2 was inaugurated, including the area between 20th and 30th Streets.
- **2014.** The Rail Yards segment from 30th to 34th Streets was inaugurated.
