
## 1 Introduction

### BACKGROUND

The 21st century has ushered in a transformative phenomenon that has reshaped our cities—the rapid proliferation of tall buildings. In an era characterized by innovation, urbanization, and the pursuit of vertical possibilities, these towering structures have emerged as defining features in our modern landscapes. Beyond their architectural significance, they reflect societal changes, economic growth, collective aspirations, adaptability, and an ceaseasing exploration of new frontiers in form and function. The narrative of these towering giants unfolds as a tale of contrasts—balancing soaring ambitions with grounded foundations, vertical growth with the deep roots of community, and the harmonious coexistence of human progress with the preservation of the intrinsic qualities that make our cities authentically human (Al-Kodmany et al., 2022; Oldfield, 2019; Wood, 2022; Yeang, 2022).

The rapid growth of skyscrapers within urban landscapes brings many challenges and complexities. The pervasive feeling of being eclipsed and overlooked amid the shadows of these tall buildings profoundly influences how individuals perceive and interact with their urban environment. As pedestrians traverse the landscape dominated by skyscrapers' colossal scale and dominance, they may grapple with emotions ranging from apprehension and detachment to estrangement. These emotions, stemming from the intimidating presence of these architectural giants, can detrimentally impact an individual's sense of participation, belonging, and overall well-being (Jacobs, 1963; Loukatoou-Sideris & Banerjee, 1998; Talen, 2000). The resulting disconnection from the urban fabric diminishes the human experience in these high-rise cityscapes, altering how people engage with and derive meaning from their surroundings. In navigating this challenge, urban planners and architects must consider strategies that balance the vertical aspirations of cities with the imperative of preserving the human scale, fostering a more inclusive, engaging, and enriching urban experience (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Gehl, 2010; Madden, 2011; Zalloom, 2020).

In the evolving landscape of urbanization, the intensification of city living gives rise to an augmented demand for urban amenities. The pursuit of greater height and density propels urban planners and architects to delve into innovative approaches, striving to harmonize towering structures with communal spaces. As cities intricately weave themselves into a denser tapestry to accommodate burgeoning populations, a crucial imperative emerges: to counterbalance the constraints imposed by reduced living spaces. A strategic resolution involves the seamless integration of diverse public plazas and parks, exhibiting variations in both size and typology. These open spaces, intricately woven into the urban fabric, assume a critical role in mitigating the impact of neighborhoods dominated by towering structures (Chakrabarti, 2013; Forsyth, 2015; Whyte, 1980). The deliberate development of open spaces at various scales emerges as a pivotal strategy, aiming to cultivate a thriving urban environment where residents can seamlessly coexist with verticality, indulging in an enriched and fulfilling quality of life. Consequently, the trajectory towards heightened urbanization unfolds as a nuanced journey, necessitating meticulous planning and thoughtful integration of public spaces to sustain the delicate equilibrium between vertical expansion and the preservation of a vibrant urban lifestyle (Alexander et al., 1977; Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Gehl, 1971; Lynch, 1960; Nielsen, 2017; Zamojski, 2021).

This research acknowledges the intrinsic benefits of low-rise environments, emphasizing their capacity to provide a more supportive setting for human scale and foster a closer connection to nature (Bharne, 2011; Forsyth, 2015; Gehl, 2010; Harbour, 2018; Jacobs, 1963; Madden, 2011; Rapoport, 1990; Talen, 2000). However, understanding the unavoidable prevalence of high-rise living in modern urban landscapes, the author sets forth a compelling mission for architects and urban designers within the pages of this book. The mission becomes a proactive endeavor, urging architects and urban planners to prioritize the infusion of human-centric elements, ensuring that we elevate the well-being and quality of high-rise cities. Therefore, rather than dismissing the reality of vertical living, the author aims to instill a sense of responsibility within the design community. Similarly, rather than endorsing the wholesale embrace of tall structures, the focus shifts towards a conscientious approach, urging professionals to prioritize human-centric design elements. The primary objective becomes straightforward: to elevate the human experience within the urban fabric of high-rise cities. This book is a proactive guide, encouraging architects and urban designers to enrich the urban realm of high-rise environments, ensuring that these spaces harmonize with the human experience and contribute positively to the overall well-being of their residents and visitors.

### BOOK OVERVIEW

This book embarks on an extraordinary journey that transcends the mere architectural aspects of high-rise cities. It explores the delicate balance between vertical aspirations and the fundamental human need for social connection, nature, and creative expression. It investigates strategies and design principles to restore the human scale of the high-rise city by incorporating pedestrian-friendly streetscapes, public plazas, promenades, public art, and ground-level amenities that foster social interaction and connectivity. By prioritizing human experience and creating walkable environments, the adverse effects of the high-rise city on the human scale can be mitigated. The discussion recognizes the importance of these spaces in moderating the potential negative impacts of tall buildings and creating more livable and inclusive urban environments. It explores the symbiotic relationship between urban architecture and human experience and delves into a world where concrete and glass meet nature, art, and community. In the following pages, you journey through a tapestry of design strategies that elevate the high-rise city beyond its functional framework, giving it a soul, character, and vibrancy that resonates with all who inhabit it.

This study investigates twenty cities, providing a foundation for understanding how to make the high-rise urban landscape more human-friendly. The methodology is rooted in firsthand experiences, with the author undertaking personal visits, engaging in photography, and conducting on-site direct observations. The cities scrutinized span prominent metropolises in the United States, including Chicago, New York City, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Detroit, Saint Louis, Indianapolis, and Miami. The international scope encompasses influential cities such as Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Hangzhou, Suzhou, Hong Kong, Singapore, London, Milan, Fukuoka, Dubai, and Kuala Lumpur. This immersive exploration delves into these cities' urban context, structure, fabric, components, and composition, offering a detailed and firsthand perspective on the endeavor to humanize high-rise environments.

Integral to the depth of insight achieved is incorporating a visual dimension with over 200 meticulously chosen photographs. Curated from the author's extensive collection amassed during numerous visits, these images are compelling storytellers. The strategic integration of visuals enhances the reader's understanding, providing a sensory and immersive experience. Embracing the wisdom that “a picture is worth a thousand words,” the book utilizes the inherent data-rich nature of photographs, imbuing the narrative with nuanced layers of meaning that extend beyond the confines of written words. Readers are informed and visually transported, fostering a comprehensive and memorable learning experience.

Diverse sources, including urban design, landscape architecture, art, and urban planning literature, contribute to the research's foundation. The collective work and profound contributions of eminent figures such as Jan Gehl in architecture and urban design, William H. Whyte in urbanism and sociology, and Kevin Lynch in urban planning serve as guiding lights, intricately shaping the conceptual framework. By synthesizing their influential perspectives, the study seeks to contribute to the discourse on humanizing high-rise cities, aligning with a commitment to understanding the intricate relationship between urban design, public spaces, and the human experience. The literature on tall buildings, particularly by the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat (CTBUH), assumes prominence, providing valuable insights into vertical urban development’s challenges, innovations, and impacts.
