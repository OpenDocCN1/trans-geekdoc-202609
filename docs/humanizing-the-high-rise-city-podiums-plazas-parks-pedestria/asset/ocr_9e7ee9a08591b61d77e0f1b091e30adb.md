The urban room concept enhances urban living by creating pedestrian-friendly environments, enhancing the human experience, and fostering a dynamic and interconnected urban fabric (Al-Kodmany, 2020a).

At their essence, urban rooms aspire to be more than just well-defined and specialized; they aim to be unique and inviting urban public spaces. This design philosophy harmonizes with the notion that these outdoor environments should be experienced and cherished by the city's inhabitants in a manner akin to appreciating various rooms within their homes. The deliberate incorporation of varied activities, attention to perceptual details, and a focus on human scale collectively define urban spaces, enhancing the overall quality and user-centric nature of the urban environment. This concept signifies a departure from conventional park design, embracing a nuanced and purposeful approach to urban planning. By prioritizing the creation of spaces that are not only functional but also engaging and meaningful, urban rooms reshape the urban fabric, fostering dynamic, interconnected, and lively environments for individuals to explore, experience, and appreciate in their daily lives (Al-Kodmany, 2020b).

# MILLENNIUM PARK, CHICAGO

Millennium Park, spanning 24.5 acres in downtown Chicago, Illinois, is a testament to urban transformation, converting a former rail yard and parking lot into a dynamic urban oasis. This farsighted project not only repurposes land but also realizes Daniel Burnham's century-old vision for a grand civic space along the lakefront, as documented by Gilfoyle in 2006. The site's history intertwines with the Illinois Central Railroad, which utilized the land since 1852, exchanging it for building a protective breakwater along the shore. In the mid-1900s, a section evolved into a parking lot until the initiation of park construction in 1997. Millennium Park officially opened in 2004, becoming a celebrated attraction and a defining feature of Chicago's urban identity, blending history, visionary design, and civic engagement in a harmonious tapestry of urban revitalization (Al-Kodmany, 2020a).

The park is a remarkable urban oasis that offers diverse attractions, including stunning artworks, elegant pavilions, lovely gardens, and seasonal exhibitions. These attractions appeal to different senses and create a contrast with the dense downtown area. One of the most striking aspects of this area is the smooth transition from the skyscraper-filled surroundings to the spacious green area with rich vegetation, vendors, music, and, most importantly, people. The park is open and free of charge for everyone, attracting people of various ages, ethnicities, and languages who come to enjoy the public, the art, and the social activities within the space. Visually, the city's skyline (two intersecting rows of impressive skyscrapers forming an L-shaped edge) frames the park and serves as a magnificent backdrop.
