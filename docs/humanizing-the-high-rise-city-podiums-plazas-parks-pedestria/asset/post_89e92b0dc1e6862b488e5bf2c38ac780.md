The Picasso Sculpture in the plaza adds a significant human dimension by highlighting the work of the Spanish artist Pablo Picasso, one of the most influential artists of the 20th century. The project was commissioned in 1963 by the architects of the Daley Center. The sculpture is made of Cor-Ten steel, the same material as the exterior of the Daley Center, stands 50 feet tall, and weighs 162 tons. It was manufactured in Gary, Indiana, and assembled in Chicago in 1967 (Kamin, 2003). The sculpture base often serves as a favored place for people to sit and children to play (Figure 3.2). Also, the plaza's seating options cater to different needs, accommodating individuals of all ages and abilities (Figure 3.6).

Richard J. Daley Plaza hosts various holiday-themed activities and events during the festive seasons. For instance, *FrankenPlaza*, a Halloween-themed festival in October, fills the space with various food vendors, tent shelters, and a stage for acrobatic performances. In the spirit of Halloween, the fountain water is dyed orange, creating a surreal yet pleasantly humorous spectacle for plaza-goers. Other festivals include the *Christkindle*market, a German-themed Christmas celebration, which hosts a multitude of ethnic-themed foods, arts, and crafts from all over the world. In addition to civic and cultural events, the Daley Center Plaza is open for private occasions such as weddings, festivals, and business gatherings.

Daley Plaza is also the rendezvous point for *Critical Mass*, a monthly exhi-bition of Chicago’s bicycle enthusiasts. At approximately 6:00 p.m. on the last Friday of every month, thousands of bicyclists descend upon the plaza, forming a large ring around the building, which blocks traffic and leaves pedestrians dumb-struck. The resulting caravan runs a few “victory laps” before abruptly continuing along a usually undetermined path throughout the city. This cultural diversity, inclusivity, and acceptance spirit make Daley Plaza a unique urban hotspot.

The plaza does not have permanent dining establishments within its imme-diate boundaries, but there are some options for food and beverages. Food trucks often park in the plaza during luncheon time and special events (Figure 3.7). These mobile eateries offer a variety of cuisines, ranging from gourmet sand-wiches and tacos to international flavors and desserts.

The Daley Plaza is a dynamic urban space that seamlessly blends vibrant social and cultural activities with serene retreats, providing visitors with a multifaceted experience. Amid the bustling events and festivities that often char-acterize the plaza, a thoughtful design element caters to the need for tranquility and relaxation. On the west edge, the plaza ingeniously integrates a “green oasis”—a verdant haven adorned with lush landscaping and a playful water fountain. This green oasis serves as a counterpoint to the lively energy of the surrounding events (Figure 3.8). The carefully curated landscaping introduces a touch of nature into the urban landscape, offering visitors a visually appealing respite from the concrete surroundings. The strategic placement of trees, shrubs, and other greenery creates a pocket of tranquility, providing shade and a sense of seclusion within the larger plaza.

The presence of the Eternal Flame Memorial within the plaza adds a poignant layer to the human narrative woven into the urban landscape. This solemn tribute serves as a profound reminder and acknowledgment of the immense sacrifices made by individuals who laid down their lives in service to their country. Situated within Daley Plaza, the Eternal Flame Memorial stands as a dignified homage to the brave men and women who made the ultimate sacrifice during significant chapters in history, spanning World War I (1914–1918), World War II (1939–1945), the Korean War (1950–1953), and the Vietnam War (1955–1975) (Keegan, 2008). This memorial symbolizes valor and selflessness and invites contemplation, fostering a collective sense of remembrance and gratitude within the heart of the bustling urban environment (Figure 3.10).

Overall, the Daley Center Plaza is an integral part of Chicago's urban fabric, offering a place for people to gather, enjoy public art, and experience the city's bustling atmosphere. Its modern design, notable artwork, and central location make it a significant and vibrant public space in downtown Chicago.

# HIGHLIGHTS

## Positive Aspects

- **Central Civic Space:** As a prominent civic space, the plaza is a focal point for public gatherings, events, protests, and celebrations.
- **Iconic Picasso Sculpture**: The iconic “Untitled” Picasso sculpture is a landmark symbol of the city’s commitment to public art.
- **Ample Open Space**: The spacious layout allows for various activities, exhibitions, and performances, accommodating diverse community needs.
- **Seating and Gathering Areas**: Benches, ledges, movable chairs, and open areas provide comfortable seating and spaces for people to gather, relax, and socialize.
- **Urban Greenery**: Landscaping with trees, planters, and greenery adds visual appeal and softens the urban environment.
- **Interactive Water Feature**: The fountain encourages interaction and play, especially during hot weather.
- **Pedestrian Connectivity**: The plaza promotes pedestrian movement, connecting the Loop with surrounding streets and attractions.
- **Public Programming**: The plaza hosts various events, including concerts, markets, rallies, and cultural festivals, fostering community engagement.
- **Public Art and Monuments**: Besides the Picasso sculpture, the plaza features art installations and memorials that enrich its cultural significance.
- **Historic Context**: The plaza’s location and history are integral to Chicago’s civic life and urban development.
- **Lunchtime Hub**: Many nearby workers use the plaza as a lunchtime destination, enhancing its role as a communal space. Food trucks park in the plaza serving lunch.
- **Nighttime Illumination**: The plaza’s lighting transforms it into an inviting space during evening hours, extending its usability.
