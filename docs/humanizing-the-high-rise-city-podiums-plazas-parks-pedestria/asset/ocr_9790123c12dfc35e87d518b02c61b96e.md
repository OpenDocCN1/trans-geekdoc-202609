The elevated design and limited access points can make it difficult for everyone to fully enjoy the park.

The High Line project in New York City is a remarkable example of urban regeneration and adaptive reuse. The project transformed an abandoned elevated railway into a public park that spans 1.5 miles along the west side of Manhattan. The park features gardens, art installations, cultural events, and panoramic views of the city and the Hudson River. The High Line project has been widely praised for its innovative design, environmental benefits, and social impact. It has also stimulated economic development and revitalization in the surrounding neighborhoods. However, the High Line faces overcrowding, loss of authenticity, gentrification, limited green space, lack of ecological diversity, maintenance and operational costs, and accessibility challenges. The park’s narrow structure limits available space for amenities and recreational activities, and its design and landscaping may compromise its historical integrity. The park’s limited green space may also restrict its capacity to serve as a fully functional urban park.

# KEY TAKEAWAYS: PLANNING AND DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Retrofitting aging and disused railways into elevated parks presents an exciting opportunity to repurpose existing infrastructure and create vibrant public spaces. The planning and design process for such projects requires careful consideration of several key factors:

+   1. **Feasibility Assessment:**

+   • Begin by conducting a feasibility assessment to evaluate the structural integrity of the railway infrastructure and its suitability for transformation into an elevated park.

+   • Assessments should consider the condition of the tracks, bridges, viaducts, and other elements to determine their capacity for supporting public use.
