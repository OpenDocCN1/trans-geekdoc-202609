## STATUE OF IRV KUPCINET, CHICAGO

The outdoor bronze statue of Irv Kupcinet, created by Preston Jackson in 2006, commemorates the influential journalist and writer in Chicago, Illinois. The statue is prominently located on the sidewalk of Wacker Drive near the intersection with Wabash Avenue, providing a significant presence step away from the picturesque Chicago Riverwalk (Figure 14.3). The sculpture captures the likeness of Irv Kupcinet (1912–2003), known for his famous “Kup’s Column,” which gained him recognition as a veteran writer. Kupcinet began in 1935 as a sportswriter for the Chicago Daily Times, later known as the Chicago Sun-Times. After eight years, the newspaper introduced “Kup’s Column,” which achieved a remarkable 60-year run. Rick Kogan, a Tribune reporter, emphasized that proving one’s identity in Chicago required having their name in boldface in Kup’s column. The statue portrays him clutching a Sun-Times final edition beneath his right arm while gesturing towards the former site of the newspaper, which is now the Trump Tower. The statue’s installation on Wacker Drive near the Chicago Riverwalk serves as a reminder of the city’s rich history and the individuals who have played a significant role in shaping its cultural fabric. Mayor Richard M. Daley feared that the passing of Irv Kupcinet would create an emptiness in Chicago, as he was strongly associated with the city, like the Picasso sculpture, Hancock Building, and Sears Tower. The bronze material used for the sculpture adds a timeless quality, reflecting the enduring legacy of Irv Kupcinet’s journalistic career (Kupcinet, 2012).

## FLAMINGO SCULPTURE, CHICAGO

The Flamingo Sculpture in Chicago is an engaging and captivating artwork that captures the attention and imagination of viewers. Created by the renowned American artist Alexander Calder (1898–1976), this sculpture stands as a prominent feature in the Federal Plaza, captivating passersby with its striking design and vibrant presence. The Flamingo Sculpture’s engaging nature is evident in its unique form and composition. Calder’s artistic vision comes to life through the sculpture’s dynamic lines, curves, and bright red color (Figure 14.4). The abstract representation of a flamingo evokes a sense of movement and vitality, inviting viewers to interpret and engage with the artwork from different perspectives. Situated in a prominent location in front of the Kluczynski Federal Building, the Flamingo Sculpture serves as a visual anchor in the urban landscape of Chicago. Its towering height (53-ft [16 m] tall) and commanding presence create a sense of awe and curiosity, drawing people in to explore its intricacies and appreciate its artistic craftsmanship. As visitors interact with the Flamingo Sculpture, they are encouraged to contemplate its form, engage with its vibrant colors, and consider the interplay between art and the surrounding architecture. The sculpture introduces a captivating juxtaposition to Mies van der Rohe’s architectural design, known for its disciplined orthogonal lines and dark facades. Mies van der Rohe, a pioneer of modernist architecture, was renowned for his minimalist approach, emphasizing clean lines and a restrained color palette. In contrast, the sculpture injects a dynamic and vibrant palette into the surroundings. This iconic artwork, commissioned by the United States General Services Administration, has been a prominent fixture since its unveiling in 1974 (Marter, 1979).

## MONUMENT WITH STANDING BEAST SCULPTURE, CHICAGO

“Monument with Standing Beast” Sculpture by Jean Dubuffet in Chicago is a monumental work of public art, a striking representation of the artist's imaginative vision. This impressive sculpture, constructed from fiberglass and painted in a white hue with black outlines, commands attention as it rises over 29 feet tall. It is situated prominently in front of the James R. Thompson Center, captivating passersby with its powerful presence (Figure 14.5). Part of Dubuffet's *Hourope* series, the Standing Beast Sculpture showcases the artist's exploration of creating forms from doodles and scribbles. This series reflects Dubuffet's unconventional approach to art, challenging traditional notions of beauty and inviting viewers to engage with their imagination and curiosity. The sculpture's bold presence and unconventional form encourage viewers to question established norms and embrace new perspectives. Placed in a prominent public space, it promotes accessibility and encourages active engagement with the artwork. The sculpture's towering stature, unique design, and challenging nature defy conventional notions of beauty and art, encouraging viewers to embrace curiosity and engage in a dynamic dialogue with the sculpture and its surrounding environment. The 10-ton Jean Dubuffet sculpture that stood in front of the James R. Thompson Center since 1984 is being relocated after Google acquired the building (Herder, 2011).

## JEAN BAPTISTE POINTE DUSABLE STATUE, CHICAGO

Jean Baptiste Pointe DuSable, an African-Caribbean born in Haiti and a fur trader, founded Chicago. During the 1770s, he established the inaugural trade station adjacent to the Chicago River, establishing the community that eventually evolved into Chicago. He is commemorated through a statue created by Erik Blome in 2009 and generously donated to the Chicago Public Art Collection by Lesley Benodin. The statue represents the historical and cultural ties that connect Chicago to its diverse roots and is an integral part of the public art landscape (Figure 14.6). It becomes an integral part of the public art landscape, accessible to all who visit and explore Chicago. By commemorating DuSable and his pivotal role as the city’s founder, the statue serves as a reminder of the contributions made by individuals from diverse backgrounds in shaping society’s fabric. It celebrates the multiculturalism and inclusivity that are fundamental to the identity of Chicago (Thompson, 2012).

## DEER SCULPTURE, CHICAGO

One of the most eye-catching artworks on the Chicago Riverwalk was “Deer,” a sculpture by Tony Tasset that occupied the “Riverbank” segment between Franklin and Lake Streets. The piece depicts a giant realistic deer made of painted fiberglass that looks over the river calmly. The deer is 12 feet tall and 20 feet long, strikingly contrasting with the urban surroundings. It attracted the attention of boat riders and pedestrians, who often stopped to take pictures (Figure 14.7). The large-scale sculpture of a deer in Riverwalk captures the essence of nature, offering a moment of reflection amid the city’s bustling urban setting. It highlights the coexistence of wildlife and human activities along the river, creating a unique visual contrast that encourages viewers to appreciate the harmonious blend of nature and urban life. The deer sculpture, installed in June 2017 and removed in November 2017, was placed in a sculpture forest on the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art grounds in Bentonville, Arkansas (Tolles, 2013).
