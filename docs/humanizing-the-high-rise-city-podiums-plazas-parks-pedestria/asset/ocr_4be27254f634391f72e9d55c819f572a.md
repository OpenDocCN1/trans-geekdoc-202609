# The Jetty

The Jetty, an expansive stretch from Wells Street to Franklin Street, is a testament to the harmonious integration of ecology, recreation, and education along the Chicago River. The Jetty is a multifaceted space designed to cater to various interests and experiences, comprising seven piers that boldly extend over the river's expanse.

This unique area serves as a haven for fishing enthusiasts, providing designated spots where individuals can engage in this leisurely pursuit. However, a pertinent reminder accompanies this recreational aspect, emphasizing the need for an appropriate fishing license to partake in the activity. As people cast their lines into the river, the Jetty transforms into a tranquil space where individuals can enjoy the solitude of fishing and witness the underwater world teeming with aquatic life (AI-Kodmany, 2020; Kamin, 2022b, 2023).

The ecological consciousness embedded in the Jetty's design extends beyond surface-level engagement. Underwater fish habitats have been strategically installed, contributing to the river's biodiversity. Floating gardens, anchored by resilient stainless-steel pylons, enhance the ecological fabric by providing a healthy habitat for fish populations. Remarkably, these floating gardens exhibit adaptability, capable of rising to 8 feet in response to flooding, showcasing a thoughtful approach to environmental resilience.

The plant selection for the floating wetlands and water gardens prioritizes water-tolerant species, further emphasizing the commitment to sustainable landscaping practices. The Jetty creates recreational opportunities, enhances ecological well-being, and serves as an educational platform. Visitors are invited to explore and interact with the aquatic ecosystem, fostering a deeper understanding of the delicate balance between urban development and environmental conservation (AI-Kodmany, 2020; Kamin, 2022a).
