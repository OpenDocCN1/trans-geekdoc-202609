## The Cove

Placed between Dearborn and Clark Streets, The Cove is a captivating urban retreat within Chicago's dynamic landscape. More than a collection of eateries, it represents a carefully orchestrated fusion of culinary delights, diverse seating options, and a thoughtfully curated landscaping theme inspired by the tranquil allure of the beach, creating a harmonious blend of urban vibrancy and natural serenity. Low-slung benches, reminiscent of beach stones, offer a unique seating experience, inviting visitors to relax and savor the ambiance. The landscape is further enriched by beach grasses, wetland plants, and trees adorned with woodland ferns, creating a coastal aesthetic that transports individuals away from the urban hustle.

Diversity in seating options is a hallmark of The Cove, ensuring visitors can choose environments that suit their preferences. Whether spreading out on the beach-inspired benches or exploring other seating choices, the space accommodates a range of activities, fostering a sense of inclusivity (Al-Kodmany, 2021; Kamin, 2022b).

The Cove’s strategic location, with its proximity to the water, adds an extra layer of allure. The shallow edges bring visitors closer to the river, providing an intimate connection and easing access to kayaks. This unique feature transforms The Cove into a culinary destination and a hub for aquatic recreation. Kayaks can be docked and rented, offering a novel way for people to engage with the urban environment.

Practical considerations also define The Cove’s design. Acknowledging its lower elevation, the planting beds are ingeniously crafted to tolerate floods when submerged by river water during storms and heavy rain events. This flood-tolerant design showcases the adaptability of urban spaces, ensuring the longevity and resilience of the landscape against natural elements.

In essence, The Cove transcends the conventional boundaries of a plaza. It is a multifaceted urban haven that seamlessly blends culinary experiences, diverse seating options, and a beach-inspired landscape, inviting visitors to indulge in the intersection of gastronomy, nature, and recreational possibilities in the heart of Chicago (Figure 9.5).

## The River Theater

The River Theater, stretching elegantly from Clark Street to Lasalle Street, is a testament to the seamless integration of urban design and natural elements within the heart of Chicago. Central to its design is a sculptural staircase that serves as a visually striking feature and doubles as a versatile seating area. This innovative approach transforms a functional structure into an inviting space, creating a harmonious blend of form and function.

A distinctive characteristic of the River Theater is the presence of a diagonal pedestrian ramp, thoughtfully designed with a gentle gradient of less than 5%. This ramp is a connective thread, seamlessly linking the Riverwalk with Upper Wacker Drive. Its accessibility design ensures that individuals of all abilities can traverse the space comfortably, promoting inclusivity in this urban oasis.

Nature takes center stage in the River Theater, with seventeen mature trees boasting large canopies strategically scattered throughout the area. These trees provide welcome shade for visitors, enhancing the comfort of the space and contributing to the aesthetic appeal, adding a touch of natural beauty to the urban environment (Al-Kodmany, 2021; Chicago Riverwalk).

The incorporation of tall trees serves a dual purpose, as they attract various birds, creating an auditory backdrop of delightful twittering. This auditory experience enhances the sensory richness of the River Theater, inviting visitors to immerse themselves not only in the visual aspects of the space but also in the sounds of nature.

In essence, the River Theater transcends the conventional expectations of an urban space. It is a dynamic fusion of landscape architectural innovation and natural elements, creating an inviting environment that encourages social interaction, relaxation, and a connection with the surrounding ecosystem. As visitors ascend the sculptural staircase or traverse the diagonal ramp, they embark on a sensory journey where the urban landscape converges seamlessly with the soothing presence of mature trees and the melodies of nature (Figure 9.6).

## Waterfront Parks

## Phase 3

Phase 3, completed in 2016, unfolds along the Chicago Riverwalk from LaSalle Street to Lake Street, introducing three distinctive “urban rooms”: the Water Plaza, the Jetty, and the Riverbank (formerly known as the “Boardwalk”). Each thoughtfully designed space spans considerable lengths, with the Riverbank extending 500 feet and the Water Plaza and the Jetty spanning 300 feet each. Much like the “urban rooms” introduced in Phase 2, these spaces are strategically framed by the iconic, historic bridges, contributing to a heightened sense of visual enclosure and the unique character of the place. The deliberate delineation of these urban rooms along the riverfront enhances the overall coherence of the Riverwalk and invites visitors to explore and engage with varied environments, each offering its unique blend of urban charm and scenic beauty (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Chicago Riverwalk; Kamin, 2023).

## The Water Plaza

The Water Plaza, gracefully extending from LaSalle Street to Wells Street, emerges as a captivating aquatic haven within the heart of Chicago’s urban landscape. This expansive plaza is characterized by a long, rectangular, zero-depth water fountain adorned with sleek black granite floor. The focal point of this aquatic spectacle lies in its interactive water jets, thoughtfully designed to engage children and inspire delightful play.

Functioning as a lively splash pad, the Water Plaza transcends generational boundaries, captivating the imaginations of people of all ages and welcoming the playful antics of pets. The interactive water features create an inclusive environment, fostering a sense of community and shared enjoyment as families and friends gather to revel in the refreshing embrace of water.

The Water Plaza is crucial in compensating for the limited opportunities to directly engage with the river water. Its design acknowledges the desire for a hands-on, immersive experience with water, offering a dynamic alternative that invigorates the senses.

Beyond its role as a splash pad, the Water Plaza caters to practical needs by including public restrooms, ensuring the comfort and convenience of visitors. Mechanical equipment discreetly integrated into the plaza’s infrastructure powers the mesmerizing water jets, a testament to the thoughtful planning that underlines this urban aquatic retreat (Al-Kodmany, 2021; Chicago Riverwalk).

In essence, the Water Plaza transcends the conventional boundaries of public spaces, transforming the riverfront into an interactive playground that resonates with children’s laughter, the joyous splashes of water, and the collective appreciation of a unique urban oasis. As visitors engage with the interactive water features, the Water Plaza becomes a vibrant focal point, enriching the urban experience by seamlessly blending recreation, aesthetics, and practical amenities (Figure 9.7).
