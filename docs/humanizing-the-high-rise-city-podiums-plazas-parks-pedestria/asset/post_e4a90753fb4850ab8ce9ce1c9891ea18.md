## PARKLETS
Parklets, small public spaces born from the transformation of curbside parking spaces or medians, emerge as vibrant urban oases. These green havens, with seating, landscaping, and diverse amenities, invite pedestrians to pause, socialize, and relish the outdoors. As welcoming respites within the bustling urban fabric, parklets contribute to community building and sustainable transportation advocacy. Figure 13.12 captures the essence of these spaces, showcasing their integration into the urban landscape of downtown Chicago. Beyond functionality, parklets boast designs that elevate street aesthetics, enhancing the overall appeal of their surroundings. As testimonials to urban ingenuity, parklets exemplify the urban planners’ commitment to creating inclusive, green, and socially engaging environments, fostering a harmonious coexistence between the urban dweller and their surroundings (Litke, 2016; Koue, 2013).

### Key Takeaways: Planning and Design Principles
When integrating pedestrian networks, promenades, and streetscapes in a high-rise city, several planning and design principles should be considered to create vibrant, accessible, and people-centric urban environments. Here are fundamental principles to keep in mind:

- **1. Walkability and Accessibility:**
  - Prioritize walkability by designing pedestrian-friendly infrastructure that encourages active transportation.
  - Ensure safe, comfortable, and accessible pathways for pedestrians of all ages and abilities.
  - Minimize barriers like traffic congestion, road crossings, and physical obstructions to create a seamless and enjoyable walking experience.

- **2. Complete Streets:**
  - Embrace the concept of complete streets that accommodate various modes of transportation, including pedestrians, cyclists, and public transit.
  - Design safe, convenient, and inclusive streets for all users.
  - Provide dedicated cycling lanes, well-designed bus stops, and ample pedestrian crossings to promote multimodal connectivity.

- **3. Human Scale and Proportions:**
  - Design streetscapes and promenades with a human scale in mind.
  - Consider the proportions of buildings, street furniture, lighting, and landscaping elements to create an inviting and comfortable environment.
  - Enhance the visual appeal and character of the area by integrating architectural elements that complement the surrounding context.

- **4. Active and Passive Spaces:**
  - Incorporate a mix of active and passive spaces to cater to different activities and user preferences.
  - Include gathering areas, plazas, seating, and green spaces for relaxation and social interaction.
  - Integrate amenities such as water features, public art, and cultural installations to enhance the experience and create focal points.

- **5. Landscaping and Greenery:**
  - Integrate landscaping and greenery to create visually appealing and environmentally friendly streetscapes.
  - Include street trees, planters, and green infrastructure elements to improve air quality, reduce the urban heat island effect, and promote biodiversity.
  - Select vegetation suitable for the local climate and requires low maintenance.

- **6. Safety and Security:**
  - Design streetscapes and pedestrian networks with safety as a top priority.
  - Ensure proper lighting, clear visibility, and adequate signage to enhance safety and security.
  - Consider traffic calming measures such as raised crosswalks, speed bumps, or traffic islands to reduce vehicle speeds and create a pedestrian-friendly environment.

- **7. Placemaking and Identity:**
  - Create a sense of place by integrating elements that reflect the local culture, history, and identity.
  - Incorporate public art, wayfinding signage, or street furniture that celebrates the area's unique character.
  - Encourage community involvement in the design process to foster a sense of ownership and pride in the streetscape.

- **8. Flexibility and Adaptability:**
  - Design pedestrian networks and streetscapes with flexibility to adapt to changing needs and future growth.
  - Consider the potential for temporary or seasonal use, street closures for events, or the introduction of pop-up activities.
  - Ensure the design allows for easy modifications or expansions as the community evolves.

- **9. Sustainability Considerations:**
  - Incorporate sustainable design principles into the planning and design process.
  - Consider green infrastructure for stormwater management, energy-efficient lighting systems, or recycled materials.
  - Promote sustainable transportation modes and encourage public transit, walking, and cycling to reduce environmental impacts.

- **10. Community Engagement and Collaboration:**
  - Engage the local community, stakeholders, and relevant authorities throughout the planning and design process.
  - Seek input, conduct workshops, and involve community members in decision-making.
  - Collaborate with stakeholders, including urban planners, landscape architects, transportation authorities, and local businesses, to ensure a holistic and collaborative approach.

By following these planning and design principles, integrating pedestrian networks, promenades, and streetscapes in a dense, high-rise city can transform urban areas into vibrant, safe, and enjoyable spaces that prioritize the needs and well-being of pedestrians and promote sustainable and inclusive communities.
