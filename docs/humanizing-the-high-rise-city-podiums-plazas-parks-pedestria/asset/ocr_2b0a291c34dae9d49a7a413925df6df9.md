# BUND BULL, SHANGHAI

The Shanghai Bull, also known as the Bund Financial Bull or the Bund Bull, refers to a modified version of Arturo Di Modica's Charging Bull sculpture in New York City. This derivative artwork was installed in late April 2010 and officially revealed on The Bund in Shanghai on May 15, 2010. The work of art, weighing 13,227 pounds (6,000 kilograms), is purported to possess dimensions identical to the New York City Charging Bull in terms of height, length, and weight. However, it measures 17.1 feet (5.2 meters) in length and 10.5 feet (3.2 meters) in height. Similarly, the bronze bull features a tail that exudes a more ominous presence (Wue, 2014). The Bund Bull has garnered significant popularity, fostering enthusiastic engagement of residents and visitors (Figure 14.12).

# TERRA NATURA ANGELS SCULPTURE, LUJIAZUI PARK, SHANGHAI

Ascending to a height of 15 meters, Wu Ching-ju’s faceless fairies gracefully hover above the earth despite their substantial weight of 34 tons. “Terra Natura” is a large copper sculpture that pays tribute to Mother Earth and emphasizes the purity and delicacy of nature. Selected from over 1,000 submissions to be featured in Lujiazui Park (Lujiazui Central Greenland), this acknowledgment for Wu, facilitated by the Halcyon Gallery, her representative, was so noteworthy that the artwork, despite a construction cost of approximately RMB20 million, was donated. Originally conceived with the fairies surrounded by waste piles and titled “Searching for Oasis,” the artist’s vision faced rejection from city officials who were adamant about not portraying Shanghai as a colossal landfill.
