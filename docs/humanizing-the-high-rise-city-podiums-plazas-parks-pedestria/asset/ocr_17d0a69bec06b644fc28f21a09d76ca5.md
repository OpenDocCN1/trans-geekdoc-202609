+   • **2019.** The Spur, the concluding segment of the ancient edifice, commenced operation.

+   • **2023.** The new High Line—Moyhiran Connector opened in June. This new pathway, comprised of two intersecting bridges, allows visitors to move from anywhere on the High Line to Moyhiran Train Hall with only one street crossing.

# PARK FEATURES

The Highline’s key features include (The Highline; Lindner & Rosa, 2017; Little et al., 2016):

+   1. **Tiffany & Co. Foundation Overlook** (At Gansevoort St.): This striking balcony signifies where the southern portion of the High Line was separated in the 1980s to accommodate the construction of residential units in the former Manhattan Refrigerating Warehouse. The location is situated east of the Whitney Museum of American Art and the headquarters of Friends of the High Line.

+   2. **Donald Pels and Wendy Keys Gansevoort Woodland** (Between Gansevoort St. and 13th St.): During the summer, vines elegantly drape over the railings at this location, creating a vibrant curtain that may be seen by anyone passing by. Below is a red brick structure that serves as the last functioning meatpacking facility in the Meatpacking District. Directly to the north is a striking glass hotel, The Standard High Line.

+   3. **14th Street Passage** (Between 14th St. and 15th St.): Each evening, beginning at twilight, within this partially covered pathway, one may discover an open-air TV program called the High Line Channel. This program showcases art videos, historical pieces, recent creations, and carefully selected series.

+   4. **Diller-Von Furstenberg Sundeck & Water Feature** (Between 14th St. and 15th St.): When the High Line was devoid of any activity and served as a vacant railway track, water would naturally gather at this location. This is precisely why the designers intentionally incorporated a discreet water element in this area. During the warmer months, this location is beloved by families and individuals of all ages, providing an opportunity to enjoy wading in the water.

+   5. **Chelsea Market Passage** (Between 15th St. and 16th St.): This section of the High Line is situated on the previous site of the National Biscuit Company, also known as Nabisco, which Chelsea Market currently occupies. The Oreo cookie was developed at this location, using flour transported by trains along this route. From April to October, the food court is open-air.

+   6. **Northern Spur Preserve** (At 16th St.): This spur traverses 10th Avenue to establish a connection with the old Merchants Refrigerating Warehouse. It represents the historical botanic aspect of the High Line, with crab apples, asters, sedges, goldenrods, and alumroot that evoke the untamed period before the park was accessible to the public.
