## PEOPLE’S PARK, SHANGHAI

People’s Park is a renowned public park in the heart of Shanghai, China. It is situated in the Huangpu District, adjacent to Nanjing Road, one of Shanghai’s most famous shopping streets (See Chapter 4). It spans Middle Xizang Road in the east to North Huangpi Road in the west and stretches from West Nanjing Road in the north to People’s Square in the south. The park has eight entrances, with the primary entrance on West Nanjing Road. Covering an area of approximately 100,000 square meters (25 acres); it is an important part of the downtown and is known for its beautiful green spaces, serene atmosphere, and various recreational activities, catering to visitors of all ages. Established in 1952, it holds significant historical and cultural value for the residents of Shanghai. It is a popular gathering place for locals, tourists, and families, offering many recreational activities and relaxing spaces (Wang et al., 2016).

People’s Park encompasses humanizing aspects that enhance visitors’ experience. It serves as a vibrant community gathering place, attracting people from all walks of life. The park offers ample open spaces, plazas, and seating areas, encouraging social interactions, community engagement, and a sense of belonging. It includes walking paths, jogging tracks, and designated areas for tai chi, dancing, and board games. These amenities promote active lifestyles, physical well-being, and opportunities for leisurely enjoyment. The park often hosts art exhibitions, performances, and cultural events, including music concerts, dance shows, and traditional performances. It is also home to various cultural institutions that offer exhibitions, performances, and educational programs that promote artistic and cultural expression. This cultural enrichment adds depth to the park and provides intellectual stimulation and engagement opportunities (Chen et al., 2015).

The park comprises three distinct sections: the eastern region, the central area, and the western area. The primary point of interest in the eastern region is the Memorial to the May Thirtieth Movement. In the expansive central region, one may locate the Antarctic Stone, the Shanghai Museum of Contemporary Art, a teahouse, an outdoor theater, and a dance hall. Located along the park's central axis, the Shanghai Museum of Contemporary Art is a pioneering gallery establishment that showcases modern art and design. The museum has hosted many expansive art exhibitions, drawing in many visitors. In addition, visitors can relax, engage in a game of chess, or have a picnic on the stone tables and stools inside the rainforest of the central region. The western region boasts many landscapes with pavilions, corridors, artificial hills, pools, and pergolas, starkly contrasting with the eastern and central parts (Wang et al., 2016).

The Memorial to the May Thirtieth Movement, an anti-imperialist movement on May 30th, 1925, is located on the northeastern side of the park. It was founded in 1985 to commemorate the 60th anniversary of the movement. Unveiled in 1990, it covers 4,000 square meters and includes a stainless-steel sculpture, a bronze sculpture, and three granite stones. The central sculpture, weighing 50 tons, bears the inscriptions of “May 30th” and symbolizes the optimism and enthusiasm of Chinese people. The bronze sculpture features two workers, showcasing their bravery and determination. The middle granite stone features inscriptions by Chen Yun and Lu Ding, while the other two tablets depict the movement's history (Wang et al., 2016).

The Lotus Pond, located in the park's western section, consists of two pools and spans 3,300 square meters (3,947 square yards). Lotus flowers are cultivated in the eastern pool, while goldfish are bred in the western pool. The luxuriant morning redwoods are located to the east of the pond, while a square pavilion rises to the west. To the south of the pond is a meandering corridor and a rectangular pavilion by the water. A bamboo pavilion with eight sides is located northeast of the bridge, spanning the pond's center. During the summer, there is an intriguing exhibition of lotus flowers in the lotus pond. Visitors are welcome to visit and see the exquisite lotus blossoms (China Tour Star).

Shanghai People's Park is renowned for its central focus on the Marriage Market, establishing itself as a prominent attraction. During weekends, a considerable assembly occurs, where parents actively seek potential life partners for their children. The displayed notes present a comprehensive profile that integrates vital personal information like gender, age, occupation, educational attainment, telephone numbers, and photos of individuals seeking a life companion. Moreover, these notes elaborate on individuals' criteria for prospective spouses.

During the 1980s, the park transformed into a pivotal hub where college students and English enthusiasts congregated for meaningful conversations with foreign tourists, refining their spoken English skills. This longstanding tradition has endured and continues to exert its appeal every Sunday at the English Corner. The weekly gathering consistently draws a diverse spectrum of participants, including children, students, international visitors, and senior citizens, establishing the English Corner as an enthralling and esteemed meeting place (Travel China Guide).

People's Park is a significant historical landmark in Shanghai. It was initially a horseracing track during the Shanghai International Settlement era in the early 20th century. After the Chinese Communist Party took power in 1949, the horseracing track was transformed into a public park, which opened to the public in 1952. It is an asset for Shanghai, combining community-oriented design, cultural diversity, environmental sustainability, and historical heritage. It provides a space for people to enjoy various activities, such as relaxing, playing, learning, and interacting, improving their well-being and happiness. People's Park is more than just a park, it is a humanizing force in the urban landscape of Shanghai (Wang et al., 2016); (Figure 5.6).

## HIGHLIGHTS

### Positive Aspects

- **Historical Significance**: People's Park holds historical importance as one of Shanghai's oldest parks, and its cultural heritage adds to its charm and identity.
- **Central Location**: Situated in the city's heart, the park's central location makes it easily accessible and a popular destination for various activities.
- **Variety of Amenities**: The park offers a range of amenities, including walkways, seating areas, gardens, and ponds, catering to different preferences and activities.
- **Botanical Garden**: The park's botanical garden houses a diverse collection of plants, offering a learning experience and a peaceful setting.
- **Cultural Elements**: The park features statues, sculptures, and other cultural elements that reflect the city's history and provide points of interest for visitors.
- **Recreational Activities**: People's Park hosts various recreational activities, such as dancing, Tai Chi, and cultural performances, contributing to its lively atmosphere.

### Areas for Improvement

- **Wayfinding and Signage**: Improved wayfinding elements and signage could enhance visitors' ability to navigate the park's expansive areas and amenities.
- **Equitable Access**: Ensuring that the park is accessible to individuals with disabilities is vital for creating an inclusive space that welcomes everyone.
- **Program Diversity**: Continuously diversifying the programming and events can cater to a broader range of interests and age groups, ensuring that there's always something for everyone.
