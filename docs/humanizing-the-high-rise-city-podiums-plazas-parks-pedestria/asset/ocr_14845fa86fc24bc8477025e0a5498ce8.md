The park centers the development and offers scenic views of the sur- rounding skyscrapers, such as Aqua Tower and Aon Center. It encompasses a playground designed for young children, a designated area to accommodate dogs, an interactive water feature, and meticulously maintained greenery. One may traverse the park by enjoying its picturesque pedestrian trails. Furthermore, abundant expanses of open areas are available to unfurl a picnic blanket. It is a great place to enjoy nature and culture in the city’s heart. Lakeshore East Park was established in 2005 with financial support from the public (Hill, 2021). From its early stages, the landscape architect firm Site Design Group Limited was involved in the project, collaborating with the master plan architects to create the open space rules that would subsequently inform the park’s design and development (Parakh et al., 2017).

Lakeshore East Park exemplifies several humanizing aspects that enhance the experience of residents and visitors. The park functions as a "green oasis" in the Chicago Loop, the city’s central business district, by providing a lush and serene green space within the bustling cityscape, offering a respite from the urban environment, and allowing people to connect with nature, relax, and recharge. The park’s design prioritizes pedestrians, providing well-designed walkways, accessible pathways, and convenient connections to surrounding neighborhoods. This pedestrian-friendly approach enhances safety, encour- ages active transportation, and facilitates easy access for residents and visitors. Furthermore, the park’s extensive landscaping, including trees, shrubs, and flowers, creates an inviting and calming atmosphere. Also, the park serves as a community gathering place, bringing people together from the surrounding high-rise residential buildings and the broader neighborhood. It offers various seating areas, open lawns, and pathways that encourage social interactions and create opportunities for residents to engage with one another, fostering a sense of community and belonging (Hill, 2021).

Upon entering the park from the east and west ends, visitors encounter five pools divided by areas covered in decomposed granite and adorned with carefully selected shade trees. A majestic limestone staircase is located at the park’s southern entrance, effectively handling a substantial difference in eleva- tion throughout the site, approximately 25 feet from the south side to the north. This staircase also forms an elevated terrace that offers panoramic views of the park. The northern entrance is distinguished by extended bench walls, a robust geometric pavement design, and a progression of landscaped plantings that gradually transform into tranquil open areas (Parakh et al., 2017).

The 4.6-acre park features two sweeping promenades of white pavers, the primary means of east-west movement. Chicago’s horticulutral heritage is com- memorated with forty-six decorative plants, offering a dynamic array of colors that vary with the seasons. A sequence of floral courtyards runs beside the western water garden, replicating the shape and size of the walkways.
