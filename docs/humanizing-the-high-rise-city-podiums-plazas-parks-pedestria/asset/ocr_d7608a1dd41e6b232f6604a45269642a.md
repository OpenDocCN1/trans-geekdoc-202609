A noteworthy aspect of the Concourse Building's podium is its connectivity to the adjacent Nicoll Highway MRT station. This intentional linkage provides convenient and seamless access for the public, enhancing the building's accessibility and integrating it into the broader urban infrastructure.

Situated above the podium, the apartments of the Concourse Building offer residents breathtaking views of the river and the city skyline. This strategic placement capitalizes on the scenic surroundings and underscores the building's commitment to providing a premium living experience.

In summary, the Concourse Building stands out for its octagonal office tower, supported by striking pilots, and its podium characterized by a reverse S-curve. Integrating a multilevel shopping center with a sky-lit atrium, coupled with connectivity to public transit, makes the Concourse Building a notable example of modern and functional urban architecture in Singapore (Rohan, 2014).

# **Jin Mao Tower, Shangha** **i [1999]**

The Jin Mao Tower is a mixed-use skyscraper in Shanghai, China, combining traditional Chinese architecture with neo-futurism. Adrian Smith designed the tower during his tenure at Skidmore, Owings & Merrill. The tower has 88 floors, and its shape is based on the number eight, considered auspicious in Chinese culture. The building features a pagoda-style profile, an octagonal core, and an eight-lobed exterior wall. The tower houses offices, a shopping mall, and the Grand Hyatt Shanghai Hotel, the highest hotel in the world when completed in 1999.
