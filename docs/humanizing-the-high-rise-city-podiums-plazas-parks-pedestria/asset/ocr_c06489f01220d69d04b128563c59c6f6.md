# Saint Louis, Missouri

Saint Louis, Missouri, boasts a rich historical tapestry seamlessly interwoven with a vibrant contemporary presence. Founded in 1764 by French fur traders, it has played a significant role in the westward expansion of the United States, earning the nickname “Gateway to the West.” St. Louis is part of the Greater St. Louis metropolitan area and has a diverse economy with strengths in healthcare, finance, manufacturing, and transportation. The city is home to world-class cultural institutions like the Saint Louis Symphony Orchestra, Saint Louis Art Museum, and Missouri Botanical Garden. St. Louis’ sports culture thrives with passionate fans supporting teams like the St. Louis Cardinals and St. Louis Blues. Educational institutions like Washington University and Saint Louis University contribute to the city’s intellectual vibrancy. Despite challenges related to urban development and economic transitions, efforts are being made to revitalize neighborhoods and enhance cultural amenities (Proctor, 2018).

# Citygarden

Citygarden is a 3-acre urban park and sculpture garden in St. Louis, Missouri, owned by the City and maintained by a private foundation, the Gateway Foundation. The site is situated within the confines of Eighth, Tenth, Market, and Chestnut Streets, specifically in the “Gateway Mall” area, just a few blocks west of the iconic Gateway Arch and the majestic Mississippi River. The park’s prime downtown location makes it easily accessible to residents, workers, and visitors. Art and nature come together in the park’s exquisitely landscaped gardens, which feature a wide variety of plants, trees, and flowers. During the warmer months, guests can enjoy the park’s interactive features, such as fountains, pools, and water jets, while also relaxing, picnicking, and taking leisurely strolls. The location consisted of two vacant grass plots before being transformed into a garden and park. Citygarden was officially inaugurated on June 30, 2009, and commenced operations on July 1, 2009 (Citygarden; Proctor, 2018).

Subsequently, the garden has assumed a major role in revitalizing the city’s central area. In 2011, the American Society of Landscape Architects (ASLA) honored the project with an award. The Professional Award Jury explained that the project’s influence transcends its physical boundaries, serving as a testament to St. Louis’ dedication to enhancing public spaces and contributing to a broader national conversation on urban environments. The triumph of this initiative has emboldened the city to participate in the Arch Competition, underscoring the significance of thoughtful design and the landscape architect’s adept integration of art within the natural surroundings (ASLA).
