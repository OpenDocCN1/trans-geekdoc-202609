
The park centers the development and offers scenic views of the surrounding skyscrapers, such as Aqua Tower and Aon Center. It encompasses a playground designed for young children, a designated area to accommodate dogs, an interactive water feature, and meticulously maintained greenery. One may traverse the park by enjoying its picturesque pedestrian trails. Furthermore, abundant expanses of open areas are available to unfurl a picnic blanket. It is a great place to enjoy nature and culture in the city’s heart. Lakeshore East Park was established in 2005 with financial support from the public (Hill, 2021). From its early stages, the landscape architect firm Site Design Group Limited was involved in the project, collaborating with the master plan architects to create the open space rules that would subsequently inform the park’s design and development (Parakh et al., 2017).

Lakeshore East Park exemplifies several humanizing aspects that enhance the experience of residents and visitors. The park functions as a "green oasis" in the Chicago Loop, the city’s central business district, by providing a lush and serene green space within the bustling cityscape, offering a respite from the urban environment, and allowing people to connect with nature, relax, and recharge. The park’s design prioritizes pedestrians, providing well-designed walkways, accessible pathways, and convenient connections to surrounding neighborhoods. This pedestrian-friendly approach enhances safety, encourages active transportation, and facilitates easy access for residents and visitors. Furthermore, the park’s extensive landscaping, including trees, shrubs, and flowers, creates an inviting and calming atmosphere. Also, the park serves as a community gathering place, bringing people together from the surrounding high-rise residential buildings and the broader neighborhood. It offers various seating areas, open lawns, and pathways that encourage social interactions and create opportunities for residents to engage with one another, fostering a sense of community and belonging (Hill, 2021).

Upon entering the park from the east and west ends, visitors encounter five pools divided by areas covered in decomposed granite and adorned with carefully selected shade trees. A majestic limestone staircase is located at the park’s southern entrance, effectively handling a substantial difference in elevation throughout the site, approximately 25 feet from the south side to the north. This staircase also forms an elevated terrace that offers panoramic views of the park. The northern entrance is distinguished by extended bench walls, a robust geometric pavement design, and a progression of landscaped plantings that gradually transform into tranquil open areas (Parakh et al., 2017).

The 4.6-acre park features two sweeping promenades of white pavers, the primary means of east-west movement. Chicago’s horticultural heritage is commemorated with forty-six decorative plants, offering a dynamic array of colors that vary with the seasons. A sequence of floral courtyards runs beside the western water garden, replicating the shape and size of the walkways. The composition incorporates various families of site furniture and lighting, pavement made from different materials and colors, and a diverse range of Lannon Stone, granite, cedar wood, and metals. These elements combine to create a contemporary design showcasing a unique selection of materials (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013).

The dog park is in a sloping area in the southeast corner, providing a secure and protected space for owners to engage in recreational activities with their dogs. Inside the designated dog area is an enclosed area where pet owners may allow their pets to run freely without a leash. The dog park features three hierarchical berms covered in sod, which highlight the six-foot difference in elevation. A short stone fountain wall cuts through the tallest mounds, releasing water into a winding channel that gathers at the bottom of a small hill (Hill, 2021).

The children's garden is where the promenades and the North Grant axis meet. It consists of a circular plaza with a water feature that may be interacted with and a safe play surface. The playground provides an enjoyable area for play on a surface covered with rubber. The central play structure resembles a ship, with vibrant stairs, slides, and observation platforms. Additionally, sheltered areas are available for parents to observe while protected from direct sunlight (Figure 5.2).

## HIGHLIGHTS

### *Positive Aspects*
- **Urban Retreat:** Amid the city's skyscrapers, Lakeshore East Park offers a peaceful retreat where individuals can escape the urban hustle.
- **Greenery:** It offers a lush green environment with well-maintained lawns, trees, and plantings, creating a pleasant natural setting.
- **Urban Integration:** Lakeshore East Park is integrated into a mixed-use development, providing a green sanctuary amid the high-rise buildings, contributing to the urban fabric.
- **Water Features:** The park features water elements such as fountains, creating a serene and visually appealing atmosphere.
- **Central Location:** Situated near Lake Michigan and adjacent to Millennium Park, the park attracts residents and visitors exploring downtown.
- **Playground and Amenities:** Including a playground, dog park, and seating areas enhances the park's functionality and accommodates various user needs.
- **Landscaping:** The park's landscaping includes a mix of greenery, flowers, and trees, creating a pleasant natural environment within the urban context.
- **Dog-Friendly:** Lakeshore East Park is dog-friendly, providing spaces for pet owners to exercise and socialize with their furry companions.
- **Pedestrian-Friendly:** The park's design and pathways encourage pedestrian movement and connectivity within the Lakeshore East neighborhood.

### *Areas for Improvement*
- **Sunlight:** The park is surrounded by high-rise buildings that block the natural sunlight and create a sense of confinement. This may reduce the enjoyment of the park for people who prefer open and airy spaces.
- **Wayfinding:** Clear signage and wayfinding elements can help visitors navigate the park's amenities and attractions more effectively.
- **Seating Areas:** The park offers limited seating areas and options.
- **Program Diversity:** Expanding the range of programming and events can cater to a broader audience and encourage community engagement.
- **Amenities:** The park lacks amenities such as restaurants, cafes, and restrooms.
- **Parking:** The park does not have its parking lot, so visitors must pay for street parking or public transportation.

## YERBA BUENA GARDENS, SAN FRANCISCO

Yerba Buena Gardens is a public park in downtown San Francisco, California. Spanning approximately five acres, it offers numerous humanizing aspects that enhance the experience of residents and visitors by providing a variety of amenities and featuring lush green spaces, trees, open spaces, gardens, and well-maintained landscaping. These elements provide visual relief from the urban environment and create a sense of tranquility and connection with nature. The greenery contributes to improved air quality, shade, and a welcoming atmosphere for visitors (Newfield, 2007).

The park is a vibrant community gathering place, attracting people from diverse backgrounds. It offers open spaces, plazas, and meeting areas, encouraging social interactions, community engagement, and a sense of belonging. It provides a welcoming environment where visitors can come together, relax, and enjoy recreational activities. It hosts yearly events and festivals, such as live music performances. These events unite the community, encourage social interaction, and create shared experiences and enjoyment opportunities. They contribute to the park’s lively and inclusive atmosphere. The Gardens have playgrounds and play areas suitable for children of different ages. There are also water features where visitors can relax and cool off.

The Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial at Yerba Buena Gardens is a meaningful homage to the renowned champion of civil rights, representing the park’s harmonious blend of urban serenity and cultural veneration. The focal point of this homage is a striking waterfall that gracefully descends over a granite wall, creating a visual cascade that symbolizes the enduring impact of Dr. King’s legacy. As the water flows, it echoes the unstoppable force of justice and equality, embodying the ideals for which Dr. King fervently advocated. Integrated into the memorial are inscriptions of Dr. King’s inspiring quotes etched onto the granite surface. These words are enduring reminders of the wisdom, passion, and vision that characterized Dr. King’s advocacy for civil rights. Visitors are invited to reflect upon these powerful messages, creating a contemplative space within the vibrant surroundings. Danish artist Lin Utzon created the walls of the Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial and Waterfall (Lee & Yoon, 2008).
