# Public Parks

+   - **Equitable Access**: Accessibility features, such as pathways and seating for individuals with disabilities, could be further improved to ensure inclusivity.
- **Seating and Shade**: While the park offers seating options, there have been discussions about the need for additional seating and increased shaded areas, particularly during hot weather.
- **Sustainable Practices**: Incorporating sustainable design elements, such as energy-efficient lighting and water conservation measures, aligns with New York City's environmental goals.
- **Interactive Elements**: The park could benefit from introducing more interactive and educational elements to enhance visitor engagement.
- **Safety**: Concerns about safety and security, especially during the evening hours, are relevant, highlighting the importance of maintaining a visible security presence.
- **Commercialization**: There have been debates about the balance between commercial activities in the park, such as food kiosks and events, and the park's role as a serene urban oasis.
- **Historical Interpretation**: Although the park holds historical significance, there is room for improvement in seamlessly integrating its historical context into both its design and the overall visitor experience.

Through its inviting green spaces, community engagement, cultural programming, recreational activities, and attention to detail, Bryant Park provides a humanizing experience in the heart of the bustling city. It offers a space for connection, relaxation, and enjoyment, fostering a sense of community and enhancing its visitors' overall quality of life. However, critics of the park argue for improvements in maintenance, equitable access, seating, shade, sustainable practices, interactive elements, safety, balance between commercial activities, and historical interpretation while also highlighting the need for sustainable practices and improved visitor engagement (Manning & More, 2002).

# LAKESHORE EAST PARK, CHICAGO

Lakeshore East Park is a beautiful urban oasis in downtown Chicago, Illinois. This park centers the Lakeshore East neighborhood and provides a serene and green space for residents and visitors. Situated prominently at the point where the Chicago River meets Lake Michigan, the 28-acre Lakeshore East project in the Chicago Loop was one of the city's final available plots of land. This mixed-use development encompasses 4,950 residential units, 1,500 hotel rooms, 2.2 million square feet of gross commercial space, 770,000 square feet of retail space, and an elementary school (Hill, 2021). It is accessible by public transportation and the underground pedway system that connects it to other downtown attractions. Lakeshore East is also within walking distance of Millennium Park, Grant Park, Navy Pier, the Magnificent Mile, and the Chicago Riverwalk (ArchDaily, 2009; Gang & Wolf, 2019).
