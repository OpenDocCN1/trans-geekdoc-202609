Green walls can vary in size, complexity, and design, ranging from small installations to expansive vertical gardens covering entire building exteriors.

Green walls typically consist of the following components (Sheweka & Magdy, 2011; Wong & Baldwin, 2016):

- **Support Structure**: The support structure is the foundation for growing plants. It includes a framework, trellis, or modular system that is securely attached to the wall or façade.
- **Growing Medium**: The growing medium is a soil-like substrate that provides a medium for plant roots to anchor and receive nutrients. It is designed to be lightweight and porous, allowing for proper root growth and water retention.
- **Plants**: Various plant species, including ornamental and functional plants, can be chosen for green walls. The selection of plants depends on factors such as light conditions, climate, and the desired aesthetic.
- **Irrigation System**: Green walls require a reliable irrigation system to deliver water and nutrients to plants. Irrigation can be achieved through drip systems, hydroponic methods, or automated misting.
- **Drainage**: Proper drainage is crucial to prevent water accumulation and ensure the health of the plants. Drainage systems are designed to remove excess water and prevent root rot.

Green walls offer several benefits, both aesthetic and functional (Cuce, 2017):

- **Aesthetic Enhancement**: Green walls add natural beauty and visual appeal to spaces, softening hard architectural lines and creating a soothing and inviting atmosphere.
- **Air Purification**: Plants in green walls help improve air quality by absorbing pollutants and releasing oxygen, contributing to a healthier indoor environment.
- **Sound Insulation**: The foliage of green walls can serve as a sound barrier, absorbing and diffusing noise and improving acoustic conditions.
- **Biophilic Connection**: Greenery promotes a connection to nature, linked to improved well-being and reduced stress.
- **Thermal Regulation**: Green walls can regulate indoor temperatures by providing insulation and reducing heat absorption.
- **Space Optimization**: Green walls are ideal for areas with limited horizontal space, allowing the integration of greenery in urban environments.
- **Branding and Identity**: Incorporating green walls can reflect a commitment to sustainability and environmental consciousness.
- **Biodiversity**: Depending on plant selection, green walls can support biodiversity by providing habitats for insects and tiny organisms.

Green walls can be found in various settings, including commercial buildings, offices, hotels, shopping centers, and public spaces, contributing to a harmonious blend of nature and architecture. See the example of the Shanghai Tower’s indoor spaces (Figure 11.7).

## Key Takeaways: Planning and Design Principles

Integrating sky parks and gardens in tall buildings requires thoughtful planning and design to maximize their functionality, aesthetics, and sustainability. Here are some critical considerations for the planning and design process:

1. **Structural and Engineering Assessment**:
    - Whether working with old or new buildings, structural engineers should evaluate the feasibility of incorporating green spaces at various heights to ensure the building can support the additional loads.

2. **Concept Development**:
    - Develop a clear concept and vision for the sky park or garden, considering the purpose, theme, desired user experience, and overall design objectives.
    - Identify the unique selling points and desired features, such as seating areas, walking paths, recreational spaces, water features, or panoramic viewpoints.

3. **Site Analysis and Microclimate Considerations**:
    - For outdoor parks, conduct a thorough site analysis to understand the microclimate and environmental conditions of the building’s location.
    - Factors such as sunlight exposure, wind patterns, temperature variations, and noise levels should inform the design.
    - Use this information to select appropriate plant species, design shading structures, and consider the need for wind mitigation measures.

4. **Landscape Design and Plant Selection**:
    - Collaborate with landscape architects and horticulturists to design the green spaces within the building. Consider the chosen plant species’ aesthetics, functionality, and maintenance requirements.
    - Select vegetation that can thrive in a specific microclimate and is easily maintained in an elevated environment.
    - Integrate green walls, vertical gardens, hanging plants, or rooftop vegetation to maximize greenery and create a pleasant atmosphere (Tian & Jin, 2011).

5. **Accessibility and Circulation**:
    - Ensure the sky park or garden is accessible to building occupants and visitors.
    - Design safe and convenient access points, including elevators, staircases, or dedicated entrances.
    - Consider universal design principles to ensure accessibility for people of all abilities.
    - Create a coherent circulation design that effectively directs people through the different sections of the sky park or garden.

6. **Amenities and Functionality**:
    - Incorporate amenities and functional spaces within the sky park or garden to enhance user experience.
    - Include seating areas, gathering spaces, recreational facilities, and possibly food and beverage outlets.

7. **Sustainability Considerations**:
    - Integrate sustainable design practices into the sky park or garden.
    - Utilize green infrastructure elements such as rain gardens, living walls, or green roofs to manage stormwater runoff and improve insulation.
    - Incorporate energy-efficient lighting and irrigation systems.
    - Explore opportunities for vertical farming, composting, or recycling within the space.

8. **Maintenance and Management**:
    - Develop a maintenance and management plan to ensure the long-term sustainability and attractiveness of the sky park or garden.
    - Consider the required maintenance tasks, including pruning, irrigation, and regular inspections.
    - Collaborate with building management or external partners to establish ongoing maintenance responsibilities, schedules, and resources.

9. **Safety and Security**:
    - Prioritize safety and security in the sky park or garden design. Implement appropriate lighting, signage, and wayfinding elements.
    - Consider the installation of safety barriers or railings where necessary.
    - Collaborate with building management and security teams to establish protocols and ensure the safety of users.

10. **Community Engagement and Programming**:
    - Engage with building occupants and the surrounding community to foster a sense of ownership and encourage sky park or garden usage.
    - Develop programming initiatives such as fitness classes, workshops, or cultural events to activate the space and promote social interaction.

By considering these factors and engaging with relevant experts and stakeholders, the planning and design of sky parks and gardens in tall buildings can create stunning and functional green spaces that enhance the well-being of occupants, promote sustainability, and contribute to the overall livability of urban environments.
