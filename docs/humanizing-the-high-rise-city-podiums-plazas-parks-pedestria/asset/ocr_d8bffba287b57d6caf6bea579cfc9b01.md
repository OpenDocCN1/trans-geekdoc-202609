+   * **Double Horizon:** Sarah Sze's Double Horizon, a 5,500-pound boulder split open like a geode, uses tiles to create pixellated sky images, highlighting the fragility of time and the desire for permanence.

+   * **Deep Gradient/Suspect Terrain:** John Roloff's 18-foot-high sculpture, “Deep Gradient/Suspect Terrain,” creates an illusion of land and convention space, symbolizing the ocean and its surface.

+   * **Roll:** Brendon Monroe's “Roll” is a modern, fluid mural inspired by water and air movement, resembling a layer of fog over San Francisco’s coastal mountains below the Paseo bridge.

+   * **Genesis:** Christine Corday's “Genesis” entrance to Moscone North and Yerba Buena Gardens features massive stainless steel arc segments and a concrete form.

+   * **PointCloud:** Leo Villareal’s “PointCloud” light installation features 50,000 full-color LEDs and 800 mirrored stainless-steel rods, supporting the world’s largest light art installation, “The Bay Lights” on San Francisco’s Bay Bridge.

+   * **Oche Wat Te Ou:** In 1993, Jaune Quick-to-See Smith and James Lunain created the Oche Wat Te Ou, a reflection garden resembling Ohlone traditions, featuring a semicircular wood wall and Ohlone basket designs.

+   * **Urge:** Chico MacMurtrie’s interactive bronze sculpture, Urge to Stand, features physical mirroring and gaze, symbolizing human unity.

+   * **Three Dancing Figures | Untitled:** Keith Haring's 1989 untitled aluminum sculpture, initiated by the San Francisco Arts Commission, is a vibrant, primary-colored artwork at Howard and 3rd Streets.

+   * **Shaking Man:** Terry Allen’s bronze statue of a multidimensional business executive greets visitors at Yerba Buena Gardens, showcasing motion and gestures with interconnected figures.

An inviting and easily accessible urban park, Yerba Buena Gardens blends beautifully with its environment. People of all abilities may easily access and explore the park because of its various entrances, walkways, and transit link- ages. The park’s accessibility allows everyone to experience its humanizing qualities. Yerba Buena Gardens is more than just a park in San Francisco. It is a community hub celebrating the city’s diversity, creativity, and vitality. Its green spaces, recreational amenities, public art, and cultural enrichment allow people to relax, play, learn, and connect. Yerba Buena Gardens enriches the urban envi- ronment and the quality of life for residents and visitors (Figure 5.3).
