Along the east side, a large mosaic called *Four Seasons* by Marc Chagall
is on the upper level. It is a mosaic mural that depicts scenes from different
seasons and cultures, reflecting Chagall's vision of humanity and nature. The
21-meter (70 foot)-long, 4-meter (14-foot)-high, and 3 meter (10 foot)-wide
mural contains 4,000 square feet (372 square meters) of mosaic tiles that were
imported from Italy, France, Norway, Belgium, and Israel. The scenes include
a blue winter landscape with snowflakes and ice skaters, a spring scene with
flowers and lovers, a summer scene with sunflowers and musicians, and an
autumn scene with harvest and animals. The mural was commissioned by
the First National Bank of Chicago in 1974 and was unveiled in 1978. It is one
of Chagall's most prominent public works and a popular attraction for visitors
and locals alike. Initially, the mural was displayed in an open-air setting and,
therefore, subjected to Chicago's harsh winter, requiring the mural to be reno-vated in 1988 with the replacement of 25% of its tiles. In 1996, Skidmore,
Owning, and Merrill (SOM) designed a steel and glass shelter to protect the
Four Seasons from further weather damage (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Kamin, 2022;
Figure 3.14).

Overall, Chase Tower Plaza offers a welcoming and accessible outdoor envi-ronment in the heart of downtown Chicago. Its design, public art, and vibrant
atmosphere make it a notable public space for residents, workers, and visitors
to enjoy and engage with the surrounding urban fabric.
