# ## 9 Waterfront Parks

High-rise cities benefit immensely from waterfront parks as integral components of urban planning. These parks counterbalance the vertical density of towering structures, offering residents and visitors essential recreational opportunities, scenic beauty, and cultural spaces. Beyond their aesthetic contribution, waterfront parks enhance the overall quality of life by fostering community interaction, supporting biodiversity, and acting as climate-resilient green spaces. Waterfront parks place a premium on granting public access to the water and fostering diverse recreational activities such as kayaking, canoeing, fishing, boating, and swimming, all within a scenic and tranquil environment. Including boardwalks, piers, and docks is standard, extending the park experience onto the water. These features not only enhance accessibility but also invite visitors to engage in various water-related activities, fostering a sense of connection with the aquatic surroundings. Serving as dynamic hubs for active and passive recreation, these parks contribute to the psychological well-being of individuals, attract tourism, and promote sustainable urban development. By integrating waterfront parks, high-rise cities create vibrant, accessible, and resilient urban environments that celebrate the synergy between nature and architecture.

Cities are retrofitting aging waterfronts for several reasons (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Miradyanti et al., 2021):

+   1. **Economic Revitalization:** Aging waterfront areas often suffer from neglect and underutilization, leading to economic decline. Retrofitting these areas allows cities to attract investment, stimulate economic activity, and create jobs. By transforming aging waterfronts into vibrant and appealing destinations, cities can enhance their economic competitiveness and generate revenue through tourism, commercial activities, and waterfront-oriented businesses.
2. **Enhanced Public Space:** Retrofitting aging waterfronts allows cities to create attractive public spaces that cater to the needs and desires of residents and visitors. These spaces can offer opportunities for recreation, relaxation, social interaction, and cultural events. By providing well-designed and accessible public spaces, cities can improve the overall quality of life and contribute to the well-being of their communities.
