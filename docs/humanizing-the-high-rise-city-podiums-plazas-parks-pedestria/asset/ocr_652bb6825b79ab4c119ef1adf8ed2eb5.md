Greener-covered towers are a new generation of tall buildings characterized by substantial integration of vegetation. They aim to improve the quality of urban life by reducing air pollution, enhancing biodiversity, creating microclimates, and providing aesthetic and psychological benefits. Vegetation-clad towers are vital in humanizing skyscrapers by providing proximity to nature and its related advantages for the individuals residing or working in these edifices.

Here are some ways in which greener-covered towers contribute to humanizing tall buildings (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Boeri & Insuza, 2010; Guo et al., 2022; Yang & Chen, 2020):

+   1. **Connection to Nature:** By default, tall buildings communicate a feeling of detachment from the natural environment. Consequently, greener-covered towers help bridge this gap by introducing living plants and vegetation, creating a visual and sensory connection to nature. Vegetations provide a calming and refreshing ambience, enhancing well-being and reducing the sense of being disconnected from the natural world.

+   2. **Improved Aesthetics:** Adding greener softens the appearance of tall buildings, making them more visually appealing and harmonious with their surroundings. The vibrant colors, textures, and shapes of plants and foliage create a visually stimulating environment that can enhance the quality of life for building occupants and passersby.

+   3. **Health and Well-being Benefits:** Vegetation in greener-covered towers brings numerous health benefits. Research has shown that exposure to green spaces can reduce stress, improve mood, and enhance overall well-being. The sight of greener and the potential for increased natural light and better air quality can contribute to a healthier and more pleasant indoor environment.

+   4. **Noise Reduction:** Vegetation in greener-covered towers can help mitigate noise pollution from the surrounding urban environment. Plants act as natural sound absorbers, reducing noise levels and creating a quieter and more peaceful atmosphere inside the building. This can improve the quality of life for occupants and contribute to a more comfortable living or working environment.

+   5. **Thermal Comfort:** Greener-covered towers can help regulate indoor temperatures by providing natural insulation. The plants and foliage on the building's exterior help reduce heat gain in the summer and heat loss in the winter, contributing to energy efficiency and creating a more comfortable indoor climate. This can improve thermal comfort and reduce reliance on heating and cooling systems.
