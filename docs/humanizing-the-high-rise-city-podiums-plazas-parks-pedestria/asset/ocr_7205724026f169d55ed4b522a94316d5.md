# GARDEN ZONES

The High Line’s celebrated flora is influenced by the indigenous vegetation that thrived in this area before the park’s accessibility to the public. Visitors can investigate distinct environments uniquely adjusted to the microclimate formed by the surrounding urban landscape’s sun exposure, shadow, water availability, and wind patterns. Table 10.1 summarizes these garden zones and their vegetative features. Note that zones overlap with the park’s features described earlier.

**Table 10.1** Garden Zones along the High Line, Highlighting the Extensive Variety of Vegetation and Plants

| Zone | Name | Location | Garden Features: Summary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Donald Pels and Wendy Keys Gansevoort Woodland | At Gansevoort St. | It features gray birch and serviceberry trees, elevated steel planters, and a lush wisteria and hydrangea vine curtain. |
| 2 | Washington Grasslands & Woodland Edge | At 13th St. | Washington Grasslands Prairie showcases grasses and sun-loving perennials, while the Woodland Edge features shade-tolerant perennials, grasses, and woody plants. |
| 3 | Diller-von Furstenberg Sundeck & Water Feature | Between 14th St. and 15th St. | Kids and barefoot tourists alike adore this water feature. Raised steel planters along the route are home to wetlands plants such as swamp milkweed, rose mallow, and cattails. |
| 4 | Hudson River Overlook | Between 14th St. and 15th St. | It features barges, tugboats, sailboats, ocean liners, and sunsets. The planting beds between 14th Street and Chelsea Market Passage contain native plants, creating a striking silhouette against the skyline. |
| 5 | Northern Spur Preserve | At 16th St. | It includes sedges, asters, and crab apples, reimagines the High Line’s former landscape, crossing 10th Avenue to reach the Merchants Refrigerating Company Warehouse and offering stunning views. |
