
## 14 Public Art

As explained in Chapter 8, many high-rise cities face the problem of architectural blandness, where similar building designs fill the skyline and create a visual uniformity that reduces the distinctiveness and identity of the place. The lack of variety in architectural styles can weaken a city’s unique character, turning it into a landscape marked by generic structures. This blandness affects aesthetic diversity, adding to a lively urban environment and creating navigation and landmark recognition difficulties. The loss of architectural diversity may influence the sense of place, making it hard for residents and visitors to establish a meaningful bond with the cityscape.

In response to the challenge of architectural monotony and the overwhelming nature of towering structures in high-rise cities, a compelling solution lies in embracing public art installations to inject character into the urban landscape. Public art, with its ability to transform and enrich the public realm, is a powerful tool to counterbalance the imposing nature of uniform building designs. Artistic installations, ranging from sculptures and murals to interactive installations, introduce a layer of creativity and cultural expression, breaking the visual monotony and creating points of interest throughout the cityscape. These installations serve as aesthetic focal points and contribute to the unique identity of different neighborhoods within the high-rise city.

Public art is crucial in humanizing cities by bringing artistic expression and creativity into urban environments. Here are some critical roles of public art in this context (Clammer, 2014; Finkelpearl, 2000; Girard, 2013; Sommer, 2013):

- **Enhancing Aesthetics.** Public art adds beauty, visual interest, and creativity by injecting color, form, and artistic elements into cities, making the environment more visually appealing and engaging. It breaks the monotony of the built environment and creates a sense of surprise and delight for residents and visitors.
- **Creating Landmarks and Identity:** A city’s public artworks have the potential to become famous symbols. These features help to define a neighborhood or district and give it its personality. People use them as landmarks to find their way around cities. Public art landmarks represent a city's history and culture and increase civic pride and a feeling of belonging among locals.
- **Encouraging Social Interaction:** Public art provides social interaction and community engagement spaces. Installations such as sculptures, interactive artworks, or performance spaces create opportunities for people to gather, connect, and share experiences. They catalyze conversation, bringing people together from different backgrounds and fostering a sense of community.
- **Stimulating Cultural Expression:** Various forms of cultural expression are showcased and celebrated through public art. A city's history, traditions, and tales can be reflected in it, which can promote cultural variety and inclusivity. Artists in the community can find a voice and an audience through public art projects like murals, performances, or installations, which can enrich the city's cultural landscape.
- **Activating Public Spaces:** Public art activates public spaces and revitalizes underutilized areas by creating a sense of place, transforming empty plazas, vacant walls, or neglected spaces, and inviting people to engage with their surroundings. It encourages people to spend time outdoors, promotes walkability, and activates areas that may go unnoticed.
- **Provoking Thought and Dialogue:** Prompting thinking and conversation, public art has the power to disprove assumptions. Public discourse and understanding are sparked by artistic works that tackle political, social, or environmental concerns. A more involved and educated populace can result from their ability to stimulate critical thinking, increase empathy, and dispel myths.
- **Enhancing Well-being and Quality of Life:** Public art can uplift spirits, inspire creativity, and improve the well-being of individuals in a dense urban environment. It offers respite, contemplation, and inspiration amid the hustle and bustle of city life. Public art also contributes to a sense of pride and ownership in the community, fostering a positive urban experience.

The following examples illustrate how public art can help humanize the high-rise city.

### THE BRIDGEHOUSES’ SCULPTURES, CHICAGO

In 1928, four relief sculptures portraying scenes from Chicago's history were added to the outward-facing walls of the bridgehouses along Michigan Avenue. These sculptures serve as captivating visual representations of significant moments in the city's past. The northern bridgehouses feature sculptures commissioned by William Wrigley, Jr. and created by James Earle Fraser. "The Discoverers" depicts explorers Louis Joliet, Jacques Marquette, René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, and Henri de Tonti, commemorating their exploration of the region. “The Pioneers” showcases John Kinzie leading a group through the wilderness, symbolizing the city’s early settlement and pioneering spirit (Figure 14.1). On the southern bridgehouses, sculptures commissioned by the B. F. Ferguson Monument Fund and created by Henry Hering depict critical moments in Chicago’s history. “Defense” portrays Ensign George Ronan in a scene from the Battle of Fort Dearborn in 1812, representing the city’s involvement in the conflict. “Regeneration” captures the resilience of Chicagoans as workers rebuild the city after the devastating Great Chicago Fire of 1871, symbolizing the city’s ability to overcome adversity and thrive. These sculptures, created by talented artists of their time, bring Chicago’s history to life through visual storytelling. They serve as permanent reminders of the city’s past, honoring the explorers, pioneers, defenders, and rebuilders who shaped Chicago into the vibrant metropolis it is today. The placement of these sculptures on the bridgehouses along Michigan Avenue ensures their visibility to both residents and visitors, adding cultural and historical significance to the city’s iconic thoroughfare. They invite viewers to engage with the city’s history, sparking curiosity and appreciation for the stories they represent. They enrich the urban landscape, serving as enduring tributes to the city’s past and inspiring a connection to its history (Al-Kodmany, 2021; Chicago Monuments Project).

### THE HEALD SQUARE MONUMENT, CHICAGO

Lorado Taft’s Heald Square Monument is a remarkable artwork that pays tribute to three Revolutionary War heroes. The central figure of the monument is George Washington (1732–1799), a revered figure in American history. He stands tall and commanding, symbolizing his leadership and pivotal role in the American Revolution. Flanking Washington on his left is Haym Salomon, a Jewish businessman who played a crucial role in financing the war effort. At the same time, Robert Morris, a prominent merchant and statesman, stands on his right. This sculpture captures the essence of these three heroic figures, representing their contributions to the American Revolution and the founding of the United States. This 11-foot-high bronze sculpture stands atop a six-foot-high granite base, creating a significant presence on Lower Wacker Drive, between Wabash and State Street, on the south side of the Chicago River (Figure 14.2). The bronze material used for the sculpture adds a sense of permanence and durability, underscoring the enduring legacy of these heroes and their impact on American history. The placement of the Heald Square Monument on Lower Wacker Drive allows it to be seen and appreciated by both pedestrians and motorists passing through the bustling area.
