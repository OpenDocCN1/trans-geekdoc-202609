The plaza is also home to several artworks, such as the bronze Statue of Atlas, the Channel Gardens with seasonal displays of flowers and fountains, and the iconic Christmas tree lit yearly in a festive ceremony. Originally and best known for over 50 years as the RCA Building, it features a grand architectural design characterized by Art Deco influences and showcases a series of elegant buildings, including the GE Building (General Electric Building, now known as the Comcast Building), which stands as the complex's center-piece. The buildings are known for their distinctive limestone façades, intricate detailing, and iconic rooftop decorations (Fitzgerald, 2003; Weisman, 1951).

The Rink at Rockefeller Center is a popular ice-skating rink that operates in winter, attracting locals and tourists. The Channel Gardens is a prominent feature located in Rockefeller Center. It serves as an attractive pedestrian walkway connecting Fifth Avenue and Rockefeller Plaza. The Channel Gardens consists of a series of planters and fountains that run along the central axis of the Rockefeller Center. It undergoes seasonal transformations throughout the year, showcasing beautiful displays of flowers, plants, and decorations that reflect the changing seasons. Every year, a massive Christmas tree is erected in the plaza, attracting millions of visitors who come to see its stunning decorations. The tree is often adorned with thousands of colorful lights and a Swarovski crystal star. The Statue of Prometheus in Rockefeller Center is a famous sculpture in the Lower Plaza of Rockefeller Center. Designed by the American sculptor Paul Manship, the statue depicts the Greek mythological figure Prometheus, known for stealing fire from the gods and giving it to humanity. The sculpture stands at the center of the Lower Plaza, directly in front of the iconic ice-skating rink. It was installed in 1934 and has become one of Rockefeller Center's most recognizable and enduring symbols. The statue is made of gilded bronze and stands approximately 18 feet tall. Prometheus is depicted holding a flame above his head, symbolizing the gift of fire. The sculpture is highly detailed, showcasing Manship's skills in sculpting dynamic and expressive forms. In action, the skating rink and the flow of skaters are often portrayed alongside Prometheus. Considering the historical backdrop of the 1930s, which included the Great Depression and the approaching World War II, as well as the emergence of 20th-century Modernism, this sculpture made of gilded, cast bronze gains substantial significance (Weisman, 1951; (Figure 3.17).

The plaza serves as a venue for various cultural events and public gatherings throughout the year. These events include concerts, art installations, exhibitions, and seasonal festivals. The plaza's expansive space and iconic backdrop make it an ideal location for large-scale public celebrations and performances. The plaza is a popular destination for tourists and locals who enjoy its art, history, and entertainment options. Rockefeller Center Plaza is more than just a public space. It symbolizes New York City's culture, creativity, and vitality. It is a place where people can connect with each other and the world. It is a place where dreams are made and celebrated.
