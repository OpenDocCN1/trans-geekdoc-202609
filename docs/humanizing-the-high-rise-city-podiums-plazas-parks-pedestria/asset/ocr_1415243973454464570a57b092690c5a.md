The trail’s carefully curated design incorporates lush landscaping, verdant trees, and diverse plants, contributing to its aesthetic appeal. Additionally, the inclusion of thoughtfully designed play areas, stylish LED lighting fixtures, comfortable furniture, artistic railing, and welcoming seating areas enhances the overall experience for visitors. Public restrooms provide convenience, ensuring a visitor-friendly environment. Despite its early completion, ongoing projects focusing on retrofitting and improvement underscore a commitment to continuous enhancement, maintaining the Recreational Trail as a dynamic and cherished urban oasis (Figure 9.2).

The western section of the Chicago Riverwalk, stretching from Michigan Avenue to Lake Street, offers a distinct contrast to its eastern counterpart. Unlike the natural park-like ambiance of the east, the “west part” embodies the essence of civic space. Here, the landscape takes on a more urban character, reflecting the dynamic and bustling atmosphere of the city. As visitors traverse this stretch, they are immersed in an environment that seamlessly blends urban design with recreational elements, providing a multifaceted experience within the heart of Chicago’s vibrant urban fabric.

The west part consists of block-long sections or “bays” that contain civic plazas. It was constructed in three phases (Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3), as follows.

# Phase 1

Phase 1, spanning from Michigan Avenue to State Street, was finalized in 2009 and encompasses noteworthy landmarks, including the McCormick Bridgehouse & Chicago River Museum and Chicago’s Vietnam Veterans Memorial. Two pivotal pedestrian connections at the river level were strategically incorporated to enhance the path’s continuity. In the previous layout, the pedestrian experience was hindered and dissuasive, as individuals were required to ascend stairways to the upper level of Wacker Drive, cross a city street, and then descend once more to reach the river level. The introduction of under-bridge crossings effectively addressed this challenge, providing pedestrians with a seamless and uninterrupted route along the Chicago River (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Chicago Riverwalk; Kamin, 2015, 2023).
