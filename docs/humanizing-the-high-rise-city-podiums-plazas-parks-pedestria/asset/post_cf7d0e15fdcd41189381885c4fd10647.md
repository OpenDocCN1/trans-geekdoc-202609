## Gate of the Orient, Suzhou [2013]

The Gate of the Orient, a 302-meter-tall skyscraper in Suzhou, China, symbolizes the city's historic gardens and canals. Completed in 2013, the building offers a panoramic view of Jinji Lake and the surrounding urban landscape. Amenities and facilities for the building's residents, employees, and guests are housed in the podium.

The podium design of the Gate of the Orient is a remarkable departure from traditional, boxy structures, introducing a sculptural and curving form that imparts a dynamic sense of movement and visual intrigue. Carefully calibrated dimensions ensure the podium contributes to a visually pleasant setting without overwhelming or dominating pedestrians, creating a harmonious urban environment.

### Podiums

One noteworthy aspect is the deliberate contrast between the podium’s extended horizontality and the background tower’s verticality. This intentional design choice adds a layer of visual interest, and the sloping roof of the podium not only serves a functional purpose but symbolically echoes the tower’s broad arch, forming a cohesive and aesthetically pleasing composition (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Binder, 2015).

The podium’s ground-level architecture goes beyond mere functionality, offering broad walkways, plazas, and open spaces that invite pedestrians into a welcoming and user-friendly environment. This design encourages social contact, leisure, and relaxation, enhancing the overall experience and fostering a connection to the natural world.

To meet the needs of pedestrians, the spatial arrangement includes comfortable seating areas, shade structures, landscaping, and navigational signage, ensuring easy navigation and a pleasant experience for visitors. The design, therefore, pays meticulous attention to utility, ergonomics, and proportions, aligning with the principles of human scale and creating a podium that seamlessly integrates into the urban fabric.

In conclusion, the Gate of the Orient in Suzhou’s podium design stands out for its commitment to respecting the human scale, balancing aesthetics with functionality, and providing an environment that enriches the experience of both residents and visitors alike (Figure 2.28).

## PARKROYAL COLLECTION Pickering, Singapore [2013]

The PARKROYAL COLLECTION Pickering in Singapore is renowned for its innovative architectural design that emphasizes human scale by integrating biophilic design principles and showcasing aesthetic appeal. The 16-floor, 75-meter building blends sustainable design principles, lush greenery, and modern aesthetics (Beatley, 2016). The striking façade features terraces and balconies covered in lush vegetation, creating a vertical garden effect and a refreshing contrast to the urban landscape (See Chapter 13). The project was completed in 2013.

The podium design of PARKROYAL COLLECTION Pickering is a testament to its visual appeal and seamless integration of contemporary architecture with organic forms. Striking a balance between sleek lines, curved shapes, and sustainable materials, the design exudes elegance and sophistication. This attention to detail contributes to a visually captivating podium harmonious with its natural surroundings.

The multisensory experience is a key design feature, with water elements such as cascading waterfalls and reflective pools adding a soothing auditory and visual dimension. These water features enhance the overall ambiance and create a connection to nature, fostering a serene environment for visitors.

Natural materials, including wood and stone, are crucial in adding a tactile and warm quality to the podium. This incorporation of organic elements contributes to the sensory experience and aligns with biophilic design principles, creating a space where guests can connect with nature amid the urban setting (Al-Kodmany, 2023; Beatley, 2016).

Crucially, the podium design respects human scale, creating a welcoming and accessible environment for individuals to navigate comfortably. This consideration for the pedestrian experience is pivotal, ensuring the space is inviting and accommodating.

In summary, the PARKROYAL COLLECTION Pickering's podium design stands out for its visual appeal, commitment to sustainability, and emphasis on sensory experiences. By seamlessly blending architectural sophistication with natural elements, the podium creates an engaging and aesthetically pleasing space for visitors to enjoy and connect with nature (Figure 2.29).

## CapitaLand Building, Hangzhou [2017]

CapitaLand Building, also known as Raffles City Hangzhou, is in Hangzhou, one of China’s most picturesque cities. It is the eighth Raffles City development in China by CapitaLand. Completed in 2017, the architectural design of the CapitaLand Building is inspired by the movement of the Qiantang River and the historic cityscape of Hangzhou. The two streamlined towers twist and rise to 250 meters, creating a dynamic and elegant landmark along the green axis of the city’s new CBD (Qian, 2015).

The podium design of CapitaLand Building is characterized by a sculptured six-story pedestal that serves as a crucial link connecting the towers to the broader complex. This multifunctional pedestal has been thoughtfully designed to accommodate various amenities, including parking facilities, shops, restaurants, and entertainment venues. Additionally, it features a direct underground connection to the nearby metro stop, enhancing accessibility for the inhabitants and visitors.

An integral aspect of the podium’s design is its commitment to human-scale proportions, ensuring aesthetic appeal and practical functionality. The podium’s swooping and arching glass façade creates a striking visual impression, instilling a sense of arrival. This architectural feature contributes to an overall atmosphere of openness and transparency, enhancing the visual experience for those approaching the building.

Furthermore, a landscaped plaza, serving as an additional podium component, expands the public realm around the complex. This outdoor space adds a touch of greenery and fosters a lively and inviting environment for both inhabitants and guests. The plaza’s design aligns with contemporary urban planning principles, creating a communal space that encourages social interaction and contributes to the well-being of the building’s occupants.

In summary, the CapitaLand Building’s podium design in Hangzhou integrates functionality, aesthetics, and human-scale considerations. The sculptured pedestal, with its diverse amenities and visually striking features, enhances the overall appeal of the complex while providing a welcoming and engaging environment for the community (Figure 2.30).

## River Point Tower, Chicago [2017]

Designed by Pickard Chilton Architecture + Master Planning based in New Haven, CT, and completed in 2017, the 52-story River Point is a dramatic landmark addition to the City of Chicago. The tower’s striking image is created by a sleekly sculpted form and a shimmering convex façade featuring an exuberant, silver-blue curving glass wall that creates a robust spatial kinship with its neighboring building, 333 W. Wacker Drive, which also features a tremendous curving green glass curtainwall.

The convex façade displays a tall, tilted parabolic arch at its base, echoed by a shallower arch at the tower’s top. The arch at the base is structural: the whole façade and columns tilt due to the active Amtrak and Metra railroad tracks and subway portion of the CTA Blue Line underneath. However, the arch at the top is decorative (Al-Kodmany, 2020; Kamin, 2016, 2022).
