# 4 Streets as Plazas

How would you like to live in a place where the streets are not for cars but for people? That’s the idea behind streets as pedestrian plazas, an innovative way of designing urban spaces that makes them more enjoyable, lively, and diverse. Streets as plazas, also known as pedestrianized streets or shared spaces, refer to transforming streets into pedestrian-friendly public spaces prioritizing walking, cycling, and social activities over vehicular traffic. This approach aims to create vibrant and accessible urban environments that promote human interaction, eco-nomic vitality, and community engagement (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Banerjee, 2001; Whyte, 1980).

The “Streets as Plazas” concept represents a transformative urban phi-losophy that reimagines traditional thoroughfares as dynamic public spaces, transcending their conventional roles as mere conduits for vehicular traffic. In this innovative approach to urban design, streets are envisioned as multifunctional plazas, vibrant and inclusive communal areas that prioritize pedestrians, com-munity engagement, and cultural activities. This paradigm shift challenges the conventional understanding of streets as utilitarian passages, emphasizing their potential to serve as focal points for social interaction, recreation, and artistic expression (Oranratmañee & Sachakul, 2014).

At the core of the “Streets as Plazas” concept is a commitment to enhancing the quality of urban life by creating spaces that encourage a sense of community and connectivity. By prioritizing pedestrian-friendly features, such as widened pathways, green spaces, and inviting street furniture, these transformed streets aim to foster a more walkable and accessible urban environment. The benefits extend beyond more aesthetics, influencing residents’ physical and mental well-being as they embrace a more active and social lifestyle within their immediate surroundings (Bertolini, 2020; Simpson, 2000).

Moreover, the concept recognizes streets’ intrinsic cultural and historical significance within the urban landscape. By reimagining them as plazas, cities can tap into the potential of these spaces to become dynamic stages for cultural events, public art installations, and community celebrations. Integrating cultural elements adds vibrancy to the urban fabric and contributes to a more profound sense of place identity, fostering pride and connection among residents (Elabb & Hallowell, 2014; Gehl, 1971, 2010; Nordahl, 2014).
