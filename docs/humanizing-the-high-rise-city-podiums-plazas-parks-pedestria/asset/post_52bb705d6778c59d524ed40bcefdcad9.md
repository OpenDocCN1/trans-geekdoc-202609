## Wrigley Square

Wrigley Square, situated northwest of Millennium Park, houses the Millennium Monument, symbolizing architectural history and philanthropy. Its circular colonnade pays homage to an earlier structure that once adorned Grant Park, invoking a sense of nostalgia through its Classical architectural style. The columns create a timeless ambiance reminiscent of ancient structures, establishing a connection to the past. Notably, the names of the generous donors who played a pivotal role in making Millennium Park a reality are inscribed on the colonnade, a lasting acknowledgment of their contributions.

At the heart of the monument, a tranquil fountain beckons visitors, providing a serene space for relaxation and contemplation. Also, a large lawn before the monument adds to the scene's qualities of grandeur, elegance, and spaciousness (Figure 7.5). This calm and elegant setting starkly contrasts with the adjacent lively and famous Crown Fountain, offering a diverse range of experiences within the dynamic urban landscape of Millennium Park. The Millennium Monument becomes a focal point where history, architecture, and philanthropy converge to create a space of beauty and reflection within the bustling urban environment (Gilfoyle, 2006).

## Lurie Garden

Lurie Garden, a verdant expanse covering five acres, emerges as a “prairie oasis” in the southern reaches of Millennium Park, juxtaposed against Gehry’s Bandshell and the Great Lawn. This ecological haven seamlessly melds landscape architecture with environmental consciousness, creating a dynamic and ever-changing tapestry of natural beauty (Figure 7.6). A celebration of biodiversity, the garden boasts over 240 varieties of perennial plants, predominantly native to North America and Illinois. Its transformative allure is accentuated by the seasonal ebb and flow, offering visitors an ever-evolving panorama. Lurie Garden stands as a testament to the harmonious integration of ecological principles and landscape design, providing a serene escape within the heart of the bustling urban landscape of Millennium Park (Gifford, 2006).

Beyond its visual splendor, Lurie Garden is a haven for wildlife, attracting butterflies, honeybees, and songbirds. The deliberate use of natural materials and a commitment to chemical-free maintenance contribute to a thriving ecosystem. This dedication to ecological sensitivity aligns with the city's motto, "Urbs in Horto" (City in a Garden), echoing Chicago's historical metamorphosis from marshland to a green urban oasis. Lurie Garden not only stands as an aesthetic marvel but also embodies sustainability principles, fostering a harmonious coexistence between urban development and the natural world. Its commitment to biodiversity and environmentally conscious practices positions it as a model for integrating ecological principles within the urban fabric (Al-Kodmany, 2020b).

Lurie Garden is a testament to the coexistence of nature and urbanity in contrast to the formal plantings elsewhere in the park and the rigidity of the city's urban grid. Its organic layout and diverse plant life break away from traditional landscape design, offering a reprieve from the structured surroundings. With its immersive greenery, the garden enriches Millennium Park. It pays homage to Chicago's deep-rooted commitment to cultivating a harmonious blend of city and nature, seamlessly merging history, ecology, and aesthetics. Lurie Garden's unique design and ecological consciousness contribute to the park's multifaceted character, providing visitors with a tranquil escape within the vibrant urban landscape of Millennium Park.

Lurie Garden integrates a linear pool, offering a tranquil space for visitors to unwind and appreciate the surrounding natural scenery. The garden's design, partly concealed by tall, illuminated hedges, imparts a sense of intimacy and mystery, symbolizing the resilience of the "City of Big Shoulders." As day transitions to night, the illuminated hedges contribute to the garden's enchanting atmosphere. Linked to the Art Institute by a pedestrian bridge, Lurie Garden establishes a seamless connection between art and nature. Named after philanthropist Ann Lurie, whose generous donation ensures its upkeep, the garden is a testament to the collaborative vision of Gustafson Guthrie Nichol Ltd, Seattle, and artist Piet Oudolf from the Netherlands.

## McCormick Tribune Plaza and Ice Rink

The McCormick Tribune Plaza and Ice Rink, situated near the iconic Cloud Gate sculpture, transforms into a vibrant focal point within Millennium Park, offering an ever-changing array of attractions that align with the seasons. During the cold season, typically from mid-November to mid-March, the plaza undergoes an enchanting metamorphosis into a cherished ice-skating rink, spanning an expansive 16,000 square feet (1,487 square meters) (Figure 7.8). Against the glittering backdrop of Cloud Gate, the rink becomes a winter wonderland, attracting a diverse audience of locals and tourists. Here, amid the lively atmosphere, visitors delight in the timeless joy of gliding on ice, creating a festive ambiance that encapsulates the season's spirit.

The McCormick Tribune Plaza seamlessly transitions into a charming outdoor dining area as the weather warms. Positioned against the spectacular backdrop of Cloud Gate and the bustling Michigan Avenue, the plaza becomes a scenic oasis where visitors can relish delectable meals while soaking in the breathtaking views. This transformation reflects Millennium Park's adaptability and year-round appeal, catering to diverse preferences and ensuring a captivating experience for visitors, whether they seek the thrill of winter sports or the leisurely enjoyment of open-air dining. The McCormick Tribune Plaza, with its dual role as an enchanting ice-skating rink and a picturesque dining destination, adds an extra layer of vibrancy to the Cloud Gate experience, enhancing Millennium Park's status as a dynamic urban sanctuary (Gilfoyle, 2006).

In summary, Millennium Park, a 24.5-acre public park in downtown Chicago, Illinois, is a popular attraction with millions of visitors annually. Designed in 1997 to celebrate the millennium and revitalize the former rail yard site, the park faced challenges such as cost overruns, design changes, environmental issues, and legal disputes. Despite these challenges, Millennium Park has been praised for its innovative design, artistic value, and economic impact. The park has won awards and honors, including the American Institute of Architects' Honor Award, the Rudy Bruner Award for Urban Excellence, and the Urban Land Institute's Award for Excellence. The park has generated significant revenue for the city through tourism, taxes, and private donations, adding value to nearby properties and creating permanent jobs. The park showcases Chicago's creativity, diversity, and vitality while providing recreation and relaxation for residents and visitors.
