The outdoor plaza at 311 South Wacker Plaza is a dynamic and lively space that improves the quality of life for tenants and visitors alike. It is a place where people can unwind, socialize, and have fun in the city’s center (Saliga & Clarke, 1998).

One of the most striking features of 311 South Wacker Drive is its winter garden and lobby. The winter garden is a two-level glass-ceilinged space that spans five stories and is filled with greenery and a fountain. The fountain is adorned with a bronze sculpture by Raymond Kaskey called “Gem of the Lakes,” representing Chicago as a city of broad shoulders and engineering feats. The sculpture is a 12-foot-tall heroic figure wearing a cape, symbolizing the reversal of the Chicago River’s flow. The figure holds a shell that forms the fountain’s basin and is taken from the city seal. The sculpture looks over the garden from the Wacker entrance and is a tribute to Chicago’s history and culture. The winter garden and lobby of 311 South Wacker Drive are a welcoming oasis for visitors and tenants alike. The lobby connects to the one-acre plaza seamlessly (Pridmore & Larson, 2005).
