## Westin Bund Center Shanghai, Shanghai [2002]

In the heart of Shanghai, the Westin Bund Center is a 50-story hotel tower characterized by a Postmodernist architectural style. Designed by John Portman & Associates, the building was completed in 2002. One of the design's unique aspects is the crown's geometry, which consists of 16 indicator lights that form a circular pattern. The crown symbolizes the unity and harmony of the city and its people (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Binder, 2015). Another unique aspect is the dramatic glass staircase that connects the lobby and the atrium, creating a sense of transparency and openness.

The Westin Bund Center Shanghai podium design is a testament to thoughtful and refined architecture, focusing on creating a welcoming and harmonious environment for visitors. At the building’s entrance, a serene water feature takes center stage, adding a touch of tranquility and elegance to the space. This water feature acts as a visual focal point and contributes to a serene atmosphere, enhancing the overall experience for those approaching the entrance.

An exotic canopy crowns the entrance, providing both artistic charm and functional significance. This canopy adds a distinctive architectural feature and serves as a shelter, offering protection from the elements for those entering or exiting the building. The exotic design of the canopy contributes to the overall sense of elegance and sophistication, making a strong visual statement.

Abundant natural illumination is crucial in the entryway design, ensuring the space is well-lit and inviting. Natural light floods the area, creating a bright and airy ambience. This design choice enhances visibility and brings a sense of openness to the entrance, fostering a connection with the surrounding environment.

The minimalist and refined white color scheme of the podium aligns seamlessly with the architectural style of the West Bund public edifices. This design creates a sense of unity and continuity within the broader urban context, establishing a visual language that complements the surrounding structures.

In summary, the Westin Bund Center Shanghai podium design has a serene water feature, abundant natural illumination, lush foliage, and an exotic canopy. Combined with a minimalist white color scheme, these elements are meticulously tailored to accommodate the human scale, ensuring visitors’ welcoming and aesthetically pleasing experience (Figure 2.15).

## One North Wacker, Chicago [2002]

One North Wacker, formerly UBS Tower, is a 50-story, 199-meter (653-foot)-tall skyscraper in Chicago’s Loop. Completed in 2002, the building was designed when Postmodernist architectural values shifted to contemporary styles. The building provides 163,000 square meters (1,754,000 square feet) of office space and amenities, including a conference center, health club, and a two-level underground garage (Goettsch, 2012). Designed by architectural firm Goettsch Partners, the building was sold for the highest per-square-foot price of any downtown Chicago office building since the late 1980s.

The podium design of One North Wacker showcases a careful consideration of transparency, aesthetics, and security, contributing to a dynamic and integrated urban experience. A highly transparent, cable-supported enclosure, constructed with low-iron glass and a non-reflecting coating, is featured at the building’s base. This design creates a seamless visual connection between the exterior and interior spaces, effectively integrating the lobby into the surrounding streetscape. The transparency of the base invites natural light into the lobby, fostering an open and inviting atmosphere within (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Goettsch, 2012).

To enhance the overall quality and sophistication of the building, high-quality finishing materials were employed indoors and outdoors. The lobby, elevated to four stories, stands as a grand and welcoming space. Its height contributes to a sense of openness and spatial continuity, further blurring the distinction between the interior and exterior environments.

The building’s entrance is marked by a gesture of hospitality, featuring circular granite seating spaces on the east and south sides of the outdoor plaza. These seating spaces provide a place for visitors to rest and engage with the urban surroundings and serve a dual purpose as planters while fulfilling security requirements mandated for skyscrapers post-9/11. Incorporating these planters seamlessly integrates security measures with landscape design, ensuring a safe and pleasant experience for occupants and visitors alike.

With its circular seating and planters, the outdoor plaza becomes a functional and aesthetically pleasing extension of the building’s interior spaces. This intentional blending of interior and exterior elements enhances the overall connectivity of the building with its urban context, creating a lively and engaging environment for both occupants and passersby.

In summary, the building’s base features a highly transparent, cable-supported enclosure with low-iron glass and a non-reflecting coating. This transparent base makes exterior spaces blend with interior spaces, allowing the lobby to become part of the streetscape. High-quality finishing materials were used indoors and outdoors, and the lobby’s height was elevated to four stories. The building’s entrance is a welcome gesture, with circular granite seating spaces on the east and south sides of the outdoor plaza. These planters function as security barriers, a requirement imposed on skyscrapers after 9/11 (Figure 2.16).

## 111 South Wacker, Chicago [2005]

111 South Wacker Drive is a 51-story, 208-meter (681-foot)-tall skyscraper in Chicago’s Loop, completed in 2005. The building’s architecture is striking and contemporary, featuring a sleek and elegant façade combining glass, steel, and other high-quality materials. The design incorporates clean lines, smooth surfaces, and a modern aesthetic, reflecting the sophistication and innovation of its architectural approach. The well-proportioned form and thoughtful composition contribute to its visual appeal and harmonious integration with the surrounding urban context.

The podium design of 111 South Wacker is a masterful composition that prioritizes openness, visibility, and a welcoming atmosphere. Anchored by a 12-story base, the building features a four-story lobby and an eight-story parking garage. The lobby, a central element of the base, stands out for its remarkable transparency, achieved through the use of a cable-supported enclosure with low-iron glass and a non-reflecting coating. This design choice allows for a seamless visual connection between the interior and exterior, creating an inviting and open ambience (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Goettsch, 2012).

Notably, the transition from the tower’s perimeter columns to only four lobby-level columns, spanning an impressive 25 meters (82 feet) between them, enhances the sense of openness in the lobby. Behind the lobby, the building’s core is clad in white marble, further emphasizing the commitment to visibility and openness.
