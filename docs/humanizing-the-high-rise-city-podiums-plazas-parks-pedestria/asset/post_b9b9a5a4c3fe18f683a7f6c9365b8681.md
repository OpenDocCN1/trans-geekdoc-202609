## BOSCO VERTICALE, MILAN

Bosco Verticale (Vertical Forest) is a pioneering example and the epitome of the greenery-covered high-rise building model. Its boldness, significant height, and extensive greeneries have promoted this project among the most remarkable. Nestled in Milan, Italy, Bosco Verticale consists of two residential towers renowned for seamlessly incorporating an abundance of plants and trees into their design. The visionary architecture is credited to the esteemed firm Stefano Boeri Architetti. The design was inspired by the traditional ivy-covered buildings in Italy but multiplied to create a living facade that absorbs CO₂, reduces energy consumption, and protects against noise and radiation. Bosco Verticale is a landmark of green design that demonstrates the possibility of integrating nature and urbanism harmoniously (Belcher et al., 2018; Flannery et al., 2015; Boeri & Insulza, 2010; Ishween, 2021; Varro, 2017).

### Architectural Design

Bosco Verticale comprises two towers measuring 80 meters and 112 meters in height, respectively. The towers have a unique shape with a graceful profile. They feature a contemporary design aesthetic, incorporating clean lines and a combination of glass, spacious balconies, and concrete elements. The defining feature of Bosco Verticale is the numerous balconies protruding from the facade of each tower. Crafted to cultivate a “vertical forest” ambiance, the balconies of Bosco Verticale exhibit diverse sizes and configurations, enriching the overall architectural composition with visual intrigue and dimension. The towers are designed with sustainability in mind. They include rainwater harvesting systems, photovoltaic panels for solar energy generation, and high-performance building envelopes to enhance energy efficiency. The integration of greenery also contributes to improved air quality and insulation (Flannery et al., 2015; Ishween, 2021; Varrato, 2017).

### Plant and Tree Integration

The selection of plant species for Bosco Verticale was carefully considered to ensure the vitality and sustainability of the vertical forest. The chosen plants include various trees, shrubs, and other vegetation suited to the local climate. The selection prioritizes species with unique root systems, adaptability to urban environments, and resistance to wind and pollution. The balconies of Bosco Verticale accommodate a different selection of plants, creating a diverse and visually dynamic facade. The two skyscrapers are adorned with various deciduous plants, such as beeches, yellow acacias, oaks, maples, ash trees, ferns, and ivy, in addition to the evergreen species. The trees are strategically placed at various heights, with taller trees on the lower floors to provide residents privacy and maximize visual impact. Light exposure, wind patterns, and artistic composition are some considerations that went into the intentional distribution of the plants throughout the balconies. The trees are supported by a complex system of structural elements and specially designed planters (Belcher et al., 2018; Boeri & Insulza, 2010). The ultimate configuration creates a lush and layered effect, resembling a natural forest ecosystem or a vertical forest (Figure 12.1).

The ongoing maintenance and care of the plants and trees in Bosco Verticale are critical to their longevity and vitality. A dedicated team of horticulturists and experts oversees the regular pruning, watering, and fertilization of the vegetation. Careful monitoring and management ensure that the greenery remains healthy and well-maintained.

### Awards and Recognition

Since its completion, Bosco Verticale has received several recognitions, certifications, and awards for its innovative and sustainable design, including:

- The 2014 International Highrise Award
- The 2015 Best Tall Building Worldwide by the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat (CTBUH)
- The 2018 Emporis Skyscraper Award

Overall, the architectural design and plant integration in Bosco Verticale aims to create a harmonious and sustainable living environment. The towers' unique form, combined with the extensive presence of vegetation, contributes to the aesthetic appeal, ecological value, and overall well-being of the residents and the surrounding urban context.

## ACROS FUKUOKA PREFECTURAL INTERNATIONAL HALL, FUKUOKA

Located in the heart of Fukuoka, Japan, the ACROS Fukuoka Prefectural International Hall is a hub for international, cultural, and informational interaction. It aimed to provide an innovative solution to a prevalent urban issue: combining a developer’s desire for lucrative site use with the public’s need for open green space. By developing an innovative agro-urban model, Fukuoka’s strategy satisfies both objectives.

Designed by pioneering green architect Emilio Ambasz, the Asian Cross Roads Over the Sea (ACROS) is a 17-story civic center building completed in 1995. The building integrates nearly 100,000 square meters of park space onto fifteen stepped, vegetated terraces with staircase-shaped rooftops that ascend the entire structure's height. Each terrace floor features a variety of gardens for contemplation, relaxation, and retreat from the city's congestion (Belogolovsky, 2020; Rinaldi, 2010; Majerska-Palubicka & Latussek, 2021).

### Architectural Design

ACROS Fukuoka features a stepped architectural design comprising a series of terraced levels that gradually ascend toward the top. The building resembles a giant staircase or pyramid, creating a visually striking profile and providing multiple levels for green spaces. Each terrace of ACROS Fukuoka serves as a garden, creating a cascading effect of lush greenery. The gardens are accessible to the public and provide opportunities for relaxation, recreation, and interaction with nature. The building exhibits a contemporary architectural style with clean lines, geometric shapes, and a combination of glass, steel, and concrete materials. The design seamlessly blends with the surrounding urban environment while offering a striking contrast with the natural elements incorporated within (Rinaldi, 2010; Majerska-Palubicka & Latussek, 2021).

### Plant and Tree Integration

The terraced levels of ACROS Fukuoka are adorned with various plants and trees. Each terrace is a mini-garden featuring different plant species and landscape designs. The gardens create a tranquil and visually appealing environment, inviting people to connect with nature within an urban setting. ACROS Fukuoka incorporates extensive green roofs covering various levels of the building. These green roofs provide additional green spaces, offer insulation, absorb rainwater, reduce the heat island effect, and contribute to energy efficiency.

ACROS Fukuoka showcases various plant species, including trees, shrubs, flowers, and groundcover plants. The selection encompasses native and non-native species chosen for their aesthetic qualities, adaptability to the local climate, and ability to provide shade and visual interest. The plant integration in ACROS Fukuoka follows sustainable principles. The building employs efficient irrigation systems, rainwater harvesting, and careful plant selection to reduce water consumption and ensure the long-term health of the vegetation (Figure 12.2).

At the center of ACROS Fukuoka is a large atrium called the “Symphony Hall Atrium.” It is a grand glass structure with soaring ceiling. Sunlight filters through, creating ethereal patterns and illuminating vibrant foliage, offering visitors a serene oasis in the heart of the building (Belogolovsky, 2020).
