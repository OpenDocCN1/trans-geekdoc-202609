# Areas for *Improvement*

+   * **Limited Green Space:** The plaza's landscaping and greenery might be relatively limited, especially considering the surrounding urban environment, which could impact its ability to provide a true natural oasis.
* **Scale and Proportion:** Given the towering height of the John Hancock Center, the plaza's size and layout might feel disproportionate, potentially leading to a lack of coherence in the overall space.
* **Functionality:** The plaza's design might not fully cater to the diverse needs of visitors, potentially limiting its usability for different activities and user groups.
* **Weather Considerations:** The plaza might lack sufficient weather protection, making it less enjoyable during inclement weather or extreme temperatures.
* **Program Variety:** While the plaza hosts events, more diverse programming could be introduced to cater to a broader range of interests and demographics.
* **Community Engagement:** There could be opportunities to engage the local community more actively in the plaza's planning, programming, and stewardship.

# 311 SOUTH WACKER DRIVE, CHICAGO

311 South Wacker Drive Plaza is a privately owned public space (POPS) located in the heart of downtown Chicago, Illinois. It is surrounded by significant skyscrapers, including the 108-floor Willis Tower (formerly Sears Tower), the 60-floor South Tower of the Franklin Center, also known as the AT&T Corporate Center, and the 311 South Wacker Drive building. The neighboring Willis Tower is a well-known tourist destination with over 1.5 million visitors yearly. Consequently, the plaza is ideally situated for foot traffic and serves as an urban oasis in the middle of a busy area. The presence of masses of people in the area improves the chances of attracting visitors and the usability of the plaza. Further, its corner location makes this plaza particularly recognizable (Kamin, 2022; Saliga & Clarke, 1998).

The plaza benefits from convenient access to public transportation, with nearby CTA stations and bus stops providing easy connectivity to other parts of the city. It is also situated next to a tour bus stop for the Willis Tower. The plaza is an accessible and inclusive public space that welcomes all visitors, regardless of their physical abilities. Its flat surface and lack of barriers make navigating and enjoying the space accessible for everyone. Its surface matches the height of the surrounding sidewalks, making walking or rolling on it easy. Therefore, it does not require any special equipment like ramps or elevators for visitors with disabilities, who can enjoy the plaza without any barriers (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013).

The plaza offers a respite from the hustle and bustle of the surroundings. Measuring almost an acre (4,047 square meters), 311 S. Wacker Plaza is the most enormous grassy lawn in the Loop. It contrasts with a "Modernist" plaza that features large, paved surfaces, as with the previously discussed Richard J. Daley Plaza.
