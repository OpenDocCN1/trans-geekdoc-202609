## Lippo Center, Hong Kong [1988]

The Lippo Center, a landmark skyscraper complex in Admiralty, Hong Kong, consists of two office towers completed in 1988. Designed by famed architect Paul Rudolph, the complex features distinctive cladding of dark blue glass and aluminum, strikingly contrasting the surrounding buildings. The Lippo Center is a recognizable structure in Hong Kong’s skyline and has been featured in various films and TV shows.

The podium design of the Lippo Center is a testament to careful architectural consideration, ensuring a harmonious integration with the surrounding environment and a human-scale experience. The multi-story podium is meticulously crafted with a keen eye on proportions and dimensions, seeking to establish a balance that resonates with individuals interacting with the space. This deliberate approach contributes to a podium that serves functional purposes and enhances the architectural ensemble’s overall aesthetic and experiential qualities.

A distinctive feature of the Lippo Center’s podium is the intentional exposure of the towers’ round mega-columns. This design choice adds a visual dynamic to the structure and highlights a generously proportioned staircase leading to the lobby. The staircase becomes a focal point, inviting individuals to ascend toward the building’s entrance, creating a sense of movement and engagement (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Alba, 2003; Rohan, 2014).

The interplay of vertical and horizontal elements further defines the podium’s character. The strong verticality of the mega columns is juxtaposed with the horizontality of the base, creating a visually dynamic composition. Some columns terminate at different elevations within the building, while others extend to the ground level, adding complexity and interest to the architectural narrative. This interplay of elements contributes to a podium that is not only functional but also visually striking.

The color scheme enhances the podium’s recognition and identity. In contrast with the dark blue reflective glass, the whitish columns create a distinctive visual profile for the base. This color interplay adds a layer of sophistication to the podium, making it stand out within the urban landscape (Alba, 2003; Rohan, 2014).

In summary, the podium design of the Lippo Center is a thoughtful composition of elements, including exposed mega columns, a prominent staircase, and a dynamic interplay of geometric shapes and patterns. These design choices collectively contribute to a podium that is not only visually appealing but also attuned to the human scale, offering a harmonious and engaging experience for those interacting with this iconic Hong Kong structure (Figure 2.11).

## Heller International Building, Chicago [1992]

The Heller International Building (500 West Monroe) is a 45-story skyscraper in the West Loop area of Chicago, IL. The 600-foot (183-meter)-tall building was designed by Skidmore, Owings & Merrill and completed in 1992. The Postmodern-style building has an area of 965,000 square feet and houses offices, retail, and other commercial spaces. The building has a distinctive turret-like structure on its southeast corner, reaching the highest point, and is illuminated at night.

The arcade of the Heller International Building stands out as a distinctive and noteworthy feature, adding character and functionality to the skyscraper's design. Comprising a series of double openings spanning the building's base, the arcade serves practical and aesthetic purposes.

Functionally, the arcade creates a covered pedestrian walkway, offering shelter and a visually engaging pathway. This utilitarian feature contrasts visually with the skyscraper's rectangular tower above. The arcade's design introduces an element of dynamism and movement, breaking away from the rigid geometry of the tower and adding a layer of architectural interest at the street level (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Kamin, 2022).

Structurally, the arcade is supported by steel columns clad in granite and limestone, harmonizing with the materials used in the facade of the building. This material consistency contributes to a seamless integration of the arcade with the overall aesthetic of the skyscraper. The choice of materials also adds a touch of elegance, reinforcing the building's visual appeal.

Beyond its role as a covered walkway, the arcade serves as an entrance to the building's lobby. The entrance is marked by a hollowed-out rotunda, creating a sense of grandeur and emphasizing the transition from the exterior to the interior space. The lobby, featuring a vaulted ceiling, adds to the overall luxurious ambiance of the building (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Saliga & Clarke, 1998).

The building’s entrance has undergone recent renovations and remodeling, indicating a commitment to maintaining the structure’s relevance and functionality. While these changes may have somewhat altered the original design, they also reflect an adaptive approach to contemporary needs and design preferences.

In summary, the Heller International Building’s arcade is a distinctive architectural element that combines practicality with aesthetic appeal. ## Concourse Building, Singapore [1994]

The Concourse Building is a mixed-use complex in Singapore designed by Paul Rudolph. Completed in 1994, it comprises a 41-story office tower, a three-level retail podium, and nine stories of serviced apartments. The architectural design of the Concourse Building reflects Rudolph’s vision of a tropical skyscraper that responds to the climate and context of Singapore. The building uses solar shading, wide overhangs, communal gardens, and external balconies to create a comfortable and lush environment for the occupants.

The Concourse Building boasts an innovative design with an octagonal office tower supported by 12 massive pilots. This distinctive architectural feature provides structural support and contributes to the building’s dynamic and geometric expression.

The podium of the Concourse Building is a critical focal point, exhibiting a unique design shaped by a reverse S-curve that elegantly follows the contours of the site boundary. This design choice imparts a sense of fluidity and visual interest to the building’s base. A multilevel shopping center is strategically organized around a three-story sky-lit atrium within the podium. This design enhances the aesthetic appeal of the interior spaces and allows natural light to permeate the shopping area, creating an inviting and vibrant atmosphere (Figure 2.13).

A noteworthy aspect of the Concourse Building's podium is its connectivity to the adjacent Nicoll Highway MRT station. This intentional linkage provides convenient and seamless access for the public, enhancing the building's accessibility and integrating it into the broader urban infrastructure.

Situated above the podium, the apartments of the Concourse Building offer residents breathtaking views of the river and the city skyline. This strategic placement capitalizes on the scenic surroundings and underscores the building's commitment to providing a premium living experience.

In summary, the Concourse Building stands out for its octagonal office tower, supported by striking pilots, and its podium characterized by a reverse S-curve. Integrating a multilevel shopping center with a sky-lit atrium, coupled with connectivity to public transit, makes the Concourse Building a notable example of modern and functional urban architecture in Singapore (Rohan, 2014).
