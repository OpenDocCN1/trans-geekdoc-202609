Bentley Bay South Tower is a 26-story, 79-meter (259-foot)-tall residential condominium building in Miami Beach, Florida. Its sister building, Bentley Bay North Tower, is a 24-story residential condominium. Both towers were designed by Arquitectonica and completed in 2005. The Bentley Bay complex rises in sail-like elegance near the shore of Biscayne Bay.

The garage space at the south end of Bentley Bay South Tower stands out as a distinctive and innovative feature within the complex. Spanning four stories, this parking facility introduces a unique element by incorporating “green” walls that employ the Jakob Rope System. This system integrates wire rope, wire mesh, and steel pipes, allowing plants to climb freely over the structure.

Notably, the steel pipes are arranged in a semi-vertical and random order, creating a visually striking contrast with the overall horizontality of the building. The regular protrusion of slabs on each floor further emphasizes the building’s horizontal lines, making the green walls a dynamic and eye-catching addition to the architectural composition.

Beyond their aesthetic appeal, these green walls serve a dual purpose. Firstly, they contribute to environmental sustainability by promoting air purification. The plants climbing the walls actively engage in the natural air filtration process, enhancing the overall air quality in the vicinity. Secondly, integrating greenery produces an interesting visual effect that significantly deviates from the traditional and often austere appearance of conventional parking garages (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Shulman et al., 2010).

By incorporating nature into the garage space, Bentley Bay South Tower addresses practical concerns related to air quality and transforms the parking facility’s visual experience. The juxtaposition of greenery against the structural elements of the garage creates a harmonious blend of nature and architecture, offering an aesthetically pleasing and environmentally conscious solution to parking infrastructure. In essence, the garage space becomes more than just a utilitarian structure; it becomes a visually engaging and environmentally responsive element that contributes to the overall appeal and sustainability of the Bentley Bay South Tower complex (Figure 2.18).

## **Shimao International Plaza, Shanghai [2006]**

Shimao International Plaza is a skyscraper in the Huangpu District of Shanghai, China. It was designed by Ingenhoven Overdick and Partners, a German architectural firm specializing in ecological and sustainable buildings. Completed in 2006 after five years of construction, the building has a height of 333 meters and consists of 60 floors above ground. The building features a distinctive triangular shape that narrows as it rises, creating a sleek and elegant silhouette. The shape also reduces the wind load and optimizes the use of space. The facade comprises glass and aluminum panels, with horizontal louvers that provide shading and reduce energy consumption.

The podium design of Shimao International Plaza is conceived to craft a vibrant and interactive urban space that mirrors the vitality and diversity of the city. As one looks towards the apex of the podium, a captivating arrangement of glass panels unfolds, adopting a staggered pattern that imparts a pronounced sense of movement and rhythm. This deliberate arrangement contributes to the visual dynamism of the space and allows natural light to permeate the interior.

A prominent feature of the podium is the expansive space-frame canopy that extends from the structure's pinnacle, creating a distinctive roofline that stands in deliberate contrast to the tower's verticality. This canopy comprises triangular modules of varying sizes and angles, resulting in a dynamic and sculptural form. The irregular arrangement of these modules adds an artistic flair to the canopy, creating an intriguing interplay of light and shadow (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Binder, 2015).

Notably, the carved-in design of the podium serves a dual purpose. Beyond its aesthetic significance, this design element protects pedestrians, shielding them from the elements and ensuring a comfortable and sheltered experience within the urban space. The large, round columns supporting the upper floors contribute to the podium's structural integrity and serve as distinctive design elements that enhance the overall visual appeal.

In essence, the podium design of Shimao International Plaza is a thoughtful integration of artistic expression, functionality, and protection. By incorporating dynamic elements such as the staggered glass panels, the striking space-frame canopy, and the protective carved-in design, the podium creates an aesthetically engaging urban environment that is responsive to the practical needs of those who inhabit and traverse the space. The result is a harmonious blend of form and function, contributing to the overall architectural identity of Shimao International Plaza (Figure 2.19).

## **One Lujiazui, Shanghai [2008]**

One Lujiazui (Development Tower) is in the Lujiazui Financial and Trade District (LFTD) in Shanghai, China. The 47-story, 269-meter (883-foot)-tall tower contains mixed-use functions, including commercial office space, residential apartments, and a shopping center. Designed by Tomohiko Yananashi of Nikken Sekkei, a Japanese architectural firm, the building was completed in 2008 and comprises an internal concrete structure and a glass exterior.

The podium design of One Lujiazui is a distinctive feature that sets the building apart. Notably, the base comprises two slanted wings that flank a projected and gently curved glass entrance. This architectural configuration creates a visually striking and dynamic effect. A notable contrast is achieved through the interplay between the transparent entryway and the opaque white wings. The juxtaposition of materials and transparency levels is accentuated by the opposing motions of the descending wings and the ascending entrance (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Al-Kodmany, 2017; Binder, 2015).

Adding to the visual interest, the wings exhibit a zigzag, stepped profile, contributing to the animated and dynamic appearance of the podium. This zigzag motif adds a sense of movement to the structure and introduces a unique architectural rhythm. The wings’ stepped design enhances the podium’s three-dimensional quality, creating a visually engaging and contemporary aesthetic.

The powerful contrast, dynamic angles, and stepped profile of the wings make the base of One Lujiazui an architectural focal point. The interplay between transparency and opacity, coupled with the distinctive shapes and profiles, serves as aesthetic purpose and contributes to the overall identity of the building. The podium design, with its unique features, establishes a sense of architectural dynamism and visual interest, making One Lujiazui a notable and memorable presence in the urban landscape of Shanghai (Figure 2.20).
