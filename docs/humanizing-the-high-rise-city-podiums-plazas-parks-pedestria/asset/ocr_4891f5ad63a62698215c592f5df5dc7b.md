This connection between consumers and local artisans fosters a sense of community and pride as individuals become patrons of the unique talents that define Chicago's artistic landscape.

The Picasso Sculpture in the plaza adds a significant human dimension by highlighting the work of the Spanish artist Pablo Picasso, one of the most influential artists of the 20th century. The project was commissioned in 1963 by the architects of the Daley Center. The sculpture is made of Cor-Ten steel, the same material as the exterior of the Daley Center, stands 50 feet tall, and weighs 162 tons. It was manufactured in Gary, Indiana, and assembled in Chicago in 1967 (Kamin, 2003). The sculpture base often serves as a favored place for people to sit and children to play (Figure 3.2). Also, the plaza's seating options cater to different needs, accommodating individuals of all ages and abilities (Figure 3.6).
