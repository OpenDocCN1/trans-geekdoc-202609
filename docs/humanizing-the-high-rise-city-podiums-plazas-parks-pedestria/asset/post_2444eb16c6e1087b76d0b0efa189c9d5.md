## Chapter 6: City-Wide Parks

City-wide parks serve as urban oases that provide extensive green spaces, recreational facilities, and cultural amenities, contributing to residents’ overall well-being and quality of life in densely populated urban areas. Chapter 6 explores how city-wide parks enhance the human experience in high-rise cities. These giant green spaces span urban areas and profoundly impact urban life’s social, economic, and environmental aspects. City-wide parks foster social interactions, community bonds, and economic activity by providing shared public spaces, job opportunities, and local business growth by attracting visitors. They function as urban lungs, reduce air pollution and heat island effects, and improve air quality while providing habitat for biodiversity and supporting plant and animal species. The chapter showcases successful city-wide park projects worldwide, analyzing design considerations, management strategies, and community engagement approaches that have transformed urban landscapes and enhanced human experiences. In short, Chapter 6 highlights the role of city-wide parks in promoting holistic urban development in high-rise cities, offering insights for planning, designing, and managing these spaces and emphasizing their importance in creating inclusive, prosperous, and ecologically balanced urban environments.

## Chapter 7: Urban-Rooms Parks

The “urban-room” park concept refers to a design approach in urban planning and landscape architecture where outdoor spaces within a larger urban context are designed to create a sense of enclosure, intimacy, and distinctiveness. Like rooms within a building that provide defined spaces for various activities, “urban rooms” are outdoor spaces that offer a more contained and immersive experience within the broader urban environment. Chapter 7 delves into the concept of urban rooms, which create a sense of enclosure and intimacy within large open spaces and parks while promoting various uses and activities. The chapter showcases Millennium Park in Chicago, which used the urban room concept to create different spaces within its more extensive area. The concept improves these parks’ social, cultural, economic, and environmental outcomes by creating smaller, enclosed spaces that host social interactions and cultural events. The urban room concept also enhances park walkability by reducing perceived distances between different attractions, creating a more pedestrian-friendly environment that motivates visitors to walk and experience the park on foot. This supports active lifestyles and increases the overall accessibility and usability of the parks. As such, the urban-room park concept aims to enhance the quality of urban life by providing diverse outdoor spaces that cater to different activities and moods. These spaces offer an alternative to the expansive nature of open parks, creating intimate and memorable settings that contribute to the overall livability of high-rise cities. In summary, Chapter 7 is a valuable guide for urban designers, planners, and park managers who want to create engaging, inclusive urban-room parks.

## Chapter 8: Sculpture Parks

Sculpture parks are outdoor spaces designed to showcase sculptures and other three-dimensional artworks in a natural setting with the urban environment. These parks act as platforms for artists to exhibit their work on a larger scale while providing visitors with a dynamic and engaging experience outdoors. They combine art, nature, and architecture, providing a sensory-rich experience and inspiring creativity, contrasting with the monotonous skyscraper landscape of high-rise cities. Chapter 8 examines how sculpture parks can transform high-rise city life, emphasizing their role as innovative spaces that display artistic creativity and celebrate diverse forms of sculptures. It gives valuable insights and recommendations for urban designers, planners, and park managers, promoting the integration of sculpture as a vital part of public spaces, improving the human experience, and fostering a deeper appreciation for art, nature, and the built environment. In summary, Chapter 8 inspires and guides for creating captivating sculpture parks that enrich high-rise cities’ cultural, social, and visual vibrancy.

## Chapter 9: Waterfront Parks

Waterfront parks are public recreational spaces located alongside bodies of water, such as rivers, lakes, oceans, or other aquatic environments. These parks are designed to allow people to enjoy outdoor activities, relax, and appreciate natural and urban waterfront environments. They offer a range of amenities and attractions that capitalize on their proximity to water, creating a unique and often picturesque setting for recreation and leisure. Chapter 9 highlights the importance of waterfront parks in enhancing the humaneness of high-rise cities by balancing the vertical density of buildings with accessible green spaces, providing numerous benefits to residents and the urban environment. These parks create captivating environments that combine natural elements with social, sporting, and recreational activities, fostering lively spaces that serve residents and tourists. The availability of attractive waterfront parks boosts tourism, stimulates local businesses, creates jobs, and generates economic growth. Green infrastructure, ecological restoration, and flood mitigation strategies improve urban landscape resilience, support biodiversity, and restore natural habitats. The chapter uses the Chicago Riverfront as a powerful case study to demonstrate these concepts, highlighting the successful transformation of the riverfront into an engaging public space that incorporates natural elements, recreational amenities, cultural programming, and public art installations. In essence, Chapter 9 emphasizes the importance of waterfront parks in humanizing high-rise cities, enhancing livability, health, aesthetics, and cultural richness while balancing urban intensity and natural serenity.

## Chapter 10: Elevated Parks

Elevated parks refer to creating green spaces, gardens, recreational areas, or public parks on elevated structures, such as bridges, viaducts, highways, or other elevated infrastructure. This innovative approach allows urban areas to maximize limited space by repurposing elevated structures that might remain underutilized or solely dedicated to transportation. Chapter 10 argues that elevated parks can significantly humanize high-rise cities by providing much-needed green spaces, recreational areas, and communal gathering spots in otherwise densely built urban environments. Elevated parks enhance urban living by incorporating natural elements like plants, trees, and greenery into the landscape. This connection to nature relieves the concrete and steel of high-density areas, creating a refreshing and inviting atmosphere. The chapter uses the High Line in Manhattan, New York City, as a leading example to show the advantages and benefits of elevated parks. It also discusses challenges such as upkeep costs, fair access, visitor management, and possible gentrification impacts. In essence, elevated parks provide an innovative solution to balance the density of high-rise cities with the human need for open, natural spaces. By offering places to relax, connect, and experience nature, these parks enhance residents' overall quality of life and contribute to the humanization of densely built urban environments. In summary, Chapter 10 offers valuable lessons for urban planners, policymakers, and communities who want to use elevated parks as tools for urban renewal.

## Chapter 11: Sky Parks and Gardens

Sky parks and gardens are innovative ways of bringing nature to elevated urban spaces, such as rooftops and upper floors. Chapter 11 argues that sky parks and gardens are crucial in humanizing high-rise cities by introducing elements of nature, greenery, and outdoor spaces into urban environments often characterized by vertical density and limited ground-level open areas. These elevated green spaces create a more livable, balanced, and harmonious citiescape, offering numerous benefits to residents, visitors, and the overall urban ecosystem. Dense cities can feel overwhelming due to the constant hustle and bustle. Sky parks and gardens provide residents with an oasis of tranquility where they can escape the fast-paced urban environment and reconnect with nature. The presence of greenery and plants in sky parks and gardens offers a visual break from the concrete and steel of high-rise buildings, providing a calming and restorative atmosphere. The chapter presents real-world examples of sky parks and gardens from different cities, demonstrating the diverse approaches and designs used in creating these spaces. These examples inspire urban planners, architects, and developers to integrate nature into vertical urban landscapes. In short, Chapter 11 showcases how sky parks and gardens enhance the quality of life in high-rise cities by providing green spaces, promoting well-being, and fostering community interaction, showcasing how innovative design can create functional and emotionally enriching urban environments.
