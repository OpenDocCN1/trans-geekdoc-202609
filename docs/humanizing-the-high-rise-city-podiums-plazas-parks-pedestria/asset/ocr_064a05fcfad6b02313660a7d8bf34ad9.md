Green walls can vary in size, complexity, and design, ranging from small installations to expansive vertical gardens covering entire building exteriors.

Green walls typically consist of the following components (Sheweka & Magdy, 2011; Wong & Baldwin, 2016):

+   * **Support Structure**: The support structure is the foundation for growing plants. It includes a framework, trellis, or modular system that is securely attached to the wall or façade.
* **Growing Medium**: The growing medium is a soil-like substrate that provides a medium for plant roots to anchor and receive nutrients. It is designed to be lightweight and porous, allowing for proper root growth and water retention.
* **Plants**: Various plant species, including ornamental and functional plants, can be chosen for green walls. The selection of plants depends on factors such as light conditions, climate, and the desired aesthetic.
* **Irrigation System**: Green walls require a reliable irrigation system to deliver plant water and nutrients. Irrigation can be achieved through drip systems, hydroponic methods, or automated misting.
* **Drainage**: Proper drainage is crucial to prevent water accumulation and ensure the health of the plants. Drainage systems are designed to remove excess water and prevent root rot.

Green walls offer several benefits, both aesthetic and functional (Cuce, 2017):

+   * **Aesthetic Enhancement**: Green walls add natural beauty and visual appeal to spaces, softening hard architectural lines and creating a soothing and inviting atmosphere.
