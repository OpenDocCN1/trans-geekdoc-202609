### Chicago Riverwalk, Chicago

Nestled along the shores of Lake Michigan, Chicago stands as a dynamic metropolis renowned for its architectural marvels, cultural richness, and vibrant urban life. At the heart of the city's scenic beauty lies the Chicago Riverwalk, an iconic waterfront promenade that meanders through the city center along the Chicago River (See Chapter 9). This pedestrian-friendly pathway is a captivating urban oasis, offering residents and visitors stunning views of the city's renowned architecture, including the iconic Trump Tower and Tribune Tower.

The Riverwalk is lined with cafes, parks, and recreational spaces, creating a lively atmosphere for strolls, outdoor dining, and cultural events. As a symbol of Chicago's commitment to urban revitalization, the Riverwalk has transformed the riverfront into a social and recreational hub, enhancing the city's charm and providing a dynamic space for locals and tourists to engage with the architectural and natural beauty that defines Chicago.

The Chicago Riverwalk is a crucial link in the pedestrian and biking networks, seamlessly connecting Chicago Downtown, the Chicago Loop, and the Lake Michigan pathways to the iconic Navy Pier (Al-Kodmany, 2020).

1. **Downtown and Chicago Loop Connection:** The Riverwalk integrates with the city's grid of sidewalks and pedestrian pathways, providing a smooth transition for those exploring the bustling downtown area and the Loop. Visitors can easily traverse the Riverwalk, enjoying its scenic views and recreational spaces while seamlessly moving between the Riverwalk and the downtown streets.

2. **Lake Michigan Pathways:** For individuals navigating the Lake Michigan pathways, the Riverwalk is a picturesque route extending connectivity from the lakefront to the heart of the city. It offers an inviting path along the Chicago River, enhancing the overall experience for pedestrians and cyclists traveling to or from Lake Michigan.

3. **Navy Pier Connection:** The Riverwalk is a gateway to the Navy Pier, one of Chicago's premier attractions. Pedestrians and cyclists can follow designated routes, enjoying a scenic journey along the riverbanks until they reach Navy Pier. This connection enhances accessibility for those exploring the Riverwalk and the iconic entertainment destination.

The Riverwalk's design and incorporation into Chicago's urban setting promote a pleasant experience for pedestrians and bikers. Thoughtful planning and connectivity ensure that individuals can easily incorporate the Riverwalk into their journeys, whether strolling along the river or accessing the vibrant attractions (Figures 13.4 and 13.5).

### Chicago Pedway, Chicago

The Chicago Pedway, an extensive network of underground and elevated tunnels, bridges, and concourses, offers several humanizing aspects that enhance the experience for pedestrians in downtown Chicago, as follows (Al-Kodmany, 2020; Zhang, 2012):

1. **Shelter and Protection:** The Pedway provides shelter from Chicago’s harsh weather conditions, such as extreme cold, snow, or rain. It allows pedestrians to navigate the city comfortably, avoiding exposure to inclement weather. This humanizing aspect ensures that people can move around the city without the hindrances of adverse weather conditions.

2. **Art Installations and Cultural Exhibits:** Various sections of the Pedway showcase art installations, sculptures, and cultural exhibits. These creative elements add aesthetic appeal and cultural enrichment to the underground passageways, providing pedestrians with visual stimulation and artistic expression within the urban environment.

3. **Wayfinding and Signage:** The Pedway incorporates clear wayfinding signage and maps to guide pedestrians through its extensive network. These navigational aids help pedestrians easily navigate the complex system, ensuring a seamless and user-friendly experience. Well-placed signage and directional markers contribute to a sense of comfort and security for pedestrians exploring the Pedway.

4. **Retail and Dining Options:** The Pedway features a variety of retail stores, boutiques, restaurants, cafes, and food courts. These amenities allow pedestrians to take breaks, grab a meal, or engage in shopping while traversing the Pedway. The availability of such facilities adds convenience and enhances the overall experience for pedestrians.

5. **Public Seating Areas:** Within the Pedway are designated seating areas where pedestrians can rest and take a break from their journeys. These seating spaces provide a comfortable and welcoming environment for individuals to relax, socialize, or observe their surroundings. They contribute to a sense of community and offer respite during long walks.

6. **Connection to Major Landmarks and Transit Hubs:** The Pedway connects to significant landmarks, attractions, and transit hubs in downtown Chicago, such as Millennium Park, City Hall, and Union Station. This connectivity makes it easier for pedestrians to access critical destinations without navigating through heavy street traffic. It enhances the convenience and accessibility of the city's amenities and services.

Through these humanizing aspects, the Chicago Pedway provides pedestrians with a convenient and comfortable experience, combining functionality with art, culture, and connectivity elements. It offers a unique underground and elevated urban landscape that promotes accessibility, protection from weather elements, and a diverse range of amenities for pedestrians to enjoy while exploring the city.

### Sky Ring and Elevated Walkways, Lujiazui, Shanghai

Lujiazui, situated in the Pudong district of Shanghai, is a glaring symbol of the city's rapid transformation into a global financial and trade hub. This iconic financial district, often referred to as the “New Bund,” boasts a stunning skyline adorned with some of the world's tallest and most futuristic skyscrapers. At the forefront of Lujiazui's architectural ensemble is the Shanghai Tower, a remarkable feat of engineering, along with the Jin Mao Tower and the Shanghai World Financial Center (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013).

These structures define the cityscape, creating a modern and dynamic silhouette against the Huangpu River. Lujiazui is a business powerhouse and a cultural and recreational destination, featuring the Lujiazui Central Green Space and the Oriental Pearl Tower (See Chapter 5). Lujiazui continues to captivate visitors with its towering landmarks and vibrant city life as a testament to Shanghai's economic progress and urban sophistication.
