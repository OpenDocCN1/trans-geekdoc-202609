Jacques Brownson, a member of the renowned firm C. F. The plaza was initially known as the Chicago Civic Center but was renamed for Mayor J. Daley in 1976. Renaming the building and the plaza highlights an essential human dimension, for it commemorates and honors the outstanding efforts of Richard J. Daley, who served as the mayor of Chicago from 1955 to 1976. Indeed, Mayor Richard J. Daley is widely credited with transforming Chicago into a world-class city during his 21-year tenure as mayor (Keegan, 2008).

The plaza’s location is so central to the city that it attracts over 10,000 people daily. Bringing together large crowds and attracting diverse people encourages visitors to interact and engage in conversations, enabling the plaza to serve as a prime social and cultural hub. The Daley Center Plaza is accessible and inclusive. Its flat surface and design comply with accessibility standards, ensuring people with disabilities can navigate space comfortably. A stairway and elevators connect the plaza with the Washington-Clark CTA Station and Chicago’s underground pedway system, a network of pedestrian tunnels and concourses providing indoor connections between buildings and transportation centers (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Kamin, 2022; Figure 3.1).
