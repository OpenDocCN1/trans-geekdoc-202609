## REAL FAKE SCULPTURE, CHICAGO

In 2017, the City of Chicago installed a six-foot-tall sculpture called “Real Fake” near the Trump International Hotel and Tower, spelling out just two words in thick, metallic paint finish, golden letters: “REAL FAKE” (Figure 14.8). The 350-pound fiberglass sculpture, part of a public art installation along the Chicago Riverwalk, is a sharp critique of President Trump’s repeated denial of media criticism as “fake news,” even in the face of his fraudulent activities. The sculpture, created by artist Scott Reeder, challenges viewers to question the authenticity of information and constructed narratives that shape our understanding of the world. The sculpture, first installed in 2013 at Art Basel Miami Beach, was on loan to Chicago. It serves as a commentary on contemporary society’s complex interplay between reality, perception, and truth. The sculpture’s prominent placement in front of the Trump Tower, a symbol of power and influence, invited reflection on the role of media, politics, and public perception in shaping our collective reality. The decision to install the sculpture in such a prominent location reflects the city’s commitment to fostering dialogue and critical thinking among residents and visitors. Public art can challenge and inspire individuals to question prevailing narratives and engage in meaningful conversations about the nature of truth and representation (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Voon, 2017).

## THE LOVE SCULPTURE, NEW YORK CITY

The Love Sculpture in New York City is a three-dimensional representation of the word “LOVE” in bold capital letters, arranged in a stacked and tilted manner (Figure 14.9). Created by American artist Robert Indiana (1928–2018) in 1970, the sculpture has captured locals’ and visitors’ hearts and imaginations. Its simple yet powerful message resonated with people from diverse backgrounds, cultures, and languages, transcending barriers and inspiring a sense of connection and harmony. Located in the heart of Manhattan, the sculpture has become an iconic landmark, attracting countless tourists who flock to see and interact with this enduring symbol of love. ## MOTHER AND A BABY STATUE IN EAST NANJING ROAD, SHANGHAI

One of the iconic landmarks in Shanghai is the Mother with a Baby and Pram statue, located on East Nanjing Road, a bustling, pedestrian commercial street (Figure 14.10). Nanjing Road is divided into East Nanjing Road and West Nanjing Road. East Nanjing Road is a pedestrian street with many shops, restaurants, and entertainment venues (See Chapter 4). West Nanjing Road is a more upscale area that showcases modern architecture and luxury brands. Along the street, visitors can also find historical landmarks such as the Jing’an Temple (See Chapter 3). East Nanjing Road is one of Shanghai’s most well-liked and energetic shopping avenues. It travels around 1.5 kilometers, from the Bund to People’s Square. Along the route, guests may discover a variety of shops, eateries, cafés, and entertainment options to suit their interests and financial constraints. ## MOTHER WITH SON STATUE IN EAST NANJING ROAD

The bronze statue of mother and son on East Nanjing Road in Shanghai is a work of art by Chinese sculptor Chen Yifei. It was unveiled in 2002 to commemorate the 100th anniversary of Nanjing Road, one of Shanghai’s most famous and historic streets since the late 19th century. The statue depicts a mother holding her son’s hand as they walk along the bustling street, symbolizing the love and warmth of family life (Figure 14.11). The statue is located near Henderson Metropolitan Shopping Center and Mall on the eastern end of East Nanjing Road. It is one of several public artworks that have adorned Nanjing Road. The bronze statue of mother and son on the Nanjing Road is a popular attraction for tourists and locals alike, who often take photos with it or admire its artistic details. It reflects the history and culture of Shanghai, as well as the universal theme of family bonds (Wue, 2014).

## BUND BULL, SHANGHAI

The Shanghai Bull, also known as the Bund Financial Bull or the Bund Bull, refers to a modified version of Arturo Di Modica's Charging Bull sculpture in New York City. This derivative artwork was installed in late April 2010 and officially revealed on The Bund in Shanghai on May 15, 2010. The work of art, weighing 13,227 pounds (6,000 kilograms), is purported to possess dimensions identical to the New York City Charging Bull in terms of height, length, and weight. However, it measures 17.1 feet (5.2 meters) in length and 10.5 feet (3.2 meters) in height. Similarly, the bronze bull features a tail that exudes a more ominous presence (Wue, 2014). The Bund Bull has garnered significant popularity, fostering enthusiastic engagement of residents and visitors (Figure 14.12).
