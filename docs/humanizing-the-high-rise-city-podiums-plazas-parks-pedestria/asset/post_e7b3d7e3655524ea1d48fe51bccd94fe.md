## 4 Streets as Plazas

How would you like to live in a place where the streets are not for cars but for people? That’s the idea behind streets as pedestrian plazas, an innovative way of designing urban spaces that makes them more enjoyable, lively, and diverse. Streets as plazas, also known as pedestrianized streets or shared spaces, refer to transforming streets into pedestrian-friendly public spaces prioritizing walking, cycling, and social activities over vehicular traffic. This approach aims to create vibrant and accessible urban environments that promote human interaction, economic vitality, and community engagement (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Banerjee, 2001; Whyte, 1980).

The “Streets as Plazas” concept represents a transformative urban philosophy that reimagines traditional thoroughfares as dynamic public spaces, transcending their conventional roles as mere conduits for vehicular traffic. In this innovative approach to urban design, streets are envisioned as multifunctional plazas, vibrant and inclusive communal areas that prioritize pedestrians, community engagement, and cultural activities. This paradigm shift challenges the conventional understanding of streets as utilitarian passages, emphasizing their potential to serve as focal points for social interaction, recreation, and artistic expression (Oranratmañee & Sachakul, 2014).

At the core of the “Streets as Plazas” concept is a commitment to enhancing the quality of urban life by creating spaces that encourage a sense of community and connectivity. By prioritizing pedestrian-friendly features, such as widened pathways, green spaces, and inviting street furniture, these transformed streets aim to foster a more walkable and accessible urban environment. The benefits extend beyond aesthetics, influencing residents’ physical and mental well-being as they embrace a more active and social lifestyle within their immediate surroundings (Bertolini, 2020; Simpson, 2000).

Moreover, the concept recognizes streets’ intrinsic cultural and historical significance within the urban landscape. By reimagining them as plazas, cities can tap into the potential of these spaces to become dynamic stages for cultural events, public art installations, and community celebrations. Integrating cultural elements adds vibrancy to the urban fabric and contributes to a more profound sense of place identity, fostering pride and connection among residents (Elabb & Hallowell, 2014; Gehl, 1971, 2010; Nordahl, 2014).

In the subsequent exploration of “Streets as Plazas,” we delve into two case studies, Times Square and East Nanjing Road, that exemplify the successful implementation of this concept, shedding light on the tangible benefits it brings to communities, the economy, and the overall urban experience. Through this lens, we uncover how urban designers, architects, and communities collaboratively shape a more inclusive and engaging urban design.

### TIMES SQUARE, NEW YORK CITY

Nestled at the crossroads of Broadway and Seventh Avenue in the heart of New York City, Times Square is a pedestrian haven with wide sidewalks and plazas, making it a popular gathering place for both locals and tourists. Its expansive layout allows it to gracefully accommodate massive crowds, creating a dynamic and inclusive space that pulses with the energy of New York City. Times Square offers a variety of attractions, including shops, theaters, restaurants, and entertainment venues, promoting relaxation and enjoyment in public seating areas and benches. There is a vast array of shopping, including Broadway shows, restaurants of the highest caliber, and street performances by gifted artists. More than a bustling nexus of commerce, Times Square is a cultural melting pot, a stage for Broadway’s finest performances, and a symbol of New York’s unyielding spirit. With its dazzling lights, diverse attractions, and the perpetual hum of life, Times Square transcends its geographical coordinates; it becomes a living, breathing entity, a microcosm of the city’s ceaseless energy and the epitome of the New York experience (Gehl et al., 2008; Sagaly n, 2003a, 2023b; Times Square).

Times Square, designed as a pedestrian zone with its central area featuring ample open space, is a versatile canvas for many activities. The thoughtful design places a premium on seating options, strategically inviting people to gather, unwind, and immerse themselves in the lively atmosphere. A diverse array of seating choices unfolds throughout the square, from traditional benches, ledges, and steps to purposefully positioned and movable public furniture. These seating solutions extend beyond mere functionality; they play a crucial role in fostering social interactions, creating a dynamic and inviting environment. In the heart of New York City, Times Square’s seating amenities cater to the varied needs of the crowd, enhancing the overall experience of this iconic urban space where diverse communities converge (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Goldwyn, 2014; Figure 4.1).

The “Red Steps” in Times Square is a popular public space that serves as a gathering place for locals and tourists, offering a vantage point to view the surrounding billboards and the vibrant atmosphere. The stairs, rising to a height of 26 feet and consisting of 27 steps, are visually arresting and serve as a center point for visitors to enjoy the bustling energy of the space. The staircase’s design integrates accessibility elements, making it wheelchair accessible. The stairs also provide an excellent viewpoint of Times Square’s busy activity and are a great place to people-watch. Tourists enjoy sitting on the steps in the colorful mosaic of people, billboards, and street entertainers that make Times Square distinctive (Times Square).
