# BOSCO VERTICALE, MILAN

Bosco Verticale (Vertical Forest) is a pioneering example and the epitome of the greenery-covered high-rise building model. Its boldness, significant height, and extensive greeneries have promoted this project among the most remarkable. Nestled in Milan, Italy, Bosco Verticale consists of two residential towers renowned for seamlessly incorporating an abundance of plants and trees into their design. The visionary architecture is credited to the esteemed firm Stefano Boeri Architetti. The design was inspired by the traditional ivy-covered buildings in Italy but multiplied to create a living facade that absorbs CO $_{2}$, reduces energy consumption, and protects against noise and radiation. Bosco Verticale is a landmark of green design that demonstrates the possibility of integrating nature and urbanism harmoniously (Belcher et al., 2018; Flannery et al., 2015; Boeri & Insulza, 2010; Ishween, 2021; Varro, 2017).

# Architectural Design

Bosco Verticale comprises two towers measuring 80 meters and 112 meters in height, respectively. The towers have a unique shape with a graceful profile. They feature a contemporary design aesthetic, incorporating clean lines and a combination of glass, spacious balconies, and concrete elements. The defining feature of Bosco Verticale is the numerous balconies protruding from the facade of each tower. Crafted to cultivate a “vertical forest” ambiance, the balconies of Bosco Verticale exhibit diverse sizes and configurations, enriching the overall architectural composition with visual intrigue and dimension.
