
## City-Wide Parks

City-wide public parks are ample green spaces that cover urban areas and provide a variety of amenities for the entire city and its population. Within their sprawling spaces, a diverse spectrum of amenities and facilities is meticulously curated, catering to the multifaceted dimensions of recreation, education, and conservation. Unlike the localized nature of neighborhood parks, which intimately serve specific communities, city-wide parks are masterfully designed to bestow their manifold recreational, cultural, and environmental benefits upon a broader and more diverse population. Oversight of these monumental landscapes typically falls under the purview of local government entities or non-profit organizations, transforming them into comprehensive hubs of activity. These carefully crafted and diligently maintained sanctuaries are an enduring testament to a city's unwavering commitment to elevating the collective well-being and enhancing the quality of life for all its inhabitants (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Forsyth, 2021; Parakh et al., 2017; Talen, 2010).

City-wide and local public parks offer ample benefits (Refer to Chapter 5) but differ in scale and impact. Here are some differences (Tate, 2015; Scott & Jackson, 1996):

- City-wide parks are larger, serving a broader population and offering extensive recreational opportunities like sports fields, playgrounds, and cultural amenities. They foster social interactions and community by providing a common ground for residents to engage in activities and events. On the other hand, neighborhood parks are smaller and cater to local communities, offering accessible green spaces for relaxation, social interaction, and localized recreational activities.
- City-wide parks offer various leisure activities, such as hiking trails, sports facilities, picnic areas, and water bodies for boating or fishing. Neighborhood parks, on the other hand, provide localized opportunities for physical exercise, such as jogging paths, basketball courts, or playgrounds. Both types contribute to individuals' well-being and health by encouraging active lifestyles.
- City-wide parks become tourist attractions, drawing visitors and contributing to the city's economy. They attract visitors across the city and beyond, fostering interactions among people from different neighborhoods, backgrounds, and cultures. They serve as gathering places for community events, festivals, and celebrations, promoting social connections and cultural exchange. On a smaller scale, regular parks play a similar role in fostering community bonds within immediate neighborhoods.
- The larger footprint of city-wide parks allows them to provide more excellent environmental advantages by significantly mitigating the urban heat island effect, improving air quality, and providing habitat for biodiversity. These parks act as green lungs within the city, absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen, contributing to a healthier urban environment. City-wide parks contribute to a city's resilience in the face of climate change. Green spaces help absorb rainwater, reduce flooding, and contribute to temperature regulation, making the city more adaptable to environmental challenges. Regular parks also offer similar environmental benefits on a smaller scale, providing pockets of greener, shade, and habitat for local flora and fauna.
- Because of their massive scale, city-wide parks provide a horizontal counterpoint to the verticality of high-rise structures, contributing to a more balanced and visually appealing urban landscape. This dynamic interplay contributes to achieving a harmonious and visually captivating urban landscape. The vast green spaces spread across the city provide a necessary contrast to the dominance of tall buildings, offering residents and visitors a respite from the urban verticality. This deliberate juxtaposition enhances the overall aesthetics of the cityscape, fostering a sense of balance and diversity in the architectural and natural elements.

City-wide public parks play a crucial role in humanizing high-rise cities. They provide essential green spaces for residents, foster community engagement, contribute to the city's ecological sustainability, and enhance the overall quality of life in urban environments dominated by tall structures. Integrating extensive public parks is a crucial element in creating a well-rounded, livable, and resilient high-rise cityscape (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Parakh et al., 2017).

The following case studies illustrate the roles of city-wide parks in humanizing the high-rise city.

### Central Park, New York City

Central Park, located in Manhattan, New York City, is the first public landscaped park in the United States. In 1853, state authorities purchased land from 59th to 106th Streets, between Fifth and Eighth Avenues, formerly used for modest agricultural plots and communities due to its rugged and marshy soil. The park was designed by landscape architects Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux and constructed from 1858 to 1873, covering 6% of Manhattan's total land area, equivalent to 843 acres (341 hectares or 1.3 square miles) (Rosenzweig & Blackmar, 1992). It stretches for 2.5 miles and has a width of half a mile, attracting over 15 million visitors yearly, more than any park in the nation.

The Central Park Conservancy, a private, non-profit organization, oversees the management and maintenance of the park through a contractual agreement with the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation (Slavecek, 2009; Sutton & Anderson, 2016).

Central Park, a serene green oasis in Manhattan, offers residents and visitors a tranquil space to connect with nature, relax, and recharge, offering a much-needed escape from the densely built environment. The park is home to various attractions, including landscapes such as the Ramble and Lake, Hallett Nature Sanctuary, the Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Reservoir, and Sheep Meadow; amusement attractions, including the Central Park Zoo, Central Park Carousel, and Wollman Rink; and formal spaces such as the Central Park Mall and Bethesda Terrace. Central Park offers recreational activities such as walking paths, bike lanes, sports fields, playgrounds, baseball and soccer fields, carousel, skating rinks, formal gardens, commemorative monuments, and concert and theater venues, contributing to the well-being and quality of life of its residents by promoting a healthier lifestyle and mitigating the negative impacts of sedentary urban living (Slavecek, 2009; Sutton & Anderson, 2016).

Here are some of the park’s key elements:

1. **Great Lawn:** The Great Lawn, a 55-acre green pasture situated at the heart of Central Park, is renowned as one of the world’s most famous lawns and an excellent spot for leisure and picnicking.
2. **Cherry Hill:** The Hill derives its name from the exquisite cherry blossom trees that flourish there in spring, but it is primarily renowned for its impressive 14-foot fountain.
3. **Conservatory Garden:** Situated at the 5th Avenue and 105th Street intersection, Conservatory Garden is Central Park’s sole formal garden.
4. **Conservatory Water:** Inspired by the model boat ponds of late 19th-century Paris, it offers children and adults the opportunity to enjoy the delight of sailing model boats.
5. **Cherry Blossom Trees:** From April to May, the Kwanzan and Yoshino Cherry trees simultaneously blossom in Central Park.
6. **Bethesda Terrace Arcade:** It is a vaulted indoor pathway located in the park’s heart, connecting the Mall to Bethesda Fountain and Central Park Lake.
7. **Bethesda Terrace:** Regarded as an impressive architectural masterpiece, the Bethesda Terrace is situated at the 72nd Street and Cross Drive intersection, offering stunning panoramic views of the Central Park Lake and trees.
8. **Bethesda Fountain:** Standing as one of New York’s largest fountains, Bethesda Fountain offers an ideal setting for people-watching, socializing with friends, or simply unwinding amidst the breathtaking vistas.
9. **Belvedere Castle:** Derived from the Italian term “beautiful view,” Belvedere Castle offers park visitors sweeping vistas that encompass several of Central Park’s most exquisite landmarks.
