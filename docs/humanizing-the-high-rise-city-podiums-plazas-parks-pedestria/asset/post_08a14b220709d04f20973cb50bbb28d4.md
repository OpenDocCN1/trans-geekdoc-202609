## The Jetty

The Jetty, an expansive stretch from Wells Street to Franklin Street, is a testament to the harmonious integration of ecology, recreation, and education along the Chicago River. The Jetty is a multifaceted space designed to cater to various interests and experiences, comprising seven piers that boldly extend over the river's expanse.

This unique area serves as a haven for fishing enthusiasts, providing designated spots where individuals can engage in this leisurely pursuit. However, a pertinent reminder accompanies this recreational aspect, emphasizing the need for an appropriate fishing license to partake in the activity. As people cast their lines into the river, the Jetty transforms into a tranquil space where individuals can enjoy the solitude of fishing and witness the underwater world teeming with aquatic life (AI-Kodmany, 2020; Kamin, 2022b, 2023).

The ecological consciousness embedded in the Jetty's design extends beyond surface-level engagement. Underwater fish habitats have been strategically installed, contributing to the river's biodiversity. Floating gardens, anchored by resilient stainless-steel pylons, enhance the ecological fabric by providing a healthy habitat for fish populations. Remarkably, these floating gardens exhibit adaptability, capable of rising to 8 feet in response to flooding, showcasing a thoughtful approach to environmental resilience.

The plant selection for the floating wetlands and water gardens prioritizes water-tolerant species, further emphasizing the commitment to sustainable landscaping practices. The Jetty creates recreational opportunities, enhances ecological well-being, and serves as an educational platform. Visitors are invited to explore and interact with the aquatic ecosystem, fostering a deeper understanding of the delicate balance between urban development and environmental conservation (AI-Kodmany, 2020; Kamin, 2022a).

### Waterfront Parks

Fish ecologists used creative intervention techniques to support subsurface aquatic habitats in this area. For example, they integrated seven limnetic “habitat curtains,” each consisting of steel frames and wire mesh, from which nylon ropes dangle. These are made to make algae grow on them, providing food for fish. These curtains also offer an artificial filamentous substrate for inhabiting sessile organisms such as barnacles. Ecologists wrapped around and attached “pole hulas” to the underwater structural poles closer to the walkway. Pole hulas promote algae growth and the breeding of amphibious insects, which are primary food sources for fish.

Further, ecologists installed caisson-mounted “lunkers,” porous steel cylinders that offer a place for fish to shelter from powerful river currents and aquatic predators, larger fish, and mammals. Fish lunkers were installed on seven of the caissons away from the walkway. A 10-inch (25.4-centimeter) gap between the lunker and the caisson provides a shelter for fish. Interestingly, these ecological techniques implemented in the Jetty were partially inspired by the “fish hotel” project that attracted and provided habitat for river species before the Riverwalk was built (Hill, 2019, p. 236).

In essence, the Jetty emerges as a dynamic riverfront space where the serenity of fishing, the richness of ecological habitats, and the opportunity for education converge, creating a holistic and engaging environment that resonates with diverse interests and promotes a sustainable coexistence between the urban and natural realms (Figure 9.8).

### The Riverbank

The Riverbank, encompassing the final stretch of the Chicago Riverwalk from Franklin Street to Lake Street, provides a serene and spacious retreat along the river’s edge. This space serves as a multifunctional area, fostering a connection between the urban landscape and the natural beauty of the waterway.

At the core of the Riverbank’s design is a sprawling lawn, inviting people to sit, unwind, and immerse themselves in the dynamic activities of the river. The carefully manicured green space enhances the ambiance, offering a tranquil setting amid the urban hustle.

Strategically placed Adirondack chairs further enrich the visitor experience, providing comfortable spots for relaxation, conversation, and the enjoyment of the abundant sunshine. This thoughtful addition creates an inviting environment, encouraging individuals to linger, socialize, and savor the scenic surroundings.

A vital feature of the Riverbank is the pedestrian-friendly and wheelchair-accessible ramp that seamlessly links Upper Wacker Drive with the Riverwalk. This inclusive design ensures the space is easily accessible to all, fostering community and openness.

As part of the continuous urban expansion, the city is actively investigating options for reconstructing this area. This progressive approach emphasizes the dedication to improving Riverbank’s functionality and visual attractiveness according to the changing demands and preferences of the community.

In conclusion, the Riverbank is a pivotal component of the Chicago Riverwalk, offering a harmonious blend of greenery, accessibility, and social space. Whether visitors seek a moment of solitude or a shared experience with others, the Riverbank provides a versatile and inviting setting along the scenic backdrop of the Chicago River (Figure 9.9).

## Under-Bridge Crossings

The Chicago Riverwalk project demonstrates foresight and innovation in ensuring a seamless and continuous experience for pedestrians. Under-bridge crossings, strategically positioned beneath existing bridges, allow uninterrupted pedestrian flow from Lake Street to Lakeshore. These under-bridges not only provide practical utility but also showcase thoughtful design elements. Canopies are incorporated into these crossings, serving a dual purpose. Firstly, they protect pedestrians from debris falling from the bridge deck above, enhancing safety and comfort.

Secondly, the canopies contribute to the aesthetic appeal of the Riverwalk by creating an architectural element that complements the surrounding environment. The choice of stainless-steel material for these under-bridges is not merely functional but adds an artistic dimension to the project. The reflective properties of stainless steel beautifully mirror the texture and color of the river's water, creating a dynamic interplay between the structure and its aquatic surroundings. Moreover, as pedestrians move through these spaces, their motions become part of this reflective spectacle, further enhancing the visual experience (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Kamin, 2022a, 2023).

Beyond their functional and aesthetic contributions, the under-bridges serve a crucial urban design purpose. These crossings delineate distinct sections by creating natural breaks along the Riverwalk's various areas, allowing visitors to transition seamlessly between different environments. This deliberate urban planning adds to the overall coherence and navigability of the Riverwalk, ensuring that it remains functional and visually captivating. In essence, the under-bridge crossings exemplify the project's commitment to creating a holistic and user-friendly experience, where practical considerations align seamlessly with architectural aesthetics, contributing to the success and continuity of the Chicago Riverwalk (Figure 9.10).
