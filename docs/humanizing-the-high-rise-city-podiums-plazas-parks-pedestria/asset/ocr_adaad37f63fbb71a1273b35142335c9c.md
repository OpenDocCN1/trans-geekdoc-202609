Employing traditional bronze but enveloping it in white paint, he achieves an unconventional sense of lightness. Unlike traditional monuments, Claassen's rabbit sculptures are grounded, offering a departure from typical public sculpture perspectives. Through the use of rabbits, Claassen introduces a "down-to-earth" quality to his art, adding a touch of whimsy and relatability to his distinctive approach.

+   8. **La Riviere by Aristide Maillol:** Maillol's later works primarily center around the depiction of the female nude. In contrast to the usual themes of calmness, tranquility, and emotional restraint found in sculptures like La Riviere, Maillol explores a departure from these principles. Through a deliberate placement of the figure near the ground and extending the head and arms beyond the pedestal, he introduces a sense of instability and motion. The figure, with flexed legs suggestive of movement, captures the essence of water, deviating from Maillol's more serene portrayals to evoke a dynamic and fluid energy.

+   9. **Scarecrow by Donald Baechler:** The genesis of Scarecrow was inspired by the artist's agricultural estate in upstate New York. Baechler envisioned placing a sculpture suspended above the rolling hills and barns, drawing parallels to the Christ the Redeemer sculpture atop Mount Corcovado in Rio de Janeiro. Depicting the scarecrow as a solitary figure in a vast field, devoid of life and engagement with its surroundings, Baechler captures an essence akin to an inanimate observer. The inaugural sculpture of the Scarecrows series is on display at Citygarden. Additionally, the artist crafted a duplicate, now positioned on his East Coast farm.

+   10. **Bird by Laura Ford:** Ford crafts whimsical and disconcerting hybrid organisms by blending human and animal features, a process influenced by her keen observation of her children and reflections on her own childhood experiences. Growing up exposed to sideshows and fairgrounds, these early encounters left a profound and lasting imprint on her worldview. This exposure has equipped Ford with a unique ability to seamlessly navigate between the tangible and intangible, the eerie and delightful, and the humorous and malicious realms in her art.

Citygarden Sculpture Park is a vibrant testament to the rich tapestry of artistic expression, evident in its diverse collection of sculptures. Each artwork encapsulates a unique amalgamation of styles, themes, and artistic approaches, fostering a captivating visual dialogue within the park's landscape. The sculptures reflect a broad spectrum of creative visions, from Igor Mitoraj's archaically inspired Eros Bendato to the whimsically enlarged Big Suit by Jim Dine. The park's commitment to diversity lies in the individuality of each sculpture and the harmonious interplay of these artistic elements. Visitors encounter a dynamic fusion of contemporary aesthetics, cultural influences, and varied conceptual foundations, creating an immersive and thought-provoking experience. Citygarden's embrace of diverse artistic voices enhances its role as a dynamic cultural space where the evolving language of sculpture continually unfolds, engaging the public in a captivating exploration of artistic pluralism (Pactor, 2018).
