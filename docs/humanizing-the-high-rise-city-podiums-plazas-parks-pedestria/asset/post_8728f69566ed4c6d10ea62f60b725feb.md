
# Areas for Improvement

- **Limited Shade:** The plaza lacks substantial shading elements, making it uncomfortable during hot weather and limiting its usability.
- **Weather Dependency:** The lack of covered areas or weather protection can restrict the plaza's use during rain or extreme temperatures.
- **Intense Urban Activity:** The high foot traffic and frequent events can create a crowded and noisy atmosphere, detracting from its tranquility.
- **Maintenance Challenges:** Maintaining the open space and public amenities might be challenging due to heavy use and various activities.
- **Retail and Amenities:** Limited options for food, beverages, and amenities might reduce the convenience and comfort of plaza visitors.

# Chase Tower Plaza, Chicago

Chase Tower Plaza (the Exelon Plaza) is a public space in the heart of Chicago, surrounded by some of the city's most iconic landmarks. It covers an area of approximately 1.5 acres and features a large fountain, seating areas, and a variety of trees and plants. The plaza is a popular spot for office workers to take a break or have lunch, and it also hosts events throughout the year, such as concerts and farmers' markets. It is a vibrant and inviting public space that adds to the character of downtown Chicago. The plaza is home to Chase Tower, a 60-story skyscraper completed in 1969 and the tallest building inside the Chicago "L" (short for elevated) Loop. Chase Tower has a distinctive curved shape and houses the headquarters of Chase Bank and Exelon, among other tenants (Al-Kodmany and Ali, 2013; Al-Kodmany, 2017; Kamin, 2022; Saliga & Clarke, 1998).

The plaza features a jet fountain, a clock tower, and a colorful ceramic mural by Marc Chagall called *Four Seasons*. One of the most eye-catching features of the plaza is the fountain, which occupies a central position. The fountain comprises a level basin with nine square sections, each with a water jet that shoots to different heights. The basin is raised above the ground and can be seen from the street even when the water is not running. When the water is turned on, it creates a dynamic and captivating spectacle that attracts the attention of the pedestrians who walk by. The fountain has a wide rim surrounding the basin, holding a thin water layer flowing gently over the edge. This water layer is separated from the deeper basin by a metal lip that prevents the water from spilling over. This design discourages visitors from sitting on the rim or entering the fountain but allows them to interact with water by touching or splashing it lightly. The fountain is a source of delight and relaxation for the visitors and a symbol of the plaza's elegance and vitality. Being inside the Chase Plaza can be a truly special experience, given how the space is separated from the city and how the surrounding skyscrapers fade into the background (Figure 3.11). The wall of the sunken level moves to the forefront, creating a human-scale environment (Pridmore & Larson, 2005).

The plaza offers various seating options, including steps, ledges, and stone benches (Figures 3.12 and 3.13). Movable chairs can be arranged at different spots and angles, creating a dynamic and inviting space for people to relax and socialize. During lunch hours, Chase Tower temporarily sets out tables and chairs along the edges of the lower levels to accommodate employees and visitors. The plaza offers many eatery options, from fast food to fine dining and catering to different tastes and budgets. It is most heavily used during lunch hours by those who work in Chase Tower and the neighboring buildings. They enjoy the convenience and variety of the food choices, as well as the pleasant atmosphere of the plaza. The vegetation in the plaza is chiefly covered with hedges, but these plants are bright and colorful, adding vibrancy to the space. The plants are carefully maintained and arranged in geometric patterns, contrasting with the seating shapes and patterns (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Kamin, 2022).

Along the east side, a large mosaic called *Four Seasons* by Marc Chagall is on the upper level. It is a mosaic mural that depicts scenes from different seasons and cultures, reflecting Chagall's vision of humanity and nature. The 21-meter (70 foot)-long, 4-meter (14-foot)-high, and 3 meter (10 foot)-wide mural contains 4,000 square feet (372 square meters) of mosaic tiles that were imported from Italy, France, Norway, Belgium, and Israel. The scenes include a blue winter landscape with snowflakes and ice skaters, a spring scene with flowers and lovers, a summer scene with sunflowers and musicians, and an autumn scene with harvest and animals. The mural was commissioned by the First National Bank of Chicago in 1974 and was unveiled in 1978. It is one of Chagall's most prominent public works and a popular attraction for visitors and locals alike. Initially, the mural was displayed in an open-air setting and, therefore, subjected to Chicago's harsh winter, requiring the mural to be renovated in 1988 with the replacement of 25% of its tiles. In 1996, Skidmore, Owings, and Merrill (SOM) designed a steel and glass shelter to protect the *Four Seasons* from further weather damage (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Kamin, 2022; Figure 3.14).

Overall, Chase Tower Plaza offers a welcoming and accessible outdoor environment in the heart of downtown Chicago. Its design, public art, and vibrant atmosphere make it a notable public space for residents, workers, and visitors to enjoy and engage with the surrounding urban fabric.

# Highlights

- **Positive Aspects**
  - **Central Location:** The plaza's prime location in the heart of downtown Chicago makes it easily accessible and a focal point for both residents and visitors.
  - **Spaciousness:** The plaza provides ample open space, allowing for a sense of openness and room for various activities.
  - **Seating Areas:** The presence of benches, seating walls, and movable chairs provides comfortable spots for relaxation, socialization, and people-watching.
  - **Landscaping:** Well-maintained plantings, trees, and greenery create a refreshing environment and aesthetic appeal.
  - **Water Feature:** The plaza's water feature adds a dynamic element, creating a soothing ambience and serving as a focal point.
  - **Outdoor Dining:** The availability of outdoor seating and dining spaces enhances the urban experience, allowing people to enjoy meals in an alfresco setting.
  - **Public Art:** *Four Seasons* by Marc Chagall is a mosaic mural that depicts scenes from different seasons and cultures, reflecting Chagall's vision of universal harmony and peace.
  - **Pedestrian Connectivity:** The plaza facilitates pedestrian movement and links the Chase Tower and the surrounding urban fabric.
  - **Urban Respite:** Amid the city's hustle and bustle, Chase Tower Plaza offers a retreat where individuals can find respite and relaxation.

- **Areas for Improvement**
  - **Weather Dependence:** Lack of weather protection can restrict the plaza's usability during adverse weather conditions.
