# ■ Public Art

+   2. Stakeholder Engagement:
- Engage with local communities, residents, businesses, and relevant stakeholders in the planning and design process.
- Seek their input, involve them in decision-making, and consider their perspectives and aspirations.
- This engagement fosters a sense of ownership, builds connections, and ensures that public art reflects the needs and interests of the community.

+   3. Artistic Diversity and Innovation:
- Embrace various artistic styles, mediums, and approaches to encourage diversity and innovation in public art.
- Explore various forms of expression, such as sculptures, murals, installations, digital art, or performance art, to enrich the urban fabric and create engaging experiences for residents and visitors.

+   4. Scale and Proportion:
- Consider the scale and proportion of public art to its surroundings, including the high-rise buildings.
- Ensure that the artwork is visible and legible from different viewpoints and distances, accounting for the verticality and density of the urban environment.
- Balance the size of the artwork with the available space and the human scale of the pedestrian experience.

+   5. Safety and Structural Considerations:
- Collaborate with structural engineers and art experts to ensure public art installations’ safety and structural integrity.
- Consider the impact of wind, vibrations, and environmental factors on the durability and stability of the artwork.
- Comply with building codes, regulations, and safety standards to protect the public and the artwork.

+   6. Interactivity and Engagement:
- Incorporate interactivity or audience engagement elements to create a dynamic and participatory experience.
- Consider incorporating technologies, sound, light, or interactive features that allow viewers to interact with the artwork, fostering a deeper connection between the public and art and making it a more memorable and meaningful experience.
