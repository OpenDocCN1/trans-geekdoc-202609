## 5 Public Parks

High-rise living, while symbolizing modernity and urban sophistication, introduces a dichotomy of spatial constraints that residents grapple with daily. The inherent allure of towering skyscrapers often means sacrificing the luxury of spacious living areas, both indoors and outdoors. Within these vertical communities, inhabitants navigate their daily lives in compact spaces, giving rise to a palpable sense of claustrophobia. The absence of immediate access to the outdoors and the soothing touch of nature amplifies this sensation, fostering a deep-seated yearning for a more expansive and rejuvenating living environment. The desire for green expanses is not merely an aesthetic preference; it represents a fundamental human need for connection with the natural world, a desire that transcends the concrete and glass confines of high-rise abodes. In this pursuit of an enriched living experience, public parks emerge as vital havens, offering residents a communal escape where the boundaries of vertical living dissolve, replaced by the revitalizing embrace of nature (Kent, 2011; Southworth, 2011).

Public parks are essential retreats in the densely populated metropolitan environment, intentionally placed to alleviate the constraints of limited living areas. These verdant oases, interspersed amid the concrete jungle, play a pivotal role in providing residents with a much-needed respite from the constraints of their habitats. Within the leafy spaces of these green havens, individuals are granted an escape from the confined quarters of their apartments, allowing them to immerse themselves in nature's refreshing and soothing embrace. The meticulously designed landscapes, adorned with lush foliage, winding pathways, and open expanses, offer a tangible escape where residents can unwind and revel in moments of quiet reflection (Al-Kodmany, 2018; Tate, 2015).

Beyond remediating the scarcity of private green spaces in their residential units, public parks are pivotal in cultivating a sense of community within the urban fabric. These communal green areas transcend their functional role and transform into integral gathering points for residents searching for solace, social interaction, and recreational activities. Parks act as democratic spaces, fostering a sense of inclusivity where diverse communities converge, creating shared experiences and building social connections (Jim & Chen, 2010).

Public parks are vital for holding various cultural activities, such as festivals and performances, which strengthen social bonds, promote shared identity, and improve the quality of life in urban areas surrounded by towering structures. They transform into vibrant cultural hubs, acting as focal points for numerous major events and large gatherings that bring communities together and become dynamic spaces for cultural expression, fostering a rich social tapestry within the context of high-rise living. This dual function, both as a social nexus and a cultural hub, underscores the multifaceted significance of expansive green spaces within the fabric of high-rise cities (Hussain et al., 2010; Parakh et al., 2017).

Green spaces are pivotal in enhancing the urban fabric by introducing biodiversity and fostering miniature ecosystems, contributing to environmental sustainability within urban landscapes. Beyond their aesthetic appeal, these verdant expanses serve as vital reservoirs of biodiversity, providing habitats for various plant and animal species. The interconnected web of life within these green areas contributes to ecological balance, preserving local flora and fauna. Additionally, introducing greenery in urban environments mitigates climate change, reduces urban heat island effects, and improves air quality, acting as a natural filter for pollutants and enhancing overall environmental health. Integrating biodiversity into the urban fabric enriches the visual appeal of high-rise living and plays a crucial role in creating sustainable and harmonious cityscapes (Giffordy, 2006; Peters, 2010).

The following case studies highlight the roles of public parks in humanizing the high-rise city.

### BRYANT PARK, NEW YORK CITY

Bryant Park, a renowned public park in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, was established in 1686 and named after William Cullen Bryant, an acclaimed American poet and editor. It spans approximately 6.5 acres and offers diverse amenities and activities. It is situated between 40th and 42nd Streets and Fifth and Sixth Avenues near famous landmarks such as Times Square and the New York Public Library (Chapter 4). Its central location, adjacent to subway stations, makes it easily accessible and a convenient destination for residents and visitors (Tate, 2015; Bryant Park).

Numerous welcoming features at Bryant Park make it an excellent place for people to relax and have fun. Due to its location in Midtown Manhattan and its abundance of verdant vegetation, the park is called a “green oasis.” In the middle of the city’s hectic streets and towering skyscrapers, it offers a welcome escape to a more tranquil setting. Attracting both locals and tourists, it is a lively community gathering spot. There is plenty of room for people to gather, unwind, and mingle on the park’s main grass, in the seating areas, and at the outdoor tables. The open and friendly environment fosters a sense of community and belonging (Manshel, 2020).
