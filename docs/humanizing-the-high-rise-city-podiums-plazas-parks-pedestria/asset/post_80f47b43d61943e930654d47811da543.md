### TOURISTS SCULPTURE, LUJIAZUI PARK, SHANGHAI

The Tourists Sculpture is a bronze artwork that depicts a group of people taking pictures and enjoying the view of the skyscrapers in Lujiazui Park, Lujiazui, the financial district of Shanghai (Figure 14.14). The sculpture was created by Chen Yifei, a renowned Chinese painter and sculptor, and was installed in Lujiazui Park in 2001. The sculpture is meant to reflect the contrast between the traditional and the modern, the local and the global, and the dynamic and the static in Shanghai. It depicts a mesmerizing scene of a group of folks energetically taking shots of the tall structures, effectively showcasing the city's vibrant combination of classic and contemporary aspects. The sculpture is intricately designed, capturing a specific moment and offering a thought-provoking reflection on the contrast between Shanghai's cultural history and its rapid modernization (Zheng, 2019).

### KEY TAKEAWAYS: PLANNING AND DESIGN PRINCIPLES

When integrating public art in a dense, high-rise city, several planning and design principles should be considered to ensure its successful integration and impact. Here are fundamental principles to keep in mind:

- 1. **Contextual Integration:**
  - Consider the city’s unique characteristics, history, and culture and the specific neighborhood or site where the public art will be located.
  - Ensure the artwork reflects and responds to the surrounding environment, architecture, and community context.
- 2. **Stakeholder Engagement:**
  - Engage with local communities, residents, businesses, and relevant stakeholders in the planning and design process.
  - Seek their input, involve them in decision-making, and consider their perspectives and aspirations.
  - This engagement fosters a sense of ownership, builds connections, and ensures that public art reflects the needs and interests of the community.
- 3. **Artistic Diversity and Innovation:**
  - Embrace various artistic styles, mediums, and approaches to encourage diversity and innovation in public art.
  - Explore various forms of expression, such as sculptures, murals, installations, digital art, or performance art, to enrich the urban fabric and create engaging experiences for residents and visitors.
- 4. **Scale and Proportion:**
  - Consider the scale and proportion of public art to its surroundings, including the high-rise buildings.
  - Ensure that the artwork is visible and legible from different viewpoints and distances, accounting for the verticality and density of the urban environment.
  - Balance the size of the artwork with the available space and the human scale of the pedestrian experience.
- 5. **Safety and Structural Considerations:**
  - Collaborate with structural engineers and art experts to ensure public art installations’ safety and structural integrity.
  - Consider the impact of wind, vibrations, and environmental factors on the durability and stability of the artwork.
  - Comply with building codes, regulations, and safety standards to protect the public and the artwork.
- 6. **Interactivity and Engagement:**
  - Incorporate interactivity or audience engagement elements to create a dynamic and participatory experience.
  - Consider incorporating technologies, sound, light, or interactive features that allow viewers to interact with the artwork, fostering a deeper connection between the public and art and making it a more memorable and meaningful experience.
- 7. **Cultural and Social Relevance:**
  - Ensure public art reflects and celebrates the city's and its communities' diversity, history, and cultural identity.
  - Consider themes and narratives that resonate with the local population and address social issues or promote inclusivity. Public art can catalyze dialogue, reflection, and community engagement.
- 8. **Maintenance and Sustainability:**
  - Develop a long-term maintenance plan for the public art installations to ensure their preservation and longevity.
  - Consider the materials used, their durability, and the required maintenance activities.
  - If applicable, incorporate sustainable practices, such as using eco-friendly materials, energy-efficient lighting, or rainwater harvesting for irrigation.
- 9. **Signage and Interpretation:**
  - Install clear signage and interpretive elements that offer comprehensive information about the artwork, the artist, and the underlying concept, enhancing public understanding, appreciation, and engagement with the art.
- 10. **Collaboration and Partnerships:**
  - Foster collaboration between artists, urban planners, architects, local government, developers, and community organizations.
  - Embrace public-private partnerships to fund, implement, and maintain public art projects.
  - This collaboration ensures diverse expertise, resources, and a shared commitment to integrating public art in the city.

By adhering to these planning and design principles, integrating public art in a dense, high-rise city can enhance the urban environment, enrich the cultural fabric, and foster a sense of identity, belonging, and civic pride among residents and visitors.
