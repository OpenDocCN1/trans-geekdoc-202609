# 111 South Wacker, Chicago [2005]

111 South Wacker Drive is a 51-story, 208-meter (681-foot)-tall skyscraper in Chicago’s Loop, completed in 2005. The building’s architecture is striking and contemporary, featuring a sleek and elegant façade combining glass, steel, and other high-quality materials. The design incorporates clean lines, smooth surfaces, and a modern aesthetic, reflecting the sophistication and innovation of its architectural approach. The well-proportioned form and thoughtful composition contribute to its visual appeal and harmonious integration with the surrounding urban context.

The podium design of 111 South Wacker is a masterful composition that prioritizes openness, visibility, and a welcoming atmosphere. Anchored by a 12-story base, the building features a four-story lobby and an eight-story parking garage. The lobby, a central element of the base, stands out for its remarkable transparency, achieved through the use of a cable-supported enclosure with low-iron glass and a non-reflecting coating. This design choice allows for a seamless visual connection between the interior and exterior, creating an inviting and open ambience (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Goettsch, 2012).

Notably, the transition from the tower’s perimeter columns to only four lobby-level columns, spanning an impressive 25 meters (82 feet) between them, enhances the sense of openness in the lobby. Behind the lobby, the building’s core is clad in white marble, further emphasizing the commitment to visibility and openness.

A distinctive feature of the podium is the circular ramp leading to the parking garage, which generates an intricate tiling pattern.
