The Sky Garden can be characterized as a multilevel indoor structure resembling a "hill." Visitors can access the garden by the stairs located on the eastward and westward sides of the structure. The stairs are easily ascended due to their steps having a low vertical distance between each. Additionally, an elevator is available for anyone unable to navigate the stairs. The south-facing hillside features many well-designed terraces equipped with benches, allowing visitors to rest and observe the plants up close.

The garden showcases plants from the Mediterranean and South Africa that are highly resistant to drought. Notable plant species include *Strelitzia reginae* (Bird of Paradise), *Agapanthus* (African Lily), *Kniphofia* (Red Hot Poker), and *Agave attenuata* (Lion's Tail). Gillespies, a landscape architecture firm based in London, designed the Sky Garden (Viñoly et al., 2015).

The following highlights the key aspects of the Sky Garden:

- **Serene Environment:** The Sky Garden features beautifully landscaped gardens with various plants and foliage. The interior space is beautifully landscaped with various lush plants, trees, and flowers, creating a serene environment reminiscent of a tropical garden. It is designed to provide visitors with a tranquil retreat where they can relax, socialize, and enjoy the beauty of nature (Reike, 2015; Viñoly et al., 2015). Within the Sky Garden, several designated areas serve different purposes. The main central area provides ample seating, allowing visitors to enjoy the panoramic views while sitting and admiring the surroundings. Some walkways and pathways guide visitors through the garden, providing opportunities to explore different sections and enjoy different cityscape perspectives. Overall, the garden is a leafy indoor space, a perfect place to get botanical inspiration while remaining indoors. It is helpful in a place like London, known for its rainy weather.

- **Floor-to-Ceiling Windows and Roof:** The indoor area of the Sky Garden features a stunning glass dome-like structure. The roof is designed with a unique shape, resembling an expansive greenhouse that allows natural light to flood the space. The Sky Garden also features floor-to-ceiling glass windows, providing unobstructed views of London. Visitors can enjoy stunning vistas of iconic landmarks such as the Shard, The Gherkin, St Paul's Cathedral, Tower Bridge, the River Thames, and more.

- **Observation Decks:** In addition to the indoor spaces, the Sky Garden offers an open-air terrace that allows visitors to step outside and experience the fresh air while enjoying the spectacular views. Visitors can marvel at the city's architectural wonders and capture stunning photographs of the London skyline. As visitors traverse the deck, they can immerse themselves in the dynamic energy of London, spotting famous landmarks and offering an unforgettable experience that uniquely invites visitors to connect with the city.

- **Restaurants and Bars:** The Sky Garden is not only a place of natural beauty but also offers various dining options. It is home to a range of restaurants and bars, allowing visitors to indulge in culinary delights while surrounded by the enchanting ambiance of the garden. To ensure a pleasant and comfortable experience for visitors, Sky Garden provides amenities such as restrooms, cloakroom facilities, and dedicated staff members who can assist and answer any queries.

- **Events Space**: The Sky Garden can also be used as an events space. It has hosted various events, from corporate functions to private celebrations, taking advantage of its one-of-a-kind setting.

- **Public Access**: Unlike traditional observation decks that typically charge an entrance fee, the Sky Garden is unique in that it offers free public access. Visitors can enjoy food, drink, and party options, wander, and stroll around the exquisitely landscaped gardens and observation decks (Figure 11.5).

One of the main criticisms is the limited accessibility to the Sky Garden. Entry is free, but visitors must book tickets in advance, and availability can be limited. Some argue that this creates exclusivity and prevents spontaneous visits by locals and tourists. Further, the Sky Garden can often get crowded, especially during peak hours and tourist seasons (Reike, 2015).

The limited space and high visitor volume can diminish the experience and make it difficult to appreciate the views and tranquility of the garden entirely. Also, due to the all-glass envelope, thermal insulation is poor. The garden could be too hot in summer or too cold in winter. The lack of adequate climate control systems has been a common complaint among visitors.

Overall, the Sky Garden at 20 Fenchurch Street offers a unique and captivating experience for locals and tourists alike. It combines stunning architecture, breathtaking views, and a lush botanical setting to create an urban oasis that showcases the harmonious integration of nature within a tall building.

## Shanghai Tower, Shanghai

The Shanghai Tower, a 632-meter (2,073-foot) skyscraper in Shanghai, China, is an iconic landmark with a distinctive design and impressive height and is the third-tallest building in the world. Designed by Gensler, the tower was constructed between 2008 and 2015. It serves multiple functions, such as office spaces, retail areas, hotels, and observation decks. Its unique twisting spiral form provides structural stability and reduces wind loads. The tower has 128 floors above-ground and 5 below-ground floors.

The tower's 118th-story observation deck offers a 360-degree panoramic viewing hall. A double-skin facade, rainwater collecting systems, wind turbines, and modern air filtration systems are some of the sustainable elements of the skyscraper. The Shanghai Tower symbolizes Shanghai's modernization and economic development, making it a popular tourist attraction and a significant landmark in the city's skyline (Binder, 2015).

The sky gardens in the Shanghai Tower are a series of lush green spaces located at various heights within the building. These gardens serve as communal areas for relaxation, recreation, and socialization, offering stunning panoramic cityscape views. They are strategically positioned near common areas, such as restaurants, cafés, and event spaces (Al-Kodmany, 2018a).

The design of the sky gardens draws inspiration from traditional Chinese gardens, incorporating elements such as water features, rocks, trees, plants, and shrubs. These green elements not only enhance the aesthetic appeal of the spaces but also improve air quality and reduce the building's carbon footprint. The aim is to create a harmonious and serene environment that blends with the modern architecture of the tower.

The sky gardens are designed to maximize natural lighting. Including large glass panels ensures ample daylight penetrates the spaces, reducing the need for artificial lighting during the daytime. This design approach promotes energy efficiency and creates a pleasant indoor environment (Figure 11.6).

The Shanghai Tower's sky gardens have been criticized for their restricted public access, scarce vegetation, and inadequate recreational facilities. The tower is a notable feature of the cityscape, but the sky gardens are mainly intended for building tenants and guests, limiting public appreciation. The scarce vegetation may reduce the quality and expected advantages of a garden-like setting. Although the sky gardens offer magnificent city vistas, they lack practical use beyond giving visual enjoyment. This limitation hinders their effectiveness for activities such as work, leisure, or social interactions (Binder, 2015).

## GREEN WALLS

Green walls, also known as living walls or vertical gardens, are architectural features that consist of vegetation growing vertically on a structure, such as a wall or a façade. These walls are intentionally designed to incorporate plants and greenery, creating a natural and vibrant display within indoor or outdoor spaces.
