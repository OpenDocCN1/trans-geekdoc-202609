# INTERCONTINENTAL HANGZHOU (THE SUN)

One of the most remarkable structures in Hangzhou CBD is the gleaming golden sphere InterContinental Hotel. The Hangzhou International Conference Center incorporates the principles of iconicity, functionality, and innovation. It follows a spherical design concept inspired by the sun, with a dominant color scheme centered around golden hues. The curtain wall of the building combines modern composite panels with golden-tinted glass. The entire edifice exudes grandeur, resembling a colossal golden sun that brilliantly reflects and enhances the significance of Hangzhou, cementing the city's status as a romantic capital. The hotel is a component of a cohesive complex that includes the Hangzhou International Conference Center, Shopping Center, Grand Theater, and parks. The Hangzhou International Conference Centre is a prestigious 5-star Intercontinental Hotel and Conference Centre, recognized for its exceptional quality and service. The conference center's capacity to hold international conferences and offer guests luxurious housing is made possible by its 739 rooms. The Conference Centre comprises a 1,000-seat auditorium with six distinct language interpretation booths. The structure, designed by Canadian architect Carlos Ott, was completed in 2009 (Al-Kodmany & Ali, 2013; Binder, 2015).

# HANGZHOU GRAND THEATER (THE MOON)

Hangzhou Grand Theater, built in the newly established Hangzhou Central Business District, is located across from InterContinental Hangzhou and adjacent to the river. The municipality has devised a comprehensive strategy to eventually incorporate a convention center, a new municipal building, and a sizable shopping mall. The city plans to establish a new commercial hub, separate from the downtown district along West Lake, connected by a newly constructed boulevard, offering land for new construction projects.

The Hangzhou Grand Theatre, designed to resemble a sculpture, is the main cultural establishment in the capital city of Zhejiang Province. The moon-shaped building, characterized by intricate double-curved glass facades and a titanium roof, includes the surrounding green space inside its design, resembling a pearl within its oyster shell. The facility includes an Opera Hall with a seating capacity of 1,600 in an inverted conical shape, a Concert Hall with a seating capacity of 600, a Theatre with a seating capacity of 400, and a rooftop Chinese restaurant open 24 hours a day. The restaurant offers views of the outdoor amphitheater and the river. The venue features adaptable flooring, walls, ceilings, and acoustic materials, enabling performances of both Eastern and Western repertoires and special lighting effects to synchronize activities. The project was completed in 2004 (Al-Kodmany, 2017; Liu et al., 2011).
