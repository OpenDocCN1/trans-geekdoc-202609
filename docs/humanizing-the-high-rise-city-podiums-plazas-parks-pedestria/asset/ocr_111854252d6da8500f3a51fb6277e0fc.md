# Marina Bay Sands, Singapore

Marina Bay Sands is a world-renowned integrated resort in Singapore, designed by architect Moshe Safdie and owned by the Las Vegas Sands Corporation. With over 2,500 rooms and suites, the hotel rooms offer stunning views of the bay. Guests can access various facilities, including a fitness center, spa, swimming pool, and various entertainment options, including the Sands Theatre, Grand Theatre, ArtScience Museum, and The Shoppes at Marina Bay Sands. The casino, one of the largest and most luxurious in the world, offers a wide array of table games, slot machines, and electronic gaming machines. The resort offers extensive dining options (Safdie, 2011; Rowe et al., 2019).

The SkyPark is one of the most remarkable features of Marina Bay Sands. It is a 340-meter-long and 40-meter-wide structure that connects the resort’s three towers at a height of 200 meters. It resembles a giant surfboard resting on the towers, creating a striking contrast with the Singapore skyline. The SkyPark is more than just a visual spectacle. It is also a green sanctuary with landscaped gardens, jogging trails, and an infinity pool that offers breathtaking views of the city. The SkyPark also boasts fine dining and drinking options that cater to different tastes and preferences. It is a versatile venue that can host various events, such as concerts, exhibitions, and private parties. At night, visitors can enjoy the dazzling light and sound shows at Gardens by the Bay, which creates a mesmerizing ambiance (Bourg-Meyer et al., 2018). The resort’s unique architecture, as well as curved and cantilevered SkyPark, create a dramatic visual impact against the Singapore skyline (Figure 11.1).

Here are details of the key features of the SkyPark:

Observation Deck: The observation deck at the SkyPark of Marina Bay Sands is a prominent feature that provides an unparalleled vantage point for guests and visitors to enjoy panoramic views of Singapore’s skyline. Visitors can gaze in every direction to enjoy the diverse and dynamic cityscape, including modern architectural marvels and iconic natural features. The Singapore River is a central element of the city, and from the SkyPark’s observation deck, one can have an unobstructed view of this historic waterway. The river has played a significant role in Singapore’s development and is bordered by a mix of historical and modern structures. Similarly, The Gardens by the Bay, a futuristic and award-winning garden featuring the iconic Supertree Grove, is a prominent element on the skyline (See Chapter 6).
