# Suggestions for deploying ML in molecular electrochemistry

Intrigued by our own discussions shown above; here, we present some take-home messages and suggestions in the context of addressing the long-standing issue of mechanistic ambiguity and advancing the field of molecular electrochemistry for a wider utilization of mechanistic investigation.

(a) Accommodate a probabilistic approach in mechanistic studies. As discussed above, the existence of mechanism ambiguity in electrochemistry necessitates a probability-driven analysis that currently is underdeveloped. Our recent work [18] demonstrates the possibility of simultaneously considering multiple possible mechanisms and accounting for the mechanism ambiguity as a distribution of probabilities. Indeed, one of the main reasons that we advocate for a broader utility of ML in molecular electrochemistry is the wide adoption of probabilistic methods in deep learning models [25,26] that will transform how we analyze electrochemistry.

(b) Adopt ML-friendly electrochemical data structures. The existing data structures from electrochemical characterization methods are mostly suited for the generation of two-dimensional plots that humans are capable of analyzing. As ML models are well capable of receiving high-dimensional data inputs, a wide adoption of ML-aided analysis should encourage the use of information-rich high-dimensional data structures, such as tensor inputs in lieu of pixelated images in recent examples [11,17,18] that encompass not only a single electrochemical characterization but also multiple cauterization methods for simultaneous analysis. Yet, one caveat here is that the data structure should cater to the specifics in the desired application and avoid excessive data interpolation in order to mitigate the undesired misinterpretation and loss of analysis accuracy.

(c) Be aware of the constraints from ML models trained by simulated data. While existing ML models are

trained and developed with simulated electrochemical data, it is important to acknowledge that those ML models are limited to training set; hence, the boundaries and ranges of parameters deployed during the establishment of simulated training data. Any discussion regarding the applicability and validity of ML-based analysis should consider such a potential limitation. Moreover, it is desirable and critical to train/refine the developed ML models with experimental ones in order to accommodate practical factors in electrochemical testing, such as uncompensated resistance and capacitance and mitigate potential missassignments.

(d) Promote interpretability in ML-aided analysis. One common criticism to ML-based research is the “black box” nature and the lack of interpretability for ML algorithms. To counter such a caveat and promote trustworthiness, the development of ML-aided analysis in electrochemistry should consider yielding outputs, such as the “attention” plot in our recent work [18], for greater model interpretability. A more interpretable ML-aided analysis will not only help detect and mitigate bias but also justify the validity of the developed ML models, as well as suggest hidden data features that may have escaped human inspection, thus enriching our fundamental understanding about electrochemical characterizations.

(e) Broader multidisciplinary collaborations. As we develop probability-driven electrochemical analysis aided by ML, collaborations among electrochemists, statisticians, and computer scientists will be inevitable and essential. We envision greater advances in molecular electrochemistry originated from the synergies among the beauty of accumulated insights into electrochemistry, the rigor of statistical interpretation, and the fascinating design in ML architectures.

(f) An open community with great accessibility. Given the wide scope of molecular electrochemistry, the envisioned advance can only take place in an open research community. The development, testing, and validation of ML models rely on the rich understanding of electrochemical mechanisms and researchers’ provision of simulated and experimental data. Meanwhile, open-access ML models tailored to electrochemical research will broaden the adoption of ML-aided analysis, facilitate other research topics in electrochemistry, and help educate the next-generation researchers well versed in programming, ML, and statistical analysis. It is the collective efforts of the whole community that will dictate the future of ML adoption in electrochemistry.
