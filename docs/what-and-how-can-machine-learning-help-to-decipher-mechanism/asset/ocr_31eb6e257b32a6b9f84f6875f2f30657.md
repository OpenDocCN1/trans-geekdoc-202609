Schematic representation of machine-learning-aided mechanistic analysis. (a) two potential applications of machine learning (ML) in mechanistic analysis based on cyclic voltammograms. Left: ML models provide an estimated likelihood of different mechanisms and guide the iterative traditional process. Another potential topic for improved mechanistic study is the combined, simultaneous analysis of multiple electrochemical characterization techniques (Figure 2b). One hypothesis that the authors hold is that electrochemical perturbation for mechanistic information should benefit from different techniques with their complementary temporal resolutions. Thus, one

corollary is that transient and high-frequency measurements, such as pulse voltammetry [23] and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy [24], should accompany cyclic voltammetry for mechanistic studies, thanks to the former techniques’ reach in shorter time scales (10^6 Hz or 1 µs). Current analysis of pulse voltammetry and impedance data in conjunction with cyclic voltammetry commands separate two-dimensional plots, additional expert training, and an iterative procedure similar to the one in cyclic voltammetry that analysis is sequentially conducted for individual electrochemical techniques [19].
