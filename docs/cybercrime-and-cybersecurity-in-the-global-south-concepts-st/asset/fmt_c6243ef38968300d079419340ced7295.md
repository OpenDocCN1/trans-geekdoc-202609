
## 12.6 Ontological Representation, Classification, Comparison and Evaluation

### 12.6.1 Our Cybercrime Classification Ontological Representation in Protégé OWL

In this section, we translate our conceptual model ([Figure 12.1](#ch12.xhtml#fig12_1)) to our CCO, implemented in Protégé OWL ([http://protege.stanford.edu/](http://protege.stanford.edu)). The conceptual model is a straightforward translation into OWL classes, as shown in [Figure 12.2](#ch12.xhtml#fig12_2). Object properties used to link instances of classes together are also shown. Our CCO artefact is the explicit formal specification of the characteristics with which to classify cybercrimes and the relationships among them.

### 12.6.2 Classifying and Storing Two Real World Cyber Attack Events

In the following sub-sections, we demonstrate the utility of our artefact by using our Protégé OWL CCO to store and classify two real-world cybercrimes: `EmailHacked_NudePhotosUploaded` and `LulzSecAssoicateIndicted`. Also in this section, we compare how the previously identified classification schemes and this study's classification scheme would classify the cybercrimes.
