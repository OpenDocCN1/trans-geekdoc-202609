The symbols of the metalanguage are referred to as *terminals*. These may include characters, keywords, operators, etc. The words of the guest language are called *non-terminals* and are abstractions of terminal symbols. Terminals are considered atomic, as they are not reducible to other instructions. As such, terminals may be referred to as *atoms*, in accordance with concepts discussed in the *Atoms & Abstractions* chapter.

For a message to have meaning, as between a sender and a receiver, or between a coder and a machine, there must be shared rules of grammar.

Symbolically, a rule for a context-free grammar might be:

```
X → x
```

where *X* is a non-terminal symbol and *x* is a terminal symbol. This means that when *X* is encountered it gets replaced with *x*, regardless of the context surrounding *X*.

A rule for a context-sensitive grammar might be:

```
XY → xY
```
