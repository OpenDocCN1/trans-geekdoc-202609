A statement which is not declarative is not a proposition.
Consider:

*Hello World!*
*What time is it?*

These are not propositions because they cannot be evaluated as
true or false.

A single proposition outside the context of prior propositions
can only be accepted as true, as there is no prior contradicting
proposition. That a proposition contradicts your *apriori*
*knowledge* does not make it false unless its logic includes a
prior contradictory assertion.

For example, there may be the statement:

*The sky is dark.*

We may disagree with that statement. But, our disagreement
would depend on reasoning that is outside of this isolated logic,
our *apriori knowledge*.

predicates in the statement in question with symbols, such as s
for sky and d for dark, as follows:

*s is d*
