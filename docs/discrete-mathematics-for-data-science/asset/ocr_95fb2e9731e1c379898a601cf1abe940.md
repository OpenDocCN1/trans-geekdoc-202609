H can be computed before and after sending a message, by the sender and receiver, in order to determine if noise was introduced into the message.

# 22.5 JOINT ENTROPY

Joint entropy quantifies the uncertainty of two random variables taken together. Given that H(A) and H(B) represent the entropy for different sets of outcomes, their joint entropy is the union of these sets. If the sets are independent, then the joint entropy is the sum of their individual entropies.

Joint entropy can be expressed as:

```
H(A, B) = -\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^m P_i(A_i, B_j) \times log_2 P(A_i, B_j)
= H(A) + H(B | A)
```

where H(A_i, B_j) \in H(A) \cup H(B)

# 22.6 CONDITIONAL ENTROPY

There are cases in which one variable depends on another. For example, coincident signals may actually entail some causal relationship. The discrete random variable A may be interpreted as a case while the discrete random variable B is considered an effect.

Consider that the entropy of B pertains to outcomes a_i, where H(B — A) is the weighted average of H(B — A = a_i) over all values a_i that B may take.

Conditional entropy for B given a single event a_i can be expressed as:

```
H(B | a_i) = -\sum_{j=1}^m P(b_j | a_i) \times log_2 P(b_j, a_i)
```

Conditional entropy for B given events of A can be expressed as:

```
H(B | A) = -\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^m P(a_i, b_j) \times log_2 P(b_j | a_i)
= H(A, B) - H(B)
```

where H(A_i, B_j) \in H(A) \cap H(B).
