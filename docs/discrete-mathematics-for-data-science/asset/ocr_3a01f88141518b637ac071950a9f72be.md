Recall in the chapter on *Computability* the discussion of *finite automata*. Such systems that are deterministic produce a predetermined output state that is always the same for given inputs. Conversely, non-deterministic systems depend on possible or probable inputs, which are not known to the system in advance. In the context of Information Theory, deterministic finite automata may be considered *noiseless channels*. In that sense, probabilistic finite automata would be considered *noisy channels*.

For information to be conveyed from sender to receiver, both must utilize the same set of symbols and rules. Otherwise, communication is not possible.

The probability of receiving a message depends on the quality of the data in its initial state from a sender, as well as its state after its transfer, storage, retrieval, and any data conditioning on the side of the receiver. The conditioning aspect of a data transmission entails eliminating noise. Information Theory accounts for this probability in a measure called *Shannon entropy*, or just *entropy*, which is computed based on the symbols comprising *messages* and *noise*.2
