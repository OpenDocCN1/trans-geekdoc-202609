## 3.5 NUMBER BASE NOTATION

To prevent ambiguity, the base of a number may be denoted beside the number. For example, consider that the number 16 in decimal is 10000 in binary and 10 in hexadecimal. Therefore, unless we know the number's base, we are faced with some ambiguity – if you saw “10” without knowing the base, you would not know what number is actually intended.

Depending your programming environment, the hexadecimal number 10 might be written as 10₁₆, 10r, or 10h. It may also appear with any of the following prefixes: x10, 0x10, \x10, $10, #10, or & 10.

A similar pattern of prefixes can represent other number bases. For example:

- 0b is for binary.
- 0o is for octal.
- 0d is for decimal.
- 0x is for hexadecimal.

For example, binary 1010 will be prefixed with 0b, a zero and the letter b, which results in 0b1010.

## 3.6 COMPUTER TERMINOLOGY VS MATH TERMINOLOGY

Computer hardware ingests natural numbers as non-negative integers, including 0. In formal mathematics, natural numbers are defined as positive integers, without 0.

Practitioners of computer science generally accept the mantissa of a number to be the significant digits (significand) of a floating-point number. For example, the number 24.68 is represented by the mantissa (or significand) 2468 multiplied by a power of 10 as follows:

24.68 = 2468 × 10⁻²

Conversely, Mathematics has two definitions for the mantissa. One is the fractional part of a number, that part of the number to the right of the decimal place, such as the digits 68 in the number 24.68. Another is the first part of a number in scientific notation, such as the 2.45 in 2.45 × 10³.

### Abstract

Being able to convert numbers from a one format to another often allows the data scientist to expedite analysis. This skill also lends insight into the tasks of distributing and storing data, as well as debugging programs.

Numbers can be represented in various ways using different bases, differing degrees of precision, or different symbols. Furthermore, numeric values can be normalized and standardized relative to a range of values. These are important considerations during the preprocessing stage of data analysis, after raw data is retrieved from a source and before statistical analysis takes place.

Changing a number's base representation should not be confused with changing its type. Base conversion does not change the value of the number, only the symbols that accord with another base.
