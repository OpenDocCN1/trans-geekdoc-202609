For \( k \in \mathbb{W} \), assume that the statement “ \( P(k) : 3^k - 1 \) is even” is true. This means that in our work below, we can replace \( 3^k - 1 \) with \( 2m \), for some \( m \in \mathbb{W} \). (\( 3^k - 1 \) contains a factor of 2.)

Consider \( P(k + 1) : 3^{k+1} - 1 \). We must show that \( 3^{k+1} - 1 \) is also even.

```
3^{k+1} − 1 = 3 · 3^k − 1
               = 3(3^k − 1 + 1) − 1
               = 3(2m + 1) − 1, for some m ∈ W
               = 6m + 3 − 1
               = 6m + 2
               = 2(3m + 1)
```

Since \( 3^{k+1} - 1 \) contains a factor of 2, \( 3^{k+1} - 1 \) is even.

Therefore, by the inductive hypothesis, \( 3^n - 1 \) is even, for all \( n \in \mathbb{W} \).
