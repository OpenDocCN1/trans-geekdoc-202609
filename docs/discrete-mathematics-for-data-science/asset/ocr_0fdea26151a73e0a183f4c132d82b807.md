# An equivalent epop postfix expression:

```
p NOT q OR
```

The fact that \( p \rightarrow q \) is equivalent to \( \neg p \lor q \) can be verified using a truth table. The verification will be left as an exercise.

Given an implication, the first proposition may be referred to as a hypothesis or *antecedent* and the latter one referred to as the *conditional proposition*. The evaluation of the implication may be referred to as the *conclusion* or *consequent*.

The truth table associated with implication is:

| p | q | \( p \rightarrow q \) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | \( p \rightarrow q \) is true as both p and q are false. |
| 0 | 1 | 1 | \( p \rightarrow q \) is always true if p is false. |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | \( p \rightarrow q \) is false only when p is true and q is false. |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | \( p \rightarrow q \) is true when both p and q are true. |

An *implication*, \( p \rightarrow q \), is associated with the following conditional propositions:

+   * Converse: \( q \rightarrow p \). Swap the places of the hypothesis and conclusion.
+   * Inverse: \( \neg p \rightarrow \neg q \). Negate both the hypothesis and conclusion.
+   * Contrapositive: \( \neg q \rightarrow \neg p \). Negate both the hypothesis and conclusion and swap them.

If a condition is true *if and only if* another condition exists, then we have a *bi-implication*, denoted either as \( \leftrightarrow \) or *iff*.
