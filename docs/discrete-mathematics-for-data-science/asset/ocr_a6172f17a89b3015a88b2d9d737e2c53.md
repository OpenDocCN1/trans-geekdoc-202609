The effectiveness of compression can be measured as the size of the compressed data set relative to the original data set.

Lossy methods of compression allow some bits to be lost after decompression. This may acceptable for some types of data, such as sound and graphics. The ear and the eye may not be able to perceive the missing bits. Conversely, lossless methods of compression are generally necessary for record keeping. For example, information from medical diagnostics and financial transactions must often be complete.

Huffman Encoding is a type of lossless compression that encodes and decodes using a binary tree called a Huffman tree.

As an example, suppose that we have a text message consisting of 120 characters, using only the characters A, B, C, X, Y, and Z. These characters have different frequencies of occurrence in the message. Their probabilities and entropy are given as follows:

| Symbol | Probability | Entropy | Entropy total |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | 27 / 120 | 0.48 | 13.07 |
| B | 9 / 120 | 0.28 | 2.52 |
| C | 21 / 120 | 0.44 | 9.24 |
| X | 15 / 120 | 0.38 | 5.63 |
| Y | 12 / 120 | 0.33 | 3.99 |
| Z | 16 / 120 | 0.39 | 6.20 |

To compress or decompress, start with one of the symbols, such as ‘A’, to signify the root node of the Huffman tree. Left branches in the tree will be labeled with 0s and right branches will be labeled with 1s.

Re-order the symbols in terms of frequency:

| B | Y | X | Z | C | A |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 9 | 12 | 15 | 16 | 21 | 27 |
