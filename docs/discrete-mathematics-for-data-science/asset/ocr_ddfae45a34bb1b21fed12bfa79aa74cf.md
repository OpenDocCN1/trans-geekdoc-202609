In computer science, algorithm efficiency is also known by the terms *time complexity* and *algorithm complexity*. An algorithm's complexity and its efficiency go hand-in-hand.

Analysis of algorithms for their time complexity allows us to estimate the relative performance of different algorithms and whether a problem might be solved quickly or slowly. These are important considerations for data science, where processing *Big Data* can require a significant investment in computing resources and run-time before a useful solution is discovered.²

Algorithms are intended to solve problems. However, their computability for a given problem may not be consistent, meaning that a problem may sometimes have no solution and, at other times, there may be multiple solutions. Such undetermined output may be described as *probabilistic*.

In the *Theory of Computation*, problems are classified as *computable* or *decidable*. Computable problems are those for which computational solutions can be generated in a finite amount of time. Decidable problems are those for which a correct “yes” or “no” (or true or false) answer can be produced in a finite amount of time.
