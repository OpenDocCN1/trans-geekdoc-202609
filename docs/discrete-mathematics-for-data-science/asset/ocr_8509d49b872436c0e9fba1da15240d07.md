Empirical data is that which is encountered outside a model. Such data may be considered factual and may be recorded from the outside world. In contrast, logical data is internal to models, as it may be assumed or induced logically. The overlap of these concepts with respect to machine states is the essence of *computability*.

# ## 9.1 NP-COMPLETENESS

*NP-completeness* refers to problems for which there is a verifiable solution. *NP Completeness Theory* is concerned with *P* and *NP* problems. *P problems* are solved in *polynomial time* (also referred to as an algorithm's time *complexity*).<sup>1</sup> *NP problems* are solved in *non-deterministic polynomial time*.

The solution to a *deterministic problem* will be a certain expected result for a given set of inputs. In contrast, the solution to a *non-deterministic problem* can vary, even with the same set of inputs.

Polynomial time is a measure of the amount of work that an algorithm must perform in order to solve a given problem. Polynomial time is expressed in terms of *Big-O* notation, such as *O(n<sup>k</sup>)*, where *n* is the input size and *k* is a constant. The “O” refers to the *order of magnitude*, the exponent *k*. (Hence, it may be said that an algorithm has a certain “Big-O”).
