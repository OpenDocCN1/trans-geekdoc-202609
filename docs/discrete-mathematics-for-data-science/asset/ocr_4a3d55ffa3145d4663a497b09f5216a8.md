The square root of the sample variance gives us the *sample standard deviation*, denoted s:

```
s = \sqrt{s^2}
```

Using \( n - 1 \) for the denominator and the prior example's list of lengths (in feet) 19, 21, 21, 24, 25, 28, consider the following statistics:

```
x = 23 ft
range = 27 - 19 = 8 ft
sample variance = s^2 = \frac{(19-23)^2+(21-23)^2+\cdots+(28-23)^2}{6-1} \approx 10.8 ft^2
sample standard deviation = s = \sqrt{s^2} \approx 3.3 ft
```

A somewhat intuitive perspective of degrees of freedom ( \( n - 1 \) ) is afforded by *Set Theory*. Consider that in the *transitive relation* of three elements, \( a \leftrightarrow \mu \leftrightarrow b \), there exists two direct relations, \( a \leftrightarrow \mu \) and \( \mu \leftrightarrow c \). That is, the set \( S = \{ a \mu b \} \) has the *successor relation* \( R = \{ (a, \mu)(\mu, c) \} \), where the first relation represents the difference \( a - \mu \), the second relation represents the difference \( \mu - b \), and \( \mu \) is the mean value of the elements. The number of successor relations is clearly \( n - 1 \).
