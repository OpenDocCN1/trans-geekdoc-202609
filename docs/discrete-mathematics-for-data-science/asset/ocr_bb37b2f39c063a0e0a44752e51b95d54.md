+   - One independent variable exhibits a high r-squared with other independent variables.
+   - Parameter estimates vary significantly between sample data sets.
+   - Forecasts significantly change after removing weakly correlated variables.
+   - There are high *Variance Inflation Factors*, defined below.

During the process of reducing dimensionality, our objective is to retain uncorrelated factors. Uncorrelated factors are also referred to as *orthogonal factors*.

Perhaps the simplest method to avoid dimensionality problems is to examine the category names of the predictors. Look for possible redundancy and irrelevance, and then drop those predictors that would likely duplicate the effects of other predictors, or are just not relevant to model. For example, if we are forecasting the S & P 500 index, we can probably ignore data from the S & P 100 index, as well as data on geese migration patterns.
