A famous paper published in 1952 by Harry Markowitz, titled
_Portfolio Selection_, suggested that the optimal number of
stocks for a portfolio is approximately twenty.³ That finding
has been corroborated by other studies since then,
concluding that the optimal number is roughly the same.

# Key implications for investment portfolios:

+   - Beyond a certain number of stocks, there is no reduction
+   in portfolio volatility.
+   
+   - We can eliminate certain holdings without increasing the
+   portfolio's overall risk.
+   
+   - A random selection of twenty stocks should give about
+   the same return as the general market.
+   
+   - A selection of twenty stocks out-performs (or under-
+   performs) the general market if it includes at least one
+   non-correlating stock that out-performs (under-performs)
+   the general market.
