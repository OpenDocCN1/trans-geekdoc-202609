Why does the most significant digit of 10,236 correspond with the 2¹³ column for binary? Because 2¹³ is the largest power of 2 that is less than 10,000. Therefore, we have 2¹³ × 1 for that column.

Conversely, the next most significant digit of 10,236 is 0, corresponding with the 2¹². Therefore, we have 2¹ × 0 for that column. We continue that progression, from left to right, until arriving at the *least significant* (rightmost) digit, 2⁰. At that point, the binary equivalent of decimal 10,236 is revealed as 1001111111100 in binary.

Here is an example showing the columnar correspondence of hexadecimal 27FC and decimal 10236.

| 16³ | 16² | 16¹ | 16⁰ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4906 | 256 | 16 | 1 |
| 2 | 7 | F | C |

Where 2 × 4096 + 7 × 256 + 15 × 16 + 12 × 1 = 0d10236.

From there, the columnar correspondence of a number's hexadecimal and decimal notation provides a fast way to convert the hexadecimal to binary. This is because each hexadecimal digit can be represented by four binary bits. So, if you memorized the four-bit patterns in Table 4.1, then you will be more adept at converting hexadecimal to binary. For example:

+   - 2 is 0010
+   - 7 is 0111
+   - F is 1111
+   - C is 1100

Therefore, 0x27FC is: 0010 0111 1111 1100, or 0b0001001111111100.
