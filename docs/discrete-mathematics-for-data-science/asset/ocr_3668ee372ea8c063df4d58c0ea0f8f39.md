Note the two carried bits. Consider that 1 + 1 = 10 in binary, since 1 is carried to the next column.

Two's complement binary numbers are distinguished with a prefix, such as tc. That way the prior result becomes tc11110100 or tc0b11110100.

Because the two's complement of a number is used to represent it as a negative number, a sign bit is not relevant. That is why we ignore the most significant bit when taking the two's complement of a signed number. For 8-bit signed numbers, this leaves a range from -128 to +127 (or from -2<sup>8-1</sup> to +2<sup>8-1</sup> - 1).

By the way, we should expect that taking the two's complement of zero is 0 (referred to as the weird number). For example, lets see what happens when we take the two's complement of 0b00000000.
