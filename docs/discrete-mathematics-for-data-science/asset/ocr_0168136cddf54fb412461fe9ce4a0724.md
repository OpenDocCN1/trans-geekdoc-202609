A parser enforces token sequences in accordance with the grammar of a given programming language. Without imposing an expected order, context free grammars would otherwise be ambiguous.

The output of the parser will be a series of primitive (terminal) words. These terminal words may be indexed to correspond to a parse tree, also called a concrete parse tree. If the parse tree maps abstract words to primitive words, then this parse tree may also be called an abstract syntax tree.
