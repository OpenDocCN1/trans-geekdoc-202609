# ABSTRACT

Researchers have extensively used convolutional neural networks to detect meteor falls on Earth. However, when dealing with limited available data, these networks may need more robustness to classify new real-world images correctly. This study proposes an optimized training approach of a pre-trained model with an attention mechanism to achieve better generalization results in such a scenario. We compare two architectures, an optimized base model and another version with an attention mechanism. Furthermore, we present a new and publicly available optical meteor dataset that merges several public data sources. We used the merged dataset to train classification models combined with a stratified five-fold cross-validation strategy to determine the reliability of the prediction. The experimental results from both architectures showed good and similar performance. To further determine the best architecture, we performed an additional analysis with visual explanations in new observations. The architecture with an attention mechanism was the best model achieving a false alarm ratio of 2.6% and an accuracy of 97%.

# 1. Introduction

When meteoroids in space enter Earth's upper atmosphere and begin to glow, they become meteors. When a meteor falls, the excitation of air molecules produces the light and color of its trail. Meteors travel in different orbits around the Sun and can strike Earth from any angle. Unlike other pursuits of observation in astronomy, meteor observers do not need expensive equipment to spot them (Lunsford, 2009).

Detecting meteor falls plays a critical role in understanding the formation of the Solar System, because we can obtain much relevant information from meteor observations, such as direction, speeds, and masses (Koschny et al., 2019).

There are geographically distributed meteor network observation communities worldwide with the help of citizen scientists. The Brazilian Meteor Observation Network (BRAMON) is one of the largest meteor monitoring networks in the world, considering the number of stations and the covered area, (Betzel, 2022). However, cameras inevitably capture events similar to meteors, such as aircraft, satellites, clouds, flashing lights, and atmospheric events, which challenge the detection by humans or systems. Therefore, it is essential to establish a robust classifier to discriminate meteors from other sorts of objects correctly.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) have achieved remarkable results in computer vision, surpassing humans in applications such as object recognition (He et al., 2015). Therefore, we can attach attention mechanisms to CNNs to select relevant features. Previous studies have adopted CNN classification models to predict the “meteor” or “non-meteor” class of optical observations. Although this approach leads to compelling results, it is much more data-hungry due to the number of parameters to include in the estimation process (Yamashita et al., 2018). This situation can lead to a lack of robustness when dealing with limited training data due to overfitting.

In this study, we investigate the use of attention mechanisms with optimized CNN architecture training to achieve better generalization results when detecting meteors in sky images. We show that this approach has the potential to overcome the need for large volumes of data and improves the prediction robustness in the application of meteor detection. Furthermore, we present a new and publicly available optical observation of 7000 balanced meteors and non-meteors images by merging three public data sources to aid future work. We also performed a visual explanation analysis with the Grad-CAM algorithm on new images to determine the best architecture by analyzing the features to which the classifiers pay more attention.
