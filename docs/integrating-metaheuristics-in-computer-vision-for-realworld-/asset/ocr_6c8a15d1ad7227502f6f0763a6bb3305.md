In drug repositioning, new uses for already-available medications are found faster and less expensive. Different computational drug repositioning strategies exist. Computational drug repositioning strategies can be loosely categorized into three groups using the COVID-19 pandemic as an example: (i) drug/target network-based models, (ii) structure-based approaches, and (iii) AI approaches. There are two different categories of network-based methods: network-based clustering and network-based propagation [11]. Over the past few years, data-driven ML applications have expanded dramatically, and they are now are a crucial tool in the early phases of drug discovery. Several factors, including the speedy accumulation of practical experimental data (such as Drug Bank, ChEMBL, PDB, PubChem, and PDBbind), the advent of modern ML algorithms, libraries, and affordably priced computing power, are driving this trend.

ML techniques attempt to learn from past data and foresee qualities rather than immediately computing them based on a physics-based understanding [14].
