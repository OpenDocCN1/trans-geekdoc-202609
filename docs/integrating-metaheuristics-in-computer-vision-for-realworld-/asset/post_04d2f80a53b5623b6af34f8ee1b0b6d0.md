

$$\vec{D}_1 = \vec{D}_{\alpha} - \vec{A}_1 \cdot (\vec{S}_{\alpha})\\
\vec{D}_2 = \vec{D}_{\beta} - \vec{A}_2 \cdot (\vec{S}_{\beta})\\
\vec{D}_3 = \vec{D}_{\delta} - \vec{A}_3 \cdot (\vec{S}_{\delta})$$
(13.12)

$$\vec{D}_1(t+1) = \frac{\vec{D}_1 + \vec{D}_2 + \vec{D}_3}{3}$$
(13.13)

The presence of vectors A and C renders the Grey Wolf algorithm stochastic. To avoid local minima, the vector fluctuates within a predetermined range.

$$\vec{A} = 2\vec{v} \cdot \vec{r}_1 - v$$
(13.14)

$$\vec{C} = 2 \cdot \vec{r}_2$$
(13.15)

Refer to Eq. (13.14) to establish the exploration and exploitation phases. If \( |\vec{A}| < 1 \), the wolves will attack the prey; otherwise, \( |\vec{A}| > 1 \) changes to the exploration operator, who conducts a global search. Component v linearly declines from 2 to 0, while the random vectors \( r_1, r_2 \) range from 0 to 1. Refer to Eq. (13.15) for more information on the C parameter, which adds weight to the exploration process. The scale runs from 0 to 2.

## 13.2.9 Arithmetic Algorithm

Abualigah et al. [6] proposed the arithmetic optimization algorithm (AOA); it employs straightforward arithmetic operators to find the optimal solution. As a first step, the algorithm is designed to produce a population of randomly generated solutions. During an operation, the operators calculate the ideal locations of adjacent optimal solutions. The best solution obtained thus far is used to update each solution. Typically, AOA algorithms involve searching the search space for the optimal solution. Exploration and exploitation are typically the two phases of this procedure. During the exploration phase, the algorithm generates a variety of solutions by randomly or heuristically traversing the search space. The objective of the exploration phase is to examine as much of the search space as feasible in order to identify potential areas where the optimal solution may exist while preventing local solutions. Equation 13.16 demonstrates that the search phase is influenced by Math Optimizer Accelerated (MOA) function. Following that, enhancement of the provided solution is carried out using Exploitation. Few of the works mentioned in literature can be found in Mahajan et al. [45, 46]. The configuration of both process parts is detailed in the subsections that follow.

$$MOA(itr) = min + itr \times \left( \frac{max - min}{maxIt} \right)$$
(13.16)

### Exploration Phase

The exploration step involves using fundamental mathematical operators to search the entire region for the best possible solution. Equation 13.17 provides a mathematical description of the procedure.

\[ X_{i,j}(itr + 1) = \begin{cases} best(X_j) \div (MOP + \epsilon) \times ((ub_j - lb_j) \times Mu + lb_j), \ if \ r_2 < 0.5 \\ best(X_j) \times MOP \times ((ub_j - lb_j) \times Mu + lb_j), \ otherwise \end{cases} \]
(13.17)

Where \( X_{i,j}(itr + 1) \) represents the \( ith \) solution for the next step, \( X_{i,j}(itr + 1) \) represents the \( jth \) position in the \( ith \) solution for the current step, and \( best(X) \) represents the \( jth \) place in the best-achieved result. \( \epsilon \) is a non-deterministic variable utilized to expand and explore spaces. In addition, the minimum and maximum values at the \( jth \) position are indicated by \( lb_j \) and \( ub_j \), respectively. While \( r2 \) is chosen at random, the control parameter \( Mu \) helps to fine-tune the search procedure.

As stated in equation 13.18, the Math Optimizer probability (MOP), is the function’s value at the \( t \)-th iteration, \( MOP(itr) \) is a coefficient, \( itr \) represents the current step, while \( maxIt \) is the utmost number of iterations. It is the number of iterations that can be used. The sensitivity parameter \( \alpha \) is defined. It is utilized for accuracy during the exploitation phase, and its value is maintained at 5. However, the density and dispersion rate of these operators makes it difficult to approach the target.

\[ MOP(itr) = 1 - \left( \frac{itr}{maxIt} \right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}} \]
(13.18)

### Exploitation Phase

In this phase, mathematical operators are used to refine the result. This phase is determined by the MOA function value. As shown in Equation 13.19, the addition and/or subtraction search method is utilized for search refinements.

\[ X_{i,j}(itr + 1) = \begin{cases} best(X_j) \div (MOP + \epsilon) \times ((ub_j - lb_j) \times Mu + lb_j), \ if \ r_3 < 0.5 \\ best(X_j) \times MOP \times ((ub_j - lb_j) \times Mu + lb_j), \ otherwise \end{cases} \]
(13.19)

Where \( r_3 \) is an integer generated at random by the procedure. If \( r_3 \) is less than 0.5, the subtraction is applied; otherwise, the addition operator is utilized. The stochastic parameter \( Mu \) is designed to circumvent problems with local minima. Additionally, it facilitates exploration throughout iterations.

## 13.2.10 Aquila Optimization Algorithm

In the year 2021, Abualigah et al. [5] proposed a new swarm intelligence algorithm called Aquila Optimizer (AO). The Aquila Optimizer attempts to solve optimization problems by emulating the hunting process of eagles. The algorithm begins by arbitrarily generating a population of initial candidate solutions in the search space. Eagles search the solution space by arbitrarily selecting and calculating the fitness values of potential prey. The eagles then decide based on their fitness values whether to continue hunting their current prey or move to a new one. Aquila possesses four distinct hunting strategies for different prey categories. Aquila is capable of rapidly adjusting its hunting tactics for various targets before launching an attack with its incredibly fast speed. The mathematical framework is briefly explained here.

### Phase 1: Expanded exploration (X1): high soar with a vertical stoop

The Aquila hovers far above the ground and exhaustively conducts a thorough search and then dives straight down toward its target upon locating its prey. Following is a mathematical representation of this behavior:

$$X_1(t+1) = X_{best}(t) \times \left(1 - \frac{t}{T}\right) + (X_M(t) - X_{best}(t) \times r1)$$
(13.20)

$$X_M(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N X_j(t)$$
(13.21)

where \(X_{best}(t)\) is the best-achieved location at the current step and \(X_M(t)\) is the average position of all Aquilas. The current step is denoted by \(t\), and \(T\) represents the highest allowed iteration. \(N\) represents the total population size, and \(r1\) is an arbitrary number between 0 and 1.

### Phase 2: Narrowed exploration (X2): contour flight

This is Aquila's preferred method of hunting. After descending within the designated region and flying around the target, it attacks the prey with short gliding. The position is updated using the following formula:

$$X_2(t+1) = X_{best}(t) \times LF(D) + X_R(t) - (y - x) \times rand()$$
(13.22)

$$LF(D) = s \frac{m \times \sigma}{|\nu|^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}$$
(13.23)

where \(X_R(t)\) represents a random Aquila position, \(D\) is the number of dimensions, and \(rand()\) represents a random number between 0 and 1. The Levy flight function, abbreviated as \(LF(D)\), is shown below:

$$LF(D) = s \frac{m \times \sigma}{|\nu|^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}$$
(13.24)

m and v are random numbers ranging from 0 to 1, whereas s and β are fixed values of 0.01 and 1.5, respectively. In the search, y and x indicate the spiral shape, which is determined as follows:

$$x = d \times \sin(\theta) \quad (13.25)$$

$$y = d \times \cos(\theta) \quad (13.26)$$

$$d = rand() + 0.00565 \quad (13.27)$$

$$\theta = -\omega \times D_1 + \frac{3 \times \pi}{2} \quad (13.28)$$

where D₁ is an array of numbers from 1 to the dimension size (D), ω is 0.005, and t is the number of iterations in the search between [1, 20].

### Phase 3: Expanded exploitation (X3): low flight with a slow descent attack

Once the target/prey area has been broadly defined, the Aquila descends vertically to commence the initial attack. AO utilizes the designated zone to approach and assault the target. The following mathematical expression is an illustration of this behavior:

$$X_3(t + 1) = (X_{best}(t) - X_M(t)) \times \alpha - rand() + ((u_b - l_b) \times rand() + l_b) \times \delta$$
(13.29)

where X_best(t) is the best possible location that has been reached so far and X_M(t) is the average value of the current position. The upper and lower bounds of the optimization problem are denoted by u_b and l_b, respectively. rand represents a random number between 0 and 1, whereas \(\alpha\) and \(\delta\) are exploitation adjustment parameters and the value for both of them is set to 0.1.

### Phase 4: Narrowed exploitation (X4): walking and grabbing prey

The Aquila follows the prey's route before attacking it on the ground. This behavior can be expressed mathematically as:

$$X_4(t + 1) = QF(t) \times X_{best}(t) - (G_1 \times X(t) \times rand()) - G_2 \times LF(D) + rand() \times G_1$$
(13.30)

where X(t) represents the current position and QF(t) is the quality function's value that is being utilized to weigh the search strategy. G₁, a random number between -1 and 1, is Aquila's parameter to track prey movement. G₂ represents the glide path, which drops steadily from 2 to 0 when pursuing prey. rand() returns a random number ranging from 0 to 1.
