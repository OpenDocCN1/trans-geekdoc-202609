# Abstract

Content-based image retrieval (CBIR) enables users to retrieve photos based on their visual content rather than textual keywords or tags. Based on visual elements including color, texture, shape, and composition, it entails looking for digital photos in a sizable database. By various approaches, the visual characteristics of query image is compared with the photos in the database, including feature extraction, indexing, and similarity measurement. Large image collections are now possible through advancements in data storage and image capturing technology. In this case, it is essential to create the right communication system and manage those collections effectively. The utmost popular methods make use of that are known as CBIR technologies. These types of systems essentially look for photos that match a user-defined pattern or specification (such as a shape sketch or an example of an image). Their objective is to facilitate image retrieval based on content qualities, which are typically stored into feature vectors and include attributes like shape, color, and texture. The ability for automatic retrieval is one of the advantage of CBIR approach over the conventional keyword-based approach, that often requires time-intensive note of database images. Many applications, including surveillance, medical imaging, and e-commerce, can benefit from these systems. Many well-known information retrieval engines, including Google Images and Bing Images, are built on the CBIR platform. As computer vision and machine learning technologies have grown, it has become more complex and advanced. But it still has certain issues to work out, to cope with images of poor quality and strike a good balance between recall and precision.
