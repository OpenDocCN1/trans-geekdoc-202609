# 15.3.1.2.1 Color Histogram

In CBIR systems, color histograms are the primary tool for representing the color information of images. A type of bar graph known as a color histogram is one in which each and individual bars represent an actual color in e currently used color space. A color histogram is a rough illustration of the likelihood of the three color channels’ combined values. By dividing the data range into bins of equal sizes, the histogram’s most prevalent form can be derived. The likelihood that a pixel will fall into a certain bin is then determined for each bin by counting and normalising the number of colours of the pixel in a picture which fall into each bin to total points. The fact that the colour histogram does not account for the spatial data contained in pixels is one of its fundamental flaws.

The fact that two quite diverse photos have a similar colour distribution allows us to compare them. The cumulated colour histogram, first forth by Stricker and Orengo, is an enhancement of the colour histogram approach. The benefits of the suggested approach over the traditional colour histogram approach were shown by their findings. Nevertheless, the approach has the drawback that there is no obvious way to organise bins when there are multiple-dimensional histograms.

# 15.3.1.2.2 Color Moments

Stricker and Orengo suggested employing the colour moments technique to get around the quantization issues. Color moments, are the statistical moments of colour probability distributions, are often employed in retrieval systems, particularly when a picture only contains the item itself. It has been shown that the mean, variance, and skewness color moments can be used to accurately represent the colour distributions of photographs. The issue with colour moments is that they have the same shortcomings as the colour histogram technique in that they do not encode any spatial information surrounding colour inside an image.

# 15.3.1.2.3 Color Correlogram

The color correlogram is a method for recording an image’s color information. Unlike to color histograms and moments, this approach adds spatial data in the encoded color information, which solves some of the issues with previous representations. The color correlogram has the advantages of being easy to compute, including color spatial correlation, and being able to describe the worldwide distribution of color spatial correlation.
