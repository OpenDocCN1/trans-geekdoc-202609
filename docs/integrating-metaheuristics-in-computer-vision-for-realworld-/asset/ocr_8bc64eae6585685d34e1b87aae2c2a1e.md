Simple brute force methods are neither practicable nor desirable for finding optimal solutions, despite the ever-increasing computing capacity of today’s computers. Algorithm efficiency is essential to practically all applications. There are a wide variety of various optimization algorithms, including evolutionary algorithms like evolutionary strategy and genetic algorithm, as well as more conventional approaches like gradient-based algorithms, Tabu search, and the interior-point method [1]. New algorithms like the ant colony optimizer (ACO) [2], particle swarm optimizer (PSO) [3], bat algorithm (BA) [4], Aquila optimizer (AO) [5], and arithmetic optimizer (AO) [6] are all based on ideas from nature. A few other techniques in different fields are discussed in Sharma et al., Mahajan et al., and Singh et al. [7–9].

Every optimization algorithm has some advantages and disadvantages. With the help of this biologically-inspired paradigm, researchers began to explore the cooperative systems in the natural world that they might use to develop nature-inspired algorithms. This collaborative approach has led to the investigation of these algorithms for their beneficial purpose by mitigating their drawbacks [10, 11]. For example: in the Gradient-Based method, the variations are represented by differential equations with space and time gradients. The downside of this method is its reliance on noise, occlusions, and discontinuities. Similarly, the gravitational algorithm (GA)-based method can be difficult to optimize and can be time-consuming to run. Additionally, they may not find the global optimum and can be stuck in local optima. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) has a slow speed of convergence. Grey Wolf Optimization algorithm (GWO) has the disadvantages of slow convergence speed, low solution accuracy, and easily falling into the local optimum. Similarly, arithmetic optimizer (AO) has an inconsistent convergence rate.

# 13.1.1 Motivation

Although swarm intelligence and nature-inspired computation have long been areas of interest to mankind, significant advancements have been achieved since the 1990s. Recent years have seen advances in computationally effective and viable algorithms that are inspired by nature. Due to their immense intrinsic benefits, these algorithms are used to solve a wide variety of practical problems. The primary objective of this chapter is to offer readers a unified consortium containing a summarized view of these developments, with an emphasis on optimization techniques.
