It is observed that tumor mass beyond 2 mm in size cannot grow without nutritional support [3]. This requirement of cancer is fulfilled by neovasculariture (formation of new vessel). Cancer neovasculariture is the formation of new blood vessel from preexisting vessel. The formation of blood vasculature is a rate-limiting step and hence a hallmark step in carcinogenesis [6]. Blood vessel formation is a comprehensive process with multiple steps that involves large-scale interaction between stromal and epithelial cells [6]. Angiogenesis is initiated in the preneoplastic lesion and subsequently support the tumor progression, invasion, and metastasis. Angiogenesis is controlled by vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), along with, basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), platelet derived growth factor (PDGF), and several other chemokines/cytokines, which are secreted by tumor cells as well as stromal cells [7–9]. In addition, angiopietin (Ang)–Tie system, Ephrin–Eph pathway, and Delta–Notch signalling pathways have key role in angiogenesis regulation [10]. Based on this observation, various VEGF inhibitors for the treatment of advanced cancer has been approved by FDA [11]. However, due to the acquired resistance against these therapies, effectiveness of therapeutic drugs is limited, thus, creating the idea of further deep knowledge of angiogenesis. Moreover, machine learning or artificial intelligence is a recently developing approach in biomedical field. Various different machine learning or deep learning tolls are currently in use for angiogenesis observation, biomarker evaluation, and prognosis calculation [12]. # 16.2 History of Angiogenesis Discovery

The term angiogenesis was first to come into existence in 1787 by a British surgeon John Hunter [13]. Further, a study reported the vascular structure of tumors in the 1860s [14]. In 1907, a vascularnet was visualized in human tumor specimens using bismuth oil injections, and intra-arterial injections [15]. Then from 1930 to 1940, several investigations began to determine molecular events in neovascularization. Ide et al. and Algire et al. in the late 1930s and early 1940s observed new vasculature formation “angiogenesis” in the tumor mass [14–17]. Then in 1982, Ziche and Gullino analyzed neoplastic transformation in in vivo and in vitro cancer models [18]. Further, Brem and colleagues noted the formation of leaky capillaries in rabbit iris after human breast neoplastic lesion implementation [19].

# 16.3 Overview of Angiogenesis

Neovascularization is a complex phenomenon that involves multiple interactions between tumor stromal cells and tumor epithelial cells. Angiogenic “switch” is caused by hypoxia which the primary event and subsequently involves the release of several proangiogenic mediator (e.g., growth factors, chemokines, and cytokines) that stimulate endothelial cell activation.
