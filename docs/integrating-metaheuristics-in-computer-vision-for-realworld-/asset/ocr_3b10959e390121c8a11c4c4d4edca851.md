# 2.3.3.1 Image Processing Technique

Image processing is a method for modifying digital photos to raise its calibre or glean valuable information from them. It involves analysing, enhancing, and editing photos using a variety of mathematical techniques.

Image filtering is one example of an image processing technique that is used to eliminate noise or other undesired elements from a picture. This is commonly accomplished by applying a filter, which is a mathematical function to the pixels in the image. Image filters are classified into three types: low-pass filters, high-pass filters, and median filters. Low-pass filters soften a picture by eliminating high-frequency noise or sharp edges, whereas high-pass filters increase edges and other high frequency characteristics. Salt-and-pepper noise, which appears as white or black pixels randomly spread throughout a picture, is removed using median filters.

Image enhancement is another image processing approach that includes increasing the quality or clarity of a picture. Several approaches, such as contrast stretching, histogram equalization, and colour correction, can be used to accomplish this. Contrast stretching is the process of increasing the range of brightness levels in an image in order to make it more aesthetically attractive. Histogram equalization is a technique for adjusting visual contrast by dispersing pixel values throughout the whole brightness range. Color correction is the process of adjusting an image’s color balance to make it look more natural and realistic.

Another major image processing approach is image segmentation, which involves splitting an image into areas or items of interest. This may be accomplished through the use of several approaches such as thresholding, edge detection, and region growth. Thresholding is the process of separating an image into two or more regions based on a threshold value, whereas edge detection is the process of determining the borders between image regions. The process of growing regions entails recognizing areas based on their resemblance to nearby pixels.
