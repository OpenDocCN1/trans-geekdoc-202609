Further events include the release of proteolytic enzymes that degrade ECM (extracellular Matrix), migration of endothelial cells through degraded ECM, proliferation of endothelial cells, and eventually capillary tube formation [20]. Following this, endothelial cells get differentiated into tip, stalk and tube cells, in which on-proliferative tip cells expresses Delta-like ligand 4 (DLL4) and present at the front of new vessels. Highly proliferative stalk cells express Notch-1 and form trunk of vessel [21]. Tube cells are non-proliferating cells and contains lumen that gives the final shape to vessel. In addition, pericytes cells, express platelet-derived growth factor receptor beta (PDGFR-β), and alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and cover the vessel from outside [22].

# 16.4 Angiogenesis in Carcinogenesis

Several in vivo and in vitro experimental evidence supported the correlation of aggressive carcinogenesis with increased angiogenesis. For example, in breast carcinoma in situ (CIS), a severe form of dysplasia, an increase in angiogenic density was reported in invasive tumor mass [23]. Further, in the cervix cancer, a remarkable increase in Microvessel density was also seen [24]. Histopathologically, an increase in angiogenesis density was also confirmed in lung and breast metaplasia, dysplasia, and CIS [25, 26]. Moreover, overexpression of VEGF and PDEGF has been determined in breast, cervix, and oesophagus carcinoma [27]. Similarly, high levels of MMPs and TIMPs have been observed in tumor lesions as they progress from CIS to invasive carcinoma (Figure 16.1) [28, 29].
