### 17.5.2 Root Mean Square Error

Root mean square error (RMSE) is used to compare two images, usually an original image and a treated image. The square root of the average of the squared deviations between the corresponding pixels of the two pictures is used to calculate RMSE. The RMSE can be written mathematically as:

```
RMSE = sqrt(1/n * sum((I1 (i, j) - I2(i, j))^2))   (17.2)
```

### 17.5.3 Peak Signal to Noise Ratio

Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR) is a commonly used metric in image processing for evaluating the quality of an image compared to its original version. It is a measure of the ratio of a signal's maximal possible power to the power of corrupting noise that compromises the accuracy of image. PSNR in image enhancement is a quantitative indicator of the post-processing image quality improvement. The higher value of PSNR indicates better quality of enhanced image [14]. PSNR is calculated as follows:

```
PSNR = 10 * log10[(MaxI)^2/MSE]   (17.3)
```

where Max I is the maximum possible pixel value of the image (usually 255 for an 8-bit image), and MSE is the mean squared error between the original image and the enhanced image [15].

### 17.5.4 Normalized Coefficient

The normalized coefficient (NC), also known as the normalized cross-correlation coefficient, is a measure of the similarity between two images or image patches. The NC is often used to align two images, such as aligning a low-resolution image with a high-resolution image. It can also be used to identify similar regions within an image or between images, which can be useful in techniques, such as image segmentation and object recognition. The NC is calculated by taking the dot product of two image patches and dividing it by the product of their root mean square (RMS) values. The formula for calculating the NC is as follows:

```
NC = \sum (I(a,b) - \mu I)(T(a,b) - \mu T)/(\sigma I \sigma T)   (17.4)
```

where I and T are the two image patches being compared, \mu I and \mu T are their respective mean values, \sigma I and \sigma T are their respective standard deviations, and (a, b) represents the pixel coordinates of the patch. The NC ranges from –1 to 1, where a value of 1 indicates a perfect match between the two patches, 0 indicates no correlation, and –1 indicates a perfect negative correlation.

### 17.5.5 Distance Reciprocal Distortion Metric

The Distance Reciprocal Distortion Metric (DRDM) is a metric used to quantify the quality of an image. The DRDM measures the degree of distortion between an input image and its corresponding output image. It takes into account the distortion caused by the image processing algorithm used for enhancement. The metric calculates the distance between the input and output images, and then computes the reciprocal of that distance [17]. The DRDM formula for enhancement of an image can be expressed as follows:

$$DRDM = \frac{1}{D}$$ (17.5)

where D is the distance measure between the input image I and its corresponding output image O after enhancement. The distance measure can be any standard measure of image distortion such as mean squared error (MSE), peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), or Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). For example, if we use MSE as the distance measure, then the DRDM formula can be written as:

$$DRDM = \frac{1}{MSE(I,O)}$$ (17.6)

Similarly, if we use PSNR as the distance measure, then the DRDM formula can be written as:

$$DRDM = \frac{1}{PSNR(I,O)}$$ (17.7)

And if we use SSIM as the distance measure, then the DRDM formula can be written as:

$$DRDM = \frac{1}{SSIM(I,O)}$$ (17.8)

In all cases, a higher value of DRDM indicates a better quality enhancement, since it means that the distortion between the input and output images is lower.

## 17.7 Conclusion

In this work, we are trying to restore old books and other historical documents that have been damaged due to various reasons such as bad paper, ink smearing or subsequent damage. In our solution, we have used segmentation and clustering techniques to classify and refine the original text. We used a combination of fuzzy logic and neutrosophic methods, the other being k-means integration and local thresholding. It was concluded that the combination of fuzzy logic and neutrosophic set provides better results in comparison to K-means and local thresholding based on MSE and PSNR parameters. On the other hand, K-mean provides better results than neutrosophic set + fuzzy logic and local thresholding based on NC parameters.
