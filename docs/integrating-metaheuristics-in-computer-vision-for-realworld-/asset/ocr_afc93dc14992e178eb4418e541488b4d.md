# 13.2.9 Arithmetic Algorithm

Abualigah _et al._ [6] proposed the arithmetic optimization algorithm (AOA); it employs straightforward arithmetic operators to find the optimal solution. As a first step, the algorithm is designed to produce a population of randomly generated solutions. During an operation, the operators calculate the ideal locations of adjacent optimal solutions. The best solution obtained thus far is used to update each solution. Typically, AOA algorithms involve searching the search space for the optimal solution. Exploration and exploitation are typically the two phases of this procedure. During the exploration phase, the algorithm generates a variety of solutions by randomly or heuristically traversing the search space. The objective of the exploration phase is to examine as much of the search space as feasible in order to identify potential areas where the optimal solution may exist while preventing local solutions. Equation 13.16 demonstrates that the search phase is influenced by Math Optimizer Accelerated (MOA) function. Following that, enhancement of the provided solution is carried out using Exploitation. Few of the works mentioned in literature can be found in Mahajan _et al._ [45, 46]. The configuration of both process parts is detailed in the subsections that follow.
