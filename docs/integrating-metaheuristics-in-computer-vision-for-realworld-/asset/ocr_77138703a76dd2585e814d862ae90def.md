# 1.9 Conclusion

Although the diagnostic tools for ischemic stroke, which has been used from the past few decades and still in the use for the diagnosis for the same, such as CT, MRI, the new emerging technologies, like electromyography, electroencephalography, positron emission tomography, other blood-based biomarker as well as artificial intelligence–based diagnostic tools are in use for the rapid and cost-effective detection of particular damage in the brain. Moreover, rehabilitation and long-term recovery after an ischemic stroke continue to be a major issue for healthcare professionals and patients because there is no viable therapeutic strategy. Nanotechnology is the boon for the therapeutic advances in ischemic stroke treatment through nanomedicines; for controlling abnormalities, to provide oxygen to ischemic brain and nanomedicines that target downstream ischemic cascade events such as tissue healing, inflammatory response and oxidative stress may be more effective stroke treatments. As presented in this review, Carbon nanomaterials have a lot of potential for use in the treatment and diagnostic of ischemic stroke. CNT development is still in its infancy, and considerable work must be done before they can be used in real world application. When used in, in vivo and in vitro models to study the pathophysiological and biochemical pathways behind stroke, CNTs ARE thought to have the most ability as nanobiosensors.

# Future Perspectives

Gold standard imaging methods like CT and MRI are presently used extensively in the recognition and assessment of stroke and its subtypes. These imaging services are primarily found in economically underdeveloped areas, compact hospitals, and dispensaries. Deferring diagnosis and treatment of a stroke results in worse prognosis for a patient. Numerous stroke cases go undetected and many stroke patients go untreated because of high expense of CT and MRI, the low level of explosion to imaging facilities, and the lack of imaging specialists, especially in developing nations. The potential exists to shorten treatment times, lessen the severity of brain destruction, and greatly enhance the quality of life for stroke individual and their caregivers through the development of low cost and more accessible diagnostic tools to quickly identify Acute ischemic stroke. Other than the CT and MRI the cost and time effective diagnostic techniques EMG and EEG are introduced, Frequency–domain and time–domain analyses of surface EMG are most commonly used to measure muscle contraction velocity and examine motor neurons and innervation zones. An EEG instrument that is reasonably priced, easy to set up, and understandable is required.
