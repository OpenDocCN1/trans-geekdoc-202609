The craft of deceiving through a digital platform in order to generate money by forging the loss of an individual is what we call “as digital transactional fraud”. Poor governance of banks along with less prominent checks on system transactions is the key contributor of rising digital frauds [2]. Breaching of security in banking industry is also an explanation to the question why banking frauds exist and perspire. These projects withhold the dare to bring a revolution in the thinking process to develop security-based transactional system. This projects works in accordance of the enrichments of data science and machine learning algorithms, based on datasets training to devise a program that would distinguish between a fraud and legit transaction.

The dataset used for the said program consists of 2,84,807 transactions made in a span of 2 days. This dataset has a total of 31 columns out of which only three columns are known to us, i.e., time, amount, class. The other 28, namely V1–V28, are kept confidential, and so what these parameters are for actually is kept hidden from the internet. However, it can be assumed to have confidential details like, transaction ID, transaction location, customer details, etc. The need for this kind of data exploration has arisen due to inefficiency of banks to keep the customer data refrained from any sort of mass data leakage, which is a prime cause of any sort of frauds [3]. After seeing the significant rise in the number of active frauds through online banking channels and understanding the need of prevention of money loss due to these unethical interfaces, efforts and security program were created as an initiative toward digital banking privacy, as said by Prof. Vikas Palekar and his team [4]. At present, banks are undertaking certain measures to combat the issue of digital frauds which include:

+   a) Keeping a check on the working of their employees as surveys has proven that more than 70% frauds take place due to leakage of information given by the bank officials.
+   b) Transactional monitoring which includes the concept of suspicious activity reports (SAR).
+   c) Use of real time data enrichment tools that enhances the KYC data of customer which is obtained from open source database, digital services, and social networks.
