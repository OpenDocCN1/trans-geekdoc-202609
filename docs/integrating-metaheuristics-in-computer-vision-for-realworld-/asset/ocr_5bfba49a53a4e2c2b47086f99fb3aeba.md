# 17.6.1 Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio

PSNR is a metric commonly used in image processing to measure the quality of a reconstructed or compressed image compared to its original image. It is determined as the ratio of the maximum potential of the signal to noise power, which has an impact on the accuracy of the signal's representation. Higher values of PSNR, which is measured in decibels (dB), correspond to higher image quality. PSNR values between 30 and 50 dB indicate generally good image quality, while values below 30 dB indicate poor quality. Figure 17.7 shows the value of PSNR results of different techniques [10]. The results show that the suggested technique performs better than the current benchmark.

# 17.6.2 Normalized Coefficient

The similarity between two images or sections of images is measured by the normalizing coefficient. In order to align two images, such as a low quality image with a high resolution image, the Normalized Coefficient is frequently utilized.

The normalization coefficient varies from –1 to 1; a value of 1 denotes an exact match between the two patches, while a value of 0 denotes a lack of correlation and a value of -1 denotes a poor correlation [16].
