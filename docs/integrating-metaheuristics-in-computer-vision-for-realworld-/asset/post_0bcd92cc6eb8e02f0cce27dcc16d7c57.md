## 2.3 The Basics of Computer Vision

### 2.3.1 Computer Vision

Using cameras, data, and algorithms rather than the retina, optic nerve, and visual cortex, computer vision teaches a computer for carrying out these tasks. However, it must do so in a much shorter amount of time. Systems that have been taught to check items or keep an eye on production machinery may inspect hundreds of items or tasks per minute and find errors or issues that cannot be seen. The use of computer vision grows quickly across a range of sectors, from manufacturing and cars to energy and utilities.

### 2.3.2 Implementation of Computer Vision

A computer’s vision to comprehend and analyze visual input is achieved via the application of algorithms, methods, and models. Image capture, preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification are some of the procedures that are taken to accomplish this. Using cameras, sensors, or other imaging equipment, image acquisition includes acquiring visual data. The captured pictures are subsequently cleaned up and improved using preprocessing techniques, such as reducing noise, fixing distortions, and altering the color balance. Feature extraction includes detecting essential properties or aspects of the photos, such as edges, corners, textures, or form. CNNs, which are intended to learn from and extract characteristics from enormous volumes of visual input, are frequently used for this purpose. By classifying photos based on their attributes, machine learning techniques are used. Support vector machines (SVMs), decision trees, or deep learning algorithms like CNNs can all be used for this. In general, the topic of computer vision is complicated, involving a variety of methods and models, and it is ever-evolving as new studies are undertaken and new applications are created.

### 2.3.3 Applications of Computer Vision

Several businesses and areas use computer vision in a variety of ways. Here are a few illustrations: Autonomous vehicles: Computer vision is essential for autonomous vehicles to safely navigate the highways. Real-time road condition analysis and object detection are both done with the aid of cameras and sensors. Medical imaging: Computer vision is used to interpret medical pictures, such as X-rays, CT scans, and MRIs. It can assist in the detection of malignancies, the detection of abnormalities, and surgical planning. The identification and recognition of faces using computer vision has applications in security, law enforcement, authentication, and manufacturing.

#### 2.3.3.1 Image Processing Technique

Image processing is a method for modifying digital photos to raise its calibre or glean valuable information from them. It involves analysing, enhancing, and editing photos using a variety of mathematical techniques.

Image filtering is one example of an image processing technique that is used to eliminate noise or other undesired elements from a picture. This is commonly accomplished by applying a filter, which is a mathematical function to the pixels in the image. Image filters are classified into three types: low-pass filters, high-pass filters, and median filters. Low-pass filters soften a picture by eliminating high-frequency noise or sharp edges, whereas high-pass filters increase edges and other high frequency characteristics. Salt-and-pepper noise, which appears as white or black pixels randomly spread throughout a picture, is removed using median filters.

Image enhancement is another image processing approach that includes increasing the quality or clarity of a picture. Several approaches, such as contrast stretching, histogram equalization, and colour correction, can be used to accomplish this. Contrast stretching is the process of increasing the range of brightness levels in an image in order to make it more aesthetically attractive. Histogram equalization is a technique for adjusting visual contrast by dispersing pixel values throughout the whole brightness range. Color correction is the process of adjusting an image’s color balance to make it look more natural and realistic.

Another major image processing approach is image segmentation, which involves splitting an image into areas or items of interest. This may be accomplished through the use of several approaches such as thresholding, edge detection, and region growth. Thresholding is the process of separating an image into two or more regions based on a threshold value, whereas edge detection is the process of determining the borders between image regions. The process of growing regions entails recognizing areas based on their resemblance to nearby pixels.

Overall, image processing techniques have a wide range of applications, including medical imaging, remote sensing, computer vision, and photography. They are critical in increasing picture quality, collecting usable information, and allowing for additional processing and interpretation.

#### 2.3.3.2 Feature Extraction and Feature Selection Technique

Feature Extraction and Feature Selection are two machine learning approaches that are often used to prepare data for model development. The process of translating raw data into a set of features that may be utilized for machine learning is known as feature extraction. In other terms, it is the extraction of the most relevant information from the supplied data. Typically, this is accomplished by applying different mathematical and statistical approaches to raw data in order to minimize its dimensionality and identify the most essential characteristics. In image processing, for example, feature extraction techniques may be used to extract picture edges, corners, and other critical characteristics that can be utilized for object recognition. The process of picking a subset of the most essential characteristics from the original collection of features, on the other hand, is known as feature selection. The purpose of feature selection is to reduce the quantity of input characteristics in order to increase the accuracy and efficiency of a machine learning model. This is often accomplished by assessing the significance of each characteristic using statistical measures such as correlation or by employing various algorithms to evaluate the significance of each feature. In general, feature extraction is used to generate a new collection of features from raw data, whereas feature selection is used to choose the most essential characteristics from the original set. Both strategies are significant in machine learning, and which one to utilize depends on the application and the type of data being studied.

#### 2.3.3.3 Object Recognition Algorithm

Object recognition is a computer vision approach that identifies and locates items in a video or picture stream.

There are several object recognition algorithms that may be utilized; however, the most prevalent ones are:

- 1. Haar Cascade Classifier: The Haar Cascade Classifier is a machine learning-based system that detects objects using a collection of attributes. It is a simple and quick technique that may be used to identify objects in real time. The approach works by using a set of positive and negative pictures to train a classifier. The positive photographs include the thing we are looking for, whilst the negative images do not. These attributes are then used by the algorithm to recognize the item in a fresh picture or video stream.

- 2. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are a sort of deep learning algorithm that has proven to be extremely effective at object recognition. CNNs are inspired by the organization of the human visual system and can learn characteristics from raw data automatically. CNNs are made up of layers such as convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers. These layers collaborate to extract and categorize characteristics from the input picture.
