## Fusion of Phase and Local Features for CBIR

Pooja Sharma

Department of Computer Science & Engineering, I K Gujral Punjab Technical University, Jalandhar, Punjab, India

## Abstract

In this chapter, content-based image retrieval is carried out using global and local features of images. The complete process is based on concepts of amalgamation. The synthesized features of Zernike moments magnitude and phase coefficients are applied to designate the global features of images with local features, which are obtained through histograms by considering distance of centroid of image with linear edges fetched using Hough transform. The processing speed is increased by incorporating efficient algorithms for the calculation of Zernike moments and Hough transform. Overall similarity among training and test images is computed using Bray-Curtis similarity measure, which provides normalized distances and helps upgrade the retrieval rate. The proposed method is capable of retrieving transformed images as well, i.e., the proposed system is robust to photometric and geometric transformations. The results of our comprehensive experiments establish the dominance of the proposed synthesized methodology over other similar methods.

Keywords: Zernike moments, Hough transform, rotation invariance, precision and recall

## 7.1 Introduction

With the progression in the use of images both in the industry and academic world, researchers are encouraged to develop accurate and efficient methods to retrieve relevant images from large databases. The retrieval of images is rather depends on their visual contents just as in Google Lens instead of labor-intensive manual explanations. In an image, the shape of an object is considered to be a very important visual content rather than color and texture. The color features do not provide tangible human perception [1–3]. If a similar object has different colors, for instance, a ball’s color in the first image is red and in another image the color is blue, then both images will suppose to be two dissimilar images, although they are similar in shape. The texture features do not provide semantic information of the image. Hence, shape is counted a significant pictorial feature in entity recognition and retrieval.

The significance of shape based retrieval can be viewed by referring [4–7]. Accordingly, in our approach to an effective image retrieval system we pay attention to shape based image retrieval. The research for an effective shape descriptor and similarity measure is the primary issue in image retrieval applications and one of the crucial aspects of a shape descriptor is to be robust to geometric transformations (rotation, scale, and translation) and photometric transformations (noise, blur, and JPEG compression).

Various shape representation and matching techniques have been proposed during the past two decades. The shape based representation techniques are usually categorized into two classes, namely, contour based descriptors (CBDs) and region based descriptors (RBDs). The CBDs are more popular as compared to RBDs due to the fact that human perception is primarily consistent with the contour/outline of the shape. Besides, in some shape associated applications, the outline or shape of the contour is the only key interest while the innermost details are not obligatory. The contour based methods include curvature scale space [8], Fourier descriptors [9], histograms of centroid distances [10], contour flexibility [11], elastic matching [12], etc. However, some limitations exist with contour based methods. The first one is that the CBDs extract information only from the boundary of the shape and neglect the essential information contained in the shape interior region, which is more important in some applications. Second, CBDs are more prone to noise and variations because they exploit small part of image information, i.e., contour only. Third, in some cases the image contour is not available for instance, it is tedious to extract contour from texture images unlike trademark images. These limitations can be addressed by RBDs. The RBDs are more robust as they utilize all the shape information hence, they can be applied to all types of images including distorted and defected shapes, which is a common problem in CBDs. The region based methods include grid descriptor [13], generic Fourier descriptor GFD [14], Moment invariants and functions of moments [15–18], Zernike moment descriptors (ZMDs) [19], etc. Although moment based RBDs provide attractive characteristics for image retrieval, they do suffer from high computational complexity. Other types of RBDs include scale invariant feature transform (SIFT) [20], Histograms of oriented gradients (HOG) [21], and speeded up robust features (SURF) [22], etc. SIFT is based on the difference of Gaussians (DOG) and utilizes a circular window to search for a possible location of a keypoint. However, the image content in a circular window is not robust to affine transformations [36]. The HOG descriptor is based on SIFT and edge orientation histograms. The participation of global features in images is elaborated by Oliva and Torralba [23]. Apart from that image classification using convolutional neural networks is described in Ong et al. and Pathak et al. [45–47].

CBIR using the combination of CBDs and moments based RBDs is found to be very effective. CBDs provide information regarding the local change in the boundary of an image and do not provide interior details of the image content. On the other hand, RBDs provide interior details of image content while less emphasizing on local variations in an image. Therefore, CBDs and RBDs are assumed to be complementary to each other with their own advantages and disadvantages. Thus, if both descriptors are combined, the performance of the image retrieval system can be improved as observed by Jain and Vailaya, Wei et al., and Qi et al. [24–26]. In the study by Jain and Vailaya [24], a synthesized approach to trademark retrieval in which histograms of edge directions are used to extract local features of images and invariant moments are used to extract global features. The descriptor is invariant to translation but not to scale and rotation. In the studies by Wei et al. [25] and Qi et al. [26], other synthesized approaches to trademark retrieval are provided.

Although the hybrid approaches presented by Wei et al. and Qi et al. [25, 26] provide good results, they suffer from some limitations. First, the curvature based local features suggested by Wei et al. [25] are prone to image noise as the second order derivative is required to compute curvature. Second, although the normalized histograms of the distances do not support association between adjacent edge points. This is an attractive proposition for representing adjacency between edge points. However, it suffers from high radius when two adjacent points are quite close to each other and the variation in radii is quite significant. This affects the correct formation of histograms that weakens the insightful of local features.

The moment based global features are characterized by minimum information redundancy and robustness to noise due to summation operation in moment computation. Apart from being rotation invariant, they can be made translation invariant and scale invariant after some geometric transformations. Among the moment based invariants, the ZMs are found to be the best shape descriptors in many applications [27–31], as they have least information redundancy and possess best robustness to noise [17]. Inspired by these properties, Wei et al. [25] use ZMs as global features. However, the authors exploit only first four orders of moments with magnitude components without using the phase coefficients, which are inadequate to represent the full characteristics of an image. It is observed that ZMs phase coefficients are more effective than magnitude for shape description [32–36]. However, unlike magnitude coefficients, the phase coefficients of ZMs do not exhibit rotation invariance property. In order to make phase coefficients rotation invariant, various approaches have been proposed [33–36]. These approaches first compute the rotation angle between the test and train images then phase correction is performed for the purpose of matching. The similarity matching based on ZMs magnitude and phase coefficients has been found to produce good results in several applications.

In this chapter, we provide an effective hybrid approach by combining the local and global features for image retrieval, which resolves the problems of existing hybrid approaches. The local features are based on the Hough transform of the edge points, which are obtained using Canny edge detector, and the global features are based on ZMs. Hough transform is a very robust method for detecting linear edges in an edge image. Based on summation operation, it is robust to noise. Linear edges provide adjacency property of the local feature points. All edge points falling on a line (having same slope and intercept) constitute a linear edge in the Hough transform. After detecting the linear edges, the histograms are computed. The histograms so obtained are invariant to rotation, translation, and scale. For representing global features, we use both ZMs magnitude and phase coefficients. A correction is made to phase coefficients of ZMs by estimating the rotation angle using the method proposed by Revaud et al. [35]. Although ZMs and Hough transform are computation intensive, we employ fast algorithms [37, 39] to overcome this limitation. ZMs and Hough transform together provide significantly improved results as both are robust to geometric as well as photometric transformations, which is demonstrated by widespread set of experiments on numerous image databases. The discriminative power of the proposed system is demonstrated in the later section of this chapter. To improve the performance of our system further, we use an operative comparison measure known as Sorensen’s distance [40, 41], also called Bray-Curtis distance.
