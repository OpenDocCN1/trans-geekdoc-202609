# 16.5 Molecular Mechanisms of Angiogenesis Formation

Different multiple kinds of proteins have been identified and characterized as angiogenic activators, such as VEGF, bFGF, angiogenin, TGF-α/β, TNF-α, PDEGF, G-CSF, PGF, IL-8, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), and epidermal growth factor (EGF) (Figure 16.1) [30]. The primary stimulator for angiogenesis is hypoxia that triggers hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (Hif-1α) [31]. Hif-1α operates in association with Hippel–Lindau (von VHL) protein that targets HiF-1α for ubiquitination under normoxic conditions (Figure 16.1) [32]. Paracrine or autocrine release of the above-mentioned factors stimulates endothelial progenitor cells for proliferation. Among all these, an important stimulating factor is a member of vascular endothelial growth factor family. There are different members of this family includes VEGF-A, B, C, and D, and their receptors VEGFR-1, 2, and 3. Because of their crucial role in angiogenesis, FDA has approved many VEGF inhibitors (for example, bevacizumab, sorafenib, dasatinib, regorafenib, and cabozantinib) to treatment the advanced stage cancer [33]. Further, the key role of MMPs (matrix metalloproteinase) has also been determined in angiogenesis, that degrade the ECM and form a way for endothelial cell migration, proliferation, and vessel formation. Cyclooxygenase (COX) catalyzes the formation of prostaglandin and thromboxane fromarachidonic acid. The role of COX-1 and COX-2 has been evaluated in angiogenesis induction in the co-culture of colon cancer cells and endothelial cells [34]. The activity of COX-1/2 enzyme helps in adherence of cells to ECM, and hence angiogenesis induction [35]. COX-2 also induces the synthesis of VEGF. Angiogenesis also involves Ang-1 and Ang-2 and tyrosine kinase receptor Tie-2 system. Interaction of Ang-1 with their receptor maintain and stabilize mature blood vessels. However, Ang-2 works as an antagonist of Ang-1 stabilizing its action. During carcinogenesis Ang-1 and Ang-2 has increased expression in tumor cells. Although, the expression of Ang-2 is highly overexpressed compared to Ang-1 and Tie2 [36]. In addition, endothelial-1 (ET-1) and its receptors are also involved in cancer angiogenesis regulation. Endothelin-1 along with its receptors ETAR/ETBR showed overexpression in tumor that has been correlated with high levels of VEGF and higher amount of microvessel density [37]. The role of PDGF-β/PDGFR signalling axis has also been determined in establishment, development, and differentiation of blood vessels, which recruit the maturation of pericytes and maturation of microvasculature in the tumor tissue [38]. Moreover, The notch signaling pathway (Notch1 receptors, DLL4, and Jagged-1 ligand) also has regulatory role in vasculature formation through endothelial cell maturation in [39, 40], renal cell carcinoma [41], bladder carcinoma [42], pancreatic carcinoma [43], and prostate carcinoma [44]. Multiple studies also determined the role of epigenetic regulation in angiogenesis. A wide range of angiogenesis initiation and regulation related miRNAs has been profiled in almost all types of human cancers [45]. For instant, miRNAs such as miR-17-92, let-7f, miR-27b, miR-222/221 etc. showed a significant role in the angiogenesis regulation [46].
