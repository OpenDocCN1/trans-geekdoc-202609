## 2.8 Future Direction in Object Detection and Tracking

### 2.8.1 Future Plans for Object Tracking and Detection

Item detection and tracking have made substantial strides lately and are expected to do so going forward. Here are some possible future developments in the area: 1. Deep learning-based methodologies:

For object detection and tracking, a number of well-liked deep learning-based techniques are employed, including:

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): CNNs are a type of neural network that excel at tasks requiring the processing of images. To work, they apply filters to the input image in order to extract features at various scales and locations. CNNs have been demonstrated to produce state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets and are frequently employed for object detection and tracking applications.
- Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs): RNNs are a class of neural networks that are especially useful for jobs that need sequences of events, including video processing. By storing the temporal dependencies between successive frames, RNNs can be employed for object tracking.
- R-CNNs, or region-based convolutional neural networks: An R-CNN is a type of neural network that functions by first producing a list of region proposals, or potential places for objects in the input image. After extracting features from each region suggestion, the network categorizes each one as an item or a background. It has been demonstrated that R-CNNs excel in object detection tasks with high accuracy.
- YOLO: This well-known real-time object detection algorithm predicts the bounding box and class probabilities for each cell by dividing the input image into a grid of cells. Real-time applications benefit from YOLO’s high accuracy and quick inference times, which have been demonstrated.

Although deep learning-based algorithms have already significantly improved object detection and tracking, there are still numerous opportunities for advancement in the future. These are some potential future paths for object recognition and tracking methods based on deep learning:

- Increased effectiveness: Deep learning models can be expensive to implement in real-time applications due to their computational complexity. More effective deep learning architectures, such as lightweight models or models that can operate on low-power hardware, will likely be the subject of future research.
- Improved accuracy: Deep learning models have produced outstanding outcomes in object detection and tracking, but there is still opportunity for development. Future research may focus on establishing novel loss functions, regularization approaches, or optimization strategies to increase the accuracy of deep learning models.
- Fusion of many modes: Deep learning models can be trained on multiple data modalities, such as images, videos, or LiDAR data. Future research may focus on developing models that can effectively fuse these modalities to improve object detection and tracking performance.
- Robustness to domain shift: Deep learning models may be sensitive to changes in the distribution of the data, which makes them less reliable in situations when the input data may be different from the training data. The development of techniques, such as unsupervised domain adaptation or meta-learning, to increase the robustness of deep learning models to domain change may be the main goal of future research.
- Explainability: It can be tricky to grasp how deep learning models make judgments since they can be challenging to interpret. The development of techniques, such as attention processes or visualizations, to make deep learning models more transparent and understandable may be the main goal of future research.

Deep learning-based algorithms in object detection and tracking have a wide range of future potential applications. The accuracy, effectiveness, and robustness of these methods could be considerably increased with further study and development in this area, increasing their usefulness for real-world applications.

### 2.8.1.1 Multi-object Tracking

The process of tracking many objects in a video sequence over time is referred to as multi-object tracking. Multi-object tracking entails tracking numerous items at once, as opposed to single-object tracking, which focuses on following a single object of interest during the whole video sequence. Multi-object tracking is a tough process due to various issues, including occlusion, object appearance changes, and object interactions [15].

There are various methods for multi-object tracking, such as:

- Tracking by detection: In this method, objects are first identified in each frame using an object detector, and then associations between the identifications in different frames are made to create tracks. This method is frequently employed in multi-object tracking and has been demonstrated to produce cutting-edge outcomes on benchmark datasets.
- Online tracking: This approach involves tracking objects in real-time as new frames arrive. Online tracking algorithms must be fast and efficient to operate in real-time applications.
- Data association: Tracks are created by linking detections across frames using data association. Solving the data association problem, which is sometimes framed as an optimization problem, is a vital step in multi-object tracking.
- Deep learning-based techniques: Deep learning-based approaches, particularly in situations where object appearance changes significantly over time, have demonstrated promising results in multi-object tracking. In these methods, deep neural networks are often trained to directly predict object tracks from input data.

Research on multi-object tracking is ongoing, and this technology has a lot of promising applications in the future. These are some probable directions for the future:

- Increased accuracy: Accurately associating detections over frames is one of the primary issues in multi-object tracking, particularly in circumstances with occlusions and busy scenes. Future work may concentrate on creating stronger algorithms that can manage these difficult situations and boost tracking precision.
- Real-time performance is necessary for many real-world applications, including autonomous driving. The development of more effective algorithms that can run in real-time even on low-power devices could be the main goal of future research.
- Object interaction modeling: In many scenarios, objects interact with each other, which can make tracking more challenging. Future research may focus on developing models that can effectively handle object interactions and improve tracking accuracy in these scenarios.
- Multi-modal tracking: Multi-modal tracking involves tracking objects across different sensor modalities, such as cameras and lidar. Future studies might concentrate on creating algorithms that can combine data from many sensor modalities to increase tracking precision.
- Privacy-preserving tracking: As concerns around privacy continue to grow, there may be an increased focus on developing privacy-preserving tracking algorithms that can operate without storing or transmitting sensitive data.

Multi-object tracking is a rapidly evolving field, and there are numerous exciting possibilities for future research and development. These technologies have the potential to open up new applications in industries like robotics, surveillance, and autonomous vehicles as they advance.
