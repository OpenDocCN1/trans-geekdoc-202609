# 16.7 Machine Learning Approaches in Angiogenesis

Over the last decades, several imaging techniques are emerged that allow researchers to investigate microstructures more thoroughly on smaller scales. Quantifying morphological features, however, was and still is often achieved only semi-quantitatively by analyzing contrast or fluorescence units of an image and remained somewhat elusive and time-consuming. The lack of reproducible morphological quantification techniques may also have led researchers to execute excessive staining and the use of advanced imaging techniques where phase contrast microscopy might have been a sufficient and inexpensive alternative. Advances in computer technology have made it possible to noninvasively quantify tumor angiogenesis [52]. Machine learning is one such technology that involves analytical strategies which are recently been applied to biomarker discovery in digital pathology images (Figure 16.2) [53]. With the availability of deep learning techniques, researchers are able to easily and accurately quantify such morphological structures nowadays and are freed up to focus on advancing science. In an attempt involving deep learning, automatic recognition of cancerous tissue with millions of discrete parameters, hindering reasonable interpretation of discriminative features is carried out [54]. Algorithms are developed that can recognize the specific predefined angiogenesis features in images and also can measure their abundance with great accuracy [54]. One study developed deep learning-based angiogenesis quantification software and done assessment of the angiogenesis of angiosarcomas in the in vivo CAM model. This method is part of the proprietary commercially available software-as-a-service solution IKOSA platform (KML Vision GmbH, Graz, Austria) [55]. Another used non-redundant dataset of anti-angiogenic and non-anti-angiogenic peptides and predicted the angiogenesis by employing effective machine learning based R package (AntAngioCOOL). This study suggested that AntAngioCOOL can effectively identify anti-angiogenic peptides, with sensitivity of 88%, specificity of 77% and accuracy of 75% [56]. Further, a different study quantify the angiogenesis 2-step machine learning framework which utilized a set of 14 genes and correlated them with good and poor disease-free survival groups (14VF HR = 2.4, 14GT HR = 3.33) [57].
