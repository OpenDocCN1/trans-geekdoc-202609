### 5.2.1.5.3 Pathway

The pathway approach permits locating genes engaged in general signaling networks or biological pathways or in addition to GWAS-based single candidate genes, providing a list of proteins for therapeutic target development. Each pathway contains a variety of interactions between molecules, and specific functions, such as cell division, are triggered by signaling transmission from molecule to molecule. As a result, flaws in any one of the pathway's steps may cause the pathway to malfunction and ultimately lead to disease [31]. The route method is useful for identifying the underlying connections between diseases. More efficient treatments for illnesses like chronic myelogenous leukaemia and multiple sclerosis have already been provided with the use of pathway techniques. Understanding all of the pathways and their functions inside of human cells might make it easier to cure some of the most challenging diseases. Overall, disease target selection and subsequent treatment development are greatly facilitated by pathway/network-based techniques [32, 33].

### 5.2.1.5.4 PheWAS

Because of the integration of EHRs for various illness types from various racial groups with genomic data, precision medicine is perceived from a new perspective. As a result, the phenome-wide association study (PheWAS), a cutting-edge paradigm that integrates data from both GWAS and EHRs from major cohort studies, has emerged [34]. The genotype-phenotype link was broadened by the PheWAS, which also enabled researchers to find new uses for drugs that had previously been given [35]. As an illustration, consider the study that merged the PheWAS and Drug Bank to evaluate possible drug candidates for the treatment of both uncommon and common illnesses. There were 52,966 drug-disease combos discovered in that research, with approximately a third of these pairings being supported by writings, ongoing clinical studies, and pre-existing drug-disease associations [36].

## 5.3 Challenges and Opportunities for Drug Repurposing

Traditional drug development methods are expensive, risky, and expensive endeavours. As a result, drug repositioning has received attention recently and expedites the release of medications for clinical usage [37, 38]. Drug repositioning, however, is a difficult process that takes into account a variety of elements, including technology, business strategies, patients, investments, and market demands [39, 40]. Despite the existence of numerous medical databases, choosing the best strategy to fully utilize vast volumes of medical data remains a difficulty [41]. It is critical to creating fresh strategies for drug repositioning. Opportunities are not without difficulties. A chance finding in the 1920s led to the first instance of drug repositioning [42]. More strategies for accelerating the repositioning of drugs have been put forth after almost a century of development. Drug repositioning has achieved significant success as a result [43, 44]. The performance of drug repositioning in this situation was improved by the introduction of massive ML algorithms.

## 5.4 Conclusion

In this study, we discuss the current status of IoT, AI, ML, cloud computing, big data, and digital twin in drug repositioning. There are many methods and strategies for predicting a drug's accuracy, but because different parameters, algorithms, and programs apply to each software, a drug's accuracy against a target in one software may not be the same in another. By stabilizing the programs and algorithms and reducing the mistakes, these variations can be overcome. With the enlargement of technology and data, AI performance has no horizon and is increasing quickly every day. The successful discovery of potentially repositioned drugs is possible using a variety of techniques. Newly developed computational drug repositioning sector would result from the successful fusion of various approaches and the data that is now accessible. Next-generation computational techniques like disease-disease association, molecular docking, MD simulation, network medicine, integrated biological network, AI, drug–drug interaction, and ML have recently been developed, making it possible to reposition drugs effectively. The pharmaceutical sector has mostly focused on ML- and AI-based techniques, which have significantly aided in target protein and novel chemical search, as well as property prediction. We can speed up the development of new drugs and significantly advance human health by creating machine-learning techniques. The methods created for big data integrate data from a variety of viewpoints, simplify the process of screening drug candidates, and enable the prediction of drug toxicity, considerably accelerating the development of novel treatments and improving their efficacy. With its flexible pricing structure and dynamic resource allocation, cloud computing is quickly becoming a top choice for repositioning pharmaceutical products. Digital twin instances and aggregates enhance reproducibility by lowering bias and promoting learning and the discovery of new knowledge (by ensuring target populations are accurately represented in the corresponding digital twin representations) when transferring from the “small” big data of one individual to the big data of one or more populations.
