## Abstract

Drug repositioning is a technique for discovering new uses for investigational and approved drugs that are beyond the scope of the original medication indication. It enhances the drug’s therapeutic value, which raises the success rate. There are different approaches which offer several advantages over developing a novel drug for a given indication. In this study, we have discussed various strategies for drug repositioning like the use of the internet of things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), cloud computing, big data digital twin, etc. AI and ML allow for the rapid development of drugs, including the repositioning of existing drugs. IoT has penetrated the majority of application domains, the most important being in the field of healthcare. The integration of drug repositioning with the internet of things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), cloud computing, big data and digital twining, further enriches its significance and research potential with novel opportunities and unique challenges. The objective of this review is to demonstrate the value of these computational tools for association prediction and drug repositioning in novel disease indications. A huge volume of biological and biomedical data is being produced right now as a result of the quick development of high-performance technology. This enables the creation of new opportunities for drug repositioning through the application of computational techniques and models based on biological networks.

**Keywords:** Artificial intelligence, machine learning, internet of things, drug repositioning, digital twin, big data, cloud computing

### 5.1 Introduction

Industry 4.0, often known as the fourth industrial revolution, uses cyber-physical technology to increase the system’s intelligence. In order to produce drugs efficiently and at a low cost while maintaining high-quality standards, the utilization autonomous systems and devices essential [1]. Technologies connected to Industry 4.0 used in the medication development process could have a significant positive impact on the biomedical and pharmaceutical industries because a new pharmaceutical product or drug requires a significantly longer and more expensive R&D period than a regular product does.

Drug development is a sequential process that includes drug discovery, preclinical research, clinical testing, approval, and post-market surveillance [2]. The possibility to analyse the libraries of many compounds that already exist to locate a similar structure that fits with a certain target is another benefit of using computers in drug research. The use of computational approaches allows researchers to examine how a ligand—a substance that can be designated as an experimental drug—interacts with a target site (such as a protein or cellular receptor), change a ligand's structure to improve its affinity, clarify action, or lessen toxicity. The most reliable replacement for paper-based recording systems, which can be very reliable and useful in various areas of medicine, is cloud technology. With the incorporation of cloud computing with the new methodology, it is now possible to find molecules that have a higher affinity and effectiveness for biological targets. Traditional algorithms are ineffective at identifying the proper material that can properly bind and activate the receptor or validating drug target sites. However, utilizing a combination of novel modeling and molecular dynamic methodologies with cloud computing, it may be possible to identify a promising molecule or a novel location or target of activation that is otherwise impossible to identify [3]. Unlike physical theories that are based on physical formulae, like quantum chemistry, machine learning (ML) methods create broad mathematical models among empirical data of molecules to predict the physical, biological, and chemical properties of new compounds [4]. Organizations in the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industries can now adjust their network policies with more effectiveness and performance in a variety of areas owing to current digitalization. ### 5.2 Drug Repositioning

Drug repositioning is more efficient than traditional de novo drug development methods in terms of saving time and money has gained significant attention in pharmaceutical research and industry. This is because it offers the possibility for finding new applications for already-approved medications as well as for developing new medications.

Drug repositioning is sometimes referred to as therapeutic switching, drug redirection, medication re-tasking, drug repurposing, and drug reprofiling [5]. Drug repositioning reportedly makes the discovery of drug process much simpler because it not only expedites the process but also skips clinical and preclinical research pertaining to the ADMET features, problems with drug optimization, and increases the success rate of drug development [6]. The process of designing a medicine is time-consuming, expensive, and high-risk because it must go through several iterations. The FDA's strong approval is necessary before the manufactured drug may be given, and it takes close to 10 to 15 years to reach the market. (Figure 5.1) [7]. The majority of the most well-known and effective drug repurposing stories (like those involving minoxidil, sildenafil, valproic acid and aspirin) have originated from unstructured (or “field”) discovery processes that frequently rely on a drug’s pharmacology (such as an off-target adverse effect) to resolve a clinical problem from a different field. These stories have not all arisen from coincidental observations. But in recent years, the drug discovery community has vowed to put organized, methodical, data-driven medication repurposing methodologies into practice, most of which incorporate computational support.
