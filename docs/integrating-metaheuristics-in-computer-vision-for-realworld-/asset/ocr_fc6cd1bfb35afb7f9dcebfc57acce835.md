The major challenge in exoplanet detection is to reliably forecast whether or not exoplanets exist in a star’s system given a dataset of observations of the star, including measurements of the star’s intensity and other properties [4]. Due to the weak signals that these far-off planets emit, finding exoplanets is a difficult process. Other astronomical processes, such as noise from the star or other celestial objects in the field of view, frequently obliterate these signals. The kind of exoplanet, distance from the host star, and orientation of the planet’s orbit can all have a substantial impact on the signals that exoplanets emit.

These problems can be addressed by using machine learning, a type of artificial intelligence which involves teaching algorithms to spot patterns in data, to evaluate the data and find patterns that might point to the existence of an exoplanet.
