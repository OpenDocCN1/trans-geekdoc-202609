# Abstract

Exoplanet prediction using machine learning algorithms has the potential to significantly advance our understanding of these mysterious celestial bodies. In this project, a variety of machine learning algorithms were used, including support vector machines, logistic regression, k-nearest neighbours, random forest, and extreme gradient boosting, to make predictions about the existence of exoplanets and which their type. For this two datasets are used. With one dataset, a model that predicts the existence of an exoplanet was made and with other dataset, a model that predicts the type of an exoplanet was made. The goal was to identify patterns in the data and to make accurate predictions about the existence and characteristics of exoplanets. To evaluate the performance of the different algorithms, a range of metrics were used, including accuracy, precision, and recall. The results showed that these algorithms are highly effective at finding patterns in the data and making accurate predictions about the existence of an exoplanet around a star, with accuracies ranging from 98% to 99%. The second model which predicts the type of exoplanet whether it is a Gas-giant, Terrestrial or other also achieved as satisfying accuracy of 84%. In addition, confusion matrices of various algorithms and a feature importance plot were used to provide a more detailed analysis of the performance of the algorithms and to understand which features or variables were most influential in making predictions. Overall, this exoplanet prediction model has provided a compelling demonstration of the capabilities of machine learning algorithms and the potential for these methods to revolutionize our understanding of the cosmos.
