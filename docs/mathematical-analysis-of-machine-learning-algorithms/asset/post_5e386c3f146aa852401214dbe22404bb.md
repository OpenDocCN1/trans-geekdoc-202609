
Equation (a) used the fact that for each arm $a$, with $a_t = a$, $\hat{n}_{t-1}(a_t)$ takes values from $m = 1$ to $m = \hat{n}_T(a) - 1$, once for each value. In the derivation of the last inequality, we used Jensen’s inequality and the concavity of $\sqrt{z}$, which implies that

$$\sum_{a=1}^{K} \sqrt{\hat{n}_T(a)-1} \le \sqrt{K \sum_{a=1}^{K} (\hat{n}_T(a)-1)} = \sqrt{K(T-K)}.$$

This implies the desired bound.

## Example 16.4

Assume in Theorem 16.3, $\mu(a_*) \approx 0$. In such case, we can use the following multiplicative Chernoff bound in Theorem 13.5 to obtain an upper confidence bound. We know that with probability at least $1 - \delta$,

$$\mu(a) \le \frac{e}{e-1} \hat{\mu}_t(a) + \frac{e \ln(1/\delta)}{(e-1)\hat{n}_t(a)} \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{n}_t(a)=m.$$

This implies that we may use an upper confidence bound

$$\hat{c}_t(a) = \frac{1}{e-1} \hat{\mu}_t(a) + \frac{e \ln\left((2\hat{n}_t(a)+1)^2/\delta\right)}{(e-1)\hat{n}_t(a)}$$

in Algorithm 16.1. In order to obtain a better regret bound than that of Theorem 16.3, we also need to use the multiplicative Chernoff bound for the lower confidence interval, and then repeat the analysis of Theorem 16.3 with such a multiplicative lower confidence interval bound. We leave it as an exercise.

## Example 16.5

Assume for each arm $a$, the reward $r_t(a)$ is a sub-Gaussian random variable, but different arms have different reward distributions:

$$\ln \mathbb{E} \exp(\lambda r_t(a)) \le \lambda \mathbb{E} r_t(a) + \frac{\lambda^2}{2} M(a)^2,$$

where $M(a)$ is known. Then one can obtain a bound similar to Theorem 16.3, with an arm-dependent UCB estimate involving $M(a)$.

One can also obtain a refined version of Lemma 16.2 that relies on the gap between $a_*$ and any other $a$.

## Lemma 16.6

Let $a_* \in \arg \max_a \mu(a)$. Let

$$\delta_1 = \Pr[\exists t > K : \mu(a_*) > \hat{\mu}_{t-1}(a_*) + \hat{c}_{t-1}(a_*)],$$

$$\delta_2 = \Pr[\exists t > K, a \in \{1,\dots,K\}\setminus\{a_*\} : \mu(a) < \hat{\mu}_{t-1}(a) - \hat{c}'_{t-1}(a)].$$

Define $\Delta(a) = \mu(a_*) - \mu(a)$, and

$$T(a) = \max \{m : \Delta(a) \le \hat{c}_{t-1}(a) + \hat{c}'_{t-1}(a), \hat{n}_{t-1}(a) = m, K < t \le T\} \cup \{0\}.$$

Then for Algorithm 16.1, we have with probability at least $1 - \delta_1 - \delta_2$,

$$\mathrm{REG}_T \le \inf_{\Delta_0>0} \left[ \sum_{a=1}^{K} T(a)\Delta(a)\mathbf{1}(\Delta(a) > \Delta_0) + (T-K)\Delta_0 \right] + \sum_{a=1}^{K} \Delta(a).$$

### Proof

The proof of Lemma 16.2 shows that with probability at least \(1-\delta\), for all \(t \ge K+1\),

$$\mu(a_*) \le \mu(a_t) + [\hat{c}'_{t-1}(a_t)+\hat{c}_{t-1}(a_t)]\mathbf{1}(a_t\ne a_*),$$

which implies that

$$\Delta(a_t) \le [\hat{c}'_{t-1}(a_t)+\hat{c}_{t-1}(a_t)]\mathbf{1}(a_t\ne a_*).$$

Using the assumption of the lemma, we obtain for all \(a\ne a_*\) and \(1\le t\le T\), \(\hat{n}_{t-1}(a)\mathbf{1}(a_t=a)\le T(a)\).

It follows that for all \(a\ne a_*\), let \(t\) be the last time such that \(a_t=a\), then \(t\le T\) and \(\hat{n}_T(a)=\hat{n}_{t-1}(a_t)+1\le T(a)+1\). We thus obtain the following regret bound for any \(\Delta_0\ge0\):

$$\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\mu(a_*)-\mu(a_t)|=\sum_{a=1}^{K}\hat{n}_T(a)\Delta(a)$$

$$\le \sum_{a=1}^{K}\Delta(a)+\sum_{a=1}^{K}(\hat{n}_T(a)-1)\Delta_0+\sum_{a=1}^{K}(\hat{n}_T(a)-1)\Delta(a)\mathbf{1}(\Delta(a)>\Delta_0)$$

$$\le \sum_{a=1}^{K}\Delta(a)+(T-K)\Delta_0+\sum_{a=1}^{K}T(a)\Delta(a)\mathbf{1}(\Delta(a)>\Delta_0).$$

The second inequality used \(\sum_{a=1}^{K}\hat{n}_T(a)=T-K\) and \(\hat{n}_T(a)-1\le T(a)\). This implies the bound.

We note that in Lemma 16.6, \(\delta_1\) is still the failure probability for the upper confidence bound, and \(\delta_2\) is the failure probability for the lower confidence bound. The intuition of the quantity \(T(a)\) is that after we have pulled arm \(a\) for more than \(T(a)\) times, the confidence interval for \(a\) becomes smaller than the gap \(\Delta(a)\), which means that we will not choose arm \(a\) in the future. That is, \(T(a)\) is the maximum number of times that one will pull a particular arm \(a\). This means that the regret caused by choosing \(a\) is upper bounded by \(T(a)\Delta(a)\).

Using Lemma 16.6, we can now obtain a gap-dependent bound in Theorem 16.7.

## Theorem 16.7

Assume that the reward \(r_t(a)\in[0,1]\). Let \(a_*\in\arg\max_a\mu(a)\).

With a choice of

$$\hat{c}_t(a)=\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2(\hat{n}_t(a)+1)^2/\delta)}{2\hat{n}_t(a)}}.$$

we have

$$\mathrm{REG}_T\le \inf_{\Delta_0>0}\left[(T-K)\Delta_0+\sum_{a=1}^{K}\frac{2\ln(2KT^2/\delta)}{\Delta(a)}\mathbf{1}(\Delta(a)>\Delta_0)\right]+\sum_{a=1}^{K}\Delta(a).$$

### Proof

From the proof of Theorem 16.3, we know that with the choice of

$\hat{c}'_t(a) = \sqrt{\frac{\ln\left(2K(\hat{n}_t(a)+1)^2/\delta\right)}{2\hat{n}_t(a)}}$

the inequality $\Delta(a) \le \hat{c}_{t-1}(a) + \hat{c}'_{t-1}(a)$ in the definition of $T(a)$ implies that

$\Delta(a) \le \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2KT^2/\delta)}{\hat{n}_{t-1}(a)}}$

This implies that $\hat{n}_{t-1}(a) \le \frac{2\ln(2KT^2/\delta)}{\Delta(a)^2}$.

Therefore $T(a) \le \frac{2\ln(2KT^2/\delta)}{\Delta(a)^2}$. This implies the desired result from Lemma 16.6.

## Example 16.8

We may take $\Delta_0 = 0$ in the gap-dependent regret bound of Theorem 16.7, and obtain $\mathrm{REG}_T \le \sum_{a=1}^K \frac{2\ln(2KT^2/\delta)}{\Delta(a)} + K$.

## Example 16.9

The gap-dependent regret bound of Theorem 16.7 implies the gap-independent regret bound of Theorem 16.3. In fact, if we take $\Delta_0 = \sqrt{\frac{K\ln(KT/\delta)}{T}}$, then we obtain a regret of $\mathrm{REG}_T = O\left(\sqrt{KT\ln(KT/\delta)}\right)$ from Theorem 16.7.

Results we have obtained so far require the construction of UCB knowing the confidence level $\delta$. This might be inconvenient for some applications. In the following, we show that using a similar analysis, it is possible to obtain a regret analysis with a construction of UCB that does not depend on either $T$ or $\delta$. In the analysis, we will first introduce a lemma to replace Lemma 16.6 without the need to consider events that hold uniformly for all $t$. Lemma 16.10 is a variant of Lemma 16.6 that considers each specific time step $t$ separately. It leads to a similar result that holds in expectation.

## Lemma 16.10

Let $a_* \in \arg\max_a \mu(a)$. For $t > K$, define $\delta_1(t) = \Pr[\mu(a_*) > \hat{\mu}_{t-1}(a_*) + \hat{c}_{t-1}(a_*)]$, $\delta_2(t) = \Pr[\exists a \in \{1,\dots,K\}\setminus\{a_*\} : \mu(a) < \hat{\mu}_{t-1}(a) - \hat{c}'_{t-1}(a)]$, and let

$\delta=\sum_{t>K}[\delta_1(t)+\delta_2(t)].$

Define $\Delta(a)=\mu(a_*)-\mu(a)$, $M=\sup_a\Delta(a)$, and

$T(a)=\max\{m:\Delta(a)\le \hat c_{t-1}(a)+\hat c'_{t-1}(a),\hat n_{t-1}(a)=m, K<t\le T\}\cup\{0\}$.

Then for Algorithm 16.1, we have

$\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{REG}_T\le \inf_{\Delta_0>0}\left[\sum_{a=1}^{K}T(a)\Delta(a)\mathbf{1}(\Delta(a)>\Delta_0)+(T-K)\Delta_0+(K+\delta)M\right].$

### Proof

We obtain for any fixed $t>K$ that with probability $1-\delta_1(t)$, $$\mu(a_*)\le \hat\mu_{t-1}(a_t)+\hat c_{t-1}(a_t).$$

Moreover, with probability $1-\delta_2(t)$, $$\hat\mu_{t-1}(a_t)\le \mu(a_t)+\hat c'_{t-1}(a_t).$$

It follows from the same derivation of Lemma 16.2 that with probability $1-\delta_1(t)-\delta_2(t)$, $$\Delta(a_t)=\mu(a_*)-\hat\mu_{t-1}(a_t)\le \hat c_{t-1}(a_t)+\hat c'_{t-1}(a_t).$$

This implies that $\hat n_{t-1}(a_t)\le T(a_t)$. It follows that for all $t>K$,

$\Pr(\hat n_{t-1}(a_t)>T(a_t))\le \delta_1(t)+\delta_2(t).$ \tag{16.4}

Now let

$\delta(a)=\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\,\mathbf{1}(a_t=a,\hat n_{t-1}(a)>T(a)),$

then

$\sum_{a=1}^{K}\delta(a)=\sum_{a=1}^{K}\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\Pr(a_t=a,\hat n_{t-1}(a)>T(a))$  
$\qquad=\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\Pr(\hat n_{t-1}(a_t)>T(a_t))\le\delta.$

The last inequality used (16.4) and the definition of $\delta$.

Now we have for any fixed $a$,

$\mathbb{E}\,\hat n_T(a)=1+\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbf{1}(a_t=a)$

$\qquad=1+\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\,\mathbf{1}(a_t=a,\hat n_{t-1}(a)\le T(a))+\sum_{t=K+1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\,\mathbf{1}(a_t=a,\hat n_{t-1}(a)>T(a))$

$\qquad\le 1+T(a)+\delta(a).$ \tag{16.5}
