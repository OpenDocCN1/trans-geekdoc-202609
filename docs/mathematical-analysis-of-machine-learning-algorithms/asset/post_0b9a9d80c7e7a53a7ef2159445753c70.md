
**Example 7.20** Consider a parametric model, where \(\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d\) is a compact set, and \(w_* \in \arg\min_{w\in\Omega}\phi(w,\mathcal{D})\). Assume that \(\phi(w,z)-\phi(w_*,z)\le c_2\|w-w_*\|_2\), and \(-\ln p_0(\{w\in\Omega:\|w-w_*\|_2\le\epsilon\})\ge c_1+c_2'd\ln(1/\epsilon)\). Then there exists \(c_3'>0\) such that for \(\beta n\ge 1\),

\[
\inf_{\epsilon>0}\left[\epsilon-\frac{\ln p_0(\Omega(\epsilon))}{\beta(n+1)}\right]=\frac{c_2'c_3'd\ln(\beta n)}{\beta n}.
\]

The oracle inequality in Theorem 7.19 becomes

\[
c_2\mathbb{E}_{S_n}\mathbb{E}_{A}\phi(A(S_n),\mathcal{D})\le \phi(w_*,\mathcal{D})+\frac{c_3'd\ln(\beta n)}{\beta n}+\beta e^{\beta M}c_0^2.
\]

**Example 7.21** Consider the least squares problem of Example 3.18:

\[
\phi(w,z)=(f(w,x)-y)^2-(f(w_*,x)-y)^2,\qquad (f(w,x)-y)^2\in[0,1],
\]

and we assume that \(f(w_*,x)=\mathbb{E}[y|x]\) with \(w_*\in\Omega\). Then the variance condition is satisfied with \(c_0=0\) and \(c_1=2\). We can take \(\beta\) so that \(4\beta e^{\beta}<1\). In the parametric model case of Example 7.20, this leads to a fast rate

\[
\mathbb{E}_{S_n}\mathbb{E}_{w\sim\mathcal{E}(S_n)}(f(w,X)-Y)^2\le \mathbb{E}_{(X,Y)}(f(w_*,X)-Y)^2+O\left(\frac{d\ln n}{n}\right).
\]

## 7.5 Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics

Similar to SGD, which solves ERM, the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) algorithm can be used to sample from the Gibbs distribution. The resulting algorithm, described in Algorithm 7.2, is a slight modification of Algorithm 7.1 with the addition of Gaussian noise \(\epsilon_t\) at each step.

```text
Algorithm 7.2 Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics Algorithm

Input: \(S_n\), \(\phi(w,z)\), \(p_0\), learning rates \(\{\eta_t\}\)
Output: \(w_T\)

1. Draw \(w_0\sim p_0\)
2. for \(t=1,2,\ldots,T\) do
3.   Randomly pick \(Z\sim S_n\) uniformly at random
4.   Randomly generate \(\epsilon_t\sim N(0,I)\)
5.   Let \(\widetilde{w}_t=w_{t-1}-\eta_t\nabla\phi(w_{t-1},Z)+\sqrt{2\eta_t/\beta}\,\epsilon_t\)
6.   Let \(w_t=\operatorname{proj}_{\Omega}(\widetilde{w}_t)\), where \(\operatorname{proj}_{\Omega}(v)=\arg\min_{u\in\Omega}\|u-v\|_2^2\)

Return: \(w_T\)
```

Since the addition of Gaussian noise is independent of the data, the stability analysis of SGD still holds. For strongly convex functions, we can thus obtain the following result. The proof is left as an exercise.

**Theorem 7.22** *Assume that* \(\bar{\phi}(w,z)=\phi(w,z)+h(w)\) *is* \(\lambda\)-*strongly convex and* \(L\)-*smooth in* \(w\) *on* \(\mathbb{R}^d\). *Moreover, assume* \(\phi(w,z)\) *is* \(G\)-*Lipschitz on* \(\Omega\). *Define* \(b_0=0\), *and for* \(t\ge 1\),

\[
b_t=(1-\eta_t\lambda)b_{t-1}+\frac{2\eta_t}{n}G^2,
\]

where \(\eta_t\in[0,1/L]\). Then after \(T\) steps, Algorithm 7.2 is \(\varepsilon=b_T\) uniformly stable. The result also holds for any random convex combinations of \(\{w_t:t\le T\}\) with combination coefficients from a known distribution.

It is also possible to derive stability result with nonconvex functions for SGLD. However, it is simpler to analyze the nonstochastic version (often referred to as unadjusted Langevin algorithm, or ULA), where line 5 of Algorithm 7.2 is replaced by the full gradient

\[
\tilde w_t = w_{t-1} - \eta_t \nabla \tilde\phi(w_{t-1}, S_n) + \sqrt{2\eta_t/\beta}\,\epsilon_t. \tag{7.13}
\]

**Theorem 7.23** *Assume that for all \(z,z'\), \(\tilde\phi(w,z)-\tilde\phi(w,z')\) is a \(G\)-Lipschitz function of \(w\) on \(\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d\) (but \(\tilde\phi\) is not necessarily convex):*

\[
\|\nabla\tilde\phi(w,z)-\nabla\tilde\phi(w,z')\|_2\le G.
\]

*Assume also that* \(\sup_{w,w'\in\Omega}[\phi(w,z)-\phi(w',z)]\le M\) *for all \(z\). Then after \(T\) steps, ULA (with line 5 of Algorithm 7.2 replaced by (7.13)) is \(\varepsilon_T\) uniformly stable with*

\[
\varepsilon_T=\frac{MG}{4n}\sqrt{2\beta\sum_{t=1}^T\eta_t}.
\]

**Proof** Consider \(S_n\sim\mathcal{D}^n\) and \(S'_n=(S_n\setminus\{Z_n\})\cup\{Z'_n\}\), where \(Z'_n\sim\mathcal{D}\) is a sample independent of \(S_n\). Let \(w_t\) and \(w'_t\) be the outputs of ULA with \(S_n\) and \(S'_n\) respectively. Moreover, let \(p_t\) be the distribution of \(w_t\); let \(p_{t,t-1}(w_t,w_{t-1})\) be the joint distribution of \((w_t,w_{t-1})\); let \(\tilde p_{t,t-1}(\tilde w_t,w_{t-1})\) be the joint distribution of \((\tilde w_t,w_{t-1})\). Similarly, we define \(p'_t\), \(p'_{t,t-1}\), and \(\tilde p'_{t,t-1}\).

We now let \(\bar w_t(w)=w-\eta_t\nabla\tilde\phi(w,S_n)\) and \(\bar w'_t(w)=w-\eta_t\nabla\tilde\phi(w,S'_n)\). Then

\[
\tilde p_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w_{t-1}=w)=\mathcal{N}\!\left(\bar w_t(w),\frac{2\eta_t}{\beta}I\right),\qquad
\tilde p'_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w'_{t-1}=w)=\mathcal{N}\!\left(\bar w'_t(w),\frac{2\eta_t}{\beta}I\right).
\]

We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\tilde p_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w_{t-1}=w)\,\big\|\, \tilde p'_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w'_{t-1}=w)\bigr)
&=\mathrm{KL}\!\left(\mathcal{N}\!\left(\bar w_t(w),\frac{2\eta_t}{\beta}I\right)\,\Big\|\, \mathcal{N}\!\left(\bar w'_t(w),\frac{2\eta_t}{\beta}I\right)\right)\\
&=\frac{\beta\|\bar w_t(w)-\bar w'_t(w)\|_2^2}{4\eta_t}\\
&\le\frac{\beta\eta_t G^2}{4n^2}. \tag{7.14}
\end{aligned}
\]

The second equality used the formula for the KL-divergence of two Gaussian distributions. The last inequality used the Lipschitz condition of \(\tilde\phi\). We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{KL}(p_t\|p'_t)-\mathrm{KL}(p_{t-1}\|p'_{t-1})
&=\mathrm{KL}(p_{t,t-1}\|p'_{t,t-1})\\
&\quad-\mathbb{E}_{w\sim p_t}\mathrm{KL}(p_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w_t=w)\|p'_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w'_t=w))\\
&\quad-\mathrm{KL}(p_{t-1}\|p'_{t-1})\\
&\le\mathrm{KL}(p_{t,t-1}\|p'_{t,t-1})-\mathrm{KL}(p_{t-1}\|p'_{t-1})\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim p_{t-1}}\mathrm{KL}(p_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w_{t-1}=w)\|p'_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w'_{t-1}=w))\\
&\le\mathbb{E}_{w\sim p_{t-1}}\mathrm{KL}(\tilde p_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w_{t-1}=w)\|\tilde p'_{t,t-1}(\cdot\mid w'_{t-1}=w))\\
&\le\frac{\beta\eta_t G^2}{4n^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first inequality used the fact that KL-divergence is always nonnegative. The second inequality used the data processing inequality for KL-divergence (see Theorem B.4). The last inequality used (7.14).

By summing over \(t=1\) to \(t=T\), and noting that \(p_0=p'_0\), we obtain

\[
\mathrm{KL}(p_T\|p'_T)\le\frac{\beta G^2\sum_{t=1}^T\eta_t}{4n^2}. \tag{7.15}
\]

Therefore for any \(z\), we have

\[
\mathbb{E}_A\phi(A(S'_n),z)-\mathbb{E}_A\phi(A(S_n),z)
\le M\|p_T-p'_T\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le M\sqrt{0.5\,\mathrm{KL}(p_T\|p'_T)}
\le\frac{MG}{4n}\sqrt{2\beta\sum_{t=1}^T\eta_t}.
\]

The first inequality used the definition of TV-norm in Proposition B.7. The second inequality used Pinsker's inequality of Theorem B.9. The last inequality used (7.15). This implies the desired result.

The stability result in Theorem 7.23 can be applied to nonconvex functions. Moreover, the final bound depends on \(\beta\). When \(\beta\to0\), we obtain a more and more stable algorithm. This is consistent with the analysis of the Gibbs algorithm. In comparison, the result of Theorem 7.22, which only applies to convex functions, does not depend on \(\beta\).

## 7.6 Concentration of Uniformly Stable Leave-One-Out Estimate

This section proves a concentration inequality for an appropriately defined leave-one-out estimate of a uniformly stable algorithm, which is analogous to McDiarmid's inequality and Talagrand's inequality for empirical processes (see Chapter 6). Theorem 7.3 shows that this result can be used to obtain high-probability generalization bounds for uniformly stable algorithms.

**Theorem 7.24** Assume that \(g(S_n;z)\) is zero-mean with respect to \(z\) for all \(S_n\): \(\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mathcal{D}}g(S_n;Z)=0\).

Assume also that \(g(S_n;z)\) is an \(\varepsilon\) uniformly stable function. That is, for all \(z\in\mathcal{Z}\), and \(S'_n\) that differs from \(S_n\) by one element, we have \(|g(S_n;z)-g(S'_n;z)|\le\varepsilon\).

Assume that \(S_{n+1}\sim\mathcal{D}^{n+1}\) contains \(n+1\) iid samples from \(\mathcal{D}\). Let

\[
\bar{g}(S_{n+1})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[g(S_{n+1}^{(i)};Z_i)-\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}^{(i)}}g(S_{n+1}^{(i)};Z_i)\right],
\]

where \(S_{n+1}^{(i)}=\{Z_1,\ldots,Z_{i-1},Z_{n+1},Z_{i+1},\ldots,Z_n\}\). Then for all \(0\le\lambda\le0.4/\varepsilon\),

\[
\ln\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{L}\bar{g}(S_{n+1})\right)\le1.5\lambda^2\varepsilon^2,
\]

where \(L=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\). This implies that with probability at least \(1-\delta\),

\[
\bar{g}(S_{n+1})\le L\varepsilon+2.5L\varepsilon\ln(1/\delta).
\]

The proof requires the following result from Boucheron et al. (2003).

**Proposition 7.25** *Consider a functional \(\tilde{g}(S)\), where \(S=[Z_1,\ldots,Z_m]\sim\mathcal{D}^m\) contains \(m\) iid samples from \(\mathcal{D}\). Let \(S'=(Z'_1,\ldots,Z'_m)\sim\mathcal{D}^m\) be independent of \(S\), and set \(S^{(i)}=[Z_1,\ldots,Z_{i-1},Z'_i,Z_{i+1},\ldots,Z_m]\). Define*

\[
V_+(S)=\mathbb{E}_{S'}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\tilde{g}(S)-\tilde{g}(S^{(i)})\right)\mathbf{1}\{\tilde{g}(S)>\tilde{g}(S^{(i)})\}\;\middle|\;S\right].
\]

*Assume that there exist positive constants \(a\) and \(b\) such that*

\[
V_+(S)\le a\tilde{g}(S)+b,
\]

*then for \(\lambda\in(0,1/a)\),*

\[
\ln\mathbb{E}_S\exp(\lambda\tilde{g}(S))\le\lambda\mathbb{E}_S\tilde{g}(S)+\frac{\lambda^2}{1-a\lambda}\left(a\mathbb{E}_S\tilde{g}(S)+b\right).
\]

Note that for the sake of simplicity, in the following proof our notation assumes that \(g(S_n,z)\) is invariant to the order of elements in \(S_n\). The analysis itself holds without this assumption, but directly working with the general version requires a more complex notation, which creates unnecessary difficulty for understanding the main argument. We will thus write the proof with this assumption so that the key idea of the proof is easier to follow.

Consider a fixed \(S_{n+1}=\{Z_1,\ldots,Z_{n+1}\}\). We define for \(i\le m\le n\),

\[
S_m^{(i)}=\{Z_1,\ldots,Z_{i-1},Z_{n+1},Z_{i+1},\ldots,Z_m\},
\]

and with this notation, we have \(S_{n+1}^{(i)}=S_n^{(i)}\).

Now for all \(1\le m'\le m\le n\), we define

\[
\bar{g}_{m',m}(S_{n+1})=\frac{1}{m'}\sum_{i=1}^{m'}\left[g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)-\mathbb{E}_{S_m^{(i)}}g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)\right].
\]

It follows that \(\bar{g}(S_{n+1})=\bar{g}_{n,n}(S_{n+1})\).

Given \(m'<m\le n\), we denote by \(\tilde{S}_{m'}\) a uniformly selected subset of \(S_m\) of size \(m'\). It is easy to see by symmetry that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar{g}_{m,m}(S_{n+1})
&=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)-\mathbb{E}_{S_m^{(i)}}g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{S}_{m'}}\frac{1}{m'}\sum_{Z_i\in\tilde{S}_{m'}}\left[g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)-\mathbb{E}_{S_m^{(i)}}g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore Jensen's inequality implies that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\ln\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\exp\left(\lambda'\bar{g}_{m,m}(S_{n+1})\right)
&\le\ln\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{S}_{m'}}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda'}{m'}\sum_{Z_i\in\tilde{S}_{m'}}\left[g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)-\mathbb{E}_{S_m^{(i)}}g(S_{n+1}^{(i)},Z_i)\right]\right)\\
&=\ln\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\exp\left(\lambda'\bar{g}_{m',m}(S_{n+1})\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
