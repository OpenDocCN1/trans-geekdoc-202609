Moreover, consider any function

$$\sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j\psi(\theta_j, x): \|w\|_1 \le A,\ w_j \ge 0,\ \theta_j \in \Theta$$

and $\sigma_i \in \{\pm1\}$, we know that under the conditions of the theorem,

$$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j\psi(\theta_j, X_i) &= \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i\psi(\theta_j, X_i) \\
&\le \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j \sup_{j'} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i\psi(\theta_{j'}, X_i) \\
&= \|w\|_1 \sup_{j} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i\psi(\theta_j, X_i) \\
&\le \|w\|_1 \sup_{\psi\in\Psi} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i\psi(X_i) \\
&\le A \sup_{\psi\in\Psi} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i\psi(X_i).
\end{aligned}$$

The first inequality used $w_j \ge 0$. The last inequality used the fact that $\|w\|_1 \le A$ and $\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i\psi(X_i) \ge 0$. This implies that

$$R(\mathcal{F}_{A,L_1}^{+}(\Psi),\mathcal{S}_n) \le A \cdot R(\Psi,\mathcal{S}_n),$$

and thus we obtain the second desired equality of the theorem. The proof of the first equality of the theorem is similar. The third equality of the theorem holds because the condition implies that

$$R(\mathcal{F}_{A,L_1}(\Psi),\mathcal{S}_n) = R(\mathcal{F}_{A,L_1}^{+}(\Psi),\mathcal{S}_n).$$

This proves the desired result.

The following example shows that for a sparse target function, $L_1$ regularization is preferred over $L_2$ regularization due to smaller Rademacher complexity.

# **Example 10.9**  Assume that $|\Psi| = N$, then $|\Psi \cup -\Psi| \le 2N$. From Theorem 6.23, we have

$$R(\Psi \cup -\Psi,\mathcal{S}_n) \le \|\psi\|_{L_2(\mathcal{S}_n)} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2N)}{n}}.$$

Theorem 10.8 implies that

$$R(\mathcal{F}_{A,L_1}(\Psi),\mathcal{S}_n) \le A \sup_{\psi\in\Psi} \|\psi\|_{L_2(\mathcal{S}_n)} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2N)}{n}}.$$

If $|\psi(x)| \le B$ for all $\psi \in \Psi$, then

$$R(\mathcal{F}_{A,L_1}(\Psi),\mathcal{S}_n) \le AB \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2N)}{n}}.$$
