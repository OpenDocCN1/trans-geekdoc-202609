
The result is superior to what can be obtained from Theorem 7.8 when $\sigma^2$ is small.

## 7.3 Stochastic Gradient Descent

Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) has been widely used in practical machine learning applications. Since it approximately implements the ERM method, one may use empirical process and uniform convergence to study its generalization behavior. However, one challenge is the lack of covering number bounds for general convex objective functions, although covering number results for special cases such as linear function classes exist. Another consideration is that in many applications, one may need to run SGD for finite iterations without achieving convergence to the minimum solution of ERM. In such a case, it is often much easier to obtain generalization analysis for SGD using the stability analysis, as demonstrated in Hardt et al. (2016).

### Algorithm 7.1 Stochastic Gradient Descent Algorithm

**Input:** $S_n$, $\phi(w,z)$, $w_0$, learning rates $\{\eta_t\}$

**Output:** $w_T$

1. for $t = 1,2,\ldots,T$ do
2. Randomly pick $Z \sim S_n$
3. Let $w_t = \operatorname{proj}_{\Omega}(w_{t-1} - \eta_t \nabla \bar{\phi}(w_{t-1}, Z))$
4. where $\operatorname{proj}_{\Omega}(v) = \arg\min_{u \in \Omega} \|u-v\|_2^2$

**Return:** $w_T$

A key lemma in Hardt et al. (2016) to analyze SGD for smooth convex function is the contraction property of SGD, as follows.

### Lemma 7.12 (SGD Contraction)
Assume $\bar{\phi}(w)$ is an $L$-smooth and $\lambda$-strongly convex function of $w$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Given any $w,w' \in \mathbb{R}^d$, we have for all $\eta \in [0,1/L]$,

$$
\left\|\operatorname{proj}_{\Omega}(w-\eta\nabla\bar{\phi}(w))-\operatorname{proj}_{\Omega}(w'-\eta\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'))\right\|_2 \le (1-\lambda\eta)\|w-w'\|_2.
$$

*Proof*  Let

$$
\Delta_1=\bar{\phi}(w)-\bar{\phi}(w')-\nabla\bar{\phi}(w')^\top(w-w'),
$$
$$
\Delta_2=\bar{\phi}(w')-\bar{\phi}(w)-\nabla\bar{\phi}(w)^\top(w'-w).
$$

We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|(w-\eta\nabla\bar{\phi}(w))-(w'-\eta\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'))\|_2^2
&=\|w-w'\|_2^2-2\eta(\nabla\bar{\phi}(w)-\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'))^\top(w-w')+\eta^2\|\nabla\bar{\phi}(w)-\nabla\bar{\phi}(w')\|_2^2\\
&=\|w-w'\|_2^2-2\eta(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)+\eta^2\|\nabla\bar{\phi}(w)-\nabla\bar{\phi}(w')\|_2^2\\
&\le \|w-w'\|_2^2-2\eta(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)+\eta^2L(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)\\
&\le \|w-w'\|_2^2-\eta(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)\le (1-\eta\lambda)\|w-w'\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the derivation, the first inequality used Proposition A.7. The second inequality used $\eta L \leq 1$. The third inequality used the strong convexity. We can now obtain the desired result by noticing that $\|\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(u)-\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(v)\|_2 \leq \|u-v\|_2$. $\square$

We have the following uniform stability result for the SGD procedure. The proof is similar to that of Theorem 7.14, and we leave it as an exercise.

### Theorem 7.13
*Assume that $\bar\phi(w,z)=\phi(w,z)+h(w)$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth in $w$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Moreover, assume $\phi(w,z)$ is $G$ Lipschitz on $\Omega$. Define $b_0=0$, and for $t \geq 1$,*

$$b_t = (1 - \eta_t \lambda) b_{t-1} + \frac{2 \eta_t}{n} G^2,$$

*where $\eta_t \in [0,1/L]$. Then after $T$ steps, Algorithm 7.1 is $\epsilon = b_T$ uniformly stable with respect to $\phi(w,z)$. The result also holds for an arbitrary convex combination of the form $\sum_{t=0}^T \alpha_t w_t$ as the output of Algorithm 7.1, as long as the convex coefficient $\alpha_t$ (where $\alpha_t \geq 0$ and $\sum_{t=0}^T \alpha_t = 1$) are drawn from a known distribution.*

We also have the following more refined result for SGD using the leave-one-out stability analysis.

### Theorem 7.14
*Assume that $\bar\phi(w,z)=\phi(w,z)+h(w)$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth in $w$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Moreover, assume $\phi(w,z)$ is $G(z)$ Lipschitz on $\Omega$. Define $b_0=0$, and for $t \geq 1$,*

$$b_t = (1 - \eta_t \lambda) b_{t-1} + \frac{2 \eta_t}{n+1} \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}} G(Z)^2,$$

*where $\eta_t \in [0,1/L]$. We have the following result for Algorithm 7.1,*

$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}_n} \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}} \phi(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_n), \mathcal{D}) \leq \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}_{n+1}} \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}} \phi(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}_{n+1}), \mathcal{S}_{n+1}) + b_T,$$

*where we use $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}$ to denote the randomization in SGD. The result also holds for an arbitrary convex combination of the form $\sum_{t=0}^T \alpha_t w_t$ as the output of Algorithm 7.1, as long as the convex coefficient $\alpha_t$ (where $\alpha_t \geq 0$ and $\sum_{t=0}^T \alpha_t = 1$) is drawn from a known distribution.*

*Proof* Let $w_t$ be the intermediate steps of SGD on $\mathcal{S}_n$, and $w_t'$ be the intermediate steps of SGD on $\mathcal{S}_{n+1} = \mathcal{S}_n \cup \{Z_{n+1}\}$. We consider a coupling of $w_t$ and $w_t'$, with the same randomization for $w_t$ and $w_t'$, except when we choose $Z = Z_{n+1}$ for update of $w_t'$, we choose $Z = Z_i$ for updating of $w_t$ with $i$ drawn uniformly from $[n]$. It follows from Lemma 7.12 that with this coupling, at each time $t$, with probability $n/(n+1)$, we choose the same $Z_i$ to update both $w_t$ and $w_t'$:

$$\|w_t - w_t'\|_2 \leq (1 - \lambda \eta_t) \|w_{t-1} - w_{t-1}'\|_2.$$

With probability $1/(n+1)$, we have

$$\begin{aligned}
\|w_t-w'_t\|_2
&\le \|[w_{t-1}-\eta_t\nabla\bar{\phi}(w_{t-1},Z_i)]-[w'_{t-1}-\eta_t\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'_{t-1},Z_i)]\|_2\\
&\quad+\eta_t\|\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'_{t-1},Z_i)-\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'_{t-1},Z_{n+1})\|_2\\
&\le (1-\lambda\eta_t)\|w_{t-1}-w'_{t-1}\|_2+\eta_t(\|\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'_{t-1},Z_{n+1})\|_2+\|\nabla\bar{\phi}(w'_{t-1},Z_i)\|_2),
\end{aligned}$$

where $i$ is uniformly from $[n]$. Note that the second inequality used Lemma 7.12 again. Therefore,

$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\|w_t-w'_t\|_2\le (1-\eta_t\lambda)\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\|w_{t-1}-w'_{t-1}\|_2+\frac{\eta_t}{n(n+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(G(Z_i)+G(Z_{n+1})).$$

We now define

$$s_t=\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\|w_t-w'_t\|_2G(Z_{n+1}),$$

then we have

$$\begin{aligned}
s_t&\le (1-\eta_t\lambda)s_{t-1}+\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\frac{\eta_t}{n(n+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(G(Z_i)+G(Z_{n+1}))G(Z_{n+1})\\
&=(1-\eta_t\lambda)s_{t-1}+\frac{\eta_t}{n+1}\left[\mathbb{E}_{D}G(Z)^2+(\mathbb{E}_{D}G(Z))^2\right]\\
&\le (1-\eta_t\lambda)s_{t-1}+\frac{2\eta_t}{n+1}\mathbb{E}_{D}G(Z)^2.
\end{aligned}$$

It follows from the definition of $b_t$ that $s_t\le b_t$. Therefore,

$$\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\|w_t-w'_t\|_2G(Z_{n+1})\le b_t\le b_T. \tag{7.8}$$

Assume that $\mathcal A(S_n)$ returns an arbitrary convex combination $\sum_{t=0}^{T}\alpha_t w_t$, and $\mathcal A(S_{n+1})$ returns $\sum_{t=0}^{T}\alpha'_t w'_t$ with the same random coefficients $\alpha_t$ from the same known distribution. Let $e(Z_{n+1},S_{n+1})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\phi(\mathcal A(S_n),Z_{n+1})-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\phi(\mathcal A(S_{n+1}),Z_{n+1})$, then from the Lipschitz condition of $\phi(w,Z_{n+1})$ and (7.8), we obtain

$$\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}e(Z_{n+1},S_{n+1})\le \mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\|\mathcal A(S_n)-\mathcal A(S_{n+1})\|_2G(Z_{n+1})\le b_T.$$

We obtain from Theorem 7.7 that

$$\mathbb{E}_{S_n}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\phi(\mathcal A(S_n),D)\le \mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\phi(\mathcal A(S_{n+1}),S_{n+1})+b_T.$$

This proves the desired result.

### Example 7.15
We can obtain an oracle inequality from Theorem 7.14 by assuming further that after $T$ SGD steps, Algorithm 7.1 approximately solves the empirical risk minimization problem as

$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\phi(\mathcal A(S_{n+1}),S_{n+1})\le \inf_{w\in\Omega}\bar{\phi}(w,S_{n+1})+\epsilon_T(S_{n+1})$$

for some $\epsilon_T(\cdot)\ge 0$. Here $\mathcal A$ may return a convex combination of $w_t$ for $t\le T$ (see Section 14.4 for examples of such convergence results). Using such a result, we obtain from Theorem 7.14 the following oracle inequality:

$$\mathbb{E}_{S_n}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal A}\phi(\mathcal A(S_n),D)\le \inf_{w\in\Omega}\bar{\phi}(w,D)+\mathbb{E}_{S_{n+1}}\epsilon_T(S_{n+1})+b_T.$$
