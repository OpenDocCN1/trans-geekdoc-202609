
$r \in [0,1]$ from a distribution $q_a$ that depends on the arm $a$. Our goal is to find the best arm $\theta \in \Theta = A$ with the largest expected reward $\mathbb{E}_{r\sim q_a}[r]$, and the loss $Q(\theta,q) = \sup_a \mathbb{E}_{r\sim q_a}[r] - \mathbb{E}_{r\sim q_\theta}[r]$. In this case, a policy $\pi_t$ is a probability distribution over $A$. The learning algorithm defines a probability distribution $\hat{q}(S_{t-1})$ over $A$ at each time, and draws $a_t \sim \hat{q}(S_{t-1})$. The observation $Z_t$ is the reward $r_t$ which is drawn from $q_{a_t}$.

In this example, the learned policy affects the observation, but it does not need to interact with the environment. In contextual bandits and reinforcement learning, the policy interacts with the environment.

## Example 13.19 (Contextual Bandits)

In contextual bandits, we consider a context space $X$ and action space $A$. Given a context $x \in X$, we can take an action $a \in A$, and observe a reward $r \sim q_{x,a}$. A policy $\pi$ is a map $X \to \Delta(A)$, where $\Delta(A)$ denotes the set of probability distributions over $A$ (with an appropriately defined sigma algebra). The policy $\pi_t$ interacts with the environment to generate the next observation as the environment generates $x_t$, the player takes an action $a_t \sim \pi_t(x_t)$, and then the environment generates the reward $r_t \sim q_{x_t,a_t}$.

For sequential estimation problems, we may define the minimax risk similar to Definition 12.9 as follows.

## Definition 13.20

Consider an environment distribution family $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}$ and a learning algorithm distribution family $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}$. Then the worst-case expected risk of a learning algorithm $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}$ with respect to $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}$ is given by

$$
r_n(\hat{q}, \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}, Q) = \sup_{q \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}} \mathbb{E}_{\theta, S_n \sim p(\cdot|\hat{q},q)} Q(\theta,q),
$$

where $p(\cdot|\hat{q},q)$ is defined in (13.8). Moreover, the minimax risk is defined as

$$
r_n(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}, \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}, Q) = \inf_{\hat{q} \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}} r_n(\hat{q}, \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}, Q).
$$

We note that in the sequential decision setting, the following result for KL-divergence is a straightforward extension of Theorem B.6 by using the chain-rule of KL-divergence.

## Lemma 13.21

Let $\mathcal{P}' \subset \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}$ be a finite subset. For any $q_0 \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}$ and $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}$, assume that for any step $t$ and history $S_{t-1}$,

$$
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|} \sum_{q' \in \mathcal{P}'} \mathrm{KL}(q_0(\cdot|\hat{q}(S_{t-1}), S_{t-1}) \| q'(\cdot|\hat{q}(S_{t-1}), S_{t-1})) \le \beta_t^2.
$$

Then for any learning algorithm $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}$, and sequence of size $n$, we have

$$
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|} \sum_{q' \in \mathcal{P}'} \mathrm{KL}(p(\cdot|\hat{q}, q_0) \| p(\cdot|\hat{q}, q')) \le \sum_{t=1}^{n} \beta_t^2.
$$

### Proof

We have

$$
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|}\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{P}'} \mathrm{KL}(p(\cdot|\hat{q},q_0)\|p(\cdot|\hat{q},q'))
= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|}\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{P}'} \mathbb{E}_{S_n\sim p(\cdot|\hat{q},q_0)} \ln\left(\frac{p(S_n|\hat{q},q_0)}{p(S_n|\hat{q},q')}\right)
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|}\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{P}'} \mathbb{E}_{S_n\sim p(\cdot|\hat{q},q_0)}\left[\ln\left(\frac{p(S_{n-1}|\hat{q},q_0)}{p(S_{n-1}|\hat{q},q')}\right)+\ln\frac{q_0(Z_n|\hat{q}(S_{n-1}),S_{n-1})}{q'(Z_n|\hat{q}(S_{n-1}),S_{n-1})}\right]
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|}\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{P}'} \mathbb{E}_{S_n\sim p(\cdot|\hat{q},q_0)}\left[\ln\left(\frac{p(S_{n-1}|\hat{q},q_0)}{p(S_{n-1}|\hat{q},q')}\right)+\mathrm{KL}\left(q_0(\cdot|\hat{q},S_{n-1})\|q'(\cdot|\hat{q},S_{n-1})\right)\right]
$$

$$
\le \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}'|}\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{P}'} \mathbb{E}_{S_{n-1}\sim p(\cdot|\hat{q},q_0)} \ln\left(\frac{p(S_{n-1}|\hat{q},q_0)}{p(S_{n-1}|\hat{q},q')}\right)+\beta_n^2
$$

$$
\le \cdots \le \sum_{t=1}^{n}\beta_t^2.
$$

This implies the bound.

By using Lemma 13.21 to replace Theorem B.6 with $\alpha = 1$, we obtain the following straightforward generalization of Theorem 12.15 from Fano’s inequality.

## Theorem 13.22

Consider a finite distribution family $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}} = \{q^1,\ldots,q^N\}$. Assume for some $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}$ that, for all time steps $t$ and histories $S_{t-1}$,

$$
\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}} \mathrm{KL}\big(q(\cdot|\hat{q}(S_{t-1}),S_{t-1})\,\|\,q'(\cdot|\hat{q}(S_{t-1}),S_{t-1})\big) \le \beta_t^2.
$$

Let $Q$ be a loss function on $\Theta \times \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}$, and

$$
m = \sup_{\theta\in\Theta} \big|\{k : Q(\theta,q^k) < \varepsilon\}\big|.
$$

If $m \le N/2$ and

$$
\ln(N/m) \ge \ln 4 + 2\sum_{t=1}^{n}\beta_t^2,
$$

then

$$
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \Pr_{\theta,S_n\sim p(\cdot|\hat{q},q^j)}\big(Q(\theta,q^j) < \varepsilon\big) \le 0.5,
$$

where the probability also includes possible randomization in $\mathcal{A}$. If $Q(\cdot,\cdot)$ is non-negative, then this implies that $r_n(\hat{q},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}},Q) \ge 0.5\varepsilon$.

We can also obtain the following result from Fano’s inequality in Theorem 13.22, which is stated in a style that is similar to Theorem 12.28, but with Hellinger distance replaced by KL-divergence.

## Corollary 13.23

Let $d \ge 32$ be an integer and let $\mathcal{P}_d = \{q^\tau : \tau \in \{-1,1\}^d\}$ contain $2^d$ probability measures. Suppose that the loss function $Q$ can be decomposed as

$$
Q(\theta,q)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}Q_j(\theta,q),
$$

where $Q_j \ge 0$ are all nonnegative. For any $j$ and $\tau$, let $\tau^{-[j]}$ be the index that differs with $\tau$ only by one coordinate $j$. Assume there exists $\epsilon \ge 0$ and $\beta_t \ge 0$ such that

$$
\forall\tau:\quad [Q_j(\theta,q^{\tau})+Q_j(\theta,q^{\tau^{-[j]}})]\ge\epsilon.
$$

Assume for some $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}$,

$$
\sup_{q,q'\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\sup_{S_{t-1}}\mathrm{KL}\big(q(\cdot|\hat{q}(S_{t-1}),S_{t-1})\,\|\,q'(\cdot|\hat{q}(S_{t-1}),S_{t-1})\big)\le\beta_t^2.
$$

If $\sum_{t=1}^n \beta_t^2 \le (d/32 - 1)$, then $r_n(\hat{q},\mathcal{P}_d,Q) \ge d\epsilon/16$.

### Proof

Consider drawing $\tau_i \in \{\pm1\}^d$ from the uniform distribution $i=1,\dots,N$, where $N = \lfloor\exp(d/16)\rfloor$. For any fixed $1 \le i < j \le N$, using the Chernoff bound with $|\tau_i - \tau_j| \in [0,2]^d$ and $\mathbb{E}\|\tau_i - \tau_j\|_1 = d$, we obtain

$$
\Pr\big(\|\tau_i - \tau_j\|_1 \le d/2\big) \le \exp(-d/8).
$$

Therefore

$$
\Pr\big(\exists i \ne j : \|\tau_i - \tau_j\|_1 \le d/2\big) \le N(N-1)\exp(-d/8) < 1.
$$

It follows there exists a subset $T$ of $\{\pm1\}^d$ with $N$ members so that for $\tau_i \ne \tau_j \in T$,

$$
\frac12\|\tau_i - \tau_j\|_1 \ge d/4.
$$

This implies that

$$
Q(\theta,q^{\tau_i})+Q(\theta,q^{\tau_j}) \ge d\epsilon/4.
$$

It follows that

$$
m = \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\big|\{k: Q(\theta,q^{\tau_k}) < d\epsilon/8\}\big| = 1.
$$

Since the condition of the corollary implies that

$$
\ln(N) \ge \ln 4 + 2\sum_{t=1}^n \beta_t^2,
$$

the desired result is a direct consequence of Theorem 13.22.

While Theorem 13.22 and Corollary 13.23 can be applied to online learning problems, it is not convenient to use for bandit problems. For bandit problems, we will employ the following result which directly follows from Assouad's lemma.

$$
Q(\theta,q)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}Q_j(\theta,q).
$$

where $Q_j\ge 0$ are all nonnegative. For each $j$, $\tau\sim_j\tau'$ if $\tau=\tau'$ or if $\tau$ and $\tau'$ differ by only one component $j$. Assume that there exists $\epsilon \ge 0$ and $\beta_{j,t} \ge 0$ such that

$$
\forall\tau'\sim_j\tau,\ \tau'\ne\tau:\quad [Q_j(\theta,q^{\tau})+Q_j(\theta,q^{\tau'})]\ge\epsilon,
$$

and there exists $q_j^{\tau}$ such that all $\tau'\sim_j\tau$ map to the same value: $q_j^{\tau'}=q_j^{\tau}$. Given any learning algorithm $\hat{q}$, if for all $\tau \in \{1,\ldots,m\}^d$, $j \in [d]$, time step $t$, and $S_{t-1}$,

$$
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\tau'\sim_j\tau}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(q_j^{\tau}(\cdot\mid\hat{q}(S_{t-1}),S_{t-1})\,\|\,q^{\tau'}(\cdot\mid\hat{q}(S_{t-1}),S_{t-1})\bigr)\le\beta_{j,t}^{2},

$$

$$
\le\frac{1}{dm^{d}}\sum_{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{\substack{\tau'\sim_j\tau,\ \tau'\ne\tau}}\left[\|p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q_j^{\tau})-p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q^{\tau'})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\|p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q_j^{\tau'})-p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q^{\tau'})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]
$$

$$

$$

$$
\le\frac{2}{m^{d}}\sum_{\tau}\sqrt{\frac{1}{dm}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\tau'\sim_j\tau}\|p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q_j^{\tau})-p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q^{\tau'})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^{2}}
$$

$$
\le\frac{2}{m^{d}}\sum_{\tau}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2dm}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\tau'\sim_j\tau}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q_j^{\tau})\,\|\,p(\cdot\mid\hat{q},q^{\tau'})\bigr)}

$$
\le\sqrt{\frac{2}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\beta_{j,t}^{2}}.
$$
