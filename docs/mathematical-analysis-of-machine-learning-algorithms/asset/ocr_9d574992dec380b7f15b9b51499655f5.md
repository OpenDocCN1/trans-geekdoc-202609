```
**Algorithm 18.6** V-type Model-Based Posterior Sampling Algorithm

**Input:** \(\lambda, \eta, \tilde{\eta}, \tilde{\pi}, T, p_0, \mathcal{M}\)

1. **for** \(t = 1, 2, \ldots, T\) **do**
2. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Observe \(x_t^1\)
3. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Draw
   \[
   M_t \sim p_t(M \mid x_t^1, S_{t-1})
   \]
   according to
   \[
   p_t(M \mid x_t^1, S_{t-1}) \propto p_0(M) \exp\left(\lambda \sum_{s=1}^{t} f_M(x_s^1) + \sum_{s=1}^{t-1} L_s^{h_s}(M)\right),
   \]
   with
   \[
   L_s^{h}(M) = -\tilde{\eta}(R_M^h(x_s^h, a_s^h) - r_s^h)^2 + \eta \ln P_M^h(x_s^{h+1} \mid x_s^h, a_s^h).
   \]
4. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Draw Let \(\pi_t = \pi_{M_t}\)
5. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Play policy \(\pi_t\) and observe trajectory \((x_t, a_t, r_t)\) up to \(x_t^{h_t}\) with uniformly random \(h_t\)
6. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Play random policy \(\alpha_t^{h_t} \sim \tilde{\pi}^{h_t}(\cdot \mid x_t^{h_t})\), and observe \((r_t^{h_t}, x_t^{h_t+1})\)
7. **return** randomly chosen \(\pi_t\) from \(t = 1\) to \(t = T\)
```

Proposition 18.60 shows that in general, model-based RL problems can be easier to solve than model-free problems. In fact, there can be a significant separation between model-free method and model-based method for certain problems such as factored MDPs.

**Example 18.61** (Factored MDP (Kearns and Koller, 1999)) Let \(d \ge 1\) and let \(\Omega\) be a small finite set. Define the context space \(\mathcal{X} = \Omega^d\), with the natural partition by time. For a state \(x^h \in \mathcal{X}\), we use \(x^h[i] \subset \Omega\) for \(i \in [d]\) to denote the \(i\)th component of \(x^h\), and for a subset of state variables. For each \(i \in [d]\), the parent of \(i\), \(pa_i \subset [d]\) are the subset of state variables that directly influence \(i\). In factored MDPs, the transition probability \(P\) factorizes according to

\[
P^h(x^{h+1} \mid x^h, a^h) = \prod_{i=1}^{d} P_{(i)}^h[x^{h+1}[i] \mid x^h[pa_i], a^h].
\]

It is known that factored MDPs are learnable using a model-based approach in polynomial time, with well-behaved witness Bellman factorization. However any model-free method has a complexity exponential in \(H\) (Sun et al., 2019).
