# 3 Partitioning of the reachability matrix into different levels

In this step, the final reachability matrix will be used (Table 16.5), to develop
adjacency reachability R(si) sets and adjacency antecedent A(si) sets. R(si) con-
sists of constituent i itself and other constituents, i.e., constituents in the row
that it may affect, whereas the antecedent A(si) consists of constituents i and the
other constituents, i.e., constituents in the column that may affect it.

R(si) ∩ A(si) results in the common constituents in both R(si) and A(si).
Then a comparison between R(si) and R(si) ∩ A(si) is made. The success
factor, for which R(si) and R(si) ∩ A(si) are equal, is judged as the topmost
factor in the hierarchy and is assigned Level I. Once top-level constitu-
tents are identified, they are separated from the remaining constituents. This
iterative procedure is continued until the levels of all variables are obtained.
