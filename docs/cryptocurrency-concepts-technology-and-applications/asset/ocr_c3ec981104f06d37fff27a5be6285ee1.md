Cryptojacking is a particularly common tactic for mining Monero cryptocurrency (Kamps et al. 2022). Covert pre-mines are another type of cryptocurrency mining crime whereby a project claims there is cap on the supply of its cryptocurrency, but someone is able to mine more. Bitcoin Private is an example of this, with $3.9 million in coins covertly pre-mined prior to its launch (Kamps et al. 2022).

Finally, *miner front-running* (also called *miner extractable value*) refers to Ethereum miners ordering transactions (including their own) in a way that generates profit for them based on upcoming transactions they observe in the Ethereum mempool* (Daian et al. 2019). This is similar to traditional financial market front-running, where a trader trades an asset before an upcoming non-public trade (that is known to them) which will affect the asset's price (Mitchell 2021).

In cryptocurrency markets, miners could execute trades to manipulate prices based on knowledge they obtained about upcoming trades (Xu et al. 2022) or extract profits by copying a trade in the mempool, executing theirs first, and stealing the original trader's profit. They could also deploy bots to this end (Daian et al. 2019). Miner extractable value also includes back-trading (making a trade after another for financial gain) or sandwich attacks (combining front-running and back-running) (Xu et al. 2022).

# **Money Laundering**

*Money laundering* is a key cryptocurrency crime concern. Europol estimated that in 2018 criminals laundered $5.5 billion using cryptocurrencies (Corcoran 2018). Money laundering is the process of integrating proceeds of crime into the financial system so they can be used without being connected to the predicate offense from which they derived. Scholars have also pointed to the risk of the use of DeFi (Wronka 2021b) and NFTs (Das et al. 2022)for money laundering.

Cryptocurrency-based money laundering tends to follow the same process of placement, layering, and integration seen in fiat-based money laundering (Desmond et al. 2019). Placement means putting the “dirty” money into the financial system (Albrecht et al. 2019). In the case of cryptocurrencies, this would take the form of a criminal depositing fiat currency into a cryptocurrency exchange (Durrant and Natarajan 2019).

The layering process follows, where money launderers seek to obfuscate the origin and trail of their funds (Albrecht et al. 2019).
