Yet again, the cultural and behavioral reservations may suggest certain roles and decisions that can be automated to be left in the hands of actual people. # Conclusion

Governance is a core challenge and yet one of the central value propositions of blockchain technology. In this chapter, I highlighted the key dimensions of how to approach the governance question in a blockchain-based system. Each type of governance decision is interlinked to each other at a techno-social level. System designers need to be aware of these interlinkages across various types of governance decisions. This does not mean that there is a hierarchy or a sequence of governance decisions that can be deduced from the presented model. Rather, the design of blockchain governance would require an iterative process of assessment where not only IT experts but also business and ethical experts may take part.

Depending on the area of application, legislative, market, political-administrative, and socio-technological framework conditions may accentuate certain choices in blockchain governance. The existence or absence of a regulatory framework concerning data privacy, token technology, smart contracts, and DAOs can limit the available choices in governance design. Furthermore, trust dynamics among the blockchain network and public values connected to the area of application are also crucial to assessing the design choices in blockchain-based solutions. These social factors complement and are influenced by the presence or absence of technological means and capabilities in the network of users.

Lastly, the multifaceted and value-laden challenges connected with the governance question is leading to innovative and tech-driven approaches across blockchain communities. Bounded curves, quadratic voting, and token economic models are some evolving innovative solutions to regulate the behavior of users and communities in blockchain networks. These solutions are currently tested and developed in fringe networks outside of the mainstream enterprise applications. However, their innovative character to address certain conundrums in the “governance paradox” and challenges of the financial and institutional systems appear to be promising and interesting for the future applications of this technology.
