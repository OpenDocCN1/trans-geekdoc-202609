finds that a one-standard deviation increase in new crypto-accepting business venues increases volatility by nearly 20%.

Smales (2022b) investigates whether investor attention has a connection with cryptocurrency crash risk, finding that there is a significant relationship but that it is concentrated in the tails of the crash risk distribution. The attention–crash risk relationship is positive when crash risk is low and negative when crash risk is high. The positive attention–volatility relationship is consistent with the theoretical stock market model of Andrei and Hasler (2014). That is, an increased level of investor attention results in a higher volatility because more attention allows information to be reflected into prices more quickly.

# Liquidity

Liquidity is another important component of market activity. Without sufficient liquidity, it is not possible to buy and sell cryptocurrencies easily or quickly, and implementation of any trading strategies would be prohibitively expensive.

Smales (2022a) finds that higher levels of investor attention can reduce liquidity, largely because of the greater volatility associated with this attention. Conversely, Choi (2021) finds an increase in Twitter activity leads to a short-term improvement in liquidity, while Yao et al. (2021) find that investor attention reduces idiosyncratic risk for cryptocurrencies by increasing liquidity. This effect is more noticeable for newer and smaller cryptocurrencies, which are absent from the sample used by Smales (2022a).

# Concluding Remarks

Since its 2009 inception, the cryptocurrency market has grown tremendously, and with a market capitalisation approaching $1.2 trillion (having peaked near $3 trillion), it is a non-trivial market. Similar to the novel technologies that have come before (e.g., railroads, telephones, and the internet), cryptocurrencies offer great potential but are difficult to understand. Their unique characteristics might reshape the entire financial ecosystem, democratising the system in the process, but there is great uncertainty as to the precise mechanism by which this will occur.

Since cryptocurrencies are essentially a piece of code that does not typically generate cashflows, it is argued that they lack intrinsic value, and so any value that is ascribed to them is likely to be driven by behavioral factors. The substantial price gains and extreme return volatility exhibited by cryptocurrencies has grabbed the attention of a range of investors, suggesting that investor attention is a particularly important behavioral factor to consider.
