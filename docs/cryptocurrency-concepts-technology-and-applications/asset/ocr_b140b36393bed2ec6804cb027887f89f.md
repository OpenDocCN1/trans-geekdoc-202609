Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies that are pegged to a hard currency in a ratio of 1:1. Stablecoins can address some problems of cryptocurrencies; for example, they can mitigate the volatility problem of cryptocurrency (Smales 2021).

However, they also create inefficiencies and liquidity risks in the financial system, which is undesirable to financial system regulators. For this reason, many CBDC advocates argue that CBDCs, not stablecoins, will be the operational currency in the modern financial system and in the future, simply because CBDC design offers benefits that outweigh the benefits of stablecoins (Allen, Gu, and Jagtiani 2022).

Finally, CBDCs also have their own challenges. For instance, interest-bearing CBDCs can lead to a run on bank deposits, which poses funding risks to commercial banks (Garratt and Zhu 2021). Also, many countries have a weak digital payment infrastructure that could lead to financial exclusion for many people if CBDCs are deployed using insufficient technological infrastructure. There is also the risk of cyber attacks if CBDCs are deployed using insufficient technological infrastructure, as well as the risk of unwarranted and inappropriate government surveillance of the CBDC transactions of individuals and corporations (Atako 2021).

Despite these challenges, CBDCs appear to offer more benefits that are very appealing and advantageous compared to its risks. For instance, they can make payments cheaper, safer, faster, and more reliable. In contrast, cryptocurrencies and stablecoins have not yet proven themselves to offer cheaper or faster payments. This is making CBDC become more appealing than cryptocurrencies and stablecoins for day-to-day transactions.

This chapter presented a discussion of central bank digital currency research and developments. It defined CBDC and highlighted the differences and similarities between CBDC and cryptocurrency. The review of recent CBDC research showed that CBDC can offer numerous benefits and also pose some risks. There have been calls to exercise caution in developing and adopting CBDC. Central banks are being urged to seek international collaboration and coordination in developing a CBDC. Regarding CBDC developments, only a few countries have issued a CBDC. Many countries have shown interest in researching the possibility of issuing a CBDC, while other countries are building specific knowledge and expertise about CBDCs before deciding to do so.

Interest in information about CBDC is also growing among members of the public, and such interest may lead to a decline in interest about cryptocurrency.
