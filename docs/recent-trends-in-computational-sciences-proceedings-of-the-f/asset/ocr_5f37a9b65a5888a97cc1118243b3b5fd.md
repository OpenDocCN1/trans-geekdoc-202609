# Wearable electrogastrogram perspective for healthcare applications

C.P. Vijay, A. Amrutha, D.C. Vinitha, R. Rohith & N. Sriram
ORCID ID: 0000-0002-0525-2368, 0000-5206-858X, 0000-0003-3096, 0009-0092-4219-0503

ABSTRACT: The digestive system, which includes the esophagus, duodenum, small intestine, large intestine, and rectum, is responsible for the body's main chemical reactions, with the stomach playing a crucial role. Unfortunately, many people experience gastric dysrhythmia, which can result in symptoms such as nausea, early satiety, bloating, and abdominal pain. To better understand this condition, Electrography (EGG) is recommended. EGG is a non-invasive method that records gastric myoelectric contractions. Although EGG has been the dominant method used to date and has consistently demonstrated associations with arrhythmias and gastroparesis, it has limitations such as low quality and incomplete sensitivity and specificity. This article provides a review of methods used to analyze arrhythmias using EGG, the correlation between wearable electrogastrogram (EGG) and electromyogram (EMG), the anatomy and physiology of EGG, and its clinical significance. It also covers current practices in India and other Asian countries and the importance of EGG in long-term monitoring.

*Keywords: Electrogastrogram (EGG), electromyogram (EMG), gastrointestinal activity, wearable device*

# 1 INTRODUCTION

Electrography is a non-invasive method to record gastric myoelectric contractions. In the late 1800s, Santiago Ramón y discovered interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC), which recognized as the pacemakers of the gastrointestinal system and the regulators of gastrointestinal homeostasis. However, the challenge was to measure the activity of these cells in a meaningful way. In 1922, Walter C Alvarez conducted on the electrical activity of the GI tract at Berkeley. Electrography (EGG) has become an important tool for objectively assessing gastric motility disorders associated with various diseases. EGG is capable of measuring two types of gastric electrical activity: electrical response and electrical control activity, which refers to rhythmic electrophysiological signals in the gastrointestinal (GI) tract. By analysing these signals, healthcare providers can gain insights into the underlying mechanisms of gastric motility disorders and develop targeted treatment plans to alleviate patient symptoms. The frequency range of gastric activity is around 3 cycles per minute (CPM), and using EGG, one can observe abnormalities in electrical activity. The EGG technique involves the use of cutaneous electrodes on the skin of the abdomen over the stomach to record myoelectric activity.

This technique is non-invasive and painless, making it a valuable tool for diagnosing and monitoring gastric motility disorders. By detecting and analysing the electrical signals generated by the smooth muscle cells of the stomach, EGG can provide insights into the frequency and amplitude of gastric contractions, as well as the coordination of peristals.
