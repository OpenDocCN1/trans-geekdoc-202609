# Literature review on industrial human activity and ergonomic risk analysis

Kavitha Jayaram, M.S. Brandi, Deepti Rajakumar, M. Gautham & B.M. Bhuvan
Computer Science and Engineering, B.N.M.I.T, Bengaluru, Karnataka

ABSTRACT: Safety is one the most essential protocols and a moral responsibility that must be followed in any industry. Hazardous industries, such as fire departments, construction departments, and manufacturing units, require proper training to minimize dangerous accidents caused by heavy machinery. Some accidents that can occur are electrocution, falling from a height, or being hit by a moving vehicle. A study conducted by the Bureau of Laboratory Statistics states that 31% of work-related injuries have caused Musculoskeletal Disorders. Since prevention is better than cure, we bring to the limelight the various works carried out for this purpose. In this article, a review of how AI models can be used for anomaly detection, feature recognition and rule-based posture recognition is done along the various methods to collect the data for ergonomic analysis which can be used to broaden the scope of possible solutions to reduce industrial hazards.

# 1 INTRODUCTION

Work injuries are a major drawback in any industry that requires the construction, maintenance, or repair of structures, which reduces the efficiency and motivation of workers and causes a delay in production, which in turn results in poor feedback. Organizations that work on dangerous activities are high in numbers around the world and the probability of accidents and injuries are high. The different workplaces are designed and arranged such that their employees fit in the requirements. This process is known as ergonomics, which is a branch of science that deals with human limitations and abilities that can be enhanced by tools and environments. Industrial ergonomics is a subset of this which aligns job requirements with the worker's physical needs to perform it. An ergonomics analyzes the various tasks that laborers need to perform in an environment and predicts its effects on their health. This is essential for any workplace to reduce the possible risks that can occur. Identification of such ergonomically related problems was traditionally carried out one to many sensors.

With the world's technology evolving towards artificial intelligence, the same problem can be addressed using computer vision, a sub-field of AI that deals with interpreting information from digital images or videos. At the Dartmouth Conference in New Hampshire in 1956, the scientific field of artificial intelligence was established [1]. Since AI is applied in various sectors to minimize human tasks. Researchers in these fields are eager to come up with models that are robust and efficient. There are instances when AI in deployment failed to make proper predictions. One such example could be the tragic diagnosis of COVID-19 and the Triage model, which was useless if not harmful to the lack of good quality data [2].

Identifying the ergonomic situations in industrial sectors involves an analysis of the posture of individuals performing the various tasks. There is a need to know about the existing works carried out to solve this issue and identify the gap that can be filled effectively.
