The maximum frequency and pattern of gastric contractions are determined by the gastric wave. Gastric dysrhythmias include Bradygastria, Tachygastria and Arrhythmia. The average gastric waves in human are about 2-4 cpm. Whereas, gastric waves in Bradygastria range from and Tachygastria ranges from 4-9 cpm [1]. To find the number of cpm in a particular patient, a wearable device which calculates the gastric wave is used, which senses the gastric movement of the sensors placed on the abdominal region. The observed waves are in form of potential. In the past, measuring the electrogastrogram and intestinal electrocardi for an extended period without the interference of respiration was a challenging task. However, a new invention has been introduced that involves an apparatus and method for measuring the electrogastrogram and intestinal electrogastrogram. This method involves using a low pass filter that eliminates high-frequency signals that are not included in the potential of the stomach and bowel. The filter is inserted between an amplifier for biological signals and a recorder that records the amplified signals. The method also ensures a linear relationship between the input signal's frequency of the low-pass filter and the output signal.

# IMPORTANCE OF ELECTROGASTOGRAM (EGG) AND ELECTROMYOGRAM (EMG)

Electromyography (EMG) is a diagnostic method which evaluates the health condition of muscles and the nerve cells (neurons) that control them. Motor neurons transmit signals that cause muscles to contract and relax. These signals are translated into graph form using EMG. The results of EMG help to identify disorders of muscle, nerve and disorders affecting the neuro-muscular connection [4]. Some symptoms that require EMG include: tingling, numbness, weakness, cramp or pain, involuntary muscle twitching. The results of EMG help to determine the underlying cause for the symptoms which possibly include: muscular dystrophy (group of conditions that weakens or damage the muscle over time), myasthenia gravis (weakness in the voluntary muscles), radioulnar pinched nerve (carpal tunnel syndrome), amyloid lateral sclerosis.

EMG electrodes are utilized to detect the biochemical activity within a person's muscle. There are two primary types of EMG electrodes, namely surface electrodes (also known as skin electrodes) and inserted electrodes. The latter can be further classified into two types: needle electrodes and wire electrodes.

The objective of this EMG system is to assist individuals who are equal or easily to assess their muscle strength level and obtain beneficial muscle signals for rehabilitation purposes [3].
