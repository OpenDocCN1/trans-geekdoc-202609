Yaming Wang, Xiangyang Peng, in their paper refer to a convolutional neural network that does both optimization and reconstruction, which entails the optimization of the camera matrix and the rebuilding of the three-dimensional structure along the help of the two modules firstly image optimisation module and second a reconstruction method. Although this technique is often helpful, there is an issue when amounts of data are computed, the reconstruction currently has the drawback of being sensitive to the metrics for giving a smooth output. Shikhar Ravakumar in his paper propose a lightweight, market-ready framework for capturing facial performance data that only monocular video input, integrates active facial monitoring appearance modes with 3D shape pre-constraints and leverages to achieve an integrated objective function. The author uses a prebuilt attention derived from 3D tracking technologies to impose a constraint on the information loss and produce more logical outcomes and a Blendshape model adjustment technique to manage noise detected in producing camera features and parameters while matching 20 channels. Finally, they demonstrate that utilizing spatial limitations will result in more accurate results if the choice of Blendshapes has an impact. [3]

Lu Miao and Zigang Deng based their paper on a monocular RGB video input where the research effort captures human-specific facial models with high-precision details and offers a unique method for real-time reconstruction of a high-resolution facial geometry. The shape-shaping approach first reconstructs a rough face model from the input video, then recovers layers, albedo texture, and displacement by matching the generated face with the RGB incoming video. Reconstruction of high resolution facial geometry and features in real time, is done by exploiting geometry-based techniques. The system operates automatically for a new user and doesn't need any offline preparation. Any faulty detections could result in incorrect subject identities or head postures. The system reconstructs broad head movements and facial expressions in real-time, as well as subtle wrinkles, lighting, and albedo. The refinement of the shadow surface from the human face shape is also reliably using the new hierarchical reconstruction techniques. [4]

From these papers, it can be inferred that a novel rigid stabilization method can be used via a dynamic rigidity approach to stabilize monotonic real-time face tracking given a realistic performance data set created using rigid expression parameters that are based on ground truth. In this study, Chen Cao, Menglei Chai, and colleagues make that an effective rigid tracking system uses a unique method for learning the parameters of dynamic rigidity using facial performance data set. [5]

Guo Zhou, Beeler et al., show a monocular real time face tracking that blends an expressive region base model with the restrictions of features and dense image processing techniques. Also, the system is required to solve jaw pose which plays the main role in visual pipelines is achieved by the system by learning a non-linear mapping from the skin to the underlying jaw motion on a data set where the jaw poses have been acquired by direct observation, and to re-target the mapping to subjects. [6]

Another approach by Jian Li, Zhengfei Kuang et al., show a self-supervised neural network to learn blends from a template of expressions which, a comprehensive library of over 400 facial scans consists of varying pore details, different expressions, and identities. [7]

Shou-I Yu Hyun Park, Jae Shin Yoon et al., provide another approach to circumvent by using a novel network that can directly generate a face from a 2D picture, we can eliminate the need for specific input data. The domain mismatch between the labeled and uncontrolled states is addressed by self-supervised domain adaptation based on “consecutive frame consistency”. [9]

Paula Toledo, Javier Rivera and Acosta, propose a technique for acquiring dynamic features of face appearance. This study presents a time-varying model of appearances and surface details that may be inferred from multiview pictures without the need for multiplexed lighting. However, due to the passive acquisition arrangement, the authors impose a few limits on the appearing estimation by modeling the time diffused reflectance as a shift in albedo rather than any underlying scattering factors. ## 3 SURVEY ON FACIAL ANIMATION

Chen Cao, Agarwal et al., in their demonstrate how to use video to produce real-time face animation on a 3D avatar. In order to account for fluctuations caused by light and other elements frequently found in nature, such as facial hair growth, cosmetics, and skin imperfections, this approach utilizes a highly accurate custom face model. There are two steps in method, the first is to learn Real-time inference from face regression with room to avatar 3D forms and other textures solving the lighting model’s parameters analytically by brief using the model’s parameter pairs (rigid, non-rigid) and source photos. A real-time regression model is then improved using a free video. In the paper, it only models the face for animation but not the following features as hair, neck, or shoulder. [11]

Cheng, Wu, Takashi Shirotake et al., propose an iterative learning framework for effective and precise tracking of face performance. This method alternates between the modeling step, which uses monitored meshes and contour maps to teach the deep learningbased statistical method, and the monitoring step, which takes the model’s predictions of geometrical and texture from the measured images and optimizes the predicted geometry by reducing picture, geometrical, and facial feature errors. [12]

## 4 CONCLUSION

The essence of facial capture and animation lies in the ability to recreate human facial expressions and emotions through technology realistically. This has been made possible through advancements in computer graphics and machine learning algorithms, which enable the accurate capture and mapping of facial movements onto digital characters. Technology has revolutionized the entertainment industry by enabling the creation of lifelike and emotionally engaging characters in films, games, and virtual reality experiences.

The article focuses on reviewing research and work aimed at improving the efficiency and precision of facial motion capture and animation process. Moreover, it highlights the approach towards seamless facial capture and animation which intends to introduce a new degree of realism to the field of entertainment by enabling the construction of realistic and emotionally compelling characters.

In conclusion, the field of facial capture and animation continues to evolve and has the potential to bring about new and exciting applications in the future.
