# RELATED WORK

Recently, more and more car manufacturers, such as BMW has begun designing blind spot monitoring systems for their cars to prevent rear-end collisions of blind spots. There are many types of blind spot point warnings such as radar, ultrasonic and camera detection systems [6]. On an ultrasonic based BDSS have relatively low cost but detection range is relatively short, which requires more time for detection when using a car, its resolution is also relatively low [2]. Vision based system compared to other systems is more sensitive to the surrounding environment and the number of false detection (alarms) will decrease lateral resolution increased [3]. Sensors and radars are used in BDSS to improve detection accuracy. For example, Wong et al. [7] presented a system that used ultrasonic sensors and four cameras mounted on a car to collect data about its surroundings. This system would predict if a vehicle collision happens after processing the collected data was processed. Visual cameras are mostly installed under the wings or mirrors on both sides of the vehicle to detect moving object [12]. In 2012, a vision-based system [13] was proposed. This is a blind spot monitoring system that can detect vehicles by feature extraction from images captured by car-mounted cameras. This drives driver potential blind spots when changing lanes while avoiding collisions. Fernandez et [14] considered that blind spot monitoring could be supported by systems based on passive sensors, such as video cameras, or active sensors including radar and laser sensors. In addition, blind spot monitoring can cover zones behind the camera that are 20 meters long and 4 meters wide on each side. In 2014, Zheng et al. [15] based on motion and static features established BDSS and divided the ground into four regions. Baek et al. [16] performed vision-based object detection on the sides of vehicle blind spots and adapted the cascade classifier HOG for vehicle detection. Different from existing work, in this paper, we propose an algorithm based on Machine learning, our contributions are:

+   (A) data augmentation using from four cameras

+   (B) Mean shift algorithm for blind spot monitoring.

+   (C) Outperformed results of blind spot monitoring.
