According to [5], the use of the Internet of Things can be helpful for tracking COVID-19 patients through a connected network. This technology involves large amounts of data, as well as cloud computing, to gather information from remote monitoring methods such as telecare and scheduled check-ups. The goal of this design is to lower mortality rates and hospitalizations by directing patients to the nearest hospital. The system can generate alerts based on analysis of medical records. According to [6], this paper presents a proposal for an internet of things (IoT) system for real-time electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring that can function in real-time and forward-mode modes. Real-time mode can be challenging due to transmission delays and packet loss, which can affect the accuracy of the data, especially for real-time ECG signal transmission. The proposed system is intended for use in hospitals, allowing doctors or nurses to view the ECG signals at any time and place using a computer or smartphone, without going to the patient. This system could potentially reduce the time and cost of travel for patients, especially those living in suburban or rural areas. In work, it may be possible to incorporate an ECG self-interpretation algorithm to detect abnormal ECG signals and trigger an alert. The system could also be expanded by adding additional health sensors to collect different health parameters.

According to [7], the Body Area Network (BAN) is made up of small sensors that are placed on the human body to measure various physiological parameters. These sensors, known as Wearable BAN (WBAN), transmit all the data they collect to a wireless module called WBAN which uses radio frequency (RF) technology to transmit the data to a mobile device that serves as a monitor. The paper focuses on the design of a smart wearable healthcare system Health Mate, which is based on WBAN and is distributed to monitor body parameters to a smartphone via IEEE 802.15.4 and to a cloud server for storage in a database. Doctors can access the data in realtime and remotely through the internet to monitor their patients. The Health Mate system uses a Fi module to transmit all sent data to a mobile application, server, and website, and the transmission speed is fast due to the low latency in this process. The aim of this research was to develop the Health Mate system as a Smart Wearable System that allows patients to monitor their vital body parameters and enables remote monitoring for doctors. In addition the project was achieved and the components were successfully tested, but the Health Mate system needs further refinement and additional features in order to be implemented in real-world applications.

According to [8], in this study, the healthcare system not only developed a passive system that alerts caregivers when necessary, but also implemented a proactive notification system to assist users in managing their health. The system utilizes wearable devices, such as smart clothes and a healthy watch, to record users' physiological signs and behaviors. If the values fall outside of the standard range, the care notification system will inform the caregiver and the user's family, who then monitor the situation in real-time. In daily life, the system can continue to monitor physiological signals and unusual events over a long period of time, allowing caregivers to have a more comprehensive and systematic understanding of the resident's needs and provide accurate and efficient services to improve the quality of care. In addition to proactive alerting, the care notification system also includes various additional features, such as medication reminders, appointment scheduling, and health tracking, to help users manage their overall health and well-being.

The ROAMM system is a technology that helps people keep track of their health in real-time. It uses sensors and wearable devices to collect personal health data, and analyzes it to identify any potential health issues. If any are found, it sends notifications to the individual and their healthcare provider. This system also includes an Android app that allows users to send emergency alerts, and is customized and automated for ease of use. Additionally, it can monitor heart rate data from the iPhone's Health App and use sensors and an arduino controller to monitor patients and send data to an IoT server. The goal of this system is to reduce mortality and hospital admissions by alerting healthcare professionals in emergency situations. The system also allows doctors to access ECG data in real-time via a cloud server utilizes wireless sensor network and body area network to transmit data and has an API that determines when to send alerts based on user profiles.
