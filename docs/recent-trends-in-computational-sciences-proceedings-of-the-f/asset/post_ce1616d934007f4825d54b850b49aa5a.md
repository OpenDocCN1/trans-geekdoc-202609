## 2 DERIVATION AND PRESENTATION OF THE CHANNEL CAPACITY

In this part of the paper, we analyze SC combiner with multiple branches in a wireless radio communication system by $k-\mu$ fading and $k-\mu$ CCI. The block-scheme of this type of combiner is presented in [8, Figure 1]. Its input signals are $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_L$. The input CCI envelopes are $y_1, y_2, \dots, y_L$. The output values are $x$ and $y$. The SC receiver selects the antenna from the branch where the SIR value is the highest.

### 2.1 Distribution of the envelope at the input branch has the $k-\mu$ distribution [9]:

The PDF of

$p_{y_i}(y_i) = \frac{2e^{-k\mu}}{\mu k} \sum_{l_i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_i} \mu^{l_i} \kappa_i^{l_i} y_i^{2l_i+2\mu-1}}{\Gamma(l_i+1) \Omega_i^{l_i}} (\frac{1+\kappa}{\Omega_i})^{\mu}$

where $k$ is called Rician factor. It is actually the ratio of the dominant component and other components. The parameter $\mu$ denotes a number of clusters in the propagation scatter. $\Gamma(t)$ is Gamma function, and mean signals are marked with $\Omega_i, 1, 2, \dots, L$. The CCI is derived from $k-\mu$ distribution

$p_{y_i}(y_i) = \frac{2e^{-k\mu}}{\mu k} \sum_{l_i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_i} \mu^{l_i} \kappa_i^{l_i} y_i^{2l_i+2\mu-1}}{\Gamma(l_i+1) \Omega_i^{l_i}} (\frac{1+\kappa}{\Omega_i})^{\mu}$

with positive $y_i$. The mean values of the CCI are $s_i = 1, 2, \dots, L$. The ratios of signals and CCIs at each SC receiver are $z_i = x_i/y_i$. Their PDFs are on [10]:

$p_{z_i}(z_i) = |\int_{0}^{\infty} y_i p_{x_i}(y_i) p_{y_i}(y_i) dy_i| = \frac{2e^{-k\mu}}{\mu k} \sum_{l_i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_i} \mu^{l_i} \kappa_i^{l_i} y_i^{2l_i+2\mu-1}}{\Gamma(l_i+1) \Omega_i^{l_i}} (\frac{1+\kappa}{\Omega_i})^{\mu} \frac{(\Omega_i+z_i^2)^{l_i}}{\Gamma(l_i)\Gamma(\mu)}$

The CDF of $z_i$ is [11]:

$F_{z_i}(t) = \begin{cases} 0 & t \leq 0 \\ \frac{1}{e^{k\mu}} \sum_{l_1=0}^{\infty} \sum_{l_2=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_2}}{l_1!l_2!} \frac{\Gamma(l_1+l_2+2)}{\Gamma(l_1+\mu)\Gamma(l_2+\mu)} B(t; \frac{S_i}{\Omega_i}) & t > 0 \end{cases}$

If the Incomplete Beta function $B(x,q)$ appearing in previous expression is presented in shape of [12], the CDF is obtained in next form:

$F_{z_i}(z) = \frac{1}{e^{k\mu}} \sum_{l_1=0}^{\infty} \sum_{l_2=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_2}}{l_1!l_2!} \frac{(\mu)^{l_1}(\mu)^{l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+l_2+2)} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_1}( \mu)^{l_2+l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+\mu)\Gamma(l_2+\mu)} (\frac{S_i}{\Omega_i})^{l_1+l_2}$

The SC receiver output SIR is maximal value of all SIRs $z_i, z = \max(z_1, z_2, \dots, z_L)$. Accordingly, the output will have a new PDF as [13]:

$P_z(z) = L_z(z)F_z(z)^{L-1} = \frac{2}{L} \sum_{l_1=0}^{\infty} \sum_{l_2=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_2}}{l_1!l_2!} \frac{(\mu)^{l_1}(\mu)^{l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+l_2+2)} (1-z)^{l_1+l_2+2L-1} \frac{1}{e^{k\mu}} \sum_{l_1=0}^{\infty} \sum_{l_2=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_2}}{l_1!l_2!} \frac{(\mu)^{l_1}(\mu)^{l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+l_2+2)} (\frac{S_i}{\Omega_i})^{l_1+l_2}$

## 2. Derivation of the channel capacity

Channel capacity, as one of the most important performance measure of wireless system, is defined as the maximum speed at which data can be transferred through the channel [14]. The normalized CC is:

$CC = \frac{1}{B} \int_0^{\infty} z \ln(1+z)p_z(z)dz,$

where CC is Shannon capacity in bits/s, and $B$ is marked transmission bandwidth in Hz. When the logarithmic function introduced in the following shape:

$\ln(1+x) = \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{i}x^{i+1}}{i+1}$

is used, normalized CC will be:

$CC = \frac{1}{B} \int_0^{\infty} \frac{2z}{L} \sum_{l_1=0}^{\infty} \sum_{l_2=0}^{\infty} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_2}}{l_1!l_2!} \frac{(\mu)^{l_1}(\mu)^{l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+l_2+2)} \frac{\Gamma(l_1+l_2+2)}{\Gamma(l_1+\mu)\Gamma(l_2+\mu)} (1-z)^{l_1+l_2+2L-1} \frac{(\mu)^{l_1}(\mu)^{l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+\mu)\Gamma(l_2+\mu)} \frac{(k\mu)^{l_1+l_1}(\mu)^{l_2+l_2}}{\Gamma(l_1+\mu)\Gamma(l_2+\mu)} (\frac{S_i}{\Omega_i})^{l_1+l_2}$
