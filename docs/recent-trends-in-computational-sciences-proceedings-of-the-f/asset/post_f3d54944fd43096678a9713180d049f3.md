ABSTRACT: Many social media offer a low-cost mode of communication that makes it possible for anybody to immediately connect with countless ideals. Because of this, anybody can produce anything on these networks, and whoever is intrigued by the material can acquire it, it represents a revolutionary transformation in our community. On the other hand, it has resulted in the emergence of a number of problems, of which one is the propagation and distribution of materials that contain hateful rhetoric. The recognition and surveillance of inciting hatred are becoming an increasingly difficult problem as a whole, as for individuals, strategists, and scholars. In spite of attempts to leverage automated processes for identification, the capabilities of these methods remain a way from adequate, which consistently demands further study on the subject. This study presents a comprehensive assessment of the previous research that has been done in this area, with a particular interest in natural language processing (NLP) algorithms. Our goal is to gain an understanding of the prevalence of offensive speech in social media platforms found online, the most popular manifestations of hatred, as well the impact outburst has on hateful texts.

# 4 HATE SPEECH AND NLP

### 4.1 Hate speech

Using language in a way that incites hatred is a nuanced topic that is intricately tied to the interactions among diverse groups of people. Various groups and people have attempted to establish criteria for identifying hate speech. Hate speech, the term is often understood as objectionable communication directed towards a group or a person on the basis of traits (like ethnicity, faith, or gender). According to United Nations, the definition of hate speech is an interaction in statement, text, or attitudes that damages or uses derogatory term or hate speech with regard to an individual or group because of who they are; this includes religious, ethnic background, national origin, racial group, appearance, descendance, sex, as well as all other identity factors [21]. It is possible for hate speech to be communicated across any expression, such as pictures, comics, items, actions, and symbolism, as well as being distributed either online or offline. Communication that is motivated by hate is 'discriminating,' which means it is opinionated, discriminatory, or inflexible, or it can be 'judgmental,' which means it is prejudicial or disparaging of a person or group. In conventional media, hate speech is published and disseminated the capacity to be done quickly, deeply, and widely [22]. The comparative longevity of hostile material on the web is another source of concern, given that it has the potential to reappear as well as gain prominence the longer it stays.
