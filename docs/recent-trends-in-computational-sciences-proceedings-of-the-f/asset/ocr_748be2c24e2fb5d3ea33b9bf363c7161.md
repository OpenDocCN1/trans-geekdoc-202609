# Revolution in agriculture sector using blockchain technology

Abdul Taleem, K. Paramesha & M.G. Vinay

Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Vidyvardhaka College of Engineering, Mysore, Karnataka

ABSTRACT: In recent years, blockchain technology has gotten a lot of attention. The introduction of blockchain smart contracts in trust-free contexts enables the implementation of decentralized apps is one explanation for this new year. As smart contracts become more widely used attacks exploiting their flaws will surely increase. Several ways have been investigated to mitigate these assaults and avoid breaches such as documenting vulnerabilities or model testing using formal verification. These methods however fall short of capturing blockchain and user behaviors.
Agriculture is gradually becoming a significant financial industry with global implications. Real-time monitoring of the weather and the condition of the soil, along improved food quality, what are driving the technological, ecological, and economical trend. The study explores the exciting notion of farming agriculture with smart contract technology to produce not better agricultural products but also better supply chains and agricultural logistics, resulting in a variety of advantages for all parties involved. With the use of numerical and categorical data, the emphasis is on calculating similarity metrics for tuples to reflect soil and climate conditions. In addition a high-level language for creating smart contracts meant for the Ethereum Virtual Machine is provided with a sample implementation of one measure. The final stage for smart contracts that depend on physical objects is to highlight elements of agricultural asset digitalization.

Keywords: blockchain, traceability, smart agriculture, smart farming, smart contracts, solidity, ethereum virtual machine (evm)

# 1 INTRODUCTION

About 17.4% of the Indian economy comes from agriculture, which also employs 54.6% of the workforce. India is the world leader in the producing fruits and vegetables. The largest producer of milk is India. The technological revolution has significantly altered farming practices in India.
Higer-yield crops have been created as a result of the Green Revolution, which has also fostered outdated and ineffective farming practices. However, because the majority of Indian farmers lack formal education, they are unable to keep up with the rapid changes. They have been tricked into spending farming necessities.
