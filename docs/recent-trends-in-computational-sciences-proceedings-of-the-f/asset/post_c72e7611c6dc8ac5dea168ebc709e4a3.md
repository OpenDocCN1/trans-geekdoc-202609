
Despite its utility in clinical practice, EGG has some limitations due to the use of cutaneous electrodes. The EGG signals recorded using these electrodes can be affected by various types of artifacts. For instance, electrical activity originating in the small intestine and respiratory muscles can produce frequencies in the range of 9-13 Hz and 12-24 Hz, respectively. Cardiac action potentials also produce frequencies around 60 Hz. These artifacts potentially affect the accuracy and reliability of EGG analysis, making it challenging to develop appropriate gastric waves from other sources of electrical activity. To minimize these artifacts, researchers and clinicians should carefully design and develop appropriate methods for signal processing, and digital filters can be used to remove these artifacts. However, the electrodes can be placed for more detailed recordings. However, the patient should be in a comfortable and relaxed position, and any potential sources of interference, such as electrical or metal objects, should be kept away from the recording area. The recording typically lasts 30 minutes to several hours, depending on the purpose of the examination. During the recording, the patient may be asked to take deep breaths or swallow water to stimulate gastric activity. After the recording is complete, the data is analysed and interpreted by a healthcare professional to diagnose any abnormalities in the gastric activity [8].
