# ## INTRODUCTION

The most often prescribed diagnostics in the medical world today are chest X-rays (CXR), which are essential for identifying pulmonary problems. An estimate shows that around 110 million Chest X-rays are carried out once a year in the USA, among which only 39,500–40,000 clinicians have said to provide the actual reading. Due to advancement in smartphone technology, taking photographs or pictures of CXRs and forwarding them to their colleagues for obtaining instant opinions has become a routine for all doctors. In reality "turn time is key," radiologists are estimated to spend more time on reporting than on "quality of their work." Providing more importance to reverse time can result in confusion, subset reports, mis-diagnosis, and lack of communication with care physicians, specifically in rustic areas where doctors depend on "teleradiology" for CXR interpretation. All of these factors have a significant negative impact on treatment and result in life-altering decisions for patients. For instance, abnormality chest X-Ray images or imported from ChestRay14, which has the largest database, is applied with convolutional neural network (CNN) which is known as the "test data" after being pre-processed, to further classify them into normal images, abnormal images and types. Although, the feasibility of using smartphones for automation is yet to be evaluated, "image-based teleconsultation" with smartphones has gradually increased with respect to its popularity where specific deep learning models have been implemented to read anomalies in chest radiographs.
