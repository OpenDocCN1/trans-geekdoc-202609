## RELATED WORK

Over the years, researchers have proposed several CADx systems for liver disease diagnosis. Shreeja and Hariharan [3] detected liver with Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) using Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) features and Naive Bayes classifiers. Although they reported good accuracy, their method required radiologists to delineate the Region Of Interest (ROI) manually. Manual contouring is the gold standard in medical image segmentation but is very time-consuming and defeating the purpose of a CADx system. Nayak et al. [4] used modified growing on histogram features to segment the liver. The logistic regression Muthuswamy [5] extracted the liver through Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) clustering and used GLCM features and Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier for liver abnormality. Krishna et al. [6] used Segmentation-Based Fractal Texture Analysis (SFTA) and SVM for classification. At [7] proposed a CAD system using Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for classifying Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC). The liver and tumor segments were given through Fully Convolutional Network (FCN). Brunetti et al. [8] classified HCC using Google Inception v3 network. Sun et al. [9] investigated the significance of histogram and GLCM based features in identifying colorectal metastasis in CT images. The ROI was, however, manually delineated.

### 3.1 Dataset description

The CT data used in the work were mainly obtained from Kasturba Medical Centre (KMC), Manipal after approval from the institutional ethics committee. Some CT images for semantic segmentation were taken from a public database, 3-IRCADb [10]. For training the DL model for segmentation, 2428 CT images were used, out of which 2242 images were from the public database and 186 images were from KMC, Manipal. The training and test sets for classification comprised 400 and 100 CT images, respectively. The training dataset had 200 images in each normal and abnormal conditions. The test set had 100 CT images with 50 images from each category. The abnormal images had with different lesions like cysts, hepatocellular carcinoma, hemangioma.

### 3.2 Liver segmentation

Liver segmentation was done using DeepLabv3+ network. Its architecture comprises an encoder and a decoder as depicted in Figure 2. The encoder employs ResNet-50 as the backbone network and Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP). ResNet-50 performs feature extraction and the ASPP block applies atrous convolutions at multiple scales to capture multiple contextual information. The decoder reconstructs the output with dimension same as the input [11]. ResNet-50 is a CNN with fifty layers (Figure 5). It skip connections to overcome the problem of vanishing gradients.

![](img/a35f8e852e0ee65e528c95ad0821783b_39_0.png)

Figure 2. Architecture of the DeepLabv3+ network.

![](img/a35f8e852e0ee65e528c95ad0821783b_39_1.png)

Figure 3. Structure of ResNet-50 network.

For training the DL model 2428 CT images and their ground truths were considered. ITK-SNAP tool [12] was used for ground truth delineation under the supervision of an expert radiologist. The model was trained with Stochastic Descent with Momentum (SGDM) optimizer for 100 epochs. The momentum chosen was 0.9 and a constant learning rate of 0.001 was considered.
