According to study, a large percentage of students at the undergraduate level suffer from migraine, with sleep apnea being the primary culprit [5]. In addition to occurrences of fatigue, irritability, headache pain, and comorbidities, migraine is linked to greater family load, elevated direct and indirect medical costs, and all of these factors [6]. The categories of primary headaches are tension-type headache, trigeminal autonalgia, and other migraine diseases, according to the International Headache Society (IHS 2018). Migraine with and without aura is one of two primary kinds of migraine. The symptoms of the migraine include pain that just appear before the headache are referred to as an aura. Chronic migraine, which differs from episodic migraine, occurs at least 15 days a month for at least 3 months and seems to have migraines at least eight days per month, is another classification of migraine (IHS 2013). Nearly 12% of Americans experience migraine year, and those who suffer severely lessening of life quality (QoL), diminished functioning, and co-existing psychiatric illnesses are some of the consequences of migraine [8].

As a result, it is challenging to address the effects of migraine due to the inherent characteristics of migraine headaches as problems related to daily life. Patients may actually be restricted in their daily life effectively during ictal phases and tasks [9]. A disability score of 0.7 from the World Health Organization places migraine among the most incapacitating illnesses (WHO). Most students have been observed to self-manage, which can result in improper management and overconstraining analgesics, which can lead to resistance, in the current climate of rising headache [10]. Stress and inconsistent sleep are two common causes in the lives of medical students. They are frequently exposed to pressure because of their deferred responsibility for their coursework, the requirement for numerous exams, and their lengthy educational careers.

Additionally significant migraine triggers include diet and nutrition, particularly for children and teenagers. The primary triggers include fasting, chocolate, cheese, and alcohol. Food can have an impact by both directly stimulating brain stem and cortical pathways, which causes vasoconstriction and release of serotonin and norepinephrine [11]. In addition to weather and environment, skipping meals, loud sounds, and menstrual cycles are cited poor sleep and related stress as common triggers [12]. However, studies aimed at prevalence and effects of migraine among Indian and International colleges have not been conducted. The present study sought to better understand the prevalence and effects of migraine among university students in Greater Noida, India.

# 2 MATERIALS AND METHODS

Various university students of Greater Noida participated in the study, between the period of 1st April 2022 and the 21st June 2022 through a questionnaire-based study. Both male and female undergraduate, postgraduate, and PhD students from various universities in Greater Noida, India, were the target population. Indians and students from different backgrounds were enrolled in the study with the age categories from 18 to 32 years or older. All students received HIT-6 (headache impact test) questionnaire, and those who answered “yes” to the question about experiencing a headache were chosen.
