## 4 OBSERVATIONS

It is observed that many of the drift detection methods presume the complete accessibility of labeled streams to analyze data distribution as well as classifier performance. The work [23] offers an entirely unsupervised and semi-supervised approach to develop the applicability of the existing drift detection techniques that are hardly labeled. The work [24] depicts the dependency of their concept drift detector on data distribution change detector, instead of actual class labels. The aim of the paper is to classify and confirm continuity operation of data stream mining under the influence of concept drift. ## Wearable electroastrogram perspective for healthcare applications

C.P. Vijay, A. Amrutha, D.C. Vinitha, R. Rohith

ABSTRACT: The digestive system, which includes the esophagus, duodenum, small intestine, large intestine, and rectum, is responsible for the body's main reactions. Unfortunately, many people experience gastric dyshythmia, which can result in nausea, early satiety, bloating, and abdominal pain. To better understand this condition, Electrography (EGG) is recommended. EGG is a non-invasive method that records myoelectric contractions. Although EGG has been the dominant method used to date and has consistently demonstrated associations with arrhythmias and gastropis, it has limitations such as low quality and sensitivity. This article provides a review of methods used to analyze arrhythmias using EGG, the correlation between wearable electroastrogram (EGG) and electromyogram (EMG), the anatomy and physiology of EGG, and its clinical significance.

*Keywords: Electrogastrogram (EGG), electromyogram (EMG), gastrointestinal activity, wearable device*

## 1 INTRODUCTION

Electrography is a non-invasive method to record myoelectric contractions. In the late 1800s, Santiago Ramón y discovered interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC), which recognized as the pacemakers of the gastrointestinal system and the regulators of gastrointestinal homeostasis. However, the challenge was to measure the activity of these cells in a meaningful way. In 1922, Walter C Alvarez conducted on the electrical activity of the GI tract at Berkeley. Electrography (EGG) has become an important tool for objectively assessing gastric motility disorders. EGG is capable of measuring two types of gastric electrical activity: electrical response and electrical control activity, which refers to rhythmic electrophysiological signals in the GI tract. By analysing these signals, healthcare providers can gain insights into the underlying mechanisms of gastric motility disorders and develop targeted treatment plans to alleviate patient symptoms. The frequency range of gastric activity is around 3 cycles per minute (CPM), and using EGG, one can observe abnormalities in electrical activity. The EGG technique involves the use of cutaneous electrodes on the skin of the abdomen over the stomach to record myoelectric activity.
