# MICROCONTROLLER BOARDS OVERVIEW

The other interesting pillar of the research presented in this paper is the open-source hardware (OSH) movement. OSH movement promotes open hardware design, meaning that the designed devices are composed of low-cost and available components, with additionally available schemes, wiring diagrams, PCB layout data, documentation, source code, and code examples.

Arduino microcontroller boards are one of the most used open-source hardware platforms. The numerous examples using Arduino boards can be easily accessed by published makers and homemade projects. Arduino already has a wide implementation in academic research and engineering education as evident in Table 1, presented in Section 3. The main advantage of this platform is the motivating factor for investigating the usage of Arduino and its clones as fog/edge computing devices and its findings, with the implementation of AI techniques.

With this research and its findings, we will have a deeper view of the possibility of using Arduino and its clones in AI-supported edge and systems. The proof of usability of Arduino boards in building prototype platforms and testbeds will be a low-cost platform for teaching AI-related topics within curricula. Although today exist, a AI friendly to the platform, such as Arduino Nano 33 BLE and Arduino Porta HT, this research includes NodeMCU ESP8266, NodeMCU ESP32, Wemos ESP32, Wemos R1, and Wemos R32. The reason for this approach is that those boards have significantly lower costs than the aforementioned boards. Besides, that those boards have integrated communication modules which make them suitable for integration in wireless sensor networks, IoT, and edge computing systems.

# 5 WIRELESS SENSOR NETWORK FOR SOLAR RADIATION DATA ACQUISITION

An example of implementing AI-to-edge devices is given in the scenario of a wireless network designed for solar radiation acquisition. The motivation for designing this sensor network is based on a necessity for experimentation with solar-powered sensor nodes and education in that field as. The complexity of the systems based on sensor nodes, the large and constantly growing number of nodes, and the need for their constant operation leads to the importance of deploying solar-powered sensor devices.

The sensor network presented in this paper is designed to collect solar radiation data. Data can be obtained using the energy consumption of sensor nodes, helping in mapping solar radiation, and enhance performance through node deployment. The sensor network is shown in Figure 1. The presented network is designed to be used as (1) the platform for the development of energy-efficient solar-powered sensor nodes; (2) the platform in Mechanical engineering courses to analyze solar panels, their efficiency, and dependency on various ambient conditions; and (3) the platform for scientific research on solar radiation acquisition and analyses.

The planned configuration of the sensor nodes can be like DHT-11 or DHT-22 for ambient temperature and humidity, and for a solar temperature measuring, BH1750 and UV sensor for light intensity measuring; ACS712 or INA169 current sensor, MAX471 current and voltage sensor, etc. Sensor nodes have additionally the solar charger using managing Li-Po battery 500 mAh charging and discharging. The solar panel dimensions range from 160mm x 138mm to 130 mm x 87 mm x 2.5 mm. The panel's efficiency is 16%, voltage is 5V, and power is 3W.
