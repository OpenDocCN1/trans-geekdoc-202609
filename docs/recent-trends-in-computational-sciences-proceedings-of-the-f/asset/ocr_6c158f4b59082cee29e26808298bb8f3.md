# 2 CONCEPT DRIFT

The concept drift definition is flexible enough to be defined informally depending on the use of the concept. For example, "the statistical properties of target concept changes over time in a situation" [11]. Finding the exact points of change can be very challenging, as the change between two distributions can be gradual and has to be differentiated from noise that can affect the concept. Consequently, change detection algorithms have to be robust (resistant to noise) and at the same time changes in the dataset, forming a stability-plasticity trade-off [12]. It is also essential for the algorithms to be able to reliably detect the drift in a shorter time than the next instance arrives and also executing the detection in a constant complexity [13]. A learner should know the sequential positioning of examples in the stream or in an out-of-order. particularly in cases the arrival of the samples with latency or in an out-of-order. The differentiation between the local and global drifts [15] is also based on the drift influence on the properties or reports of the problem concept. Over the years, the research has proposed various methods to detect concept drifts in data streams [16]. The proposed techniques are classified into (1) windowing technique, (2) ensemble classifiers, and (3) drift detectors. Among these, the windowing technique presents a manageable supporting mechanism for saving the latest data as well the size of the window helps for maintaining accuracy. Ensemble classifier gives the best to modify any of the methods, their classifier structure and aggregation methods based on concept drifts. A drifting window is one of the most popular only. The Sliding window technique saves the instances of the recent data in a limited number. Sliding window plus traditional learning algorithms can construct classifiers for data data streams. The efficiency of drift detection is often when sudden changes quickly, if fixed size window technique is used. The small size window drops stability accuracy (in periods) because the large size window gives away to see the shifts occurring in the data streams [17]. In addition to drift detectors and windowing technique, and for learning from data streams, the ensemble is also the most common technique. In ensembles, two types, ensemble, and Block-based ensemble are preferred to handle data changes. To update the classifier, one learns incrementally after every instance of data and a Block-based ensemble processes the data that is blocked and then the classifiers are upgraded. Abrupt drifts are found by the ensemble effectively, however, they are unable to detect the gradual drift which appears during the data stream over a reasonable period of time.
