Upon detecting the concept drift in the data stream, the fixed classification models can yield inaccurate results [7]. For instance, consumer buying preferences can vary because of weather shifts or economic fluctuations. Accordingly, business intelligence entities and users of statistical prediction methods must identify which concept within data-streams in a timely manner. The learning process for the classifier must be a continuous process as the model captures the future by learning current concepts. This continuous learning process ensures that any drift occurring in the streams can be tracked. The classifiers used for data-streams must be able to learn the patterns through a single scan and should be capable of performing classification at any time. Further, the model must be able to learn the concept drifts over time and also preserve historical concepts. Typical classification of underlying concept drift should be “speed of change”. The change is considered as abrupt if the shift from one concept to the next concept occurs suddenly and happens after few instances, it is considered as gradual. Alternatively, if the target concept is constant but data distribution shifts over time, it is considered as virtual [8]. However, in practical applications, both real and virtual concepts appear together [9]. The classification of drift as abrupt or gradual depends on its type upon observation. Sudden shifts over data streams represent sudden-drifts, and on the other hand, slow transformation denoted gradual drift. An optimal classification model must be able to identify the concept drifts in the data-streams to minimize accuracy lost during the concept-drift. Numerous methods are put to determine concept drift, and the size of the prominent include online-algorithms, ensembles, and drift-detection methods. Classification of error rates is the foundational concept for most of the drift detection methods [10]. Whenever any concept drift occurs the classifier that belongs to the older chunks becomes incompatible with the upcoming data chunks, and due to this classified new chunk's error rate increases. Hence, error rate becomes the crucial parameter when detecting the concept drift.

## 2 CONCEPT DRIFT

The concept drift definition is flexible enough to be defined informally depending on the use of the concept. For example, "the statistical properties of target concept changes over time in a situation" [11]. Finding the exact points of change can be very challenging, as the change between two distributions can be gradual and has to be differentiated from noise that can affect the concept. Consequently, change detection algorithms have to be robust (resistant to noise) and at the same time changes in the dataset, forming a stability-plasticity trade-off [12]. It is also essential for the algorithms to be able to reliably detect the drift in a shorter time than the next instance arrives and also executing the detection in a constant complexity [13]. A learner should know the sequential positioning of examples in the stream or in an out-of-order, particularly in cases the arrival of the samples with latency or in an out-of-order. The differentiation between the local and global drifts [15] is also based on the drift influence on the properties or reports of the problem concept. Over the years, the research has proposed various methods to detect concept drifts in data streams [16]. The proposed techniques are classified into:

- (1) windowing technique
- (2) ensemble classifiers
- (3) drift detectors

Among these, the windowing technique presents a manageable supporting mechanism for saving the latest data as well the size of the window helps for maintaining accuracy. Ensemble classifier gives the best to modify any of the methods, their classifier structure and aggregation methods based on concept drifts. A drifting window is one of the most popular only. The Sliding window technique saves the instances of the recent data in a limited number. Sliding window plus traditional learning algorithms can construct classifiers for data data streams. The efficiency of drift detection is often when sudden changes quickly, if fixed size window technique is used. The small size window drops stability accuracy (in periods) because the large size window gives away to see the shifts occurring in the data streams [17]. In addition to drift detectors and windowing technique, and for learning from data streams, the ensemble is also the most common technique. In ensembles, two types, ensemble, and Block-based ensemble are preferred to handle data changes. To update the classifier, one learns incrementally after every instance of data and a Block-based ensemble processes the data that is blocked and then the classifiers are upgraded. Abrupt drifts are found by the ensemble effectively, however, they are unable to detect the gradual drift which appears during the data stream over a reasonable period of time. The research work [17] presents Accuracy Updated Ensemble (AUE2). The concept drift of weighted base classifiers in the ensembles is adapted to the AUE2. The work [19], using kernel density estimation for detecting the Twitter hotspot cluster activities as well as finding the hidden patterns in spatial attributes that are chosen from spatotemporal texts narrative, presented a framework. ## 3 ENSEMBLE METHODS

The popular methods Bagging and Boosting, which are presented in the work [21] is used to improve the weak learners' algorithms accuracy. However, using different strategies, these methods utilize trained classifiers on the training data and aggregate every classifier response; then it combines in an ensemble to better predictions. Mainly, for training, bagging method uses various randomly produced bootstrap samples, through the repeated sampling of the test set. On the contrary, boosting method trains multiple classifiers, utilizing multiple data distributions, proved diversity of the classifiers and varying according to the early forecasting. However, attention is needed that several boosting algorithms provide guarantees for their results.
