According to [5], the use of the Internet of Things can be helpful for tracking COVID-19 patients through a connected network. This technology involves large amounts of data, as well as cloud computing, to gather information from remote monitoring methods such as telecare and scheduled check-ups. The goal of this design is to lower mortality rates and hospitalizations by directing patients to the nearest hospital. The system can generate alerts based on analysis of medical records. After testing positive for COVID-19, a patient may be given sensors to monitor their blood oxygen levels, heart rate, and body temperature from an isolation center. This data will be collected from the patient and sent to an IoT server via the cloud. Previous research suggests that remote monitoring is important for monitoring COVID patients and for using IoT in the event of an abnormal condition. One of the key benefits of COVID-19 patients is to continuously monitor their health condition, which can help to prevent the spread of the virus by reducing the need for in-person visits.

According to [6], this paper presents a proposal for an internet of things (IoT) system for real-time electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring that can function in real-time and forward-mode modes. Real-time mode can be challenging due to transmission delays and packet loss, which can affect the accuracy of the data, especially for real-time ECG signal transmission. The proposed system is intended for use in hospitals, allowing doctors or nurses to view the ECG signals at any time and place using a computer or smartphone, without going to the patient. This system could potentially reduce the time and cost of travel for patients, especially those living in suburban or rural areas. In work, it may be possible to incorporate an ECG self-interpretation algorithm to detect abnormal ECG signals and trigger an alert. The system could also be expanded by adding additional health sensors to collect different health parameters.

According to [7], the Body Area Network (BAN) is made up of small sensors that are placed on the human body to measure various physiological parameters. These sensors, known as Wearable BAN (WBAN), transmit all the data they collect to a wireless module called WBAN which uses radio frequency (RF) technology to transmit the data to a mobile device that serves as a monitor. The paper focuses on the design of a smart wearable healthcare system Health Mate, which is based on WBAN and is distributed to monitor body parameters to a smartphone via IEEE 802.15.4 and to a cloud server for storage in a database. Doctors can access the data in realtime and remotely through the internet to monitor their patients. The Health Mate system uses a Fi module to transmit all sent data to a mobile application, server, and website, and the transmission speed is fast due to the low latency in this process. The aim of this research was to develop the Health Mate system as a Smart Wearable System that allows patients to monitor their vital body parameters and enables remote monitoring for doctors. In addition the project was achieved and the components were successfully tested, but the Health Mate system needs further refinement and additional features in order to be implemented in real-world applications.
