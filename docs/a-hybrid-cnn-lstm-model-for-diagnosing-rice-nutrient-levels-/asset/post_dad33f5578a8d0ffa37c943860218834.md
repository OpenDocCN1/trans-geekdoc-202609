
To evaluate the recognition effect of the ResNet101-SE-LSTM model, we used the accuracy (Acc), precision (Pre), recall (Rec) and F1-scores (F1) as the evaluation metrics for measuring the model performance. The calculations of Acc, Pre, Rec and F1 are shown in equations (1-4).

$Acc = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + FP + FN + TN}$ (1)

$P_{re} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$ (2)

$R_{ec} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$ (3)

$F_1 = \frac{2P_{re}R_{ec}}{P_{re} + R_{ec}}$ (4)

where TP represents that positive instances are predicted to be positive, FN represents that positive instances are predicted to be negative, FP represents that negative instances are predicted to be positive, and TN represents that negative instances are predicted to be negative.

### 2.7 Machine identification methods based on rice physiological traits

To distinguish the developmental differences of different N and K interactions, we collected data for the classical plant physiological traits and extended the test data using Monte Carlo samplers (Metropolis and Ulam 1949). These physiological traits included the dry matter weight, N and K contents and accumulation in plant tissues (leaf, stem and sheath, and panicle), total above-ground biomass, specific leaf weight, green leaf weight, leaf area index, plant height and tiller numbers. Two machine learning models, Support Vector Machine (SVM) and random forest, were trained for diagnosing the rice nutrient levels under the interaction of the N and K treatments. The results were compared with the diagnostic results of CNN-LSTM to indirectly evaluate the feasibility of the CNN-LSTM model.

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Identification results of different models

Seven feature extraction networks, namely VGG16, AlexNet, GoogLeNet, DenseNet, inceptionV3, ResNet101, and ResNet101-SE, were combined with LSTM model and used to diagnose the N and K nutrition levels of HHZ at EPIS. The diagnostic results are shown in Table 3 and Fig. 5. AlexNet presented the worst performance of the 16 nutrient level diagnoses, with an accuracy of 73.94%, and its Area Under Curve (AUC) was the lowest, at 0.76. ResNet101 had relatively good performance, with an accuracy of 83.81% and a corresponding AUC of 0.90. After adding the SE module based on the channel domain to ResNet101, the precision and recall of ResNet101-SE increased by 4.94% and 2.45% respectively, and the AUC increased to 0.93, which presented the best performance among all the tested CNN feature networks. Therefore, ResNet101-SE was used to extract the rice features.

### 3.2 Models with or without time-series

In order to evaluate the diagnostic effect of the temporal and non-temporal models, the ResNet101-SE and ResNet101-SE-LSTM models were tested on the two rice varieties at different growth stages. The diagnostic results are shown in Table 4. The accuracy, precision and recall of ResNet101-SE are less than 60% for diagnosing the N and K levels at TS. These evaluation values increased to over 70% when ResNet101-SE was used to diagnose the N and K levels at EPIS. The ResNet101-SE-LSTM model presents good performance for diagnosing the N and K levels at INS and EPIS. The accuracy of the ResNet101-SE-LSTM model is more than 10% higher than that of the non-temporal model in the same period when diagnosing the N and K levels at INS of both HHZ and XS134, and the F1-scores of the model in diagnosing the N and K levels of the two varieties of rice is more than 85% at EPIS, which means that combining the model ResNet101 with the Long Short-Term Memory model can help to improve the model recognition ability.

Plants have different responses to nutrient stress in different growth stages, and their phenotypic characteristics are dynamic. Therefore, a single growth stage cannot reflect the overall growth state of plants. In addition, determining the optimal growth stage for trophic level diagnosis or developing separate models for different growth stages is a difficult task and may not necessarily provide reliable results for making fertilization decisions. Traditional deep learning classifiers only have information extracted at the current stage and cannot learn the dependence relationship between data from rice at different growth stages, resulting in low prediction ability (Table 4). On the other hand, LSTM is a type of recurrent neural network composed of self-recurrent cells, which allow information to be transmitted internally and thus are better suited for processing multi-temporal data.

Confusion matrices of the ResNet101-SE-LSTM for HHZ and XS134 were generated to prove that our proposed framework can distinguish different nutrient levels (see Fig. 6). ResNet101-SE-LSTM showed superior performance in both conventional *indica* rice (HHZ) (Fig. 6-A) and conventional *japonica* rice (XS134) (Fig. 6-B), and it could effectively diagnose the 16 nutrient levels after different N and K interaction treatments. In the test of HHZ, N0K0, N0K1, N0K2 and N0K3 had some mutual misjudgments, and their accuracy rates were 72.00%, 80.00%, 84.00% and 86.00%, respectively. In the test of XS134, N1K0, N1K1, N1K2 and N1K3 had some misjudgments, and their accuracy rates were 76.00%, 80.00%, 84.00% and 88.00%, respectively. Among these combinations, N1K0 and N1K1 had the most severe misjudgments, but most of the misclassifications occurred with different K treatments at the same N level. In conclusion, the effect of N on the comprehensive growth of rice is significantly higher than that of K.

### 3.3 Model generalization ability

To test the generalization ability of our proposed model, the model was trained on the 2021 dataset and evaluated using the 2022 dataset. The results showed that the accuracy and precision of HHZ were both above 81.00%, and the accuracy and precision of XS were both above 82.00% (Table 5). These results mean that the model has an excellent generalization ability. In the complex paddy environments, our advanced framework is able to extract the rice population structure, leaf morphology, and its dynamic changes from processed images to accurately diagnose the plant nutritional levels. All these procedures are automatically performed by deep learning without any human intervention, which provides great convenience for the intelligent monitoring of rice nutritional levels.

### 3.4 Comparison of machine learning and deep learning models

To further investigate the contribution of the deep features to the diagnosis of nutrient levels in rice, we trained the SVM and random forest models to classify and identify the nutrient levels of rice in EPIS based on the rice physiological data. Table 6 shows the results of these two models in identifying the nutrient levels of the two rice varieties. The accuracy of the SVM model is generally higher than that of the random forest model, and the recognition accuracies of HHZ and XS134 reached 86.00% and 81.00%, respectively. ## 4 Discussion

### 4.1 Nutrient diagnosis based on the analysis of single time point and multi-temporal crop images

Recent nutrient diagnosis studies on single time point crop images by convolutional neural networks have made some progress (Condori et al. 2017; Tran et al. 2019; Wu et al. 2020; Xiong et al. 2020; Yao et al. 2021). For example, Wu et al. (2020) collected the color images and five-channel multispectral data at a specific time and trained models for the identification of corn nutrient status. The results showed that the identification accuracies based on color images and multispectral data were 84.7% and 90.5%, respectively, which could quickly provide the nutrient status of corn crops without destroying the crop plants. Tran et al. (2019) proposed a method that applies the statistical method of ensemble averaging to the Inception-ResNet v2 and autoencoder models, achieving 91% accuracy in the nutritional diagnosis of tomato plant growth images under greenhouse conditions.

All the above studies focused on a single key time node during crop growth, and the selection of the key time node is easily obtained through research experience or on-site visual observations. However, the symptoms caused by different types of nutrient deficiency may be similar at different growth stages of the crop, and the nutrient deficiency symptoms in crop plants may not be evident at the selected time node (Vitosuek et al. 2009). In fact, the phenotypes caused by nutrient stress can appear in crops throughout the whole growth dynamic period, so phenotype analysis should consider time series data. Although neural networks have shown good performance in diagnosing crop nutrient deficiency, their combination with time series analysis models, such as LSTM, will be more consistent with the facts. LSTM has been applied in processing time series datasets, such as emotion recognition (Son 2017) and disease prediction (Dua et al. 2020). Besides, in laboratory conditions, Taghavi Namin et al. (2018) studied plant growth and dynamic information through LSTM, and used these characteristics as important differential phenotypes for the nutritional classification of Arabidopsis thaliana, achieving classification accuracy of 93%.
