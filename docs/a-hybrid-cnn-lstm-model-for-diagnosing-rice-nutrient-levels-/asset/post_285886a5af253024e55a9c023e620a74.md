
## A hybrid CNN-LSTM model for diagnosing rice nutrient levels at the rice panicle initiation stage

LIAO Fu-bing1, FENG Xiang-qian2, 3, LI Zi-qiu1, WANG Dan-ying2, XU Chun-mei2, CHU Guang2, MA Heng-yu2, YAO Qing1, CHEN Song2#

1 School of Computer Science and Technology, Zhejiang Sci-Tech University, Hangzhou 310018, P.R.China

2 China National Rice Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Hangzhou 310006, P.R.China

3 School of Agriculture, Yangtze University, Jingzhou 434025, P.R.China

### Abstract

Nitrogen (N) and potassium (K) are two key mineral nutrient elements involved in rice growth. Accurate diagnosis of N and K status is very important for the rational application of fertilizers at a specific rice growth stage. Therefore, we propose a hybrid model for diagnosing rice nutrient levels at the early panicle initiation stage (EPIS), which combines a convolutional neural network (CNN) with an attention mechanism and a long short-term memory network (LSTM). The model was validated on a large set of sequential images collected by an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) from rice canopies at different growth stages during a two-year experiment. Compared with VGG16, AlexNet, GoogleNet, DenseNet, and InceptionV3, ResNet101 combined with LSTM obtained the highest average accuracy of 83.81% on the dataset of Huanghuazhan (HHZ, an indica cultivar). When tested on the datasets of HHZ and Xiu-shui 134 (XS134, a japonica rice variety) in 2021, the ResNet101-LSTM model enhanced with the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block achieved the highest accuracies of 85.38% and 88.38%, respectively. Through the cross-dataset method, the average accuracies on the HHZ and XS134 datasets tested in 2022 were 81.25% and 82.50%, respectively, showing a good generalization. Our proposed model works with the dynamic information of different rice growth stages and can efficiently diagnose different rice nutrient status levels at EPIS, which are helpful for making practical decisions regarding rational fertilization treatments at the panicle initiation stage.

Keywords: dynamic model of deep learning, UAV, rice panicle initiation, nutrient level diagnosis, image classification.

### 1 Introduction

Nitrogen (N) and potassium (K) are not only the key nutritional factors affecting rice photosynthesis, population growth and grain production, but they are also the key factors determining rice yields and food security (Vitousek et al. 2009; Hou et al. 2019). Therefore, it is of great significance to accurately judge the N and K requirements of rice and rationally optimize field fertilization in order to achieve high-quality and high-yield rice. Panicle initiation (PI) is a period of rapid growth and development of the rice young panicles. During this period, the rational application of panicle fertilizer is very critical, and the N and K nutrients in fertilizer can be effectively absorbed to increase the number of spikelets per panicle and the panicle size, which increases rice yields (Liu et al. 2021). At present, the methods for diagnosing rice nutrient requirements mainly involve appearance diagnosis, chemical diagnosis and modern nutrient diagnosis. The appearance diagnosis method mainly relies on technicians with long-term diagnostic experience to visually observe the colors, shapes and growth rates of rice stems and leaves (Chen and Wang 2014; Sun et al. 2018). It is subjective, which can easily lead to misdiagnosis of the rice nutritional status. Chemical diagnosis methods mainly measure the critical concentrations of nitrogen in different plant organs by chemical analysis methods, including total nitrogen diagnosis and nitrate diagnosis (Zhang et al. 2022). But these methods have the problems of destructive sampling and complicated analytical operations, so they are difficult to popularize and apply to nutrient level diagnosis on a large scale. Modern nutrient level diagnosis methods mainly include the chlorophyll measurement method and the hyperspectral remote sensing method. Relevant studies have shown that the nitrogen content of rice leaves has a positive correlation with the chlorophyll content, and the Soil and Plant Analyzer Development (SPAD) value has a linear correlation with chlorophyll, which makes this method useful for nitrogen diagnosis in field crops (Ghosh et al. 2020). Although this method is widely used in nitrogen nutrient diagnosis (Peng et al. 2010; Ali et al. 2015; Ling et al. 2017; Baral et al. 2021), it still has some disadvantages. For example, the SPAD value is greatly affected by light, so multiple measurements are needed to effectively reduce the variability, and it is not suitable for large-scale farmland testing. The hyperspectral remote sensing method mainly analyzes the spectral differences of the target objects. The spectral reflection features of plant canopies are affected by many factors, such as the water content of plant leaves, the geometric structure of the plant canopy and the soil coverage (Wang et al. 2018; Zheng et al. 2019), which greatly limits the reliability and accessibility of using remote sensors for crop nitrogen diagnosis. Therefore, the current application of panicle fertilizer in production mainly depends on the experience and diagnostic knowledge of agricultural experts and technicians. With the aging of the agricultural population in recent years, there is an urgent need to develop an intelligent, efficient, nondestructive and easy method for diagnosing nutrient requirements during rice plant growth.

With the rapid development of digital image processing and artificial intelligence, many studies have focused on the diagnostic methods of crop nutrient status based on images. These methods can be divided into the traditional image pattern recognition methods and deep learning methods. The crop symptoms in response to the lack of nutrient elements are recognized by traditional classification models based on various image features, which mainly include morphological features (Chen and Wang 2014; Sun et al. 2018), texture features (Wood et al. 2012), color features (Sun et al. 2018) and local features (Gu et al. 2012; Li et al. 2015). The steps in traditional pattern recognition methods usually involve first removing the background, then manually designing the image features, and finally training the classifier. Generally, a high accuracy rate can be obtained on the test set. However, the biodiversity and complex backgrounds in field situations cause the traditional pattern recognition methods to show weak robustness and poor generalization ability. With the excellent performance of convolutional neural network (CNN) in large-scale image recognition (Li S X et al. 2022; Sun G J et al. 2022), nutrient deficiency identification methods based on CNN in maize (Condori et al. 2017; Wu et al. 2020), soybean (Xiong et al. 2020), tomato (Tran et al. 2019) and rice (Yao et al. 2021) have shown excellent recognition results. However, the nutrient diagnosis of crops in these studies is usually based on sets of images collected at a single time point. In fact, the responses of crops to nutrient deficiency occur throughout the long growth period of crops. Thus far, many scholars have conducted some studies on the time series data of crops such as corn and wheat, and the results have shown that the fusion of time-series data significantly improves the accuracy of yield estimations (Lin et al. 2020; Li Q et al. 2022; Sun Z Z et al. 2022).

The nutrient level of the rice canopy is related to the population structure, leaf morphology, tiller distribution, other physiological traits and their dynamic changes (Vitousek et al. 2009). Due to the complexity of plant structures and nutrient-stress symptoms at the different development stages of rice, the real-time features of crop population growth combined with their dynamic changes should be considered in order to accurately monitor the rice population nutrient status. In a two-year experiment, we first established rice populations at different nutrient levels, and then used UAV to take time series images, in order to build a population of images. Then, CNN was used to extract the high-dimensional features from the image, and this was combined with LSTM to enhance the feature correlations in the time dimension. This approach was able to improve the accuracy of nutrient level diagnosis in the early panicle initiation stage, which is of great significance for realizing the refined management of panicle fertilization and increasing production and income.

### 2 Materials and methods

Figure 1 shows the flowchart of our experiments. The rice canopy images collected by an RGB camera installed in the UAV were used to train different models for rice early nutrition diagnosis. At the same time, the rice physiological data were collected to train two machine learning models for diagnosing rice nutrient levels. Finally, these models were compared to verify the performance of the hybrid CNN-LSTM model.

### 2.1 Experiment design

The experiments were carried out in the rice field (30°04′N, 119°55′E) of the China National Rice Research Institute (CNRRI) in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, China, in 2021 and 2022. The physicochemical properties of soil were as follows: soil alkaline hydrolyzable nitrogen content 115.37 mg kg-1, available phosphorus (P) 14.20 mg kg-1, available potassium 140.19 mg kg-1, organic matter content 30.48 g kg-1, cation exchange capacity 18.37 mg kg-1 and pH 5.83. An overview of the experimental fields is shown in Fig. 2.

The two tested rice cultivars were Huanghuazhan (HHZ, a variety of conventional indica rice) and Xiushui (XS134, a variety of conventional japonica rice). The rice planting method adopted a split-split-plot randomized block design, with rice varieties as the main plot, N fertilizer level as the split plot, and K fertilizer levels as subplots; so the two varieties of rice were treated with 16 combinations of four different N and K dosages (Table 1). Three replicate blocks were set up to form 96 plots. In both experiments, N fertilizer was applied once 2-3 days before transplanting and once during the rice tillering stage, with a dosage ratio of 5:3, and K fertilizer was applied once 2-3 days before transplanting.
