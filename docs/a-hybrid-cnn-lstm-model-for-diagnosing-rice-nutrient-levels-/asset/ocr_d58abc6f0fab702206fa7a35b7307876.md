# 4.2 The crop growth status responses to N and K treatments

The status of growth in a crop can originate from abiotic/biotic factors in artificial and/or
circumstantial pathways. Among these factors, N and K are the two dominant nutrients affecting
the crop plant growth on the rice paddy. The N benefits tillering production and panicle
development at the vegetative and reproductive stages, respectively, which helps to improve the
amount, the size, and the weight of the panicle, and results in a high yield at maturity (Liu et al.
2021). In addition, N and K are also critical for leaf photosynthesis (Coskun et al. 2017), and have
direct or indirect relationships with canopy size, color, and shape (Che et al. 2016). As the N and K
are extensively involved in various physiological metabolism processes of plants, it is difficult to
distinguish the growth status using one or two indicators. Therefore, an integrated diagnosis with
various physiological traits might be a promising way to evaluate the crop growth status at key
growth stages. In the current study, the SVM and random forest models were used to evaluate the
crop growth status caused by N and K treatments based on the artificially determined crop
physiological traits, and the SVM model had 86.00% and 81.00% recognition accuracies in HHZ
and XS134, respectively. However, it should be noted that the collection of physiological traits was
time-consuming, laborious and involved destructive sampling, which is not suitable for production
applications.
