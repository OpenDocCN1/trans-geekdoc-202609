### 4.2 The crop growth status responses to N and K treatments

The status of growth in a crop can originate from abiotic/biotic factors in artificial and/or circumstantial pathways. Among these factors, N and K are the two dominant nutrients affecting the crop plant growth on the rice paddy. The N benefits tillering production and panicle development at the vegetative and reproductive stages, respectively, which helps to improve the amount, the size, and the weight of the panicle, and results in a high yield at maturity (Liu et al. 2021). In addition, N and K are also critical for leaf photosynthesis (Coskun et al. 2017), and have direct or indirect relationships with canopy size, color, and shape (Che et al. 2016). As the N and K are extensively involved in various physiological metabolism processes of plants, it is difficult to distinguish the growth status using one or two indicators. Therefore, an integrated diagnosis with various physiological traits might be a promising way to evaluate the crop growth status at key growth stages. In the current study, the SVM and random forest models were used to evaluate the crop growth status caused by N and K treatments based on the artificially determined crop physiological traits, and the SVM model had 86.00% and 81.00% recognition accuracies in HHZ and XS134, respectively. However, it should be noted that the collection of physiological traits was time-consuming, laborious and involved destructive sampling, which is not suitable for production applications.

In recent decades, reports on N/K fertilizer application have mainly focused on testing the amount and/or types of fertilizer at key growth stages by evaluating the final output (Ma et al. 2019; Huang et al. 2021), which is not only time-labor intensive, but also relies on the environmental conditions and variety. Therefore, a simple technique for the diagnosis of rice nutritional status is needed for fertilizer topdressing application. Reports have indicated that the timing of topdressing at EPIS has a great impact on improving the source capacity (Ma et al. 2019). However, due to the quantitative effects of N and K on crop growth and yield performance, the plant growth status responses to different levels of N/K treatment were progressive and the symptoms were difficult to distinguish at a time when the differences among the treatment levels were small, which has resulted in few quantitative fertilization studies based on the actual nutrient levels before PI (Li et al. 2017; Wang et al. 2021). On the other hand, the application of N and K fertilizers would affect rice growth in both spatial (canopy morphological changes) and temporal (i.e., tillering/LAI dynamic) modes. The N and K contents affect rice growth and respond to rice phenotype. In this study, a spatiotemporal convolutional network model was developed to diagnose the crop growth status resulting from different N and K treatments before PIS. The results show that the effect of the ResNet101-SE-LSTM model is consistent with that of the SVM model based on rice physiological traits. The results can help to improve the accuracy of topdressing fertilizer application at panicle initiation.

### 4.3 Relationship between crop nutrient levels and yield prediction

The traditional yield prediction models of crops usually adopt the data fitting method using environmental factors and crop growth factors (Gonzalez-Sanchez et al. 2014; Khaki and Wang 2019; Yao et al. 2020). Environmental factors include soil type, light and CO2. Crop growth factors include the crop growth and development rate. In recent years, remote sensing technology combined with spectroscopy has been used to predict crop yields in practical environments. For example, Latwal et al. (2019) used agrometeorological regression models, empirical models based on remote sensing indices, and semi-physical models to estimate crop yields for rapeseed and mustard at the district level. Kang et al. (2021) analyzed six vegetation indices based on multi-spectral images of UAVs and used them to develop regression models for predicting rice yield and protein content. Panda et al. (2010) used a BP neural network (BPNN) modeling method to verify the effectiveness of four spectral vegetation indices (NDVI, green vegetation index (GVI), soil adjusted vegetation index (SAVI) and vertical vegetation index (PVI)) in corn crop yield prediction.

Most of these studies are primarily based on the numerical regression of existing factors for yield fitting. The main factors include the N and K absorption content of rice at the current stage, as N and K play significant roles in crop health and development (Vitousek et al. 2009; Hou et al. 2019). At the same time, the environmental factors and cost require a prudent application of N and K (Chlingaryan et al. 2018). Therefore, it is challenging to establish a dynamic model of the topdressing amount and yield variation with reference to the nutrient level in order to achieve optimal N and K management. We classified the different nutrient levels of rice at EPIS and continued to keep the rice growing until maturity without dynamic fertilization. This allowed us to form rice populations with different yields, which is basically consistent with the study of Wang et al. (2013) in which different N fertilizers caused rice populations with different yields. The early nutrient levels of rice will affect the rice yield in the future. The diagnosis of rice nutrient levels at EPIS is a simple way to realize the fine management of panicle fertilizer applications. In the future, we will focus on the relationships of rice early nutrient levels, fertilization amount and yield prediction.

## 5 Conclusion

In this study, a dynamic model of deep learning based on ResNet101 combined with the SE module and LSTM (ResNet101-SE-LSTM) was proposed to explore the feasibility of using rice canopy images collected by UAV to identify nutrient levels in 2021 and 2022. Seven convolutional neural networks were trained to identify the N and K nutrient levels. The results showed that the ResNet101-SE model can effectively extract image features from the spatial and temporal domains for the recognition of rice nutrient levels. This study also shows the importance of adding time domain extraction to a single dynamic model for diagnosing nutrition levels. In our experiment, the ResNet101-SE-LSTM model achieved the highest overall accuracies of 85.88% and 88.38% for HHZ and XS134, respectively, in 2021. Our model also showed relatively good generalization ability, with the overall accuracies of 81.25% and 82.50% for HHZ and XS134, respectively, in 2022. Compared with traditional methods involving the destructive detection of rice nutritional status, our method provides a way to identify the rice nutrient levels in EPIS, which can provide field workers with real-time data and references during rice fertilization in order to increase rice yields.
