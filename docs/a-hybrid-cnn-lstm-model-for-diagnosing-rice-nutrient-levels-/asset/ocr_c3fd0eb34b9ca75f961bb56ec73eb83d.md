# 1 Introduction

Nitrogen (N) and potassium (K) are not only the key nutritional factors affecting rice photosynthesis, population growth and grain production, but they are also the key factors determining rice yields and food security (Vitousek et al. 2009; Hou et al. 2019). Therefore, it is of great significance to accurately judge the N and K requirements of rice and rationally optimize field fertilization in order to achieve high-quality and high-yield rice. Panicle initiation (PI) is a period of rapid growth and development of the rice young panicles. During this period, the rational application of panicle fertilizer is very critical, and the N and K nutrients in fertilizer can be effectively absorbed to increase the number of spikelets per panicle and the panicle size, which increases rice yields (Liu et al. 2021). At present, the methods for diagnosing rice nutrient requirements mainly involve appearance diagnosis, chemical diagnosis and modern nutrient diagnosis. The appearance diagnosis method mainly relies on technicians with long-term diagnostic experience to visually observe the colors, shapes and growth rates of rice stems and leaves (Chen and Wang 2014; Sun et al. 2018). It is subjective, which can easily lead to misdiagnosis of the rice nutritional status. Chemical diagnosis methods mainly measure the critical concentrations of nitrogen in different plant organs by chemical analysis methods, including total nitrogen diagnosis and nitrate diagnosis (Zhang et al. 2022). But these methods have the problems of destructive sampling and complicated analytical operations, so they are difficult to popularize and apply to nutrient level diagnosis on a large scale. Modern nutrient level diagnosis methods mainly include the chlorophyll measurement method and the hyperspectral remote sensing method. Relevant studies have shown that the nitrogen content of rice leaves has a positive correlation with the chlorophyll content, and the Soil and Plant Analyzer Development (SPAD) value has a linear correlation with chlorophyll, which makes this method useful for nitrogen diagnosis in field crops (Ghosh et al. 2020). Although this method is widely used in nitrogen nutrient diagnosis (Peng et al. 2010; Ali et al. 2015; Ling et al. 2017; Baral et al. 2021), it still has some disadvantages. For example, the SPAD value is greatly affected by light, so multiple measurements are needed to effectively reduce the variability, and it is not suitable for large-scale farmland testing.
