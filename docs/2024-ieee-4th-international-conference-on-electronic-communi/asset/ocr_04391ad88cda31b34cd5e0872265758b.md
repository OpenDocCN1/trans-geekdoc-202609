# Sports Broadcasting: How Big Data Technology Impacts the Viewer Experience in Baseball Broadcasting †

Pi-Yun An

Department of Communication Arts, Chaoyang University of Technology, Taichung 41310, Taiwan; piyunan@gm.cyut.edu.tw

† Presented at the 2024 IEEE 4th International Conference on Electronic Communications, Internet of Things and Big Data, Taipei, Taiwan, 19–21 April 2024.

Abstract: Baseball is one of the most esteemed professional sports in Taiwan, and its broadcast production techniques are highly developed. Coupled with the sport’s inherently statistical nature, the application of big data was explored in professional baseball broadcasting. Through systematic analysis and understanding of the data collection and utilization of big data in the USA and Taiwan, the results of this study provide the basis for the application and technical capabilities of big data analysis in Taiwanese baseball broadcasting in the future.

Keywords: big data; sport broadcasting; baseball

# 1. Introduction

In the sports industry, data analysis is a longstanding practice. The precise measurement of a player’s value enhances their athletic abilities and skills, consequently improving the efficacy of tactics and training programs. In recent years, big data applications have been used widely, diversifying their range. Sporting events are the critical domain for big data usage. It is utilized for player training and selection and for predicting individual athletic performances. Its significant impact is observed in professional sports teams or leagues in Europe and the United States. Advanced analytical systems are used to gather players’ on-field performances, motion techniques, game results, and others, accumulating vast amounts of data for subsequent analysis. Apart from aiding teams and leagues in player training, selection, and performance prediction, broadcasters use these data to display real-time statistics during games, enabling commentators to provide deeper insights and increase the viewers’ engagement and enjoyment.

# 2. Technology and Sports Events

Technology and sporting events have shared an inseparable relationship, especially during the Olympic Games, which serve as an excellent opportunity for host countries and companies to showcase their technological prowess. The 2012 London Olympics introduced data analysis technology into athletic competitions [1], while the 2016 Rio Olympics became the first to employ VR and 360-degree technology in event broadcasts [2]. The emergence of uncrewed aerial drones broke the constraints of traditional cameras limited by the venue. The 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics witnessed the deployment of 5G mobile broadband and high-speed broadcasting, significantly enhancing the quality of viewing experiences [3]. Additionally, uncrewed vehicles were introduced to transport athletes and spectators. The 2020 Tokyo Olympics, impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic, utilized innovative technologies to overcome restrictions on in-person attendance. Utilizing the high-speed transmission characteristics of 5G and specially positioned sports cameras, audiences were able to view the competitions from the athletes’ perspectives.
