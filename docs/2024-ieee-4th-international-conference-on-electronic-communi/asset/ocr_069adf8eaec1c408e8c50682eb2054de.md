# 2. Literature Review

# 2.1. MALL and AI

MALL is a language learning app for mobile devices. It integrates various language learning activities and prioritizes personal learning. The app is appropriate for students to learn anywhere and anytime [6]. Reciprocal learning can also be carried out using mobile devices in MALL, because it can accommodate collaborative learning between students through mobile device connectivity [7]. Language learning can be more comprehensive with multiple functions [8]. Integrating AI with MALL can revolutionize language learning, making it more personalized and enabling real-time language practice.

AI is used in language learning such as chatbots, virtual reality platforms, and personalized learning management systems to increase effective learning and learner satisfaction and motivation [9–11]. The use of AI in language learning activities improves the instruction and acquisition of language [12,13]. However, the effectiveness of language learning supported by AI depends on the involvement of teachers and students, and the role of AI in learning has received attention in research [14,15].

# 2.2. HiNative App for Foreign Language Learning

The HiNative application is known as an application that connects two groups of users, language learners and native speakers, using a question-and-answer method between the two groups [16,17]. HiNative supports the foreign language learning process, especially in aspects such as vocabulary learning, sentence structure, pronunciation, and the target language’s culture [18–20].

# 3. Material and Methods

A descriptive qualitative analysis approach was used to determine Mandarin learners’ perceptions of HiNative apps. The participants were 56 students majoring in Mandarin at a university in Indonesia. The participants in this research were accustomed to using the discussion forum. They used the HiNative apps intensively for a week and filled out a questionnaire. The questionnaire was created based on the technology acceptance model (TAM) framework and using a five-point Likert scale. TAM is a theoretical framework used in information systems to test the attitudes and behavior of mobile app users. The questionnaire items were formulated in three aspects: usability (PEOU), interaction (PU), and portability (PO). The usability aspect focused on a user-friendly interface and straightforward interaction, leading to a positive perception of ease of use. The interaction aspect focused on participants’ communication with native speakers through existing mobile apps, supporting language skills and cultural understanding. Portability referred to the ease of access obtained, influenced by the mobile apps’ ease of use and usefulness.
