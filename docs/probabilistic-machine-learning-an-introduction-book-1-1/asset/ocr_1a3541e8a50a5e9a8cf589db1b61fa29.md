If we post-multiply **X** by a diagonal matrix **S** = diag(**s**), where **s** is a *D*-vector, then we just scale each column of **X** by the corresponding element in **s**.

**X** diag(**s**) = \begin{pmatrix} x_{1,1} & \cdots & x_{1,D} \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ x_{N,1} & \cdots & x_{N,D} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} s_1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ 0 & \cdots & s_D \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} s_1 x_{1,1} & \cdots & s_D x_{1,D} \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ s_1 x_{N,1} & \cdots & s_D x_{N,D} \end{pmatrix} \tag{C.80}

Thus we can rewrite the standardization operation from Sec. 10.2.8 in matrix form as follows:

standardize(**X**) = (**X** − **1**_N **μ**^T) diag(**σ**)^{−1} \tag{C.81}

where **μ** = \bar{\mathbf{x}} is the empirical mean, and **σ** is a vector of the empirical standard deviations.

# C.2.4.3 Sum of squares and scatter matrix

The **sum of squares matrix** is *D* × *D* matrix defined by

**S**_0 \triangleq **X**^T **X** = \sum_{n=1}^{N} **x**_n **x**_n^T = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \begin{pmatrix} x_{n,1}^2 & \cdots & x_{n,1}x_{n,D} \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ x_{n,D}x_{n,1} & \cdots & x_{n,D}^2 \end{pmatrix} \tag{C.82}

The **scatter matrix** is a *D* × *D* matrix defined by

**S**_\bar{\mathbf{x}} \triangleq \sum_{n=1}^{N} (**x**_n − \bar{\mathbf{x}})(**x**_n − \bar{\mathbf{x}})^T = \left(\sum_{n} **x**_n **x**_n^T\right) − N \bar{\mathbf{x}} \bar{\mathbf{x}}^T \tag{C.83}

We see that this is the sum of squares matrix applied to the mean-centered data. More precisely, define \tilde{\mathbf{X}} to be a version of **X** where we subtract the mean \bar{\mathbf{x}} = \frac{1}{N} **X**^T **1**_N off every row. Hence we can compute the centered data matrix using

\tilde{\mathbf{X}} = **X** − **1** \bar{\mathbf{x}}^T = **X** − \frac{1}{N} **1**_N **1**_N^T **X** = **C**_N **X** \tag{C.84}

where

**C**_N \triangleq **I**_N − \frac{1}{N} **1**_N **1**_N^T \tag{C.85}

is the **centering matrix**. The scatter matrix can now be computed as follows:

**S**_\bar{\mathbf{x}} = \tilde{\mathbf{X}}^T \tilde{\mathbf{X}} = **X**^T **C**_N^T **C**_N **X** = **X**^T **C**_N **X** \tag{C.86}

where we exploited the fact that **C**_N is symmetric and idempotent, i.e., **C**_N^k = **C**_N for *k* = 1, 2, … (since once we subtract the mean, subtracting it again has no effect).
