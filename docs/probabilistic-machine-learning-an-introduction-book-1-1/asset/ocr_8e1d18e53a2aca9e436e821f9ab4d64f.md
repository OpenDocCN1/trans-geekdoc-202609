Now we will consider the variance of this estimate. Let us make a second order Taylor series approximation:

$$0 = l'(\hat{\theta}) \approx l'(\theta^*) + (\hat{\theta} - \theta^*) l''(\theta^*) \tag{E.41}$$ $$ (\hat{\theta} - \theta^*) \approx \frac{-l'(\theta^*)}{l''(\theta^*)} \tag{E.42}$$ $$N^{\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{\theta} - \theta^*) \approx \frac{-N^{-\frac{1}{2}} l'(\theta^*)}{N^{-1} l''(\theta^*)} \tag{E.43}$$

Let us first consider the numerator. Its expectation is given by $$\mathbb{E}\left[-N^{-\frac{1}{2}} l'(\theta^*)\right] = N^{-\frac{1}{2}} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \log p(x_n|\theta^*)\right] = 0 \tag{E.44}$$

and hence its variance is given by $$\mathbb{V}\left[-N^{-\frac{1}{2}} l'(\theta^*)\right] = N^{-1} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \log p(x_n|\theta^*)\right)^2\right] = F(\theta^*) \tag{E.45}$$

Now consider the denominator. By the law of large numbers, we have $$\frac{1}{N} l''(\theta^*) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2} \log p(x_n|\theta^*) \to \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2} \log p(x|\theta^*)\right] = -F(\theta^*) \tag{E.46}$$

using Eq. (E.2). Hence $$N^{\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{\theta} - \theta^*) \approx \frac{N^{-\frac{1}{2}} l'(\theta^*)}{F(\theta^*)} \tag{E.47}$$ where $F(\theta^*)$ is a constant, and $l'(\theta^*)$ is a random variable (since it depends on the data), and so $$\mathbb{E}\left[N^{\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{\theta} - \theta^*)\right] \approx 0 \tag{E.48}$$ $$\mathbb{V}\left[N^{\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{\theta} - \theta^*)\right] \approx \frac{F(\theta^*)}{F^2(\theta^*)} = \frac{1}{F(\theta^*)} \tag{E.49}$$ $$\mathbb{V}\left[\hat{\theta} - \theta^*\right] \approx \frac{1}{N F(\theta^*)} \tag{E.50}$$

We have computed the mean and variance of the distribution of the MLE. To show it is Gaussian, we can invoke the central limit theorem, since the log likelihood is a sum of iid random variables.
