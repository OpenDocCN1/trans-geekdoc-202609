The graph p(h|D) on the right is the weight given to hypothesis h. By taking a weighed sum of dots, we get p(y ∈ h|D) (top). Adapted from Figure 2.9 of [Ten99]. Generated by numbers_game.py.

The first term, log p(D|h), is the log of the likelihood, p(D|h). The second term, log p(h), is the log of the prior. As the data set increases in size, the log likelihood grows in magnitude, but the log prior term remains constant. We thus say that the likelihood overwhelms the prior. In this context, a reasonable approximation to the MAP estimate is to ignore the prior term, and just pick the maximum likelihood estimate or MLE, which is defined as

h_mle ≜ argmax_h p(D|h) = argmax_h log p(D|h) = argmax_h Σ_{n=1}^N log p(y_n|h) (2.21)

Suppose we approximate the posterior by a single point estimate ĥ, might be the MAP estimate or MLE. We can represent this degenerate distribution as a single point mass

p(h|D) ≈ I(h = ĥ) (2.22)

where I() is the indicator function defined in Eq. (1.3). The corresponding posterior predictive distribution becomes

p(y|D) ≈ Σ_h p(y|h) I(h = ĥ) = p(y|ĥ) (2.23)
