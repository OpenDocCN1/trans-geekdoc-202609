This is sometimes called the conservation of probability mass principle, and is illustrated in Fig. 7.24. On the horizontal axis we plot all possible data sets in order of increasing complexity (measured in some abstract sense). On the vertical axis we plot the predictions of 3 possible models: a simple one, M1; a medium one, M2; and a complex one, M3. We also indicate the actually observed data D0 by a vertical line. Model 1 is too simple and assigns low probability to D0. Model 3 also assigns D0 relatively low probability, because it can predict many data sets, and hence it spreads its probability quite widely and thinly. Model 2 is “just right”: it predicts the observed data with a reasonable degree of confidence, but does not predict too many other things. Hence model 2 is the most probable model.
