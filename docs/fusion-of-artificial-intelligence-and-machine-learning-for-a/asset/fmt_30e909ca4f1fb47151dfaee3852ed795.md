
Crop yield heavily relies on proper disease management. The uniform spraying of agrochemicals over the cropping area is the most widely used method, but it results in significant chemical wastage, high costs, and environmental damage in both open-field and covered cultivation. Farmers and experienced individuals have traditionally relied on their naked eyes to recognize crop diseases based on their experience and traditional wisdom. However, this visual disease scanning method is time-consuming and labor-intensive, making it impractical for large farms. For plant disease detection, modern techniques such as traditional ML and CNN-based DL classification methods outperform older methods (expert judgment) in terms of time savings and detection accuracy \[[37](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_37)\].

## 9.5.1 Traditional Machine Learning (ML) Models

The color feature was used to classify diseased and healthy plants \[[38](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_38)\]. The RGB color space was transformed to YCbCr and CIELAB. Image smoothing was performed using a Median filter. The OTSU thresholding technique was used for image segmentation. The only remaining problem is distinguishing the same-colored veins from spots. Authors proposed a diseases detection model for Brinjal crop leaves. An unsupervised machine algorithm called K-means clustering algorithm was used for background pixel removal. Gabor filter was used to extract features from healthy and unhealthy Brinjal crop leaves. ANN was used for the classification of healthy and unhealthy plant leaves of Brinjal.

A model was developed for detecting the presence of illness in potato plant leaves that combines ML and image processing \[[39](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_39)\]. Training and testing were done on images obtained from the ‘Plant Village’ dataset. Before proceeding to training and testing, the image background was removed, and a mask image was created. Color and texture features were extracted from the masked images and these features were used as input to ML models. Texture features from the images were extracted using the GLCM method. SVM was used as a classifier. The accuracy of the disease detection model was around 95% when tested on 300 images.

GLCM texture analysis was employed by Mahmud et al. \[[21](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_21)\] to detect strawberry powdery mildew disease under natural and artificial cloud lighting circumstances. After converting the color image to an HSI (Hue, Saturation, and Intensity) image, 10 characteristics were retrieved from the HSI color channel. The angles of offset and orientation were 1? and 0?, respectively. Texture features of diseased and healthy plants were extracted from four channels (green ratio, hue, saturation, and intensity) using a GLCM. A total of 50 texture features were recovered from photos; however, stepwise discriminant analysis was used to choose 25 features for illness identification. Discriminant function analysis was used for the classification of diseased and healthy plants. Mahmud et al. \[[40](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_40)\] used GLCM texture analysis for feature extraction from strawberry powdery mildew disease images. They also used the ANN technique to process images and classify them as disease and non-disease images. The researchers employed a back-propagation ANN with a tanh sigmoid activation function and a 15,000-epoch size. Field site-I had the highest recall, precision, and F-measure, respectively, of 82.09%, 87.65%, and 85.09%. Field sites-II and III had the lowest recall, precision, and F-measure. Due to high wind speeds (>8 km/h), the presence of another illness (spider mite), leaf tilt, and overlap, a small divergence in system performance was found. A HSI sensor was used for acquiring images of sugar beet diseases (healthy and unhealthy) \[[41](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_41)\]. This study was conducted to identify sugar beet disease. The SVM algorithm and 11 spectral vegetation indices were used for the classification of sugar beet disease. The classification accuracy of the model was reported as 97% in classifying images into healthy and unhealthy.

## 9.5.2 CNN-Based Deep Learning (DL) Models

A smartphone-assisted disease detection system has been developed \[[42](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_42)\]. The study was primarily motivated by the increasing global usage of smartphones and recent advancements in computer vision through DL. A deep CNN was trained to identify 14 crop species and 26 diseases using a public dataset of 54,306 photos of healthy and damaged plant leaves, captured under controlled conditions. These images were obtained from the ‘Plant Village’ dataset and resized to 256 x 256 pixels. Various combinations of training and testing splits were explored. Two approaches were followed in developing the DL models. The first approach involved using pre-trained transfer learning models for classification model development. In the second approach, a CNN-based model for classification was built from scratch. The impact of image type on classification accuracy was investigated, with color images, grayscale images, and background-removed images used as inputs to the network. The accuracy of the two models was evaluated using the F1-score. The average F1-score for GoogleLeNet and AlexNet was 98.86% and 98.48%, respectively. The classification accuracy of the model reached up to 99.35%.

A CNN-based DL network was developed to classify tubers into five classes, namely healthy and four disease classes \[[43](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_43)\]. The dataset contains potatoes of different shapes, sizes, and tones acquired under normal conditions. The dataset was manually labeled by a subject matter specialist. The total dataset was divided into various combinations of train and test splits to observe the impact on model accuracy. The highest classification accuracy achieved was 96% with a training dataset size of 90% and a testing dataset size of 10%. GoogleLeNet and Inception v3 models were implemented on TensorFlow to detect two types of pests and three diseases in Cassava crops \[[44](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_44)\]. The dataset consisted of 11,670 images used for training, validation, and testing stages. The performance metric used was the confusion matrix. The classification accuracy ranged from 80% to 93%.

Waheed et al. \[[45](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_45)\] utilized an optimized DenseNet DL model for the detection and classification of three corn leaf diseases. The total number of trainable parameters was 77,612. The optimized DenseNet’s classification accuracy was compared with VGGNet, XceptionNet, EfficientNet, and NASNet. The DL models were trained on a dataset of 12,332 images, each with an image resolution of 250×250×3. Data augmentation techniques such as cropping, padding, and horizontal flipping were employed to increase the relevant data. The accuracy of the DenseNet model was found to be 98.06%. In the future, the researchers intend to develop a mobile application for corn leaf disease detection and classification.
