
AlexNet, VGG16, and VGG19 CNN models were utilized for the detection of diseases in rice fields \[[46](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_46)\]. Three models were trained to classify four types of paddy diseases, namely Brown spot, Bacterial blight, Tungro, and Blast. The SVM classifier was employed for the detection of these four types of rice diseases. In this study, CNN models utilized a feature extractor from 5,932 disease images. In the second stage, the extracted features were inputted into the SVM classifier. AlexNet (feature layer: fc8) and ResNet50 with the SVM algorithm exhibited superior classification performance compared to other algorithms, achieving an F1-score of 98.38%. A CNN-based DL model was trained with 10,000 labeled images for the detection of Cassava crop diseases \[[47](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_47)\]. The images were processed using the adaptive histogram equalization technique. The accuracy of the model ranged from 76.9% to 99.30%. A CNN and ANN model were employed for the detection of plant diseases \[[48](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_48)\]. Feature extraction with CNN and ANN classification yielded better results than other supervised ML models under different positions or leaf angles in field conditions.

## 9.6 Factors Affecting on Disease and Weed Detection Using ML and DL Techniques

In comparison to laboratory conditions, imaging in the open field is a challenging task. Texture and shape features have shown promising results in classification problems, but their performance is affected by factors such as high wind \[[21](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_21)\], overlapping, and high weed density. Crop factors, including leaf angle, leaf overlapping \[[26](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_26)\], and leaf density, also play a role. The overlapping of leaves and wind speed can impact the performance of classifiers in real field conditions. Calculating shape features becomes difficult when leaves are overlapped or damaged by insects. Therefore, considering texture features along with shape features can improve the performance of ML models. Illumination intensity varies throughout the day \[[49](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_49)\], and wind speed is a major concern in the field. Uncontrolled outdoor illumination can affect the performance of imaging sensors. The accuracy and precision of classification models are influenced by the quality of input images. Artificial lighting conditions and protective cloth can help safeguard the performance of imaging sensors \[[21](#ch9.xhtml#ref9_21)\]. Weed detection and classification can be enhanced using computer vision-based ML and DL algorithms, without any adverse environmental effects. These advancements in science and technology will empower robots to detect pests and apply insecticides to diseased plants in the future. Furthermore, robots will be able to apply herbicides in a site-specific manner, minimizing the need for herbicides.

## 9.7 Advantages And Disadvantages Of Disease And Weed Detection Using Machine Learning (Ml) And Deep Learning (Dl) Techniques

The advantage of traditional ML and DL-based classification is that it is simpler and faster than traditional scanning techniques or manual classification techniques. The main advantage of traditional ML-based classification or regression is that a small amount of data is needed for training the model compared to DL models. Additionally, the training time for ML models is shorter and does not require high GPU for computation. DL models can be converted into `TensorFlow Lite` and deployed on any microcontroller, such as `Raspberry Pi`. Furthermore, spraying units can be attached to the microcontroller to achieve site-specific herbicide or pesticide application. The disadvantage of DL is that it requires a large dataset to develop DL models. Generating data is time-consuming, and environmental factors such as rain, clouds, light, and wind can affect the data collection process. DL models are computationally intensive and require `GPU` and `TPU`.
