The unicity point for a random digraphic substitution cipher, like the one pictured above, is 1,460.61.18 That is, we’d need about 1,461 characters of ciphertext in order to be able to expect a unique solution.

The Playfair Cipher was invented by Charles Wheatstone (Figure 4.10), who described it in 1854.19 As Wheatstone and Playfair were both British (and friends), this did not lead to a huge controversy like the Newton–Leibniz feud over the discovery of calculus.
