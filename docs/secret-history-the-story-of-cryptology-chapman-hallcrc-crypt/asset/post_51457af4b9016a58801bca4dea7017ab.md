At the moment, we don’t have enough information to decide which is correct, so we begin to pursue each possibility further. For Case 1, we look for a column that can appear at the beginning of the chain we’re building such that a T would be joined to an E in columns 10↔15. We have four columns that do this: 7↔4, 12↔20, 13↔19, and 18↔14. Initially, 7↔4 looks best, because it pairs two Ts and Es, whereas the others only pair one T and E. However, there is no column that can be placed between 7↔4 and 10↔15 to place an H between each T and E. Recall, H is identified as FA in Case 1, so it is not enough to find a column that has the same letter pair in each of these two positions; we also want that pair to be FA. (12↔20 offers an FA in one of those positions, but then the other T-E would have some other letter in the middle, which is possible...) Now suppose 12↔20 leads into Case 1. Then we’d want an FA in position 17 of some other column (to fill in the H), and we don’t have it anywhere. Moving on to 13↔19, we’d want an FA in position 11. We are given this by 3↔9. The last case is the same: For 18↔14, we’d want an FA in position 11 and we are given this by 3↔9. So, there are three reasonable possibilities, with the last two being slightly favored, because they don’t require a non-H to appear between T and E anywhere.
