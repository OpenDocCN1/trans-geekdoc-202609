# 20.3.3 MixColumns

In this step, each column of the state is viewed as a polynomial of degree 3 or less. For example, the following column

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a_0\\
a_1\\
a_2\\
a_3
\end{pmatrix}
$$

is viewed as $a(x)=a_3x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0$. However, the coefficients, $a_3$, $a_2$, $a_1$, and $a_0$, are all bytes. That is, the coefficients themselves form polynomials that may be added or multiplied modulo the irreducible polynomial $x^8+x^4+x^3+x+1$ from the SubBytes step.

In the MixColumns step, each column, expressed as a polynomial, is multiplied by the polynomial $c(x)=3x^3+x^2+x+2$. It is then reduced modulo $x^4+1$, so that it may still be expressed as a column (i.e., a polynomial of degree 3 or smaller).

Working modulo $x^4+1$ is a bit different than modulo $x^8+x^4+x^3+x+1$. First of all, $x^4+1$ is reducible! So a randomly chosen $c(x)$ needn't be invertible. For this reason, $c(x)$ had to be chosen carefully, but how was $x^4+1$ chosen? It was picked so that products could be easily reduced. Moding out by $x^4+1$ is the same as defining $x^4=-1$, but $-1=1\pmod 2$, so we have $x^4=1$. This allows us to very easily reduce powers of $x$. We have $x^5=x$, $x^6=x^2$, $x^7=x^3$, and $x^8=x^0=1$. In general, $x^n=x^{n\pmod 4}$. Thus,

$$c(x)a(x)=(3x^3+x^2+x+2)(a_3x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0)$$
$$=3a_3x^6+3a_2x^5+3a_1x^4+3a_0x^3+ a_3x^5+a_2x^4+a_1x^3+a_0x^2+ a_3x^4+a_2x^3+a_1x^2+a_0x+2a_3x^3+2a_2x^2+2a_1x+2a_0$$
