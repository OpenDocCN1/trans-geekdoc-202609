# ## 8.6 Practical Magic

The collection of deciphered Japanese intercepts was codenamed Magic. Magic was in operation before Pearl Harbor was attacked by Admiral Yamamoto’s forces. A question that has been long debated is whether or not such decipherment should have given us advance warning of the attack. The consensus view is that they didn’t provide a detailed warning. There was no Purple message saying that Pearl Harbor, specifically, would be attacked, or even that an attack was imminent. Still, there was enough, when the intercepts were taken in the context of the times, to generate a general warning that would include Pearl Harbor. Recall, Purple was a diplomatic cipher, not military. Perfect understanding of the Japanese Naval code could have provided a timely warning, but that particular code, JN-25, was only partially broken at that time. David Kahn examined the Pearl Harbor issue in great detail in the first chapter of *The Codebreakers* and the reader is referred there for more information.
