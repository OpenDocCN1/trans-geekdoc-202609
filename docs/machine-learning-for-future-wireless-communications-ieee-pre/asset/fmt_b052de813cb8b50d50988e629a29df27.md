
# Support Vector Regression

The basic idea of support vector regression theory is presented in Smola and Schölkopf ([2004](#c17.xhtml#c17-bib-0103)). Given training data with ![images](images/c17-i0295.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow></math> dimensional variables and 1 target‐variable ![images](images/c17-i0296.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mo stretchy="false">&#123;</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">x</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>,</mo><msub><mi>y</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>,</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">x</mi><mn>2</mn></msub><mo>,</mo><msub><mi>y</mi><mn>2</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>,</mo><mi>&#8943;</mi><mspace width="0.3em"/><mo>,</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">x</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo>,</mo><msub><mi>y</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo stretchy="false">&#125;</mo></mrow></math>, where ![images](images/c17-i0297.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">x</mi><mo>&#8712;</mo><msup><mi>&#8476;</mi><mi>m</mi></msup></mrow></math> and ![images](images/c17-i0298.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>y</mi><mo>&#8712;</mo><mi>&#8476;</mi></mrow></math>, the objective is to find a function `f(x)` that returns the best fit, taking into account certain margin `epsilon`, and that at the same time is as flat as possible. As shown in [Figure 17.26](#c17.xhtml#c17-fig-0026), certain deviation (at most `epsilon`) from the actually obtained targets `y_i` is tolerated for all the training data.

Assuming that the relationship between `x` and `y` is approximately linear, `f(x)` takes the form

where `w` is the vector of coefficients and `b` is an intercept or bias term. In the case of Eq. ([17.47](#c17.xhtml#c17-disp-0047)), flatness means that the solution has to consider a small `w`, i.e. a regularization condition (as in Ridge regression) consisting of minimizing the Euclidean norm `||w||_2` (also know as `L2`-norm `||w||_2^2`). Formally, we can write this problem as a convex optimization problem such as
