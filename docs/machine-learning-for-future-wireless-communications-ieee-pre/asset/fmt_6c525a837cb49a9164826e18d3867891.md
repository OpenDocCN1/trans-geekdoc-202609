### 3.2.1.4 Beamforming and Coordination Models

#### Analog Combiners

We assume that each UE has a single RF chain, where combiners at the UE consist of phase shifters only. This design is commonly used in mmWave literature due to its simple implementation. Letting ![images](images/c03-i0058.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msubsup><mrow><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">w</mi></mrow><mi>u</mi><mtext>UE</mtext></msubsup><mo>&#8712;</mo><msup><mi mathvariant="double-struck">C</mi><mrow><msub><mi>N</mi><mtext>UE</mtext></msub></mrow></msup></mrow></math> denote the combining vector of UE ![images](images/c03-i0059.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>u</mi></mrow></math>, each combiner is designed to maximize the link budget (Ayach et al., [2012](#c03a.xhtml#c03-bib-0004)) to its serving BS. Formally, ![images](images/c03-i0060.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msubsup><mrow><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">w</mi></mrow><mi>u</mi><mtext>UE</mtext></msubsup><mo>=</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">a</mi><mtext>UE</mtext></msub><mfenced open="(" close=")" separators=""><mrow><msubsup><mi>&#952;</mi><mrow><mi>b</mi><mi>u</mi><msup><mi>n</mi><mo>&#9733;</mo></msup></mrow><mtext>UE</mtext></msubsup></mrow></mfenced></mrow></math>, where ![images](images/c03-i0061.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msup><mi>n</mi><mo>&#9733;</mo></msup><mo>=</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">arg</mi><msub><mi mathvariant="normal">max</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">&#124;</mo><msub><mi>g</mi><mrow><mi>b</mi><mi>u</mi><mi>n</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">&#124;</mo></mrow></math>, i.e. the combiner is matched to the receive array response having the highest path gain. Evidently, this design assumes that channel gains and angles of arrival (AoAs) are available at each UE.
