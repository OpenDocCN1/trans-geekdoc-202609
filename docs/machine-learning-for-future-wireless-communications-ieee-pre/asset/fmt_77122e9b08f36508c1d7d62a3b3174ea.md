
In (Farsad et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0014)), a `DL`‐based approach for joint source‐channel coding of a text was proposed. This approach is quite similar to the idea presented in (O'Shea and Hoydis, [2017](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0029)), where the source is jointly trained with the channel to reduce transmission distortion. This architecture makes use of a long short‐term memory (`LSTM`)‐based model, as proposed in (Graves and Schmidhuber, [2005](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0016), Hasim et al., [2014](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0019)), over a binary erasure channel (`BEC`) to ensure a gain in word error rate (`WER`) when compared to separate source‐channel word processing. However, the fixed‐length word‐processing scheme does not allow flexibility at the incoming source. Similarly, an end‐to‐end neural network (`NN`) system was proposed for communication over the air between two software‐defined radios (`SDRs`) in (Dörner et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0013)).

The concept of an end‐to‐end `NN` was also extended for `MIMO` relays in (Sun and Jing, [2012](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0034)). In this work, channel training and coherent decoding under estimated channel error were studied for relay networks, and the setup of a single‐antenna as transmitter, ![images](images/c15-i0001.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>R</mi></mrow></math> distinct antenna relays, and a single ![images](images/c15-i0002.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>R</mi></mrow></math> antenna receiver is considered. Note that many coherent cooperative decoding methods assume perfect and global channel state information (`CSI`), which is in reality imperfect, especially in cooperative relay networks. Two types of approaches were considered: decoding where `CSI` is assumed noise‐free, and matched decoding where the noise estimation is evaluated. Simulations demonstrate that at least ![images](images/c15-i0003.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mn>3</mn><mi>R</mi></mrow></math> symbol intervals of training are needed for mismatched decoding versus ![images](images/c15-i0004.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>R</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow></math> for matched decoding to achieve full diversity. In addition, adaptive decoding was presented to compensate for the complexity in the matched decoding. Several works concerning `NN`s applied to `MIMO` systems can be found in (Wen et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0039), O'Shea and Hoydis, [2017](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0029), Samuel et al., [2017](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0033)).

It was established that `DL` decoding for linear block codes is equivalent to deriving the maximum energy function of a `NN` (Bruck and Blaum, [1989](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0008)). In order to maximize the energy function of a `NN`, (Bruck and Blaum, [1989](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0008)) suggested that decoding of `FEC` codes can be done through maximizing polynomials over the ![images](images/c15-i0005.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow></math>‐cube for a ![images](images/c15-i0006.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>N</mi><mo>,</mo><mi>K</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></math> block code, where ![images](images/c15-i0007.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow></math> is the length and ![images](images/c15-i0008.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>K</mi></mrow></math> is the dimension of the code. With similar logic, (Tallini and Cull, [1995](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0036)) predicted that the NP‐complete problem of receiving an error‐free message through a channel can be solved through `NN`. In (Wu et al., [2002](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0040)), a neural structure was described as a perceptron with a higher‐order polynomial as a discriminant function. A ![images](images/c15-i0009.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mn>2</mn><mi>m</mi></msup><mo>&#8722;</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>,</mo><msup><mn>2</mn><mi>m</mi></msup><mo>&#8722;</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>&#8722;</mo><mi>m</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></math> Hamming code was decoded through only ![images](images/c15-i0010.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>m</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></math> assigned weights on each perceptron, ![images](images/c15-i0011.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow></math> being a positive integer. This architecture combines two layers: the first is made of a set of parity bits, and the second is a linear classifier. This proves that high‐order codes such as Bose Chaudhuri Hocquenghem (`BCH`) codes can be learned successfully through a multilayer perceptron (`MLP`).

In channel coding, `DL`‐based decoders can provide improvements in error probabilities over conventional decoders. In (Nachmani et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0028)), the conventional belief propagation (`BP`) decoding algorithm is formalized as a partially connected `NN`. In addition, by assigning trainable weights to the `BP`‐based `NN`, neural `BP` decoders can achieve the same performance as the conventional sum‐product `BP` decoding algorithm with a significantly smaller number of iterations. In (Kim et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0022)), the sequential decoding of turbo and convolutional codes (Berrou et al., [1993](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0007), Viterbi, [1971](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0037)) was performed through recurrent neural network (`RNN`)‐based models. It has been demonstrated that a trained `RNN` architecture can decode these codes over additive white Gaussian noise (`AWGN`) channels with performance near that of the maximum a posteriori (`MAP`) decoding given by the Bahl‐Cocke‐Jelinek‐Raviv (`BCJR`) algorithm and the maximum likelihood (`ML`) decoding given by Viterbi algorithm for sequential codes. This result was confirmed in (Kim et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0022)) through the same architecture and extended to codes with larger block lengths. In (Bennatan et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0006)), a syndrome‐based `DL` technique was proposed to decode linear block codes. For a `BCH` code of length 127, an 11‐layer vanilla `MLP` and a `RNN` of 4 stacks were considered separately to estimate the channel noise rather than the transmitted codeword. The syndrome decoding and soft channel reliabilities were helpful to eliminate the problem of overfitting of the training codeword set. `BCH` decoding performance was compared under traditional `BP` decoding, syndrome‐based vanilla `MLP`, and syndrome‐based stacked `RNN`. Results showed that syndrome‐based stacked `RNN` performance approached that of the ordered statistics decoding (`OSD`) of order 2 while outperforming that of the syndrome‐based vanilla `MLP` at the cost of high complexity and latency.

In the following section, we will focus on `DL` techniques used for polar codes as a case study of `DL` for `FEC` codes.
