### 16.5.5 Prediction‐Assisted MIMO‐OFDM

To further shed light on the mechanism of channel prediction, transmit antenna selection in a `MIMO` system with ![images](images/c16-i0322.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mi>N</mi><mi>t</mi></msub></mrow></math> transmit and ![images](images/c16-i0323.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mi>N</mi><mi>r</mi></msub></mrow></math> receive antennas in a frequency‐selective fading channel is depicted as a representative application example. A frequency‐selective channel can be converted into ![images](images/c16-i0324.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow></math> orthogonal flat‐fading sub‐carriers by means of a fast Fourier transform (`FFT`) demodulator at the receiver and an inverse `FFT` (`IFFT`) modulator at the transmitter, in combination with the utilization of cyclic prefix (`CP`), as shown in [Figure 16.9](#c16.xhtml#c16-fig-0009). There exist two selection strategies for `TAS`: bulk or per‐tone, as mentioned in (Zhang and Nabar, [2008](#c16.xhtml#c16-bib-0045)). Without loss of generality, the latter is used for a clear illustration, i.e. each sub‐carrier decides its best antenna individually instead of the same selection for all sub‐carriers.
