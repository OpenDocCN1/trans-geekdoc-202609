### 9.3.2 Solution for the Markov Decision

In the `MDP` model, the policy iteration and value iteration dynamic programming (Sutton et al., [1998](#c09.xhtml#c09-bib-0024)) methods are two well‐known methods to obtain the optimal policy. However, these two methods both require prior knowledge of the environmental dynamics, which is hard to estimate beforehand in a practical view. In other words, we can hardly obtain prior knowledge of ![images](images/c09-i0308.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>R</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>s</mi><mo>,</mo><mi>a</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></math> and ![images](images/c09-i0309.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>P</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>s</mi><mo>,</mo><mi>a</mi><mo>,</mo><msup><mi>s</mi><mo>&#8242;</mo></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></math> in the `AMC` scenario. In this case, exploration of the state‐action‐reward relationship is required to query the environment.
