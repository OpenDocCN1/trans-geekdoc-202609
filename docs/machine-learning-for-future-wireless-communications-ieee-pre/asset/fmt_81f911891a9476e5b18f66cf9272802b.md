In the performance analysis, we assume that the wireless channel ![images](images/c09-i0083.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="bold">H</mi><mi>n</mi></msub></mrow></math> undergoes quasi‐static block fading, meaning the channel states remain constant across all OFDM symbols within one packet, but are independent for different packets. In addition to this assumption, the following assumptions (Daniels et al., [2008](#c09.xhtml#c09-bib-0006)) are also employed in our analysis:

* `Fixed packet length`: All packets are set to 128 bytes. Hence, for a given bit error rate, all packets have the same expected PER.
* `Perfect synchronization`: All packets are synchronized perfectly in both the time domain and frequency domain. Therefore, any time and frequency offsets can be ignored.
* `Estimation`: Unlike (Daniels et al., [2008](#c09.xhtml#c09-bib-0006)), we assume that the noise power and channels estimation are estimated at receivers. Corresponding to IEEE 802.11n, legacy long training field (L‐LTF) symbols are used to estimate the noise power, while the high‐throughput long training field (HT‐LTF) is used to estimate the channel state.
* `Linear equalization`: A linear zero‐forcing (ZF) equalizer is employed at the receiver to combat inter‐symbol interference (ISI).
