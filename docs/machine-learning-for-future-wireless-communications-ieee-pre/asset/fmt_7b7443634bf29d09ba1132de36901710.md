### 5.3.5 Multi‐Class Classification

In [Subsections 5.3.2](#c05.xhtml#c05-sec-0010) and [5.3.4](#c05.xhtml#c05-sec-0012), the network optimization problems of DP allocation and bias value selection have been modeled as multi‐class classification problems. An outline of how these classification problems can be solved using `SVMs` and `ANNs` is provided in the following. Let ![images](images/c05-i0113.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">h</mi><mi>t</mi></msub></mrow></math> be the feature vector obtained by feature extraction for allocation ([5.18](#c05.xhtml#c05-disp-0023)) and for range expansion ([5.27](#c05.xhtml#c05-disp-0045)) for the training sample ![images](images/c05-i0114.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>t</mi></mrow></math>. The class labels corresponding to all training data samples is given in the vector ![images](images/c05-i0115.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">y</mi><mo>=</mo><msup><mrow><mfenced open="\[" close="\]" separators=""><mrow><msub><mi>y</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>,</mo><mi>&#8230;</mi><mo>,</mo><msub><mi>y</mi><mi>T</mi></msub></mrow></mfenced></mrow><mo>&#8868;</mo></msup></mrow></math>. During the training of a classifier based on support vector machines, a hyperplane ![images](images/c05-i0116.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msup><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">&#969;</mi><mo>&#8868;</mo></msup><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">h</mi><mo>+</mo><mi>b</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow></math> is determined that best separates the feature vectors (datapoints) into two classes. `SVMs` are large‐margin classifiers, which means they aim to maximize the margin between the hyperplane and the closest data points. Since for large training sets the feature vectors generally are not linearly separable by a hyperplane, two modifications for `SVMs` have been established. The first modification is soft‐threshold training, where the hyperplane does not have to strictly separate the two classes of data points. The resulting mis‐classifications are discouraged during the training of the `SVM`. The second modification is the kernel trick, where a function ![images](images/c05-i0117.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>&#977;</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">h</mi><mi>t</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></math> maps the attribute vector ![images](images/c05-i0118.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="bold-italic">h</mi><mi>t</mi></msub></mrow></math> onto the higher‐dimensional lifted feature space of dimension ![images](images/c05-i0119.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>L</mi></mrow></math>. In this feature space, for example, polynomial combinations of the attributes are used as training features.
