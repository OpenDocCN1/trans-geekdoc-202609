
# Joint Decoder-Noise Estimator

`[Figure 15.4](#c15.xhtml#c15-fig-0004)` depicts a joint decoder‐noise estimator approach derived from (Liang et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0025), Bennatan et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0006)), where the noise estimator is a `DL` model such as `NN`, `CNN`, or `RNN`. Unlike off‐the‐shelf `DL` decoders, which try to directly predict the message words given the channel `LLRs`, the joint decoder and noise estimator approach only utilizes off‐the‐shelf `DL` models to denoise the channel `LLR` values, while the main decoding algorithm is still carried out by conventional `BP` decoding.

![Figure 15.4 A joint BP decoder‐DL noise estimator as proposed in (Liang et al., 2018). The input of the noise estimator is the syndrome of the estimated codeword and the magnitude of the estimated channel LLRs (Bennatan et al., 2018).](images/c15f004.jpg)

`[Figure 15.4](#c15.xhtml#R_c15-fig-0004)` A joint `BP` decoder‐`DL` noise estimator as proposed in (Liang et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0025)). The input of the noise estimator is the syndrome of the estimated codeword and the magnitude of the estimated channel `LLRs` (Bennatan et al., [2018](#c15.xhtml#c15-bib-0006)).

The iterative decoding algorithm in `[Figure 15.4](#c15.xhtml#c15-fig-0004)` starts with the first decoding attempt by running the conventional `BP` decoding given the channel input ![images](images/c15-i0083.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="bold">LLR</mi><mi>x</mi></msub></mrow></math>. The channel `LLR` values are then updated by flipping the signs at certain positions predicted by the noise estimator, which results in the denoised channel `LLR` values, ![images](images/c15-i0091.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><msub><mrow><mi mathvariant="bold">LL</mi><msup><mi mathvariant="bold">R</mi><mo mathvariant="bold">&#8242;</mo></msup></mrow><mi>x</mi></msub></mrow></math>. Another `BP` decoding attempt is then carried out given the denoised `LLR` values. Finally, the decoding is terminated if the mentioned termination condition is satisfied or a predetermined maximum number of decoding attempts is reached.
