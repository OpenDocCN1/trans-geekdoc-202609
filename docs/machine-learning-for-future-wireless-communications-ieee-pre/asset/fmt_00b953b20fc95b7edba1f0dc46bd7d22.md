
Low‐resolution (e.g. 1![images](images/c10-i0002.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mo>&#8764;</mo></mrow></math>3‐bit precision) `ADCs` have been regarded as a cost‐effective solution to reduce power consumption of future wireless systems that include massive multiple‐input and multiple‐output (`MIMO`) systems and wideband communication systems (Nossek and Ivrlač, [2006](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0017); Singh et al., [2009](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0022); Dabeer and Madhow, [2010](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0002); Mo and Heath, [2015](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0012)). In spite of the benefits, the use of low‐resolution `ADCs` gives rise to numerous challenges. One challenge is that it is difficult to obtain accurate channel‐state information at the receiver (`CSIR`) with conventional pilot‐based channel estimation techniques. In addition, conventional data‐detection methods, developed for linear `MIMO` systems, provide poor detection performance due to the nonlinearity at the `ADCs`.

Extensive research has been performed to resolve the channel‐estimation and data‐detection problems in uplink massive `MIMO` systems with one‐bit `ADCs` Jacobsson et al. ([2015](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0004),[2017](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0005)), Risi et al. ([2014](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0019)), Choi et al. ([2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0001)), Li et al. ([2017](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0009)), Mollén et al. ([2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0013),[2017](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0014)), Wang et al. ([2015](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0025)), Liang and Zhang ([2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0010)), Wen et al. ([2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0026)). For the channel‐estimation problem in such systems, numerous methods have been developed to improve the accuracy of `CSIR`. The developed methods include a least‐squares (`LS`)‐based method (Risi et al., [2014](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0019)), a maximum‐likelihood–based method (Choi et al., [2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0001)), and a method using Bussgang decomposition (Li et al., [2017](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0009)). For the data‐detection problem, optimal maximum‐likelihood detection (`MLD`) has been introduced in (Choi et al., [2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0001)), and some other low‐complexity methods have also been developed in (Mollén et al., [2016](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0013),[2017](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0014); Jeon et al., [2018a](#c10.xhtml#c10-bib-0006)).

These works can be extended into a multihop `MIMO` system, called a *distributed uplink `MIMO` system*, where an end‐to‐end channel transfer function between ![images](images/c10-i0003.png)<math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mrow><mi>K</mi></mrow></math> sources and the data center is highly nonlinear. Thus, it is extremely challenging to estimate such a channel transfer function with a limited number of quantized pilot signals. This motivates the consideration of a data‐driven supervised learning (`SL`) based detector where pilot signals (or training data) are exploited to directly learn a `MIMO` detector, rather than estimating a complex channel transfer function. In this chapter, we will explain various `SL`‐based detectors for nonlinear multihop `MIMO` communication systems.

The rest of this chapter is organized as follows. [Section 10.2](#c10.xhtml#c10-sec-0002) is devoted to the problem formulation and system model of a multihop `MIMO` system with one‐bit `ADCs`. [Section 10.3](#c10.xhtml#c10-sec-0003) explains `MIMO` detectors with regard to `SL` Learning for considered system. In this section, these detectors are described as being in two categories: non‐parametric learning and parametric learning. [Section 10.4](#c10.xhtml#c10-sec-0006) introduces a low‐complexity `SL` detector that applies clustering algorithms on `SL` detectors with parametric learning. [Section 10.5](#c10.xhtml#c10-sec-0007) shows the performance of introduced `SL` detectors via simulation. [Section 10.6](#c10.xhtml#c10-sec-0008) will offer some further discussion and conclusions.
