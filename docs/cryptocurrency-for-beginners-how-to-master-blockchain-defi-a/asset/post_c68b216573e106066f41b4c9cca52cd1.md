## 3.5.6 B-Money

Wei Dai came up with B-money as an early proposal for an "anonymous, distributed electronic cash system." Satoshi Nakamoto made a reference to B-money when he created the world-famous Bitcoin. B-money was first offered in 1998 by Wei Dai, a graduate of Washington University. His plan was to create a free online financial system. So that no one can impose taxes or compel people to do anything, few people will remember B-Money, which was first introduced to the public in 1998, more than ten years before bitcoins. B-Money, on the other hand, is very different from bitcoins, despite the fact that the creator of the first, Wei Dai, also wanted to create an unnamed and distributed method of issuing coins. Technical documents (link) (the so-called "white paper") containing the same basic principles as modern cryptocurrency were published to speed up the implementation of B-money. The "digital aliases" are expected to be able to send as well as receive money through a decentralized network, as well as ensure the execution of contracts between themselves without the involvement of a third party. The use of a proof of work function as a means of creating money is proposed in the first protocol. Dai's B-Money was proposed in the context of cypherpunks mailing-list discussions about Hashcash, the first symmetric proof-of-work function, which was also printed on the same mailing list the previous year - in May 1997. (Bitcoin uses the hashcash cost function as the proof-of-work during coin minting, just like the B-money proposal.) Money is transferred in B-Money by transmitting the transaction to all participants, who all keep track of each other's accounts. Contracts with the possibility of reparation in the event of default can be made with a third party agreeing to act as arbitrator. If no agreement is reached, each party broadcasts arguments or evidence in its favor, and each participant calculates the reparations/fines in his own account. The accounts can be kept only by a subset of participants, the "servers". These accounts are necessary to be published, and the participants who do exchanges verify their balances by asking a large number of them in the second protocol. The participants also make sure the money supply isn't inflated. To become a server, you must post a bond, which is forfeited if the server is found to be dishonest. An alternative method of creating money is proposed, involving an auction in which participants bid on the solution of known-complexity computational problems. Regretfully, the B-Money project has remained stuck since the white paper was implemented.

## 3.5.7 PayPal

![](img/9e732599f379d4f1dd55a1fd64d01531_172_0.png)

PayPal (PYPL) is a true e-commerce behemoth with a bright future and a devoted user base that is expected to grow as more young people embrace the digital-payment platform. The company is profitable, with revenue exceeding $15 billion in 2018, and truly global in scope, with the platform supporting 100 currencies in over 200 global markets. The conventional wisdom is that PayPal will continue to grow and dominate the digital payment space, despite increased competition for a share of the lucrative e-commerce payments market - one of the most lucrative in an expanding business and consumer technology market.

### The History of PayPal

Although PayPal's history isn't particularly lengthy, there have been numerous ebbs, flows, and tipping points throughout the company's two-decade-plus existence. When technology entrepreneurs Peter Thiel and Max Levchin founded PayPal in 1998, it wasn't even called PayPal. Confinity was the company's original name, and it was founded on the premise of providing consumers and businesses with low-cost, almost effortless digital payments. As the internet's grip on global consumers tightened, the company wisely targeted an underserved market: banking and payments.

The founders' concept was simple and effective: persuade customers to share their email addresses, banking information, and credit card numbers in exchange for quick, low-cost payments. Within three years of opening its doors, the company had processed more than $3 billion in payments from 10.2 million individual consumers as well as 2.6 million commercial customers. After deciding to focus solely on payments made over the internet, the founders changed their name to PayPal two years later. Thiel and Levchin cleverly exploited a gap in the payments market, when no one was really concentrating on a digital payment platform for consumers and businesses, a gap that the duo brilliantly exploited as PayPal took off at the turn of the century.

### PayPal timeline

PayPal's growth trajectory accelerated in its early years, and the company soon set some impressive growth benchmarks.

### December 1998

### October 1999

### January 2000

PayPal's founders and managers noticed that end-users were asking commercial partners as well as other buyers to sign up for PayPal in order to facilitate faster payments. PayPal paved the way for eBay payments on the site almost immediately, boosting PayPal's user base significantly.

### March 2000

PayPal surpasses the one million customer target.

### June 2001

The founders replace the Confinity name with its official name PayPal.

### February 2002

With the issuance of new company stock, PayPal (PYPL-Nasdaq) goes public. The IPO is a success, with PayPal stock rising by more than 50% and closing at $20 per share on its first day of trading.

### October 2002

In a much-touted "marriage" between digital auction as well as online payment services, PayPal is sold to eBay for $1.5 billion.

### February 2006

PayPal crosses one hundred million end-user accounts.

### April 2006

### December 2008

PayPal moves close to the one hundred million user accounts user mark.

### September 2013

### July 2015

PayPal buys Xoom, a digital money transfer firm, for $890 million. This enables the company to accelerate the handling of international payments.

### February 2017

PayPal launches its first peer-to-peer payment system. This helped the company establish a solid foothold in the emerging P2P marketplace.

The company has begun to delve deep into the lucrative but volatile cryptocurrency market, concentrating on the technology's underlying Blockchain platform and avoiding Bitcoin - at least for now. In addition, the company is expanding its presence in India, a key global market, by hiring 1,200 new company technology specialists to concentrate on mobile as well as other emerging payment technologies in India. It also announces a partnership with Facebook (FB) - Get Facebook, Inc. Class A Report Marketplace, which company officials believe has the potential to outperform its long-time partner eBay in terms of financial performance. Overall, investors and analysts see PayPal as a leading player in the digital payments sector, and despite fierce competition from fintech companies and major global banks for the same customers, the company appears well-positioned for financial growth in the future, with a rising share price a near-certainty.
