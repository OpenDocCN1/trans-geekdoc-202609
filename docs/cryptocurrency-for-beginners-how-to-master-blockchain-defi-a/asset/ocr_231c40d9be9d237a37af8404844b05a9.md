In 2007, the US government ordered E-gold to block several E-gold accounts that were providing services similar to E-gold before indicting E-gold for a variety of criminal offenses. The main argument against E-gold was that it did not go far enough to prevent illegal activity by failing to implement a mechanism that prevented fraudulent users from simply creating new accounts as well as transferring their E-gold to them. E-gold was also unlicensed money transmitting business, despite the fact that it was not actually transferring money. Money-transmitting businesses in the United States are required by law to obtain a license, which includes keeping certain legal information on the users who create accounts. As a result, e-creators golds were found guilty of similar charges, including conspiracy. However, the legal ramifications for the company and corporate governance were minor, and E-gold was able to reach an agreement with the federal government, which allowed legitimate users to withdraw funds from their accounts, similar to how online poker players can do. E-gold is still operational today, but it no longer accepts new accounts or e-gold transfers from one user to another.
