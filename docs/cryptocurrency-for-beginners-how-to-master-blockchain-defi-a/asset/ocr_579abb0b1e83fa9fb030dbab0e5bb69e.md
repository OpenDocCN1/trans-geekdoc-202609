It helps consumers, as was the case with barter trading, to purchase goods and services that they need without having to trade product for product. Because of its capacity to store buying power, individuals can conveniently make plans and build specialized economic activities. A company concerned with cell phone assembly, for example, will purchase new appliances, recruit and pay workers, and spread into other areas. The value of fiat money depends on how the economy of a nation works, how the country rules itself and the effects on interest rates of these variables. It is possible that a nation with political instability would have a weakening economy and inflated oil prices, making it impossible for individuals to import goods if they would need them. When the public has enough faith in the ability of the currency to serve as a storage medium for buying power, a fiat currency works well. It must also be backed up by the government's full credit, which gives a declaration and prints it as a legal tender for financial transactions.

# 2.2.3 Advantages and Disadvantages of Fiat Currency

Unlike commodity-based currency, such as gold, copper and silver, the most significant aspect of fiat money is the stability of its value. As governments and banks stepped in to shield their economies from the regular busts of the economic cycle, the use of fiat money became widespread in the 20th century. Because of the daily market cycle and occasional recessions, commodity-based currencies became volatile.
