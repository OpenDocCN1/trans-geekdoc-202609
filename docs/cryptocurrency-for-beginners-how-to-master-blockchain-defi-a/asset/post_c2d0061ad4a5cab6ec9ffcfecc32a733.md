### History

Tether (USDT), formerly known as "Realcoin," is a cryptocurrency that aims to maintain a fixed 1:1 exchange rate with the US dollar. It was founded in July 2014 by Brock Pierce, Craig Sellars, and Reeve Collins. Built on Mastercoin (Omni), a protocol layer for Bitcoin that introduced the stablecoin concept to the world in 2012, Tether was one of the first stablecoins created. Tether is the most widely used stable coin today, having pioneered what is now known as a fiat-collateralized stablecoin model. Tether was created to address two critical issues with existing cryptocurrencies: high volatility as well as fiat currency-to-cryptocurrency convertibility. Tether was created to address these perceived issues by creating a cryptocurrency that is fully backed 1:1 by bank deposits of US dollars. USDT tokens are represented as ERC-20 tokens on Ethereum, while Tether uses the Omni layer to represent USDT tokens on Bitcoin. While the tokens themselves operate on a decentralized network, Tether Ltd, based in Hong Kong, is solely responsible for the creation and redemption of tokens, as well as the 1:1 deposit backing. USDT has been primarily used by exchanges as a substitute for fiat currencies, reducing or eliminating the need for external banking relationships. Tether has always been the subject of controversy since its inception in 2014, owing to the company's failure to provide audited financial statements demonstrating adequate reserves to back USDT. Tether has a history of not disclosing its banking relationships, only doing so recently in November 2018 to quell concerns about its solvency raised by Bitfinex, an associated crypto exchange. Tether updated its disclosure statement in March 2019, claiming that its tokens are no longer fully backed by US dollar deposits. Tether is now 100 percent backed by reserves, which include traditional currency as well as cash equivalents, as well as other assets and receivables from Tether's loans to third parties, including affiliated entities, from time to time.
