Cryptocurrencies are not the same as equities because they are not shares. You don’t own any stock in the firm and don’t get any dividends. If a business issues a cryptocurrency, the business will likely profit or be purchased, with no benefits to you. Even if a firm is doing well, its currency might fall in value.

# You Overtrade

Some investors, typically novices, desire to make 20 deals every day. This is quite risky. Many of them eventually lose money due to fees or just because they make terrible deals and then sell more to make up for their losses. The truth is that there aren’t 20 excellent trade chances every day. Too much trading leads to poor decision-making. Overtrading might potentially raise your tax bill.

# You Chase Cheap Coins

Don’t waste your time chasing cheap pennies with fantasies of Lambos or private aircraft. Many inexperienced entrepreneurs in the crypto world purchase low-cost coins in the hopes of making large profits. This is a very typical pitfall. There are a number of elements that influence the price of a coin, including two major ones: the flowing supply and the coin’s real-world worth.

# You Research Poorly

The procedure will take some time. However, the more study you do, the better you’ll become at it. You can find each coin’s release thread and website on BitcoinTalk.org. Examine the coin’s economy, including its market capitalization, trade volume, value management, and total vs. circulating quantity. Compare and contrast the views of industry professionals.
