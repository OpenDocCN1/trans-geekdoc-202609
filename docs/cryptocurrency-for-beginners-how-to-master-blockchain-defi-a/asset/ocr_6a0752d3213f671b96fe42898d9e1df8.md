The blockchain is a data recording mechanism that makes it difficult or impossible to modify, hack, or cheat the system. A blockchain is basically a digital transaction ledger that is duplicated and spread on the blockchain throughout the whole network of computing systems. Each block in the chain includes a number of transactions, and a record of that transaction is added to the ledger of every party every time a new transaction happens on the blockchain. Distributed Ledger Technology is defined as a shared network operated by many users (DLT). The blockchain is a type of DLT in which an immutable cryptographic signature called a hash is registered in transactions. This suggests that if one block of one chain was altered, it would be automatically obvious that it had been corrupted. They would have to change every block in the chain through all the distributed iterations of the chain if hackers were to corrupt a blockchain structure. As blocks are attached to the chain, block chains such as Bitcoin and Ethereum are actively and continuously increasing, which greatly contributes to the stability of the ledger. The blockchain carries the promise of transactional openness for companies, the potential to build stable, real-time contact networks with collaborators worldwide to facilitate everything from supply chains to payment networks to real estate transactions to the exchange of health data.
