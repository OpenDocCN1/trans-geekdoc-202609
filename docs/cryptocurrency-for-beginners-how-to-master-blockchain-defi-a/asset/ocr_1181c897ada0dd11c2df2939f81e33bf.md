# 3.5.6 B-Money

Wei Dai came up with B-money as an early proposal for an "anonymous, distributed electronic cash system." Satoshi Nakamoto made a reference to B-money when he created the world-famous Bitcoin. B-money was first offered in 1998 by Wei Dai, a graduate of Washington University. His plan was to create a free online financial system. So that no one can impose taxes or compel people to do anything, few people will remember B-Money, which was first introduced to the public in 1998, more than ten years before bitcoins. B-Money, on the other hand, is very different from bitcoins, despite the fact that the creator of the first, Wei Da (Wei Da), also wanted to create an unnaimed and distributed method of issuing coins. Technical documents (link) (the so-called "white paper") containing the same basic principles as modern cryptocurrency were published to speed up the implementation of B-money. The "digital aliases" are expected to be able to send as well as receive money through a decentralized network, as well as ensure the execution of contracts between themselves without the involvement of a third party. The use of a proof of work function as a means of creating money is proposed in the first protocol. Dai's B-Money was proposed in the context of cypherpunks mailing-list discussions about Hashcash, the first symmetric proof-of-work function, which was also printed on the same mailing list the previous year - in May 1997. (Bitcoin uses the hashcash cost function as the proof-of-work during coin minting, just like the B-money proposal.) Money is transferred in B-Money by transmitting the transaction to all participants, who all keep track of each other's accounts. Contracts with the possibility of reparation in the event of default can be made with a third party agreeing to act as arbitrator.
