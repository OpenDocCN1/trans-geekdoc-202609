## 3.5.16 EOS

![](img/9e732599f379d4f1dd55a1fd64d01531_196_1.png)

EOS is a decentralized blockchain-based platform for developing, hosting, and running business applications or dApps. EOS was launched in June 2018 after Block.one raised $4.1 billion in cryptocurrency through an initial coin offering. EOS is the name of the open-source software developed by this company. On the network, EOS cryptocurrency tokens are used as a payment system.

### What is EOS?

EOS provides core functionality that enables companies and individuals to create blockchain-based applications in the same way that web-based applications are created. EOS provides secure access as well as authentication, permissions, data hosting, usage management, as well as dApp-to-Internet communication. EOS is backed by a web toolkit store that aims to make app development as painless as possible. EOS is made up of two main components: the EOS.IO software and EOS tokens. To use network resources and build and run DApps, a developer-only needs to hold EOS coins rather than spend them. Token holders who do not have any apps running can rent or allocate their bandwidth to other participants who do. EOS was founded by Dan Larimer, who is also the founder and creator of well-known platforms such as Bitshares and Steem. It is currently owned by the Block.one company.

## Initial Failure

Although cryptocurrencies were not legally mined until 2009, in previous years, there were other attempts to set up a digital currency. In 1998, two founders of cryptocurrencies, Wei Dai and Nick Szabo published ideas on the prospect of currency decentralization. Both of their proposals revolved around digital currencies as a means to replace conventional financial system inefficiencies, such as spending money on paper and coin currency formation. Although no proposal came to fruition, more than a decade later, both Dai and Szabo became early cryptocurrency adopters. The first true cryptocurrency’s creation, Bitcoin, was a result of these initial failures.

## October 2008

The world of crypto currency started when a white paper Bitcoin was published by a person or group of people: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System under the pseudonym of Satoshi Nakamoto. This publication explained what Bitcoin is why it is equivalent to conventional financial schemes, and by introducing the blockchain to the world, it appeared to fix the missing piece in digital currencies for the first time. The paper by Nakamoto followed up on some of the early concepts that struggled to appear a decade ago and became, as we know it, the base for crypto currencies.

## March 2010

More than a year after official trade, in the form of the now-discontinued BitcoinMarket.com, the first crypto currency trading market was introduced.
