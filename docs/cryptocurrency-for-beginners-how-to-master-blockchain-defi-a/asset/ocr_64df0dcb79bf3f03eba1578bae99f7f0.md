# 1.6 Cryptocurrency in the Future

When it comes to cryptocurrencies, the globe is plainly split. On the one hand, proponents like Bill Gates, Al Gore, and Russell Brand argue that cryptocurrencies are superior to traditional currencies. On the other hand, there are some who are against it, such as Warren Gates, Paul Krugman, and Steven Shiller. Krugman and Shiller, both Nobel Laureates in economics, see it as a Ponzi scam and a vehicle for criminal activity.

There will be a clash in the future between regulations and anonymity. Because a number of cryptocurrencies have been connected to terrorist activities, governments may wish to control how they operate. Cryptocurrencies, but on the other side, place a strong focus on ensuring that users stay anonymous.

According to futurists, cryptocurrencies will account for 25% of national currencies by 2030, implying that a large portion of the globe will begin to trust bitcoin as a method of trade. It will become more widely recognized by merchants and consumers, and it will retain its volatile character, implying that prices are expected to vary as they have over the previous several years.

# 1.7 Blockchain

Blockchain technology is a method of storing data in such a manner that it is hard or impossible to alter, hack, or defraud it. A blockchain is a digital log of transactions that is copied and distributed throughout the blockchain's complete network of computer systems. Each block on the chain comprises a number of trades, and each time a new action takes place on the database, a note of that event is added to the Ledger of each participant.
