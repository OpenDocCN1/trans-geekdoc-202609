### 2.2.3 Advantages and Disadvantages of Fiat Currency

Unlike commodity-based currency, such as gold, copper and silver, the most significant aspect of fiat money is the stability of its value. As governments and banks stepped in to shield their economies from the regular busts of the economic cycle, the use of fiat money became widespread in the 20th century. Because of the daily market cycle and occasional recessions, commodity-based currencies became volatile. Central banks will, if they will need, print or retain paper currency, allowing them greater power over the flow of capital, interest rates, and liquidity. For instance, the management of money supply and demand by the Federal Reserve helped it to handle the 2008 Global Financial Crisis by inflicting more damage to the U.S. financial system and the global economy. The global financial crisis showed otherwise, although fiat money is seen as a more robust currency that can provide a cushion against recessions. While the money supply is regulated by the Federal Reserve, it was not enough to avert the unfolding of the crisis. The restricted supply of gold renders it a more stable currency as compared to fiat money that has an infinite supply.

### 2.3.1 The Origin of Money

There are three major theories with regard to the origin of money:

### Money was created for trading purposes

For trade purposes, most economists believe that money evolved because it was more flexible in contrast to bartering. This meant that currency was in itself a precious asset, such as cattle in ancient civilizations, gold and silver by weight later, and gold and silver coins eventually. All this sounds fair enough when you know that it is an attempt to explain the roots of metallic currency; otherwise, cattle don't fall into this definition of metallic money. Moreover, a high degree of growth would be expected through metallic money: it would suggest an acknowledgment of private property in contrast to tribal property: it would imply recognition of contracts and compliance by a legal structure. At the same time, cattle are much easier to be recognized as money since it is easy for a primitive culture to value, to unilaterally impose a value without a legal structure.

### Money was created for social purposes

The second theory says that for social reasons, money was created, such as fixing a bride's price or blood money for someone killed or wounded by another tribe.

### Money was created for religious purposes

The third theory says that money was created for religious purposes. In his book Holy Gold, Bernard Lam notes that the root of money was in the Eastern temples. It was used as the mandated sacrifice to the gods and for the priests' payment.

### The monetization of gold

Around 1500 BC and 1000 BC, by weight standard, the medium of exchange changed from a cattle standard to gold. In transforming gold into money, the temples played a major role. Gold and silver have accumulated in the temples over the years. For decorative purposes, so much was needed. The fact that so much gold was collected by the temples must have been a key factor behind the decision to turn it into money or monetize it. The theory is that by monetizing it, the priests would have wanted to use up some of this excess gold: they may have estimated, for example, that 1 cow was worth 130 grams of gold. The priests must have been paid for their services overtime, such as guidance on where to grow crops, and would have come up with a standard fee for these services. The theory that by using arbitrary decisions, the priests calculated the value of gold contradicts the origin of money trading, which suggests that the value of gold was determined by the tasks performed in mining it and shaping it into a regular unit of money.

### Money through ages

Applying reasoning to these three hypotheses, one can imagine how a cow was a common unit of money in ancient Ireland and Greece. In those times, one slave girl was worth 3-4 cows in both these two ancient civilizations. It is easy to value a cow; how old the cow is, how many calves the cow is likely to have; how much milk the cow is likely to produce; the value of its hide and beef, its manure, its pedigree, etc. But how do you value, say, 130 grains of gold, which seems to have been the normal weight of a gold monetary unit in ancient times? The priests had the authority in the general public; they had an ample supply of gold; they calculated the price for their services; and when they wanted to monetize gold, they could determine its worth in comparison to the price of the normal unit of account of money than in nature, which was a cow. The hypothesis of the origin of money in western society that makes more sense is that money was produced for religious reasons. Money was given worth by order by the temples' priests. Therefore money, in the form of gold or silver by weight, was the first fiat currency. It had meaning both as a form of payment and even as a commodity.

### Aristotle, Greek Philosopher (384 BC – 322 BC)

Clearly, Aristotle grasped the essence of capital in the 4th century BC, nearly 2,500 years ago. Money is not an asset, and like gold or silver, it cannot be mined. Money, like wheat or barley, is not a crop to be harvested. Money is not a cow or a goat-like animal. Money's essence is that it is a lawful invention. A creature of the law is money. The Greek word for money is 'nomisma,' which means the statute or binding tradition. Aristotle described money as an abstract legal force which for the common good, was publicly regulated.

### Money is the fourth branch of Government

Three branches of government are alluded to by political scientists: the Executive, the Legislature and the Judiciary. Stephen Zarlenga claims persuasively and cogently in The Forgotten Science of Finance that there is a fourth branch of government called The Money Power (whether we know it or not). Money Power is the authority in any given country to issue money. This term was invented by Martin Van Buren (8th US President, 1782–1862). In specifically mentioning that The Money Force is by far the strongest of all government branches, Stephen Zarlenga has made the biggest contribution to monetary reform. The fact that this is not typically known as such has contributed to a pronounced distortion of society and corruption.

## 2.4 New Types of Currencies

Attempts have been made in recent years to establish new types of currency that are not regulated by banks or governments. It should, however, have the same characteristics as currency, such as being scarce but easy to carry and transfer, and so on. Cryptocurrencies, also known as digital currencies, meet all of these criteria. Bitcoin is an example of a cryptocurrency that is based on the blockchain method, in which transactions are verified by a group of people rather than a single person. The disadvantage of cryptocurrencies like bitcoins is that currency control is once again concentrated in the hands of a few individuals. As a result, the goal of decentralization has not been fully realized.
