Individuals employed the barter system in the caveman period, in which products and services were transferred between two or more people. Someone may, for example, swap 7 apples for 7 oranges. Because of its obvious shortcomings, the barter fell out of favor:

- There is no universally accepted measure of value: you must determine how many of your belongings you are ready to sell for other belongings, and not all belongings may be split.
- Everyone's needs must be met: if you have someone to trade, someone must want it, and you must desire what another person is providing.
- A living animal, for example, cannot be divided into smaller components. Unlike our current cash, which fits in such a wallet or is saved on a mobile phone, the items are difficult to transfer.
- The money went through a few revisions once people recognized the barter system wasn't working too well: The first official money was struck in 110 B.C., jewel florins were introduced and used throughout Europe in 1250 A.D., but from 1600 to 1900, paper currency gained great appeal and was used all over the globe. It's how modern cash, as we know it, was born.
