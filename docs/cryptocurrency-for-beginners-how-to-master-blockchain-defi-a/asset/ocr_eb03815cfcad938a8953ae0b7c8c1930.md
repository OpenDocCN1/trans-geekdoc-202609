# 2.2.1 History of Fiat Money

In the 10th century, Fiat money emerged from China, primarily in the dynasties of the Yuan, Tang, Song, and Ming. There was a strong demand for metallic currency in the Tang Dynasty (618-907) that surpassed the availability of precious metals. The individuals were acquainted with the use of credit slips, and paper or paper drafts were readily accepted by them. A lack of coins caused people to turn from coins to notes. There was a thriving industry in the Tchetchuan area during the Song Dynasty (960-1276), which led to a lack of copper money.
