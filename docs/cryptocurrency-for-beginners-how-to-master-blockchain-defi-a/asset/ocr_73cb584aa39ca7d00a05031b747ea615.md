The bit gold scheme proposed by Szabo is non-fungible. This implies that various quantities of bit gold must be merged to produce a single transaction. Rather than a centralized authority managing its levers, the bit gold relies on a decentralized and distributed system of trust amongst the numerous nodes—or participating computers—that make up its network.

# How is Bit Gold different from Bitcoin?

Despite the parallels between bit-gold and bitcoin, the two initiatives have several fundamental distinctions. According to Szabo, the difficulty of mining bit gold will alter over time. It wouldn’t inherently get easier or harder, but there would be peaks and troughs in the quantity of bit gold that could be produced at certain periods. Bitcoin, on the other hand, is designed to increase the difficulty of mining bitcoin over time. (Bitcoins have a finite quantity as well.) More significantly, unlike bitcoin, bit gold was never intended to be utilized as a medium of exchange. Bit gold was designed as a reserve currency for a different sort of electronic money. In the days before fiat money, bit gold was intended to serve as tangible reserves.

According to Szabo, parties must invest a large level of confidence in order for transactions to take effect in the traditional financial system.
