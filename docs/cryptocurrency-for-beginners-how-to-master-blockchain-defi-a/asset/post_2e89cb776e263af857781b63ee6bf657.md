## Dollar-cost average.

Dollar-cost average, or DCA, is indeed an investment approach in which a certain sum of funds is invested on a regular basis rather than all at once. In the same manner that consistently collecting profits smooths out price risk, investors can weather market fluctuations this way. You invest a specific amount throughout bull and down markets when you use a DCA strategy. Buying whenever the cryptocurrency market is down enables investors to get assets for a low price and sell them later for a profit. Cryptocurrency is a brand-new asset class that has generated a lot of buzz. However, the use of DCA can help to keep the hype under control. "DCA also helps to eliminate emotions from your new market positions and helps to overlook the short term in favor of long positions by acquiring your crypto over time."

## 5.5 Safety and security

Organizations that handle cryptocurrencies (or "cryptos") must guarantee that they are following the Cryptocurrency Security Standard and making efforts to safeguard transactions (CCSS). Let's take a look at a few critical (pun intended) areas to think about when it comes to safeguarding any information systems that hold, receive, or deal with cryptocurrencies like Litecoin, Bitcoin, and Ethereum.

### Wallet/Key Usage

It's also crucial to keep track of how your Bitcoin wallet and keys are being used. Risks including lost or stolen keys, as well as the unintended revelation of the wallet holder's identity, may be prevented by following best practices such as:

- Using keys/seeds exclusively in trustworthy contexts
- Verifying identities, references, and history of every key/seed holder
- Assigning duplicate keys to each wallet for recovery reasons

The company must be able to manage who has exposure to cryptographic data and who can act on it. Keyholders must go through extensive training in order to understand their duties and processes. You should have mechanisms in place to withdraw access when employees leave the organization, in addition to adequate onboarding. The Bitcoin information system may be made more secure by using "least privilege principles," which provide users just the access they need to execute their jobs and nothing more.

### Key Storage

An organization must preserve its cryptocurrency keys with the same care that it would protect the keys to a bank vault. Where applicable, they should be kept utilizing encryption, private keys, and physical locks. The backup cryptographic key should also be saved safely (on paper, digitally, or in another format) and protected from the elements.

### Other Considerations

It's also critical to implement a key compromise policy. Having a procedure in place that dictates what should be done if an encryption key and its holder is stolen may help to limit risk and losses.

In addition, a data sterilization policy is required. Because data may stay on electronic media even after erasure, you must ensure that your employees are aware of the hazards. By giving qualified staff access to technologies that execute safe data erasure on retired equipment like servers, hard disk drives, and portable storage, you can avoid information leakage.

Organizations dealing in cryptocurrencies must also maintain frequent proofs of reserve for compliance concerns. Audit trails are also an incredibly useful tool for determining how unanticipated security issues arose and swiftly resolving anomalies in order to restore consistency to the information system.

### Ongoing Assessment

Your organization's information system's builders and maintainers are undoubtedly technically adept, knowledgeable, and experienced. Even the finest cardiac doctor, however, would seek an objective diagnosis from another specialist. Bringing in an independent specialist to detect dangers and control problems might help you prevent Bitcoin system issues that are often ignored or underestimated by employees.
