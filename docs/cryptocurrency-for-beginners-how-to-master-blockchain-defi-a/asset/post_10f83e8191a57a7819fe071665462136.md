
## 1.6 Cryptocurrency in the Future

When it comes to cryptocurrencies, the globe is plainly split. On the one hand, proponents like Bill Gates, Al Gore, and Russell Brand argue that cryptocurrencies are superior to traditional currencies. On the other hand, there are some who are against it, such as Warren Buffett, Paul Krugman, and Robert Shiller. Krugman and Shiller, both Nobel Laureates in economics, see it as a Ponzi scam and a vehicle for criminal activity.

There will be a clash in the future between regulations and anonymity. Because a number of cryptocurrencies have been connected to terrorist activities, governments may wish to control how they operate. Cryptocurrencies, but on the other side, place a strong focus on ensuring that users stay anonymous.

According to futurists, cryptocurrencies will account for 25% of national currencies by 2030, implying that a large portion of the globe will begin to trust bitcoin as a method of trade. It will become more widely recognized by merchants and consumers, and it will retain its volatile character, implying that prices are expected to vary as they have over the previous several years.

## 1.7 Blockchain

Blockchain technology is a method of storing data in such a manner that it is hard or impossible to alter, hack, or defraud it. A blockchain is a digital log of transactions that is copied and distributed throughout the blockchain's complete network of computer systems. Each block on the chain comprises a number of trades, and each time a new action takes place on the database, a note of that event is added to the Ledger of each participant.

![](img/9e732599f379d4f1dd55a1fd64d01531_26_0.png)

Distributed Ledger is a decentralized database that is administered by various people. Blockchain is a sort of distributed ledger technology in which information is recorded using a hash, which is an immutable electronic signature.

### The Properties of Distributed Ledger Technology

### Secure

Each record is encrypted separately.

### Immutable

Any records that have been verified are irrevocable and cannot be modified.

### Programmable

A blockchain may be programmed (Smart contracts, etc.)

### Unanimous

The legitimacy of each record is agreed upon by all network participants.

### Anonymous

Participants' identities are either anonymous or pseudonymous.

### Timestamped

A block contains a transaction timestamp.

### Distributed

For total transparency, all network members get a shared ledger.

This implies that if a single block in a chain is modified, it will be instantly clear that the chain has been tampered with. Hackers would have to modify every block to the blockchain across all distributed copies of the chain if they intended to destroy a blockchain system.

Blockchains like Cryptocurrencies are continuously expanding as new blocks are added to the blockchain, increasing the integrity of the Ledger dramatically.

### The hype around blockchain technology

Many efforts to generate digital money have been made previously, but they have all failed.

The most pressing problem is one of trust. How can we believe that if someone invents new money termed the X dollar, they won't award themselves a million dollars or take your X amount of dollars?

Bitcoin was created to address this issue by using a blockchain, which is a form of a database. A person in control of most regular databases, such as a SQL database, may make changes to the data. Blockchain is unique in that no one is in control; instead, the individuals who utilize it run it. Bitcoins can’t be counterfeit, hacked, or double-spent, so those who hold them may be certain that they’re worth something.

## 1.8 Decentralization

The movement of power and choice from a centralized entity to a dispersed network is referred to as decentralization in the blockchain. Decentralized networks aim to limit the amount of confidence that members must put in one another and prevent them from exerting control or power over others in ways that harm the network’s performance.

### Why decentralization matters

The notion of decentralization is not new. Three basic network designs are often examined when developing a technological solution: centralized, distributed, and decentralized. While decentralized networks are often used in blockchain technology, a blockchain application cannot simply be classified as decentralized or not. Rather, decentralization should be extended to all components of a blockchain program on a sliding scale. Greater or fairer service may be accomplished by decentralizing resource utilization and access in an application. Decentralization has certain drawbacks, such as decreased transaction throughput, but the benefits of increased stability and levels of service outweigh the drawbacks.

### Benefits of decentralization

![](img/9e732599f379d4f1dd55a1fd64d01531_29_0.png)

- Optimizes Resource distribution

Decentralization may also aid in resource distribution optimization, ensuring that stated services are delivered with improved performance and reliability, as well as a lower risk of catastrophic failure.

- Improves data reconciliation

Companies often share information with their partners. This data is then modified and kept in each party's silos, only to be resurfaced when it's time to transfer it downstream. Each time data is converted, the possibility of data loss or inaccurate data entering the workstream increases. Every entity gets access to a true, shared view of information thanks to decentralized data storage.

- Provides a trustless environment

No one needs to recognize or trust anybody other in a decentralized public blockchain. In the format of a public ledger, each member of the network owns a duplicate of the same data. If a member's Ledger is tampered with or damaged in any manner, the rest of the network's members will reject it.

- Reduces points of weakness

Decentralization may help to mitigate sources of vulnerability in systems when single actors are too reliant. Systemic failures might result from these flaws, such as inability to deliver expected service or ineffective service owing to resource depletion, recurrent outages, bottlenecks, a lack of appropriate incentives for effective service, or corruption.

![](img/9e732599f379d4f1dd55a1fd64d01531_30_0.png)

### How decentralization compares

Wherever possible, decentralization should be used. Because it's a blockchain application, it doesn't have to be completely decentralized. Any blockchain solution's purpose is to provide what its customers need, which may or may not incorporate specific degrees of decentralization.

### Building blockchain applications leveraging the decentralization

Decentralization varies depending on the blockchain protocol, distributed application, Non-Governmental Organization, or any blockchain technology solution. The maturity of a solution, the time-proven dependability of its incentive systems and consensus procedures, and the capacity of the founders to strike the proper balance determine the adoption level. Many DAOs, for example, have components at various stages of decentralization: oracles may be partially decentralized, smart contracts may be fully centralized, and the governance framework for adjusting parameters may be community-driven and decentralized.

Decentralized cryptocurrency solutions are being researched and used by organizations of all types, sizes, and industries on a larger scale. Applications that offer quick international or emergency relief to persons in need without the involvement of a bank, government, or third-party agency are significant examples. Alternatively, programs that let users control their own user identity and data. Today, social media networks, businesses, and other groups sell this information for a profit, with no value to the user. It would be easier to make it egalitarian for everyone if it were decentralized.

To put it another way, blockchain decentralization is an information foundation that keeps a collection of materials and trades through a peer-to-peer network. It's possible that the "resource" isn't only cash or transaction records but also ownership, agreements, goods, and other information. Unlike previous peer-to-peer networks, a blockchain does not replicate the value that is sent. However, taking everything into account, it seems that a score has been transferred from one participant to the next.
