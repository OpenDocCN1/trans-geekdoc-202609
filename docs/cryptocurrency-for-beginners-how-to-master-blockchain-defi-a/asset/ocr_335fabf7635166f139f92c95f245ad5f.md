# Building blockchain applications leveraging the decentralization

Decentralization varies depending on the blockchain protocol, distributed
application, Non-Governmental Organization, or any blockchain technology
solution. The maturity of a solution, the time-proven dependability of its
incentive systems and consensus procedures, and the capacity of the
founders to strike the proper balance determine the adoption level. Many
DAOs, for example, have components at various stages of decentralization:
oracles may be partially decentralized, smart contracts may be fully
centralized, and the governance framework for adjusting parameters may be
community-driven and decentralized.

Decentralized cryptocurrency solutions are being researched and used by
organizations of all types, sizes, and industries on a larger scale.
Applications that offer quick international or emergency relief to persons in
need without the involvement of a bank, government, or third-party agency
are significant examples. Alternatively, programs that let users control their
own user identity and data. Today, social media networks, businesses, and
other groups sell this information for a profit, with no value to the user. It
would be easier to make it egalitarian for everyone if it were decentralized.

To put it another way, blockchain decentralization is an information
foundation that keeps a collection of materials and trades through a peer-to-
peer network. It's possible that the "resource" isn't only cash or transaction
records but also ownership, agreements, goods, and other information.
Unlike previous peer-to-peer networks, a blockchain does not replicate the
value that is sent. However, taking everything into account, it seems that a
score has been transferred from one participant to the next.
