# 3.5.4 Flooz

![](bbox=[0.215, 0.122, 0.78, 0.345])

Flooz.com was a poster child for the late-nineties tech boom, which pertered out by 2001. Flooz was compared to coupons or airline miles that could be accumulated through promotional campaigns or purchased directly from Flooz's central platform as a digital tool for merchants accepting payment on the still relatively young Internet. Flooz raised up to $35 million in venture capital over the course of its three years in business, betting on merchant demand for new and exciting payment mechanisms. Flooz spent no money on its advertising campaign, even enlisting the help of comedienne Whoopi Goldberg for a series of TV commercials. Despite the momentum, Flooz reportedly ended in failure to attract a large number of users and merchants, and the company's platform's criminal activities would ultimately prove too much for it. By August 2001, the company's demise had been hastened by rapidly declining investor support as well as allegations of criminal activity on its platform. According to a 2001 New York Times report, Russian criminal groups are suspected of using stolen credit cards to buy online currency and launder illicit funds, prompting a federal investigation. An approximate $300,000 was stolen, according to a Flooz source. Shortly after the fraud allegations surfaced, Flooz shut down after failing to find a buyer.
