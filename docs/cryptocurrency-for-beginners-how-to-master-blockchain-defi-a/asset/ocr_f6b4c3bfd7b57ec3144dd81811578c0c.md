Hash-cash was a forerunner to digital money. It was Satoshi's inspiration for Bitcoin's proof-of-work implementation. We must first deal with Hash-cash in order to comprehend Bitcoin's consensus mechanism and proof-of-work. Adam Back created hash-cash in 1997 as a way of preventing email spam. Email spam was an annoyance to anyone with an email account, even in 1997. This was due to a fundamental problem, according to Adam Back: all you had to do to email someone was send a few packets to a mail server. They made the decision to charge for emails. Sending an email was so cheap that it was practically free. Even though honest users only send a few emails per day, spammers can send tens of millions at little or no cost. Consider how much it would cost to send an email for a penny. This is fine for a regular user who has a few pennies to spare each day. Spammers, on the other hand, would have to pay millions of pennies, effectively ending their spam campaigns. You'll notice that this is a little backward: charging a penny per email presupposes the existence of digital pennies with which to pay.
