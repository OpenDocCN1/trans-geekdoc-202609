# What else do you need to know?

The most crucial thing to understand about cryptography is that it boils down to having both secret keys. A public key is similar to an email address in that it is shared in order to transmit and receive payments. You should not ever reveal your private key, just as you would never disclose your email password. This is because your private key is what lets you really access your money.

It's hazardous to put your money on a cryptocurrency exchange. You're basically handing over your secret key to the market, and if something goes wrong, you'll be waving them goodbye (here is where the unregulated bit comes in). Transferring your assets to a "wallet" - where you can keep and control your crypto – is a better approach from a security sense. To put it another way, a wallet allows you to retain that secret key in your possession for safekeeping. Wallets also exist in a range of designs and sizes, with some supporting just one cryptocurrency while others are supporting multiple, as well as a number of additional services.
