While the tokens themselves operate on a decentralized network, Tether Ltd, based in Hong Kong, is solely responsible for the creation and redemption of tokens, as well as the 1:1 deposit backing. USDT has been primarily used by exchanges as a substitute for fiat currencies, reducing or eliminating the need for external banking relationships. Tether has always been the subject of controversy since its inception in 2014, owing to the company's failure to provide audited financial statements demonstrating adequate reserves to back USDT. Tether has a history of not disclosing its banking relationships, only doing so recently in November 2018 to quell concerns about its solvency raised by Bitfinex, an associated crypto exchange. Tether updated its disclosure statement in March 2019, claiming that its tokens are no longer fully backed by US dollar deposits. Tether is now 100 percent backed by reserves, which include traditional currency as well as cash equivalents, as well as other assets and receivables from Tether's loans to third parties, including affiliated entities, from time to time.
