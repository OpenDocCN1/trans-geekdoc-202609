## Proof of Work

"Consensus protocol" is a way of confirming activities on a blockchain whereby an algorithm generates a mathematical puzzle for computers to solve. Every participating machine, known as a "miner," solves a mathematical challenge that aids in verifying a set of transactions known as a block, which is subsequently added to the blockchain. The first system to do so effectively gets awarded with a modest sum of bitcoin for its efforts.

The competition to resolve blockchain problems may need significant computer processing power and energy. In reality, this implies that after factoring in the expenses of electricity and computer resources, miners may struggle to break even with the cryptocurrency they get for confirming transactions.

## How Can You Mine Cryptocurrency?

Mining refers to the process of releasing new units of bitcoin into the world in return for confirming transactions. While it is theoretically conceivable for the common individual to mine cryptocurrencies, it is becoming more difficult with proof-of-work platforms like Bitcoin. Even as the Bitcoin community expands, it becomes more complex, requiring more processing power. It used to be possible for the typical customer to do just that, but it’s now prohibitively costly. There are just too many individuals who have optimized their equipment and technologies to outcompete.

Understand that mining Proof of Work cryptocurrency takes a lot of energy. According to estimates, Bitcoin farms use 0.21 percent of the world’s energy. This is almost the same quantity of energy that Switzerland consumes in a year. According to estimates, most Bitcoin miners use 60 percent to 80 percent of their earnings to pay power expenses.

Although mining cryptocurrency in a proof-of-work system is unfeasible for the common individual, the proof of stake model needs less high-powered computation since validators are picked randomly depending on the level people stake. However, it does need the possession of a cryptocurrency to participate. If you don’t have any crypto, you won’t stake anything.

## 1.4 What is Cryptography?

Cryptography is a means of protecting communications in the face of third parties with malicious intent—that is, third parties that intend to steal your information or overhear your conversation—by using encryption and decryption. Among the computational algorithms that cryptography employs are SHA-256, which would be the hashing method that Bitcoin employs; a public key, which is similar to digital information of the user that is meant to share with everybody; and a secret key, which is similar to electronic signatures of the user that is kept private.

## Bitcoin Transactions and Cryptography

A typical bitcoin transaction details include who you want to transfer bitcoin to and how many bitcoin you want to send. Following that, the data is processed via a hashing method. The SHA-256 algorithm is used by Bitcoin, as previously stated. The output is subsequently signed using the user's private key, which allows the user to be uniquely identified. The cryptographically signed output is subsequently sent around the network for verification by other users. The sender's public key is used to do this.

Miners are users who examine a transaction to see if it is genuine or not. The transaction, along with numerous others, is then uploaded to the network, where the data can no longer be modified.

## 1.5 Ethereum vs. Bitcoin

You now realize that Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that runs on blockchain technology and employs an end-to-end network to conduct transactions. The Ethereum network accepts Ether, which is another prominent digital money. The Ethereum network is an accessible platform for developing and deploying decentralized applications based on blockchain technology.

## Differences

Bitcoin is a digital currency that may transmit money to other people. It functions in a manner that is quite similar to that of real-world cash. Within the Ethereum platform, Ether is utilized as a currency, but it may also be used for real-world transactions. Bitcoin transactions are done mechanically, which means you must complete them yourself when you want them completed. With Ether, you can perform transactions manually or automatically—they're programmed, which means they'll only happen if specific criteria are satisfied. A bitcoin transaction takes roughly 10 minutes to complete, which is the time it takes for a block to also be digitally signed. It takes roughly 20 seconds to complete a transaction using Ether.

The number of bitcoins that may exist is limited to 21 million. By the year 2140, this figure is expected to be attained. Ether is anticipated to last a long time and be limited to 100 million units. Bitcoin is used for goods or services exchanges, whereas Ether uses blockchain technology to build a record that triggers a transaction when a specific condition is satisfied. Finally, the SHA-256 algorithm is used by Bitcoin, whereas the ethash method is used by Ethereum. 1 bitcoin is worth USD 8741.81 since May 2020, while 1 ether is worth $190.00.

## Similarities

At the moment, bitcoins and Ethereum are the most popular and valued cryptocurrencies. Both employ blockchain, in which events are put to a container known as a block, and a series of blocks are formed in which information cannot be changed. The money is mined via a mechanism known as proof of work, which involves solving a mathematical challenge before a block can be added to the network. Finally, bitcoins and Ether are both extensively utilized worldwide.
