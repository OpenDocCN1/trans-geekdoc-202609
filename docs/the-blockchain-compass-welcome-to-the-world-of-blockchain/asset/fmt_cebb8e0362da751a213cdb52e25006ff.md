
# Blockchain Solutions for the Pharma Industry

According to estimates from WHO, one in ten medical products (diagnostic kits, vaccines and pills) are either falsified or substandard in low- and middle-income nations of the world. In some locations, the challenge is that of theft and as disclosed by the BSI Group, the national standards body of the UK estimates that thefts involving pharmaceutical cargo are more than $1 billion annually. The UK and US actually account for about half of all the theft recorded. This situation may eventually be some kind of practice run for the health care supply chains that may likely face even more concentrated pressure. This explains why the industry is focusing more on distributed ledger technology.

## Supply Chain Issues in Pharma Industry

Some experts in the healthcare industry believe that maintaining the supply chain is one of the biggest obstacles facing the pharmaceutical industry. It is a very complex and risky process. One of the issues linked to supply chain is falsified drugs. When someone goes to a pharmacist to obtain a prescription, they assume that the medicine is of the highest standard, stored appropriately in the right conditions, authentic and was never tampered with. Unfortunately, there are several cases where falsified drugs entered the supply chain and this is a serious threat to the health of the users. Just to make things clearer, a falsified medicine includes:

*   Medications with the wrong ingredients
*   Drugs that were stolen
*   Medicines with little or no active ingredient
*   Tampered drug
*   Medicines with fake packaging.

The big question here is how can patients or users determine if the medicines they are buying were stored in the appropriate conditions while they were moved to a pharmacy? How can users determine if the medicines contain the right strength and quantity of the medicine? Based on the existing structures, all that a pharmacist needs to do is to depend on the various stakeholders like the manufacturer, wholesaler and courier to put in place the correct procedures.

When the drugs finally arrive at their destination (a pharmacy or hospital), the pharmacist must also make sure that the medicines are truly the appropriate standard before releasing them to patients. You will agree with me that the entire journey is not transparent enough because a pharmacist may need to go through an audit trail to ensure the quality and standard of the medicines and this takes quite some time. There are several parties in the pharmacy supply chain and just one of them can significantly influence the quality of the final product.

For instance, the manufacturers need to ensure that each tablet contains the right formulation, strength and was properly stored. Manufacturers must also ensure that the drugs are suitable for human consumption. Wholesalers, logistics companies or suppliers also play a role in the entire process as they must ensure that the quality of the medicines is not affected by things like humidity or temperature. They must also prevent criminals from intercepting or tampering with the medicines and that they are not stolen by criminals.

These are challenges that blockchain technology can easily resolve. ### Data Disparity in Pharma Industry

The development stage of the pharma industry is also the core of the industry since it is the stage where companies require data for experiments. This will help them to understand and come up with new medicines that will help cure various diseases. But the challenge most pharmaceutical companies experience is data disparity as they attempt to solve a specific disease. The cause of data disparity emanates from the silos of data that have been stored over time which functions by linking other platforms.

The reason why data disparity has become a major issue for pharmaceutical companies is that they have to depend on various sources for data and they are unable to properly verify the uploaded data that different sources share. Among the factors that lead to data disparity is the different structure and models adopted by various data sources and departments.

### Blockchain Solution in Pharmaceutical Supply Chain

Blockchain technology can transform the entire pharmaceutical supply chain in multiple ways. Since there are different stages and processes involved in the industry, it is possible to scan barcodes at all stages of the process and the details would be stored in a distributed ledger. The distributed ledger like blockchain will record and create an audit trail of the entire journey of a drug. It is also possible to add sensors to the supply chain to help record temperature or humidity in the digital ledger. This aspect is extremely vital for medicines that need a specific temperature for storage in the fridge like insulin or other specially produced drugs.

One of the projects that is working on ways to leverage blockchain in making medical supply chain more efficient is MediLedger. One of the merits of blockchain-based platforms is that competitors can easily collaborate on the same platform to ensure the realization of a common goal such as ensuring the safety of drugs or eliminating counterfeit drugs. They can comfortably collaborate without having to share sensitive information.

The MediLedger Network is a consortium that is backed by more than 20 organizations including Genentech, Gilead, AmerisourceBergen, Pfizer, McKesson, and Amgen. The underlying technology behind the project is a startup called Chronicled and the focus of the consortium is on pharma supply chains. MediLedger's first major solution is a verification system that simplifies the process of verifying the authenticity of a verified drug. This process is often a very difficult one especially when we consider that over 60 million units of saleable medicines are returned each year as revealed by Healthcare Distribution Alliance.

Before returned drugs are resold to wholesalers, they must be verified as authentic and the existing process requires wholesalers to contact manufacturers to assist them in tracking down the drug's serial number. This process could require as much as 48 hours before it can be completed. By leveraging blockchain it would be possible for wholesale distributors to carry out the same verification exercise in less than a second simply by making use of a barcode scanner.

This will in turn qualify the drug for commercial distribution immediately while all manufacturers maintain control over their data. Hospitals and pharmacists can also make use of this rapid serial number verification. All that a staff of a hospital or pharmacy needs to verify the authenticity of a drug while placing it on a shelf is a barcode scanner and web browser. They do not require any other sophisticated or expensive infrastructure.
