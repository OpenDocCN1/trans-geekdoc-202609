
## 2. Blockchain and ATMs

Another interesting solution for the remittance industry is the combination of blockchain technology with ATMs. This combination can render effective solutions for individuals sending and receiving money around the world. With this solution, the influence of intermediaries as well as central agencies will be reduced or even eliminated and this will lower the turnaround time required for settlements.

## 3. Mobile Wallets

Crypto wallets are increasingly becoming popular and the interesting thing is that they are blockchain-based. Mobile crypto wallets provide several functionalities once they have been installed on a smartphone. Anyone can transfer funds internationally and some of these wallets allow a fast exchange between digital currencies and fiat money. The recipients of funds sent via mobile wallet do not need a bank account since the funds are sent from one wallet to another. They simply require a smartphone, internet access and also a compatible mobile wallet. Examples of some common mobile wallets include Luno, Coinomi, etc.

Undoubtedly, remittances remain a crucial source of funds for millions of individuals in developing nations. Although remittances can potentially contribute toward development in these countries, high cost is its greatest challenge. The emergence of smartphones as well as the creation of new business models have provided better alternatives to the existing channels. But cryptocurrency as well as the underlying distributed ledger technology offers peer-to-peer transactions which results in more benefits and faster ways of sending and receiving funds from any location around the globe.

## Banking and Lending

The industry that is highly prone to attacks is the banking industry. This is why many banks invest huge sums of money to secure their operations. It is possible to eliminate the majority of the threats facing the banks or the risks of fraud in all aspects of banking which can even apply to a trading platform. Other issues that a DLT-based banking system can resolve include administrative cost and operational risks since it can render the system immutable and transparent.

Remember, one of the features of blockchain technology is traceability as well as a permanent historical record of every single transaction on the blockchain. This implies that blockchain can back up all transactions that take place in the bank. Generally, the core aspects of banking include mortgage, transactions, payment services and loans. But as we all know, most of these services are executed with traditional centralized systems.

This also explains why the services are often slow and wasteful since there are so many intermediaries in the operations of banks. A good example is the process of information verification, proper credit scoring, processing of loans and the release of funds which could take between 30 to 60 days before someone can successfully secure a mortgage. In fact, for small or medium enterprises to obtain a business loan, it could take between 60 to 90 days. There are key aspects of the banking industry that DLT can help resolve and we shall explore them now.

### Fraud Prevention

Among the leading challenges facing the banking industry is that of the increasing rate of fraud and cyber-attacks. Globally, when it comes to the costs associated with cybercrime, the industry with the highest average cost in 2018 was the banking industry. In 2017, cybercrime cost the industry about $16.7 million and this figure increased to $18.4 million in 2018. Just like other centralized systems, bank ledgers are stored in centralized servers and this implies that they are more prone to cyberattacks. All the vital banking information are stored in a single location which creates a single point of failure. The increasing skills of hackers and cybercriminals enable them to bypass most of these security systems and engage in fraud and data breaches. Of course, this issue is not only being experienced by the banking industry as other industries such as the oil and gas and even meat companies have been affected just like the recent one that took place in the United States.

Blockchain offers unique solutions to the issue of fraud, thanks to its decentralized structure. The decentralized architecture of blockchain makes it less susceptible to fraud. It offers complete transparency and real-time execution of payments. Organizations can lower their risk as well as administrative burden, save money and time simply by making use of blockchain.

### KYC

Just like other use cases, blockchain can also help to solve the issue of delays in banking transactions. One of the aspects of banking transactions that involves a lot of duplication of effort between banks and intermediaries is the KYC process. Despite the high compliance costs, banks that fail to properly adhere to the KYC guidelines are faced with large penalties. Data from a recent Thomas Reuters Survey indicates that banks spend as much as £40 million annually just to comply with KYC guidelines. With blockchain, it is possible to store KYC data on the blockchain and banks can even leverage digital identity solutions that are also emerging to deal with KYC compliance issues.

SWIFT has actually created a KYC Registry and a good number of banks (about 1,125 banks) now share KYC documentation but there is still so much to do since this represents just 16 percent of the 7,000 banks on their network. While there are some challenges with security and privacy of KYC details, if the information is stored on a private blockchain, then issues of privacy and security will be drastically reduced.

### How Blockchain is Disrupting Lending

There has been a spike in the number of loans underwritten by alternative lending platforms. Records show that consumer lending by the fintech consumer lending platform, Experian has more than doubled within four years. In 2015, it grew from 22.4 percent share of personal loan originations to 49.4 percent in 2019. The truth is that banks are not just the only firms losing grounds here; traditional finance companies and credit unions are also affected as they continuously cede market share too.

The main reason for this new trend is the ability of new industry entrants to innovate courtesy of their smart approaches as well as the less complicated regulatory restrictions they encounter. These new players in the lending space are taking advantage of blockchain and data analytics to make their products more transparent, faster, more efficient and easier to access. If banks must remain relevant, then they need to create new ways to compete, especially as non-bank lending platforms are increasingly becoming popular.

More blockchain-powered platforms are emerging while fintechs are providing more diverse products. Interestingly many banks are now increasing their digital capabilities in response to the threats from fintech and decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms. However, their efforts are still insufficient as banks need to aggressively lower their operating costs, embrace partnerships and completely overhaul their lending processes to reflect new technological developments.
