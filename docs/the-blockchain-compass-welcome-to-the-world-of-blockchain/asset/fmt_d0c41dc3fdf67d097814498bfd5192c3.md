## Payment and Remittances

![Image](Image00038.jpg)

In August 2019, the World Bank came out with a press release that global remittances from low-and middle-income countries increased to $529 billion. This indicates an increase of 9.6 percent compared to its previous record high of $483 billion back in 2017. Also, the total global remittances that include both low-and middle-income countries and high-income countries jumped from $633 billion to $689 billion. The Bank disclosed that the most expensive channels of remittances were banks as they charged fees that were as high as 11 percent averagely within the first quarter of 2019. Records also show that the next most expensive were post offices as they cost over 7 percent.

The consulting firm, KPMG in its 2019 Global Payments report, estimated that the size of B2B cross-border payments would be as much as $15.5 trillion in 2018. The statistics is simply a strong indication of the size of the international payment sector as well as how it affects businesses, people and the countries where the funds are received. In fact, the World Bank predicted that remittances are on course to becoming the largest means of external financing, especially in developing nations.

This also means that it can influence the growth and development of these countries. This also explains why the United Nation’s Sustainable Development Goals target for 2030 is to drastically lower costs associated with remittances made by immigrants which is currently an average of 7.1 percent to as low as 3 percent.

![Image](Image00039.jpg)

Apart from the high fees, majority of remittance firms depend on the services of third-party services as well as financial institutions. What really renders the present system grossly inefficient is the need for the services of several intermediaries. These services are not only expensive but it often takes time as money transfers could take days and in some cases weeks. Distributed ledger technology can provide the remittance industry with more efficient alternatives. One other issue with the current remittance industry has to do with the recipients of the funds.

Many of these recipients live in developing countries and they do not have access to a bank account. Unfortunately, most remittances are sent through formal banking systems. Even when these individuals have a bank account, they may need to travel long distances to collect their money from the nearest branch of the bank. Some lack proper means of identification which might be required for banking services too. This is why many remittances are carried out via informal means.

This situation is fast changing with the emergence of blockchain technology and digital wallets. Blockchain has the potential to completely transform the existing global remittance channels. With mobile wallets, anyone with a smartphone and access to the internet can enjoy several functionalities and this includes the ability to transfer funds internationally.

More blockchain-based remittance projects are emerging in the crypto space and their focus is on simplifying the whole process. They are eliminating the need for unnecessary third-party services to create frictionless and almost instant payment services. The blockchain network does not depend on a slow transaction approval process like traditional services that often pass through different mediators with so much manual work involved.

Blockchain solutions can execute financial transactions in any location by making use of a distributed network of nodes. The traditional banking system is not as fast as blockchain technology because the technology can enable more reliable and faster payment solutions at a significantly reduced cost. Undoubtedly, blockchain has the right solutions to the major challenges facing the remittance industry – long transaction time and high fees.
