## Credential Notarization

You can see this as putting seals on your ID. It is possible for an identity blockchain platform to put a hash address to your data on the digital identity blockchain platform. This does not also imply that what will be stored on the ledger will only be your credentials. Instead, the hash address will serve as a timestamp and provide some kind of electronic seal. So, an institution can choose to provide the hash for a degree on the platform whenever a student graduates.

With the hash provided, a student will be aware of when the degree was issued as well as proof of his degree certificate. Whenever the graduate now wants to work with the degree certificate, he can easily prove the authenticity of the qualification courtesy of the hash provided by the university. This is an excellent way to transform the way we handle certification from a respected university. It also helps to eliminate the issue of falsified certificates that many organizations face whenever they are hiring a new worker.

## Control of Consent and Access Rights

A digital identity blockchain platform can also help to control access to your credentials. There are cases where you would prefer sharing your personal information on different platforms only for a limited time. You can grant an organization you want to work with temporary access just to confirm whether you truly have the notarized credentials or not. To achieve this, you can make it a kind of transaction that has an expiry date. Once the access expires, then things would be back the way they were before and they would lose access to the information. On the part of the company or organization that was temporarily given access to your credentials, they would be required to delete the information and provide a piece of evidence that they did so on the platform.

### Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI)

![Image](Image00053.jpg)

It may sound a little bit tricky to explain the meaning of SSI. What it means, in reality, is that a person would be the sole owner of the analog and digital versions of their identity. This also implies that an organization can enjoy this kind of privilege too. Every user that has a Self-Sovereign Identity has the power to determine who would have access to their data. They can verify their identities online using SSI and are also responsible for maintaining it. Considering the nature of this kind of identity, the most suitable technology for it would be distributed ledger technology.

This is because blockchain's decentralized nature ensures the absence of any third party. But for the system to function fully and successfully, it would require several principles. Blockchain-enabled Self-Sovereign Identity platforms can function alongside GDPR Law by using these guiding principles. Every platform will have to offer ten basic principles of SSI. So, what are the principles?

1.  **Existence:** Users on the platform should have an independent existence and should also be in digital form too.
2.  **Access:** Every Self-Sovereign Identity should offer users fill access. This implies that users on the platform can access their information and also access other claims without depending on any third parties. However, since the data has signed verification that cannot be modified, users may not be able to modify all the data.
3.  **Control:** This principle means that users are in complete control of their identities. There is another angle to the principle of control since there are several instances where users may not have complete ownership of the Self-Sovereign Identity. However, the user still gets the chance to limit how their identity is handled by other parties in such instances.
4.  **Transparency:** There is a need for transparency for all algorithms or systems handling the identities. It would be impossible for users to ensure that the Self-Sovereign Identity platform is properly managing their identities without an open system.
5.  **Portability:** It should be easy for users on the platform to transport their identity anytime they need it. What this feature implies, in reality, is that users can easily export their identities and also make use of them in different scenarios.
6.  **Persistence:** A good Self-Sovereign Identity platform will ensure the longevity of all identities. So, in the event that the system is going through an upgrade, users on the platform can still maintain their previous data just as they were before the upgrade.
7.  **Interoperability:** The absence of excellent interoperability eliminates the flexibility of identities. So, for a Self-Sovereign Identity platform to be effective, then it should be acceptable in any location.
8.  **Minimization:** This principle means that as much as possible, users' identities should not be completely exposed whenever third parties want to access them. For instance, when a third party requires access to a user's age, all the system should disclose to that particular user is the age and not the user's exact birth date. There is really no need to overexpose the identity of a user.
9.  **Consent:** Third parties cannot access the information of users regardless of the reasons. In a situation where a third party wants to access the information of any user, the user must grant the third-party full consent. Without obtaining the permission of a user, no one can access the user's identity.
10. **Protection:** Users should have full rights to their Self-Sovereign Identity regardless of what happens. This means that all SSI platforms must maintain this standard no matter the pressure.

## Five Pillars of Every Blockchain-Based Decentralized Identity Solution

The current estimate for the identification of data breaches is about seven months. But with the inherent characteristics of blockchain which ensures transparency, trust and user control, many organizations and governments are increasingly exploring its potentials. However, every blockchain-based solution for decentralized digital identity should possess five pillars and these are the things that ensure the viability of such solutions.

![Image](Image00054.jpg)

1.  **Privacy:** The combination of blockchain encryption with digital signature guarantees “Privacy by Design” courtesy of pseudonymization. By adding the digital signature to every single transaction that a user makes, transactions will also be foolproof.
2.  **Simplicity:** It should be easy for users on the platform to transport their identity anytime they need it. What this feature implies, in reality, is that users can easily export their identities and also make use of them in different scenarios.
3.  **Security:** Users should have full rights on the Self-Sovereign Identity platforms regardless of what happens. This means that all SSI platforms must maintain this standard no matter the pressure.
4.  **Integrity:** When it comes to integrity, then unlike traditional identity systems, the decentralized digital identity should provide users the ability to maintain their identity on various nodes in the network. There should not be a single point of failure which ensures that hackers cannot easily break the integrity of the data.
