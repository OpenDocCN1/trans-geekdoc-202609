
# BaaS (Blockchain as a Service) Overview

A straightforward definition of BaaS is that it is a cloud-based service that allows users to create their digital products while leveraging blockchain technology. You can also see BaaS as a distributed ledger version of Software-as-a-service (SaaS) where businesses around the world subscribe to this service and gain access to cloud-based software. Examples of some of the digital products include decentralized applications (dApps) as well as other services that function without the need for setup demands of a complete blockchain-based platform.

## Details of How BaaS Works

It involves a process where a third-party company installs, hosts and even maintains blockchain networks for other users. This third party that is also regarded as an external service provider decides to establish all the required blockchain technology as well as infrastructure and in return charges a fee. The external service provider will handle maintenance and infrastructure issues.

The truth is that the role of blockchain-as-a-service providers is just like a web hosting provider. They provide a platform where customers can take advantage of their cloud-based solutions. Also, BaaS assists various organizations in developing and hosting blockchain apps as well as smart contracts in a blockchain platform that cloud-based service providers manage and administer. They offer support services such as suitable resource allocation, bandwidth management, data security features and hosting requirements.

The advantage of this service is that enterprises do not need to bother about the daily complexities of managing and operating a blockchain. Instead, they can now focus mainly on their core business activities while BaaS providers handle the smooth functioning of the underlying technology.

## The Importance of Blockchain as a Service

There is a growing interest by businesses to leverage blockchain technology, however, most businesses are faced with the operational overhead and technical complexities involved in creating, configuring and even operating a blockchain. Also, the maintenance of the blockchain infrastructure is usually a major barrier. In terms of setting up infrastructure and maintaining it, blockchain demands huge investment and when compared to other traditional databases, it is often more resource-intensive.

One more point I should add here is that blockchain requires huge bandwidth and a lot of energy. So, BaaS has so much to offer businesses around the world and has recently been gaining remarkable traction for many reasons. For most organizations, pairing cloud services with BaaS may turn out to be very valuable. Businesses can deal with most of their pain points by tailoring integrations courtesy of the personalized flexibility of BaaS. The BaaS solution can help them resolve complex challenges around cost, efficiency and transparency in a straightforward way.

This will significantly lower the barriers to entry for enterprise blockchain applications. Companies can easily leverage all the merits of blockchain technology – accountability, minimization of trust, improved transparency and data security. They will enjoy these benefits without the need to develop their blockchain ecosystem. In fact, they do not even have to invest in the required computing resources. ## A Surge in Demand for Blockchain Experts

Distributed ledger technology is still a new technology and there is a need for more enlightenment and education in this field. Blockchain is already spreading to different industries and more use cases are emerging every day. This also means that there would be a need for more individuals to learn about the technology.

Of course, the number of blockchain experts is on the increase, but blockchain implementation is growing rapidly and this will lead to a sharp rise in the demand for blockchain experts. Many universities and colleges are making more efforts to catch up with this need. Also, organizations around the world are not left out as they are making efforts to develop their existing talents through training programs specifically meant for the development and management of blockchain platforms.

However, the rate at which students with the right skills are graduating is insufficient to meet the demand. Companies like IBM have shown their concern over the inadequate number of blockchain talents and even referred to the situation as a "significant inhibitor" to the adoption of blockchain technology by existing companies. Coin Rivert, in 2018 revealed that there was a 2,000 percent year-on-year growth in the demand for blockchain experts and this data was obtained from Upwork.com.

## Government Regulation in a World that is Increasingly Getting Decentralized

A look at the architecture of blockchain technology will undoubtedly explain the reasons for its inherent challenges to legacy institutions and traditional regulation and governance approaches. We have seen from our discussion so far that there is a remarkable increase in enterprise blockchains. This has so far allowed for traditional governance and regulation mechanisms.

However, we will likely experience more challenges with an increase in the use of permissionless or public distributed ledger technology. It is impossible to regulate blockchains and trying to regulate them is like attempting to regulate all ledgers people have on their system – both online and offline. However, the underlying use cases of blockchain technology will certainly be regulated. Governments around the world will do this in collaboration with different regulators from several jurisdictions and others involved.

Although there is presently no precise combination of governance mechanisms for blockchain technology, when one is available, it is most likely to involve the combination of "regulatory levers" that may be pulled to identify the appropriate balance.

The regulation of blockchain faces some key dilemmas. For instance, we all know that regulation is essential to help protect users while ensuring security. However, when there is excessive regulation, it may eventually stifle innovation, especially in the financial sector. This also explains why the current dialogue on blockchain regulation should be sustained to ensure proper resolution to every aspect of its governance, especially when it has to do with privacy and security. States that desire to adopt decentralized business models may carry out several modifications to their existing legal frameworks.
