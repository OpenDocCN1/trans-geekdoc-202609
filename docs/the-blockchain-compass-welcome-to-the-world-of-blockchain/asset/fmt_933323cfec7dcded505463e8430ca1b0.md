
# Integration of Blockchain and Other Emerging Technologies

Interestingly, the core values of blockchain are in perfect alignment with the demands of present verification and authentication solutions for goods – transparency, immutability and security. The ability of DLTs to facilitate speedy transit and trust is driving the increased interest of many organizations in these technologies. When we consider the distance that many COVID-19 vaccines need to travel before getting to the point where they will be used, the number of handoffs that will take place between the point of manufacture to the point where the drugs will be administered, then you will discover that blockchain solutions can significantly help to not only increase accountability but also make the authentication process easy.

Blockchain technology will ensure real-time visibility of the distribution of vaccines as well as the chain of custody starting from the point where the vaccines were manufactured to where they will be administered to people. This means that blind spots across private and public entities will be eradicated. Also, it will significantly reduce risk via preemptive detection and providing real-time notification of disruptions in supply chain, cases of adverse reaction, spoilage, fraud and any unplanned events. Blockchain technology can also help with the monitoring of the storage conditions and recall traceability which will further increase the efficacy of the vaccines and enhance trust among all stakeholders.

### Integration of Blockchain and Internet of Things (IoT)

Among the top new technologies that will transform the way we handle data and function in a digital world include the Internet of Things (IoT), Artificial Intelligence and of course, blockchain technology. They are all powerful technologies on their own, but enterprises can actually leverage the combination of some of these technologies like IoT and blockchain in an incredible way. Some experts call this the Blockchain of Things (BIoT). Available data from a study carried out in 2018 by Aftrex Market Research discovered that by 2026, the combination of the IoT and blockchain market will reach $254.31 billion.

Several industries and businesses such as telecommunications, healthcare, automotive, oil & gas and manufacturing will enjoy remarkable benefits from the combination of IoT and blockchain technology. The findings of the study suggest that combining both technologies is revolutionary and many enterprises are already getting involved. As the blockchain space matures, we are going to see more robust feature sets and numerous use case studies that will boost the confidence of organizations to get involved. Undoubtedly, the combination of IoT and blockchain will provide several benefits but I will be focusing on the most remarkable ones.

#### Lower Operational Costs

This is one of the most popular benefits that the combination holds for most enterprises – a reduction in operational costs. Since it enables the transmission of data on a peer-to-peer basis, there is no need for central control and this helps cut down on the cost of doing business. Decentralization offers us a less expensive way to deal with the scale of IoT while eradicating the issue of a single point of failure.

#### Helps to Streamline Accounting

One of the first departments of an organization that could immediately begin to enjoy the benefits of transparency offered by the combination of IoT and blockchain is the accounting department. Being aware of what all stakeholders are sharing and sending across a timestamped and linear chain is an excellent solution for businesses. For accounting purposes, having records that cannot be altered by anyone is a remarkable benefit for companies.

#### Accelerated Data Change

According to the findings made by Aftrex Market Research, accelerated data change is among the leading benefits of combining the two technologies. There is a need for a more enterprise-grade approach that could involve a permission-based blockchain to help deal with the number of IoT devices, the amount of data involved as well as the speed of transaction between all parties involved. This implies that a blockchain that is capable of minimizing the time required to handle transactions – courtesy of trusted nodes – is extremely useful to help meet the performance needs of IoT. The blockchain should have the capacity to handle IoT data exchange speed.

#### Improved Security

The IoT tech market will increasingly focus on issues of security since there is likely going to be more complex security issues that will arise. The complexities of the security challenges will emanate from the distributed and diverse nature of IoT technology. Already, we now have over 26 billion internet-connected devices. What this means is that there will be an increase in the rate of hacking as this number continues to rise. To stop malicious actors from performing their evil activities, IoT will need a structure that is different from its existing centralized architecture which happens to be among the greatest vulnerabilities of IoT networks.

One of the innate features of blockchain is security and this will be very useful considering the increased number of devices within IoT. Combining these technologies could foster secure communications while strengthening privacy agreements. When it comes to security, it does not just involve devices; it also involves human-to-device, device-to-device and human-to-human interactions.

Having a trusted distributed ledger that will transparently reveal those with access, transactions, as well as records, is a major benefit of blockchain integration with IoT. Most enterprises that have already implemented a combination of IoT and blockchain usually depend on security techniques like device authentication but when it comes to issues of security of IoT and blockchain, this is simply the beginning.
