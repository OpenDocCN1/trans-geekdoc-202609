
## PwC Report on Blockchain's Economic Potential

Also, the PwC report ascertained blockchain technology's potential to create remarkable value across various industries – public services, governments, logistics, manufacturing, retail and finance. As noted by the report, blockchain has always been seen as useful for digital currencies like Ethereum, but the technology has much more to offer, especially in the way private and public organizations share, use and secure data.

While various organizations deal with the impact of the global COVID-19 pandemic, a significant number of disruptive trends have witnessed a boost. This analysis is very crucial since it revealed blockchain's potential to help industries and organizations to rebuild and even reconfigure their operations marked by tremendous enhancements in transparency, efficiency and trust across society and industries.

### The Top Five Sectors to Enjoy Blockchain Application

![Image](Image00046.jpg)

Source: PwC

Part of the report leveraged economic analysis as well as industry research to assess five major application areas where blockchain has the potential to provide economic value. Based on their analysis, 2025 is believed to be a tipping point as it is expected that digital ledger technology will be adopted to a greater extent across the global economy.

- **Supply Chain Management**: Top on the list is supply chain management which we have earlier discussed. The sector has the greatest economic potential of about $962 billion. The application of blockchain technology can be far-reaching to support heavy industries such as fashion labels, and mining.
- **Payments and Financial Services**: The second sector is payments and financial services and this includes the use of cryptocurrencies or the support of financial inclusion via remittance payments. The report believes that this sector has the second-largest economic potential of $433 billion.
- **Identity Management**: Identity management ranked as the third industry that will enjoy the benefits of using blockchain technology as its economic potential is pegged at $224 billion. This sector includes personal IDs, certificates, professional credentials and several others that can help lower the case of identity theft and fraud.
- **Contracts and Dispute Resolution**: The fourth use case of blockchain which has an economic potential of $73 billion is the application of the technology in contracts courtesy of smart contracts and also in dispute resolution.
- **Customer Engagement**: In terms of customer engagement, blockchain has the potential to hit $54 billion and this includes the use of blockchain technology in loyalty programs.

### The Impact of Blockchain Across Regions of the World

![Image](Image00047.jpg)

The level of success of blockchain technology will depend greatly on how supportive a policy environment is. A business ecosystem that is willing to take advantage of the new opportunities blockchain technology offers in addition to a suitable industry mix is the right ingredient for maximizing the benefits of the technology.

The continent that is believed to witness the highest economic benefit from distributed ledger technology is Asia. In fact, the PwC report revealed that in terms of different countries, China has the potential to enjoy a net benefit of about $440 billion. Next on the list is the United States with a potential net benefit of $407 billion. Other countries that are expected to enjoy net benefits exceeding $50 billion include the UK, France, Germany, India and Japan. However, these countries will enjoy different kinds of benefits from distributed ledger technology.

For instance, manufacturing-focused economies like Germany and China will enjoy most from provenance and traceability. On the other hand, the United States is expected to benefit the most from applying blockchain technology in securitization, payments, identity and credentials.

### What about Sector Levels?

According to the PwC report, the biggest beneficiary to enjoy the benefits of blockchain technology based on sector level is likely to be the healthcare, public administration and education sectors. The report expects these sectors to capitalize on blockchain's efficiencies in identity and credentials management to enjoy a growth of about $574 billion by 2030. Communications and media and business services will also enjoy broader benefits while construction services, retailers, manufacturers and wholesalers will benefit by engaging consumers and meeting their demand for traceability and provenance courtesy of blockchain.

Another perspective that the report looked at is blockchain's potential to become part of the future strategy of organizations. Based on the outcome of research carried out by PwC with business leaders, about two-thirds (61 percent) of CEOs disclosed that digital transformation of core business processes and operations was going to be one of the top three priorities while rebuilding from the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.

The truth is that one of the greatest mistakes that companies can make, especially when it comes to implementing emerging technologies like blockchain, AI and IoT is leaving them for just the enthusiasts in the team. To function optimally, identify the strategic opportunity and value of such techs and even engender the appropriate level of collaboration in an industry; there is a need for C-Suite support. Considering the extent of economic disruption that most enterprises are facing presently, being able to successfully establish proof of concept uses that can even be extended and scaled, will help organizations to not just discover the value of emerging techs but also help them to build trust and transparency in whatever solution they want to deliver courtesy of blockchain's potential.

To ensure that the economic impact potential of blockchain technology is realized, it is crucial to properly manage its energy overhead. The increasing concerns of the impact of our activities on the planet such as the issues of climate change imply that companies must explore new models for consolidating and sharing resources while cutting down on their dependence on centralized databases as well as their overall energy usage.

## Chapter 15: The Era of Blockchain Identity

Although the concept of digital identity may appear a bit complex to you, it is not difficult to understand. It is basically any personal data that exists online and can easily be traced back to the owner. Digital identity as defined by the International Telecommunications Union is a digital representation of data regarding a particular person or organization. So, let's take pictures for example, when you upload your photos on social media either on posts you commented on, on your post or even online bank account, then they form part of your digital identity since anyone can easily identify you offline with such photo. Digital identity is sometimes regarded as the digital equivalent of the real-life identity of a user which includes several attributes:

It can go beyond the common ones which I have just listed such as age, name, physical or email address and even include sensitive information such as bank account details and health-related information.

However, you should bear in mind that digital identity is no longer regarded strictly as an extended passport or identity card which contains the same data. When it comes to the growth and viability of our global and digital economy, one thing is certain; digital identity is indispensable. It is fundamental to every single organization across all industries around the world.

The world is actually entering the era of intelligent and autonomous systems that are powered by data and machine learning. Recent trends in eCommerce, artificial intelligence, IoT and several others have shown that technology is increasingly becoming intertwined with cyber-physical systems. This has also drawn the attention of major stakeholders to the importance of data privacy and digital identity.
