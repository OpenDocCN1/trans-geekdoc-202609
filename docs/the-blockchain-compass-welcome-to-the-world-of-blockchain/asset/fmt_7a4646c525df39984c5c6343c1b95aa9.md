## The Future of CBDCs and Stablecoins

One of the future trends in the blockchain and cryptocurrency space is the increase in the number of central bank digital currencies. The COVID-19 pandemic which caused a global economic crisis has undoubtedly changed the way we do things. One aspect of our lives it impacted significantly is that of accelerating the movement toward the increased adoption of digital payments even as more people are now avoiding the use of cash due to the fears of spreading the virus. More retailers are increasingly adapting to this change by moving their operations online.

The implication of the challenges facing the world is that it has created the right conditions for experimenting with new digital means of making payments. The truth is that the way different nations coordinate the unique and potentially disruptive technologies will to a great extent determine if we will leverage the outstanding opportunities new technologies like blockchain offer while mitigating the risks involved.

Of course, central bank digital currencies (`CBDC`) and stablecoins are part of the new developments. There has been a recent increase in the number of central banks that are seriously exploring the use of `CBDC` with the Nigerian government revealing that by the end of 2021, they will be testing a digital currency for the country thereby increasing the number of countries interested in it.

A survey carried out by the bank for International Settlements (`BIS`) discovered that over 85 percent of central banks are researching `CBDC`, however, the issuance of the digital currency in many cases has not been concluded. For instance, Singapore finalized its Project Ubin which was a multi-year investigation into the potential benefits of using `CBDC` for wholesale business. China has actually been at the forefront of countries interested in `CBDC` as they have for some time been carrying out advanced testing of their digital yuan which is meant for retail use.

Other central banks that are actively researching `CBDC` include the Bank of England and the European Central Bank. One country that has gone ahead to fully commence the use of `CBDC` is the Bahamas and the Eastern Caribbean Monetary Union.

On the other hand, stablecoins are not issued by monetary authorities; instead, they are provided by private entities. They are regarded as some kind of cryptocurrency that operates on the blockchain. The difference between stablecoins and cryptocurrency is that they are controlled by certain stabilization mechanisms that help in stabilizing their prices relative to various assets like other cryptocurrencies, fiat money or commodities.

In simple terms, the value of stablecoins is pegged to other assets like the US dollars, euro, commodities such as gold and even other cryptocurrencies. Unlike digital currencies like bitcoin, stablecoins are less volatile and this is what makes them relatively more useful as means of exchange and excellent store of value. Good examples of stablecoins are Tether (`USDT`), `USDC`, `BUSD`, etc.

So, why `CBDC` and stablecoins?

Perhaps, the most significant appeal of using this new class of money is that they have the potential to facilitate cheaper and faster cross-border transactions. They can also significantly cut down on costs for consumers, enhance trade and boost global economic integration. One of the remarkable opportunities of public and private digital currencies is that they foster financial inclusion. Digital currencies can help lower most of the barriers facing low-income individuals as well as those that are hard to reach in accessing several financial services.

To ensure the integrity of all transactions involving digital currencies, it is possible to design and program various features into these currencies. I must also point out that despite the numerous benefits of these developments, they still have some challenges. There is a need for digital currencies to be regulated and this is gradually happening even as more countries are becoming more interested in this sector, especially with the surge in the value of bitcoin during the pandemic.

Experts believe that `CBDCs` as well as stablecoins that enjoy the backing of major currencies, on the macroeconomic level could actually pose financial and monetary stability risks, most especially for developing economies. We could see an increased rate of capital flight as well as volatility of exchange rates due to residents gaining access to `CBDCs` of major economies that have low inflation and strong economic fundamentals.

The World Economic Forum has been involved in exploring ways to harness and make contributions to the global understanding and decision-making for crucial policies and governance issues connected to the use of digital currencies. The organization alongside 80 other organizations is looking at priorities and also coming up with solutions. Here are the major areas of focus which the consortium that was created in 2020 identified:

*   There was a need for international cooperation to help overcome the issues connected to cross-border digital currency flows.
*   The importance of data privacy as governments need to set up the right practices for owning, sharing or acquisition of account data. This is to ensure the security of user data while protecting privacy.
*   There is a need for public and private sector collaboration.
*   It is crucial to consider technical interoperability to ensure that `CBDC` and stablecoins connect to existing as well as new systems, across borders and domestically.

## CBDCs, Stablecoins and the Impact of Government Regulations

We are likely going to witness a regulatory battle between central bank digital currencies and Stablecoins and for all dollar-backed projects to ensure their independence, they have a lot of work to do. There is a continued increase in the use of digital assets in different sectors and especially in finance. In the future, we will certainly enter a phase of real competition between privately-owned stablecoins and `CBDCs`.

Experts believe that this era will be the beginning of segregation of digital assets mainly based on their regulatory appeal and not just by functionality. In the next three to five years to come, major permissionless blockchain platforms such as Polkadot, Ethereum, Solana and several others are going to gain momentum in finance. This implies that organizations such as fintech startups, insurance firms, banks, asset managers and different centralized organizations are going to offer their services to their counterparts/clients/customers on these blockchain infrastructures.

So, there is going to be significant tension between private and permissioned platforms. Cryptography as well as distributed infrastructure has made it quite straightforward for anyone to embrace new decentralized platforms and abandon traditional financial systems without being bothered about existing regulations. This position is strongly confirmed by the increasing popularity of cryptocurrencies as internet-native financial services are becoming more popular. Most people now desire to carry out various transactions in a borderless environment.
