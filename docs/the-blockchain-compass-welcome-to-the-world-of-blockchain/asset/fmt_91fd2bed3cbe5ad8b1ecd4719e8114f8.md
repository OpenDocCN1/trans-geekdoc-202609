## 6. Significant Reduction in Costs

Health businesses and patients will enjoy a significant reduction in cost with the use of blockchain technology. Of course, it is only natural for costs to drop with increased efficiency in supply chain, elimination of counterfeit drugs and products, prevention of theft and other fraudulent acts. When health institutions utilize smart contracts, they can effectively carry out direct transactions with their partners and this cuts off expensive intermediaries.

For instance, a report by BIS Research in 2018 estimated that when there is widespread use of blockchain technology, the healthcare industry could save as much as $100 billion by 2025. These are costs related to operation costs, support function and personnel costs, IT costs, data breach-related costs, insurance frauds and counterfeit-related frauds.

## 7. Enabling Large-Scale Cooperation

Majority of the fundamental issues affecting the healthcare industry emanate from its dependence on several elements – governments, regulators, public and private organizations and several others just to function. Consequently, the entire sector is usually slow to react to conditions that are changing rapidly. A good example is a model used in the United States which is highly fragmented and primarily driven by market forces. This model is likely to create challenges for patients who may be changing their residence.

Regions such as Europe also depend mainly on universal healthcare models which has its challenges too. The major issue (on a global scale) rests on establishing frictionless cooperation between various service providers as well as between nations and governments. This is where blockchain comes in since it can enhance the distribution of information across a network, promote transparency and make regulatory compliance procedures straightforward.

A distributed ledger technology like blockchain can facilitate industry-wide initiatives such as implementing new industry standards and helping to coordinate global efforts toward dealing with health crises such as the COVID-19 pandemic. In fact, the increasing development of cross-chain systems and the increasing number of interoperability projects will eventually introduce unique ways for enhancing cooperation on a global dimension.

The outbreak of COVID-19 revealed the state of our health institutions and it is a confirmation that governments and health organizations around the globe require a more efficient way to manage health crises of this magnitude. This also explains why some organizations in the healthcare sector are exploring blockchain-based solutions. For instance, the World Health Organization is partnering with major blockchain and technology companies such as Oracle, IBM, and Microsoft to launch MiPasa. The project is a platform on DLT designed for sharing information on the pandemic. As revealed by Cointelegraph, the platform runs on Hyperledger Fabric and is working toward enabling “early detection of carriers of COVID-19 and its hotspots.”
