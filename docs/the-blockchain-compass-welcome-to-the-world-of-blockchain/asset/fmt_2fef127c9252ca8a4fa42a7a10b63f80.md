## The Current Challenges of Supply Chain

Just before we look at the challenges facing the sector, it is crucial to explain what supply chain management means just to be sure you have a clear picture of what we are discussing. Supply chain management involves the process of planning the movement of products, services, and goods – including finances and data. This encompasses the activities involved right from the point of raw material procurement to the stage of manufacturing and eventual distribution of the products to the consumers, which is the final destination. I will be using the abbreviation SCM for supply chain management to make things easier for you.

The manner most aspects of SCM are handled has a significant impact on the competitive positioning of a company in different areas like service perception, product cost, speed to market, and working capital requirements. Professionals in the supply chain industry have categorized most of the challenges facing the sector into four key pain points. These issues are not just affecting a few industries or specific regions but across the globe. They are also experienced across different industries and need to be resolved before any company in the supply chain sector can unlock the hidden value in the industry. The pain points include:

*   **Traceability:** This involves the ability to monitor events as well as metadata that is associated with a product.
*   **Compliance:** Appropriate controls and standards that will serve as evidence of compliance with regulatory conditions.
*   **Stakeholder management:** Establishment of appropriate governance that would foster risk reduction, communication, and trust among all stakeholders.
*   **Flexibility:** Ability of organizations to quickly adapt to events or challenges facing them and run different scenarios without a drastic increase in operational cost.

## Impact of Blockchain’s Features on Supply Chain

Before discussing the various blockchain supply chain use cases, let's briefly look at some of the technology's most unique features that make it extremely useful and applicable to SCM. We have earlier discussed the four greatest supply chain pain points, but let's see how these blockchain technology features can help resolve them. The four features include:

*   **Immutability:** The distributed nature of blockchain ensures that no single point of failure exists in the network. Since every single transaction is tamper-proof and timestamped, the transactions are immutable and cannot be altered, which helps to eliminate cases of fraud.
*   **Auditability:** In a permissioned blockchain, all authorized parties can instantly see all the transactions. This implies that no individual or group of individuals can modify, conceal, or erase any data that has already been added to the digital ledger.
*   **Smart contracts:** Smart contracts enable the tracking of data in real-time across the supply chain.
*   **Disintermediation:** Distributed ledger technology permits peer-to-peer interactions that can be trusted courtesy of digital signatures.
