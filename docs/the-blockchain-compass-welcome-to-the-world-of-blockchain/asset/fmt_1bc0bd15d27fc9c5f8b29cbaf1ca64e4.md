
## 5. Access Restriction Lacks Regulations

The digital identity management system of any organization must be aware of every individual who has access to specific types of applications or data. They are expected to determine the particular devices that a user is using in accessing these applications and data. When it comes to cloud services, this becomes even more severe.

Unfortunately, the majority of identity management systems lack adequate regulations, which is why a good number of users find it easy to gain access to data that they are not supposed to access. It is therefore extremely hard to offer total security when there is an absence of proper control with the right regulations. Consequently, many users suffer great loss and even lose their most-prized information. This issue is more prevalent in centralized systems.

## 6. Poor Level of Security for Identities in Browser and Devices

Browsers have become an indispensable aspect of our lives as most of the things we do are now on the internet. So, we can easily access various apps using laptops, smartphones, and different gadgets. But the problem occurs when you have too many access points to a single app. You can log into an app via your laptop, desktop, smartphone, or even with your tablet, and sometimes you do this by using a browser.

This causes a user to set up multiple risk points for any given account. In reality, it is often less difficult for hackers to gain access to your device or browser and gain control of your passwords as well as your personal data. This explains the importance of having a good security system for all the devices you use; none of your IDs will be secured without an effective identity management system.

## 7. Not Up to Date Applications

Most people may not know that there is a connection between updating applications and identifying risks. Of course, when developers create an application, they also set up good security measures to help protect users from those with evil intentions. In reality, there is certainly no perfect application, and this implies that there are bound to be bugs and loopholes, especially when several users begin to use the application. So, once a user of an application discovers a bug and files a report, the response of the developers is to come out with a security patch. But developers do not often update the applications soon enough.

Users, on the other hand, often fail to promptly update their applications so they can use the latest version of an application the moment it is released. This delay opens the window of opportunity for hackers to leverage the bugs and loopholes to steal identity information. This does not just apply to individuals using smartphones and other gadgets; it also applies to enterprises too. Enterprises are required to constantly update their applications with the appropriate security patches to avoid such risks.

## 8. Absence of a Good Device Management with an Identity in BYOD

One of the biggest challenges facing the identity management system has to do with the absence of good device management in some BYOD. In case you are unsure what BYOD means, it is simply "Bring Your Own Device." With the explosion of smartphones and other devices, it is common practice for many organizations to grant their employees permission to come to work with their devices and use them in the office.

Of course, this practice is beneficial for several reasons, and one of such reasons is that employees can also complete tasks at home with the same device. But some of these enterprises have not considered the risks associated with such a practice. For every device being used by employees in a company, they are usually protected with firewalls or other security services. This is not usually the case for personal devices, as most of them lack the same level of security measures as the ones in the office.

The implication of allowing employees to work with their devices is that once they connect to the network of their organization, they risk exposing their identity to hackers. The moment hackers gain access to the device of a single employee in such an organization, they will compromise the security of the entire network. What enterprises need in reality is a system that would ensure that those who connect to the server are only registered personal devices.

## 9. Repetitive Registration or KYC Process

Have you opened a bank account before? You remember filling a form and providing all the details required. Well, you end up doing this several times for different purposes – insurances, obtaining a loan, and several others. When we get involved in these repetitions of the same KYC protocols, we are creating room for several points for hackers to gain access to our information. Repetitive KYC processes are undoubtedly a big issue for identity management systems. All the platforms you need to register whenever you require their service have separate servers. This makes digital identities more prone to privacy breaches in addition to the stress of having to repeat the same process whenever they want to use a service, and this is indeed a waste of time.

## Blockchain Technology and Identity Management for Enterprise and Government

We can no longer say that decentralized digital identity (DDID) is merely a buzzword. Things are fast changing, and DDID has the potential to restructure the existing physical and digital identity ecosystem (which is currently centralized) into a democratized and decentralized architecture. Just to be sure you know what decentralized identity means:

> "It refers to a trust framework where the popular identifiers we all are used to like usernames can be replaced with IDs that are independent, self-owned, and will permit the exchange of data with blockchain and distributed ledger technology."

The goal of using a decentralized identity is mainly to secure transactions online and ensure privacy.

## Decentralized Digital Identity: What it is and how it Works

Another simple way to explain what decentralized identity means is to say that there would no longer be a centralized body that can have a claim over any form of identity. With a decentralized identity, your identity will remain solely yours to claim. Besides, the extra level of security that decentralized identity offers will undoubtedly amaze you. It offers a system where no single individual, organization, or third party can misuse your digital identity.

Every enterprise that makes use of blockchain for identity management will resolve the challenges associated with third-party organizations misusing digital information. There are several ways blockchain can help with digital identity.
