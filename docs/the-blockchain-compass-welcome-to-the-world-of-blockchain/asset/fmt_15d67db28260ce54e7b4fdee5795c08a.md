
# Blockchain Technology in Supply Chain Management

Generally, distributed ledger technology has the potential to significantly improve or transform any industry where trust is a major challenge. From the explanations I have made at the beginning of this chapter, it is clear that trust is one of the major challenges of supply chain management since it involves transactions where hundreds and thousands of stakeholders attempt to execute business efficiently. Here are some ways blockchain technology can make a difference in this sector.

## Replacing Slow Manual Processes

Just like I mentioned earlier, many processes involved in supply chain management, especially in the lower supply tiers are quite slow and usually depend mostly on paper. With distributed ledger technology, the process will be replaced with an end-to-end digital process that offers a greater level of transparency and visibility.

## Improves Traceability

Estimates from the Global Brand Counterfeiting report in 2018 shows that companies suffered a total loss of $323 billion globally due to online counterfeiting. In response to the increasing consumer and regulatory request for provenance information which is currently driving the need for a change in the sector, blockchain technology has the answer. Transparency is possible with distributed ledger technology and that is why many companies are embracing it. One of the most popular usefulness of blockchain in supply chain is its track and trace style of application.

With increased supply chain transparency, there will also be greater visibility across supply chain operations and this will result in improved quality, inventory utilization, drastic reduction in loss of revenue due to counterfeit products and better delivery times.

## Cuts Down Transaction Costs of Supply Chain

Since blockchain-based digital currencies can facilitate the transfer of funds across borders without intermediaries such as banks, the technology can significantly speed up payments and at the same time cut down the incurred fees. With the introduction of smart contracts like `Ethereum` and several others, it would be possible for supply chain operations to automatically trigger via smart contracts. So, a smart contract may spell out various terms of the delivery of a given product, the agreed time it should be delivered, the implication of a failed or late delivery.

Also, a smart contract can track the delivery of a product and trigger agreed actions automatically once the terms are breached. With this feature, various costs associated with third parties that were incurred during the old traditional system will be significantly reduced.

## Enhances the Entire Supply Chain Process

Theoretically, blockchain technology has the potential to protect the integrity of every transaction and information and this results in fewer errors and disagreements. Since there is less need for re-filling a missed order or product recalls, the whole process is significantly accelerated. Also, with smart contracts, there will be a reduction in the need for third parties or intermediaries.

## Some Blockchain Use Cases in Supply Chain Management

As I earlier mentioned, blockchain technology is extremely useful in transactions where trust is an issue. Supply chain is undoubtedly one industry where trust is essential in hundreds and thousands of transactions as partners try to execute their businesses efficiently. Here are the major ways distributed ledger technology can make a significant impact in this sector.

### 1. Provenance and Enhanced Traceability

One of the sectors in supply chain that will experience a significant “blockchainization” is the food supply chain, especially in the area of distributing fresh produce. For several years, one costly issue facing the industry is the issue of recalls. But the increased number of food-borne bacteria outbreaks have pushed more firms to explore what distributed ledger technology has to offer to increase the visibility and traceability of goods. More pressure is also coming from consumers who are increasingly demanding insights regarding goods' authenticity, life before hitting the shelves and provenance.

Of course, consumers are prepared to give out huge amounts of money as long as they are paying for sustainable and ethically produced goods and services. In fact, data from Nielsen reveals that about 45 percent of shoppers are prepared to pay more for high-quality products with excellent safety standards. In this sector, Walmart, in partnership with IBM is among the top companies exploring the effectiveness of blockchain-based traceability solutions that they can easily apply across their food supply chain.

The system they tested enabled the tracking of incoming food supplies by retailers starting from the farm to the store almost in real-time. They did not just explore its increased visibility, but also how distributed ledger technology can be used for controlling and monitoring the spread of food-borne bacteria which will in turn drastically reduce the rate of costly recalls. A partnership between OriginTrail and TagItSmart focused on testing the combination of blockchain technology and IoT to help prevent the issue of wine fraud.

Every hour, about 30,000 counterfeit wine bottles are being sold in China. Unfortunately, some of these counterfeit wines contain additives that are harmful and can result in terrible health issues when consumers drink them. The blockchain-based protocol of the company enables the monitoring of all wine bottles starting from the vineyard to different stores. The anti-counterfeit technology makes use of a combination of unique QR codes and photochromic ink to verify every bottle's provenance and authenticity.

Forbes report shows that distributed ledger technology is assisting with the verification of the provenance, custody and authenticity of diamonds in supply chain. Everledger is a blockchain solution developed for the diamond supply chain using the Hyperledger platform. It was designed to help eliminate fraud and illicit global diamond trading. Every single stone comes with what is regarded as a fingerprint and according to Everledger, there are about 1.6 million diamonds currently residing on the blockchain.
