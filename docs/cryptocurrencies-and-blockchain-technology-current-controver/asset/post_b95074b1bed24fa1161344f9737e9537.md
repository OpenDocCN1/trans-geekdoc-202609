# Solving Real Problems

Several uses are being adapted specifically to Africa. Firstly, blockchain-enabled remittance services are helping people send money and from Ghana, Zimbabwe, Uganda, Sierra Leone and Rwanda at a fraction of the current banking rates. Along with its advantages, the use of cryptocurrencies for remittances in Africa also has its potential drawbacks.

Chief of Mission for the International Organisation of Migration in South Africa, Richard Outs, said: “Migrants throughout Africa may feel attracted by virtual currencies as a way to remit their earnings to relatives in their country of origin. The cost of regular channels of sending remittances between many countries continues to remain high, and virtual currencies aim to offer an alternative way to save or transmit migrants' earnings. However, the fluctuations in the rates could lead to significant and unexpected decreases in the amounts relatives at home end up saving or receiving”.

In Zimbabwe, where assets lost huge amounts of value overnight during the hyperinflation period of 2008, some of its citizens have turned to cryptocurrency as a savings mechanism. According to Veragai Mabika, the founder of BitFinance (now Golix) in Zimbabwe, 37% of Zimbabweans use it for this purpose.

When long-time president Robert Mugabe was ousted by the Zimbabwean military in November, Bitcoin saw a surge in price on the Harare exchange. When uncertainty was at its peak, the price for a single bitcoin on the Harare exchange was close to double the price on global bitcoin exchanges.

> "Interest in bitcoin has peaked as people cannot send money outside or for international transactions using formal banks," Yeukai Kusanga, a trace coordinator at the Golix bitcoin exchange in Zimbabwe, told Quartz.

People have had to look for alternatives and bitcoin has been a useful solution which can be used to purchase goods on Amazon or to pay for vehicles from international suppliers and traders.

Bitcoin has been used to help for projects in Africa since 2013, where a woman in Botswana raised $1,500 for SOS Children’s Villages. It can provide a secure voting platform for countries where citizens might battle to access voting stations or face intimidation.

Blockchain is responsible for movements such as Usizo, a South Africa-based blockchain platform that allows members of the public pay electricity bills for community schools. And, in a change that will affect every African country with a seaport, blockchain is doing away with the need for paper documents in the tracking of goods by sea.

# Adoption in Africa

Thabang Mashiloane, chief executive and co-founder of Chankura, a cryptocurrency platform founded in South Africa and currently headquartered in Silicon Valley, pointed out the rising interest. “Between August and September 2017, South Africa was the top country in search,” Mashiloane told the Africa Portal.

Gareth Grobler, of cryptocurrency platform ICE3X estimates that South Africans are now involved. Marina Niforos, principal at Logos Advisors which compiled a report about blockchain in the World Bank, described the keen interest of Kenyan market. “Seventy percent of the country’s adult population holds digital,” she said.

With the most mature mobile money market in Africa, Kenya is leveraging its existing networks to allow for the easier exchange between fiat and cryptocurrency.

The lack of an official bitcoin payment gateway has done little to dampen the adoption rate of cryptocurrencies in Kenya. Quite the opposite in fact,” said Michael Kimani, chairperson of the Blockchain Association of Kenya. “People have adapted to this service gap by forming peer-to-peer networks where anyone can buy or sell.”
