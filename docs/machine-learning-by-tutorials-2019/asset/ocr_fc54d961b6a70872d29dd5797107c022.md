Note: The device's orientation affects the data you collect. For example, imagine an iPhone out in front of you and then moving it up and down, side to side, and away from you. Sensor data collected while doing would be different if the phone was held in portrait or landscape (including variations on home button position), with the screen facing away from you, to the left, right, up, down or some angle between. The gravity fields you stored are enough to determine orientation — that's actually how iOS knows when to rotate your UI — so your model can learn to identify actives in any of these situations. However, you'll need to provide plenty of training data to cover all the possibilities well for it to recognize them.

For your projects, you can handle this in one of three ways: Instruct users to position their devices a specific way and accept the model may not work if they fail to do so, collect a larger dataset that includes data from devices in all probable orientations, or apply a preprocessing step that transforms values into a known orientation. The projects in this chapter serve for the first option.

# Analyzing and preparing your data

So you've got some data. You've collected it yourself or acquired it from elsewhere, but either way, your next step is to look at it. Don't try reading every number — that way lies manners — do some analysis to see exactly what you're working with. You want to ensure there aren't any problems that might ruin the models you try to build.

So what are you looking for? Here are a few things to consider:

+   - If you didn't collect the dataset yourself, it's important to see what's there.
- Mislabeled data. Data is often labeled manually and mistakes are common.
- Poorly collected data. Sometimes mistakes are made while recording, such as misplaced sensors, incorrectly followed instructions, etc.
- Source errors. Sometimes the data source introduces errors, such as a damaged or malfunctioning device reporting bad data. And datasets made by people often contain data entry mistakes.
