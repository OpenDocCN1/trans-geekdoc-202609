Better than the model from last chapter? Um, kind of, in places, but not much. It got close on some sentences, as, “I don't usually like music,” instead of the expected translation, “I don't usually like musicals,” and “How boring!” is close to the correct “Very boring!” It also included additional correct words within translations, as “worst” and “movie” in its translation of, “Es mi peor pesadilla hecha película,” and when translating, “Muy mala película.”

While does this seem like much of improvement, keep in mind that you haven't addressed most of the problems mentioned at the end of the last chapter. For example, the dataset is too small, and you're still predicting characters with a greedy algorithm. Bidirectional models generally produce better results for tasks like translation, where useful context can appear before and after an item in a sequence.

The rest of this chapter shows some other techniques that might lead to better performance. In the next section, you'll learn about popular alternative to greedy decoding called **beam search**. In the process, you will find out this model's not as far off as it seems with many of its translations.
