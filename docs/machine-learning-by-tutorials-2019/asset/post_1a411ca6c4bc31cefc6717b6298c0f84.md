## Bidirectional RNNs

Your original model predicts the next character using only the characters that appear before it in the sentence. But is that really how people read? Consider the following two sentences and their Spanish translations (according to Google):

### Examples where context matters

| English | Spanish |
| :--- | :--- |
| She waited by the bank of the river. | Ella esperó la orilla del río. |
| She waited by the bank until he came out with the money. | Ella esperó junto hasta que salió del dinero. |

The first five words are the same in the English versions of sentences, but the first two end up in different Spanish translations. That's because the meaning of the word "bank" is different in each sentence, so you cannot know its meaning until you read the full context, including the words before and after.

In order to consider the full context surrounding each token, you can use what's called a **bidirectional recurrent network** (BRNN), which processes sequences in both directions, like this:

![](img/5720be5c3cca252e12448e68d25607b5527_0.png)

As the diagram shows, a bidirectional RNN consists of two recurrent layers: one processing the sequence from left to right and the other from right to left. These layers act independently of each other, but their outputs get merged into a single vector. In this chapter, you'll concatenate them, but other merge options, like taking the sum or average, can also work. The number of units in your bidirectional layer's output will be twice the number in its recurrent layers.

The forward and reverse layers themselves can be any recurrent type, such as the LSTMs you've worked with elsewhere in this book. However, in this chapter, you will use a new type called **gated recurrent unit**, or GRU.

GRUs were invented after LSTMs and were meant to serve the same purpose of learning longer-term relationships while training faster. Internally, they are implemented differently from LSTMs, but from a user's standpoint, the main difference is that they do not have separate hidden and cell states. Instead, they only have hidden states, which makes them a bit less complicated to work with when you have to manage states directly—like you do with the decoder in a seq2seq model.
