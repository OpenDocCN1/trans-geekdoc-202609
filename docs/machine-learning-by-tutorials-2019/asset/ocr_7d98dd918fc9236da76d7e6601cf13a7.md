# The decision boundary divides the space into two classes

This line is called the **decision boundary**. It's the job of the classifier model to learn where that decision boundary lies. Math alert: It's not really a line but a hyperplane, which is a subspace that splits the high-dimensional space into two halves. One of the benefits of being a machine learning practitioner is that you get to use cool words such as hyperplane.

The problem is that you cannot draw a line — hyperplane — through the 150,000-dimensional pixel space because ordering the images by their pixel values means that the healthy and unhealthy images are all over the place.

Since pixels capture light intensity, images that have the same color and brightness are grouped together, images that have different colors are farther apart. Apples can be red or green, in pixel space, such images are not close together. Candy can also be red or green, so you'll find pictures of apples mixed up with pictures of candy.

You cannot just look at how red or green something is to decide whether this image contains healthy or unhealthy.

All the information you need to make a classification is obviously contained in the images, but how the images are spread out over this 150,000-dimensional pixel space is not very useful. What you want instead is a space where all the healthy snacks are grouped together and all the unhealthy snacks are grouped together, too.
