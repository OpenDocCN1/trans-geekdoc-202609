So come how these layers know how to perform the segmentation task — as opposed to classification or object detection or something else? The reason is the training data. During training, the output of the network is compared to the ground-truth segmentation masks from the training data, using a suitable loss function. The training process drives the loss to be lower and lower, and so the longer you train, the more the output of the network starts to resemble the ground-truth masks.

Of course, you can clever in your choice of layers — for example, in the illustration above you see that DeepLab uses a branching structure called **spatial pyramid pooling** that makes predictions at different image scales, to capture detail at different levels.

But the point here is that it's not so much the design of the network that makes it a task; it's the data you use to train it. In this case, the training targets were segmentation masks, and so the network has learned to perform semantic segmentation. It's that simple.
