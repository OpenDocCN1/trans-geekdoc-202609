# # Chapter 14: Natural Language Classification

By Chris LaPollo

**Update Note:** Apple loves to keep us waiting. As of now, the CoreML APIs needed to update this chapter weren't quite ready. As Apple releases new APIs, we'll continue to update this chapter. We'll also notify you and let you know how to access the updated versions.

Earlier in the book, you learned how to classify images — for example, judging whether they were of cats or dogs. You've also classified sequences of sensor data as device motions. Text is just another kind of data, and you can classify it as well. But what does a class of text look like?

Is this email legitimate or spam? Customer messages praising your great work or demanding action to answer complaints? What's the topic of an article, patent or court document? These are just a few examples of text classification tasks.

There are a wide variety of techniques for extracting useful information from text, all falling under the general term **natural language processing (NLP)**. This chapter focuses on using NLP for classification, specifically using the methods Apple provides as part of its operating system. You may be familiar with NSinguisticTagger, which has been available since iOS 5. It was covered in the “Natural Language Processing” chapter of our *iOS 11 by Tutorials*, by which Apple rewrote the class to take advantage of Core ML. This chapter does not use class.
