# Analyzing and preparing your data

So you've got some data. You've collected it yourself or acquired it from elsewhere, but either way, your next step is to look at it. You want to ensure there aren't any problems that might ruin the models. Do some analysis to see exactly what you're working with.

So what are you looking for? Here are a few things to consider:

- If you didn't collect the dataset yourself, it's important to see what's there.
- Mislabeled data. Data is often labeled manually and mistakes are common.
- Poorly collected data. Sometimes mistakes are made while recording, such as incorrectly followed instructions, etc.
- Source errors. Sometimes the source introduces errors, such as a malfunctioning device reporting bad data. And datasets made by people often contain data entry mistakes.

- Incorrect data types. For example, strings where there should be numbers.

- Missing values. It's common for some rows to have values missing. You'll need to decide how to handle them — remove rows or insert values. The choice depends on your project, and there are many options to fill the values if you go that route. For example, you might use mean, median or mode value, or perhaps calculate a new value based on values from nearby rows.

- Outliers. Some variation is required to make a good dataset, but there are cases when a few samples may be too rare to worth including in your dataset. Training with them can confuse the model, reducing its overall performance, and it's sometimes better to accept that there are some things your model won't handle.

> Note: You don't have to remove such samples — you may well want your model to support them. But it is something to consider.
