# Recurrent neural networks

So far in this book you've mostly dealt with convolutional neural networks — CNNs. They're great for recognizing spatial relationships in data, as how differences between nearby pixels in a two-dimensional grid can indicate the presence of an edge in an image, and nearby edges in certain configurations can indicate the error of a dog, etc. Another kind of network, called a recurrent neural network — RNN — is designed to recognize temporal relationships. Remember, a sequence generally implies the passage of time, so really just means they recognize relationships between items in a sequence.

To do that, they look at sequences one item at a time, and produce an output for each based on the current item and on the output produced for the previous item. What does that really mean?

Consider how you read the following sentence: "The quick fox jumps over the lazy dog." You don't look at each word individually and ignore the rest, right?
