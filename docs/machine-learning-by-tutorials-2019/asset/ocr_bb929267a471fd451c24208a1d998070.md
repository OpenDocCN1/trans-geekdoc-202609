As you can see, an LSTM is a recurrent unit enhanced with an internal memory. LSTMs are used just like regular recurrent layers, but instead of processing only their input and previous output, an LSTM also considers the contents of its memory. And instead of just producing output, the LSTM can also update its memory to remember (or forget) information it thinks is important about the sequence far.

But terms like remembering, forgetting and thinking make it sound like LSTMs have more agency than they really do. Just like other parts of a neural network, the LSTM's "memory" is really just a bunch of numbers that gets manipulated by various math functions. And it doesn't really choose to remember or forget, it learns weights that cause it to react to different sequences.

> Note: LSTM units are more complex than they appear in the diagram, with each cell made up of four layers combined by various operations. If you're interested in their inner workings, check this excellent blog post. But the truth is, unless you're working to invent new types of neural network layers, you probably won't need to know those low-level details.

The important thing to know about LSTMs is that they train much more easily than the basic RNNs that come before them, and they offer better performance. Most RNNs in use today use some variation of the LSTM unit, as is the case with the activity classifier you trained in Turi Create.
