Diffusion models were originally applied to generate images directly at the pixel level; however, this is extremely expensive, currently taking hundreds of graphics processing unit (`GPU`) days to train. A substantial advance was to apply them to points in the latent space of an autoencoder model instead of in the pixel space. This approach essentially compresses the images, reducing the size of the data that the model must work with and in turn reducing the size of the model. Such `latent diffusion models (LDMs)` require vastly reduced computational resources to produce high-resolution images. ## 11.2 Introduction to Natural Language Processing and Large Language Models

`NLP`, a rich field of study with a long history, is an active application area for deep learning. As the name suggests, `NLP` investigates the processing of `natural language`, that is, language that is written or spoken naturally by humans. Since text is the primary medium by which humans communicate online, and since humans are good at understanding text but are expensive to employ for such tasks, `NLP` technologies have clear financial motivations in helping businesses understand and communicate with their (potential) customers quickly, cheaply, automatically, and at scale. The terms "`text mining`" ([Section 15.5](#Ch016_571-615_B9780443158889000169.xhtml#s0030)) and "`computational linguistics`" are roughly synonyms for `NLP` arising from the data mining and linguistics communities, respectively, differing mainly in their connotations associated with the values prioritized by those communities rather than in the technical approaches used. Text mining suggests an emphasis on the practical aspects of `NLP` and the goal of finding patterns in text data that inform industry applications, while computational linguistics suggests a focus on the study of language and a concordance with linguistic theories and principles.

Deep learning methods are presently the dominant methodology for most `NLP` tasks, but this was not always the case. Probabilistic ML techniques (see [Chapter 13](#Ch014_485-514_B9780443158889000145.xhtml#Ch014_485-514_B9780443158889000145), "`Probabilistic methods: fundamentals,`" and [Chapter 14](#Ch015_516-570_B9780443158889000157.xhtml#Ch015_516-570_B9780443158889000157), "`Advanced probabilistic methods`") are a powerful approach for performing `NLP` tasks that were popular for several decades, supplanting the earlier symbolic rule-based approaches and traditional supervised ML methodologies until deep learning methods became ascendant, particularly from 2017 onward, with the rise of transformer-based language models. As we will see in [Chapter 14](#Ch015_516-570_B9780443158889000157.xhtml#Ch015_516-570_B9780443158889000157), "`Advanced probabilistic methods,`" latent semantic analysis (`LSA`) and latent Dirichlet allocation (`LDA^b`) permit exploratory topic analysis of document collections. More recently, it has been observed that neural language modeling techniques can perform significantly better than `LSA` for preserving relationships among words; moreover, `LDA^b` also has difficulty scaling to truly massive data. Word embedding models such as `word2vec` and `GloVe`, which learn to represent dictionary words with vectors that encode their semantic meaning, were a major advance in neural approaches to `NLP` circa 2013. These simple models, which we discuss later, use a shallow neural network architecture, but form the basis for more sophisticated and powerful deep learning methods for `NLP` such as transformer-based language models.
