
### Postprocessing
Correct fairness issues in the outputs of the model, after training. One such approach is to shift the per-group classification thresholds to correct for how each demographic group is treated by the model.

As we discussed, the simple preprocessing approach of deleting the protected attributes so that the classifier cannot use them, sometimes called *fairness through unawareness* or *anticlassification*, is generally insufficient. This strategy prevents *disparate treatment*, that is, explicit discrimination by treating individuals from different protected groups differently through different criteria or standards. However, it does not prevent *disparate impact*, that is, disparity in the outcomes. Even without access to the protected attributes, there are likely to be other attributes that are correlated with the protected attributes and the class labels, which allow the model to discriminate by proxy (“*proxy variables*”). We require more sophisticated solutions.

## Fairness Metrics
Based on a machine learning model’s behavior, a *fairness metric* produces a score that assesses the extent to which the model is fair or unfair (e.g., if it exhibits a bias toward or against a particular demographic). The use of fairness metrics is the dominant approach to implementing fair AI, as they allow us to flexibly operationalize our desired fairness goals.
