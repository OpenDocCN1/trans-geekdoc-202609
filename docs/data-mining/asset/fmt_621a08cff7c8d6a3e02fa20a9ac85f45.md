The `skip-gram` model encapsulates a linguistic theory called the `distributional hypothesis`. In brief, as famously stated by an early proponent of the theory, J.R. Firth, the theory states, “You shall know a word by the company it keeps.” More concretely, the `distributional hypothesis` posits that words with similar meanings tend to occur in similar contexts; that is, their probability distributions over neighboring words are similar to each other. Thus, we can potentially use this “`distributional similarity`” to estimate meaning similarity. The `skip-gram` very directly implements the `distributional hypothesis` by way of the word embeddings as intermediaries between distributional similarity and meaning similarity. The word vectors encode the distributions of context words. By design, words that have similar distributions over context words will have similar vectors. And when words have similar vectors, we conclude that they have similar meanings ([Fig. 11.3](#Ch012_433-471_B9780443158889000121.xhtml#f0020)).
