
## Discretization

[Dougherty, Kohavi, and Sahami (1995)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur144) give a brief account of supervised and unsupervised discretization, along with experimental results comparing the entropy-based method with equal-width binning and the `1R` method. [Frank and Witten (1999)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur183) describe the effect of using the ordering information in discretized attributes. Proportional `k`-interval discretization for `Naïve Bayes` was proposed by [Yang and Webb (2001)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur582). The entropy-based method for discretization, including the use of the `MDL` stopping criterion, was developed by [Fayyad and Irani (1993)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur163). The bottom-up statistical method using the `χ²` test is due to [Kerber (1992)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur304), and its extension to an automatically determined significance level is described by [Liu and Setiono (1997)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur368). [Fulton, Kasif, and Salzberg (1995)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur200) investigate the use of dynamic programming for discretization and derive the quadratic time bound for a general impurity function (e.g., entropy) and the linear one for error-based discretization. The example used for showing the weakness of error-based discretization is adapted from [Kohavi and Sahami (1996)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur320), who were the first to clearly identify this phenomenon.

## Dimensionality Reduction

Principal component analysis is a standard technique that can be found in most statistics textbooks. [Fradkin and Madigan (2003)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur177) analyze the performance of random projections. The algorithm for partial least squares regression is from [Hastie et al. (2009)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur253). The `TF` × `IDF`<mml:math><mml:mi mathvariant="italic">TF</mml:mi><mml:mo>&#215;</mml:mo><mml:mi mathvariant="italic">IDF</mml:mi></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000078si55.png) metric is described by Witten et al. (1999b).

[Hyvärinen and Oja (2000)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur281) created the fast `ICA` method. [Duda et al. (2001)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur149) and [Murphy (2012)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur407) explain the algebra underlying solutions to Fisher’s linear discriminant analysis. [Sugiyama (2007)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur512) has extended it in two ways in a variant called “local Fisher discriminant analysis.” First, instead of means in the scatter matrix computations he uses computations between datapoints themselves, which allows the dimensionality of the reduced representation to be increased. Second, he applies Scholköpf and Smola’s (2002) kernel trick to obtain nonlinear projections.

There are many alternatives to the type of linear data projections discussed earlier. For example, multilayer perceptrons provide a way to learn data projections because their hidden layers can be treated as projections of the data. [Chapter 10](#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X.xhtml#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X), “Deep learning: fundamentals,” examines deep neural networks, including an approach for unsupervised dimensionality reduction based on a type of neural network known as an “`autoencoder`.”

## Feature Selection and Outlier Detection

The experiments on using `C4.5` to filter its own training data were reported by [John (1995)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur292). The more conservative approach of a consensus filter involving several different learning algorithms has been investigated by [Brodley and Fried (1996)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur71). [Rousseeuw and Leroy (1987)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur463) describe the detection of outliers in statistical regression, including the `least median of squares` method; they also present the telephone data of [Fig. 6.7](#Ch006_209-259_B9780443158889000078.xhtml#f0040). It was [Quinlan (1986)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur441) who noticed that removing noise from the training instance’s attributes can decrease a classifier’s performance on similarly noisy test instances, particularly at higher noise levels.

## One-Class and Novelty Detection

[Barnett and Lewis (1994)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur24) address the general topic of outliers in statistical data, while [Pearson (2005)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur430) describes the statistical approach of fitting a distribution to the target data. [Schölkopf, Williamson, Smola, Shawe-Taylor, and Platt (2000)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur480) describe the use of `support vector machines` for novelty detection, while [Abe, Zadrozny, and Langford (2006)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur3), among others, use artificial data as a second class. Combining density estimation and class probability estimation using artificial data is suggested as a generic approach to unsupervised learning by [Hastie et al. (2009)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur253), and [Hempstalk, Frank, and Witten (2008)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur261) describe it in the context of one-class classification. [Hempstalk and Frank (2008)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur260) discuss the fair comparison of one-class and multiclass classification when several classes are available at training time and we want to discriminate against an entirely new class at prediction time.

## Sampling

[Vitter (1985)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur548) explored the idea of reservoir sampling; he called the method we described algorithm `R`. Its computational complexity is `O(N)`, where `N` is the number of instances in the stream because a random number must be generated for every instance to determine whether to place it in the reservoir, and where. Vitter describes several other algorithms that improve upon `R` by reducing the number of random numbers that must be generated to produce the sample.

## Multiclass Classification

[Rifkin and Klautau (2004)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur456) show that the `one-vs-rest` method for multiclass classification can work well if appropriate parameter tuning is applied. [Friedman (1996)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur194) describes the technique of `pairwise` classification, [Fürnkranz (2002)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur201) further analyzes it, and [Hastie and Tibshirani (1998)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur252) extend it to estimate probabilities using pairwise coupling. [Fürnkranz (2003)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur202) evaluates `pairwise` classification as a technique for ensemble learning. The idea of using `error-correcting output codes` for classification gained wide acceptance after a paper by [Dietterich and Bakiri (1995)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur134); [Ricci and Aha (1998)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur452) showed how to apply such codes to nearest-neighbor classifiers. [Frank and Kramer (2004)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur182) introduce ensembles of `nested dichotomies` for multiclass problems. [Dong, Frank, and Kramer (2005)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur142) considered using `balanced nested dichotomies` rather than unrestricted random hierarchies to reduce training time.
