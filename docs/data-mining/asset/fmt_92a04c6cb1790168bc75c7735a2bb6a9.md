
The `k`-means algorithm is a classic technique, and many descriptions and variations are available ([Hartigan, 1975](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur251)). The `k`-means++ variant, which yields a significant improvement by choosing the initial seeds more carefully, was introduced as recently as 2007 by [Arthur and Vassilvitskii (2007)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur15). Our description of how to modify `k`-means to find a good value of `k` by repeatedly splitting clusters and seeing whether the split is worthwhile follows the `X`-means algorithm of [Moore and Pelleg (2000)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur404). However, instead of the MDL principle they use a probabilistic scheme called the Bayes Information Criterion ([Kass and Wasserman, 1995](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur301)). Efficient agglomerative methods for hierarchical clustering were developed by [Day and Edelsbrünner (1984)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur123), and the ideas are described in recent books ([Duda et al., 2001](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur149); [Hastie et al., 2009](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur253)). The incremental clustering procedure, based on the merging and splitting operations, was introduced in systems called `Cobweb` for nominal attributes ([Fisher, 1987](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur167)) and `Classit` for numeric attributes ([Gennari, Langley, and Fisher, 1990](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur211)). Both are based on a measure of category utility that had been defined previously ([Gluck and Corter, 1985](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur222)).

A hierarchical clustering method called `BIRCH` (for “balanced iterative reducing and clustering using hierarchies”) has been developed specifically for large multidimensional datasets, where it is necessary for efficient operation to minimize input–output costs ([Zhang, Ramakrishnan, and Livny, 1996](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur594)). It incrementally and dynamically clusters multidimensional metric data points, seeking the best clustering within given memory and time constraints. It typically finds a good clustering with a single scan of the data, which can then be improved by further scans.

The method of dealing with multiinstance learning problems by applying standard single instance learners to summarize bag-level data was applied in conjunction with support vector machines by [Gärtner, Flach, Kowalczyk, and Smola (2002)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur207). The alternative approach of aggregating the output was explained by [Frank and Xu (2003)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur187).
