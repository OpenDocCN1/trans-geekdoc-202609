
# Diffusion Models

## Overview
Currently, most of the popular AI art generation systems such as Dall-E 2 and Dall-E 3, Stable Diffusion, and Midjourney use *diffusion models*, another generative AI technique. Recall how in GANs, random noise vectors `z`, which are usually Gaussian generated, are transformed by a neural network into the desired synthetic data `x` such as images. The decoder in a `VAE`, which maps a latent encoding vector `z` (also usually with a Gaussian prior) to a realistic data point `x`, can also be thought of as performing a similar operation. Diffusion models envision a physical process in which real data is turned into random noise. The process of turning noise into realistic data, like GANs and VAEs do, is modeled as the reverse of this process and is estimated via deep neural networks. Inspired by statistical physics, this approach leads to a flexible yet tractable class of generative models that admit the training of very deep neural networks and have proven effective at image generation tasks.

## Forward Process
More concretely, consider a process in which our training data points (e.g., images) are repeatedly perturbed by adding Gaussian noise to them. Eventually, the perturbed data points would be so noisy that they become completely random Gaussian noise. This type of process is called *diffusion*. In a diffusion model, the model component that does diffusion, perturbing a training sample `x[0]` via `T` steps `x[1]`, `x[2]`…, `x[T]` is called the *forward process*, and the noise transition model is denoted `q(x[t]|x[t-1])`. This process bears some superficial resemblance to the encoder in a `VAE` in that it maps a data point to a Gaussian-distributed point, but it differs in that it consists of a prespecified noise distribution with no learned parameters (except perhaps, optionally, the variance of the noise) while a VAE’s encoder is a deep neural network.

## Reverse Process
Now, suppose we want to “undo” the diffusion process to recover the real data, or generate other realistic data points from the same distribution. Undoing noise is called, quite naturally, *denoising*. The denoising component in a diffusion model is called the *reverse process* since it undoes the forward process and is denoted `p[θ](x[t-1]|x[t])`. Here, `θ` represents the parameters of the corresponding neural networks, *denoising autoencoder* models `ϵ[θ](x[t],t)`, which predicts the mean `μ[θ](x[t],t)` and covariance `Σ[θ](x[t],t)` of the reverse transitions at each step `t` to undo the noise,

`p[θ](x[t-1]|x[t]) = N(x[t-1]; μ[θ](x[t],t), Σ[θ](x[t],t)).`
