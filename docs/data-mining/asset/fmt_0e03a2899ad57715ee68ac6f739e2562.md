
This forced us to rethink the role of theory in the book. We bit the bullet and added two new theoretically oriented chapters. [Chapter 10](#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X.xhtml#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X), “Deep learning, covers deep learning itself,” and its predecessor, [Chapter 9](#Ch010_347-389_B9780443158889000108.xhtml#Ch010_347-389_B9780443158889000108), “Probabilistic methods,” gives a principled theoretical development of probabilistic methods that is necessary to understand a host of other new algorithms. We recognize that many of our readers will not want to stomach all this theory, and we assure them that the remainder of the book has intentionally been left at a far simpler mathematical level. But this additional theoretical base puts some key material in the hands of readers who aspire to understand rapidly advancing techniques from the research world.

Developments in WEKA have proceeded apace. It now provides ways of reaching out and incorporating other languages and systems, such as the popular R statistical computing language, the Spark and Hadoop frameworks for distributed computing, the Python and Groovy languages for scripting, and the MOA system for stream-oriented learning, to name but a few. Recognizing that it is not possible, and perhaps not desirable, to document such a comprehensive and fast-evolving system in a printed book, we created a series of open online courses, *Data Mining with Weka*, *More Data Mining with Weka*, and *Advanced Data Mining with Weka*, to accompany the book (at [https://ml.cms.waikato.ac.nz/weka/](https://ml.cms.waikato.ac.nz/weka/)). The fourth edition contained numerous other updates and additions and far more references to the literature.

## Fifth Edition

Nearly 20 years ago, I (James Foulds, a.k.a. Jimmy) first learned about data mining and machine learning from this very book, and I was taught directly by its authors during my undergraduate and master’s thesis days at the University of Waikato, New Zealand. The book traveled with me in my suitcase to the United States where I completed my doctoral education and continued my academic career. It stayed with me as I began to use it to teach my own students. Writing the new edition is like a homecoming for me. It is an honor to be able to complete the cycle and bring this important book up to date to serve a new generation of up-and-coming scholars.

A lot has changed in the field since the previous edition, even as the fundamentals remain the same. We have witnessed the rise and rise of deep learning, especially generative AI and transformer-based large language models, alongside the rise and fall of almost every other facet of traditional machine learning. With the vastly increased deployment of machine learning systems, which are now ingrained in almost every facet of daily life, ethical and societal issues have come to the fore. Reflecting this reality, my priority has been to ensure comprehensive and educationally effective coverage of ethical and responsible AI, societal impacts, and current deep learning methods, which I have sought to achieve by adding two new chapters ([Chapter 7](#Ch007_261-315_B978044315888900008X.xhtml#Ch007_261-315_B978044315888900008X), “Ethics: what are the impacts of what’s been learned?,” and [Chapter 11](#Ch012_433-471_B9780443158889000121.xhtml#Ch012_433-471_B9780443158889000121), “Advanced deep learning methods”), as well as significant additions to several other chapters, notably [Chapter 10](#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X.xhtml#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X), “Deep learning: fundamentals,” and [Chapter 15](#Ch016_571-615_B9780443158889000169.xhtml#Ch016_571-615_B9780443158889000169), “Moving on: applications and their consequences.” These additions resulted in including more than 80 new references.

By popular demand I have added exercises for each chapter. Many of the exercises are multiple-choice questions. These typically correspond to testing the learning goals that I use in my courses, which allow students to verify that they “know what they need to know”; this is called *metacognition*. If you are reading this book on your own you can use the questions to check your understanding, looking back into the chapter if you are unsure of an answer. In my own courses I use multiple-choice questions primarily in two ways, both of which are facilitated by “clicker” devices or electronic polling services such as Poll Everywhere:

1) As *reading quiz* questions at the beginning of each lesson to encourage students to do the assigned reading. I do not assess these questions for accuracy, only for participation.

2) As *peer instruction* exercises. Students vote on a multiple-choice question, by themselves first, requiring them to think about it on their own. Then they discuss it with the person next to them and update their vote based on their discussion. The results are always more accurate the second time, as students understand what their peers struggle with and can explain it to them in a manner that they can understand. Pedagogical research has shown that *active learning* exercises such as peer instruction substantially improve student learning outcomes. I strongly encourage instructors to give it a try in your own courses.

My other main goal in the new edition was to make the deep learning and probabilistic methods chapters more accessible. The ease of reading has always been a strength of the book, including a conversational and personal tone, copious examples, and avoidance or delay of unnecessary mathematical details in favor of understanding and intuition. As the book was expanded to include more advanced and recent content the balance of ease and rigor became difficult to maintain. I had to tell my students, “Don’t worry if you can’t understand all the equations, just try to get through it.” How could I make those technical chapters easier to read without losing their depth?

The answer turned out to be right in front of me in the book all along. Just as the book separates an introductory treatment of the material (Part I) from a more in-depth treatment (Part II), I have separated the introductory material from the more advanced material in the deep learning and probabilistic machine learning topics. Fortunately, the more complicated deep learning material is le0ss essential now than it was in 2017, enabling me to shift much of it to the appendices. Deep learning packages calculate gradients automatically and the operations of computational graphs are increasingly done “under the hood” so it is no longer essential that students and practitioners can perform the gradient computations themselves. One of the more challenging topics, stochastic networks, has fallen out of favor. I moved the probabilistic machine learning material later in the book and split it into two chapters, containing elementary and advanced material, respectively, giving the reader a sense of “permission” to stop after learning the basics if they choose to do so. I also reorganized some of the chapters so that those with practical importance (the chapter on data transformations, now reframed as preprocessing and exploratory data analysis, and the chapter on ensemble methods) come earlier. I hope that these changes will help readers to quickly learn the most important lessons while giving them room to grow into the more challenging topics. After all, accelerating the learning process is just as important for humans as it is for machines! Let’s fire up our neurons, both real and artificial, and let the learning begin!
