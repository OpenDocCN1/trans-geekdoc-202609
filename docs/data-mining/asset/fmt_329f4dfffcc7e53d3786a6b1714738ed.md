### Conjugate Priors

In more fully Bayesian methods one treats both variables and parameters as random quantities. The use of prior distributions over parameters can provide simple and well-justified ways to regularize model parameters and avoid overfitting. Applying the Bayesian modeling philosophy and techniques can lead to simple adjustments to traditional maximum likelihood estimates. In particular the use of a conjugate prior distribution for a parameter in an appropriately defined probability model means that the posterior distribution over that parameter will remain in the same form as the prior. This makes it easy to adapt traditional maximum likelihood estimates for parameters using simple weighted averages of the maximum likelihood estimate and the relevant parameters of the conjugate prior. We will see how this works for the `Bernoulli`, `categorical`, and `Gaussian` distributions next. Other more sophisticated Bayesian manipulations are also simplified through the use of conjugacy.
