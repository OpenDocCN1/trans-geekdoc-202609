
Other useful tools and measurements include summary statistics such as the `mean`, `standard deviation`, and the five number summary: the minimum, `Q1` (the first quartile, i.e., the 25th percentile), the median, `Q3` (the third quartile, i.e., the 75th percentile), and the maximum. Boxplots visually show the information in the five number summary at a glance. Based on the five number summary we can easily obtain the interquartile range `IQR = Q3 - Q1`, which is useful as a measure of spread that is robust to outliers.

It is also valuable to analyze the relationships between attributes. Pearson’s correlation coefficient measures the linear correlation between two attributes as a number between –1 and +1, while the covariance of two attributes measures the extent to which they tend to be above or below their respective means at the same time. These measurements can be used to identify redundant attributes, which can occur when merging data from several data sources via data integration. Association rule mining, which we discussed in [Section 4.5](#Ch004_91-164_B9780443158889000054_a.xhtml#s0075), is another useful strategy for identifying relationships between the attributes in your dataset in the form of rules.

### Visualizing Data in Two Dimensions With `t-SNE` and `UMAP`

To understand the high-level structure of a dataset, it can be instructive to visualize it by projecting down to two dimensions. Non-linear manifold learning projection methods like `t-Stochastic Neighbor Embedding` (`t-SNE`) are generally a better option than linear projection methods such as `PCA`, since they do not assume that the surface that most of the points occupy (a manifold) is linear. The `t-SNE` method tries to keep points that were close together in the original embedding space close together in the projected two-dimensional space. It also tries to preserve the relationships between more distant points, but it is more relaxed about those relationships and is willing to let those drift if necessary for better preserving the relationships between closer points.

More recently, Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (`UMAP`) has arisen as a reliable and accurate alternative to `t-SNE`, which often performs better in practice. `UMAP` is based on mathematical theory from Riemannian geometry and algebraic topology. Although the theoretical underpinnings of `UMAP` are complex, in practice `UMAP` constructs its low-dimensional projections using a force-directed graph layout method, in which attractive forces and repulsive forces compete to place the projected data points at the ideal distances from other points. Edges in an appropriately chosen graph have attractive forces between points, while the nodes (corresponding to the embedded data points) repel each other. The first phase of the algorithm is to construct a particular weighted `k`-neighbor graph. Then a process reminiscent of simulated annealing—in which the attractive and repulsive forces are gradually decreased—is used to place the points in the low-dimensional embedding space while ensuring convergence to a local optimum in the algorithm’s objective function.

## 6.9 Further Reading and Bibliographic Notes

Attribute selection, under the term feature selection, has been investigated in the field of pattern recognition for decades. Backward elimination was introduced in the early 1960s ([Marill and Green, 1963](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur376)). [Kittler (1978)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur313) surveys the feature selection algorithms that have been developed for pattern recognition. Best-first search and genetic algorithms are standard artificial intelligence techniques ([Goldberg, 1989](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur223); [Winston, 1992](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur568)).

The experiments that show the performance of decision tree learners deteriorating when new attributes are added are reported by [John (1997)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur293), who gives a nice explanation of attribute selection. [Langley and Sage (1997)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur347) showed that for an instance-based learner to reach a given performance level, the number of training instances must grow exponentially with the number of attributes. The idea of finding the smallest attribute set that carves up the instances uniquely is from [Almuallin and Dietterich (1991](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur10), [1992](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur11)) and was further developed by [Liu and Setiono (1996)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur368). [Kibler and Aha (1987)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur83) and [Cardie (1993)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur83) investigated the use of decision tree algorithms to identify features for nearest-neighbor learning; [Holmes and Nevill-Manning (1995)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur273) used 1 R to order features for selection. [Kira and Rendell (1992)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur311) used instance-based methods to select features, leading to a scheme called `RELIEF` for Recursive Elimination of Features. [Gilad-Bachrach, Navot, and Tishby (2004)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur217) show how this scheme can be modified to work better with redundant attributes. The correlation-based feature selection method was developed by [Hall (2000)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur242).

The use of wrapper methods for feature selection is due to [John, Kohavi, and Pfleger (1994)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur294) and Kohavi and [John (1997)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur293), and genetic algorithms have been applied within a wrapper framework by [Vafaie and DeJong (1992)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur539) and [Cherkauer and Shavlik (1996)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur95). The selective Naïve Bayes learning scheme is due to [Langley and Sage (1994)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur346). [Guyon, Weston, Barnhill, and Vapnik (2002)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur241) present and evaluate the recursive feature elimination scheme in conjunction with support vector machines. The method of raced search was developed by [Moore and Lee (1994)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur402). [Gütlein, Frank, Hall, and Karwath (2009)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur240) investigate how to speed up scheme-specific selection for datasets with many attributes using simple ranking-based methods.
