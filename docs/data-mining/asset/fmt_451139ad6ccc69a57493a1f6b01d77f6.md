The `KL-divergence` is an information theoretic quantity that measures how much information is lost (in units of nats) when using `q`<mml:math><mml:mi>q</mml:mi></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si19.png) to represent `p`<mml:math><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si20.png). In the case of the `VAE`, the encoder receives a penalty in the loss based on how different it is from a standard normal distribution. We can train this model via backpropagation, although it requires a “*reparameterization trick*” to backpropagate through random samples.

For example, suppose wanted to find a particular target distribution of interest, `p`(`z`)<mml:math><mml:mi>p</mml:mi><mml:mrow><mml:mfenced open="(" close=")"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>z</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:mfenced></mml:mrow></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si34.png). `Variational inference` recasts this task as “find the distribution `q`(`z`)<mml:math><mml:mi>q</mml:mi><mml:mrow><mml:mfenced open="(" close=")"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>z</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:mfenced></mml:mrow></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si35.png) that is closest to `p`(`z`)<mml:math><mml:mi>p</mml:mi><mml:mrow><mml:mfenced open="(" close=")"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>z</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:mfenced></mml:mrow></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si36.png) in terms of some metric, such as `KL-divergence`.” If there were no additional constraints, solving this optimization problem would find the target distribution `p`(`z`)<mml:math><mml:mi>p</mml:mi><mml:mo stretchy="false">(</mml:mo><mml:mi>z</mml:mi><mml:mo stretchy="false">)</mml:mo></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si37.png), since `p`(`z`)<mml:math><mml:mi>p</mml:mi><mml:mrow><mml:mfenced open="(" close=")"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>z</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:mfenced></mml:mrow></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si38.png) is indeed the distribution that is closest to itself.

In practice, we typically cannot solve the optimization problem without approximating it by making simplifying assumptions. To make this search problem feasible, constraints or limitations are normally added to simplify the problem in some way. An approach that was popular in the past was to assume that all the variables in `z`<mml:math><mml:mi>z</mml:mi></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si39.png) are independent random variables. This leads to tractable updates, and the approach is called *mean-field variational inference*. However, the independence assumption is often highly detrimental to performance. Instead, `VAEs` use a more powerful and accurate simplifying assumption instead; the distributions we want to learn can be represented by neural networks. The objective function for the `VAE` is equivalent to minimizing the `KL-divergence` between the probability of the latent variables, calculated under Bayes’ rule, and the approximation from the neural network model. This strategy is computationally practical and generally performs much better than mean field variational inference due to its more flexible modeling assumption.
