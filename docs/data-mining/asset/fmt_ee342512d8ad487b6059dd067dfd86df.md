# Part II: More advanced machine learning schemes

We have seen the basic ideas of several machine learning methods and studied in detail how to assess their performance on practical data mining problems. We also addressed data preparation methods and discussed the ethical concerns that arise around these methods and how to mitigate them. Now we are well prepared to look at more powerful and advanced machine learning algorithms. Our aim is to explain these both at a conceptual level and with a fair amount of technical detail so that you can understand them fully and appreciate the key implementation issues that arise. Because of the nature of the material this second part of the book differs from Part I in how you can approach it. Most chapters can be read independently, except that [Chapter 9](#Ch010_347-389_B9780443158889000108.xhtml#Ch010_347-389_B9780443158889000108), “Extending instance-based and linear models,” [Chapter 10](#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X.xhtml#Ch011_391-432_B978044315888900011X), “Deep learning: fundamentals” and [Chapter 11](#Ch012_433-471_B9780443158889000121.xhtml#Ch012_433-471_B9780443158889000121), “Advanced deep learning methods” build on each other incrementally, as do [Chapter 13](#Ch014_485-514_B9780443158889000145.xhtml#Ch014_485-514_B9780443158889000145), “Probabilistic methods: fundamentals” and [Chapter 14](#Ch015_516-570_B9780443158889000157.xhtml#Ch015_516-570_B9780443158889000157), “Advanced probabilistic methods.”

There are substantial differences between the simple methods described in [Chapter 4](#Ch004_91-164_B9780443158889000054.xhtml#Ch004_91-164_B9780443158889000054), “Algorithms: the basic methods,” and the more sophisticated algorithms required to achieve state-of-the-art performance on many real-world problems but the principles are the same. So are the inputs and, in many cases, the outputs—the methods of knowledge representation. But the algorithms are more complex (e.g., they may have to be extended to deal with numeric attributes, missing values, and—most challenging of all—noisy data).

[Chapter 4](#Ch004_91-164_B9780443158889000054.xhtml#Ch004_91-164_B9780443158889000054), “Algorithms: the basic methods,” opened with an explanation of how to infer rudimentary rules and went on to examine probabilistic modeling and decision trees. Then we returned to rule induction and continued with association rules, linear models, the nearest-neighbor method of instance-based learning, clustering, and multi-instance learning. In Part II, including the Appendices, we develop all these topics further and also encounter some new ones.
