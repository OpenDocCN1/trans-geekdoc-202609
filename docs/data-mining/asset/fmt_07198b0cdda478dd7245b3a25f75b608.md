
Nearest-neighbor learning based on variants of the Hausdorff distance was investigated by [Wang and Zucker (2000)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur550). [Gärtner et al. (2002)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur207) experimented with the set kernel to learn `SVM` classifiers for multi-instance data. Multi-instance algorithms for rule and decision tree learning, which are not covered here, have been described by [Chevaleyre and Zucker (2001)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur96), [Blockeel, Page, and Srinivasan (2005)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur49), and Bjerring and Frank (2011). Logistic regression has been adapted for multi-instance learning by [Xu and Frank (2004)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur577) and [Ray and Craven (2005)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur449); multilayer perceptrons have been adapted by [Ramon and de Raedt (2000)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur448).

Hyperrectangles and spheres were considered as concept descriptions for multi-instance learning by [Dietterich et al. (1997)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur136) and [Auer and Ortner (2004)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur20), respectively. The diverse density method is the subject of Maron’s (1998) PhD thesis and is also described in ([Maron and Lozano-Peréz, 1997](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur378)). A quicker, heuristic variant is evaluated by Foulds and Frank (2010b).

The multi-instance literature makes many different assumptions regarding the type of concept to be learned, defining how the bag-level and instance-level class labels are connected, starting with the original assumption that a bag is labeled positive if and only if one of its instances is positive. A review of assumptions in multi-instance learning can be found in Foulds and Frank (2010a).
