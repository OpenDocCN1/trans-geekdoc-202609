
Many neural network books ([Haykin, 1994](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur256); [Bishop, 1995](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur43); [Ripley, 1996](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur457)) do not formulate backpropagation in vector-matrix terms. However, recent online courses (e.g., by Hugo Larochelle) and Rojas’ (1996) text do adopt this formulation, as we have done in this Appendix.

[Bourlard and Kamp (1988)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur58) provide a deep analysis of the relationships between autoencoders and principal component analysis. [Vincent, Larochelle, Lajoie, Bengio, and Manzagol (2010)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur547) proposed stacked denoising autoencoders and found that they outperform both stacked standard autoencoders and models based on stacking RBMs. [Cho and Chen (2014)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur97) produced state-of-the-art results on motion capture sequences by training deep autoencoders with rectified linear units using hybrid unsupervised and supervised learning.

The history of Markov random fields has roots in statistical physics in the 1920s with so-called “Ising models” of ferromagnetism. Our presentation of Boltzmann machines follows [Hinton and Sejnowski (1983)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur265) but we use matrix-vector notation and our exposition more closely resembles formulations such as that of [Salakhutdinov and Hinton (2009)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur470). Harmonium networks proposed in [Smolensky (1986)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur499) are essentially equivalent to what are now commonly referred to as RBMs.

Contrastive divergence was proposed by [Hinton (2002)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur263). The idea of using unsupervised pretraining to initialize deep networks using stacks of RBMs was popularized by [Hinton and Salakhutdinov (2006)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur264); [Salakhutdinov and Hinton (2009)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur470) give further details on the use of deep Boltzmann machines and training procedures for deep belief networks, including the variable doubling procedure and other nuances discussed earlier for greedy training of deep restricted Boltzmann machines. [Neal (1992)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur411) introduced sigmoidal belief networks. [Welling, Rosen-Zvi, and Hinton (2004)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur562) showed how to extend Boltzmann machines to categorical and continuous variables using exponential-family models. The greedy layerwise training procedure for deep Boltzmann machines in [Section D.3](#FigD1_687-703_B9780443158889000248.xhtml#s0040) is based on a procedure proposed by [Hinton and Salakhutdinov (2006)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur264) and refined by [Murphy (2012)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur407).

Hybrid supervised and unsupervised learning procedures for restricted Boltzmann machines were proposed by [McCallum, Pal, Druck, and Wang (2006)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur382) and further explored by [Larochelle and Bengio (2008)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur349). [Vincent et al. (2010)](#References_705-736_B978044315888900025X.xhtml#fur547) proposed the autoencoder approach to unsupervised pretraining; they also explored various layerwise stacking and training strategies and compared stacked RBMs with stacked autoencoders.
