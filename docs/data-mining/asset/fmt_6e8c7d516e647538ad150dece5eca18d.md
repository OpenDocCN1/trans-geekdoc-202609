Also observe that the two occurrences of the word `the` in the document have the same embedding. This is because word embeddings have a unique word embedding for each word in the dictionary but do not have a unique embedding for each token. In contrast, more recent language models such as BERT can learn `contextual embeddings` that differ for each token depending on their context.

## Word2vec Models

Models and associated learning algorithms have been developed to recover these word embeddings automatically from data. One of the word2vec models produces continuous representations of words by training a neural bag-of-words model to predict words given their context. Because word order in the context window is not captured, this is known as the “`Continuous Bag of Words`” (`CBOW`) model. In the CBOW model each word `w`<mml:math><mml:mi>w</mml:mi></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si67.png) in the dictionary is associated with a vector-space embedding `v`[`w`]<mml:math><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi mathvariant="normal">v</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi mathvariant="normal">w</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si68.png) with `k`<mml:math><mml:mi>k</mml:mi></mml:math>![Equation](images/F000121si69.png) dimensions. Given a corpus of training documents, the model learns these embeddings from data by training a classifier that uses them to predict each word token in the corpus based on its `context`, that is, the words in a small window before and after the current word ([Fig. 11.5](#Ch012_433-471_B9780443158889000121.xhtml#f0030)).

![Fig. 11.5 The CBOW word2vec model. Here, the word “jumps” is predicted given the words in a window before and after it (the context). Credit: James Foulds](images/F000121f11-05-9780443158889.jpg)
