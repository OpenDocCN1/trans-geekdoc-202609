# 10.1 INTRODUCTION

Machine maintenance is vital in any industry or plant that utilizes mechanical parts. It helps industries meet production schedules and improve safety by preventing accidents and injuries due to faulty machinery. Regular maintenance is the key point for enhancing the performance level and longevity of machines. The major classification of maintenance includes proactive and reactive maintenance. In reactive maintenance, only broken machines will be brought into working condition once they are found to be repaired [1]. But we need to wait until something goes wrong with the machine.

Proactive maintenance is carried out in three forms: preventive, conditional-based, and predictive maintenance [2]. In preventive maintenance, the machinery is maintained at predetermined intervals without considering the current state of the machines, whether it is good or bad. Condition-based maintenance does not require preplanning, but the machines need to be monitored regularly. Once a change is sensed, the machines start to display problems that can be sensed by the sensor, and the maintenance process is carried out. The third kind of proactive maintenance, predictive maintenance, is considered to be more important than the other two. Predictive maintenance combines the strength of preventive and conditional-based maintenance.

The condition and performance of the machines are regularly monitored and tracked using the real-time data collected using the sensors. The need for maintenance is predicted by analyzing the machines’ condition using this historical data. These predictions help the organization to prevent unexpected failure of the machines and hence avoid costly downtime.
