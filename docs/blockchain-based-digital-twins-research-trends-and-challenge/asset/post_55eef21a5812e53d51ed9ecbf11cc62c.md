# ABSTRACT

The concept of blockchain was first introduced in October 2008, as a mean of providing security features for Bitcoin transactions without the need for third-party intermediaries like banks. Blockchain technology operates on a peer-peer network, a distributed architecture that connects computing devices without a central server. Decentralization of data pooling, data immutability, and one-to-one communication between computers or Internet of things (IoT) devices are the key concepts of blockchain. Digital twins (DT) are virtual replicas of physical objects or systems, created using data and information from sensors, IoT devices, and other sources. By simulating the behavior and performance of real-world objects or systems, DTs can help organizations to better understand and optimize their operations, identify potential problems before they occur, and develop more effective maintenance and repair strategies. DTs are also used to support designers and engineers to test and refine their designs before they are physically built. When blockchain and DT technologies are combined they bring several benefits to various industries, especially those that rely on complex supply chains or critical infrastructure. Potential applications of these two technologies are supply chain management, asset management, smart contracts, and data sharing in various fields where security, privacy, transparency, and efficient management are the major concerns. The application where these two technologies are amalgamated is discussed in this chapter.

## 1.1.1 BLOCKCHAIN

In a centralized network, all nodes are connected to a single server. If the server fails, the operations of the other nodes will be paused and no processing of information can take place as all nodes depend on the central server. In contrast, in a decentralized network, there is no concept of a single server that monitors every other node. Each node in the network has its entity and contributes to the network. Even if a problem occurs in one node, it will not affect the flow of information and decision-making, as the other nodes in the network can continue to function. Since blockchain technology is a decentralized network, decision-making requires collaboration and consensus with other nodes [7].

The blockchain can be divided into three primary components: a distributed network, a shared ledger, and digital transactions:

- **1. Distributed Network**: The distributed network is made up of individual decision-making systems connected in a peer-to-peer network with nodes comprising network participants. Each member has an identical copy of the transactions and shares their information as a block after validating the data.
- **2. Shared Ledger**: This records the digital transactions of the users as blocks. In a shared or distributed ledger system, every transaction is shared and has a copy in each node of the peer-to-peer network. It provides a universal state layer and data that enables every actor on the internet, irrespective of different geographical locations and jurisdictions, to interact over the internet without the need for third parties like banks and to avoid double-spending problems. Any change to the shared ledger will be carried over to all copies that exist in the blockchain. Once a transaction is completed, it becomes immutable, and data cannot be tampered with or deleted by any single member in the network because every member has a copy of the complete transactions.
- **3. Digital Transactions**: These are valid information that is digitally saved in the network in an encrypted form and is authenticated by the digital signature of the person who added it. Transactions are added as blocks, and each block has a hashed cryptographic function that identifies the previous block in the blockchain.
