Storage Optimization of Blockchain: Due to the increased difficulty of running an actual copy of the ledger on a single node, Bruce introduced a groundbreaking method for managing Bitcoin transactions in which the network automatically deletes obsolete data. All non-empty addresses have their balances stored in an account tree database. A lightweight client could solve this issue. Alternatively, a new scheme called Ver Sum was developed to enable lightweight clients to exist. Ver Sum facilitates the delegation of intensive calculations over significant inputs by lightweight clients. By comparing computing results across different servers, it guarantees accuracy.

Redesigning Blockchain: The bitcoin-next generation (NG) proposal was made where the idea is to divide the fundamental block into two parts: the fundamental block, which is in charge of choosing a new leader, and the macroblocks, which is responsible for recording individual transactions. Time is separated into several eras according to the procedure. At the beginning of each epoch, miners must conduct a hash to produce a new fundamental block. Distributing macroblocks to other nodes is the node’s job that generates the critical block. Bitcoin-NG also developed the technique of the longest chain when macroblocks do not matter (heaviest chain). So, to solve this problem, a new blockchain is developed that does away with the need to prioritize block size above network security.
