Industry 3.0, also known as the computerized age, was widely adopted as a result of technological advancements starting from 1970. However, with the rise of the IoT, Industry 4.0, also called the connected age, reached its full potential. The Fourth Industrial Revolution was a watershed moment for the IoT. These years of industrial revolution are sometimes referred to as the “connected age” [1].

The advent of the IoT and soft computing laid the groundwork for semantic interoperability, which is the foundation of Industry 4.0 since it allows for a seamless flow of information between the digital and physical spheres in real time [2]. There are many technological pioneers and business leaders who are excited about the arrival of the Fifth Industrial Revolution (IR 5.0), despite the fact that Industry 4.0 is just getting started [3, 4 and 5]. Due to increased connectivity and cognitive technology, Industry 4.0 forecasts a rapid technological transformation in business and social practices [6]. Expert systems [7], cyber-physical systems [8], [9], cloud-based services [10], indoor industrial units, IoT, and AI [11] are examples of the latest technological developments that contribute to designing Industry 4.0.
