# 2.1.3 ANONYMITY

In blockchain technology, banking systems always maintain and identify valid transactions along with the valid person’s transaction details [5]. It can be easily tracked, but it does not provide enough anonymity for transactions. By using multiple addresses and interacting with the generated wallet address, a person can keep their identity private, and there is no central repository for a node’s private data. In each transaction, the addresses are associated with wallet addresses, and the transactions are publicly verified while maintaining their privacy. Still, the transaction cannot assure who issued or owned a wallet’s blockchain address. A central party does not request a user’s personally identifiable data anymore.

# 2.1.4 TRANSPARENCY

By providing decentralized and consistent transaction records, the blockchain network ensures wider network transparency. The importance of transparency cannot be underestimated in the supply chain and logistics sector networks. By constructing a transparent system, the blockchain reduces the number of forgeries, and it is obvious how the data are coordinated or automated in the procedures from inputs to several parties in smart contracts. Furthermore, the inherent transparency of blockchains frequently conflicts with organizational confidentiality rules and data protection regulations, which minimizes the transaction process.
