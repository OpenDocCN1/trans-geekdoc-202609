## 11.2.1 PROBLEM STATEMENT

Many countries' development depends on agriculture. Increasing world population and estimations show that in 2050 the population will be approximately 9 billion. Due to urbanization, people are moving to cities, specifically, 65% of people are residing in cities. It causes challenges in shelter and food production. Not only is the population increasing, but also the availability of areas is not. Due to this fact, agriculture needs to be improved in terms of production with fewer resources. The other main problem in agriculture is crops that are damaged due to diseases and insects. Even though insects help to maintain soil structure, fertility, and nutrients, pathogenic organisms like bacteria, viruses, and protozoa cause diseases in plants. This results in an average of 40% loss in agricultural production.

To overcome the problem, an idea of Digital technology is planned to monitor the farmland and crops. It enables the simulation models in the digital world to reflect in the physical world. ## 11.3 PROPOSED METHODOLOGY

Digital twin connects the virtual and real world by gathering-time data from IoT sensors and cameras. Through this process, the physical product can be represented in the digital world. This technology has a significant impact on development, production, and processes. In agricultural fields, IoT sensors such as optical sensors, electrochemical sensors, moisture sensors, location sensors, and temperature sensors are used to create a database. Additionally, the data may be stored either in a decentralized manner or centrally in the cloud. The collected data is processed using big data techniques. Subsequently, the data is tested and simulated in digital assets. Once the optimal solution is obtained from the simulation, it is applied to crops.

It helps in optimizing the performance of crops and plants. Digitalization of digital twins along with AI is more effective. Specifically, AI is employed to select the simulation options that have the highest chances so that AI reduces the amount of simulations to evaluate and to store. Blockchain contains the records of the data from simulations, which are then applied to crops based on the analysis of data. ### 11.3.2 IOT

IoT stands for Internet of Things. It is a technology that uses sensors to convert real-time data (analog) into machine-level data (digital). The proposed technology mainly involves embedded sensor systems, software, and other technologies (such as memory devices). For the proposed simulation, five sensors and a camera are required:

- **Moisture Sensor**: The proposed sensor is used to measure the humidity content of the soil. The default range is 0%RH–100%RH. It helps in reducing the water used for irrigation. If the moisture content in the soil is adequate, it stops the irrigation.
- **Temperature Sensor**: It is used to measure the temperature around the farmland. The default range is -40 to +80°C. It monitors the presence of heat in the soil. It helps to monitor whether the plant is in need of dry or wet conditions.
- **Optical Sensor**: It is used to measure the nitrogen rate. It collects data on the condition of clay, organic matter, and humidity properties of the soil.
- **Electrochemical Sensor**: These monitor the pH level and nutrient levels. Nutrients are essential for the plant to grow. By using the proposed sensor, nutrients can be provided based on the level.
- **Location Sensor**: These are positioned at different places throughout the farm field. They are used to measure the variety, height, and distance of the crop plants. They also receive information from Global Positioning System (GPS) satellites.
