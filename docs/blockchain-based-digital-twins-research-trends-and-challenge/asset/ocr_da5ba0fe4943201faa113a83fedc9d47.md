Virtual representations of patients (referred to as “health digital twins”) are created from the database of different categories of patients, their statistical medical data, and real-time modifications of patient and environmental factors are known as “physical twins” in the medical field. Human Digital Twin (HDT) can be used to simulate random impacts on the digital twin to offer insight into the predicted performance of the physical twin, presenting a ground-breaking potential in personalized medicine, diagnostic tests, and healthcare. Computational needs, data governance, clinical implementation, and product supervision are important things that need to be taken care of when converting HDT studies into clinical practice [7].

# 9.2 DEFINITION OF A DIGITAL TWIN

The digital twin is a process of modeling a system or a service that extends its lifecycle, incorporating modeling, machine learning, and inference to assist decision-making and is maintained with real-time data [8, 9]. It is divided into three parts:

+   - an object in a real-time environment;
- the digital twin is expressed in a software form; and
- data that connects the first two components.

A virtual model serves as an intermediary representation of the actual state of the thing it represents. Furthermore, it is specific to the item it represents rather than being merely general. The digital twins of two things that appear to be similar are frequently not the same [10, 11]. Figure 9.1 shows the process for defining a Digital Twin. Although a 2D or 3D CAD image is frequently connected with a digital twin, it is not necessary. A database, a list of equations, or a spreadsheet might make up the digital representation or digital model.
