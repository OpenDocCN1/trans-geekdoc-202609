
- 3. Distributed Machine Learning: In Healthcare 4.0, machine learning algorithms are utilized to analyze and predict the large amounts of data received from multiple DTs in the most effective way possible. Machine learning algorithms can help identify patterns and trends within the data that might be difficult or impossible for humans to detect. By analyzing the data in real-time, these algorithms can provide valuable insights and help healthcare providers make more informed decisions. This can lead to better health outcomes for patients and a more efficient healthcare system overall.

- 4. Decentralized Decision-Making: One of the key advantages of a decentralized healthcare system like healthcare 4.0 is that decisions can be made with the consensus of various independent entities within the system. Because the DTs are owned by independent entities, decisions must be made through a process of collaboration and consensus-building. This ensures that all stakeholders have a voice in the decision-making process and that decisions are made with a full understanding of all relevant data and perspectives. By leveraging the collective intelligence of all stakeholders within the system, decentralized healthcare can help to create a more efficient, effective, and equitable healthcare system for all.

- 5. Scalability and Robustness: To ensure that the Healthcare 4.0 system can handle various independent entities and their respective-DTs, it must be designed to be robust and capable of producing a combined output.

Because the system must maintain data from multiple independent sources, it must be able to handle and integrate diverse types of data in a way that ensures the accuracy and integrity of the data. To achieve this, the system must be designed with robustness in mind, with built-in redundancy and backup systems to ensure that data is not lost or corrupted. By combining data from multiple sources, the system can provide a more complete and accurate picture of a patient’s health, which can lead to better outcomes and a more efficient healthcare system overall.

One technology that is particularly useful in the healthcare system is the Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), which uses distributed ledger technology to ensure that all participants have the same information. This leads to more accurate predictions that can be agreed upon worldwide. DAG also provides better control over medical service providers, patients, and practitioners.

In addition, supply chains in the pharmaceutical industry can be built with transparency using blockchain technology. Smart contracts can also be used to streamline the claims process for insurance companies, ensuring that patients receive their benefits quickly and efficiently.

Many researchers proposed a semantic and scope of healthcare DT and a patient-centric mathematics was developed using a cloud data model which implements blockchain technology. They described the system protocol, its analytic features, and its feasibility. Related data of a patient can be collected for the DT to analyze and provide exact reasons for the current state of the patient and also predict the problems they may face in the future.

Public and private are two types of blockchain technology available. In a public blockchain, anyone can participate as a node, validate transactions, and write data onto the blockchain. This makes it a permissionless blockchain, meaning there are no restrictions on who can participate. Bitcoin and Ethereum are examples of popular public blockchain systems used for various applications.

In healthcare, there are also public blockchain systems that have been developed for storing and sharing medical data securely. Examples of public blockchains include MedRec, FHIR, BurstIQ, Showtime, among others. These systems aim to improve data sharing while maintaining privacy. 

Private blockchains are usually used in healthcare because of their enhanced security features. These systems require strict access control and privacy measures to protect sensitive data. They can also facilitate interoperability between different entities transparently. Some prominent private blockchains are Hyperledger Fabric, Sawtooth, Corda, Health Chain.

A framework that is a collaboration of blockchain and DT for providing alerting service like pandemic outbreaks, information for quarantine, etc., was developed [35]. They defined their framework with four layers, namely: physical layer, blockchain-based DT layer, data analytics layer, and decision-making layer. The physical layer would likely involve humans (doctors, nurses, pharmacy, government sector, etc.) and non-human participants (IoT devices like sensors, etc.) that collect data, which would then be fed into the DT layer. The data analytics layer would use machine learning and other algorithms to process and make sense of the data, while the decision-making layer would use this information to make clinical decisions. Each layer would likely have its unique challenges and opportunities as exposed in Figure 1.4.

Next is the blockchain-based DT which consists of DTs connected with blocks in the network. This node information for DTs is supplied by human and non-human participants. The data is registered by DT in terms of timestamp, owner of information, status (sensor readings), and transaction as a digital ledger. These IoT devices with sensors generate lots of digital data which they communicate with this blockchain network. The layer in the next level is the data analytics layer which consists of machine learning algorithms that receive data from the distributed ledger and give accurate predictions for decision-making. The data analytics is done with historical data stored with timestamps and live streaming data received from DT.

The next layer is the decision-making layer where the collaboration with various agencies give timely outputs like pandemic outbreaks, information for announcing quarantine, etc. It is not practical to store all patient data in every block of the blockchain. Data lakes are commonly used to store large amounts of unstructured and semi-structured data. They are designed to provide scalable storage that can handle both structured and unstructured data and allow for easy retrieval and analysis of data. In a healthcare context, data lakes can be used to store patient data that is not needed for every transaction on the blockchain but may be needed for future analysis or decision-making. The blockchain can then be used to store key metadata about the patient data, such as timestamps and hashes, which can be used to ensure the integrity and authenticity of the data.
