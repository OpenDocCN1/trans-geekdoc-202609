# 9.1 INTRODUCTION

An online representation of a real-world organization, item, or service known as a digital twin. A wind farm, a jet engine, as well as a larger entity like a skyscraper or a whole city, might have a digital twin. The physical item is modeled as a digital twin in this digital era. To collect data or information and predict the operation of physical assets and process the same, digital twin technology may be employed. It is also used to recreate both of these representations [1].

The strength of digital twins derives from the ability to better view realworld assets and data by linking them together [2]. Cross-functional teams can work together on the interactive and immersive design, development, testing, deployment, and operation of complex systems because of digital twins. They support businesses in understanding the history, seeing the current, and averting issues in the future. Insights from sales and marketing, assessment, 3D visualization, simulation, and forecasting are used to inform decision-making [3].
