# 3.3.4 TYPES OF BLOCKCHAINS

Blockchains can be divided into a variety of sorts, each with its unique characteristics, depending on how they are utilized. Generally speaking, there are three types of blockchains: public (or permissionless), private (or permitted), and consortium (or federated).

+   1. Public Blockchain: Because a public blockchain is an open and accessible network, anyone may join, conduct transactions, and take part in the consensus procedure. It also goes by the name “permissionless blockchain” and operates entirely decentralized and dispersed. Anyone can keep a copy of the blockchain and participate in the process of validating new blocks thanks to the permissionless blockchain. Typically, cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin and Ethereum employ this kind of blockchain technology. As a high number of anonymous nodes can often run on a permissionless blockchain, it is crucial to prevent malicious activity.

+   2. Private Blockchain: On the other hand, a private blockchain is an exclusive network controlled by a single entity. To publish or issue transactions on this blockchain, all parties must first receive permission from a validation process. This suggests that every node joining a private blockchain is a recognized and approved employee of a single company.
