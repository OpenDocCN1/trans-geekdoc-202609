
A private blockchain is often appropriate for a single enterprise solution and utilized as a distributed synchronized database to track information flows between several offices or people. Particularly, the incentive mechanism is not required for a private blockchain to function, hence a transaction processing charge is often not required.

- 3. **Consortium Blockchain**: If you're familiar with the settings of a private blockchain, you'll find that a consortium blockchain, also known as a federated blockchain, has a similar permission system. Consortium networks typically encompass more than one company and aid in keeping everyone on the same page. Information transactions between consortium members may be tracked using a consortium blockchain, which acts as an auditable and consistently synced distributed database. Consortium blockchains, like private blockchains, often incur no costs while processing transactions or publishing a new block, both in terms of money and computing resources. The consortium blockchain, in contrast to public and private blockchains, is widely used in massive industrial IoT systems.
