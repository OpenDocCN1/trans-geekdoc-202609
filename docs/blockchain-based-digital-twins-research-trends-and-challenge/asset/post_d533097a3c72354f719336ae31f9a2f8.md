## 1.2.4 DTS IN MINING

The use of DTs in remote monitoring and management of mine sites can help reduce operational costs and improve efficiency. By creating a digital replica of the physical mine site, operators can monitor and analyze real-time data from remote locations and use this information to identify potential issues, optimize processes, and make more informed decisions. This can also help improve safety by allowing operators to identify and respond to potential safety hazards in real time. The reliability of data generated on the site and immutably delivered to the DT is validated by blockchain technology.

DTs can be used to create a virtual replica of a mine or manufacturing site, which can be used for simulations, planning, and safety training. By creating virtual scenarios, workers can be trained in potentially dangerous situations without the risk of injury. In addition, DTs can be used to optimize production schedules and equipment maintenance, leading to increased productivity and reduced downtime. Overall, DTs have the potential to significantly improve safety, efficiency, and profitability in a variety of industries, including mining and manufacturing [30].

The use of DTs is significant in verifying the authenticity of commodities in supply chains. When coupled with Blockchain technology, the movement of a product can be monitored from its origin. DTs replicate the intended path of commodities, while Blockchain ensures that their behavior aligns with the DT, without any deviations. This tracking mechanism can detect any unlawful activity, as illegal mining, by observing any inconsistencies in the behavior of the commodities. By implementing this system, manufacturers can track their projects globally and significantly reduce theft.

## 1.2.5 DTS IN HEALTHCARE

When a patient in critical condition seeks medical attention from a new doctor, the doctor often requests a series of tests to assess the patient’s condition, even if these tests have already been performed and documented at a different medical facility. This can be due to a lack of connectivity and interoperability between different healthcare providers and information systems. As a result, the patient may have to undergo redundant tests, leading to unnecessary healthcare costs and a waste of time.

In healthcare systems, patient histories are digitally recorded as a chain of blocks in a network known as a distributed ledger. This ledger is secured by cryptography and timestamped to ensure its integrity and accuracy. The ledger is contributed to by various healthcare providers, such as nurses, surgeons, doctors, and lab technicians. Due to its decentralized nature, this data cannot be easily hacked or altered by any single party, since any changes made to the ledger must be validated by the other contributors in the network. This ensures that the data is reliable and secure and cannot be reversed or manipulated [31]. A simple use case of healthcare:

- Step 1: The information of the patient is collected by the health-care providers.
- Step 2: The patient information is stored in the database.
- Step 3: A hash key is created for each block of data and is given to the blockchain.
- Step 4: The patient key access to maintain the privacy of his/her data and decides whom to give access.
- Step 5: Once a patient has permitted a doctor to access their medical data, the smart contract code present in each block of the distributed ledger is executed. The code verifies the permission and authenticates the doctor’s identity. If the doctor’s identity is authenticated and permission is granted, the hash key is matched with the specific block where the data is stored. The smart contract then allows the doctor to access the patient’s data in that block, ensuring that the data is secure, tamper-proof, and only accessible to authorized parties.

Using blockchain technology in healthcare has two primary benefits. The first is that the system is patient-centric, which means patients own their medical records. However, they cannot modify or delete any data within the blockchain. The data is created by healthcare providers, and patients have access to it. Furthermore, no one can access a patient’s information without their explicit permission, which solves privacy concerns within the healthcare system.

The second advantage is that blockchain stores all the medical history of a patient in a secure, decentralized ledger. This means that when a patient goes to a new doctor or healthcare provider, they can access the patient’s complete medical history on the blockchain, without having to repeat any tests or procedures that have already been done. This improves the efficiency and effectiveness of healthcare delivery, as doctors can provide better, and more informed care based on the patient’s comprehensive medical history.

The outbreak of COVID-19 insists on using emerging technologies to handle the pandemic crisis. In order to acquire real-time data safely and securely, it is combined with blockchain technology, so when a case is referred worldwide the blockchain assures the history of data is present everywhere and it is immutable. A prototype of blockchain-based collaborative DT framework was discussed which fights against COVID-19 and forthcoming diseases and describes a decentralized epidemic alert system [32].

In Ref. [33], the authors have suggested various protocols for implementing the Internet of Things in Medicine (IoMT). Discussions about how medicine smart devices are interconnected were done and the application of various protocols like ZigBee protocol, Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), Time-Synchronized Mesh Protocol (TSMP), Near Field Communication (NFC), Wireless HART, etc., were explained.

Healthcare 4.0 utilizes DTs, blockchain technology, and the IoT to create an autonomous and data-driven healthcare system that enables decision-making by medical practitioners. In this system, data flows among interconnected devices in a decentralized and collaborative manner, allowing for a secure and timely exchange of information that can inform decision-making.

The predictive analytics that result from the amalgamation of these technologies can help medical practitioners make important decisions, such as recommending quarantine measures, lockdowns, and other restrictions in response to emerging health crises. This technology has the potential to significantly improve healthcare delivery by enabling more accurate and informed decision-making based on real-time data analysis.
