## 10.1 INTRODUCTION

Machine maintenance is vital in any industry or plant. It helps meet production schedules and improve safety due to faulty machinery. The major classification of maintenance includes proactive and reactive maintenance. In reactive maintenance, only broken machines will be brought into working condition once they are found to be repaired.

Proactive maintenance is carried out in three forms: preventive, conditional-based, and predictive maintenance. In preventive maintenance, the machinery is maintained at predetermined intervals without considering the current state whether it is good or bad. Condition-based maintenance does not require preplanning, but the machines need to be monitored. Once a change is sensed, the machines start to display problems that can be sensed by the sensor. The third kind of proactive maintenance, predictive maintenance, is considered to be more important than the other two. Predictive maintenance combines the strength of preventive and conditional-based maintenance.

The condition and performance of the machines are regularly monitored and tracked using the real-time data collected using the sensors. The need for maintenance is predicted by analyzing the machines’ condition using this historical data. These predictions help the organization to prevent unexpected failure of the machines and hence avoid costly downtime. The Internet of Things (IoT) plays a major role in monitoring regularly. With the advent of industrial automation and the technological boom, industries have shifted from condition-based maintenance to predictive maintenance.

The major benefits of predictive maintenance:

- 1. Fewer Failures of Machinery: Regular monitoring of the machine conditions and process systems helps to minimize failures by predicting the need for maintenance in advance.
- 2. Increased Equipment Lifespan: Prevention of failure reduces the severity of impairment and the dissemination of defects. It helps to improve the lifespan of machines.
- 3. Effective Management/Decision Making: The ability of predictive maintenance to use real-time data enables managers to make decisions on when to replace machinery.
- 4. Increased Safety: Early prediction of problems minimizes the possibility of fatal accidents and injuries.
- 5. Cost Reduction: As predictive maintenance predicts the health status of the machines and decides when the parts need to be replaced or repaired, the need for unnecessary inspection and maintenance can be avoided. Hence, the cost incurred due to this unnecessary maintenance can be eliminated.
