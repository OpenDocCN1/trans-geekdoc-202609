# 7.2.3 MANUFACTURING

There aren’t many evaluations in this field, Qi & Tao [26] provides a complete “360” viewpoint for big data associated with digital twins in engineering and industrial scenery, making it stand out as the first worth noting. It analyzes the many technologies that enable Digital Twins and argues that these cutting-edge tools are crucial to the development of smart manufacturing. Production may be optimized with the use of machine learning algorithms and “virtual clones” of a system. This study classifies important aspects of industrial development in order to come up with their aims and ideas for applying a digital twin to the manufacturing system. In which, companies may evaluate, repeat, and perfect their manufacturing procedures in a virtual environment, resulting in greater efficacy and efficiency [27].

According to Xu et al. [28], there are issues with the availability of samples for generating exact AI algorithms for new industrial processes, which is something that is also necessary for precise Digital Twins. He recommends using AI to help with problem diagnosis in a smart manufacturing setting. To guarantee that problems are kept to a minimum, the model’s frontrunning makes use of a Digital Twin, which can learn and diagnose mistakes while supplying training data. The first step of the method collects data, which may subsequently be used for transfer learning in the second phase. A Digital Twin and transfer learning is used to enhance a fault-diagnosis system. The notion is put to the test and analyzed by the writers in a real-world case study that has to do with the production of autos.
