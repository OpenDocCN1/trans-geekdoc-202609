# ABSTRACT

Digital twins (DTs) have been made possible by the quick development of computer, storage, communications, and networking technology. It is undoubtedly the digitized version of any physical feature, item, or machine. Further, it can be used for prototyping, simulation, validation, and 3-D design before the real component is built. A DT can be employed for configuration, surveillance, diagnostics, and prognostics once a physical component has been put into service. It is anticipated that the Internet of Things (IoT) and DTs will attract a lot of attention soon and will be crucial for the Healthcare Industry. However, current DT construction techniques, platforms, and tools are heavily centralized and unable to provide trustworthy data provenance, audit, and traceability. The information about transactions, logs, and history is neither safe nor impenetrable. A blockchain-based development process for DTs has been designed to enable secure and reliable traceability, availability, and data integrity of transactions, records, and data provenance. Few methods employ smart contracts to control and monitor actions taken by those participating in DT generation. This chapter deals with the analysis of Blockchain-based DT for healthcare applications. It also provides the various integration constraints and challenges faced in the healthcare Industry.

# 9.1 INTRODUCTION

An online representation of a real-world organization, item, or service known as a digital twin. A wind farm, a jet engine, as well as a larger entity like a skyscraper or a whole city, might have a digital twin. The physical item is modeled as a digital twin in this digital era. To collect data or information and predict the operation of physical assets and process the same, digital twin technology may be employed. It is also used to recreate both of these representations [1].

The strength of digital twins derives from the ability to better view real-world assets and data by linking them together [2]. Cross-functional teams can work together on the interactive and immersive design, development, testing, deployment, and operation of complex systems because of digital twins. They support businesses in understanding the history, seeing the current, and averting issues in the future. Insights from sales and marketing, assessment, 3D visualization, simulation, and forecasting are used to inform decision-making [3]. A digital twin is often computer software that uses data from the real world to replicate how a product or service might function. These programs can boost output using industry 4.0 technologies such as software analytics, artificial intelligence, and the IoT [4, 5 and 6].

Digital twins are created by integrating conceptual models using Building Information Modeling (BIM) and Geographic Information System (GIS) or scanning them to examine and analyze actual items in the real world with business and IoT data. Various data sources, including information and models, may be gathered, organized, and presented as realistic, interactive visualizations using a real-time 3D-enabled digital twin. A computer graphics technology called real-time 3D produces dynamic content faster than the human eye can see it.

Digital twins are representations in the virtual realm of the loads, connections, and movements that assets may encounter in the physical world. Customers may engage with dynamic, three-dimensional, and responsive content in real-time. They may instantly view the findings on any platform, including portable devices, computers, and augmented, mixed, and virtual reality (AR/MR/VR) devices and they can precisely simulate real-world events, what-if scenarios, and any situation conceivable in the virtual environment. Every deployment of a digital twin is different. The complexity and business effect of deployments often increase with each stage that is added. A DT can be anything from a 3D product configuration to an accurate depiction of a city-sized network or system with each of its parts dynamically connected to engineering, construction, and operating data.

The virtual models have emerged as a foundation in contemporary engineering to promote innovation and boost efficiency as a result of the development of machine learning as well other factors including big data. In other words, having one might help see important technological trends, and prevent expensive physical item breakdowns and testing procedures and services utilizing improved quantitative, monitoring, and forecasting skills.

Virtual representations of patients (referred to as “health digital twins”) are created from the database of different categories of patients, their statistical medical data, and real-time modifications of patient and environmental factors are known as “physical twins” in the medical field. Human Digital Twin (HDT) can be used to simulate random impacts on the digital twin to offer insight into the predicted performance of the physical twin. Computational needs, clinical implementation, and product supervision are important things that need to be taken care of when converting HDT studies into clinical practice [7].

# 9.2 DEFINITION OF A DIGITAL TWIN

The digital twin is a process of modeling a system or a service that extends its lifecycle, incorporating machine learning, and inference to assist decision-making and is maintained with real-time data [8, 9]. It is divided into three parts:

- an object in a real-time environment;
- the digital twin is expressed in a software form;
- data that connects the first two.

A virtual model serves as an intermediary representation of the actual state of the thing it represents. Furthermore, it is specific to the item it represents rather than being merely general. The digital twins of two things that appear to be similar are frequently not the same [10, 11]. Figure 9.1 shows the process for defining a Digital Twin. Although a 2D or 3D CAD image is frequently connected with a digital twin, it is not necessary. A database, a list of equations, or a spreadsheet might make up the digital representation or digital model.
