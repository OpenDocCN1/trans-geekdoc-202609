All the candidate blocks will be competing with each other. And at the end, the block that wins the race will be updated in the final central ledger.

The consensus algorithm may be termed the heart of any blockchain environment, also known as a crucial component of the blockchain network. Each and every blockchain network needs a protocol or rule required to be defined, which can bind participants or network members together who are involved in the blockchain network. So, when a centralized authority is missing in any network, we find another alternative to a central authority. Consensus algorithm acts as that term in the blockchain. The consensus algorithm answers the following questions in the blockchain network.

- Who can create the transaction?
- How is the transaction validated?
- How are transactions approved?
- How are blocks generated?
- How would consensus be achieved?
- How can multiple network participants approve or agree to a specific fact/rule?

Specific rules and regulations are required if we want no one to derail the system. These rules and regulations will take care of everything. There is no dependency on anyone. Everyone has to follow a group of rules/scenarios. How consensus achieves this without a central authority. The network behaves smoothly with an extensive blockchain network. Similarly, the Consensus Algorithm takes care of all the processes in the blockchain network.

## 3.4 CHALLENGES AND THE ROAD AHEAD

While blockchain has enormous promise, it currently needs to be improved to several issues that prevent its widespread adoption. We list some significant difficulties and promising developments.

## 3.4.1 SCALABILITY

When more and more transactions are added to the blockchain, managing it becomes more cumbersome. To verify blocks on the blockchain, every node must keep a copy of every transaction ever made, regardless of whether or not the funds from which it come are available. It is slow to build because its blocks are enormous, and each one takes a long time to set up; the Bitcoin blockchain cannot perform real-time transactions. Currently, only around seven transactions can be processed per second on the network. Miners prioritize transactions with a high fee; many low-value transactions may be delayed because of the limited size of blocks.

To solve the blockchain’s scalability issue, several solutions have been offered. These solutions may be broken down into two groups:

1. Storage Optimization of Blockchain: Due to the increased difficulty of running an actual copy of the ledger on a single node, Bruce introduced a groundbreaking method for managing Bitcoin transactions in which the network automatically deletes obsolete data. All non-empty addresses have their balances stored in an account tree database. A lightweight client could solve this issue. Alternatively, a new scheme called Ver Sum was developed to enable lightweight clients to exist. Ver Sum facilitates the delegation of intensive calculations over significant inputs by lightweight clients. By comparing computing results across different servers, it guarantees accuracy.

2. Redesigning Blockchain: The bitcoin-next generation (NG) proposal was made where the idea is to divide the fundamental block into two parts: the fundamental block, which is in charge of choosing a new leader, and the macroblocks, which is responsible for recording individual transactions. Time is separated into several eras according to the procedure. At the beginning of each epoch, miners must conduct a hash to produce a new fundamental block. Distributing macroblocks to other nodes is the node’s job that generates the critical block. Bitcoin-NG also developed the technique of the longest chain when macroblocks do not matter (heaviest chain). So, to solve this problem, a new blockchain is developed that does away with the need to prioritize block size above network security.

## 3.4.2 PRIVACY LEAKAGE

Using public and private keys in blockchain technology suggests that privacy may be somewhat preserved. Users can conduct secure financial transactions using their public key without revealing personal information. As illustrated, however, blockchain cannot provide transactional privacy to the public nature of all transaction values. In addition, recent research shows that it is possible to correlate Bitcoin transactions and expose the user’s personal information. This holds even if the users are behind a NAT or firewall. IP addresses may be linked to their pseudonyms according to a technique introduced by Birukov. Each client has a unique collection of connected nodes that serve as its fingerprint. However, the origin of a transaction may be tracked by learning this set. Several different approaches have been suggested to increase the privacy of blockchain, broadly falling into two categories:

- Blending: Users' addresses on a blockchain are anonymous. However, many users use the same address repeatedly, it is still possible to connect addresses to actual people. A mixing service is a service that allows users to remain anonymous by sending money to several recipients. Alice, who has address A, and lives at B, wants to send some cash to Bob. Alice and Bob's connection may be exposed if she conducts a direct transaction with input address and output address B. For this reason, Alice could give Carol a monetary gift who worked as a gobetween. Carol's transfer to Bob has several inputs and many outputs. Bob's home address is one of those included in the output. It makes it more difficult to reveal Alice and Bob's relationship. However, a dishonest third party may purposefully reveal private information about Alice and Bob. Carol may not send Bob's money but instead to her own house. When using Mix currency, this is a simple method to avoid unethical behavior. The user's requirements, such as the amount of money and the date of the transfer, are encrypted using the intermediary's private key. Moreover, if the go-between did not have the promised cash, anybody could easily confirm that the middleman had been deceived. Despite this, theft still needs to be stopped once it is discovered. The CoinJoin protocol uses a mixing server to randomly reorder the output addresses to avoid theft. Coin Shuffle is an address shuffling algorithm that uses decryption mix nets inspired by CoinJoin.
