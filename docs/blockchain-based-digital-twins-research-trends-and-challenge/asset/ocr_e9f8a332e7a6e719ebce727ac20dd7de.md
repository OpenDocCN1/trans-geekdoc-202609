When many individuals need to exchange IoT data in a secure manner, this incorporation would characterize a significant paradigm shift. For instance, comprehensive tracking of food items is crucial for guaranteeing food safety. Multiple stakeholders, as those engaged in production, feeding, handling, distribution, etc., may need to be included in food traceability efforts. Better management in these extents might upset in protection for food, enhance data sharing among participants, and save search time during a foodborne epidemic, all of which could save lives. Trustworthy data exchange may also boost the services and adoption of ecosystems in other ranges, such as smart cities and smart vehicles. Therefore, using blockchain technology can improve the IoT by providing more reliable and protected data. We believe there has never been an in-depth study of the challenges inherent in the development and rollout of DTs built on top of distributed ledger technology.

Recent research, however, has mostly focused on blockchains' potential in DTs. The findings of the considerably more crucial study classify DTs and shed light on their stages, suggested and implemented iterations, enabling methods, and potential fundamental needs. Case studies of DTs and collaboration in the automotive sector are shown, and the author discusses the advantages of adopting blockchains for DTs. Scalability issues, data privacy issues, resource-constrained device issues, and interoperability issues brought by different consensus mechanisms, negotiation systems, and smart agreements are all discussed in this research, along with other technological and societal obstacles to the widespread use of blockchain in DTs. Although it includes security features like confidentiality and integrity that are necessary to safeguard DT’s design and operational data, it glosses over other safety-related design aspects like security safeguards and part immunity.
