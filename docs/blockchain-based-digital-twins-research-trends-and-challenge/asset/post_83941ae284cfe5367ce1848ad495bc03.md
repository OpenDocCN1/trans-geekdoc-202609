## 9.4.2 ADVANTAGES OF DIGITAL TWINS

The benefits of DTs include both long and short-term. Digital twins will allow BIM to continue to provide value through the asset's lifespan in the long run. The focus of immediate gains is on operations, offering a single point of reference that could assist in saving costs, lowering risk and uncertainty, and boosting productivity.

-   For Owners: Smarter Decisions, Better Return on Investment: Owners have a difficult time managing, monitoring, and ultimately maximizing the value of their facilities when data is scattered across separate systems. Users may experience experience scenarios and get extensive data because of digital twins. It will maximize performance and lengthen the lifespan of their assets, assisting them in making smarter business decisions from the planning and design phases on.

-   Smoother Operations and Simpler Maintenance for Managers: Without having to fight with ambiguous (or nonexistent) maintenance documentation, owners and operators may begin running assets effectively right away. Owners and operators have an unsolvable problem while developing systems integrated with all forms for monitoring, controlling, and optimizing their benefits due to the transmission of analog, unclassified, and disconnected data. Due to this, owners and operators of smart buildings are unable to benefit from them and are instead left with disjointed data, erroneous information, and limited knowledge of how their assets are working. To manage things maintenance and optimizing energy use, DTs offer a digital thread that integrates cumulative data in a straightforward manner, enabling operators to concentrate on their responsibilities.

Data from facilities are always changing. Managers can see who are making adjustments and thanks to digital twins. To save waste and improve safety, managers can even forecast and labor cycles.

Businesses utilize digital twin technology for a wide range of purposes, including streamlining existing operations, training staff, and testing new goods or procedures before actually launching them in the real world, where they are more expensive and difficult to rectify any flaws.

-   To investigate the operations model portrayed by the digital twin, machine learning and artificial intelligence techniques are routinely used, regardless of wherever the actual facility is placed, even if the hardware is in space. To operate, maintain, and repair equipment while you are not physically close to them, NASA has employed pairing technology, the forerunner to digital twin technology, since the beginning of space travel. The Apollo 13 mission was saved in precisely by engineers and astronauts on Earth. Now, NASA uses digital twins to investigate future vehicles and planes.

-   They have the chance to mimic the application before it goes live; digital twin technology can assist staff in becoming familiar with the Internet of Things installation and automation. The physical environment can then benefit from the insights gained and possibilities discovered through a digital twin.

-   Providing a real-time image of what is occurring with machines or other physical assets, digital twins have proven to be very helpful in manufacturing to decrease maintenance concerns and assure optimal production productivity. By using digital twin technology on machinery in oil fields and refineries, Chevron anticipates saving millions of dollars in repairs and maintenance by 2024.

-   Digital twins might revolutionize patient care and healthcare processes. They can help in manufacturing. Surgery and other medical operations can be carried out on mannequins instead of real people due to the usage of digital twins of patients or organs. -   Even Singapore has a digital doppelanger. Consider all the factors that affect municipal management. City planners may better understand and manage energy use with the aid of digital twin technology, which also has a variety of uses that can enhance the quality of life for city residents.

## 9.4.4 FEATURES OF DIGITAL TWINS

-   1. Greater Efficiency: Before a new product enters production, digital twins can help in monitoring or mirroring production processes to attain and maintain maximum efficiency during the entire manufacturing process.

-   2. Better R&D: Due to the volume of data generated on expected performance results, the usage of DTs facilitates efficient product research and design. Before beginning production, organizations may be able to make necessary product improvements with the use of this data and insights.

-   3. Product End-of-Life: Digital twins can be used by producers to inform decisions on how to handle items that are now approaching the end of their lives and require final processing, such as recycling or other processes. They can decide which production materials to buy by employing digital twins.

-   4. Businesses and Enterprises Using Digital Twins: The adoption of digital twins is not required for all products or businesses, although they have many benefits. Not every item has the level of complexity that calls for the continual, intense inflow of sensor data that digital twins require. Furthermore, it may not always be advantageous to spend a significant sum of money on the development of a digital twin.

-   5. Better Availability and Dependability: As a practical method to boost system performance, monitoring, simulating, and controlling a network or asset is a good idea.

-   6. Reduced Risk: By decreasing asset and process-related events and preventing unscheduled downtime, researchers may safeguard the environment, the health and safety of the employees, and the company's goals.

-   7. Reduced Maintenance Expenses: Identify problems before they become breakdowns, place component orders, and plan repairs at periods that won't interfere with production objectives.

-   8. Enhanced Production: Ensure product quality by having realtime visibility into the operation of assets and processes to drive customization, respond to it, and reduce supply chain effects.
