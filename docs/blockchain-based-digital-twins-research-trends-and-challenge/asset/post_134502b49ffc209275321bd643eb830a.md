## 1.1.1.1 BASIC OPERATIONS OF BLOCKCHAIN

Figure 1.1 shows the basic architecture of blockchain where one block consists of various types of information such as network transactions, security-related documents, and product prototypes. In, the first block will be called a genesis block which means it has no previous blocks. When new information is added, it becomes a block with a reference to the previous block, forming a chain of immutable information. These chains of blocks are saved as digital ledgers across the network, which get updated with every new block added to the chain. Since the blockchain is organized in a decentralized manner, even if one block fails, the information is still available in other blocks, ensuring no loss of data. The blockchain appends new or updated information as blocks to the existing blockchain, with the history of the previous block being recorded and unalterable. Therefore, records cannot be tampered with [8].

## 1.1.1.2 BLOCKCHAIN WITH IOT

Blockchain technology has emerged as a potential solution for securing the growing volumes of real-time data generated by IoT devices and other IoT devices. With its decentralized and tamper-proof architecture, the blockchain offers a high level of security and transparency, which is crucial for ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the data. Additionally, the blockchain's ability to support smart contracts enables the automation of various processes and facilitates the efficient exchange of value, thereby enhancing the overall efficiency and effectiveness of the IoT systems. As a result, the blockchain is increasingly being integrated with IoT devices and other IoT devices to enable secure and intelligent real-time data exchange and processing [9].

Blockchain technology has the potential to bring significant benefits to various industries, including healthcare, retail, disaster management, and smart city planning. In the healthcare industry, for example, blockchain can be used to securely store and share patient data among healthcare providers, leading to better diagnosis, treatment, and care for patients. In the retail industry, blockchain can be used to create a more personalized customer experience by securely tracking and sharing customer data. In disaster management, blockchain can be used to provide an immutable and transparent record of relief efforts, ensuring that resources are being allocated effectively. And in smart city planning, blockchain can be used to securely store and share data on energy usage, transportation, and other infrastructure, leading to more efficient and sustainable cities.

Blockchain technology has the potential to reshape the future by providing secure, transparent, and decentralized solutions for various industries. Its immutability and programmability make it a more apt technology for a wide range of applications in the fields of finance, supply chain management, healthcare, and more. It eliminates the need for trusted intermediaries, allowing for a peer-to-peer network where participants can directly interact with each other without the need for intermediaries like banks, brokers, or governments. This not only reduces transaction costs but also provides greater transparency and security for the participants. As blockchain technology continues to evolve, it will likely become more integrated into our everyday lives and become a critical component of global business.

## 1.1.2 DIGITAL TWINS

With the advent of DTs, the simulation applications are more enhanced and experimentally advantageous as experimental DTs communicate with each other in the virtual world. By creating a virtual representation of a physical object and its environment, a DT provides a path for experimentation and testing in a controlled environment without the need for costly physical prototypes or risking damage to real-life objects. This enables more rapid and efficient development, optimization, and maintenance of various types of systems and processes. Additionally, the ability for DTs to communicate and collaborate with other DTs, both virtually and in the physical world, opens up more new possibilities for advanced modeling, simulation, and optimization of complex systems [10].

DTs allow for testing and analyzing the behavior of a physical object in a virtual environment before it is built in the real world. By receiving feedback from sensors and simulating various scenarios, DTs can predict the performance and potential problems of a product, which helps in reducing the time and cost of development. Additionally, the integration of various technologies like IoT, AI, and CC makes DTs more powerful and versatile in predicting and analyzing the behavior of physical objects as given in Figure 1.2.

- 1. IoT: It is a key technology in the development and operation of DTs as it provides the means to collect real-time data from sensors attached to physical objects [11]. This data is then used to create a digital replica or twin of the physical object. The DT is then used to simulate and analyze the performance of the physical object in various conditions and to identify any potential issues before their occurrence in the real world.
- 2. Extended Reality (XR): The data collected from IoT sensors is used to create a DT, which is a virtual representation of a physical object or system. This DT can be visualized in various ways, including 3D and 4D (with time as the fourth dimension) visualizations, allowing for a better understanding of the physical object and its behavior.
- 3. Cloud Technology (CC): It plays a crucial role in DT, as it provides the necessary infrastructure for storing and processing the vast amounts of data collected by IoT. The data can be stored in cloud-based servers, and DTs can be created using this data, which can then be accessed from anywhere in the world with an internet connection. This allows for easy collaboration and sharing of data, and also enables remote monitoring and management of physical objects.
- 4. AI: DTs are used to build simulations that replicate the behavior of physical objects in real life, which can then be analyzed and used to make informed decisions. The ability to simulate and predict how real-world objects will behave in different scenarios can be incredibly valuable in fields like engineering, manufacturing, and healthcare. By using DTs to model and test different scenarios, organizations can make more informed decisions and reduce the cost of costly errors or mistakes.

The concept of DTs was introduced by David Gelandt in his book Mirror Worlds in 1991. Later, in 2002, Michael Grieves from the University of Michigan coined the term “DT” and applied it to manufacturing. NASA then adopted the concept in 2010 to create digital simulations of spacecraft and capsules for testing before launching them into space. Since then, the use of DTs has expanded to various fields and industries.

The data collected by IoT components is stored in DT using appropriate data structures and user interfaces to ensure that the data is stored in an organized and usable format. This helps in simulating and analyzing the behavior of the physical entity in various conditions and predicting its performance.
