The second advantage is that blockchain stores all the medical history of a patient in a secure, decentralized ledger. This means that when a patient goes to a new doctor or healthcare provider, they can access the patient’s complete medical history on the blockchain, without having to repeat any tests or procedures that have already been done. This improves the efficiency and effectiveness of healthcare delivery, as doctors can provide better, and more informed care based on the patient’s comprehensive medical history.

The outbreak of COVID-19 insists on using emerging technologies to handle the pandemic crisis. In order to acquire real-time data safely and securely, it is combined with blockchain technology, so when a case is referred worldwide the blockchain assures the history of data is present everywhere and it is immutable. A prototype of blockchain-based collaborative DT framework was discussed which fights against COVID-19 and forthcoming diseases and describes a decentralized epidemic alert system [32].

In Ref. [33], the authors have suggested various protocols for implementing the Internet of Things in Medicine (IoMT). Discussions about how the medicine smart devices are interconnected for improving patient services were done and the application of various protocols like ZigBee protocol, Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), Time-Synchronized Mesh Protocol (TSMP), Near Field Communication (NFC), Wireless HART, etc., which are used to implement IoMT were explained.

Healthcare 4.0 utilizes DTs, blockchain technology, and the IoT to create an autonomous and data-driven healthcare system that enables decision-making by medical practitioners. In this system, data flows among interconnected devices in a decentralized and collaborative manner, allowing for a secure and timely exchange of information that can inform medical decision-making.
