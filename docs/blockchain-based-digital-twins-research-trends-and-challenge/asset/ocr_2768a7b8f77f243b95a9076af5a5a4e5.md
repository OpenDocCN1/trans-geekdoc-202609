The blockchain can be divided into three primary components: a distributed network, a shared ledger, and digital transactions:

+   1. **Distributed Network**: The distributed network is made up of individual decision-making systems connected in a peer-to-peer network with nodes comprising network participants. Each member has an identical copy of the transactions and shares their information as a block after validating the data.

+   2. **Shared Ledger**: This records the digital transactions of the users as blocks. In a shared or distributed ledger system, every transaction is shared and has a copy in each node of the peer-to-peer network. It provides a universal state layer and data that enables every actor on the internet, irrespective of different geographical locations and jurisdictions, to interact over the internet without the need for third parties like banks and to avoid double-spending problems. Any change to the shared ledger will be carried over to all copies that exist in the blockchain network. Once a transaction is completed, it becomes immutable, and data cannot be tampered with or deleted by any single member in the network because every member has a copy of the complete transactions.

+   3. **Digital Transactions**: These are valid information that is digitally saved in the network in an encrypted form and is authenticated by the digital signature of the person who added it. Transactions are added as blocks, and each block has a hashed cryptographic function that identifies the previous block in the blockchain.
