## 1.2.6 BLOCKCHAIN IN EDUCATION

Blockchain technology can be used to securely store and share educational credentials, as well as to provide a transparent and tamper-proof way of verifying the authenticity of educational and financial records. By storing student records on a blockchain, educational institutions can provide a secure and verifiable way for employers and other organizations to verify the credentials of students. This can greatly reduce the time and cost associated with background checks and other verification processes [36].

In addition, blockchain can be used to streamline the process of disbursing grants and scholarships. By using smart contracts, funds can be automatically dissursed to eligible students based on predetermined criteria, such as academic performance or financial need. This can help reduce the administration associated managing grant and scholarship programs, while also ensuring that funds are distributed fairly and transparently.

Blockchain technology enables the creation of a tamper-proof digital record that can be accessed by students and institutions alike. This eliminates the need for paper-based records that can easily get lost, damaged, or altered. By storing education records on a blockchain, students can have greater control over their data and share it with institutions or potential employers as needed. Educational institutions can also benefit from improved record keeping, as they can easily track student progress and ensure that their records are up-to-date and accurate.

## 1.2.7 BLOCKCHAIN IN CRYPTURRENCY

A cryptocurrency, or crypto, is a decentralized digital currency that uses blockchain technology to record and secure every single transaction. The “crypto” in cryptocurrency refers to cryptography that allows for the creation and processing of digital currencies that do not allow counter-feiting or spending multiple times [37].

The first decentralized cryptocurrency using blockchain technology created is Bitcoin (BTC). There are plenty of other cryptocurrencies known as altcoins (Alternative Coins) other than Bitcoin. Some of the altcoins are Tether (USDT), Ethereum (ETH), Cardano (ADA), USD Coin (USDC), Binance Coin, XRP, Litecoin, Polkadot, IOTA, Ripple, etc. During the year 2022, CoinMarketCap provided a source that there are around 22,000 cryptocurrencies worth $850 billion.

Few advantages of cryptocurrency:

- A cryptocurrency transaction is a transfer of a variety of cryptocurrencies anywhere in the world with little time on the blockchain.
- Transactions can be done easily through mobile, desktop, or hardware wallets at a low cost without having a bank account.
- Decentralized cryptocurrencies make secure systems as they use the techniques of cryptography and blockchain security.
- Crypto markets work the clock without any break.

Disadvantages of cryptocurrency:
- Understanding cryptocurrency takes time and effort.
- It is difficult for the government to track the exchange of money involving illegal transactions.
- Crypto mining requires special hardware like Graphics Processing Units, Solid-State Drives for crypto mining, Application-Specific Integrated Circuits, mining software, and a wallet.
- Crypto mining cannot be done on regular computers.
- There is no guarantee of protecting our business as there is no involvement of the Financial Conduct Authority.
- There is a chance of stopping/moving to new cryptocurrency stakeholders.
- No reverse transaction can be created if someone mistakenly sends funds to the wrong wallet address.
- Cryptocurrencies are not accepted everywhere and have limited value.

## 1.2.8 BLOCKCHAIN APPLICATION IN VOTING

The foundation of any democratic country is its election process, which citizens exercise their right to vote for the candidates they believe in. In India, representatives are elected through elections at all levels, from local bodies and panchayats to parliament. It is crucial that the election process is both transparent and reliable to maintain the credibility of the entire system. If the process is only reliable, citizens will have confidence in the system and if transparency is lacking, citizens may choose to ignore the system, which poses a significant risk to democracy [38].

In India, before 1997, the ballot box method was the primary means of conducting elections. Voters would mark their desired candidate's symbol on a printed electoral chart and cast their vote. However, in 1997, the electronic voting machine was introduced as an alternative method for conducting elections, and it has been the sole means of voting since 2004. The Election Commission of India is implementing continuous evaluation in the election process to ensure more efficient, transparent, reliable elections. Despite the use of the Electronic Voting Machine system, fraudulent voting continues to occur in elections. To address these and other issues, the introduction of blockchain and DLTs in the electoral process could be beneficial. Challenges such as voter anonymity, vote confidentiality, and end-to-end verification for voters to confirm that the vote they cast is the one they intended can be effectively addressed and implemented with these technologies.
