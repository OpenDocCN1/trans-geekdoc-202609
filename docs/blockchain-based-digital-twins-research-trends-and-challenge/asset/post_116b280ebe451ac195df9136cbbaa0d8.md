### 7.3.3 COMPONENTS OF BLOCKCHAIN

- 1. **Data Block**: Blocks that make up a blockchain reveal events that have taken place on the platform. The transactional data can be provided as information sharing or as a token transmission procedure that takes place in a system.

- 2. **Ledger Distribution**: A distributed ledger is a database that is replicated and shared among all nodes in a network. Any user on the network can access this central repository of information. Keeping track of deals is similar to how people in a network exchange information with one another. In a decentralized setting, where no trusted party is required to supervise the system's operation, the participating nodes can automatically come to an agreement using a well-established consensus method. Since each entry in the ledger is stamped with a unique cryptographic signature and timestamp, the entire database can be verified and never altered after it has been created. Any alteration to the transaction will produce a new hash inside its branch, which can be easily detected using computational speed and efficiency.

- 3. **Consensus Algorithm**: In a blockchain system, there are no centralized organizations to control and enforce the standards governing transactions or protect data from security risks. Consensus algorithms seek to update the replicated shared states and ensure that at any given time, all replicas of the shared states are synchronized and in agreement. A blockchain's consensus method is a technique for several untrustworthy nodes to decide on a single data log.

- 4. **Smart Contracts**: To monitor and conduct transactions in line with specified terms and conditions, blockchain applications known as smart contracts are used. To be more precise, a smart contract is a “digital representation of a temporary economic agreement between two or more parties.” Unlike traditional contracts, which rely on centralized authorizing entities to enforce their terms and conditions, smart contracts on a blockchain network do not require such intermediaries to function properly. Smart contracts have become more popular in the blockchain community since the first smart contract platform, Ethereum, was released in 2015. Another name for a smart contract is an “autonomous agent” or a “self-executing engine.” As soon as the criteria specified in the contract are satisfied, the code automatically carries out the contract’s established provisions, a concept known as “self-execution.”

### 3.3.4 TYPES OF BLOCKCHAINS

Blockchains can be divided into a variety of sorts, each with its unique characteristics, depending on how they are utilized. Generally speaking, there are three types of blockchains: public (or permissionless), private (or permitted), and consortium (or federated).

- 1. Public Blockchain: Because a public blockchain is an open and accessible network, anyone may join, conduct transactions, and take part in the consensus procedure. It also goes by the name “permissionless blockchain” and operates entirely decentralized and dispersed. Anyone can keep a copy of the blockchain and participate in the process of validating new blocks thanks to the permissionless blockchain. Typically, cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin and Ethereum employ this kind of blockchain technology. As a high number of anonymous nodes can often run on a permissionless blockchain, it is crucial to prevent malicious activity.

- 2. Private Blockchain: On the other hand, a private blockchain is an exclusive network controlled by a single entity. To publish or issue transactions on this blockchain, all parties must first receive permission from a validation process. This suggests that every node joining a private blockchain is a recognized and approved employee of a single company.
