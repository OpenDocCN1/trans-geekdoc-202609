If the moisture content in the soil is adequate, it stops the irrigation because, during that time, the crop doesn’t need water.
2. **Temperature Sensor**: It is used to measure the temperature around the farmland. The default range is -40 to +80°C. It monitors the presence of heat in the soil. It helps to monitor whether the plant is in need of dry or wet conditions.
3. **Optical Sensor**: It is used to measure the nitrogen rate. It collects data on the condition of clay, organic matter, and humidity properties of the soil.
4. **Electrochemical Sensor**: These monitor the pH level and nutrient levels. Nutrients are essential for the plant to grow. By using the proposed sensor, nutrients can be provided based on the level.
5. **Location Sensor**: These are positioned at different places throughout the farm field. They are used to measure the variety, height, and distance of the crop plants. They also receive information from Global Positioning System (GPS) satellites.
