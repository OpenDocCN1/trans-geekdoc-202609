# 5.4.1.4 STATISTICAL MODEL

The statistical model defines the linkages and assemblages of relations between the components that carry out the roles and behaviors. These structures are responsible for carrying out functions and behaviors. In most cases, the statistical model will consist of defining the topology, planning the layout, and designing the buffers [33]. Cracking, corrosion, and deformation are some of the phenomena that can be studied using a physical model. The kinetics, reasoning, and connections of the actual system are all replicated in a geometric model. The curves and surfaces of the 3D models can be generated in one of two ways: either by performing a 3D scan of the object or by employing equations within specialized software [35].

Image-based methods can serve as a suitable alternative to scanning technologies in cases where geometry measurements are required. Additionally, they can be supplemented by information about the object's internal structure, which can be acquired using traditional inspection techniques, thermography techniques, or radar methods, all of which enable a more in-depth examination of a physical structure. This information can be gleaned by using the aforementioned techniques. Anbalagan et al. [36] have described the process of developing digital geometry models through the use of computer-aided design.
