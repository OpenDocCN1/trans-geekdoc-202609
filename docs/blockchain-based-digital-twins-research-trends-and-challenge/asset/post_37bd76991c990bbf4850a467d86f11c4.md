## 4.2.3 APPLICATIONS OF DIGITAL TWIN

The below are examples of how digital twin applications have made an impact across many industries [5]:

- 1. Manufacturing: Digital twins are applied in the manufacturing industry to speed up product development, design customization, shop floor performance enhancement, and preventive maintenance [6].

- 2. Retail: At well-known shopping malls and individual stores, digital twins are utilized to mimic and improve the consumer experience in the retail sector.

- 3. Automotive: Virtual representations of connected automobiles are frequently created using digital twins. To find potential issues before production or when the car is put on the road, automotive companies simulate and analyze the production phase.

- 4. Healthcare: By virtualizing healthcare services, digital twins assist healthcare providers in enhancing patient care, efficiency, and effectiveness. It attempts to increase individualized care and operational efficiency of healthcare processes [7].

- 5. Smart Cities: Digital twins can assist smart cities in achieving more social, economic, and environmental sustainability.

By having access to this data, engineers can maximize productivity while preventing unplanned downtime and reducing operating costs—all with IoT systems that stand on their connectivity.

2. Ditto: Eclipse Ditto's overarching objective is to create a foundation for digital twins. The coordination between physical equipment and their virtual counterparts is the main focus. For instance, you may use weather information to improve a twin that knows its position. Consider an automobile as an example: it sends Ditto location data such as longitude and latitude every five minutes and travels from one spot to another. The weather information can be requested for the area where the automobile is now parked from a weather service in the interim. Ditto receives this data as feedback. As a result, the twin is always informed about the weather in the area where the automobile is currently parked.

- Kafka: It is commonly used as a major event streaming platform to build scalable and dependable digital twins and digital threads for real-time streaming sensors. It provides an important event streaming platform for real-time communication, processing, and integration.

- Connectivity: Real-time scalability is provided by Kafka Connect for connectivity to IoT interfaces, cloud services, and big data. Other middleware and IoT systems are not in competition with the Kafka ecosystem; rather, they are complementary.

- Digital Traces: These are used by the distributed commit log known as Kafka. Events are immutable, added, and preserved for as long as desired (possibly indefinitely with retention time = -1).

It includes client programs written in various languages such as Python, Scala, Go, Java.NET, C++, and others, as well as components like Kafka brokers, Schema Registry, Connect, REST Proxy, and others. With this level of adaptability, you can mix Kafka components with any other IoT solution to create the right Digital Twin architecture for your edge, hybrid, or global scenarios.

## 4.3 INTRODUCTION TO BLOCKCHAIN

Blockchain technology is a distributed, immutable database that monitors asset and transaction processes. A material asset, such as a home, money, vehicle, or plot, or an intangible asset, such as a patent, intellectual property, copyrights, and brand awareness, can be monitored by a blockchain network. This network can record and trade anything of value, reducing risk and lowering costs for parties during transactions.

In other words, Blockchain preserves data and information in a digital format without the need for a central authority. Data is gathered from numerous sources and organized into blocks on a Blockchain. Additionally, after they are full, these blocks link with subsequent blocks. Thus, this network of data links and chains is referred to as a blockchain. Additionally, it stores the data and uses cryptography to link the blocks together.

### 4.3.1 FEATURES OF BLOCKCHAIN

Blockchain consists of the following features [9]:

- 1. **Decentralization**: The term “decentralization” describes a blockchain network that is not controlled by a central authority or intermediary. A blockchain, on the other hand, runs on a globally dispersed network of nodes (computers) that each keeps a copy of the complete blockchain.

- 2. **Immutability**: This is a blockchain that refers to the characteristic of data being permanent and impervious to tampering once it is added. Data that has been stored in a block and uploaded to the blockchain can no longer be changed or removed. Consensus techniques and cryptographic hashing are used to achieve this.

- 3. **Consensus**: The network’s users must reach a consensus on its validity using a consensus mechanism. Only authentic and valid transactions are added to the blockchain as a result of this agreement.
