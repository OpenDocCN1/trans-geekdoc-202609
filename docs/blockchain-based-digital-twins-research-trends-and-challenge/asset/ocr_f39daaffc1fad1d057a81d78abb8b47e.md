However, the origin of a transaction may be tracked by learning this set. Several different approaches have been suggested to increase the privacy of blockchain, broadly falling into two categories:

Blending. Users' addresses on a blockchain are anonymous. However, many users use the same address repeatedly, it is still possible to connect addresses to actual people. A mixing service is a service that allows users to remain anonymous by sending money to several recipients. Alice, who has address A, and lives at B, wants to send some cash to Bob. Alice and Bob's connection may be exposed if she conducts a direct transaction with input address and output address B.

For this reason, Alice could give Carol a monetary gift who worked as a gobetween. Carol's transfer to Bob has several inputs and many outputs. Bob's home address is one of those included in the output. It makes it more difficult to reveal Alice and Bob's relationship. However, a dishonest third party may purposefully reveal private information about Alice and Bob. Carol may not send Bob's money but instead to her own house. When using Mix currency, this is a simple method to avoid unethical behavior.

The user's requirements, such as the amount of money and the date of the transfer, are encrypted using the intermediary's private key. Moreover, if the go-between did not have the promised cash, anybody could easily confirm that the middleman had been deceived. Despite this, theft still needs to be stopped once it is discovered. The CoinJoin protocol uses a mixing server to randomly reorder the output addresses to avoid theft. Coin Shuffle is an address shuffling algorithm that uses decryption mix nets inspired by CoinJoin.
