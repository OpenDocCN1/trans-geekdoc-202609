
Three important characteristics of the technology are mentioned in these qualifications:

- The goal of synchronization is to ensure that the represented thing and its digital duplicate are as similar as feasible.
- Depending on the goal, a digital twin’s update frequency or pace can be quite variable, ranging from seconds to weeks to on-demand.
- Fidelity is the level of accuracy and precision of the synchronization mechanism and the virtual representation.

Designers and engineers utilize the same modeling and CAD tools to create digital twins throughout the initial product development process. The distinction between the digital twin and the traditional twin is that the digital twin is handled via Product Lifecycle Management software and retains and updates the model for use in subsequent phases of the product’s lifespan such as inspection and maintenance.

## 9.2.2 SIMULATION VERSUS DIGITAL TWIN

Digital twins and simulations utilize the virtual model to replicate operations of any system. The digital twin is more helpful because it is essentially a virtual scenario. A simulation and a digital twin differ in scale. A digital twin may contain many simulations as necessary to investigate several processes, whereas simulations normally analyze one procedure at a time. There are likely differences. For example, real-time data is frequently advantageous for simulations.

Digital twins, on the other hand, are created to work with multiple information flows that start whenever the system receives crucial data from sensors [15]. By providing more accurate and up-to-date data from various disciplines, digital twins enhance the computational capability offered by the virtual environment. Since digital twins can examine more issues from a wider variety of angles than simulations can, they have higher potential to ultimately improve products and processes [17].

## 9.4 TYPES OF DIGITAL TWINS

Digital twins may be many different things, based on how much the technology has improved. Primary areas of operation where these twins diverge have emerged. It is typical for many kinds of digital twins to work together in a system or process:

- 1. Component Twins/Parts Twins: A component twin, which is the most compact representation of an operational component, is the essential building block of a digital twin. Twin components are almost identical yet relate to less important elements.
- 2. Process Twins: The macro-level magnification used by the process twins shows several systems working together to build a full manufacturing complex. Using process twins, it may be possible to identify the precise temporal patterns that eventually affect overall efficacy.
- 3. Asset Twins: Any time two or more things function successfully, an asset is produced. Asset twins allow researchers to study the interactions between these factors. It produces a large amount of operational data that may be analyzed to yield valuable insights.
- 4. System or Unit Twins: The next phase in the magnification process is to construct system or unit twins. It helps consumers understand how various parts interact to form an entire working system. System twins reveal how assets interact and may point to performance enhancements.

Three major categories of digital twins can be distinguished, each of which illustrates a different use for the technique:

- i. Digital Twin Prototype (DTP): Before a tangible item is created, something is done;
- ii. Digital Twin Instance (DTI): This is done following the creation of a product to test it in various scenarios.
- iii. Digital Twin Aggregate (DTA): This program compiles DTI data to assess a product’s capabilities, perform diagnostics, and evaluate operational conditions.

The broad categories may be used to accomplish a variety of activities, including system planning, product creation and redesign, logistical planning, and quality control and management. A digital twin can be used to reduce time and costs whenever testing is necessary for a product or process, whether for design, deployment, monitoring, or improvement.
