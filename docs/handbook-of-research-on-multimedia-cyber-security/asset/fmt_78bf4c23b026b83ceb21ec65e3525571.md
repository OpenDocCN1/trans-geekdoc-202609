## ABSTRACT

The data transmission on network channels has increased tremendously in the past few decades. Watermarking is a process to add a watermark as a digital signal, label, or tag into a cover media. The primary requirements of multimedia watermarking are robustness and embedding capability. Robustness is defined as the strength of an algorithm to repel noise. However, it is challenging to achieve both at the same time. The numerous characteristics of watermarking are very imperative in the multimedia watermarking system. Researchers are using watermarking schemes in various applications such as military, digital forensics, medical, and so on. Attacks on these watermarking schemes harm or uncover the secret information carried in the watermark. Potential researchers have presented various techniques for balancing or improving these concerns. This chapter reviews recent multimedia watermarking techniques on the basis of robustness and embedding capability. The characteristics, applications, and attacks on multimedia watermarking techniques are introduced.

## INTRODUCTION

During the past, data transmission has increased among various channels and networks. As with developing technologies, there is an increase in the growth and usage of transmission media. To avoid misuse and establish authenticity, watermarking techniques should be used to secure multimedia data. The malevolent copying and illegal dissemination of digital images is averted by digital watermarking by hiding the data ownership in the host image (Ganic & Eskicioglu, 2004; Patel, Mehta, & Pradhan, 2011). Watermarking is designed as a process of embedding a dual/single watermark in terms of a label, tag, or digital signal. The process of watermarking is defined on the basis of different groups and domains (Aslantas, 2008). The methods of watermarking are categorized into transform and spatial domains (Loukhaoukha, Nabti, & Zebbiche, 2014). The initially used methods are the spatial domain, a technique in which watermark embedding is performed by directly making a change in pixels. It exhibits the feature of accessibility for implementation and low computational cost. The important techniques in this area are correlation-based, spread spectrum, and Least Significant Bit (`LSB`). The potential examples of the transform domain are Discrete Fourier Transform (`DFT`), Discrete Wavelet Transforms (`DWT`), Singular Value Decomposition (`SVD`), and Discrete Cosine Transforms (`DCT`). The two different categories of digital watermark in the context of visibility are invisible and visible. The classes of invisible watermarks are fragile and robust (Aslantas, 2008). The detailed watermark classification is presented further in (Aslantas, 2008; Singh, 2017; Tsai, Huang, Kuo, Horng, 2012).

### Classification of Watermark Systems

Watermark systems are classified into three different categories (Mathon, Cayre, Bas, & Mac, 2014; Singh, Dave, & Mohan, 2014; Singh, Kumar, Singh, & Mohan, 2017; Tsai, Jhuang, & Lai, 2012) as described below in Figure 3.

*   **Blind Watermarking**: In this technique, the watermark is extracted by using a watermarked image. The applications are electronic voting systems, copyright protection, health-care, etc.
*   **Non-Blind Watermarking**: In this technique, the original image and embedded watermark are copied during the extraction process. The applications are copyright protection and covert communication.
*   **Semi-Blind Watermarking**: This technique works on a non-blind watermarking system and is deprived of the original data requirement for detection. Applications in this technique are CAD models, authentication, etc.
