*Crossover*. Crossover is performed on two parents to form two new offspring. The GA compares a randomly selected value against a crossover probability that is a property of the GA. If the random number is less than the crossover probability, crossover is performed; otherwise, the offspring are identical to the parents. If crossover occurs, one or more crossover points are generated, which determines the position in the chromosomes where parents exchange genes. Typically, crossover probability is around 0.90 to 0.95, making for high probability of performing crossover. We normally like having a higher crossover probability to keep genes mixing through the population. As we discuss later, we can always choose not to replace all parents with children, which is equivalent to children being exact duplicates of parents.
