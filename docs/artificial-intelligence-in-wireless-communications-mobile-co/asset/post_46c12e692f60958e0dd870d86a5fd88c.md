## Genetic Algorithms for Optimization

In this chapter, we discuss how genetic algorithms (GA) are used for optimization. Genetic algorithms are a class of evolutionary algorithms inspired by the theory of natural selection. They work on a population of candidate solutions, evolving them over generations. The process involves selecting the best individuals to produce the next generation based on their fitness values.

### 5.1 A Brief Review

In their simplest form, genetic algorithms work on a population of candidate solutions, each representing a potential solution to the problem. The population evolves through a series of operations: selection, crossover, and mutation. Selection chooses survival of the fittest individuals for the next generation. Crossover combines parts of two parent solutions to create offspring, while mutation introduces random changes to maintain genetic diversity. The algorithm continues until a satisfactory solution is found or a maximum number of generations is reached.

### 5.2 Simple Example: The Knapsack Problem

To explain the operation of a GA, we examine the knapsack problem. This problem takes a set of items, each with a weight and a profit. The goal is to select a subset of items that fits into a knapsack of maximum capacity without exceeding its total weight capacity. Mathematically, the knapsack problem is represented as:

$P = \max_{i=1}^{N} x_i p_i \quad (5.1)$

subject to: $\sum_{i=1}^{N} x_i w_i \leq K$

where $x_i$ is a binary variable ($x_i = 1$ if item $i$ is included, and $0$ otherwise), $p_i$ is the profit of item $i$, $w_i$ is its weight, and $K$ is the maximum capacity.

Chromosome: The problem consists of choosing the right set of items to place in the knapsack, so the chromosome represents the vector $x$.
