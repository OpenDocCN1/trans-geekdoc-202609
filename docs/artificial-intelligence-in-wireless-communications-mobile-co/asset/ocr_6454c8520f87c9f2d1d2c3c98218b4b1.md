The known access code sequence can then be subtracted from the received signal to leave any interference signals and the AWGN noise. Before subtracting the known access code, an autocorrelation is performed on it to determine its maximum correlation value. The ratio of the autocorrelation value to the peak of the cross-correlation gives the pathloss as the constant during the transmission of the access code. This ratio is used to adjust the amplitude of the known sequence to properly remove it from the received signal. Taking the average magnitude squared of this signal yields the interference pulse noise power. The remaining signal after subtraction can then be subtracted from the original received signal to leave only the transmitted signal. The average magnitude squared of this value is the received signal strength. The ratio of these two values is the SINR.

Appendix F provides more detail and the mathematical explanation of this meter probe as well simulated results that show the sensor's proper behavior. This sensor can then be used as a means of calculating the received signal power, the interference plus noise power (or just noise power if no interference is present), and the pathloss, thereby replacing the signal power and noise power probes used previously in this work.
