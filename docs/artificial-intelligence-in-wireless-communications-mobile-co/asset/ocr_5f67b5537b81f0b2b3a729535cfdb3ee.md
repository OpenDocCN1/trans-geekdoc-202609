*interp.* After filtering and resampling, the resulting signal is the transmitted signal plus noise any interference that exists within the signal bandwidth. The received signal then goes into *rxpath* where it is demodated. Out of the channel filter, *noise_pwr* calculates the noise power of the channel. The block *rss* calculates the received signal strength. Both of these calculations are described below, and together, they are used to calculate the SNR. Finally the system calculates the BER by taking the transmitted bits and compares them to the bits received after passing through the channel and demodulator. The first thousand bits of the input are dropped to ignore any transients in the system.

The channel model of Figure 3.6 adds Gaussian noise at a given noise voltage calculated from the system's noise floor, and a frequency offset to represent frequency differences between the transmitter and receiver. The path loss is simply modeled as a multiplier with a constant value calculated externally that represents the distance, a particular path loss model. The channel model is easily extended to include multipath by using a FIR filter with a given set of taps. All of the values that the channel model uses are passed externally to allow representation of different mathematical models.
