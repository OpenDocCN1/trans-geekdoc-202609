# 8.3.2 Interference (2): Sensor Problems

In the next set of experiments, we use a more interesting set of objectives from Table 8.13 to balance the wavelet properties and create waveforms that are bandwidth efficient but have high throughput, low BER, and avoid the interferers. In this first test, the cognitive engine used the information retrieved from the PSD sensor to know to avoid the interference. Table 8.14 and Figure 8.9 show, the waveform designed by the cognitive engine has 0 BER and the minimum interference power possible, where $1 	imes 10^{-12}$ is used as the minimum power to avoid errors when converting to dBm. However, the observed BER was much higher, and Figure 8.10 shows that the waveform was placed directly on top of one of the interfering signals.

A look at the results returned by the PSD sensor shows why the cognitive engine selected an improper frequency. According to the PSD sensor as seen in Table 8.15, there are only four bands to both start and end where the previous signal ended. In this representation, the interference signal from about 0.5 to 1.0 Hz (normalized frequency) cannot be detected. The cognitive engine, working with this data, did not understand the presence of the signal and therefore could not avoid it. It turns out that the PSD sensor had a small logic error that caused this representation problem, and it was easily corrected. We show this because we want to illustrate this problem to indicate the impact of incorrect information on the cognitive engine; otherwise, under the conditions presented, the waveform optimization looks correct.
