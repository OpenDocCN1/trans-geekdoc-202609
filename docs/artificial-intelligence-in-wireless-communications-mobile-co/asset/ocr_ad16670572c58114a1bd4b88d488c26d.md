That is, if any of the $n$ objectives of $v$ is less than any objective of $u$, then $u$ is inferior to $v$.

**Superiority:** $ar{u}$ is superior to $ar{v}$ iff $ar{v}$ is inferior to $ar{u}$.

**Noninferiority:** $ar{u}$ and $ar{v}$ are noninferior to one another if $ar{v}$ is neither inferior nor superior to $ar{u}$.

Pareto ranking ranks each member of the population by the number of individuals to which the given member is superior. After some number of generations, the algorithm must return a specific solution, but the population is ranked in terms of Pareto dominance where, ideally, each population is Pareto optimal, each member will be superior to none, and therefore each population will have a ranking of 0. In the end, the algorithm needs some method of selecting the member from the final population. We discuss this as we build up the particular implementation of the MOGA for Waveform optimization in the next section.

# # 5.4 Wireless System Genetic Algorithm

The wireless system genetic algorithm (WSGA) is a MOGA designed to optimize a waveform by modeling the physical radio system as a biological organism and optimizing its performance through genetic and evolutionary processes. In the WSGA, radio behavior is interpreted as a set of PHY layers represented as traits or genes of a chromosome. Other general radio functional parameters (such as antenna configuration, voice coding, equalization, retransmission requests, and spreading technique/code) are also identified as possible genes for future growth as SDR platforms develop to support each of these traits. Expansion of PHY layers is a horizontal growth while the MOGA can also extend to higher layers of the protocol stack such the MAC or network. Extension to the higher layers will require proper understanding of the objective function analysis of these layers, the genetic representation of the adjustable parameters, and the available communications platform capable of reconfiguration in the layers.
