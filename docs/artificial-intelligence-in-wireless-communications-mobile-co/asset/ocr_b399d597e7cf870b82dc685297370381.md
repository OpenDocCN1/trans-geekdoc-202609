The USRP provides analog to digital sampling with a 12-bit, 64 Msps ADC and digital to analog with 14-bit, 128 Msps DAC. It can hold two transmitter and two receiver daughterboards at a time. It is controlled and data is transmitted and received over a USB 2.0 interface.

While the USRP and GNU Radio are parallel development projects, they do not necessarily depend on one another as other SDR platforms use the USRP (e.g., [18, 19]) and other RF front ends can use GNU Radio as the signal processing system.

# 3.4.2 The USRP Version 2

An updated USRP was recently released, the USRPv2. This design offers a number of improvements to the first generation USRP. It uses higher-resolution and faster ADCs and DACs at 14-bits, 100 Msps and 16-bits, 400 Msps, respectively. It handles only one transmitter and one receiver at a time, unlike the USRPv1, but the USRPv2 is equipped with a high-speed connector to tie two USRPv2s together for multiprocessing. The USRPv2 includes a more capable FPGA, the Xilinx Spartan 3, than the USRPv1, which uses an Altera Cyclone. The USRPv2 also stores the FPGA image on an SD card instead of writing it from the host computer when first run as the USRPv1. With the SD Card and MIPs processor image programmed on the Vertex IV, the USRPv2 is capable of complete standalone operation. One of the most significant changes between USRPv1 and USRPv2 is the use of a Gigabit Ethernet (GigE) link instead of USB for transfer data. With full-duplex GigE, the USRPv2 can simultaneously transmit and receive 25 MHz bandwidth selects at complex baseband.

Throughout this book, the real differences between the USRPv1 and USRPv2 are unimportant. In the cognitive radio concept, these systems play the same role. We will generically refer to these two systems as the USRP since we will not need to further differentiate their capabilities.
