#  3. Results and Discussion

In this section, the results of the evaluation of the test data for the trained U-Net for a period of 5 years are presented and discussed. Figure 1 gives an example of input data for the trained U-Net and the ground truth output at 00:00 UTC on 22 November 2021. Estimation results (i.e., outputs) from the U-Net with the ground truth data are shown in Figure 2.

Maps of the momentum flux $ho u'v'$ are shown in Figures 3a–3c. The observed structures of the $u'$, $v'$, and $w'$ are consistent with those of the ground truth on land and south coast. Particularly, the negative and positive stripes from the Hidaka Mountains in central Hokkaido to the southeast were, with similar structures to those of the ground truth. The larger amplitudes of $u' $ and $w'$ are observed on the sea, which were not well represented.

Figure 3g show line plots of $u'$, $v'$, and $w'$ along $A'$–$B'$. The wave vector direction of gravity waves on the south coast of the analysis region. Its structures observed of the $u'$, $v'$, and $w'$ values are similar to that of the ground truth. The maximum amplitudes of $u'$, $v'$, and $w'$ along the U-Net were -4.58, -4.2, and 1.17 m, respectively. The values of $w'$ are consistent with the ground truth although the amplitudes are larger, except for the slight difference in $v'$.

The fluctuations and magnitude of $ho u'v'$ and $ho v'w'$ are shown along $C'$–$D'$ in Figures 3p–3r. The negative values of $ho u'v'$ and $ho v'w'$ were -0.83 and -0.49 m/s², respectively. The maximum of the momentum magnitude $|ho u'v'|$, $|ho v'w'|$ was 0.87 mPa for the estimation result and 0.93 mPa for the ground truth. The slight difference of the maximum $|ho u'v'|$, $|ho v'w'|$ is attributable to a phase shift in $v'$ between the estimate and the ground truth (Figure 3h). These consistent with ground truth indicate that the estimation of both the momentum flux and the wave parameters is useful using the U-Net. In other words, the downscaling from 60-km mesh data to the 5-km data can be appropriately performed using the U-Net.
