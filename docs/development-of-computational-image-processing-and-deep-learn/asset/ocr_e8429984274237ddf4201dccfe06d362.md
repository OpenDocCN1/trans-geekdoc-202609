This parametrization of orientation tensors allows the depiction of the variety of FOT via pairs of \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) (alongside a mapping \(\boldsymbol{R}\) defining the orientation coordinate system) in a two-dimensional space [85]. This representation is also known as orientation triangle, which can be seen in Figure 2.8 and also, e.g., in the works of Bauer and Böhlke [85], Gajek et al. [91], Goldberg et al. [109], Cintra et al. [101], Chung et al. [103] or Köbler et al. [110].

It simplifies the assignment of different material symmetries to the specific FOT. The weakest material symmetry of orthotropy as the gray area in Figure 2.8 is thereby defined by three points of extremal orientation states of isotropy (A), unidirectionality (B) and planar isotropy (C).
