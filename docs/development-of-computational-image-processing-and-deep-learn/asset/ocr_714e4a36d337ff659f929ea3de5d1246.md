# 2.3.2.3 Blur and noise

As Sprawls describes in [159], the main characteristics of CT imaging exacerbating the detection of structures are blur and noise. The former reduces the visibility of small objects and hence decreases image detail. The latter reduces the visibility of low-contrast objects. Fibers fit both of these descriptions. While small voxels and edge-enhancing filters can reduced blurring, small voxels absorb fewer photons and therefore increase noise, which is caused by the variation in attenuation coefficients between voxels. Noisy images can be improved by large voxels, increasing the radiation dose or using smoothing filters. The latter, however, increases blurring again. [159] Since the measures for reduced blur and noise are partially mutually exclusive, the focus of the work was on the reduction of noise, since single-fiber detection was excluded anyway. Hence, larger voxels, increased radiation dose and smoothing filters were used for image improvement.
