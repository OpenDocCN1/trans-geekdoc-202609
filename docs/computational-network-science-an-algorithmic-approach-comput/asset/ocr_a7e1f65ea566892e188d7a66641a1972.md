# CHAPTER 12
Emerging Trends

Network science discipline crosses several boundaries. We have provided a snapshot of the state of the art in network science. However, the future beckons for extensive, active research as expected. In the remainder of this chapter, I highlight the research focuses in leading institutions.

Mining social media is a burgeoning trend (Bhat and Abulaisha, 2013; Tu et al., 2013). Online social networking has irrevocably changed human social life. At an extreme, friends and family members sitting in the same room prefer to tweet or post instead of speak. Prevalence of social media creates living, digital diaries that are semipublic. Idententially, sharing of social data produces opportunities for ultrarapid reflection and analysis. An evidence for this is that politics have changed with instantaneous trend analysis. Arguing for the downside of social media proliferation, it might be argued that people are retreating digital walls for isolated lives as Putnam put it (Putnam, 2001).

On the positive side of this transformation, analyses in our age of big data (Mayer-Schönberger and Cukier, 2013) empower us to understand global shifts beyond nuance and biases at the individual level. “big” data analyses enable discoveries and deployment of solutions for social as well as medical maladies.

Advances in communication networks are herding 5G wireless cellular networks. An objective has to create globally resource-optimized and energy-efficient networks. There are well-known trade-offs between energy consumption and service delay. State-of-the-approach is to enable communication networks that are ever more adaptive to traffic variations. Game theory has continued to play a role in this development (Menache and Ozdaglar, 2011; Antoniou and Pitsllides, 2013).
