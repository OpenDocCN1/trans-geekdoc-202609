# 5.2 TIME GRAPHS

Time graphs extend the traditional notion of an evolving directed graph that captures link creation as a phenomenon in time. We address this question through creation and analysis of a series of snapshots of the data. The development of tools and methods to analyze these snapshots is therefore a timely endeavor. Blogspace offers an additional technical advantage over such approaches—if data is craw with a certain frequency, there is no notion of the precise point in time a page was created and updated. In contrast, blogspace offers a ready-made view of evolution in continuous time. As each blog adds an entry with links, there is a time stamp associated with that event. By automatically extracting these time stamps, we can pierce together a view of blogspace evolving continuously from the beginning of blog archiving to the present. We should stress that time is absolute and not merely relative in a sequence of crawls. We focus on connectivity evolution and on temporally concentrated bursts (i.e., the Kleinberg’s conception) in the evolution of blogspace (Kleinberg, 2002).

Within a community of interacting bloggers, a given topic may be the subject of intense debate for a period of time and then fade away. These bursts of activity are typed by heightened hyperlinking among the blogs involved—within a time interval. Note that a subgraph indicates a community of interest in the traditional sense that may exist among a set of blogs without ever achieving this temporal focus. Conversely, heavy linkage within a short period may appear less significant when viewed over a long time span—suggesting that the criterion for inferring pattern of links is a community is less stringent than for a static graph.
