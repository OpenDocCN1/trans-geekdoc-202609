We begin a set of global strategies and then turn to local commun-ties. We introduce graph partitioning where we divide the graphs into parts with minimal number of links between them. The number of links between two clusters is called cut size. If we divide the network in one group, that is, the undivided cut is 0. In partitioning, it might be desirable to specify the least number of groups and the target group size, even though communities cannot be divided in an optimal way. For example, consider a sports club with 12 players. Six of them play basketball and six of them play football. We would like to divide the entire sports network into communities (i.e., clusters) based on how closely players interact with one another. All who play similar sports interact with one another closely and they are friends. There are also a few footballers who interact with basketballers.
