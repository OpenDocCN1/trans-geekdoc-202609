# CHAPTER 10
Network Capital

Networks are storehouses for a variety of capital that have value and worth. In economic terms, capital is money (Marx, 1967). Physical capitals are tools and resources (Economy and Levi, 2014). These might be common tools or raw materials such as oil and gas, which are commonly accessible to a group. Cultural capital consists of language, customs, and lifestyle choices (Beemyn, 2014). Whereas diversity promotes minority, social conventions promote conformity. Political capital is largely a measure of power to persuade others (Burgmann, 2014). Democracy and human rights serve as example areas, where social forces promote them from the West to the rest of the world. Social capital (SC) is an intangible capital to manage and change relationships such as interpersonal trust made famous by the works of Pierre Bourdieu, James Coleman, and Robert Putnam (Field, 2014). There are two major perspectives on SC in networks. In the macroscopic perspective, SC for the entire network is considered. In this view, individuals do not incrementally add to the system or withdraw units of SC. Instead, the areas of interest are the system principles such as norms and conventions that provide resources for the overall social welfare. In contrast, the microscopic perspective adopted here explores how individuals gain access to resources by their positions and connections in the network.

Physical goods and services are often scattered unevenly in realworld networks. Neighborhoods, communities, and high-status nodes are similarly loosely distributed in networks. Nodes of a network often desire capital that may not be locally available to the node; thereby, they may take action to do one of the following: gain access to capital (e.g., gasoline), exert influence over nodes possessing capital (e.g., conserve energy usage), have power over capital (e.g., limit tuna fishing), and have control over flow of capital (e.g., nonproliferation of nuclear materials).\Figure 10.1 summarizes categories of capital and possible types of actions over them.
