**Definition:** SC for a node (i.e., egocentric for an individual) is the sum of SC derived from its links with direct neighbors plus the sum of SC of all its neighbors.

This egocentric definition of SC deliberately mirrors the Bonachic power index (Bonachich, 1987). This coincidence helps us exploit the to-logical position of nodes. A node that is well positioned by having a high power index (i.e., high Bonachic centrality value) similarly possesses high SC.

**Observation 3:** Nodes in a network with higher power possess SC.

**Observation 4:** Nodes in a network with bridge roles possess SC.

We consider SC to be a scalar value that can be either accumulated and consumed verbatim or used as credit. In a network, SC might be used to trade for or exchanges with others in the form of delegation of tasks. Bartering with SC can be limited to a pair of nodes through an immediate link between them. Alternatively, a node might barter anonymously with another node with which there may not be a direct link.

If the network is very large and resources are scattered widely, it may be ineffective to engage in direct interactions with resource owners due to large distances to resource owner nodes. It would be beneficial to find nodes that interface on their behalf. On the other hand, some resource owners may wish to make their resources available far beyond their immediate surroundings. It would be useful if the resource owner could have an interface agent in order to expose availability of the resource to remote parts of the NO. This is discussed in the following section.
