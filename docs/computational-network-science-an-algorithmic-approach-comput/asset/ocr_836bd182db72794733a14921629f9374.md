However, an individual who becomes a member of a high bonding network will gain access to assistance from friends, as a form of capital, from whom it has friendship relationships. Identification of capital is crucial for legitimacy of SC. It is also crucial to note that SC is meaningful only for agents whose reality result in capital benefits. Our present model assumes a finite number of single-unit capitals (i.e., resources) placed at the subset of nodes within a network.

Gaining and controlling access to resources is a predominant form of reasoning about resources. Manipulating marginal access to resources (i.e., externalities) is another common form of reasoning about resources. SC in the network is the degree to which an agent is able to handle capital in the network by social means. For our use, handling resources is considered in either one of two forms of access and activation. Access to resources is the measure of potential to reach and use resources, and that is the primary focus here. Activation is the choice of resources and types of efforts in approaching them (Hurlbert et al., 2000). The term bonding SC is meaningful only in the context of an individual, who may exploit his her social membership with a bonding measure in the network for gaining access to a form of capital available. Bridging is another common property of a network, and an individual who is exposed to a bridging feature gains access to capital that might be in the remote parts of the network and be said to be bridging SC. To say “SC exists”, we must identify the capital and beneficiaries. In fact, we consider a generalization of FSC, where F is a network feature. Examples of network features are agent clustering coefficient, bonding, bridging, and leadership. Online organizations provide a network in which agents experience bonding and bridging SC (Ellison et al., 2007). On the one hand, SC may reflect merits of a node’s current position in the network with respect to relative locations of capital that the node values in the network. If a node A’s good neighbor R has a valuable resource, A will possess a simple form of location SC and will be motivated to maintain strong ties with R in order to have access to R’s resource The farther away A is from R, A experiences diminished location SC.
