# **Definition (resource owner sharing policy with a remote node):** R’s willingness to share its resource with a remote node depends on the following four attributes:

+   (a) Influence along all shortest paths between i and R must be larger than a predefined threshold.
(b) Distance between R and the nearest alternate resource holder is small.
(c) There is a prior balance of resource sharing between i and R.
(d) R has nontrivial expected reciprocation of resources from other resource owners.

The opposite of sharing is denying resources (i.e., hoarding), which we present as a complementary concept to sharing. Next, few policies provide a few common resource denial policies.

# **Definition (hoarding policy with a neighbor):** R’s degree of hoarding is the complement of R’s willingness to share its resource.

# **Definition (prohibition hoarding policy with a non-neighbor):** R refuses to share its resource with a remote node i if there exists a weak influence link between i and R.

# **Definition (exclusivity hoarding policy):** R refuses to share its resource with any node i if R knows that i has access to a peer resource owner.

# **Definition (competitive hoarding policy):** R refuses to share its resource with a peer resource owner.

In order for a node to access a resource, it must interact with resource owners, captured in the next definition.

# **Definition (resource access):** Resource access for node i with respect to resource R from resource holder R is the willingness of R to share the resource with i.
