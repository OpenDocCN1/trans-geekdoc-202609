# Computational Network Science: An Algorithmic Approach

Family members A and D first bought an iPhone 5S at time t, so they are active and contagious. Node A attempts to influence C to buy the phone with the probability 0.6. Node C is also influenced by the other active node D with the weight 0.4. Node C can be influenced arbitrarily by either family members A or D, and this depends on the sequence of the attempts made on node C at that time. Node C is influenced by node A at time t+1, and the probability of A's success influencing C is denoted by $P_c(A, S)$, where $S$ is the set of other active family members who influence C, so $S = \{D\}$. Nodes A and D are active and noncontagious as they attempt to influence their neighbors once. These steps are repeated and influence cascades from family member $C \rightarrow F$ and $F \rightarrow I$. Eventually, the family members A, D, C, and F have new iPhone 5S and form a community, leaving others inactive as shown in Figure 6.18.

The community of family members is detected based on the independent contagion model. Initially the family members A and D have phones; they are able to diffuse information and influence other family members C, F, and I to buy the phone. This leaves family members B, F, G, H, and J not part of the diffusion and cascading process, which helps us detect the community of iPhone 5S holders in the family shown in Figure 6.19.
