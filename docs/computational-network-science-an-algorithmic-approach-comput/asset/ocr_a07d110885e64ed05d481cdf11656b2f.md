Overall, the highest SC who proposes earliest is granted access to the resource, and the corresponding SC is collected for later use. Representative brokers collect proposals from their clients and seek to deal resource coordinators who may accept bids for resource access. If a match is found, SC is exchanged and resource is granted through corresponding brokers. Coordinator agents also collect proposals from representative agents and others to offer access to the resource under their care. We assume broker agents collect a negligibly small portion of SC as for their services. For brevity, the fee is ignored in our basic protocol in order to focus on interactions for the resource.

For the resource owner willing to share its resource, it considers its neighbors collecting proposals from those who have adequate SC. The neighbor with the highest SC is granted the resource. If neighbors are not qualified, the coordinator is consulted. If the coordinator has a qualified resource-seeking client, the resource is granted in exchange for the SC. # 10.2 CONCLUSION

We outlined a variety of capital available on a network. Nodes may desire to access, influence, exert power, and control capital. This is a largely unexplored topic and our exposition is a call to arms for building useful models. We sketched a model of SC to facilitate access to physical resources for nodes that lack them. Repeated social interaction among agents leads to development of SC, which is used as a measure of access to resources. We discussed how locations of agents relative to loci of agents give them different levels of SC and resource access. We then outlined a number of policies that can be used to regulate resource access. A resource owner may relinquish access to its resource to be controlled by an intermediary, coordinator agent.
