# 3.4. Implementation

Our implementation for ImageNet follows the practice in [21, 40]. The image is resized with its shorter side randomly sampled in [256, 480] for scale augmentation [40]. A 224x224 image is randomly sampled from any of these with a horizontal flip, with the pixel-mean subtracted [21]. We adopt batch normalization (BN) [16] after every convolution before activation. We initialize the weights as in [12] and train all plain/residual nets from scratch. We use SGD with a mini-batch size of 256. The learning rate starts from 0.1 and is divided by 10 every ten epochs, and the models are trained for 60×10⁶ iterations. We use a weight decay of 0.0001 and a momentum of 0.9. We do not use dropout [13], following the policy in [7].

In testing, we use the standard 10-count testing [21]. For best results, we evaluate the fully-convolutional format as in [14, 12], and report scores at multiple scales (images are resized so that the shorter side is in (224, 256, 381, 480, 640)).
