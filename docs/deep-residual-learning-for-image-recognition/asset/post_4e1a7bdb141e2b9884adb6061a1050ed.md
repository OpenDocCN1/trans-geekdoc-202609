## Analysis of Layer Responses

Figure 7 shows the standard deviations (std) of each 3x3 layer. The responses are the outputs of 3x3 layer, after BN and before nonlinearity (ReLU/addition). For ResNets, this analysis reveals the strength of the residual functions.

We show that ResNets have smaller residual functions than their counterparts. Section 3.1 shows that the residual functions might be generally closer to zero than the non-residual units. We also notice that deeper ResNets have smaller magnitudes of responses, as seen by the comparisons among ResNet-20, 56, and 110 in Fig.7. When there are more layers, an individual layers of ResNets tend to make the signal.

## Exploring Over 1000 layers

We explore an aggressively deep model of over 1000 layers. We set $n=200$ that leads to a 1202-layer network, which is trained as residual. Our method shows no optimization difficulty, and this $10^3$-layer network is able to achieve training error <0.1% (Fig. 6, right). Its test error is still fairly good (7.93%), Table 6.

But there are still problems on such aggressively deep models. The testing result of this 1202-layer network is worse than that of our 110-layer network, although both have similar error. This is because of overfitting. The over-1202 layer network may be necessarily inaccurate (19.4M) on that data. Strong regularization as max [9] or dropout [13] is applied to obtain the best. In this paper, we use no max/dropout and just simple regularization to deepen the architectures by tracking from the difficulties of optimization. By combining with stronger regularization may improve our results, which we will show in the future.

## 4.3. Object Detection on Pascal and MS COCO

Our method has good generalization performance on other recognition tasks. Table 6 and 8 show the detection baseline results on Pascal VOC 2007 and 2012 [5] and COCO [26]. We adopt Faster R-CNN [32] as the detection method. Here we are implemented in the implementation of VGG-16 with ResNet-101. The use of both models is the same, so the gains can only be attributed to better networks. Mostly respectively, on the challenging COCO dataset we observed 0.9% increase in COCO's standard mAP[5, 95], which is a 28% relative improvement. This gain is solely due to the learned representations.
