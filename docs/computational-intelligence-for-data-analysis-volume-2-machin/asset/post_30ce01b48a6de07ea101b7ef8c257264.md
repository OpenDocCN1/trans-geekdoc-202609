### At Home

People can transfer medical data from their homes to other locales, similar to their primary care provider or a hospital, using in-home IoMT. Remote patient monitoring (RPM), for illustration, is the use of a medical device to measure parameters like blood pressure and oxygen saturation from recently discharged patients to their hospital for assessment by their doctors. This can help to prevent readmissions to the medical centre by detecting problems before they become serious. Telehealth, or the use of communication technologies for remote healthcare, adds to the flexibility by allowing recently discharged patients to communicate with their doctors whenever to handle small difficulties. Outside of the patient environment, the use of IoMT devices in conjunction with telemedicine can be beneficial for ongoing treatment. Personal emergency response systems (PERS), for illustration, can determine circumstances like a fall or a heart attack and automatically summon emergency services. PERS can give security to persons who are in trouble, similar to seniors who wish to live at home but are concerned about their safety.

### In-Person

Wearable medical devices connected to remote tracking or monitoring systems is referred to as on-body IoMT. On-body IoMT, unlike in-home IoMT, can be used outside of the home while people go about their daily activities. On-body consumer IoMT devices is wearable health trackers that anybody can buy for a particular use or share with healthcare providers. These devices can give early warning pointers for more serious health concerns, in addition to recording a typical metric like heart rate. The Apple Watch, for illustration, can warn users of abnormal cardiac measures. Clinical on-body IoMT devices are analogous to consumer IoMT devices, except they have a larger sensor selection. Diabetic individuals, for illustration, can wear glucose monitors to detect changes in their blood glucose levels. Numerous of these widgets can directly communicate data with a patient’s physicians, ensuring that they receive prompt and accurate treatment.

### At Community

The use of IoMT devices throughout a larger city or geographic area is referred to as Community IoMT. Mobility services, for illustration, are devices that track patients while they are in a car. Paramedics and first responders employ emergency response information systems to track patient parameters outside of the medical centre setting. Community IoMT includes technologies that offer remote services in addition to mobile and emergency care. Point-of-care devices, for illustration, can be used by healthcare providers in non-conventional medical settings like a field medical centre, and lockers can be used to dispense drugs to patients in locales where a traditional structure is limited or missing. IoMT devices may also be used in logistics by suppliers to help in the transfer of healthcare goods or medical devices. Sensors, for illustration, can keep an eye on the temperature- or pressure-sensitive shipping containers to ensure that quality is maintained throughout the transport process.

### In-Hospital

Hospitals must keep track of the quality and stock of their medical resources throughout time, as well as how workers and patients use the facility. IoMT sensors and other tracking devices are used by healthcare providers to track all of these interactions so that directors can gain a complete picture of what's going on. IoMT devices may also be used in logistics by suppliers to help in the supply of healthcare inventories.

## POTENTIAL OF THE INTERNET OF MEDICAL THINGS

The Internet of Things/ Internet of Medical Things has the potential to deliver further cost-effective, advanced-quality, more individualized care while also improving patient engagement in the following ways:

### Cost-Cutting

Solutions like the IoMT are becoming even more relevant as the United States spends 18 percent of its annual GDP on healthcare (a figure that is anticipated to climb). The “digital revolution” is expected to save more than $300 billion in healthcare spending, according to estimates. According to the Dell blog (through Goldman Sachs), the largest actual savings opportunity is in chronic disease management, which is worth more than $200 billion. It is also worth highlighting the “infinitely vast” savings prospects in the domain of behaviour modification in the treatment of diseases like obesity and smoking cessation.

### Better Care

Devices that are part of the IoT/IoMT can provide remote data to caregivers and clinicians, such as temperature, heart rates, and glucose levels, that was previously unavailable. The capacity to communicate health data automatically can aid in determining risk for a variety of acute and chronic conditions.

### Patients with a Sense of Empowerment

Patient empowerment is critical not only in an era of patient-centred care but also in a time when we are learning just how much of a difference a patient’s attitude toward treatment can make in clinical outcomes. Telehealth will play a key role in this field, particularly in terms of using remote doctor access to modify behaviour and promote healthier living [1].

### Perception

The perception layer is in charge of carrying and collecting data (e.g., body temperature, heart rate, etc.) through the use of physical devices (e.g., sensors), and also transmitting that data to the network (i.e., Internet). In health monitoring systems, numerous sensors communicate to guarantee that the patient is always covered and receives help as instantly as possible. The four types of medical devices engage with stakeholders (e.g., physicians and patients) in the following ways:

*Wearable Gadgets:* Wearable widgets, like smartwatches, give for accurate, endless, and real-time patient monitoring. They are:

- Position sensors: Patients’ locations are tracked using this.
- Temperature sensors: It's used to measure the body's temperature.
- Pressure sensors: It's used to keep track of a patient’s blood pressure.
- Respiratory sensors: To keep track of how well patients are breathing.
