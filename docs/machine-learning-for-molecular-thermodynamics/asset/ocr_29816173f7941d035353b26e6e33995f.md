We will show how molecular simulation and high-throughput approaches can generate datasets and combine them by two examples. The first one is peptide design, and the second example will predict the thermodynamic properties of peptides.

# ### 4.1. Peptide design

One might obtain insights from the machine-learning-based prediction of thermodynamic properties: (1) experimental chemical databases have potential benefits when combining (2) datasets which are thorough and as many possible substructures in the test set (3) test with enough data fitting to replace the accuracy of internal mechanism completely; perhaps it will help researchers understand the mechanism while providing more accurate and targeted data.

Peptides can self-assemble into ordered structures [135,136]. Their assembly can be built using empirical forces. The π-conjugated peptides contain aromatic and symmetrical oligomers on both sides. The sequence order of aromatic residues determines the specific interactions between the aromatic rings [137–240]. DXX-DXX is a peptide with two terminal amino acids (Asp) and X which represents 20 of non-polar amino acids. In these peptides, the pH-charged moieties are protonated at a low pH, inhibiting the electrostatic repulsion between adjacent molecules.

Molecular dynamics and DFT simulations have been conducted to discuss the effects of sequence properties and π-cores on the self-assembly process and interactions. In the framework of the Fergus group [141], the self-assembly of DAFAC was investigated using molecular simulation, in which OPV is oligo-phenylenevinyl; the aggregate forms a β-sheet-like structure, which is driven by aromatic cores, hydrophobic interaction, and hydrogen bonding. The peptide-based conjugated peptides benefit from the electron delocalization along the π-core.

# ### 4. Learning with High-throughput Molecular Simulation

Molecular simulations can provide massive data for machine learning. Small features are hard to be obtained using experiments. For instance, there are few diffusion property datasets due to the experimental difficulty and nearly countless solvent-solvent combinations. High-throughput frameworks enable us to produce datasets with orders of magnitude larger than the expectations.
