These properties are determined by cores and amino acid sequences, while the variety of sequences grows exponentially with the chain length. It is difficult for traditional methods to explore this chemical space. High-throughput computing and machine learning can be used to predict and direct properties directly,

The fusion group trained a machine learning model [143] to predict the properties of DXXP-XXD oligomers with different $\pi$-cores, including naphthalimide (NAD) and pyrimidine (PYD). The property was measured by an alignment metric determined by association distance and pylendimide. We validated the oligomerization energies of 26 oligptides using MD simulations, and the regression as a function of selected descriptors using multiple linear regression (MLR) model. The alignment metric was fitted to the oligomerization energies. We utilize this method for high-throughput screening of 9826 sequences of the DXXP-XXD family.

The selection of candidates includes three steps. First, more than 5000 candidates were generated by the PADEL software framework [144]. Then, 247 candidates were selected based on the following criteria: (1) first descriptor should be stable and not change significantly with respect to the length of the peptide, (2) descriptors should be informative, (3) redundant descriptors should be removed. Finally, eight descriptors were selected after stepwise forward selection.

The screening results indicated that, compared with N, the larger PDE core increased the oligomerization free by 15 $k_BT$. The larger residues, especially phenylalanine (Phe), could increase the oligomerization free by 2.5 $k_BT$. First, CGMD simulations were conducted for an analysis of 90 peptides to obtain data for the various $\pi$-core cores in the terminal aggregates. The variational autoencoder (VAE) [147] was used to generate the latent space embedding of the DXXP-OPV3-XXD family. In the third step, Gaussian regression (GPR) was used to train a surrogate model relating the latent space embedding to the free energy of conformation. Finally, active learning was used to improve the accuracy of the GPR in the model. CGMD simulation results of 4 extras were added to the model after every prediction cycle until a cycle was completed. The selection of extras was guided by the previous model and Bayesian optimization. The researchers collected 25 additional candidates to obtain a stable model by sampling of 186 peptides and 5-fold-validation $R^2$ of 0.78.
