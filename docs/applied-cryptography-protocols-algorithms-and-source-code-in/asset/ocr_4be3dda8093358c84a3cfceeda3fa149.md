# 25.5 PUBLIC KEY PARTNERS

The five patents in Table 25.3 are held by Public Key Partners (PKP) of Sunnyvale, California, a partnership between RSADSI and Caro-Kahn, Inc.—the parent company of Cylink. [RSADSI 65 percent of the profits and Caro-Kahn 35 percent.] PKP claims that these patents, and 4,218,582 in particular, to use all uses of public-key cryptography.

In [574], PKP wrote:

[4,200,770, 4,218,582, 4,405,829, and 4,424,414] cover all known methods of practicing the art of Public Key, including the variations collectively as Elgalmal.

Due to the broad acceptance of RSA digital signatures throughout the international community, Public Key Partners strongly endorses its incorporation in a digital signature standard. We assure all parties that Public Key Partners will comply with all the policies of ANSI and the IEEE concerning the availability of licenses to practice this art. Specifically, in support of any RSA signature standard that may be adopted, Public Key Partners gives us assurance that licenses to practice RSA signatures will be available under reasonable terms and conditions on a nondiscriminatory basis.

Whether this true depends on whom you talk to. PKP's licenses mostly secret, there is no way to check if the licenses are standard. Although they claim to have never denied a license to anyone, at least two companies claim to have denied a license. PKP guards its patents closely, threatening anyone who tries to use public-key cryptography without a license. In part, this is a reaction to U.S. patent law. If you hold a patent and fail to prevent an infringement, you can lose your patent. There has been much talk about whether the patents are legal, but so far it all been talk. All legal challenges to PKP's patents have been settled before judgment.
