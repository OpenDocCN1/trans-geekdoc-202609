This protocol is self-enforcing. Either party can immediately detect cheating by the other, and no third party would participate in the actual protocol or any adjudication after the protocol has been completed. To see how this works, let's try to cheat.

If wanted to force and force heads, he has potential ways of affecting the outcome. First, he could encrypt "heads" messages in step [2]. Bob would discover this when Alice revealed her keys at step [7]. Second, she could use some other key to decrypt the message in step [4]. This would result in gibberish, which Bob would discover in step [5]. Third, she could lie about the validity of the message in step [6]. Bob would not prove that the message was not valid. Of course, Alice could refuse to participate in the protocol at any step, which point Alice's deception would be obvious to Bob. If wanted to cheat and force "tails," his options are as poor as hers. He could incorrectly encrypt a message in step [3], but Alice would discover this when he looked at the final message at step [6]. He could properly perform step [5], but this would also result in gibberish, which Alice would discover at step [6]. He could claim that he not properly perform step [5] because of some cheating on the part of Alice, could send a "tails" message to Alice at step [7]. Finally, regardless of the message he decrypted, Alice would immediately be able to check the message for authenticity at step [6].
