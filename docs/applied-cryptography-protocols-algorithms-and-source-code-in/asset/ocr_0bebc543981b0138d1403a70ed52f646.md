# 12.1 Background

The Data Encryption Standard (DES), known as the Data Encryption Algorithm
[DEA] by ANSI and the DEA-1 by the ISO, has been a worldwide standard for 20
years. Although it is showing signs of old age, it has held up remarkably against
years of cryptanalysis and it is still secure against all but possibly the most powerful
adversaries.

Development of the Standard

In the early 1970s, nonmilitary cryptographic research was haphazard. Almost no
research papers were published in the field. Most people knew that the military used
special coding equipment to communicate, but few understood the science of crypt-
tography. The National Security Agency (NSA) had considerable knowledge, but
they did not publicly admit their existence.

Buyers didn't know what they were buying. Several small companies made and
sold cryptographic equipment, primarily to overseas governments. The equipment
was all different and couldn't operate. No one really knew if it was
secure, there was no independent body to certify the security. As one government
report said [441]:

The intricacies of relating variations and working principles to the
strength of the encryption/decryption were were, and virtually
unknown to all buyers, and informed decisions as to the right type of on-
, off-line, key generation, etc., which will meet buyers' security needs, have
been most difficult to make.
