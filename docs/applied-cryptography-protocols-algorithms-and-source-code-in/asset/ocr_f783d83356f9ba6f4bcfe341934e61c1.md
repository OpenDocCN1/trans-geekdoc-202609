Other algorithms have been proposed that use ideas similar to those used in knapsack cryptystems, but these too have been broken. The Lu-Lee cryptosystem [990,13] was broken in [20,614,873]; a modification [50] is also insecure [1620]. Attacks on the Goodman-McAulay cryptosystem are in [646,747,267,268]. The Pieprzyk cryptosystem [1246] was broken by similar attacks. While a variation of the knapsack algorithm is secure—the Chor-Rivet knapsack [356], despite a "specialized attack" [743]—the amount of computation requires far less than the other algorithms discussed here. A variant, called the Powerline System, is not secure [958]. Most important, considering the ease with which all the other variations fail, it doesn't seem prudent to trust them.

# 19.3 RSA

Soon Merkel's knapsack algorithm came the first-fledged public-key algorithm, one that works for encryption and digital signatures: RSA [1328,1329]. Of all the public-key algorithms proposed over the years, RSA is by far the easiest to understand and implement.
