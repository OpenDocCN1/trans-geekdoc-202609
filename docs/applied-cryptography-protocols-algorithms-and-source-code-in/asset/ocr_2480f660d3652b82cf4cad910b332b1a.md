Since Alice nor Bob is certain of the exact point when she or he is bound, each has at least fear that she or he is throughout the protocol. At no point can Bob say: "You signed four letters and I only signed three. You are bound but I am not." Bob has no reason to continue with the protocol. Furthermore, the longer they continue, the greater the probability that a judge would rule that they are bound. Again, there is no reason not to continue with the protocol. After all, both wanted to sign the contract; they didn't want to sign before the other.

# Simultaneous Contract Signing without an Arbitrator (Not Face-to-Face)

This protocol uses the same sort of uncertainty [138]; Alice and Bob alternate baby steps toward signing until both have signed.

In the protocol, Alice and Bob exchange a series of signed messages of the form: "I agree with probability $p$, I am bound by this contract."

The recipient of this message can take it to a judge, and with probability $p$, the judge will consider the contract to be signed.

+   1) Alice and Bob agree on a date by which the signing protocol should be completed.
2) Alice and Bob decide on a probability difference that they are willing to live with. For example, Alice might decide that she is not willing to bound with a greater probability than 2 percent over Bob's probability. Call Alice's difference $a$; call Bob's difference $b$.
3) Alice sends Bob a signed message with $p=a$.
4) Bob sends Alice a signed message with $p=a+b$.
5) Let $p$ be the probability of the message received in the previous step from Bob. Alice sends Bob a signed message with $p'=p+a$ or 1, which is smaller.
6) Let $p$ be the probability of the message received in the previous step from Alice. Bob sends Alice a signed message with $p'=p+b$ or 1, which is smaller.
7) Alice and Bob continue alternating steps (5) and (6) until both have received messages with $p=1$ or until the date agree to in step (1) has passed.

As the protocol proceeds, both Alice and Bob agree to bound the contract with a greater and greater probability. For example, Alice might define $a$ as 2 percent and Bob define his $b$ as 1 percent.
