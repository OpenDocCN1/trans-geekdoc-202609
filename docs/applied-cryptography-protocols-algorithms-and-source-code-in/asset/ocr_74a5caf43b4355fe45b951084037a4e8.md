# CHAPTER 6 Esoteric Protocols

[10] Carol verifies and deletes Bob's signatures. She decrypts all the votes with her private key, checks to see that her vote is among the set of votes, signs all the votes, and sends the result to Alice, Bob, and Dave.
Each vote now looks like:

```
$S_C\{D_B\{V,R\}\}$
```

[11] Dave verifies and deletes Carol's signatures. He decrypts all the votes with his private key, checks to see that his vote is among the set of votes, signs all the votes, and sends the result to Alice, Bob, and Carol.
Each vote now looks like:

```
$S_D\{V,R\}$
```

[12] All verify and delete Dave's signature. They check to make sure that their vote is among the set of votes (by looking for their random string among)

[13] Everyone removes the random strings from each vote and tallies the votes.

Not only does this protocol work, it is also self-adjudicating. Alice, Bob, Carol, and will immediately know if someone tries to cheat. No CTF or CIA is required. To see how this works, let's try to cheat.

If someone tries to stuff the ballot, Alice will detect the attempt in step (3) when she receives more votes than she. If Alice tries to stuff the ballot, Bob will notice in step [4]

More is to substitute one vote for another. Since the votes are encrypted with public keys, anyone can create as valid votes as needed. The decryption protocol has two rounds: round one consists of steps (3) through [7], and round two consists of steps (8) through [11]. Vote substitution is detected differently in the different rounds.

If someone substitutes one vote for another round, their actions are discovered immediately. Every vote is signed and sent to all. If one or more the votes noticed that his vote is no longer in the set of votes, immediately stops the protocol because the votes are signed at every step, and everyone can backtrack through the second round of the protocol, it is easy to detect who substituted the votes.

Substituting one vote for another round of the protocol is more subtle. Alice can't do it in step (3), because Bob, Carol, or Dave will detect it in step [5], [6], or [7]. Bob could try in step (5). If he replaces Carol's or Dave's vote (remember, he knows which vote corresponds to which), Carol or Dave will notice in step [6] or [7]. They wouldn't know tampered with their vote [although they would have had to be someone who already handled the votes], but they would know that their vote was tampered with. If Bob is lucky and picks Alice's vote to replace, she won't notice until the second round. Then, she would notice her vote missing in step [8]. Still, she would not know that the votes are shuffled from one step to the other and unsigned; it is impossible for one to backtrack through the protocol to determine who tampered with the votes.
