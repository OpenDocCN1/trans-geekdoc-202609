# Parallel Zero-Knowledge Proofs

The basic zero-knowledge protocol involves n exchanges between Peggy and Victor. Why do they not all in parallel:

+   (1) Peggy uses her information and n random numbers to transform the hard problem into n different isomorphic problems. Then she uses her information and the random numbers to solve the n new hard problems.
(2) Peggy commits to the solution of the n new problems. Victor cannot use these new problems to get any information about the original problems or its solutions.
(3) Peggy reveals to Victor the n new hard problems.
(4) For each of the n hard problems, Victor asks Peggy either to:
    (a) to prove that the old and new problems are isomorphic, or,
    (b) to open the solution she committed to in step (2) and prove that it is a solution to the new problem.
(5) Peggy copies for each of the n new hard problems.

# Noninteractive Zero-Knowledge Proofs

Carol can't be convinced because the protocol is interactive, and she is not involved in the interaction. To convince Carol, and anyone else who might be interested, we need a noninteractive protocol.
