# 3.7 Secret Sharing

This is an adjudicated protocol. Trent has absolute power and can do whatever he wants. He can hand out gibberish and claim that it is a valid piece of the secret, no one will know until they try to reconstruct the secret. He can hand out a piece to Alice, Bob, Carol, and Dave, and tell everyone that only Alice, Carol, and Dave are needed to reconstruct the secret, and then fire. But since this is Trent's secret to divide, this isn't a problem.

However, this protocol has a problem: If any of the people gets power and Trent isn't around, so the message goes. If Carol has a piece of the sauce recipe, goes to work for the competition and takes her piece with her, the rest of them are out of luck. She cannot reproduce the secret, but neither Alice, Bob and Dave working together. Her piece is as critical as the message as every piece combined. Alice, Bob, or Dave knows the length of the message—nothing more. This is true because R, S, T, U, and M all have the same length, sensing anyone of them gives the length of M. Remember, M isn't split in the normal sense of the word; it is XORed with random values.

# 3.7 SECRET SHARING

You're setting up a launch program for a nuclear missile. You want to make sure that no single raving lunatic can initiate a launch. You want to make sure that no two lunatics can initiate a launch. You want to make at least three officers to be ravings lunatic before you allow a mechanical launch.

This is easy: Make a mechanical launch controller. Give each of the officers a key and require that at least three officers stick their keys in the proper slots before you allow them to blow whoever we are blowing up this week. If you're really worried, make the slots apart and require the officers to insert the keys simultaneously—you wouldn't want an officer who steals keys to be able to vaporize Lole.

We can get even more complicated. Maybe the general and two colonels are authorized to launch but, if the general is busy playing golf then it requires that the launch requires five keys. Give the general three keys and the colonels each. The general together with two colonels can launch the missile; so can the five colonels. However, a general and one colonel cannot, neither four colonels.

A more complicated scheme, called a threshold scheme, can do all of this and mathematically. At its simplest, you can take any message (a secret recipe, codes, your laundry list, etc.) and divide it into n pieces, called or shares, such that any m of them can be used to reconstruct the message. More precisely, this is called an [m,n]-threshold scheme.

With a [3,4]-threshold scheme, Trent can divide his secret sauce recipe among Alice, Bob, Carol, and Dave, such that three of them can put their shadows and reconstruct the secret. If Carol is on vacation, Alice, Bob and Dave can do it. If Bob gets over by a bus, Alice, Carol, and Dave can do it. However, if Bob gets over by a bus while Carol is on vacation, Alice and Dave can't reconstruct the message by themselves.
