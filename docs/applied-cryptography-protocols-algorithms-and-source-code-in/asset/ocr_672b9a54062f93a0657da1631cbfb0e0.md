And what about industrial espionage? There is no reason to believe that countries currently conduct industrial espionage for their important or state-run companies will refrain from doing so on key-escrowed encryption systems. Indeed, since virtually no country is going to allow other countries to oversee its intelligence operations, widespread use of escrow encryption will probably increase the use of wiretaps.

Even if with good civil rights records use key escrow only for the legitimate pursuit of criminals and terrorists, it's certain to be used elsewhere to keep track of dissidents, blackmail political opponents, and so on. Digital communications offer the opportunity to do a much thorough job of monitoring citizens' actions, opinions, purchases, and associations as are possible in an analog world.

It's not clear how this will affect commercial key escrow, except that 20 years from now, selling Turkey or China a ready-made-key-escrow system may look a lot like selling shock barons to South Africa in 1970, or building a chemical plant for Iraq in 1980. Even worse, effortless and untraceable tapping of communications may tempt a number of governments into tracking many of their citizens' communications, even those which haven't generally tried to do before. And there's no guarantee that liberal democracies will be immune to this temptation.
