# 20.4 LOGARITHM SIGNATURE SCHEMES

ElGamal, Schnorr (see Section 21.3), and DSA signature schemes are very similar. In fact, they are just three examples of a general digital signature scheme based on the Discrete Logarithm Problem. With thousands of other signature schemes, they are part of the same family [740,741,699,1184].

Choose $p$, a large prime number, and $q$, either $p-1$ or a large prime factor of $p-1$. Then choose $g$, a random number between 1 and $q$ such that $g^q \equiv 1 \mod p$. All these numbers are public, and can be common to a group of users. The private key is $x$, less than $q$. The public key is $y = g^x \mod p$.

To send a message, first choose a random $k$ less and relatively prime to $q$. If $q$ is also prime, $k$ less than $q$ works. First compute

```
$r = g^k \mod p$
```

The generalized signature equation now becomes

```
$ak = b + cx \mod q$
```

The coefficients $a, b$, and $c$ can be any of things. Each line in Table 20.4 gives six signatures.

To verify the signature, the receiver must confirm that

```
$r^a = g^{by}y \mod p$
```

This is called the verification equation.
