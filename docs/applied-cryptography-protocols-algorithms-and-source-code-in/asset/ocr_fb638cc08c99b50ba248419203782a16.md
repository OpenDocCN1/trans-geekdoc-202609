# 5.2 Zero-Knowledge Proofs of Identity

# # The Multiple Identity Fraud

There are other possible abuses to zero-knowledge proofs of identity, also discussed in [485,120]. In some implementations, there is no check when an individual registers a public key. Hence Alice can have several private keys, and therefore, identities. This can be a great help if she wants to commit tax fraud. Alice can also commit a crime and disappear. First, she creates and publishes several identities. One of them she doesn't use. Then, she uses that identity and commits a crime so that the person she identifies is the witness. The witness knows the identity of the person who committed the crime, but if Alice never uses that identity again—she's untraceable.

To prevent this, there must be some mechanism by which each person has only one identity. [120] The authors suggest the bizarre of tamperproof babies who are impossible to clone and contain a unique number as their genetic code. They also suggested having each baby have an identity at birth. (Actually, the parents would have to do this as the baby would be otherwise occupied.) This could be easily abused; parents could use multiple identities for the child's birth. In the end, the uniqueness of an individual is based on trust.

# # Renting Passports

Alice wants to travel to Zaire, but government won't give her a visa. Carol offers to rent her identity to Alice. [Bob offered first, but there were some obvious problems.] Carol sells her private key and Alice goes off to Zaire pretending to be Carol.

Carol has not only paid for her identity, but now she has a perfect alkali. She commits a crime while Alice is “Carol” has proved her identity in Zaire; how could she commit a crime back home?

Of course, Alice is free to commit crimes as well. She does so either if she leaves or after returns, near Carol's home. First she identifies herself as Carol [she has Carol's private key, so she can easily do that]; then she commits a crime and runs away. The police will be looking for Carol. Carol will claim her identity to Alice, but who would believe such a nonsensical story?

The problem is that Alice is not really proving her identity; she is proving that she knows a piece of secret information. It is the link between information and the person it belongs to that being abused. The tamperproof solution would protect this kind of fraud, as a police state where all citizens would have to prove their identity very frequently (at the end of each day, at each street corner, etc.). Biometric methods—fingerprints, retinal scanning, voiceprints, and so on—may help solve this problem.

# # Proofs of Membership

Alice wants to prove to Bob that she is a member of some super-secret organization, but she does not want to reveal her identity. This problem is similar but different from proving identity, and has also been studied [87,906,907,1201,1445]. Some solutions are related to the problem of group signatures (see Section 4.6).
