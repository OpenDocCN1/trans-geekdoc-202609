Now, here is the protocol. Alice is the seller. Bob and Carol are buyers. Alice has k-bit secrets: $S_1, S_2, \dots, S_b$. Bob wants to buy secret $S_{bj}$ and Carol wants to buy secret $S_c$.

+   Alice generates a public-key/private-key key pair and tells Bob (but Carol) the public key. She generates another public-key/private-key key and tells Carol (but not Bob) the public key.

+   Bob generates k-bit random numbers, $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_b$, and tells Carol k-bit random numbers, $C_1, C_2, \dots, C_b$, and tells them to Bob.

+   Bob encrypts $C_b$, the secret he wants to buy with the public key from Alice. He computes the FBI of $C_b$ and the result he just encrypted.

+   Carol encrypts $B_c$, the secret she wants to buy with the public key from Alice. She computes the FBI of $B_c$ and the result she just encrypted.

+   Bob takes each of the n-bit numbers $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_b$, and replaces every bit whose index is not in the FBI he received from Carol with its complement. He sends this new list of n-bit numbers, $B'_1, B'_2, \dots, B'_b$, to Alice.

+   Carol takes each of the n-bit numbers $C_1, C_2, \dots, C_b$, and replaces every bit whose index is not in the FBI she received from Bob with its complement. She sends this new list of n-bit numbers, $C'_1, C'_2, \dots, C'_b$, to Alice.

+   Alice decrypts $B'_b$ with Carol's private key, giving her k-bit numbers: $B'_1, B'_2, \dots, B'_b$. She computes $S_b \oplus B'_i$, for $i=1$ to $k$, and sends the results to Bob. (Note: The text has a typo in original logic, but following visual text provided)

+   Bob decrypts $S_c$ by XORing $C_c$ and the cth number he received from Alice.

+   Carol computes $S_c$ by XORing $B_c$ and the cth number she received from Alice.
