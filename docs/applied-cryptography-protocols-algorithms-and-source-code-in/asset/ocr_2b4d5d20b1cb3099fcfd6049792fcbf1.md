# Sharing a Secret without Revealing the Shares

These schemes have a problem. When everyone gets together to reconstruct their secret, they reveal their shares. This not be the case. If the shared secret is a private key (to a digital signature, for example), then n shareholders can complete a partial share of the document. After the nth partition has signed the shared private key and none of the shareholders learns any other shares. The point is that the secret can be reused, and you do not need a trusted processor to handle it. This concept is further explored by Yvo Desmidt and Yair Frankel [483,484].

# Verifiable Secret Sharing

Trent gives Alice, Bob, Carol, and Dave a share or at least he says does. The only way if they know if they have a valid share is to reconstruct the secret. Maybe Trent sends Bob a bad share or accidentally received a bad share through communications error. Verifiable secret sharing allows each of them to individually verify that they have a valid share, without having to reconstruct the secret [558,1235].

# Secret-Sharing Schemes with Prevention

A secret is divided among 50 people so that 10 can get together and reconstruct the secret. That is, we can make the same secret-sharing scheme with the added constraint that 20 people can get together and prevent the others from reconstructing the secret, no matter how many of them there? As it turns out, we can [145].

The math is complicated, but the basic idea is that everyone gets two shares: a “yes” share and a “no” share. When it comes time to reconstruct the secret, people submit one of their shares. The actual share they submit depends on whether they wish the reconstruct it. If there are n or more “yes” shares and fewer than n “no” shares, the secret can be reconstructed. Otherwise, it cannot.

Of course, nothing prevents a sufficient number of “yes” people from going off in a corner without they (assuming they know who they are) reconstructing the secret. In a situation where everyone submits their shares into a central computer, this scheme will work.

# Secret Sharing with Disenrollment

You've set up your secret-sharing system and you want to fire one of your shareholders. You could set up a new scheme without that person, but that's time-consuming. There are methods for coping with this system. They allow a new sharing scheme to be activated instantly once one of the participants becomes untrustworthy [1004].
