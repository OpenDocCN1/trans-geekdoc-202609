# 5.2 ZERO-KNOWLEDGE PROOFS OF IDENTITY

In the real world, we use physical tokens as proofs of identity: passports, driver's licenses, credit cards, and so on. The token contains something that links it to a person: a picture, ideally, or a signature, but it could also be as easily as a thumbprint, a retinal scan, or a x-ray. Wouldn't it be to do the same thing digitally?

Using zero-knowledge proofs as proofs of identity was first proposed by Uriel Feige, Amos Fiat, and Adi Shamer [566,567]. Alice's vice becomes a function of her "identity". Using a zero-knowledge proof, she proves that she knows her private key and other proves her identity. Algorithms for this can be found in Section 23.11.

This idea allows a person to prove his identity without any physical token. However, it's not perfect. Here are some abuses.

# The Chess Grandmaster Problem

Here's how Alice, who doesn't know the rules to chess, can defeat a grandmaster.(This is sometimes called the Chess Grandmaster Problem.) She challenges both Gary Kasparov and Anatoly Karpov to a game, at the same time and place, in separate rooms. She plays white against Kasparov and black against Karpov. Neither grandmaster knows the other.

Karpov, as white, Alice records the moves and walks into the room with Kasparov. Playing white, she makes the move against Kasparov. Kasparov makes his first move as black. Alice records the moves, walks into the room with Karpov, and makes the same move. This continues, until one wins the game and or both end in a draw.

In reality, Karpov is playing Karpov and Alice is simply acting as the been, mimicking the moves of each grandmaster on the other's board. if neither Karpov nor Kasparov knows about the other's presence, each will be impressed with Alice's play.

This kind of fraud can be used against zero-knowledge proofs of identity [485,120]. While Alice is proving her identity to Mallory, Mallory can simultaneously prove that he is Alice.
