# LaRange Interpolating Polynomial Scheme

Adi Shamir uses polynomial equations in a finite field to construct a threshold scheme [141]. Choose a prime, $p$, which is larger than the number of possible shadows and than the largest possible secret. To share a secret, generate an arbitrary polynomial of degree $m-1$. For example, if you want to create a $\{3,n\}$-threshold quadratic (three shadows are necessary to reconstruct $M$), generate a quadratic polynomial

```
$(ax^2 + bx + M) \pmod p$
```

where $p$ is a random prime larger than any of the coefficients. The coefficients $a$ and $b$ are chosen randomly, they are kept secret and discarded after the shadows are handed out. The message $M$ must be made public.

The shadows are obtained by evaluating the polynomial at $n$ points:

```
$k_i = F(x_i)$
```

In other words, the first shadow could be the polynomial evaluated at $x=1$, the second shadow could be the polynomial evaluated at $x=2$, and so forth.

Since the quadratic polynomial has three coefficients $a, b$, and $M$, any three shadows can be used to create equations. Two shadows cannot. Four or five shadows are redundant.
