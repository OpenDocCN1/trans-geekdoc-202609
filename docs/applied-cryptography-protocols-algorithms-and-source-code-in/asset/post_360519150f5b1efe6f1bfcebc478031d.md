## 3.7 SECRET SHARING

You're setting up a launch program for a nuclear missile. You want to make sure that no single raving lunatic can initiate a launch. You want to make sure that no two lunatics can initiate a launch. You want to make at least three officers to be ravings lunatic before you allow a mechanical launch.

This is easy: Make a mechanical launch controller. Give each of the officers a key and require that at least three officers stick their keys in the proper slots before you allow them to blow whoever we are blowing up this week. If you're really worried, make the slots apart and require the officers to insert the keys simultaneously—you wouldn't want an officer who steals keys to be able to vaporize Lole.

We can get even more complicated. Maybe the general and two colonels are authorized to launch but, if the general is busy playing golf then it requires that the launch requires five keys. Give the general three keys and the colonels each. The general together with two colonels can launch the missile; so can the five colonels. However, a general and one colonel cannot, neither four colonels.

A more complicated scheme, called a threshold scheme, can do all of this and mathematically. At its simplest, you can take any message (a secret recipe, codes, your laundry list, etc.) and divide it into n pieces, called shares, such that any m of them can be used to reconstruct the message. More precisely, this is called an [m,n]-threshold scheme.

With a [3,4]-threshold scheme, Trent can divide his secret sauce recipe among Alice, Bob, Carol, and Dave, such that three of them can put their shares and reconstruct the secret. If Carol is on vacation, Alice, Bob and Dave can do it. If Bob gets over by a bus, Alice, Carol, and Dave can do it. However, if Bob gets over by a bus while Carol is on vacation, Alice and Dave can't reconstruct the message by themselves.

## Verifiable Secret Sharing

Trent gives Alice, Bob, Carol, and Dave a share or at least he does. The only way if they know they have a valid share is to reconstruct the secret. Maybe Trent sends Bob a bad share or accidentally received a bad share through communications error. Verifiable secret sharing allows each of them to individually verify that they have a valid share, without having to reconstruct the actual share they submit depends on whether they wish the reconstruct. If there are n or more “yes” shares and fewer than n “no” shares, the secret can be reconstructed. Otherwise, it cannot.
