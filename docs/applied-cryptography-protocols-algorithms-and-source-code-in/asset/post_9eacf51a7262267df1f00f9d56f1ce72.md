## SKID

SKID2 and SKID3 are symmetric cryptography identification protocols developed for RACE's RIPE project [1305] (see Section 25.7). They use a MAC (to provide security and assume that Alice and Bob share a secret key, K). SKID allows Bob to prove his identity to Alice. Here's the protocol:

- (1) Alice chooses a random number, $R_A$; [The RIPE document specifies a 64-bit number). She sends it to Bob.
- (2) Bob chooses a random number, $R_B$; [The RIPE document specifies a 64-bit number). He sends it to Alice:

```
$R_B, H_K(R_A, R_B)$
```

$H_K$ is the MAC. The RIPE document suggests RIPE-MAC function—see Section 18.14) $B$ is Bob's name.

(3) Alice computes $H_k(R_A,R_B)$ and compares it with what she received from Bob. If the results are identical, then Alice knows that she is communicating with Bob.

SKID3 provides mutual authentication between Alice and Bob. Steps (1) through (3) are identical to SKID2, and then the protocol proceeds with:

- (4) Alice sends Bob: $H_k(R_B,A)$

A is Alice's name.

- (5) Bob computes $H_k(R_B,A)$, and compares it with what he received from Alice. If the results are identical, then Bob knows he is communicating with Alice.

This protocol is not secure against a man-in-the-middle attack. In general, a man-in-the-middle attack can defeat any protocol that doesn't involve a secret of some kind.

## Yahalom

In this protocol, both Alice and Bob share a secret key.

- (1) Alice sends a message to Trent consisting of her name, Bob's name, and a random number.
$A,B,R_A$

- (3) Alice decrypts the message and extracts $K$. She confirms that $R_A$ is the same value that she sent in step [1]. Then she sends Bob the message that Trent encrypted in his key.
$E_B(K,A)$

- (4) Bob decrypts the message and extracts $K$. He then generates another random value, $R_B$. He encrypts the message with $K$ and sends it to Alice.
$E_A(R_B)$

- (5) Alice decrypts the message with $K$. She generates $R_B - 1$ and encrypts it with $K$. Then she sends the message back to Bob.
$E_B(R_B-1)$

- (6) Bob decrypts the message with $K$ and verifies that it is $R_B - 1$.

At the end, Alice and Bob are each convinced they are talking to the other and not to a third party. The novelty is that Bob is the first to connect Trent, who only sends one message to Alice.
