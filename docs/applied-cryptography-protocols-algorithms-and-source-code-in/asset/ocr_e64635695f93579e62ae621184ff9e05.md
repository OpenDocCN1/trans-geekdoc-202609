In the world of commodities trading, secrets only need to be kept for minutes. In the newspaper business, today's secrets are tomorrow's headlines. Product development information might need to remain secret for a year or two. U.S. Census data are required to remain secret for 100 years.

The guest list for your sister's birthday party is only interesting to your noisy relatives. Corporate trade secrets are interesting to rival companies. Military secrets are interesting to rival militaries.

You can even specify security requirements in these terms. For example:

The key length must be such that there is a probability of no than 1 in 2^s that an attacker with $100 million to spend could break the system within one year, even using technology advances at a rate of 30 percent per annum over the period.

Future computing is hard to estimate, but here is a reasonable rule of thumb: The efficiency of computing equipment doubles every eighteen months and increases by a factor of 10 every five years. So, in 50 years the fastest computers will be 10 billion faster than today's! Remember, too, that these numbers only relate to general-purpose computers, who knows what kind of specialized cryptystem-breaking equipment will be developed in the next 50 years?

Assuming that a cryptographic algorithm will be used in 30 years, you can get some idea how secure it is. An algorithm designed today will probably not get use until until 2000, and will still be used in 2025 to encrypt messages that must remain secret until 2075 or later.

# Table 7.10
Security Requirements for Different Information

| Type of Traffic | Lifetime | Minimum Key Length |
|---|---||
| Tactical military information | minutes/hours | 56-64 bits |
| Product announcements, mergers, interest rates | days/weeks | 64 bits |
| Long-term business plans | years | 64 bits |
| Trade secrets [e.g., recipe for Coca-Cola] | decades | 112 bits |
| H-bomb secrets | >40 years | 128 bits |
| Identities of spies | >50 years | 128 bits |
| Personal affairs | >50 years | 128 bits |
| Diplomatic embarrassments | >65 years | at least 128 bits |
| U.S. census data | 100 years | at least 128 bits |
