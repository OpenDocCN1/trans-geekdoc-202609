# Digital Cash and the Perfect Crime

Digital cash has its dark side, sometimes people don't want so much privacy. Watch Alice the perfect crime [1575]:

+   (1) Alice kidnaps a baby.
(2) Alice prepares 10,000 money orders for $1000 [or as many as she wants for whatever denomination she wants].
(3) Alice binds all 10,000 money orders, using a blind signature protocol. She sends them to the authorities with the threat to kill the baby unless the following are met:
(a) Have a bank sign 10,000 money orders.
(b) Publish the results in a newspaper.
(4) The authorities comply.
(5) Alice buys a newspaper, unblinds the money orders, and starts spending. There is no way for the authorities to trace the money orders to her.
(6) Alice frees the baby.

Note that this situation is much worse than any involving physical tokens—cash. Without physical cash, the police have less opportunity to apprehend the kidnapper.
In general, digital cash isn't a good deal for criminals. The problem is that the anonymity works one way: The spender is anonymous, but the merchant is not. The merchant cannot hide the fact that they received money. Digital cash will make it easy for the government to determine how much money you have, but impossible to determine what you spent it on.
