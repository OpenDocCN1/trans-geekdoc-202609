(4) Each voter chooses a random identification number. He creates a message with that number, the validation number he received from the CLA, and his vote. He sends this message to the CTF.

(5) The CTF checks the validation number against the list it received from the CLA in step [3]. If the validation number is there, the CTF crosses it off (to prevent someone from voting twice). The CTF adds the identification number to the list of people who voted for a particular candidate and adds one to the tally.

(6) After all votes have been received, the CTF publishes the outcome, as well as lists of identification numbers and for whom their owners voted.

Like the previous protocol, each voter can look at the list of identification numbers and find his. This gives him proof that his vote was counted. Of course, all messages passing the parties in the protocol should be encrypted and signed to prevent someone from impersonating else or intercepting transmissions.
The CTF cannot modify votes because each voter will look for his identification string. If a voter doesn't find his identification string, or finds his string in a tally other than the one he voted for, he will immediately know there was foul play. The CTF cannot stuff the ballot box because many voters have been certified and their validation numbers, and will detect any modifications.
