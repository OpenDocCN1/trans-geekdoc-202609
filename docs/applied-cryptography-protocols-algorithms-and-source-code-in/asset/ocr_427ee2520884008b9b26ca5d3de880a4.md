The two-way protocol consists of the one-way protocol and then a similar wayprotocol from Bob to Alice. After executing steps (1) through (8) of the one-way protocol, the two-way protocol continues with:

+   [9] Bob generates another random number, $R_B$.
[10] Bob constructs a message $M' = [T_B, R_B, I_A, R_A, d]$, where $T_B$ is Bob's timestamp, $I_A$ is the identity of Alice and $d$ is arbitrary data. The data may be encrypted with Alice's public key, $E_A$, for security. $R_A$ is the random number generated in step [1].
[11] Bob sends $D_B[M']$ to Alice.
[12] Alice uses $E_A$ to decrypt $D_B[M']$. This verifies both Bob's signature and the integrity of the signed information.
[13] Alice checks the $I_A$ in $M'$ and confirms that the message is current.
[14] Alice checks the $T_B$ in $M'$ and confirms the message is not an old one.
[15] As an option, Alice can check the $R_B$ in $M'$ to ensure the message is not an old one replayed.

The three-way protocol accomplishes the same thing as the two-way protocol, but without timestamps. Steps [1] through [15] are identical to the two-way protocol, with $T_A = T_B = 0$.

# 24.10 PRIVACY-ENHANCED MAIL (PEM)

PEM is the Internet Privacy-Enhanced Mail standard, adopted by the Internet Architecture Board [IAB] to provide secure email over the Internet. It was designed by the Internet Research Task Force (IRTF) Privacy and Security Research Group (PSRG), and then handed over to the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) PEM Working Group. The PEM protocols provide for encryption, authentication, message integrity, and key management.
