As part of that effort, RACE established the RACE Integrity Priorities Evaluation (RIPE) to put together a portfolio of techniques to meet the anticipated security requirements of IBC.

Six European cryptography groups made up the RIPE consortium: Center for Mathematics and Computer Science, Amsterdam, Siemens AG, Philips Crypto BV, PTT Nederland, NTT Research, Katholieke Universiteit Leuven and Aarhus University. After calls for algorithms in 1989 and 1991 [1564], 32 submissions from around the world, and a 350-man election project, the consortium published RIPE Integrity Primitives [1305,1332]. The report included an introduction and some basic integrity concepts, and their primitives: MDC-4 (see Section 18.11), RIPE-MD (see Section 18.8), RIPE-MAC (see Section 16.1), IBC-HASH, KISH (see Section 3.2), RSA, COMSET and RSA key generator.

# 25.8 Conditional Access for Europe (CAFE)

Conditional Access for Europe (CAFE) is a project in the European Community's ESPR program [204,205]. Work began in December 1992 and is scheduled to be finished by the end of 1995. The consortium involved of groups for social and market studies (Cardware, Institut für Sozialforschung), software and hardware manufacturers (DigICash, Gengico, Ingenios), and cryptographers (CWI Amsterdam, PTT Research Netherlands, SPET, Sintet Delab Trondheim, Universities of Aarhus, Hildenheim and Leuven).

The goal is to develop systems for conditional access, particularly digital payment systems. Payment systems must give legal certainty to everybody at all times and as little as possible—this certainty should not depend on the tamper-resistance of any devices.

The basic device for CAFE is an electronic wallet: a small computer that looks something like a pocket calculator. It has a battery, keyboard, screen, and an infrared channel for communicating with other wallets. Every user owns and uses his own wallet, which administers his rights and guarantees its security.

A device with wallet and screen has an advantage over a smart card; it can operate independently of a terminal. A user can directly enter his password and the amount of the payment. The user does not have to give his wallet to complete a transaction, unlike the current situation with credit cards.

Additional features are:

+   - Offline transactions. The purpose of the system is to replace cash transactions; an online system would be too cumbersome.
- Loss tolerance. If a user loses his wallet, or if it breaks or is stolen, he can recover his money.
- Support for different currencies.
