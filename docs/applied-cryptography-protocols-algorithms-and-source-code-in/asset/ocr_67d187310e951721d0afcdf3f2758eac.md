# 23.4 Undeniable Digital Signatures

To sign a message, Alice computes $z = m^x \mod p$. That's all she has to do. 
Verification is a little more complicated.

+   (1) Bob chooses two random numbers, $a$ and $b$, both less than $p$, and sends
Alice:
$c = z^{(g^a)} \mod p$
(2) Alice computes $t=x^{-1} \mod {p-1}$, and sends Bob:
$d = c^d \mod p$
(3) Bob confirms that
$d = m^{g^b} \mod p$

If it is, Alice accepts the signature as genuine.
Imagine Alice and Bob went through this protocol, and Bob is convinced
that Alice signed the message. Bob wants to convince Carol, so he shows her tran-
script of the protocol. Dave, wants to convince Carol that some other per-
son signed the document. He creates a fake transcript of the protocol. First he
generates the message in step (1). Then in step (3) he generates and the fake trans-\miss from this other person in step (2). Finally, he generates the message in step (2).
Carol, both Bob and Dave's transcripts are identical. She cannot be
of the signature's validity unless she goes through the protocol herself.
Of course, if she was watching over Bob's shoulder as he completed the protocol,
she would be convinced.
There may be a problem with this signature scheme, but I know of no details.
Please pay attention to the literature before you use it.

that her signature is valid—but also has a disavowal protocol; Alice can use a zero-
knowledge interactive protocol to convince him that the signature is not valid, if

+   (1) Bob chooses two random numbers, $a$ and $b$, both less than $p$, and sends Alice:
$c = z^{g^b} \mod p$
