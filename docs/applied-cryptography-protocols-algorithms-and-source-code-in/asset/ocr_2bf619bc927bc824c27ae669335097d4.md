The protocol works for any number of buyers. If Bob, Carol, and Dave want to buy secrets, each gives each two public keys, one for each of the others. Each buyer gets a set of numbers from each other buyer. Then, they complete the protocol with Alice for each set of numbers and XOR all of their final results from Alice to get their secret. More details are in [1374.1175].

Unfortunately, a pair of dishonest parties can cheat. Alice and Carol, working together, can easily find what Bob secret: If they know the FBI of $C_b$ and Bob's encryption algorithm, they can find $b$ such that $C_b$ has the right FBI. And Bob and Carol, working together, can easily get all the secrets from Alice. If you assume honest parties, there's an easier protocol [389].

+   (1) Alice encrypts all of the secrets with RSA and sends them to Bob:
$C_i = S_i^e \mod n$

+   (2) Bob chooses his secret $C_b$, picks a random $r$, and sends $C'$ to Alice.
$C' = C_b^r \mod n$

+   (3) Alice sends $P'$.
$P' = C^d \mod n$

+   (4) Bob calculates $S_b$.
$S_b = P'^{-1} \mod n$

If the parties be dishonest, Bob can prove in zero-knowledge that he knows some $r$ that $C' = C_{b,r} \mod n$ and keep $b$ secret until Alice gives him $P'$ in step (3) [246].
