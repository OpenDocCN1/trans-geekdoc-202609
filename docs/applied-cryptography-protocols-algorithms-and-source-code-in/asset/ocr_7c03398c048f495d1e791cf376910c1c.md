In reality, the NSA has control over the export of cryptographic products. If you have a Commodity Jurisdiction [CJ], you must submit your product to the NSA for approval and submit the CJ to the State Department. After Department approval, the matter moves under the jurisdiction of the Commerce Department, which has never cared much about the export of cryptography. However, the State Department will never grant a CJ without NSA approval.

He had a point. The ITAR did cover any "unclassified information that can be, used or adapted for the design, production, manufacture, repair, overhaul, processing, engineering, development, operation, maintenance, or reconstruction" of listed materials, as well as "any technology which advances the state of art or a new art in an area of significant military applicability in the United States." And export did include transferring information both by writing and by other or visual means, including briefings and symposium in which foreign nations are present.
