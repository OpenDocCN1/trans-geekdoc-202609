# CHAPTER 6 Esoteric Protocols

This is quite an amazing protocol, so let's look at it from various angles.
Alice cheat? Her digital money order is nothing more than a string of bits, so she can copy it. Spending the first time won't be a problem; she'll complete the protocol and everything will go smoothly. The merchant will give her a random n-bit selector string in step [7] and Alice will open either the left half or right half of each i, in step [8]. In step [10], the bank will record all of this data, as well as the merchant's uniqueness string.

When she tries to use the same money order a second time, the merchant (either the same merchant or a different merchant) will give her a random selector string in step [7]. Alice must comply in step [8]; not doing so will immediately alert the merchant that something is suspicious. Now, when the merchant brings that money order with the same uniqueness string already deposited, the bank compares the opened halves of the identity strings. The odds that two random selector strings are the same are 1 in 2ⁿ unlikely to happen before the next ice age. Now, the bank finds a pair with one half opened the first time and the other half opened the second time. XORs the two halves together, and pops Alice's name. The bank knows who to spend the money order.

Note that this protocol does prevent Alice from trying; it detects cheating with almost certainty. Alice can't prevent her identity from being revealed if she cheats. She can't change her uniqueness string or any of the identity strings, because then she's signature will no longer be valid. The merchant will immediately notice in step [6].

Alice could try to snatch money order past the bank, on which the identity strings don't reveal her name, or better, whose identity strings reveal someone else's name. The odds of getting this true past the bank in step [3] are 1 in n. These aren't impossible odds, but if you make the penalty severe enough Alice won't try it. Or, you could increase the number of redundant money orders Alice makes in step [1).

Even collision between Alice and the merchant can't cheat the bank. As long as the bank signs the money order with the uniqueness string, the bank is assured of only having to make good on the money order once.

What about the bank? It can figure out that the money order it accepted from the merchant was the one she signed for! Alice is protected by the blind signature protocol in steps [2] through [5]. The bank cannot make the connection, even if it keeps records of every transaction. Even more strongly, there is no way for the bank and the merchant to get together to figure out who Alice is. Alice can walk in the store, and anonymously, make her purchase.
