# Cryptanalysis of FEAL

FEAL-4, with four rounds, was successfully cryptanalized with a chosen-plaintext attack in [201] and later demolished in [1132]. This latter attack, by Sean Murphy, was the first differential-cryptanalysis attack and required only 20 plaintexts. The designers retaliated with 8-round FEAL [1436,1437,1108] which Biham and Shamur cryptanalized the SECUROM '89 conference [1424]. Another chosen-plaintext attack, using only 1000 blocks, against FEAL-8 [610] forced the designers to give up their hands and define FEAL-N [1102,1104], with a variable number of rounds (greater than 4, or 8).

Biham and Shamur used differential cryptanalysis against FEAL-N; they could break it more quickly than brute force [with fewer than $2^n$ chosen plaintext encryptions] for N less than 32 [169]. FEAL-N requires $2^{26}$ chosen plaintexts and $2^{46.5}$ known plain to break. FEAL-8 required 2000 plaintexts and $2^{37.5}$ known plaintexts to break. FEAL-4 could be broken with eight carefully selected plaintexts.

The designers defined FEAL-N, a modification of FEAL, that accepts 128-bit keys (see Figure 13.7) [1103,1104]. Biham and Shamur showed FEAL-N with a 128-bit key is just easy to break as FEAL-N with a 64-bit key, for any value of N [169]. Recently FEAL-N was proposed, which suggests FEAL with a dynamic swapping function [1525].

There's more. Another attack against FEAL-4, requiring only 1000 known plaintexts, and FEAL-8, requiring only 20000 known plaintexts, was published in [1520]. Other attacks The best attack is by Mitsuru Matsui and Atshiro Yamaguchi [1020]. This is the first of linear cryptanalysis, and can break FEAL-4 with 5 known plaintexts, FEAL-6 with 100 known plaintexts and FEAL-8 with $2^8$ known plaintexts. Further refinements are in [64]. Differential-linear cryptanalysis can break FEAL-8 with 12 chosen plaintexts [62]. Whenever someone discovers a new cryptanalytic attack, he always to try it out on FEAL first.

# 13.5 REDOC

REDOC is another block algorithm, designed by Michael Wood for Cryptech, Inc. [1613,400]. It has a 20-bit key and an 80-bit block.

REDOC performs all of its manipulations—permutations, substitutions, and key XORs—by bytes, the algorithm is efficient in software. REDOC uses variable function units. Unlike DES, which has a fixed (almost obsolete for security) set of permutation and substitution tables, actual), REDOC uses a key-dependent and plaintext-dependent set of tables (S-boxes, actually). REDOC has 10 rounds, each round is a complicated series of manipulations on the block.
