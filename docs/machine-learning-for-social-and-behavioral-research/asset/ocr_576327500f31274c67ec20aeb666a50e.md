# 2.5 Principle #3: Labeling a Study as Exploratory or Confirmatory Is Too Simplistic

Often, the terms exploratory or confirmatory are used to provide a metascription of the research conducted and to conduct research that lies on the continuum between exploratory and confirmatory. Although this continuum is not difficult to understand, at the very least, most researchers agree on the research that falls at the end points. Fully confirmatory research entails full theoretical justification for study design and the analyses performed (these studies are often experimental in nature), whereas exploratory research lacks theoretical justification and is described as the “kitchen sink” approach. However, difficulties arise in assessing research: theoretical justification for variable inclusion, not what types of relationships are examined; partial inclusion of nonoretically justified variables; transforming a variable-based sample distribution; and studies with little theoretical justification, to name a few. As described above, this becomes increasingly difficult as the number of variables increase and as the algorithm options expand.

Machine learning is often described as the kitchen sink approach: just throw everything in and see what comes out. This is often depicted as the miners of the 1800s holding up a golden nugget, data mining. In practice, researchers often use theoretical justification for the sample and variables analyzed, and may even hypothesize on the presence of specific interactions. However, there are no hypotheses for every variable included, which variables will be most important, and the degree of prediction error. Obviously, these characteristics do not just justify a label of confirmatory research, and because there is some theoretical justification, it is not entirely exploratory research either. But how much confirmatory (or less exploratory) is this than the kitchen sink approach?
