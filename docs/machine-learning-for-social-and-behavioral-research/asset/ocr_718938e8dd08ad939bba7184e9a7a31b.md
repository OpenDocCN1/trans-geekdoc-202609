Standardizing the X variables particularly makes the interpretation of simple effects easier, as we interpret the effect conditional on the values of every other predictor at 0, or when standardized, the mean.

For interpreting the two-way interactions in the model, we use the general knowledge variable as the reference as it is present in both interactions. Going from the mean of knowledge to I standard deviation above the mean, the effect of both math and income decrease, and vice versa if we flipped the variables. What we may be observing, in fact, nonlinearity, a commonly confounding effect on interactions. However, this is why we included the specification of quadratic effects as well, since it is common for the relationship between cognitive variables and various outcomes of interest to decrease at higher values (e.g., at high IQs, small gains have little effect in comparison to lower values), thus producing a form of nonlinearity. We highlight this to caution readers against only searching for interactions with a blind eye toward nonlinearity. In the following chapter we discuss methods that are both nonlinear and search for interactions.
