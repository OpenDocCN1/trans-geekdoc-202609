# 4.3.3 Factor Analysis-Based Reliability

A common recommendation is to assess the factor structure of a scale prior to calculating reliability (e.g., Schmitt, 1996). Using a factor model, we can calculate omega total by constraining the factor variance to 1, and as well as not imparting any residual covari

```
$$\omega_{Total} = \frac{(\sum_{i=1}^k \lambda_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^k \lambda_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^k \theta_{ii}}$
```

where $\lambda_i$ is the factor loading for item i, and $	heta_{ii}$ is the residual variance for item i. More colloquially, this index of reliability can be seen as assessing the amount of variance across items attributable to the latent variable divided by the total variance. In contrast to Cronbach's alpha, omega total does not constrain the contribution of each item to be equal (hence $\lambda_i$, not $\lambda$). In our 12 Grit items, we get an estimate of 0.84 (95% CI = 0.83, 0.86).

One of the limitations of omega total, and Cronbach's alpha, is their assumption that one-factor model has reasonable fit. Extensions of these methods for alternative factor structures are beyond the scope of this chapter. Interested readers should consult Reise, Bonifay, and Haviland (2013).
