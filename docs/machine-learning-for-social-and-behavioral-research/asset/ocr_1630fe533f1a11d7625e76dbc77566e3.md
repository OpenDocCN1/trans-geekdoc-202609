# 12.6.7 Latent Space Model

Another class of network models is constructed around latent attributes rather than the observed attributes in ERGM (Snijders, 2011). One such model is the latent Euclidean space model proposed by Hoff, Raftery, and Handcock(2002). In the model, each node is assumed to be associated with a latent position in a Euclidean space. The distance of two nodes in the latent space is related to how likely they are connected in the network. $z$ be a vector of latent positions of node $i$ in the latent space. For nodes $i$ and $j$, the Euclidean distance between them is:

```
$d(i, j) = \sqrt{(z_i - z_j)(z_i - z_j)} = \sqrt{\sum_{d=1}^{D}(z_{id} - z_{jd})^2} \quad (12.7)$
```

where the symbol $(\cdot)$ is the transpose of the matrix or vector, $D$ is the dimension of the Euclidean space, $z_i = (z_{i1}, z_{i2}, \dots, z_{iD})$ and $z_j = (z_{j1}, z_{j2}, \dots, z_{jD})$ are the latent positions of nodes $i$ and $j$, respectively. With this distance function, the latent space model can be written as

```
$$\begin{cases} a_{ij} & \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p_{ij}) \ \text{logit}(p_{ij}) &= \alpha + \beta h'_{ij} - d_{ij} \end{cases} \quad (12.8)$
```

where $\alpha$ is an intercept, $h_{ij}$ is a vector of covariates and $eta$ is the associated coefficients of the covariates. The covariates can be related to the nodes/or edges of the network. The coefficient for $d_{ij}$ is fixed as $-1$. Therefore, the closer two nodes are in the latent space, the higher the probability for them to be related after controlling the covariates in the model.

To fit the latent space model, we need to find the dimension of the latent space. One way to determine it is to compare models with different latent dimensions based on BIC. For example, for the WeChat network data, we fit the model with $D=1, \dots, 9$:

```
$$\begin{cases} a_{ij} & \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p_{ij}) \ \text{logit}(p_{ij}) &= \alpha - \sqrt{\sum_{d=1}^{D}(z_{id} - z_{jd})^2} \end{cases} \quad (12.9)$
```
