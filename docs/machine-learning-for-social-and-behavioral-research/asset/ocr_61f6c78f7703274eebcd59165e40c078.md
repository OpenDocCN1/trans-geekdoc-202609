An important component to both descriptive and predictive modeling is
that each type of modeling can support the other. For instance, researchers
are more likely to trust an algorithm that they can interpret, regardless
of the predictive performance (Pearl, 2019). Furthermore, while understanding
individual relationships can be directly tied to theory, assessing predictive
performance on unseen data can add incremental information on the magnitude of effect that is likely to be seen in future research. Lastly, as
extremely complex results are difficult to assess, or simply results
also have drawbacks. For instance, we can see this in research regarding
decision trees, one of the most used machine learning algorithms that also
 the basis for more complex algorithms such as random forests. Although small trees may be highly interpretable, they may not be trusted
due their overly simplistic interpretation of the functional relationship
(Freitas, 2014).
