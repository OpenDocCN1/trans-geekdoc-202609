Additionally, instead of attempting to cover machine learning comprehensively, we take a narrower focus in primarily detailing regularization, decision trees, and ensembles of decision trees. We describe each of these in Chapters 4 through 6, and discuss how these algorithms have been combined with latent variable models in Chapters 8 through 10. By focusing on these algorithms, we omit other commonly used families of algorithms, most notably vector machines and deep learning. Support vector machines have a long history of being applied in neuroimaging data, whereas deep learning has become the state-of-the-art for image recognition and natural language processing. While we expect features of this book to include deep learning, we opted to omit both deep learning and support vector machines due to their lack of interpretation and absence in being integrated with latent variable models. Readers are referred to James et al. (2013) for an accessible introduction to support vector machines and Cholelet and Allaire (2018) for an introduction to deep learning. One may question why we primarily detail algorithms that have not changed, to a large extent, since the 1980s, but to put it simply, little progress has been made in prediction performance in more standard datasets (e.g., Hand, 2006). Similar things could be said in the context of variable selection (cf. Hastie, Tibshirani, and Tibshirani, 2020).

# 1.3.1 Prerequisites for the Book

Our goal in writing this book was to provide a background for understanding published machine learning papers or reference for researchers to publish journal articles using machine learning. With this goal, we wrote the book to be used as a standalone reference or to be used as part of a graduate machine learning course. Thus, the first part of the book requires readers to have at least a graduate level course or be familiar with that level of material. Chapter 4 specifically covers regression but does not provide a background on linear regression and only a small detail regarding the use of regression for alternative outcome types. Chapters 7 through 10 focus heavily on the use of latent variables. Chapter 7 provides a background on the use of factor analysis, the foundational technique behind the methods described in these chapters, but it is relatively brief in nature.
