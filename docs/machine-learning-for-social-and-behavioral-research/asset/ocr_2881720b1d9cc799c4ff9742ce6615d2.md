The regression lines have different intercepts leading to different predicted mathematics scores when externalizing behaviors equal zero (i.e., $eta_{0j} + u_{0j}$), and different slopes for the association between externalizing behaviors and mathematics achievement in each school (i.e., $eta_{1j} + u_{ij}$). The model in Equation 9.2 could be extended to include additional student-level variables (e.g., socioeconomic status) as well as school-level variables (e.g., school size). The school-level variables could affect the intercept (mathematics achievement when externalizing behavior equals zero) as well as the strength of the association (slope) between externalizing behavior (or any other cluster-level variable) and mathematics achievement.

In the social and behavioral sciences, the mixed-effects model in Equation 9.2 is often written with separate equations for the observation-level (level-1) and cluster-level (level-2) models. That is, the observation-level model can be written as

```
$math_{ij} = b_{0j} + b_{1j} \cdot ext_{ij} + e_{ij} \quad (9.3)$
```

where $b_{0j}$ is the random intercept or predicted math score for school $j$ / when $ext_{ij}=0$, $b_{1j}$ is the random slope and predicted rate of change in $math_{ij}$ for a one-unit change in $ext_{ij}$ in school $j$, and $e_{ij}$ is the observation-level residual.
