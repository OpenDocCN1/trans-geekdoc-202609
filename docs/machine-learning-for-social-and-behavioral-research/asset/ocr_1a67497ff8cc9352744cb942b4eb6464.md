# 4.5.4 Group Regularization

Sometimes in social and behavioral research our predictors are not completely independent from one another. Instead, there are groups of variables, for example, five items purported to measure one factor of personality, a set of demographic variables, In this case, we may not want to select individual predictors, but instead select groups of predictors. The use of the group lasso could be portrayed as a less theoretical counterpart to hierarchical regression. This refers to hierarchical entry of single or sets of predictors. This is distinguished from what has been termed hierarchical, multilevel, or mixed effects models. This later form of modeling is about the inclusion of random, opposed to solely fixed, effects to account for variability by clustering or time. Hierarchical regression is a more theoretical form of modeling where predictors are entered based on a prior specified sequence. For a scenario where the numbers of variables preclude strong theoretical specification, Yuan and Lin (2007) proposed the group lasso

```
$grplasso = \arg\min_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{P} x_{ij}\beta_j)^2 + \Lambda \sum_{g=1}^{G} \sqrt{p_g}|\beta_g||_2,\quad(4.9)$
```

where $p_g$ accounts for the varying sizes of groups and $\beta_g$ is the vector of all $\beta$ coefficients in group $g$. In the group lasso, the lasso penalty penalts at the group level, instead of individual parameters being set to 0, entire groups of parameters can be pushed to 0. This does not lead to sparsity within group, across groups of predictors. An alternative form of group regularization is for scenarios where researchers are not interested in selecting groups, but also selecting variables within those groups. This is termed bi-level variable selection (Breheny & Huang, 2009). When they wish to penalize at the group level, researchers have the option to use the group lasso, with some sparse extensions (Huang, Breheny, & Ma, 2012). One, the sparse group lasso (srplasso; Friedman, Hastie, & Tibshirani, 2010), has the same group lasso as in Equation 4.5, but adds a penalty for each individual predictor,

```
$sgrplasso = \arg\min_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{P} x_{ij}\beta_j)^2 + \lambda_1 \sum_{g=1}^{G} \sqrt{p_g}|\beta_g||_2 + \lambda_2|\beta_j||_1,\quad(4.10)$
```

where $\lambda_1$ represents the group penalization and $\lambda_2$ is for the individual $\beta_j$ coefficients. This model produces sparsity at two levels, ideal for incorporating group information into variable selection.
