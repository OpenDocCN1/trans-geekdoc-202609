# 10 Searching for Groups

Researchers are often interested in assessing heterogeneity with respect to a model of interest. For instance, one extension of finite mixture models is growth mixture models, where the estimation of latent classes is performed with respect to parameters in a latent growth curve model. Finite mixture models have been extended in this way to a number of additional models (discussed below). A related approach assesses the presence of groups with respect to a model of interest is structural equation model (SEM) (Freimer et al., 2013). In this, groups are derived through the use of decision trees and with respect to any type of SEM. Whereas mixture models estimate latent classes, SEM trees extract groups based on values of covariate variables. For this chapter, we discuss the use of both mixture models and SEM trees, as we see these two algorithms as the most relevant methods for identifying heterogeneity in social and behavioral research. We accomplish this through highlighting their similarities and distinctions, concluding with a discussion of when either algorithm may be appropriate.

# 10.1 Key Terminology

+   - Mixture model. A probabilistic model that identifies population heterogeneity through the use of latent classes.
- Structural equation model trees. An algorithm that pairs decision trees with structural equation models to identify subgroups among covariates of interest.
- Cluster analysis. An algorithm that identifies deterministic clusters of individuals in the sample of interest.
- Trees. The use of decision trees as a method for splitting the sample into distinct subgroups. See Chapter 5 for an overview of decision trees with a single outcome variable.
