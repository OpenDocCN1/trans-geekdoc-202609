# Introduction to Social Network Analysis

# 12.7 Summary

In this chapter, we introduced the basic methods for network analysis. Network data are different from the traditional data in that the nodes are naturally interrelated. Network visualization is a critical component, and should be the first step in network analysis. Although one can extract the information from a network and use it in a statistical model, many network modeling methods are available. For example, the exponential random graph models and latent space models are currently widely used.

# 12.7.1 Further Reading

+   - This chapter provides only a limited sample of the literature on social network analysis. For a more complete treatment of network analysis, one can consult the review paper by Snijders (2011) and books as Carrington, Scott, and Wasserman (2005); Scott (2017); Wasserman and Faust (1994). Many newly developed methods, such as network analysis with mediation Che et al. (2020); Liu et al. (2018) were not discussed in this chapter.
- The analysis in this chapter has focused on a binary, undirected social network. However, the methods can be easily adapted for valued, directed networks. Furthermore, network analysis is not limited to social networks, but can be and extended to many other networks such as psychological networks (Fried et al., 2017) and biological networks (Barabási, Gulbache, & Loscalco, 2011).
- The chapter only discussed cross-sectional networks. Longitudinal networks and associated analytical methods are available such as the temporal ERGM (Hanneke & Xing, 2007; Krivitsky & Handock, 2014), the stochastic actor-oriented models (Snijders, van Bunt, & Steglich, 2010), and the dynamic network analysis (Sewell & Chen, 2015)
