# 3.7.1 Sampling

A number of strategies have been developed to sample from either the positive or negative class, or both, to create new distributions that are more balanced. The simplest are random oversampling (ROS) and random undersampling (RUS). While ROS randomly samples from the minority case to produce an equal distribution of positive and negative cases, RUS randomly removes majority cases to produce an equal distribution. A more sophisticated approach that has a large degree of study is the synthetic oversampling technique (SMOTE; Chawla, Bowyer, Hall, & Kegelmeyer, 2002). SMOTE is similar to ROS, but instead of duplicating positive cases, SMOTE creates artificial minority cases by using the k-nearest neighbors for a given case. Specifically, for a particular case i, the SMOTE algorithm finds k similar minority cases to and generates synthetic cases with predictor values that represent a blend of the k-nearest neighbors.

Among the simple sampling strategies, ROS is preferred over RUS (e.g., Batista, Prati, & Mauro, 2004; Ba, Maki, & Mazurowski, 2018). Further, SMOTE has been generalized into a large number of variants, while additional strategies have also been developed to generate synthetic cases. A recent study of these strategies found that any form of oversampling performs better than no sampling, with the best performance attributed to more recent generalizations of SMOTE. The majority of the studies that have evaluated sampling methods have used benchmark datasets from computer science, making the generalizability of the findings to social and behavioral research questionable. Two recent papers focused on evaluating these strategies in simulated data that are in line with social and behavioral research (Goerbergh et al., 2022; Jacobucci & Li, 2022). While Goerbergh et al. (2022) found detrimental effects of sampling strategies with respect to calibration (defined below) and did not lead to better AUC values than no sampling. Jacobucci and Li (2022) found only slight benefits to the use of sampling strategies for both the AUC and AUPRC, and of the sampling strategies, ROS performed as well if not better across conditions than SMOTE. Note in Jacobucci and Li (2022) is that they also tested the random over sampling examples (ROS; Menardi & Torelli, 2014) which is implemented in the caret. package performs along with SMOTE. Notably, that ROS performs poorly in some conditions and is not a recommended approach.
