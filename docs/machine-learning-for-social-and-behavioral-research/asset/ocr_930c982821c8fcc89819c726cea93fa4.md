# 11.7.2 Sentiment Analysis with Valence Shifters

The previous sentiment analysis was based on individual words or phrases. One first looks up the words in a chosen lexicon and then aggregates the scores for the words together. For example, if a word "helpful" in a lexicon such as being helpful appears in the text, a score of 1 is added to the sentiment score of the text. This method is simple, but preven effective in the preceding example. However, it ignores the difference in situations such as "isn't helpful," "barely helpful," and "extremely helpful." In such cases, although the word "helpful" is positive, the preceding word "isn't" is called negator. A word like "isn't" that modifies the sign of a word is called negative. The words "very" and "extremely," on the other hand, increase the positivity of the word "helpful." Such words are called amplifiers or intensifiers. The word "barely" does not flip the meaning of "helpful" but reduces its magnitude of positivity. Words like it are de-amplifier or attenuator.

Another condition is a bit more complex as the comment here—Chemistry is actually interesting, and it's frustrating," Simply looking at the individual words, "easy" is positive, and "frustrating" is negative. But, with the negator, "not easy" becomes negative. However, with the adversative conjunction "but" in the next sentence, the previous negative sentiment should not be considered as negative more.

The above-mentioned types of words, including negator, amplifier, de-amplifier, and adversative conjunction, are collectively called valence shifters, which can alter or intensify the sentiment of words under evaluation. Valence shifters can be a serious problem. For example, for the teaching comments, about 20% of the time a negator appears with the sentiment word using the being lexicon (see Table 11.11). Therefore, to capture the sentiment of a text, such valence shifters should be taken into consideration.
