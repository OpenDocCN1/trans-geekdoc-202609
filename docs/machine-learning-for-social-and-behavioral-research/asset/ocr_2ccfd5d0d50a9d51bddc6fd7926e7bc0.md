The 5-class sample model had a higher BIC than the 3-class and 4-class models suggesting that 5 classes were too many for these data.

In the 3-class model, approximately 50% of the sample was in class 1 with 20% in class 2 and 30% in class 3. The class-specific parameter estimates were translated to the predicted probability of responding in each response category for each class. These probabilities are contained in Table 10.2. Given these predicted probabilities, we can begin to determine the nature of the three classes. Class 1 is the largest, this class had the highest mean response on all items, except item 11, which asked about the frequency of having an interest in new pursuits. Class 2 tended to have low mean responses to interests and projects changing from year to year. Thus, Class 2 was more easily distracted and participants' level of commitment was more average. Class 3 had participants who responded in the low categories to questions about diligence and overcoming setbacks; this class tended to have participants whose interests changed less than Class 2.
