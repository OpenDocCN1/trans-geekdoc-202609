# 10.2.3 Factor Mixture Models

In identifying heterogeneity, researchers often have outcome variables of interest that they wish to use as a basis for examination. When these observed outcome variables come from the same or similar scales, or are the same variables assessed at different points in time, it often makes theoretical sense to place constraints on their covariance in the form of latent factors. One of the most general formulations is factor mixture models (Lubke & Muthén, 2005), whereas classes are estimated with respect to some factor model. This model represents a combination of latent variable types, a categorical latent variable used as the basis for grouping, and a continuous latent variable that summarizes the covariance among observed outcome variables. Thus, within each of the classes, a common factor model is estimated with a subset of parameters allowed to various classes.
