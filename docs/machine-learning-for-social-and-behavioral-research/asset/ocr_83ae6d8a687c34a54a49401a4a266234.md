# 2.2 Overview

Increases in the application of machine learning have occurred simultaneously with an elevated emphasis on the use of exploratory research, best encapsulated in the term of data mining. Most conceptualizations eschew the use of hypotheses in favor of predictive modeling, or mining to see what golden nuggets are yet to be discovered. However, there is a concurrent rise in the re-emphasis of confirmatory modeling, mainly by the result of the rise in research that has not been able to reproduce findings in social and behavioral research, termed the replication crisis (e.g., Maxwell, Lau, & Howard, 2015). Indexed, questions regarding replication have spanned disciplines, along with an extension from experimental research to the application of highly complex models (e.g., Haibe-Kains et al., 2020). Therefore, the fundamental question that a book advocating for the use of machine learning has to answer is how we reconcile a advocacy for both exploratory and confirmatory research? To address this, we first define and distinguish the concepts of confirmation and exploration, concluding with our perspective on how machine learning is paired with theory and fits into the current state of science.

The use of machine learning in social and behavioral research is less a movement to replace traditional methods of analysis, and more a necessary addition given the increases in the size and kind of available data. The rationale for machine learning goes beyond being a necessity and also reflects a change in mindset. In the past, scientific culture valued theoretical simplicity, which may be captured in: ‘just’ as the ability to devise simple but evocative models is the signature of the greatest scientist overaboration and overparameterization is the mark of mediocrity.(Box, 1976, p. 176). In contrast, most recent concepts propose that truly understanding various phenomena require infinite degrees of complexity (Lin, Tegmark & Rolnick, 2017), which may be best encapsulated by the use of black algorithms, where complexity is strongly favored over parsimony (e.g., Holm, 2019). These competing notions set up a fundamental tension between old and new, parsimony versus complexity, linear models versus black algorithms. However, we do not see it in terms of a dichotomy.
