### 9.2.2 The backward process in the DDPM

In the physical world, it is easier to mix two materials than to separate them once mixed. It is the same in the digital world for a DDPM. The forward process is much easier than the backward process. The reverse or backward process is another Markov chain starting from a pure noise `x[T] ∼ p[θ](x[T]) = N(x[T]; 0, I)` and the process is going backwards to get denoised latent images step by step: `x[T−1]…, x[t], x[t−1], …, x[0]`, where `θ` represents parameters of a `U-Net`. Figure [9.4](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-6244-3ch9fig4)(C) shows the results of a backward process from a pure noise at `t = 199`. At time step `t−1`, the latent image `x[t−1]` has a PDF `p[θ](x[t−1]∣x[t]) = N(x[t−1]; μ[θ](x[t], t), β[t]I)`, where `μ[θ](x[t], t)` coming from the trained `U-Net` model.

The backward PDF `p[θ](x[t−1]∣x[t])` corresponds to a reverse of the forward PDF, and it is equal to `q(x[t−1]∣x[t], x[0])`, which is referred to as ‘forward process posterior distribution.’ Detailed derivations can be found in the DDPM paper (Ho *et al* [2020](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-6244-3ch9bib1)). The following equations ([9.3](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-6244-3ch9eqn3)) and ([9.4](#Chapter_9.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-6244-3ch9eqn4)) come from appendix [I](#Appendix1.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-6244-3AppAsA-I) for completeness

$$q(x_{t - 1}\mid x_{t},x_{0}) = \mathbb{N}(x_{t - 1};\widetilde{\mu}(x_{t},x_{0}),{\widetilde{\beta}}_{t}I) = q(x_{t}\mid x_{t - 1},x_{0})\frac{q(x_{t - 1}\mid x_{0})}{q(x_{t}\mid x_{0})}$$

(9.4)

$$\widetilde{\mu}\left( {x_{t},x_{0}} \right) = x_{t} - \frac{1 - \alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{1 - {\overline{\alpha}}_{t}}}\varepsilon$$

(9.5)

$${\widetilde{\beta}}_{t} = \frac{1 - {\overline{\alpha}}_{t - 1}}{1 - {\overline{\alpha}}_{t}}\beta_{t}$$

(9.6)

The above formulae will be used for variational lower bound loss of model training. The joint probability distribution of the backward process is:

$$p_{\theta}\left( x_{0:T} \right) = p_{\theta}\left( x_{T} \right)\prod\limits_{t = 1}^{T}{p_{\theta}\left( {x_{t - 1}\mid x_{t}} \right)} = p_{\theta}\left( {x_{0}\mid x_{1}} \right)p_{\theta}\left( {x_{1}\mid x_{2}} \right)\ldots p_{\theta}\left( {x_{T - 1}\mid x_{T}} \right)p_{\theta}\left( x_{T} \right)$$

(9.7)

From the joint possibility distribution and `∫q(x[0])dx[0] = 1`, we have the negative log likelihood (loss function) of the system for model training: `L = −log (p[θ](x[0])) = −log ∫p[θ](x[0:T])dx[1:T]`

$$L = - \left( {\int q\left( x_{0} \right)dx_{0}} \right){\ \log\ }\int{q\left( {x_{1:T}\mid x_{0}} \right)}\frac{p_{\theta}\left( x_{0:T} \right)}{q\left( {x_{1:T}\mid x_{0}} \right)}dx_{1:T}$$

(9.8)

Using Jensen's inequality, we can simplify the above formula to get the variational lower bound loss of the system (appendix [I](#Appendix1.xhtml#bk978-0-7503-6244-3AppAsA-I))

$$L < - \int q\left( x_{0} \right)dx_{0}\int q\left( {x_{1:T}\mid x_{0}} \right){\ \log\ }\frac{p_{\theta}\left( x_{0:T} \right)}{q\left( {x_{1:T}\mid x_{0}} \right)}dx_{1:T} = L_{VLB}$$
