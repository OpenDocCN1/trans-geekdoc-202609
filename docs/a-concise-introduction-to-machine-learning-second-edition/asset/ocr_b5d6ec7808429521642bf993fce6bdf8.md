In the following sections, we will use different techniques of maximizing with respect to the parameters. A note of caution is however appropriate at this point, and this is in relation to outliers. Assume that $K=2$ and all data samples are roughly grouped together apart one outlier. In the maximization process $p_1$ will tend to $(N-1)$ while $p_2$ will tend to $1/N$, $p_1(v)$ will roughly degenerate the distribution of $N-1$ samples. The likelihood can be increased again and again by concentrating the probability mass of $p_1(v)$ more and tightly around the outlier. Hence, the likelihood of $p_2(v)$ at the outlier will tend to infinity, where the region where $p_2(v)$ is nonzero becomes smaller and its area tends to zero. In this leads to the likelihood tending to infinity. Heuristics are used to identify such degenerate cases. For example, such outliers could be identified and removed from the dataset during the maximization procedure. Or $K$ reduced such that it is not possible for outliers to assign their own cluster.

Something else to be aware of that any solution has $K!$ equivalent solutions. These arise by just labelling clusters by permutations of $1,\dots,K$. This equivalence is known as *identifiability*, and is important when comparing and interpreting models. In this context, however, this is of no importance, since just one of the equivalent solutions needs to be found.
