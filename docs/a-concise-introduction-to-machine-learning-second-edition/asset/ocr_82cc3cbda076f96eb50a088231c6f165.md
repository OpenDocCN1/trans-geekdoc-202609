lower bound as

```
$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{n=1}^{N} q(z_{n,k}) \log \frac{p(\mathbf{v}_n, z_{n,k}=1)}{q(z_{n,k}=1)}$$
$$\= \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{n=1}^{N} q(z_{n,k}) \log (p(z_{n,k}=1)p(\mathbf{v}_n|z_{n,k}=1))$$$
$$\= - \sum_{k=1}^{K} q(z_{n}=1) \log q(z_n).$$
```

The lower bound has the logarithm of the data likelihood $\mathcal{L}$, if $q(z_n)$ is equal to $p(z_n|\mathbf{v}_n)$, then the KL divergence is zero.
The maximum of (6.10) is estimated by alternating between the lower bound with respect to $q(z_n)$ and with respect to $\pi_k = p(z_k=1)$ and the parameters of $p_k(\mathbf{v}) = p(z_k=1|\mathbf{v})$, $k=1, \dots, K$. The former means setting $q(z_n)$ to $p(z_n|\mathbf{v}_n)$, where the responsibilities are evaluated using the current parameters of $p_k(\mathbf{v})$. With this choice for $q(z_n)$, however, the first term of the lower bound becomes $\mathcal{L}$. The second term is independent of the parameters of $p_k(\mathbf{v})$ and hence irrelevant for the maximization. The location of the maximum is the same whether maximizing $\mathcal{L}$ or $	ilde{\mathcal{L}}$.
