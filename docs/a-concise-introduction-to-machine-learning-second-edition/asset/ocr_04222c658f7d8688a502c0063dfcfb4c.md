Let $i$ be any index in $\{1, \dots, K\}$. We incorporate the first constraint noting

$$$\mu_i = 1 - \sum_{k \neq i} \mu_k.$$

With this modification, the log likelihood is

$$$\log p(D|\mu) = \sum_{k} m_k \log \mu_k + m_j \log \mu_j \left( 1 - \sum_{k \neq j} \mu_k \right).$$

Let $j \neq i$ be any other index. The parameter $\mu_j$ appears in both sums, and the derivative with respect to $\mu_j$ is

$$$\frac{d \log p(D|\mu)}{d \mu_j} = \frac{m_j}{\mu_j} - \frac{m_i}{1 - \sum_{k \neq j} \mu_k} \frac{\mu_i}{\mu_j}.$$

Setting this to zero, we find that the ratio of $\mu_j$ to $\mu_i$ to be the same as the ratio of $m_j$ to $m_i$. Since $i$ and $j$ are freely chosen, the ratio of any pair of components of $\mu$ must be the ratio of the corresponding pair in $\mathbf{m} = (m_1, \dots, m_K)$. The only possible choice for $\mu_k$, which satisfies the second constraint that to lie between zero and one, is the MLE.

$$$\mu_k = \frac{m_k}{m_1 + \dots + m_K}, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

The vector $\mu$ of the probabilities of different outcomes needs to be estimated. The Dirichlet distribution $\text{Dir}(\mu|\alpha)$ is chosen as conjugate prior for $\mu$, where $\alpha$ is suitably chosen. The posterior density function is proportional to the product of the likelihood and the prior (which is the joint distribution):

$$$f(\mu|\alpha) \propto \prod_{k=1}^K \mu_k^{\alpha_k - 1},$$
