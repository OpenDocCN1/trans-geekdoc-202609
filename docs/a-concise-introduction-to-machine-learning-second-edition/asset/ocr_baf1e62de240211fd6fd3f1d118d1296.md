It is the percentage of time a negative sample is registered as positive. In this case the patient worries needlessly. However, this is acceptable in some cases as long as subsequent tests avoid needless procedures.

A perfect classifier would be 100% sensitive (all positives are correctly identified) and 100% specific (no negatives are incorrectly classified). In a cascade we all stages have high sensitivity so that as many possible patients with a health problem are detected. High specificity is desirable so that patients are not subjected to costly (both financial and human cost) procedures. However, in early stages a lower specificity is acceptable. Samples classified as negative at a stage are not considered in the following stages, thus decreasing the overall cost.
