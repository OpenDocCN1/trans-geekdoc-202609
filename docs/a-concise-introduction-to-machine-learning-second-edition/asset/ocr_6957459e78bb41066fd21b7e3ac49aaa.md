# # Regression

In regression, we try to find the relationship between variables. Regression is related to curve fitting, interpolation, and data prediction. As an example, we consider colour vision. Spectral colours, as seen in Figure 8.1, are evoked by a single wavelength. Colours change continuously as the wavelength changes. A physical colour is a combination of pure spectral colours. Hence there are infinitely many possibilities.

![](bbox=[0.235, 0.477, 0.766, 0.523])

Figure 8.1: Spectral colours.

Around 1854, James Clerk Maxwell Campbell & Garnett (2010) showed that all colours can be mixed by different coinations of the three primary colours, red, green and blue. He did so by using a colour-mixing top which can be seen in Figure 8.2 consisting of three paper discs overlapping by different amounts. A smaller disc contained a wedge of the sample. The brightness could be adjusted by the width of the wedge. When the top spin, the other colours would mix and one could adjust how each colour contributed until it matched the colour in the centre.

There is a biological reason for this. The human eye has only three measuring devices to perceive colour the cones. Figure 8.3 shows to which wavelengths different type of cones are sensitive. The blue peak is well separated from the red and green peaks. It is believed that the separation is evolutionarily new and that the curves will move further apart with time. Colour blindness is in most cases due to one of cone missing or malfunctioning. With three measurements, the human eye can distinguish ten million colours. This is an example of regression, shows how powerful our brain is. From only three measurements our brain is able to infer millions of colours.
