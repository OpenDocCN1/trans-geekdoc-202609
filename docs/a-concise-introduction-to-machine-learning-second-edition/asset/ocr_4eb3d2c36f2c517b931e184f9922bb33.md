where $|\cdot|$ in this case denotes the modulus of the determinant of the Jacobian matrix $J_{g^{-1}}(y)$ of $g^{-1}$ evaluated at $y$. Generally, if $f(\mathbf{x})$ is a function from $\mathbb{R}^b$ to $\mathbb{R}^a$, then the Jacobian matrix $J_f(\mathbf{x})$ is the $a 	imes b$ matrix containing the partial derivatives of $\mathbf{f}$.

```
$$$J_f(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_1} & \dots & \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_b} \ \vdots & & \vdots \ \frac{\partial f_a}{\partial x_1} & \dots & \frac{\partial f_a}{\partial x_b} \end{pmatrix}.$$
```

If $a = b$, the determinant of this matrix is called the Jacobian determinant. Confusingly, both matrix and determinant, are often just referred to as the Jacobian. The matrix can be calculated for any $a$ and $b$, while the determinant is only defined for $a = b$.
