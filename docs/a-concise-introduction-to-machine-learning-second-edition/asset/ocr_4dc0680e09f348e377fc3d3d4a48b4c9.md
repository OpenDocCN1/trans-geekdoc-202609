We combine Equations (2.5) and (2.6) to arrive at

$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-frac{x_1^2+x_2^2}{2}\right)dx_1dx_2=\int_{0}^{2\pi}r\exp\left(-frac{r^2}{2}\right)drd\theta$

$x_1$ and $x_2$ are standard normal random variables and $x=r\cos\theta$. From the above we see that $\theta$ follows the uniform distribution on the interval $(0,2\pi)$ which is the circumference of the unit circle. On the other hand, $r$ has the probability density function $r\exp(-r^2/2)$ on the $(0,\infty)$. To generate a random variable from uniform random, we use inverse transform sampling. First, we calculate

$F(r)=\int_{0}^{r}t\exp\left(-frac{t^2}{2}\right)dt=\left[-\exp\left(-frac{t^2}{2}\right)\right]_{0}^{r}=1-\exp\left(-frac{r^2}{2}\right)$.

Let $q$ be a random variable drawn from the uniform distribution on $(0,1)$. Setting $F(r)=q$ and solving for $r$, gives $r=\sqrt{-2\log(1-q)}$. Since $1-q$ is also uniformly distributed on $(0,1)$, it can be rewritten as

$r=\sqrt{-2\log q}. (3.2)

Summarizing, we generate random variables $x_1$ and $x_2$ drawn from the standard normal distribution by drawing two variables $y_1$ and $y_2$ from the uniform distribution on $(0,1)$ and letting

$x_1=\sqrt{-2\log y_1}\cos(2\pi y_2)\nx_2=\sqrt{-2\log y_1}\sin(2\pi y_2).

This is the *basic* form of the *Box-Muller transform*.

To avoid the sine and cosine, we note that $\cos\theta=x_1/r$ and $\sin\theta=x_2/r$.
