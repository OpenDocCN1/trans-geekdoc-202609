However, it is influenced by the initial proportion of balls. If initially there are only balls of one colour, the outcome is determined, then the urn will continue to contain only balls of colour. If there are disproportionately more balls of colour, we would expect there more balls of colour in the final set, since it is less likely that this colour is drawn and it becomes even more likely, since a ball of that colour is added. This is known as get-rich-richer, which is expected. Other proportions are also possible, just less likely. In $N$ approach infinity, the possibilities for the vector $x$ of proportions follow a Dirichlet distribution $Dir(\alphaalpha)$. This might seem counter-intuitive. Imagine there is initially only one black and one white ball, $K=2$ and $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=1$. Then it is equally likely that the urn contains exactly the same number of black and white balls in the end, containing only white balls apart from the initial black ball.
