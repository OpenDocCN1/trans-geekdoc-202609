This is known as the *product rule* of probability. Written in terms of the random variables, the *sum* and *product rule* of probability are

```
$p(X) = \sum_{Y} p(X, Y) = p(Y|X)p(X),\n$$
```

where the sum is all possible instances of $Y$.

From the definition of independence in (2.1), we see that $Y$ is independent of $X$, if the conditional probability $p(Y|X)$ equals the marginal probability $p(Y)$ for all outcomes. Then, the outcome of $X$ does not influence the outcome of $Y$ at all.

The joint probability of random variables is symmetric.

```
$p(X, Y) = p(Y, X).
```

Combining with the product rule gives $p(Y|X)p(X) = p(X|Y)p(Y)$. Dividing by $p(X)$ leads to Bayes' *rule*, also known as *Bayes' theorem* which relates conditional probabilities of random variables to each other:

```
$p(Y|X) = \frac{p(X|Y)p(Y)}{p(X)}$
```

Bayes' rule plays an important role within Machine Learning, sparking Bayesian subscribers. A very good account of its historic influence is given by McGraine (2011).
