$$f_y(y) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{y}} \exp(-y/2)$

This is the chi-square$(d)$ distribution, also denoted as $\chi^2$-distribution, with one degree of freedom. More generally, the probability density function of the $\chi^2$-distribution with $k$ degrees of freedom is given

$$\chi^2(x) = \frac{1}{2\Gamma(k/2)} x^{k/2-1} \exp(-x/2)$

The mean lies at $k$ and the variance is $k^2$. The mode lies at max$(k-2, 0)$. The previous result for $k=1$ follows from $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$. Figures 2.24 and 2.25 give examples of probability density function and cumulative distribution function for various degrees of freedom.
