the iteration $\mathcal{L}$ and $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$ have the same value. For two of these choices $q(z_n)$, the lower bound $\underline{\mathcal{L}}$ is drawn in blue. Its curve touches the curve of $\mathcal{L}$ at the diamond. This signifies a maximization with respect to $q(z_n)$. In the next iteration, a new set of $\pi_k = p(z_k=1)$ and the parameters of $p_k(v) = p(v|z_k)=1$, $k=1, \dots, K$ is chosen by finding the maximum of the logarithm of $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$ which coincides with the expectation of the logarithm of the complete data likelihood and drawn in red. Note that the maxima of both $\mathcal{L}$ and $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$ are the same, indicated by the vertical lines, with a maximization with respect to $\pi_k$ and the parameters of $p_k(v)$, $k=1, \dots, K$, take place.

The following idea leads us to maximize the algorithm of the data likelihood:

+   1. Initialize all parameters of $p_k(v) = p(v|z_k=1)$ and mixing coefficients $\pi_k(z_k=1)$.
2. Evaluate the responsibilities $p(z_{n,k} = 1|v_n)$ for $n=1, \dots, N$ and $k=1, \dots, K$.
3. Maximize:

```
$\sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{n=1}^N p(z_{n,k}=1|v_n) \log(p(z_{n,k}=1)p(v|z_{n,k}=1))$
```

with respect to the parameters of $p_k(v) = p(v|z_k=1)$ and mixing coefficients $\pi_k = p(z_k=1)$.

+   5. Terminate, if all changes are before the convergence threshold. Otherwise return step 3

This method is known as the *Expectation-Maximization* algorithm or EM for short. Each step increases the logarithm of the data likelihood. Listing 6.3 uses the internal implementation of the EM algorithm in MATLAB-LAB to fit a Gaussian mixture. We revisit the Expectation-Maximization technique in the context of regression where we consider latent variables. The EM algorithm is widely used and has many extensions. McLachlan & Krishna (2007) gives an overview of this.
