For $\alpha_1 = \dots = \alpha_K$ the distribution is known as *symmetric Dirichlet distribution* or *flat Dirichlet distribution*. The latter naming is misleading, since it is only flat, when all concentration parameters take the value one. The Dirichlet distribution then becomes the uniform distribution over the simplex. In this case,

```
$$\text{Dir}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\Gamma(K)}{\Gamma(1)\cdots\Gamma(1)} \prod_{k=1}^K x_k^{0}=(K-1)!$
```

which is the inverse of the volume of the simplex. For $K=3$, the 2-simplex has volume 1/2.

We see in [Figure 2.22] that for $\alpha=(5,5)^T$, the probability mass is concentrated in the center, while for $\alpha=(5,2)^T$, it is concentrated towards the opposite edge of the corner with the smallest concentration parameter. For $\alpha=(5,2)^T$, the probability mass is concentrated at the corner with the largest concentration parameter.

When one of the concentration parameters $\alpha_i$ is less than one, then the probability density function will tend to infinity at the corresponding edge $x_k=0$. For [Figure 2.22], this is visible for $\alpha=(0,8,0,2)^T$, but hardly visible for $\alpha=(0,8,0,2)^T$.
