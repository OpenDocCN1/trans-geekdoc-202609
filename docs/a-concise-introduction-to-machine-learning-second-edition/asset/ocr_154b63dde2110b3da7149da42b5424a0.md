The samples for $\alpha_n \neq 0$ in the linear combination to form the weight vector $\mathbf{w}$ are called the support vectors. This shaped the name under which the technique is known. Vapnik & Lerner (1963) invented this in 1964 as the *Generalized Primal Method*. The term generalized portrait refers to the centre of a sphere which contains patterns belonging to a certain class, but no other class. That is, it is a representation of this class.

Recall that $\mathbf{w} = -b\mathbf{w}$ and $v = \sum_{n=1}^N \alpha_n c_n \mathbf{v}_n$. Inserting this into (4.4) and (4.5), the bias $b$ is given by

$\mathbf{w} = \sum_{n=1}^N \alpha_n c_n \mathbf{v}_n.$
