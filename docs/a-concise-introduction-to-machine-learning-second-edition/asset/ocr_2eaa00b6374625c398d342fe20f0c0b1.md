$z_1$ represents an OR which is equivalent to on the right of the thin line in Figure 5.16, while $z_2$ represents NAND, or equivalently to the left of the bold line. The output combines these two results with an AND, since both have to be high at the same time.

Using several sets of hidden neurons, and therefore several layers if necessary, any region can be described as a combination of on the right or left of several lines (or hyperplanes in dimensions). This makes neural networks particularly powerful for classification. However, there is also the chance that the network overlaps containing only a few samples. That is an indication that the network will not generalize well to unseen. Typically training runs are performed several times and each resulting network evaluated on a validation set. The one performing best on the validation is then chosen. The final network is assessed on a test set. In Chapter 7 we see how their performance is enhanced when continuous, non-linear activation functions are used.

The results of neural networks are sensitive to the choice of

+   - the number of layers,
- the connection pattern,
- the initialization of weights and
- the activation functions and their parameters.
