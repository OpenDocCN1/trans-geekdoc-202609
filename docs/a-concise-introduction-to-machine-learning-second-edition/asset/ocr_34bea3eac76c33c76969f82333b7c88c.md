is known as the *beta function*, giving the distribution its name. It is related to the inverse of the binomial coefficient. Indeed, if $\alpha = m$ and $eta = n$ are integers,

```
$\frac{1}{B(m,n)} = \frac{(m+n-1)!}{(m-1)!(n-1)!} = \frac{mn}{m} \frac{m+n}{m} = \frac{m}{m+n} \frac{n}{n}$
```

With this in, we see that the beta density function has a functional form similar to the probability density function of the binomial distribution in (2.7). The main difference is that in the binomial distribution the random variable is the number of successes $m$, while in the beta distribution the random variable is $x$. It takes place of the probability of success. Because of this, the beta distribution is often seen as a distribution over proportions. It gives the probability of certain probability $x$. We will see in Section 2.6 how we can use the functional similarity to our advantage.
