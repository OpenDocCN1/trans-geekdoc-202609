The Metropolis-Hastings algorithm extends the Metropolis method to use proposal distributions which are not symmetric. In this case, the acceptance probability of a candidate $\mathbf{x}^*$ is calculated

$$\min \left(1, \frac{f(\mathbf{x}^*)g(\mathbf{x}^*, \mathbf{x}_n)}{f(\mathbf{x}_n)g(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{x}^*)}\right) = \min \left(1, \frac{f(\mathbf{x}^*)g(\mathbf{x}^*, \mathbf{x}_n)}{f(\mathbf{x}_n)g(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{x}^*)}\right)$$

The transition probability from $\mathbf{x}$ to $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ is given by

$$T(\mathbf{x}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}}) = \min \left(1, \frac{f(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{x})}{f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}})}\right)$$

We can now check whether f satisfies detailed balance. To this end,

$$f(\mathbf{x})T(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \begin{aligned} &\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \min \left(1, \frac{\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')}{f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')}}\right) &=\frac{1}{c_f} \min(f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}'), \hat{f}(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')) \&=\frac{1}{c_f} \min(f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}'), \hat{f}(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')) \&=\frac{1}{c_{f'}} g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \min \left(1, \frac{f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')}{\hat{f}(\mathbf{x}', \mathbf{x}')}g(\mathbf{x}', \mathbf{x}')}}\right) = \hat{f}(\mathbf{x})T(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}').\end{aligned}$$

Thus detailed balance is satisfied as required to generate approximate samples of the distribution described by $f$.

The Metropolis-Hastings algorithm is an example of a class of algorithms known as Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC). In general, Monte Carlo methods rely on repeated sampling to perform calculations, where a closed solution is not possible or possibly expensive. They were first developed at the Los Alamos National Laboratory. Since the work was secret, Monte Carlo, referring to the Casino, became the name. Gibbs sampling is used when it is easier to sample from the conditional distribution than the joint distribution. The Metropolis-Hastings algorithm is a special case where the proposal distribution is symmetric.
