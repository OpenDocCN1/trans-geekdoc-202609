One idea could be to use the line through the mean of each class segment and the midpoint between the sample means as the separation threshold. The sample mean of class $C_1$ is

$$\mu_i = rac{1}{N} \sum_{v \in C_i} v, \quad i = 0, 1.$ \quad (4.1)$

The line through the mean is then parametrized by

$l(a) = \mu_0 + a(\mu_1 - \mu_0).$

For $a \in [0, 1]$, we define the line segment between the sample means and the midpoint is given when $a = 1/2$, hence $b = 1/2(\mu_0 + \mu_1)$.

This approach is illustrated in Figure 4.4 where both the projection line and the separation line perpendicular to the projection line through the midpoint sample means is shown. However, it fails to take into account the *variance* within each class, which is how and in what way the samples differ from the mean. In Figure 4.5, we have also drawn the 95% *confidence ellipse* and its *axes*. This means that 95% of the samples lie within the ellipses. The shorter axis is the direction of most variation among the samples. The long axis of the confidence ellipse of the iris setosa is nearly parallel to the separation line, meaning that the data will be bunched together when projected onto the line through the means. The axes of the confidence ellipse of the iris versicolor are roughly diagonal to the separation line, meaning the projected data is spread out.
