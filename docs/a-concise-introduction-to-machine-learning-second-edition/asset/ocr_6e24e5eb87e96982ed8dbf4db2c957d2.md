Just as we moved from the Bernoulli distribution of binary variables to the categorical distribution for experiments with multiple, mutually distinct outcomes, we extend the beta distribution to a distribution of $K$ probabilities $0 \le x_k \le 1, k=1, \dots, K$, which have to sum to one:

$$\sum_{k=1}^K x_k = 1$$

The probabilities are gathered in the random variable vector $\mathbf{x} = (x_1,\dots, x_K)^T$. Because the constraints $x$ can only lie in a subset of the space of vectors of length $K$. For $K=3$, this is illustrated in Figure 2.21. The generalization to higher dimensions is known as a *simplex*. For $K=3$ the 2-simplex is the triangle.

The distribution is known as the *Dirichlet distribution* for $\mathbf{x} \sim Dir(\\alpha)$ and the probability density function is given by

$$$Dir(\\alpha) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_1 + \dots + \alpha_K)}{\Gamma(\alpha_1)\dots\Gamma(\alpha_K)} \prod_{k=1}^K x_k^{\alpha_k-1}, \quad (2.15)$$

The parameters $\alpha = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_K)^T$ are known as *concentration parameters*. They are all positive. When $K=2$, the Dirichlet distribution is the beta distribution.
