# # I KNOW THAT I KNOW NOTHING (BONUS ARTICLE)

![](bbox=[0.316, 0.26, 0.683, 0.458])

(c. 470–399 BC)

When Plato's teacher Socrates lived in Athens, his childhood friend Chairephon went to Delphi to ask the Oracle who might be the wisest man in Athens. The Oracle called Socrates, and when he heard of it, he was shocked, astonished because he did not think that he was the smartest. But the God of the Oracle was not allowed to lie, so Socrates wanted to find out what the Oracle might have meant by his saying.

Socrates sought out in Athens those men who appeared as great teachers and were therefore considered as wise. He wanted to learn from them and show that he could not be the wisest because he knew less than the persons he addressed. But again and again, he had to find out that Athens' wise teachers became insecure when questioned in detail and finally had to admit to seeing their knowledge disappear through Socrates' thirst for knowledge.
