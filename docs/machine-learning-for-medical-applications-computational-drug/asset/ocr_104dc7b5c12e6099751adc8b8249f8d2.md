Anomaly Detection: Peculiarities and outliers are well captured by denoising and sparse autoencoders in drug safety data. These models can recognize patterns of toxicity while being able to understand normal patterns in the data.

Despite their advantages, autoencoders face several challenges:

Model Complexity: Autoencoders suffer from the problem of hyperparameter and network structure selection in many aspects. Overcomplicated models distort the data and do not work as well, whereas when a model is too simple, it omits some data characteristics.

Interpretability: The embeddings of the autoencoder models are opaque and thus make it complex to explain the features that are being used to predict toxicity. The absence of interpretability tools makes it important to develop methods for visualizing and analyzing latent representations.

Computational Resources: Deep and convolutional forms of autoencoder training indeed require a great deal of memory and computing power. These difficulties can be mitigated by the use of proper hardware acceleration as well as efficient training algorithms.

Autoencoders remain a prominent direction in the development of new methods within the framework of unsupervised learning, providing useful instruments for encoding and feature extraction, as well as anomaly detection at the present stage. They are also promising for finding latent relationships in drug toxicity prediction and enhancing the safety assessment of newly developed chemicals.
