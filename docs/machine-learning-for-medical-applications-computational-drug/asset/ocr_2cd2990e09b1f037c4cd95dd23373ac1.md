# 2 Deep learning techniques in biomedical materials

# 2.1 Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for material property prediction

Basic notions on CNNs are another kind of powerful DL computing method that focuses on analyzing structures such as data that come in grids, for instance, images. CNNs were designed to work with images, mostly for processing them, but have been adapted for many purposes, including material science. CNNs appear relevant in material property predictions due to their ability to learn feature representation and spatial hierarchy from the inputs. This makes it possible to simulate complex relations between material properties and the structures of those materials.

Convolutional, pooling, and fully connected layers are some of the building blocks of most CNN structures. In our case, pooling layers are used to down sample the input and retain the most relevant aspects. The convolutional layers, on the other hand, convolve the input data with kernels to identify local features. The final layers aggregate these features to make predictions. For material property prediction, which can be achieved through CNNs, a material’s microstructure, as captured in microscopy images, can be used to predict properties such as mechanical strength, thermal conductivity, and biocompatibility. This approach is particularly useful, thanks to CNNs ability to handle the extraction of hierarchical features from data of high and even extreme complexity.

A study of CNNs for material property prediction CNNs has proved worthy of being employed for the prediction of different properties of materials. For example, CNNs have been used for the prediction of the tensile strength of composite materials from the microstructure images of the same composite material. CNNs have also been applied to estimate thermal properties based on thermal image observations. These applications show that CNNs can extract useful predictions from images, which is very beneficial for material design and optimization.
