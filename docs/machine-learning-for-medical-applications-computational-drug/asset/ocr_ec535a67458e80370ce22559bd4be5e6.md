While recombinant DNA technology can be said to have its evolution as early as 1970s, it is commonly referred to as the Age of Recombinant DNA Technology that began in 1975.

The advent of recombinant DNA technology enabled scientists to manipulate and introduce genetic material into living organisms. As shown in Figure 2, this breakthrough laid the foundation for the genetic development of therapeutic proteins such as insulin and human growth hormone. These innovations marked a significant step toward the production of medicines tailored to a patient's genetic profile, thereby advancing the principles of personalized medicine and genotype-based therapeutic interventions.
