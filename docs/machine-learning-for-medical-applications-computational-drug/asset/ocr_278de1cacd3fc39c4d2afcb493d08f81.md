# 5.4 Applications of personalized medicine in rare and genetic diseases

Molecular medicine has been defined as personalized medicine as it was accepted as a novel therapeutic modality in the management of rare and genetic disorders and carries remarkable promises to the patients who have had very few therapeutic choices in the previous treatment regimens. Personalized medicine in that respect entails matching care interventions with genetic characteristics of the individual client to enhance diagnosis and treatment plans.

In choosing comparison targets, it is therefore understood that rare and genetic diseases pose the most formidable challenge because of their nature and diversity. Conventional diagnostic techniques do not have the required efficiency, often causing the doctor to miss the correct diagnosis. These issues are solved by personalized medicine using genomics-sequencing technologies to identify such genes accountable for these diseases. Whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing are coordinated processes used in the sequencing of an individual's genomic DNA, and the former helps identify carriers of pathogenic variants that would be ignored in standard sequencing. It improves the outcomes of diagnosing because it is accurate and allows clinicians to offer early, appropriate measures.

While being used in the diagnosis of rare and hereditary disorders, the most significant application of personalized medicine is in the development of distinct drugs. Since what genetic basis such ailments have is obvious, scientists can develop drugs that would specifically address these genetic changes in diseases. This method has been particularly useful in diseases such as cystic fibrosis, for which therapies developed to address the CFTR gene mutation have made impressive performance. In the same way, developing more individualistic medication has come up with PARP inhibitors that selectively stick to cancer cells harbored by BRCA mutations raising the treatment positives in hereditary diseases like ovarian and breast cancer associated with BRCA mutations.

Another manifestation of the idea of personalized medicine that has application for rare and genetic diseases is gene therapy. Compared to symptom-focused treatments that have existing gene-based approaches, gene therapy intends to fix the source of a wide range of genetic diseases by inserting genes in a patient's cells. New developments in modern technologies like CRIPPR CAS 9 have given relatively greenish solutions to many genetic disorders including SMA (spinal muscular atrophy) and various forms of genetic blindness.

Personalized medicine also has significance in untangling complicated genetic disorder through early diagnosis and tailored medical approach. For example, in diseases like familial hypercholesterolemia, it is possible to test for genes; therefore once individuals are found to possess those genes, lifestyle changes and the use of drugs can be administered early enough to prevent the situation from escalating. Hugo-Kaplan, A, et al., report that for conditions such as phenylketonuria, patients can develop their meal plan in consideration with metabolic tests, thus avoiding severe structural backups.
