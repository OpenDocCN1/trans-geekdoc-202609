
Patient’s response to therapy and their compliance to the same can also be closely monitored in real-time by the use of machine learning algorithms. Combining EHR, wearables, and self-reports, data analysis by machine learning methodologies reveals trends suggestive of low compliance or suboptimal outcomes with the administered medications. This ensures that patient gets the best one-on-one care they require, as other treatments can be adjusted or a new one administered immediately. For example, information obtained from continuous glucose monitoring can help change insulin management in diabetic patients in order to improve glycemic control and reduce the likelihood of complications.

This predicted enabler facilitates the assimilation of multi-omics data to offer an understanding of molecular mechanisms underlying the drug response, in addition to modeling and optimization. Combining proteomics, metabolomics data and transcriptomics, and genome data, pattern recognition systems can identify potential targets and pathways involved in medication action and disease. Hence, by painting a complete picture of a patient’s biological environment and directing the decision-making process as to which treatment to prescribe, this integration enhances the accuracy of the concept of precision medicine.

## 5.2 Adaptive dosing regimens and targeted delivery strategies enabled by ML

Targeted delivery methods and other methods of variable dosing regimens that use AI to provide highly individualized medicine also belong to the new concept of individualized medicine that enhances the preciseness and efficacy of patient's medication. As a result of machine learning algorithms, medicine doses and delivery systems are adjusted to meet the need of the individual patient as per the patient’s details. It helps to increase chances of treatment, and, consequently, to minimize the side effects of the treatment.

Use cases of creating adaptive dosing regimens is another area of application of ML, and one of its main types is the dynamic modification of medication dosages, depending on a patient’s real-time data. Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data, and actual and previous medication records of patients should be utilized in training machine learning algorithms that can help predict optimal dosage plan for each patient. For instance, using fundamental data from intermediaries or sensors on glucose levels, ML algorithms are employed to adjust insulin dosage flows constantly for ailment conditions such as diabetes. This assists in keeping a good glycemic level and reduces the risk of developing hypo- or hyperglycemia. Likewise, in oncology, ML models can forecast the best chemotherapy dose for every cycle, contingent on tumor response and patient tolerance, optimizing the therapeutic benefits and minimizing the deleterious effects.

Besides real-time dosing control, the use of ML allows for the creation of dosing protocols that can denote the impact of genetics, physiological factors, and the patient's life situation. Variations of genes that influence the metabolism and transport of drugs using pharmacogenomics are incorporated within ML to predict individual reactions to drugs. The first doses as well as further modifications establish what dosage the particular patient should undergo based on the genetic background. An important benefit of this approach is that for drugs with small therapeutic windows, it can avoid toxic concentrations while making certain the desired therapeutic impact is provided.

Specific drug delivery approaches is yet another field where ML has significant improvement possibilities. Using patient data, the ML algorithms can predict the best delivery route and mechanism of each drug and for a specific patient. ML helps enhance the carriers or vectors, which include liposomes, nanoparticles, and antibody drug conjugates, so as to increase drug concentrations at the site of the disease and also minimize side effects due to distribution to other areas in the body. To improve the safety and toxicity of anticancer drugs, using, for example, machine learning approaches, the ideal size and surface chemistry of NPs can be predicted to ensure maximal uptake by tumor cells in cancer treatment.

Also, the extent of dependence of medication delivery systems on ML is unassailable when it came to optimizing release patterns. Healthcare outcomes further improve when the required and desired controlled-released MNs are designed to release a drug in a sustained and localized fashion. Through data on drug properties, disease characteristics, and patient physiology, it is possible for ML models to create a machinery that releases the drug in the desired rates. This is more helpful for diseases that would be in treatment for an extended period to maintain the patient's drug levels, for instance, chronic inflammation diseases.

The use of ML as a strategy allows combining data from multi-omics to determine the precise delivery and dose of an agent. Using genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and clinical data, it is possible to develop ML models that define specific biomarkers concerning patient response to particular drugs and delivery systems. This integrative approach also refines the coordinates of personalized medicine, helping to fine-tune the therapeutic intervention to an understanding of what the cancer patient's body terrain looks like.

Remote monitoring technologies and wearable devices incorporated into the delivery process also play a role in the concept of adaptive dosing and targeted delivery strategies. Information coming from these devices can be fed into ML models unceasingly, giving real-time information regarding the health of the patients and their response to treatment. Another advantage of real-time data monitoring is providing ongoing feedback so as to correct dosing schedules or the method of administration for improved patient outcomes throughout the illness period.
