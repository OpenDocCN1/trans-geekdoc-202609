There are physiotherapy and rehabilitation aids that are meant to supplement the physiotherapy process for patients with musculoskeletal condition that requires physiotherapy. These devices include the robotic rehabilitation systems aimed at assisting motor rehabilitation and repeating motion tasks. Other examples include exoskeleton wearable assists for locomotion and posture, and therapeutic ultrasound for tissue repair and pain relief. Through sensors incorporated in the rehabilitation equipment, patient's progress can be closely observed and the treatment adapted to the requirements of a particular client.

Obstructive sleep apnea is treated using Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) machines as a method of controlling pauses in breathing during sleep. CPAP devices provide ongoing pressure of air through a facial mask in the nose and avoid the occurrence of apneas. Today's CPAP devices have features include the autotitrating devices that adjust the pressure, depending on the feedback given at that time, and the capability of connecting the devices to other devices for monitoring and sharing of information. Electro stimulation products are used to deliver electrical signals to challenged nerves for medical treatment. Such devices include TENS that is used in order to lessen pain by manipulation of nerve contractions, and NMES used in muscle remodeling and building up. Neuro stimulators include spinal cord stimulation (SCS) and deep brain stimulators (DBS), together with implants that are used as treatments that involve electro stimulation in specific areas of the nervous system, mostly for the management of chronic pain and certain neurological disorders. Employing different parameters of electrical stimulation results in the development of a specific therapy plan to meet needs of the patient, and improve the treatment outcome.

Medical man-made organs implants are sophisticated devices used for the treatment of ailing organs. Such devices are mechanical ventricular pumps (left ventricular assist device) that help patients who have severe heart failure, with mechanical support. Artificial kidneys (dialysis machines) replace the function of filtering blood in cases of renal failure and artificial lungs (extracorporeal membrane oxygenation systems) supply limited respiratory support to patients with critical respiratory illnesses. Artificial organs are in the process of providing hope and means between organ failure and transplantation as well as significantly enhancing the quality of life of those suffering from critical organ breakdown.
