Poly(lactic acid) (PLA): PLA is a completely biodegradable polymer derived from renewable resources like corn starch. PLA is widely employed in implants and tissue scaffolds due to its biocompatibility and degradation into nonhazardous products.

Polyglycolic acid (PGA): PGA, in tandem with PLA for polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA), was also employed in sutures and drug delivery systems. Some applications include temporary implantation, as the material is biocompatible and has a relatively preprogrammed rate of degradation.

# 3.3.3 Bioactive polymers

Bioactive polymers are designed to interact with biological systems in a manner that promotes specific physiological responses:

Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS): The highly biocompatible and easily molded nature of PDMS means that it is applied to a multitude of biomedical applications such as microfluidics and soft tissue engineering. It is chemically inactive and also has the feature of being easy to process and thus is frequently used in the manufacturing of medical devices.

Polycaprolactone (PCL): PCL is an enzymatically degradable polymer with a relatively slow degradation rate, making it ideal for long-term use. Its major applications include the fabrication of tissue engineering scaffolds and controlled drug delivery systems.

Polyurethanes: It was employed in making medical products such as catheters and implants because it was strong, flexible, and could almost be considered biodegradable. Polyurethanes were adjusted for certain mechanical characteristics and interactions with biological organisms.

# 3.3.4 Biodegradable polymers

Biodegradable polymers are designed to break down into nontoxic by-products in the body, minimizing long-term impacts and reducing the need for surgical removal:

Polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA): PLGA, which is a copolymer of PLA and PGA, has been established for extensive use in drug delivery and tissue engineering applications because of its tunable degradation profile and biocompatibility. By varying the ratios of PLA to PGA, the rate of degradation and release can be controlled.

Polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA): Derived from microbial fermentation, PHAs are both biodegradable and biocompatible and can, therefore, be used in diverse aspects of medicine, including surgery, pharmaceuticals, and drug delivery.
