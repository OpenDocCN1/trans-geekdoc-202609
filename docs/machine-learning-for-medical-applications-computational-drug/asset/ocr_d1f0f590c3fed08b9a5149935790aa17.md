# Vibration sensors

Vibration sensors encompass mechanical vibrations and movements in the body with certain physiological conditions and activities. Vibration sensors for physical activity, posture, and movements include accelerometers and gyroscope found in most fitness trackers and smartwatches. These sensors help to determine and quantify the amount of physical activity in daily life, characterize gait, and perform fall detection, which improves the accessibility of musculoskeletal and neurological condition supervision.

# PPG sensors

Two PPG sensors operating at different wavelengths measure light absorption by tissues and blood in different ways to image the microvascular bed of the skin. Due to the alterations in light absorbed by the pulsatile blood flow, PPG sensors can inform estimates of the HR, BP, and vascular profiles. PPG technology has been often implemented in wearables, such as fitness trackers and smartwatches, which allow detecting various cardiovascular indicators in a noncontact way.

# 3.4 Biosensors

Biosensors are defined as a type of biomedical sensors, specifically developed to recognize biological substances and transform the interaction between these substances into an electric signal. They play an essential part in disease detection as well as treatment by giving accurate, selective, and rapid quantitative analysis of multiplex biological markers. This section aims at discussing the different categories of biosensors, the operation mechanisms of biosensors, and their practical utilization in the health sector.

# Principles of biosensor operation

The working of biosensors can be explained by means of a biological element interacting with a transducer element. The biological component has to bind with a target analyte, which is glucose. The transducer transforms this interaction into an electrical signal subsequently. Electrochemical, optical, and piezoelectric transducers can be classified as the commonly used transducers of biosensors, in which each type has its own advantage based on the situation. For instance, electrochemical biosensors are relatively preferred for glucose sensing because of their high sensitivity and user friendliness, while optical biosensors are preferred because they are label-free and facilitate biolayer sensing.

# Electrochemical biosensors

Electrochemical biosensors represent the largest and most popular biosensors because they are sensitive, easy to use, and inexpensive. These sensors quantify the variation in electrical current that accompanies the biochemical interaction of the bioreceptor with the target analyte. Some examples are blood glucose meter for diabetes, regulated by glucose oxidase as the bio receptor and electrochemical transducers, which reduce the current change.
