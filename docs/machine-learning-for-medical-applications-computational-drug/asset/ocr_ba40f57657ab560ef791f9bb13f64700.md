# ### 2.4.3 Challenges and considerations

As with every kind of modeling, there were challenges that researchers faced when applying GANs in material science. One major consideration was the nature of the synthetic data created including its realism. Supplementing this is an illustration of improperly treated GANs, where the synthetic data on material properties does not simulate the real-world data situation. The use of synthetic data in conjunction with real experimental data must be handled carefully to prevent differences that affect the model. To overcome these challenges, further research and adjustments to the methods of GANs to enhance data credibility and usability are necessary.

In the area of generating synthetic material data and the future of GANs, several avenues seem viable for further research and development. In this regard, improvements to the structures of GANs, including the integration of elaborate loss functions and better procedures for training, result in higher levels of absorption and more realistic data synthesis. Further, integrating GANs with other algorithms, including reinforcement learning (RL), provides new solutions to material science problems. I imagine that the investigation of these possibilities is essential for the further development of using GANs for synthetic data creation and for the progress of biomedical materials science.

# ## 2.5 Transfer learning for improving material design models

This approach is known as transfer learning, a powerful deep learning (DL) technique that leverages knowledge gained from one domain to enhance performance in another, related domain. In materials science, transfer learning enables the use of pre-trained models from adjacent fields to accelerate the development and optimization of biological materials. This technique is especially valuable when dataset availability is limited, as it reduces the need for extensive data collection by reusing learned features and patterns from existing models.
