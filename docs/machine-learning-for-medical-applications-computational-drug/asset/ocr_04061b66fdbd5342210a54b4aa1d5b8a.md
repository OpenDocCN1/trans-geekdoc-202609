Biomarkers are also useful in prognosis and response to treatment in neurological disorders as well. Neuroimaging biomarkers include MRI and PET that give details of the anatomical and functional abnormality of the brain thus helping in disease diagnosis at early stages and to triage treatment response. Genetic and proteomic researches in biomarkers from cerebrospinal fluid and blood adjustment biochemistry in brains are biopsy-free diagnostic and prognostic instruments.

Personalized medicine also makes use of the environmental factors that affect the patient especially on the care that is being given. Low-complexity interventions including diet and exercise can be used alongside drugs to enhance the treatment of neurological ailments. Such factors are incorporated in individualized treatment interventions, which help improve the general outcome of therapeutic approaches.

Several challenges have to be addressed in order to get to the potential of personalized medicine for neurology. Some of them are the need for vast research that enables identification of relationships between genetics and diseases, for the development of clinical decision support systems, and for integration of personalization techniques into clinical practice. Ethical concerns linked with genetics testing of patient and privacy also have to be managed adequately to retain the trust and engagement in the process of the patient.
