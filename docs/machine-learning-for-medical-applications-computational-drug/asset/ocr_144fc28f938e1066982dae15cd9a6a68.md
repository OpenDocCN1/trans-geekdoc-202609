# 6.4.1 Display of the model's actions and decisions

It was indispensable to represent in the cases how the models worked and made decisions so as to determine errors and enhance model effectiveness. Making use of saliency maps, explained in the previous sections, allows for a direct view of which parts of the input data most affect the model's behavior: that is, which aspects of the material data the model is 'looking at'. By visualizing decision boundaries and decision trees, it was possible to describe which material properties the models classify and which criteria the models apply in their decision-making. Through these visualizations, decision-makers and engineers are able to critically evaluate the goodness of fit and the reliability of the models to ensure that the predictions made are in line with prior knowledge in material science as well as other real-world constraints.

# 6.4.2 Challenges in visualizing more biomedical materials

As much as visualization techniques provide many benefits, they also have various drawbacks. One problem is the difficulty in understanding how to analyze visualizations in cases of high dimensionality. The need to convey the behavior of the model without distortion through what is being visualized is paramount if the insights are to retain their value. Additionally, flexible integration of visualization instruments with various data forms and model constructions should be used thoughtfully. Overcoming these challenges entails two areas: creating complex visualization techniques and fostering user feedback to improve the interpretation of the visualizations.

Subsequent developments in biomedical material visualization approaches are expected to trend toward usability and compatibility with other analyses. Elements of the model and the data could be more easily navigated by the user if interactive interfaces were incorporated into the visualization tools to include different views of the models and the data. Engaging visualization with real-time data streams and feedback systems provides more instantaneous results about the model's performance as well as the properties of the materials used. Therefore, in the future growth of the explainable AI field, improved visualization approaches are crucial for expanding the principles and uses of machine learning models in biomedical materials.
