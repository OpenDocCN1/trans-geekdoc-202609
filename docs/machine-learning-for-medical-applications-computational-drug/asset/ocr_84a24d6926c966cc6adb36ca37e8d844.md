# 5.4.1 EEG sensors

EEG electrodes are essential components in neurological assessments, used to detect the brain's physiological activity by capturing electrical signals from neurons. EEG sensors are commonly applied in the diagnosis and monitoring of conditions such as epilepsy, sleep disorders, and brain tumors. As illustrated in Figure 9, recent advancements have led to the development of multifunctional EEG devices that are more affordable, compact, and suitable for use in natural environments eliminating the need for bulky, complex equipment. These innovations enhance mobility and allow for continuous monitoring, facilitating the analysis of seizure activity and the evaluation of cognitive functions in the management of neurological disorders.

The study uses Functional Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (fNIRS).

fNIRS is an optical technique for evaluating the brain activity without any need for touching the head, by measuring changes in the oxygenated blood volume. This technique is used by means of fNIRS sensors to evaluate the cerebral blood flow and brain function during cognitive task's incidents. It was applied in clinical trials and received great benefits in the diagnosis of brain dysfunction in patients with stroke, TBI, and neural disorders. Advances in the field fNIRS are the introduction of portable fNIRS system in studying cortex oxygenation and cerebral blood flow continuously in real-time.
