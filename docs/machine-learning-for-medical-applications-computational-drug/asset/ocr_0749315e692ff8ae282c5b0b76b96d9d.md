# 5.5 Performance evaluation metrics: accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, ROC-AUC

There was a range of valuable quantitative measures used to estimate the performance of machine learning models in medical imaging, and all these measures together provide a comprehensive view of the models’ work. The four primary metrics that are utilized consist of ROC-AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy.

Accuracy was the percentage of photos classified correctly out of all the images that had been sorted. It shows how well it predicts the total of unknown samples. It should not be expected to obtain equal accuracy for the performance of the model in the context of unbalanced data sets where certain conditions are rare because the model is inclined toward the most frequent class.

Now you remember, recall was another name for sensitivity, which defines how well a model is capable of correctly identifying positive conditions, such as determining an illness where it exists. Indeed, in medical imaging, for instance, where missing a positive case has major implications, this statistic is very important. High sensitivity implies that the model can single out subjects with this condition, and this is a significant factor in identifying the disease early enough after diagnosis.

Accuracy of negative observations of condition absence is what specificity measures in the model. This measure helps estimate the performance of the model in rejecting false positives, which is necessary to avoid unnecessary further tests. High specificity ensures that the model can determine with a lot of certainty whether a patient is not affected by the illness.

Every point on the curve represents sensitivity together with (1-specificity) over a range of cutoffs of the diagnostic test in question. The discrimination between the positive and negative situations in the total sample was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). A higher value of AUC was achieved, meaning that over a range of threshold levels, the model performed better in distinguishing between the presence and absence of the condition.
