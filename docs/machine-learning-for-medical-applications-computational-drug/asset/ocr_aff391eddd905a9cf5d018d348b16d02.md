Temperature measurement is one of the first features of the HM process to assess the ability of the body to regulate temperature and to detect fever. Current temperature sensors are incorporated into wearable, smartwatch bands and devices, and connected to remote controllers and thermometers. This type of a sensor provides continual temperature readings, which enable users to identify febrile diseases and all other diseases that relate to temperature. New technologies in temperature measurement also help combine the temperature readings with other parameters of a patient state, which is valuable.

Multiparameter monitoring systems utilize information from various invasive/ non-invasive vital signs sensors to give the overall picture of a patient. These systems integrate the readings of pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and temperature into one platform for easy health evaluation and monitoring of trends. Multiple parameter devices are frequently employed in patient care and in telemonitoring applications for those who have a chronic illness. These systems improve the accuracy of the assessments of the health status further by integrating data from the different sensors.

# 3.2 Chronic disease management

COPD stands for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, which is one of the chronic diseases that requires the active monitoring of a patient and in offering regular treatment. On analyzing the role of management of chronic diseases, the researcher noted that it impacts patient's QOL, risk of complications, and healthcare spend. Biomedical sensing and, particularly, the technological developments of the related portable devices have greatly improved the diagnosis, follow up, and management of chronic diseases with more precision, and in real-time.

Another area that has seen continuous monitoring technologies being important is in the management of diabetes. CGM systems provide constant monitoring and tracking of blood glucose levels and provide the patient's real-time information of their glucose levels. The combination of CGM with insulin pumps has allowed for closed-looped insulin delivery systems, which adjust the dose of the insulin intake, depending on the level of glucose, hence improving glycemic control and minimizing the use of fingerstick tests. In glucose control, these technologies are useful due to the adjustment that allows for timely appropriate changes and minimization of both hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia incidences.
