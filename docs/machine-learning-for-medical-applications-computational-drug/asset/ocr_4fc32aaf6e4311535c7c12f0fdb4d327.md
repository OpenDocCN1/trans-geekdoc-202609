# 4.5.1 Continuous health monitoring

Smart clothing endowed with adaptable biocompatible sensor systems implements simultaneous, noninvasive tracking of various physiological variables including heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose levels. These sensors usually employ the use of skin-like flexible polymers and stretchable conductive fabrics. For instance, the electrocardiogram monitoring patches use biomagnetically compatible adhesives and electrically conductive materials to achieve both the heart’s electrical signal recording and the patient’s comfort with no skin chafing. The phenomenon of continuous data collection enhances the early detection of health complications and allows for early treatment.

# 4.5.2 Biocompatible materials for enhanced comfort

Wearable devices had to be comfortable for users to wear, which made their compliance necessary to monitor them effectively. Safe materials like hypoallergenic silicones and soft elastomers have been used to prevent skin rashes for the user and ensure that the brace fits well. These fabrics are designed to be “sweat-friendly” and water-repellent, addressing most of the mentioned challenges, such as bacterial formation and skin irritation. Nonetheless, user comfort enhances the applicability and reception of wearable health gadgets through the use of biocompatible materials.

# 4.5.3 Integration with digital health systems

Wearable technology, many a time, has friendly biocompatible interfaces with digital health systems for integration. Such materials continue to be developed to allow physiological information to be collected through sensors and flexible electronics into smartphones and tablets. This compatibility enhances distant tracking and evaluation of trends in the customer’s health and allows for customized control for both the client and the suppliers.
