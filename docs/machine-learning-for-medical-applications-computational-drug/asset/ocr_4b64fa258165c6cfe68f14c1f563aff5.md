Some other criteria that have an influence on the possibility and durability of a drug delivery system are its mechanical characteristics. They need to be sufficiently rigid and elastic to stand physiological loads and the other environmental factors depending on what is intended. For example, mechanical properties are desirable for the materials being applied to tissue scaffolds and implanted devices to possess adequate structural performance in the long run.

One of the most important parameters of biodegradable medication delivery systems is degradation rates. In this case, these mechanisms are created to gradually dissolve or release the medications as ingested, metabolized, or excreted from the system. In tune with the treatment schedule, the rate of degradation was smoothly and regularly matched to give controlled and sustained release kinetics that optimize the therapeutic benefits of the medicine without increasing the risk of deleterious side effects.

The development of multifunctional drug delivery systems requires the ability to carefully analyze and selectively tailor material properties. More often, conventional methods of material properties characterization and optimization are augmented with technologies such as ML. Due to ML, it becomes possible to measure the properties of the materials and the outcomes of the experiments with large matrices, thereby acting as an effective tool for predictive modeling of the drug delivery systems with greater accuracy and efficiency.
