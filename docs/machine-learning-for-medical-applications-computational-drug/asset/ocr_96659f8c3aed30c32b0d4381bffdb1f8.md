Wearable health platforms: Mobile health platforms combine all cardiovascular sensors and analyze the data to provide cardiovascular status updates and alarms for proactive cardiovascular management and treatment.

These sensors, used for cardiovascular disease risk assessment, are very essential for the diagnosis, control, and handling of any condition that affects the heart. By providing healthcare information that are accurate and updated to the physiological and pathological status of patients, they support healthcare givers in decision-making for good patient management and consequently better quality of patient life.

# 5.2 Glucose monitoring sensors for diabetes management

Self-monitoring glucose sensors are vital in diabetes because they produce essential information that allows gunners to regulate glycemic control. These sensors provide the current daily indication of blood glucose concentrations, which enables individuals to make important lifestyle and/or therapeutic modifications, as necessary. This section looks at the advancement of glucose monitoring sensors with regard to the function and usefulness in diabetes treatment.

# 5.2.1 General information on glucose monitoring devices

Several technologies are employed in glucose monitoring, each with its distinct mechanisms and applications.

Fingerstick glucometers: The oldest form of portable devices, fingerstick glucometers, uses a small amount of blood from a finger prick to determine blood sugar levels. This method also produces results in real-time and is employed on account of it versatility and inexpensive nature.

Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems: CGM systems enable patients to monitor their glucose levels at any one time with the use of a sensor placed in the subcutaneous tissue. These sensors monitor glucose levels in the interstitial fluid and relay the information to a receiver in a smartphone where one is able to get an overview of the pattern and occurrences of hyperglycemia.

Flash glucose monitoring systems: Flash glucose monitoring systems encompass a sensor placed on the skin to check on glucose in the form of interstitial fluid. Compared with CGMs, the flash systems are more discreet since users need to swipe the sensor with a reader device for glucose measurement, but at the same time offer continuous monitoring.
