# 3.5.5 Biodegradable composites

Biodegradable composites are defined as composite materials that are formed with a reinforcement of at least two separate components to obtain an improved mechanical and biological features. Such composites are composed of a matrix of bioresorbable polymers with natural and/or synthetic fiber reinforcements. The choice of materials offers the possibility to develop structures with desired mechanical parameters and, at the same time, biocompatibility. Biodegradable polymeric composites are in the process of being evaluated for use in orthopedic implant devices and for tissue engineering scaffolds, with the expectation of achieving enhanced performance and decreased late sequelae.

It is remarkable how advanced these biocompatible materials have made biomedical engineering. Different types of materials possess their own unique characteristics to meet certain challenges in the medical field, leading to improved healing for patients as well as more efficient treatment methods. Consequently, it is expected that further investigation and innovation in this area will offer new findings that enhance the characteristics of biocompatible materials and expand their applications in modern medicine.

# 4 Applications of biocompatible materials

# 4.1 Implants and prosthetics

Implants and prostheses, which are central components of modern medical treatments for numerous diseases and conditions, require biocompatible materials. These materials are designed to interact with the body, offer functional support, and improve patients’ quality of life. This section analyzes some of the functions of biocompatible materials in implants and prostheses, their advantages, disadvantages, and relevance.

# 4.1.1 Orthopedic implants

Orthopedic implants are essential for the management of skeletal disorders, arthritic conditions, and fractures. Some of these implants include bone plates and joint replacement.
