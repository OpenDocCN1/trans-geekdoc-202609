# 2.3.1 Real-time data acquisition and monitoring of data

Most biomedical sensors are capable of continuously monitoring a range of physiological indicators, including but not limited to pulse rate, blood glucose levels, body temperature, and blood oxygen saturation. This real-time data acquisition allows for ongoing updates of health parameters, facilitating rapid detection of deviations from normal values. As illustrated in Figure 1, such sensors provide immediate feedback, enabling healthcare providers to promptly adjust medical interventions and modify treatment plans when necessary. This capability enhances patient care and helps reduce the risk of acute health complications.

Another feature of anthropometry as a field of measuring human body is the high precision and accuracy of measurement.

Skill in measurement is indispensable for successful health oversight, as is the ability to maintain finite accuracy in those calculations. The development of biomedical sensors is made in such a way that they have improved precision and minimal variability in the values obtained, in order to promote accurate diagnosis.
