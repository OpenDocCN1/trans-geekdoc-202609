Data encryption: To avoid unauthorized access and breach of the patient's information, the information in EHRs on the blockchain is encrypted. By encrypting important patient identification and medical data such as diagnoses, treatments, EHR systems that are based on blockchain assure patient privacy. This disallows unauthorized users to access the granted data especially since they are encrypted. Access to the NMDB is tightly controlled such that only personnel with the appropriate credentials can access the data; the data is encrypted such that keys to such access are required to decipher the data in question.

Data minimization: To reduce the exposure of data to unauthorized parties, data minimization best practices limit the quantity of PII kept on the blockchain. Minimized to truly needed metadata hashes of the data, EHRs should remain off the blockchain. This method keeps data on the blockchain while preserving data privacy and it also allows far less exposure of patient-specific information as a result.

Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs): ZKPs are cryptographic methods that let the prover demonstrate to the verifier that a statement is true without disclosing any further information. ZKPs can be used to confirm the legitimacy of transactions of EHR data in the context of blockchain-based EHRs without revealing the underlying patient data. This makes it possible for numerous parties to validate and share data on the blockchain in a safe and private manner.

Differential privacy is a strategy for strengthening privacy that tries to safeguard individual privacy by adding noise to aggregated data, all the while enabling useful analysis of the data. Differential privacy can be used in blockchain-based electronic health record systems to aggregate and anonymize patient data prior to its storage on the blockchain. This method helps prevent patient reidentification while maintaining the quality and usefulness of the data for analysis and study by hiding individual contributions to the dataset.

Enhancing privacy: Smart contracts used on blockchain technology can impose access limitations and privacy-maintaining guidelines for electronic health record information. Smart contracts that improve privacy, for instance, include guidelines for managing consent, exchanging data, and anonymizing health records. These contracts ensure that patient privacy wishes are honored and that data is exchanged safely and openly among authorized parties by automatically enforcing privacy regulations stored in the blockchain.
