## 2 Applications of AI in target identification and validation

### 2.1 Predictive modeling for target selection

It has been established that identification and confirmation of accurate molecular targets is a critical step that determines outcomes in the subsequent phases of drug development. AI-supported predictive analytics is now an effective tool in achieving this goal. Predictive modeling for target identification also involves synthesis of multiple transcriptomic, proteomic, genomic, and clinical data with a view of prioritizing conceivable targets depending on their relevance to a specific disease. These different datasets are heterogeneous in type and the machine learning methods alone are useful for finding some pattern, which could suggest these targets play a role in pathophysiology of a given illness.

Supervised as well as unsupervised learning techniques can be applied for target selection in predictive modeling. In the supervised group, techniques like Support Vector Machines (SVMs), Random Forest, and Neural Networks can be used. These algorithms incorporate gene information from labeled datasets that include data on known disease-related genes and irrelevant genes. In this way, the model can extend knowledge from these examples and determine the propensity of other genes to be associated with the disease of interest, as illustrated in Figure 1.

Another kind of approach is to utilize network-based analysis, that is, biological networks; the data are built from molecular relations; potential data sources are PPI networks, gene regulatory networks, and signaling pathways. Diffusion-based means and network propagation algorithms are further used to rank the candidate targets according to their connection with recognized disease-related genes in these networks.

Genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, clinical, and medication response data are integrated in various approaches in integrative approaches to achieve a comprehensive understanding of the functions of disease mechanisms and potential therapeutic candidates. Since the formation of integrative models employs sophisticated techniques such as the ensemble learning and deep learning, the different forms of data being integrated can identify forms and relationships that would not have been apparent when the data is looked at singly.

### 2.2 Network analysis for identifying disease-relevant pathways

Thus, network analysis is a powerful tool when it comes to rating and defining disease-relevant pathways in the field of development of drugs. Network analysis techniques utilize the biological networks such as gene regulatory networks, protein-protein interaction networks, and signal pathways to provide comprehensive information regarding the interactions of molecular components and to unfold detailed information about the essential mechanisms underlying the development and progress of diseases.

There are many well-known uses of network analysis including one of the most common applications that is to detect pathways that are hypothesized to be abnormal in a given disease state. When combining omics data with network topology, it is possible to define subsets of genes, which should concurrently change their activity levels. These modules can be equivalent to biological pathways modules that have important involvement in various disease processes.

Several algorithms based on the networks have been proposed to rank the disease-centric pathways and modules. For instance, centrality measures typically include betweenness centrality and closeness centrality in which nodes with desirable characteristics of the network are determined based on connectivity. High-centrality node is thought to be essential for the network and represents master controllers of functions related to disease states.

Also, the network propagation algorithms spread messages within the context of the network topology toward the genes associated with other diseased nodes based on functional annotations. These algorithms can simulate numerous focal probes diffusing across a network, thereby predicting additional candidate genes likely to be involved in similar biological pathways as the identified disease genes. These tools highlight significantly overrepresented pathways within a given list of genes, helping to determine whether genetic mutations affect the function and integration of specific biological pathways, as shown in Figure 2.
