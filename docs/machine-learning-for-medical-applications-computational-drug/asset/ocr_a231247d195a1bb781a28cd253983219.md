# ## 4.4 Machine learning approaches for predicting binding free energies

Both proteins and ligands are treatments for diseases, and the binding free energies that determine the strength of molecular interactions are critical in selecting potential drugs. ML techniques in particular have garnered considerable interest in this context because the modeling of a relationship between such features and binding affinities can be intricate. A number of approaches have been proposed for computing binding free energies reliably and in reasonable time.

The most commonly used machine learning method for predicting binding free energies is the regression models. These models are built based on a set of protein-ligand complexes to which the corresponding binding affinities are experimentally measured. These basic models are identified as linear regression, random forest regression, and support vector regression (SVR). These models are therefore trained to equate binding free energy with molecular descriptors extracted from protein-ligand complexes. Using data on a variety of molecular characteristics, regression models can predict binding free energies for a number of new and unknown protein-ligand complexes.

Another machine learning method for binding free energy predictions is the deep learning models. RNNs, GNNs, and CNNs reveal the ability to learn abstract representations of molecules together with the ability to incorporate sophisticated interactions within the protein and ligand complexes. In this work, we show that by training models on three-dimensional molecular representation of protein-ligand point-cloud interactions, deep learning models achieve better accuracy and generalization when predicting binding free energies.
