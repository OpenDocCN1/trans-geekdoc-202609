# 3.4 Differences between precision medicine and personalized medicine

Precision medicine and personalized medicine, though often used interchangeably, convey distinct meanings and concepts that are crucial to healthcare. It is important to understand these differences to appreciate how each approach contributes to the development of medical treatments and patient care.

Precision medicine is a large concept in therapy and makes use of different types of data – genetic, environmental, and lifestyles, to provide more accurate treatment frameworks for groups of patients who are alike in some ways. Precision medicine seeks to modify interventions based on patterns and shared characteristics by patients and risk factors, in an effort to target the right therapy. For instance, precision medicine requires categorizing cancer types through genomic markers in order to subdivide them according to unique genetic changes in order to design therapies that effectively counter the mutations.

The translation of pharmacogenomics into clinical practice is a process of extending a number of medications and the dosages of the chosen ones for each patient. Personalized medicine implies the adjustment of the medical care to the patient's individual features. This approach goes further than the patterns common in precision medicine that has adopted an individualized approach to the disease dependent on the patient's genes, previous medical records, and preferences. It is a microbots healthcare concept that involves entering tailored treatment options for an individual patient for improved efficiency and least harm. For example, personalized medicines include prescribing a drug taking into consideration the genetic structure of the patient to achieve the optimum amount of medication that will not cause an allergic reaction.

The major contrast between the two approaches is the degree of coverage. It is mostly about classifying diseases and deriving treatments for different clusters of patients having common genetics; that is why it deals with patterns inside communities. On the other hand, personalized medicine is more patient-centered, which encompasses including an array of specific characteristics of a given patient in order to provide care that is shaped to fit exact specifications.

This analysis also highlighted another difference between the two sets of approaches: their use. While precision medicine uses large-scale information about populations to understand and modify the treatment patterns of diseases, the concept of personalized medicine is more focused on using patient's data and preferences when making decisions. Personalized medicine intends to tailor preventive measures and therapeutic interventions to specific persons and their diseases, while precision medicine intends to deliver the most effective preventive measures and therapeutic intercessions to given populations of patients with the same disease.
