One of the other rather interesting trends is the integration of machine learning with digital health technology. A large amount of health-related information is generated every day through mobile health, wearable devices and electronic health records. Such a constant flow of data can be explained and processed by applying the methods of ML for tracking patient health, potential exacerbation of the disease, and proactive changes in therapy. For instance, wearable technologies can monitor the level of vital signs and other biomarkers that are used by the ML algorithms in optimizing -dosing regimens. Through the integration of mobile and pervasive technologies, it becomes effectively possible to design and deliver a healthcare system that is easily adaptable and continuously fine-tunes treatment, depending on patients’ feedback.

One of the promising trends in the concept of the personalized medicine is the 3D manufacturing of the medication delivery devices that are designed for a given patient only. Taking into account a patient’s individual characteristics as well as his/her physiological needs, one can develop computing-aided medication delivery systems like patches, implants, and oral dosage forms. By tailor made, we mean that bioavailability, targeting and site as well as time of release of drugs, can be promoted through these systems. Highly targeted intervention approaches are now available to the global population because of the rapid prototyping and production of these bespoke systems afforded by the integration of 3D printing technology and the intelligent utilization of advanced machine learning.

Computer vision and NLP help enhance the delivery of personal medicine. Using clinical data from patients such as clinician documentation, patient-reported, or literature, NLP techniques can gain great knowledge. The application of an ML model integrates the most recent research findings as well as clinical guidelines to make certain that the particular therapies delivered are the most contemporary recognized. Electronic medical records, together with images like MRI or CT scans, can be used by computer vision to assess the progress of a disease and design the delivery systems.
