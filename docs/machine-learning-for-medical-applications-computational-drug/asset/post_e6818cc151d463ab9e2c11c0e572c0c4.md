### 2.2 EHR

The EHR format is an electronic record that consists of patient's full history, details of testing, and treatment plans. In EHRs, different characteristics become clear; EHRs provide safety in storage and sharing of information over the dangers that come with paper records that get lost and create confusion to all entire healthcare teams.

The EHR system outlined in Figure 2 organizes and links several items such as appointments and test results but also allows the healthcare professional and the individual to view and modify the record. At its core, it is an EHR system that stores patient health information in a structured way, which is backed by digital holdings for the users with the permission to access and modify them according to need. EHR systems often feature functionalities such as:

- Patient demographics: The basic information that can identify a patient includes the patient's name, age, gender, phone number, and insurance.
- Medical history: All past illnesses, surgeries, vaccines, medical and family history and any other record of the person.

Interaction between patients and healthcare staff is documented at the clinic as clinical notes, which are short descriptions of the interactions. The symptoms as well as an analysis of diagnoses and treatments have to be incorporated into the records.

### 2.3 Overview of traditional EHR management systems

EHR systems have been essential for organizing flow and storing of the healthcare information. These frameworks are often centralized databases within a healthcare facility with specific servers that are outsourced. Traditional EHRs are those that existed prior to the time when digital means are standard for storing healthcare information that doctors can update using technology.

Traditional EHR systems can be defined by emphasizing on the unification of comprehensive patient's health information from various sources including clinical encounters and laboratory tests. It means that the entire medical data of the patient are accumulated in one place; they make care effortless and assist healthcare professionals in selecting superior therapies.

Data entry and management technology brings to the table of healthcare providers specific tools in documenting the visit of the patient and reviewing past records of a patient's health as well as in ordering certain tests and prescriptions. To increase the efficiency of the documentation and to ensure the required uniformity in guarantee records these systems can have basic interfaces and can be tailored according to different medical professional positions.

Figure 3 illustrates traditional Electronic Health Record (EHR) management systems, demonstrating how a patient's health information flows from data input and storage to output processes and security controls utilized by both patients and healthcare providers. These conventional systems offer functionalities such as access monitoring, security management, and data protection to prevent unauthorized access, legal violations (such as those under HIPAA), and to ensure the confidentiality of patient information.

While traditional EHR systems are still widely adopted and considered effective, they exhibit several limitations. One significant challenge is interoperability—the difficulty in sharing and integrating data across different Health Information Systems (HIS). This limitation often leads to information silos, restricting the seamless exchange of patient records and impeding coordinated care.

Interoperability issues: Interoperability issues in EHR systems are as such understated, which leads to the isolation of patient information and limits their integration with other HIS.

Data silos: Before the implementation of Share Everywhere EHR systems, patient health information was typically stored within the boundaries of individual healthcare organizations, often in proprietary platforms. This limited the ability to aggregate and share patient data across different organizations.

Centralization and single point of failure: This was so when EHR databases were centralized and it opened the doors for data leak and congestion of the service in a way that affects patient's information.

Vendor lock-in: As proprietary EHR systems are already implemented in healthcare entities, the latter have few choices at their disposal since they cannot tailor systems.

The traditional EHR systems play a great role in enhancing digital transformation of healthcare data together with increased support for clinical documentation. Some limitations limit this system's ability particularly in the areas of data communication and security. Blockchain technology has introduced a new approach to solving these concerns and enhancing safety and confidentiality of EHR systems in innovative ways.
