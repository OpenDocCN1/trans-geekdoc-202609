# 4.2.4 Biodegradable implants for drug delivery

Medication was administered directly at the affected site through resorbable depot systems, which disintegrate within the body. The most preferred material for constructing these implants was the biocompatible and bioresorbable material that includes PLA. For this reason, these systems decrease fluctuations in systemic exposure and do away with the need for multiple injections by dispersing the medication at the point of invasion. Some of these uses include cancer therapy, where the implant offers continuous and local drug administration in the affected area, hormone replacement therapy, and birth control implants.

# 4.2.5 Injectable drug delivery systems

Injectable drug delivery systems involve the use of biodegradable and biocompatible polymers to administer drugs with minimal invasiveness. Such systems can include injectable hydrogels, microcapsular systems, or delivery via syringe. It is relatively easy to administer injectable formulations, fewer operations are required to carry them out, and it is possible to administer the drug continuously. These systems are especially beneficial in delivering biologics and vaccines, which require precise dosages and slow or sustained release.

Incorporation of biocompatible materials in drug delivery systems has enhanced the pharmaceutical field in various ways and opened new doors to deliver effective treatments and achieve better results. These systems ensure that many of the drawbacks and limitations of conventional delivery technologies are overcome, leading to improved health outcomes and better patient compliance.
