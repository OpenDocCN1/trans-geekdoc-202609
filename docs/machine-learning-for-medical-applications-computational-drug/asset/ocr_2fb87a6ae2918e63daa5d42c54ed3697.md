CNNs: Multilayer CNNs specialize in harvesting spatial hierarchies and characteristics, as they are built primarily for image analysis. CNNs can freely compose and decode complex patterns of medical images by performing convolution, pooling, and fully connected layers. CNNs have depth and design that allow feature extraction and classification across a wide range to enhance their performance on tasks such as object recognition and image segmentation.

Transfer learning: Transfer learning is the idea of taking deep learning models that have gone through the training stage and refining them for several medical imaging uses. Such a method reduces the amount of training data needed while drawing data from large datasets and using the data collected from these datasets. In this field, there are not as many labeled datasets in medical imaging, and so transfer learning is very useful.

Generative models: Some organizations and amorphous NNs, such as GANs and variational autoencoders (VAEs), are examples of deep learning that can be used in generating synthetic images. These models are useful in data augmentation and image synthesis, and they are used in enhancing the quality of medical images when training data prove hard to come by.

If it is any algorithm, then training and optimization become very important when looking at performance.

Hyperparameter tuning: A specific set of hyperparameters like learning rates, regularization parameters, and details of the structures of deep learning models is as crucial as those of conventional learning. To select the best-suited hyperparameters, a number of processes are used including grid search, random search, and the use of Bayesian optimization.

Cross-validation: To evaluate model performance and to stop at some point, different forms of cross-validation are applied, which include k-fold cross-validation. Through such methods, one is assured that models come up with the right estimates of the performance measures and can generalize future data that were not used during modeling.

Computational resources: Most of the DL models are trained on several computational platforms and elements that are widely demanded including GPUs and large-scale parallel processing. Time and resource management during the training process involves the usage of computing resources and different techniques such as early stopping and checkpointing.

In general, classical algorithms have problems with high-dimensional data and require a lot of feature engineering. In contrast, deep learning models are capable of learning complex patterns and performing feature extraction, which enhances performance in many medical image analysis applications. However, as stated before, deep learning models often require more computation and a lot of data to learn well.
