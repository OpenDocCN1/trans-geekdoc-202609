The integration of ML into biomaterials research has led to significant improvements in material design and property prediction [6]. This integration facilitates the development of more efficient and effective materials by predicting their behaviors under various conditions, thus streamlining the research and development process [7]. The increasing complexity of biomaterials, combined with the vast amount of data generated from experimental and computational studies, necessitates sophisticated analytical techniques that ML is uniquely equipped to provide [8].

Using sophisticated algorithms to improve property prediction is one of the main uses of ML in the biomaterials industry [9–11]. Conventional approaches for predicting properties frequently depend on empirical data and constrained computational models, which can be unreliable and limiting [12]. Regression models and neural networks, on the other hand, are examples of ML algorithms that can analyze massive amounts of data to identify links and patterns that are not immediately obvious [13]. Predictions of material attributes like mechanical strength, thermal stability, and chemical resistance are made possible by this capability [14]. Researchers create prediction tools that guide the development of novel biomaterials with customized features by training ML models on large datasets [15]. This approach accelerates the material development process and reduces the need for trial-and-error testing [16].

The capacity to evaluate and comprehend biomaterial data has been improved with the integration of ML with characterization methods [17, 18]. Large and complicated datasets are produced by characterization techniques, including atomic force microscopy (AFM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and X-ray diffraction (XRD), which can make it difficult to analyze the data using conventional analytical techniques. By locating important characteristics and connections in the data, ML techniques, such as clustering algorithms and dimensionality reduction, improve the study of these datasets [19]. For example, ML methods have the potential to enhance the precision of material phase identification in XRD data and surface analysis from SEM images [20]. Better knowledge of the characteristics and behaviors of biomaterials has been made possible by these advancements in characterization techniques, which improve material design and optimization [21, 22].

Looking ahead, a number of developments and trends are expected to boost ML’s contribution to biomaterials research [23]. It is anticipated that multimodal data integration, which combines several data types from various characterization methods, will offer a more thorough understanding of biomaterials [24]. The ability to analyze data in real-time will facilitate prompt feedback during material testing and manufacturing, resulting in more flexible and effective research procedures. Researchers find it simpler to comprehend and trust the predictions generated by ML models as explainable AI (XAI) advances, thereby improving the interpretability of ML models [25]. These upcoming developments should enhance ML’s capabilities in the field of biomaterials science, spurring further research and innovation.
