# 3.2 Implantable sensors for internal monitoring

Wearable and implantable sensors: Implantable sensors are the next generation devices for continuous monitoring of health status for long-term, real-time data assessment of the body's physiological parameters on internal data. They are intended to be implanted within the human body to continually measure a number of internal conditions necessary for the practitioner to make key decisions in the management of the patient.

# Materials’ biocompatibility and product design.

Implantable sensors’ design considers the organism’s compatibility due to the possibility of the formation of complications within a human body. Recent advancements in material engineering have provided biocompatible polymers, metals, and ceramics that can last in the biological environment without stimulating the formation of an inflammatory response. Further, modern technologies have allowed the development of ever smaller constituents of sensors and their implantation with less invasive actions. In addition, the increased use of flexible and conformal materials to integrate with and conform to the body’s internal tissues has increased the comfort and efficiency of such devices.

# Classification and uses of implantable sensors

Implantable sensors are designed to monitor a range of physiological parameters, each serving distinct medical purposes:

Glucose monitoring sensors: Diabetes management is closely associated with such sensors, which continuously measure the levels of glucose to ensure the proper regulation of blood sugar concentration. Electrochemical sensors use sharp and long-life elements, providing high sensitivity in the measurements, and seldom requiring calibration.

Intracranial pressure sensors: Basically, found in neurological applications, these sensors monitor intracranial pressure, which is crucial in several diseases like TBI and hydrocephalus. Among the recent developments are wireless sensors that do not require external connectors and, therefore, are not inflamed.

Cardiac sensors: ICCAs measure HR, HRV, and other data from the patients’ heart and help in the modality of detection of arrhythmias and heart failure. Progress in this area has seen the fabrication of shoe box-sized devices that can be installed within the chest cavity surrounding the heart in order to provide accurate and constant observation.

Pressure sensors: These sensors calculate pressure within the different body organs such as the bladder and the gastrointestinal system. These are bendable pressure sensors that can mimic the form of internal organs and offer almost painless measurements.

Temperature sensors: Febrile thermal condition recording require implantable temperature sensors. Recent advancements advance the precision and speed of these sensors in the clinical setting.
