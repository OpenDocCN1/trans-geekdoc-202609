# 5.3.3 Screening and assessment of biomarkers

Detection and monitoring of biomarkers directly play a significant role in improving the impact and minimizing ADRs, which are so largely associated with medication efficacy. Biomarkers can offer details about disease states, predict the effectiveness of treatment, and recognize those who may exhibit adverse reactions. For instance, selection of patients who would likely benefit from trastuzumab (Herceptin) in breast cancer treatment is made possible by the use of HER2 as a biomarker. Evaluating total scores of liver function tests can also help in establishing the chances of developing drug-induced patotoxicity, and providing early treatments to prevent further complications.

# 5.3.4 Enhancing patients compliance and health outcomes

Personalized medicine reduces the deviation probability from the prescribed course of treatment, since it is based on the initial assessment of the patient’s characteristics. Hypothesised here is the fact that the treatment tailored toward a patient’s individual genetic and metabolic constitution is more effective and better tolerated, hence patients will be more compliant. A better degree of compliance with medications increases the efficacy of treatments and decreases the risk of deterioration of health of a certain ailment. For instance, special focus can be made in the treatment of diseases like diabetes and hypertension with outcome measures that show better control of disease indicators in the long run and improved general health.

Using personalized medicine in clinical practice is of tremendous value since it improves the effectiveness of the drugs and decreases the rate of adverse reaction to medicine. Through the use of genetic, proteomic, and metabolomic information, healthcare providers can accurately dose drugs, choose therapies that are most effective, and manage risks. All these advancements enhance therapeutic intercessions and safety, resulting in enhanced patient success and the general quality of healthcare.
