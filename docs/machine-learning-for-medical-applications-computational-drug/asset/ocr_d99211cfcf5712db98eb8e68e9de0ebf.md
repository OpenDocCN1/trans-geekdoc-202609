Genomic profiling to identify specific genetic dysregulation and alterations within cancer cells is the primary approach of personal oncology. As a result of such approaches as NGS, one can identify the set of mutations that drives tumor growth and hence cancer. Oncologists can elect to use a treatment type that would selectively attack the genetic profile present in each patient’s tumor through identification of these molecular signatures. New drugs that are more effective, with fewer side effects than ordinary chemotherapy, have been developed to counter some genetic flaws. For instance, tyrosine kinase inhibitors exist for EGFR gene mutation and PARP inhibitors for BRCA1/2 gene mutations.

Individualized oncology methods are immunotherapy and targeted agents, which employ the immune response to deal with malignancies. Knowledge of the types of environments in which tumors grow and how cancers evade immune recognition has given rise to protein regulators of immune response called immune checkpoint inhibitors, which include anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibodies. More examples of what constitutes a personalized treatment plan being used to enhance the immune system’s ability to detect and destroy cancer cells are adoptive cell therapies such as CAR-T cell therapy as well as personalized cancer vaccines.

A second key area of individualized oncology care is the use of tumor biomarker testing to inform therapy. For instance, tumor mutational burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), and selected protein expression are associated with tumor sensitivity to specific treatments. High TMB and MSI are known to correlate with higher rates of response to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Emphasizing these biomarker tests in the clinical arsenal, oncologists are able to identify which treating strategy works for particular patients and provide the best possible results.

The matters demonstrated by constructive oncology indicate how important it is to monitor patients’ outcomes and adapt the therapy regimen, accordingly. Liquid biopsies in blood samples, which involve the exam of ctDNA, enable the evaluation of alteration in tumor mutation profiles. Regarding this, treatment management can be adjusted depending on the development of mutations and suitability of the existing regimen of treatment.
