This method is especially significant in drug toxicity prediction, where the model encounters different data points and may generate incorrect predictions. Adversarial inputs, for instance, should not significantly affect the performance of robust models.

**Stress Testing:** Stress testing is an evaluation of a model in worst-case scenario situations. This approach checks whether the model performs optimally in high-stress scenarios and ensures that the model remains reliable when run under a range of conditions by “stress-testing” the model, for example, by passing it data that is noisy or in some way out of distribution to the model.

**Data Perturbation:** In data perturbation, certain forms of random noise are added to its input to observe the results experienced by the model. This approach assists in establishing the reliability and variability of the model output, that is, whether the model is overfitting on some trivial variations of the data.

**Cross-Validation Across Datasets:** As for improved cross-validation techniques, it is possible to work with several datasets and obtain additional information concerning a model’s performance, including but not limited to working with diverse samples. The results obtained for different datasets also confirm the model’s ability to generalize and its consistency across various situations.

**Consistency Checks:** The second type of analysis was called consistency checks, which include checking whether the model was accurate in its predictions across different kinds of runs and datasets. One also gets a sense of whether the model has behaved similarly across the different experiments or if the model’s performance was erratic.

**Error Analysis:** The error analysis procedure was broken down to a detailed level to reveal the reasons for instabilities in model predictions. When misclassifications occur, there can be systematic reviews to determine issues that affect model stability and correct them.
