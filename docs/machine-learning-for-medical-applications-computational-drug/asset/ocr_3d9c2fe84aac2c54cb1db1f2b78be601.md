# 2.4.2 Linus Pauling

Molecular biology and genetics were enriched by two-time Nobel price winner Linus Pauling. Through his work about nature of chemical bonds and structure of proteins, particularly the investigations of sickle cell anemia, he explained how mutations caused diseases. Pauling’s work has given impetus to molecular medicine, which was at the basis of current therapies adapted to individual geno-molecular characteristics.

# 2.4.3 Baruch S. Blumberg and Robert L. Wilner

The presented paper was devoted to the substantial contributions of Robert L. Wilner and Baruch S. Blumberg for the enhancement of knowledge regarding the variations at genetic level and their correlation with diseases. The efficacy of the discovery made by Blumberg concerning hepatitis B virus and liver cancer showed that genetic and viral risks have to be considered when conducting disease risk assessment. Their work emphasized that the basis for prevention and treatment of the disease can be identified precisely with the help of the concept of the personal genome and the environment.
