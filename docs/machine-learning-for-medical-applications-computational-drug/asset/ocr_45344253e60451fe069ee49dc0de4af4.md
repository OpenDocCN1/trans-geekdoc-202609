# 4.5 Reinforcement learning in molecular docking optimization

There is a method, which is considered to enhance the molecular docking simulations, namely RL, which can enhance and partly automate one of the most critical steps in drug design. In the case of protein-ligand complex, the RL algorithms through the interactions with the environment permit an agent to learn basic dockings.

The application of reinforcement learning (RL) in molecular docking optimization has enabled more efficient conformational space sampling of ligands. RL agents iteratively explore various torsional angles and spatial orientations of ligand molecules, optimizing the client-server communication between the RL system and molecular structures, including quantum mechanical (QM) configurations. These agents receive feedback from scoring functions within the environment, allowing them to select poses with optimal ligand-binding affinities. As illustrated in Figure 10, RL-based docking strategies allow agents to learn from suboptimal docking experiences and continuously refine their approach. This leads to more efficient pose searches and improved docking outcomes with high-affinity ligand geometries.

Specific parameters of RL algorithms can be used to optimize docking protocols and scoring functions in order to enhance the reliability of molecular docking calculations. What is more, RL agents are capable of modifying parameters, such as grid spacing, search algorithms, and scoring terms in view of the docking performance. Through a process of elimination to fine-tune these parameters, the effectiveness of binding affinity predictions of RL agents improves, along with the recognition of the superior ligand poses.

RL could be used to improve and enhance molecular docking where protein-ligand interactions are of interest. RL agents can facilitate optimizing contact and hydrogen bonding between the receptor and ligand; the agents can also learn to modify the positions of protein side chains and ligand atoms.
