The advantages of miniaturized sensors include: 1) the patients’ comfort level is enhanced, 2) there is reduced intrusion, and 3) convenience has improved. For instance, telemetry glucose sensors can be incorporated in the small-size continuous glucose monitoring devices that can be worn on the body in order to monitor glucose level changes in near-real time and without much discomfort.

In biomedical sensors, nanotechnology refers to the incorporation of materials and structures that are at least one dimension less than one nanometer in order to enhance the performance of sensors. Taking advantage of the advantageous properties of nanoparticles that include increased surface area-to-volume ratio and higher reactivity, it becomes possible to develop very sensitive and selective sensors. Nanowire, nanotube, and nanoparticle have unique properties, which enhance sensitivity and specificity of the sensors to detect various physiological parameters and biomarkers.

Nanotechnology also enables formation of nanoscale sensors that can be used for small portable wearable gadgets. These nanoscale sensors have the potential of achieving much higher resolutions and therefore improve measurement of physiological parameters. For example, the use of nanoscale sensors would make it possible to measure the changes in biomarkers associated with diseases at the early stage.

Miniaturization and nanotechnology have led to availability of sophisticated sensing techniques including the nano sensors for molecular diagnostic and imaging. These innovations push the envelope to provide improved accuracy and individualized care by enabling examination of biological specimens and constant observation of cellular and molecular activity.
