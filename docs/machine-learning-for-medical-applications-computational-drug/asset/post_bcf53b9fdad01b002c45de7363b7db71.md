
DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine): DICOM is a standard for the communications and storage of medical images and related information throughout the healthcare systems. DICOM encompasses a full solution for protocols, data formatting, and methods of communication for integrated sharing, storage, and retrieval of images in various imaging specialties and healthcare organizations/centers. DICOM facilitates exchange of medical imaging data in a way that allows them to interoperate with EHRs; it guarantees availability of diagnostic images in the overall ambience of the healthcare systems.

IHE (Integrating the Healthcare Enterprise) profiles: IHE is a strategy for working with “clusters” of higher-level standards as well as defining guidelines and interoperability profiles for implementation. This defines various HL7 and DICOM use cases and how they have been implemented and exist to support various interoperability information exchange use cases such as lab result exchange, transferring medical images, and querying patient demographics. Through specification of connectivity profiles and supporting templates IHE ensures effective data exchange and integration of EHRs.

SMART on FHIR: SMART (Substitutable Medical Applications, Reusable Technologies) on FHIR was an open framework for developing cross-platform healthcare applications operating on the FHIR framework. SMART on FHIR includes specific principles and prescriptions for designing and building loosely coupled components that integrate with EHRs through FHIR interfaces. SMART on FHIR delivers through web-based technologies and applying open standards, empowers developers to build applications that are integrated with EHR systems to advance solutions for health information exchange and interoperability.

## 4.4 Blockchain-based solutions for seamless data sharing

Blockchain can provide new approaches to enable EHR data sharing, solve problems of interoperability, and promote more effective exchange of both electronic and personal health information. Some solutions utilizing blockchain technology have been proposed to improve data exchange between different EHR systems; these solutions all take advantage of unique characteristics of the blockchain platform to foster interoperability and cooperation. As shown in Figure 10, blockchain-based solutions for data sharing by integrating blockchain technology into the solution environment is illustrated for better understanding of the associated benefits such as security and efficiency of data sharing between involved parties in the healthcare system. Some of the key blockchain-based solutions for seamless data sharing in EHR systems include:

Decentralized data exchange platforms: The opposite of centralized middlemen, the decentralized data exchange based on the blockchain helps patients, healthcare organizations, and other parties to exchange and get required EHR data securely. These systems allow for dissemination of data among systems with privacy, integrity, and consent by incorporating blockchain’s dispersal of data, the cryptographic principles, and smart contract. Decentralized data exchange platforms work on the principles of multiple data systems and do not require intermediaries and a central hub to share data and allow EHR systems and healthcare facilities to exchange information.

Federated learning and data sharing networks: On the basis of blockchain technology, federated learning and data sharing networks enable cooperative studying and sharing of EHR data together with preserving data confidentiality and privacy. In federated learning, several decentralized nodes have a part of EHR data, and collaboratively train a machine learning model. When data is aggregated and analyzed, cryptographic approaches like differential privacy and homomorphic encryption safeguard patient privacy. Blockchain guarantees the integrity and traceability of model modifications and data contributions. Federated learning networks facilitate interoperability and knowledge exchange between academics and healthcare organizations by providing safe and private data sharing.

Patient-controlled data sharing platforms: These allow the ownership and control of personal health data and allow health and research recipients access to them only when appropriate. These blockchain-based systems allow clients to grant different parties access to specific EHR information while ensuring data privacy and control of access by use of digital signature and keys. Systems that patients control also means that patient data can be easily provided and accessed across multiple EHR systems and across the various settings where patients seek care.

Interoperable health information exchanges (HIEs): Interconnected HIEs leveraging information technology consult blockchain to enable a secure and standardized exchange of electronic data across greater healthcare systems. Smart contracts enable efficient handling of permission-related processes and contracts between several parties; blockchain ensures the availability and credibility of EHR data transferred through exchange implementation. Blockchain-based HIEs enable the sharing of information across care givers and patients, co-ordination of care between different providers and systems, as well as cooperation between patients through an interoperable sharing platform.

Standards-based blockchain networks: Such networks leverage standard and recognized norms and procedures for conglomerate connectivity, for instance, HL7 FHIR, for the conversions and the reconciliations of EHR information among different blockchain networks and healthcare systems. Standardized blockchain networks ensure compatibility, interconnectivity, and interchangeability of data across the healthcare systems since they conform to set data format, messaging, and integration standards embraced by the various networks that make up a standards-based blockchain. This promotes interoperability and data sharing across varied EHR systems and stakeholders.

## 4.5 Case studies of successful interoperable EHR implementations

Examples of the effective use of EHR systems connected through an interoperable network with the help of blockchain technology are described, and such cases indicate promising areas of the application of blockchain in the healthcare domain. These case descriptions demonstrate that conceptualization and strategy development are critical to creating viable and successful models of interoperability and data sharing among many stakeholders in healthcare systems. Some notable examples of successful interoperable EHR implementations include the following:

Medicalchain is an application that is run through the blockchain platform and enables patients to store their electronic health records securely and share securely with other parties like doctors and researchers. Through the use of blockchain technology, Medicalchain guarantees data security and nondisclosure of patient’s health records while creating an auditable trail. The EHR data was electronically shared by permissioned contracts, which include provision of consent for the authorized healthcare providers to access the data. Medicalchain’s interoperable EHR solution resolves health stakeholders working in unison by ensuring proper communication and thus patient care is enhanced.

Coral health: Coral Health was an EHR platform designed on blockchain characterized by seamless integration of records from one ecosystem to another. Coral Health offers a patient health record repository through blockchain, making it easier for patients, payers, and providers to share EHR data. Patient identity and data security are also maintained during the exchange of data through cryptographic techniques employed on the platform. Coral Health EHR Solution was an integrated EHR system where the sharing of data, open integration, and team approach enhance quality and operational performance of care.

Nebula Genomics: Nebula Genomics is the company that is built on blockchain technology to use it for genomic data sharing and analysis. Nebula Genomics operates on the blockchain, which is used to establish a safe and trustworthy market place for genomics data where people can sell their genomics data to researchers, pharmaceutical companies, and other healthcare providers.
