# 6.5.1 Methods for improving the interpretable nature

There are a plethora of ways available through which we can enhance the interpretability of DL models in material science. One method is visualization, where activation maps and feature importance scores are used, allowing us to identify which parts of the input data most affect the model's result. For instance, saliency maps are capable of showing where within a material's microstructure to look when focusing on the prediction in question. Another approach is to use components of the model that are easier to explain, such as attention mechanisms, which can provide more insight into how individual input features drive the model's output. Furthermore, methods such as LIME and Shapley values can be employed to provide local interpretable model-agnostic explanations for a single prediction, thus offering the client information on specific decision-making processes.

# 6.5.2 Trade-offs and limitations

Still, it must be pointed out that no configuration of an interpretation and a set of interpretability techniques were without flaws and that trade-offs exist. Increasing interpretability requires models to be simplified and boundaries to be approximated, both of which generally deteriorate model performance. Meeting interpretability with high predictive performance is a delicate process that needs to be tuned and tested. In addition, the interpretability method can be effective depending on the type of material science data as well as the complexity of a certain model, so the better approach is to select the most appropriate technique for the purpose.

Interpretation of DL for material science was crucial to reaching higher levels of supervised contingence capable of supplying a higher degree of understanding regarding the functioning of the models. The focus was on making interpretability inherent to the processes of model engineering, with new visualizations designed to better present high-level material data. Implementers of interpretability methods accounted for compositional semantics and other domain considerations pertinent to material science, updating interpretability methods to be more meaningful and useful. As these advancements continue to unfold, the aim remains to offer better, clearer, and more trustworthy data regarding material characteristics from DL models to enable improved material design and analysis.
