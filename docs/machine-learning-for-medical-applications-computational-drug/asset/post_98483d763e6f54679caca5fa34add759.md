
Economic constraints also influence specific patient's access to the new biomedical sensors and devices. Patients with few resources would be hard put to afford such gadgets and the cost of sustaining them. The use of devices during treatment means an additional cost, which is financed by the patient himself, as also the expense related to the care of devices, apart from the cost of repair, which is also additionally paid by the patient himself. Economic barriers can be addressed through case approaches between healthcare providers, policy makers, and device manufacturers to ensure that the latest technologies are provided to all stakeholders, including the needy and the indigent.

### 5.5 Technical and operational issues

It can be illustrated that technology and operation factors are the major concerns in biomedical smart sensors and related devices manufacturing. Solving these issues acts as a precondition for achieving satisfactory levels of device stability, efficiency, and consumer enjoyment. There are a number of technical and operation challenges within these technologies affecting the application and uptake of these solutions.

Accuracy and reliability of the sensors that are used in developing biomedical devices are always questionable. Biosensors should be accurate in order to detect physiologic parameters such as temperature, heart rate, and blood glucose. The variations of the sensors can be caused by aspects such as environmental, users, and degradation of the devices. These problems call for frequent calibration and maintenance for the system to offer a standard performance. Future developments include improved methods of calibration and the invention of sensors with self-calibration in a bid to minimize the number of manual adjustments.

The data fusion derived from multiple sensors and devices presents tremendous technical difficulties. Mobile biomedical devices, in particular, require integration with EHR systems as well as other healthcare applications and technologies. The interoperability of the systems is a significant challenge to ensure that the whole picture of patient's health condition is represented. It is important to establish unified formats and communication protocols in order to make integrations and consequently data from various sources can be combined and analyzed. Solving the issues of interaction involves the action of manufacturers of devices, application developers, and healthcare institutions to set universal norms.

Battery management is always a major issue in the use of wearable and implantable biomedical systems because of their need for long-time use. Short battery life affects the device's functionality and, often, user adherence, especially for devices that need to be used for extended periods continuously. Battery technology, electronic parts efficiency, and efficient power use are central to prolonging battery longevity and lessen the need to charge batteries. This has prompted researchers to look for other solutions of power supply, including using power from body movements, to improve device sustainability.

## 6 Future directions

### 6.1 Emerging technologies

Advanced technologies that are on the rise are a clear determinant of biomedical sensors and devices that are on display because they act as the development engine for biomedical health monitoring systems. Developments in this area hold potential to improve the accuracy, speed, and coverage of diagnosis methods and treatment approaches in medicine. Of the many subfields of engineering, several of them are expected to bring a more profound influence in the area of biomedical devices with the advancement of technology.

With the help of AI and ML, biomedical sensors and devices have become more versatile and efficient to develop. The use of these technologies allows handling a massive amount of health data to identify patterns and potentially unusual occurrences, otherwise not visible to the naked eye. AI algorithms can help in effective execution of predictive analysis, customization of treatments, and improving device functionality, based on the results obtained from the real-world data, making progressive changes. Emerging technology in the AI diagnostic tools, including image recognition and natural language processing, are predicted to enhance the technologies embedded in devices and assist in clinical decision-making.

Nanotechnology is preparing a whole field of nano sensors that are expected to be very sensitive and specific in detecting biochemical alteration on the molecular level. These can be used as biosensors, for signs of early disease, metrics of biomarkers, and as drug delivery systems. Development of nanoparticles and nanowires offers the opportunity to develop highly sensitive and selective sensors suitable for integration into wearable devices. The capability of doing real-time molecular diagnoses can be useful in enhancing and optimizing human health.

IoMT is a nascent technology, which bridges medical devices and systems through a common network, the internet, to share real-time data and perform remote monitoring. IoMT relates to a broader class of associated technology such as body wearables, remote monitoring equipment, and connected medical tools. Advanced solutions for IoMT technology seek to streamline device communication, data exchange and integration and patient monitoring.

Wearable biosensors have now become important in life-extending technologies, which facilitate tracking of health conditions. Such devices, which can be attached on the body of the individual or into fabrics, are used for monitoring of various physiological activities of the human body, including heart rate, blood glucose level, and respiratory movement. New developments in fabrics, composites, and coating, have improved wearability and biosensitivity of wearable biosensors. Such devices afford the means by which individuals can always monitor their health status and take necessary action, based on the data acquired.

There is an escalating growth in remote therapeutics and telemedicine due to emerging and existing technologies in communications and virtual care. Thus, telemedicine stations facilitate consultations and diagnostics, as well as in defining a treatment strategy; therapeutic tele-devices involve the subject in a direct, individual interaction. Advancements in Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR) technologies are improving accessibility of and support for remote therapeutic interventions. The use of telemedicine, in conjunction with diagnostic and therapeutic equipment, is to introduce new solutions that are important for the development of medicine, increase patients’ attendance, and contribute to better management of chronic diseases.

Biomedical sensors and devices remain a critical enabler of personalized and precision health since they offer highly specific and individualized health information.

Molecular diagnostics and genomic analysis are rapidly advancing fields in healthcare, enabling the development of personalized treatment strategies tailored to individual patients.
