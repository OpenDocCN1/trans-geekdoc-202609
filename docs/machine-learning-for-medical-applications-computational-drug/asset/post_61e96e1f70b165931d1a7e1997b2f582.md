OCT is used mostly in ophthalmology applications that help in the diagnosis of various diseases through imaging the retina and the anterior segment of the eye, including macular degeneration, glaucoma, and diabetic retinopathy. The technology has also been used in other medical specialties such as cardiology and dermatology because it allows for high-resolution imaging of tissue characteristics.

Endoscopic cameras are particularly developed for observing the cavities and organs of the human body using minimally invasive techniques. These cameras are placed on a flexible endoscope inserted into the body via a natural duct or surgical opening. In endoscopy, fiber optic cameras transmit pictures of the digestive tract, respiratory system, and other body cavities to monitors for further examination, diagnosis, and treatment of various diseases. Modern types of endoscopic equipment, including high-definition and narrow-band imaging, have improved the diagnostic and therapeutic capacities of these instruments.

Electromyography (EMG) sensors are devices that record the electrical activity of muscles and nerves, and so are useful to diagnose neuromuscular disorders. EMG sensors can be used to measure the strength of a muscle, identify abnormalities in electrical activity, and determine the condition of a nerve. Information acquired from EMG testing plays a role in identifying diseases, including muscular dystrophy, peripheral neuropathy, and carpal tunnel syndrome. Recent technological developments carried out on EMG include portability and wearable technology, where EMG has been advanced and is used to give a broad basis for the evaluation of muscles and nerves.

### 2.5 Therapeutic devices

Therapeutic devices are technologies used to diagnose and combat different ailments and illnesses. Such devices have significant value in various aspects of patient care since they are used in providing optimized treatment schedules, boosting individual patient recovery, and facilitating continuing healthcare in the community. Therapeutic devices have undergone tremendous developments over the last few decades and have increased the prospects of medical procedures.

Drug delivery systems are complex structures intended for the delivery of drugs to the patient's body and tissue in a calibrated and precisely measured manner. These systems include the infusion pump that gives a continuous delivery of such things as insulin and antibiotic agents. The other forms of advanced drug delivery involve biodegradable implants, where the material used releases the drug in a controlled manner on an agreed timetable without the need for further injections. Examples of systems include microneedles and transdermal patches, making drug delivery less invasive, thereby improving patient satisfaction. These systems are designed to work as efficiently as possible in terms of the intended therapeutic effects without compromising with its side effects, and give the best results possible for the kind of treatment needed by the patient.

There are physiotherapy and rehabilitation aids that are meant to supplement the physiotherapy process for patients with musculoskeletal conditions that require physiotherapy. These devices include the robotic rehabilitation systems aimed at assisting motor rehabilitation and repetitive motion tasks. Other examples include exoskeleton wearable assistive devices for locomotion and posture, and therapeutic ultrasound for tissue repair and pain relief. Through sensors incorporated in the rehabilitation equipment, patient's progress can be closely observed and the treatment adapted to the requirements of a particular client.

Obstructive sleep apnea is treated using Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) machines as a method of controlling pauses in breathing during sleep. CPAP devices provide ongoing pressure of air through a facial mask on the nose and avoid the occurrence of apneas. Today's CPAP devices have features that include auto-titrating, which adjusts the pressure depending on the feedback given at that time, and the capability of connecting the devices to other devices for monitoring and sharing of information. As we have highlighted, CPAP use enhances sleep quality, thereby minimizing the necessity of additional sleep and the diseases related to it, such as cardiovascular disease.

Electrostimulation products are used to deliver electrical signals to damaged nerves for medical treatment. Such devices include TENS that is used in order to lessen pain by manipulation of nerve contractions, and NMES used in muscle remodeling and building up. Neurostimulators include spinal cord stimulation (SCS) and deep brain stimulators (DBS), together with implants that are used as treatments involving electrostimulation in specific areas of the nervous system, mostly for the management of chronic pain and certain neurological disorders. Employing different parameters of electrical stimulation results in the development of a specific therapy plan to meet the needs of the patient and improve the treatment outcome.

Medical man-made organ implants are sophisticated devices used for the treatment of ailing organs. Such devices include mechanical ventricular pumps (left ventricular assist devices) that help patients who have severe heart failure with mechanical support. Artificial kidneys (dialysis machines) replace the function of filtering blood in cases of renal failure, and artificial lungs (extracorporeal membrane oxygenation systems) supply limited respiratory support to patients with critical respiratory illnesses. Artificial organs are in the process of providing hope and a means between organ failure and transplantation, as well as significantly enhancing the quality of life of those suffering from critical organ breakdown.

## 3 Functionality and applications

### 3.1 Vital signs monitoring

Supplementary to what has been discussed, the recording of the vital signs is a core part of the working process since it involves essential information of a patient's body functions. This practice makes it possible to spot trends that in turn point toward a medical condition that needs treatment, as well as management of chronic illnesses. Significant development exists in sensor technology, thereby strengthening the efficiency of vital signs monitoring, contributing to positive patient outcomes as well as the enhancement of healthcare systems.

Heart rate and ECG are two important parameters in diagnosing cardiovascular disorders. Pulse oximeters measure the number of pulse repetitions in one minute, giving details of the pattern and efficiency of the heart. Smartwatches and chest straps provide prolonged monitoring and analysis of the wearer's heart rate. ECG, on the other hand, records electrical activity of the heart such as heart rate irregularities, lack of blood supply to the heart muscles, and other heart-related ailments. The complex ECG devices help in diagnosing and monitoring various heart conditions, as well as offering detailed evaluations, apart from maintaining long-term records of the patient.

Tracking of the pressure within the blood vessels is relevant in evaluating the overall heart health and treating ailments like hypertension. Digital monitors track blood pressure automatically and give accurate systolic and diastolic values to enable frequent checks and effective treatment. There is a need to monitor SpO₂ using pulse oximeters as a means of assessing respiratory compromise and, specifically, hypoxemia. A pulse oximeter operates through the use of infrared light, which is used to estimate the percentage of oxygenated Hb and is very useful for monitoring respiratory diseases as well as for oxygen therapy.

Respiratory rate measurement is a significant means of assessing respiratory health as well as general health status. By observing the respiratory rate over time, the client is easily able to identify any abnormalities such as tachypnea, which is indicative of a number of underlying health problems.
