# 1 Introduction

High throughput screening (HTS) is applied as the key method in the search for new medical materials and drugs [1, 2]. Through the automated process of testing large databases of compounds, HTS expedites the discovery process of potentially useful compounds with references to therapeutic potential [3]. Formerly, HTS relied on robotic systems, which was applied to thousands of samples at a time and created a large data [4]. Raw research data at this scale too presented a problem in as much as analysis and interpretation was concerned [5]. In order to overcome these challenges, scientists use machine learning (ML) as an effective tool for improving HTS performance [6]. With the integration of MLP-based algorithms to the HTS workflows, it became easier to perform the data analysis while enhancing the predictive power and increasing the efficiency of discovering the materials [7].

Machine learning (ML) has lately become paramount to data analysis in HTS due to its advanced ability at pattern recognition and predictive modeling [8]. Due to the use of data mining, ML algorithms can handle the data load quantities and distinguish patterns or causations not easily discovered by other conventional analytical means [9]. These capabilities are considered most useful in material discovery where predicting properties and performance of new found materials prior to physical testing definitely reduces costs [11]. They also evolve and become more efficient as more data is fed into them, hence the added bonus of a speeding up of the screening process [11].

This integration also attends to several issues that are associated with the handling of data and understanding of the models that are deployed during HTS [12]. As was seen in HTS, the amount of emergent data is vast, limiting the ability to obtain informative results without the assistance of sophisticated computational software [13, 14]. To deal with this issue, with an aim of optimizing learning, [ML techniques] tend to alleviate this complexity through aspects like dimensionality reduction and feature selection [15]. Further, interpretability becomes an essential issue, which is critical when assessing the results of the ML to determine if it would work in the real world [16]. This chapter discusses several approaches to increase model interpretability and nonexpert use of ML in HTS [17].

In addition to streamlining the screening processes and enhancing data interpretation, excellent results that ML-enabled HTS have shown are its capabilities to go beyond typical limitations of traditional approaches to material discovery [18]. For example, ML algorithms are used for the accurate result prediction of the screening experiments that are involved in extensive and boring physical testing [19]. It is in this aspect that the use of new computer simulation capabilities can be utilized to predict and top up the list of the more possible lists for new potential inventions to be explored in order to shorten the development period and bring down the expenses [20]. Using case studies, the chapter describes how innovation has been achieved through ML-driven HTS even in the field of medical materials, revealing the opportunities of the approach to material science and drug discovery [21].
