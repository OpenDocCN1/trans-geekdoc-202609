Issues relating to privacy and security pose great influence to the acceptability of biomedical devices, especially those that entail information through mobile technologies. It is also important that users feel that their data is handled and protected from unwanted access hacks. Accurate data management procedures, strong encryption procedures, and a transparent privacy parasol are prerequisites to garner the trust of the users. Adherence to legal requirements like GDPR and HIPAA is also useful to discharge responsibilities regarding protection of users’ privacy to boost the confidence of the device users.

But in this case, the extent and the cost of the biomedical devices can affect the modem acceptance and compliance of such gadgets. The high costs consequently affects the usage of devices within specific demographics, and as a result, obstructs its usage across the general public. Other costs, such as those that are recurrent by way of maintenance and cost of consumables, are also important factors affecting usage by users. Some of the challenges include cost of accessing devices, insurance, and/or subsidies for a device, which can be scaled to meet the user’s need at a cheaper price.

Biomedical devices cannot be utilized properly if they cannot be easily fit into the lifestyles of the users. Acceptance rates are lower for devices that demand radical alteration of existing activities of the users. This integration can be improved by standardizing devices according to the lifestyles of the users as well as by providing clear and optimizable feedback. Simple functions like auto report generation, low interference from users, and adaptability with other technologies like smart phones help integrate easily and ensure compliance.

# 5.4 Cost and accessibility

Pricing and ease of access are the main barriers toward popular adoption and deployment of state-of-art biomedical sensors and devices. These affect the deployment of technology across the regions and the population in general and hence the overall efficiency of health services.

These critical biomedical sensors and devices are developed and produced through heavy capital investment.
