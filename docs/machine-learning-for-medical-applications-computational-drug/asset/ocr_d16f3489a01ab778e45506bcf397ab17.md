# 1 Introduction

It is important and essential in health systems to be able to share medical data to encourage stakeholders’ collaboration, to build quests, as well as to enhance patients’ outcomes [1, 2]. Their acquisition is greatly complicated by many factors that cause challenges to their smooth flow [3]. The traditional ways of passing information based on the realm of hierarchical relational data patterns that in some cases are centered on consolidating data repositories for the distributed systems, face these challenges mostly from the aspect of modality of data integration [4]. Such barriers affect coordination and integration, which are requisite for effective caring and sharing of information in caregiving practices [5]. Besides these, growing concerns for data accuracy and privacy show lack of better methods that can protect personal medical information [6]. Because of the daily new cases surrounding hackers and unauthorized access to medical data that are reported, there is need to find new innovative solutions pertaining to the privacy-preserving health information exchange [7].

Privacy and data integrity are the two cornerstone features in health information that present standards governing the privacy of patient information [8]. Privacy allows the individual the discretion of who has access to his/her information on health; it also aims at preventing unauthorized disclosure of information [9]. Ensuring that patient’s identity cannot be easily identified is important before people can be comfortable with the idea of placing their “faith” in a doctor-patient relationship, as well as in the healthcare field where private and personal details of an individual vary from one person to the other. Privacy should be well-protected more so since infringement of this right has extreme repercussions that go to the extent of causing devastating loss of reputation in addition to the legal consequences a person is bound to face as a result [10].

In similar respect, so as to guarantee that medical records are coherent, accurate, and reliable in the duration of one’s lifetime, integrity of data has to be upheld [11]. Information that is inaccurate usually leads to wrong diagnosis or wrong treatment subjecting the patient to high risk [12]. Managers use more data in their firm’s healthcare decisions, and consequently, data must be accurate and reliable for clinical decision purposes [13]. Interference with the data in any way negates the basis of evidence-based medicines and undermines the oft-proclaimed health system integrity [14].

Because such targets are closely related to healthcare organizations, it is essential to set adequate procedures regarding data accuracy and privacy in those enterprises [15]. This suggests UAV control through employing highly secure measures that reduce chances of data leakages and prohibition of other unfavorable associated access tools including, audit trails, access to limits, and encryption [16]. With respect to data security and integrity, blockchain technologies provide potential directions for creating a decentralized, non-tampering blockchain to handle efficient transactional records and data assurance [17].
