First developed as a diagnostic tool in the mid-twentieth century, ultrasound imaging has undergone many developments since the 1990s. Sound waves of high frequency are utilized in recent ultrasonic technology to yield real-time images of internal structures. Progress in Doppler imaging and 3D/4D ultrasound has increased the number of fields in which it is possible to apply this equipment, for the assessment of organ function, blood circulation, and fetal growth.

A major development was in imaging technologies in the 2000s with the shift from film to digital in this technology. The advancement of the diagnosis procedure has been made, radiation dosage has been reduced, and the pictures obtained have been made clearer by the use of digital radiography, computed radiography, and digital mammography. EHRs have been implemented, and there was consolidation of digital imaging, which has facilitated enhancement of data stewardship and retrieval.

In the 2010s, machine learning and artificial intelligence in medical imaging have advanced to a new level. The use of AI algorithms and machine learning models for automatic image analysis for the identification of diseases and diagnostic support was progressing. All of these technologies offer the potential for individualized therapy, enhanced diagnostic methods, and refined means to better analyze visual representations.
