Liposomal formulations are considered as valuable drug delivery systems that are distinguished by their open and closed lipid layers containing active substances. ML is used effectively to enhance the release kinetics of liposomal formulation based on bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy. By incorporating the experimental data on the physicochemical properties of liposomes, such as composition and drug loading, supervised learning models allow estimation of the time-course release profiles of the drug in different physiological environments. Statistical models and neural networks measure coefficients between formulation attributes and release profiles to effectively increase liposomal life span, release duration, and bioavailability.

Injectable systems consist of implantable polymers that provide long-term drug delivery to tissues of interest while minimizing side effects on the rest of the body. Advanced signal processing and regression techniques are employed to model and enhance biomaterials and implantable devices, enabling the prediction of how drug molecules interact with encapsulating matrices for controlled release. Biophysical characteristics such as device size, drug dissolution rate, and tissue response are analyzed using decision tree algorithms and support vector machines (SVMs) to optimize release profiles over extended durations. ML algorithms have assisted in the deployment of PM approaches in oncology through giving customized drug release profiles based on given patient and tumor attributes. Using a multiomics approach combining genomic information, biomarker signature, and clinical data, ML-based models define chemotherapeutic regimens and drug release profiles associated with patient characteristics. Multidimensional datasets are fed to the neural networks and deep learning architectures to sort the patient populations based on treatment response and disease prognosis indicators, to fine-tune the method of dosing and timing of the application of therapies and drugs. These targeted approaches improve therapy effectiveness and reduced toxicity, and support precision medicine projects based on the timely alterations to the drug delivery system.

Polymeric nanoparticles are preferred for many therapeutic therapies as the material is able to offer a controllable release mechanism and be tasked with targeted direction delivery. To further enhance nanoparticle formulation, ML-integrated techniques are employed in the prediction of particle size distribution, surface modification, and encapsulation rates.
