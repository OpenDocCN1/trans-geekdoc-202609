# ## 6.2 Balancing biocompatibility with functionality

A major challenge in the use and development of biocompatible materials lies in achieving an optimal balance between biocompatibility and functionality. While biocompatibility ensures that the material interacts safely with biological systems, functionality pertains to the material’s performance and effectiveness in its intended biomedical application. Striking this balance is particularly critical in the design of medical devices and implants, where both safety and operational efficiency are essential for successful clinical outcomes. Figure 11 illustrates the trade-off and interdependence between biocompatibility and functionality, emphasizing the need for materials that do not compromise one aspect in favor of the other.

# ### 6.2.1 Trade-offs between biocompatibility and mechanical properties

The first issue that was critical was the task of adjusting the biocompatibility and mechanical properties of the material. For example, materials that can be used in orthopedic implants must have the features of high strength and high durability since they work under mechanical load. The development of these mechanical properties can be problematic because they create inflammations in neighboring tissues. Thus, material scientists have to pay attention to the material design and selection such that the mechanical strength is good enough but does not produce disruptive impacts on the body.
