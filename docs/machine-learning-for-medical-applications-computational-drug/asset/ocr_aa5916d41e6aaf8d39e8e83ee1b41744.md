# Optical biosensors

Optical biosensors work by using light to identify any variation in the biological processes. These sensors generally use surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and fluorescence instrumental methods for evaluating the bioreceptor’s analyte binding events. Some features of optical biosensors include high sensitivity, so there is no need for a label and thus the possibility to monitor the process in real-time, for example, in the case of pathogen detection, where changes in the optical property show the presence of certain bacteria. Optical biosensors are widely used in genomics and proteomics for the identification of targeted nucleic acid in a heterogeneous background.

# Piezoelectric biosensors

Piezoelectric biosensors utilize the piezoelectric effect, which occurs when an electrical charge is generated in a piezoelectric material due to mechanical strain. In these biosensors, the binding of a biological analyte to the bioreceptor causes a change in mass on the surface of the piezoelectric material, leading to a shift in its resonant frequency. This type of biosensor allows biomolecular interactions to be monitored in real time with high sensitivity. For example, it can be used to identify biomolecules such as proteins, where the frequency shift is proportional to the analyte concentration, enabling quantitative measurement of biological fluids.

# Biosensors in healthcare

The demand for biosensors in healthcare remains immense because they allow for accurate, fast, and highly localized measurement. They are used in point-of-care diagnostics as a medical instrument for regular and at-home screening, including glucose monitoring for diabetes, cholesterol tests, and some screening for infections. Biosensors are also used in the field of personalized medicine, so that enablers can control the particular biomarkers to address targeted patients. Besides, biosensors are used in drug development and clinical research to give information on drug reactions and drug effectiveness. The continuous innovation in biosensor technology such as advances in multi-biosensor and biosensors integrated with digital health technologies is anticipated to continue to increase their use in the health sector.

Biosensors are a promising and developing category of biomedical sensors, where improvements in the sensors’ performance and potential uses are continually being pursued.
