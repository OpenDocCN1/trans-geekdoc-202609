# 6.1.2 Calibration procedures

Calibration refers to the process of aligning a sensor's output with a precise, measurable reference value. This involves using reference devices to compare and adjust the sensor's readings, ensuring continued accuracy over time. As shown in Figure 10, periodic calibration is essential to maintain sensor performance, especially in the face of environmental changes, component wear, and signal drift from original settings. The primary goal is to guarantee accurate data output, and when discrepancies occur, adjustments are made based on standard reference values. Regular calibration enhances the reliability of biomedical sensors and underscores the importance of robust, validated calibration procedures.
