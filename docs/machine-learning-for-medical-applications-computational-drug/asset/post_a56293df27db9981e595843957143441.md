
## 2.4 Transition from analog to digital imaging

From an analog film to an analog format to a digital film and finally becoming a digital format was the major change in medical image processing, thus posing a major change in the acquisition, processing, and analysis involved in medical image processing. Predigital diagnostic imaging technologies, for example, X-ray and other modalities using films, relied on the use of photographic films to capture images. These films, as with the advent of film in the first place, came with some disadvantages such as low dynamic range, not being resistant to artifacts and a need for the storage medium to be physical.

Figure 4: Transition from analog to digital imaging.

Different generations of digital imaging technologies have been considered a great development in medical imaging. A digital imaging system acquires images electronically through the use of sensors and then processes them into signals. This shift from analog to digital imaging introduced several key improvements:

- **Enhanced image quality and resolution:** Digital imaging systems are seen to have better imaging results than analog ones. CCDs and photostimulable phosphor plates have better definition and are more conspicuous compared to film for diagnosis. The option of a digital format also enables changes to contrast and brightness of an image to be made without losing quality, thus increasing the value of diagnostic imaging.
- **Increased efficiency and speed:** Digital imaging applies to faster image acquisition and image processing techniques. While we have fields that always use analog films, which require the use of chemicals in their development, on the other hand, images can be easily obtained digitally, and these obtained images are easier to review. This efficiency cuts the time needed for diagnosis and improves operational flow within care structures, therefore accelerating patient care.
- **Advanced image processing capabilities:** Digital imaging provides the possibility to apply a vast number of advanced image processing techniques. Digital images can be conditioned, for example, for noise reduction and edge detection enhancement, so they can be better analyzed and interpreted. This capability is especially noticeable during processes like the automated detection of abnormalities and the measurement of body structures.
- **Ease of storage and retrieval:** Digital photographs should be electronically saved, reducing the odds of a picture getting lost, and no physical storage is necessary. PACS, an electronic storage system, is suited for managing archives and recalling images quickly. It helps in retaining medical records for a long time and allows easy retrieval of images to be used in providing comparisons.
- **Integration with other technologies:** The digital modality of medical images provides interoperability with other IT systems. Digital imaging data can be linked with EHRs, where patients can be managed digitally from the start. It is also possible to share digital images in networks and apply telemedicine and consulting.
- **Development of advanced imaging modalities:** The shift to digital equipment provided a basis for improving current imaging techniques. Nowadays, such methods have become available as digital subtraction angiography, digital mammography, and CT, which provide the possibility of improving diagnostic abilities and increasing the accuracy of treatment.

## 2.5 Impact of computational advances on image processing

Computational upgrades have dramatically influenced medical image processing information, ensuring extensive enhancement across the category of imaging systems. In its beginning, medical image processing was hampered by first-generation computational devices that set upper bounds on the sophistication of image analysis algorithms and the amount of image data that was manageable. With the advancement in computational power and the development of better algorithms, the potential to provide better interpretational analysis of medical images steadily escalated.

Advances in higher-performing and energy-efficient processors have made it possible to execute sophisticated image processing algorithms, which were heretofore impossible. Recent improvements in computational power have enabled the use of sophisticated computational techniques including image enlargement, image clearing from noise, and feature detection. All of these enhancements have a clear connection to an increase in image definition and the overall diagnostic capabilities, which result in lower probabilities of mistakes when interpreting medical images.

Through parallel processing and high-speed computers, the processing of large numbers of image data has become faster. Huge data volumes are produced in fields such as CT and MRI, which have represented great value here. These strong computational performances allow for the analysis and interpretation of medical pictures at a speed and with an accuracy never achieved before, due to new methods of computational intelligence such as machine learning and deep learning.

GPUs, originally designed to draw graphics for computer games, are currently one of the most suitable devices to perform the parallel computations necessary for deep learning and image processing. There are numerous calculations that can be performed at once, and it has been made feasible to train large NNs and perform sophisticated image analytical tasks.

## 3 Basics of machine learning

### 3.1 Definition and key concepts of machine learning

One of the subdomains of artificial intelligence, called “machine learning,” focuses on developing algorithms that allow computers to make conclusions based on data analysis. It involves developing models for which code needs not to be written for each application; instead, it should be able to understand and infer. If one asked an expert in artificial intelligence in the 1980s what they were doing, they would say that machine learning was the field founded on the idea that systems improve efficiency through experiences gathered from data analysis.

- **Algorithms:** The basic building blocks of learning systems are machine learning algorithms. Mathematical analysis is used to generate patterns and predict future occurrences, and developed learned data, including SVMs and linear regression, together with unsupervised learning techniques, including principal component analysis, are the main roles of algorithms. It becomes clearer when explaining that the kind of issue being solved and the characteristics of the data determine the best method.

- **Models:** A model is an algorithm that describes a real-life process by using data that has been put into an equation. A machine learning model is developed from previous experience in an attempt to determine patterns and further plan. Consequently, the generalization from the training data to new data unseen by the model is used to determine its performance.

- **Training and Testing:** To reduce the error associated with the system, a machine learning model was trained by providing it with data to work on. This also enabled the model to automatically tune its parameters. Validation checks the efficiency of the model through a completely unknown set of data that was not involved in the development of solutions. This facilitates measuring the performance of how well the model fits new data.

- **Features and labels:** The variables upon which the model depends when making its predictions are known as features. The values that the model is targeted to predict are referred to as labels. For instance, in medical imaging, extracting features can mean identifying a pattern or pixel values in an image, while labels can indicate whether a disease is present.

This was due to the fact that both overfitting and underfitting occur when a model becomes too specialized in reading the intricate details and sometimes noise of the training data set, only to perform poorly when it is tested on new data. If a model is too simple to pinpoint patterns in the data, then there is underfitting of the model. These two factors indicate that model complexity and generalization must be optimized if the model is to achieve the best results.

- **Supervised versus unsupervised learning:** Supervised learning indeed involves training the model using data that already has a result that needs to be predicted. It is used in problems such as regression and classification. A primary goal of unsupervised learning consists of making use of data that is not categorized in an attempt to find various forms of clustering. It is used in dimensionality reduction and clustering.
