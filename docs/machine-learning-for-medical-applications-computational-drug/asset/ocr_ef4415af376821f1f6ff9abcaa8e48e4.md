Precision medicine in oncology: In the context of cancer, targeted drug delivery concepts are designed to address treatment resistance due to tumor heterogeneity and heterogeneity of the neoplastic cell population. In this aspect, genomics sequencing data, tumor biomarkers, and imaging diagnostics incorporate the ML algorithms in order to determine drug sensitivity and resistance mechanism and also the best duration of the treatment. Because drug release profiles are made to fit features unique to tumors and patient responses, personal medicine projects ultimately improve the overall life of cancer patients, reduce the toxic side effects of therapy, and increase survival.

Adaptive drug release strategies: Smart pill delivery systems that use ML algorithms in real-time monitoring enabling real-time control of drug release rates to counter changes in the patient’s physiology or therapeutically needed doses. Thus, through constant receiving of data from wearable sensors, bioassays of biofluid samples, and imaging techniques, the ML models can alter the drug delivery parameters to achieve therapeutic levels within the desired window and avoid the “on off/on off” effect of systemic drug variation. Designed to maximize treatment outcomes, they also improve patients’ comfort and provide individualized approach to chronic diseases and urgent care.

Clinical translation and regulatory considerations: To this end, the use of customized drug delivery systems requires compliance with regulatory requirements and bureaucratic standards as well as applicable ethical principles for the protection of patients’ data and optimization of treatment outcomes. Clinicians and biomedical engineers work with regulatory bodies in meeting the standards in the practice and improvement of innovative practice in personalized medicine. These pieces of evidence help support the concept of POC and bring drug delivery technologies into the clinic and standard of care.

# 3.5 Regulatory challenges and opportunities in ML-driven drug release optimization

The incorporation of ML into drug release optimization is a monumental leap in drug development providing infinite possibilities to modify therapeutic effectiveness and, thus, patients’ lives. Alongside the possibilities offered for development by ML-driven approaches, these approaches face challenges relating to regulation that determines how they are implemented, verified, and deployed within clinical environments. Knowledge of these regulations as well as potential benefits is crucial in finding out how to implement ML to the drug delivery systems most efficiently.

Regulatory framework and compliance standards are crucial in the global deployment of medications, which must adhere to stringent guidelines set by international health authorities such as the European Medicines Agency (EMA), the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and their counterparts worldwide.
