# 5.4.2 ICP monitors

ICP sensors are essential for patients developing certain conditions, such as traumatic brain injury (TBI). These sensors keep a check on the pressure that is exerted inside the cranial cavity and contain helpful information about the condition of the brain. Today's ICP monitors are made with sophisticated materials and technologies to provide a high degree of precision and durability. Sometimes, they have wireless features and connectivity with other monitoring systems, which enable intracranial pressure to be monitored continually and from a distance.

# 5.4.3 Brain-computer interface (BCI)

BCIs are a new form of neurological monitoring that enable the brain to interface with other devices. BCIs rely on different modes of sensing, including electroencephalogram and implantable sensors, which help in identifying the brain signals. These systems are employed to embrace individuals with quadriplegic disorders and others who are not capable of voluntary movement; the technique allows manual control of assistive technology, solely through brain exercises. Current innovations in BCI concern itself with how the signals are made finer as well as how the hardware maybe rendered more comfortable and how the new generation of prosthetic devices as well as communication appliances are incorporated into the system.

# 5.4.4 Portable electrical stimulation apparatus

Portable neurostimulation devices also provide a unique paradigm to neurological monitoring and treatment. Treatment tools employ electrical currents to adjust the putative neuronal activity to help patients with conditions such as chronic pain, depression, and Parkinson's disease. New trends in wearable neurostimulation are emerging; these include but are not limited to miniature, easily wearable devices, which can be used for continuous stimulation. Many of these devices also include some sort of apparatus for measuring physical responses to stimulation and adjust the therapy regimen according to the feedback received.
