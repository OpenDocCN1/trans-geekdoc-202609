# 6.3 LIME (local interpretable model-agnostic explanations)  
for understanding material models

LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations) is one of the most widely adopted techniques in Explainable AI (XAI), designed to reveal how machine learning models make specific predictions. In materials science, where models often involve complex, nonlinear relationships between material properties and outcomes, LIME proves invaluable by offering interpretable, instance-level explanations. It enables researchers and practitioners to understand individual model decisions by approximating the model behavior locally around a specific prediction. This transparency enhances trust in material models, supports informed decision-making, and fosters societal acceptance of AI-driven material design. # 6.3.1 Mechanism of LIME

LIME, on the other hand, approximates complex, nontransparent models with simpler, more comprehensible models in specific circumstances in order to explain decisions. The original approach was to create a series of samples that are perturbed around a particular occurrence in a similar way to make predictions for a given data set. The behavior of the complicated model in the vicinity of the targeted instance was then refined by using these samples to train a locally explainable model, such as a decision tree. Thus, the interpretable model generated provides information that is easier for end-users to understand, including which factors were most influential in making the forecast for the specific instance, after learning more about the model’s decision-making process.
