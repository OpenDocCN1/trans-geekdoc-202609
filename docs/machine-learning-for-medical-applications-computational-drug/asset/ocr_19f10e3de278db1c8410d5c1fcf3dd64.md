# 6.2 Predictive modeling for toxicity assessment

Toxicogenomics is a new and valuable tool in preclinical testing; predictive modeling of toxicity assessment helps to determine possible adverse effects of drug candidates at the initial stages of the drug development process. These models employ AI methods to parse structural arrangements, biological information, and chemical characteristics to predict and rank potential compounds to possess benign toxicology to facilitate their subsequent examination.

Chemical structure- and physicochemical property-based toxicity prediction represents a vital application of predictive modeling in modern toxicity assessment. To decode the complex relationships between chemical descriptors and toxicity endpoints, various machine learning algorithms including Random Forests, Support Vector Machines (SVMs), and Neural Networks are applied to datasets comprising compounds with known toxicological profiles. These models enable the prediction of toxic effects, such as hepatotoxicity and cardiotoxicity, based on features like molecular structure, key molecular fingerprints, and other physicochemical properties. As illustrated in Figure 14, such predictive models are instrumental in identifying potentially hazardous compounds early in the drug development pipeline.

The accuracy and robustness of toxicity predictions and the toxicity assessment by predictive modeling enable the incorporation of many types of data such as animal, clinical, and in vitro toxicological data. The algorithms can accumulate information from different sources and, depending on them, reveal certain risks that can be left unnoticed if an individual dataset was analyzed. This integrative approach to toxicity assessment improves the assurance of the compound safety profile; it advances the decision-making process for drug development.
