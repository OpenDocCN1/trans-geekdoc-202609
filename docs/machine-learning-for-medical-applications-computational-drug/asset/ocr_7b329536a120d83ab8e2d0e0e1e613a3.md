# 3.1 Wearable sensors for continuous monitoring

Wearable sensors have recently emerged as a vital component of continuous health assessment due to their advantages in real-time and efficient monitoring of an individual's well-being. Designed to be integrated into various wearable devices, these sensors continuously measure key physiological parameters. # Types of wearable sensors

Wearable sensors cover a large range of technologies based on individual health monitoring requirements. Common types include:

+   - **Accelerometers and gyroscopes:** The first kind of sensors is responsible for the detection of motion and position, for monitoring of the physical activity, for analysis of gait, and for position correction. They are popular in wrist-wearable devices for tracking exercise and the overall activity during exercise sessions.
+   - **Photoplethysmograms (PPG):** PPG sensors optically measure motion, reflecting changes in blood flow and offering details of heart rate, blood oxygen, and stress. They are normally used in smartwatches and fitness trackers.
+   - **ECG:** ECG sensors are used to record the electrical impulses of the heart; they can record complications in heart rates and health. Technology-enhanced wearables today have features such as ECG scanners in watches for continuous monitoring of heart and any abnormality.
+   - **Thermistors and thermocouples:** These sensors determine the temperature within the body and across the skin to help determine fever, hypothermia, and such other heat-related disorders.
