MEMS technology offers several advantages in biomedical applications.

Miniaturization: MEMS sensors are different from traditional sensors in that they are much smaller, enabling wearable / implantable applications. This miniaturization improves patients’ comfort and allows overall supervision without interrupting their normal lives.

High sensitivity and accuracy: MEMS sensors are highly sensitive and accurate in measuring physiological parameters. Since wireless MEMS technology for acquiring signals is extremely accurate, it provides accurate data needed for diagnosing and managing illnesses.

Low power consumption: MEMS devices are extremely low-power devices, thus they are well suitable in battery-powered wearable devices. This low power consumption has the effect of extending the useful life of equipment and lowering the required maintenance frequency.

# Possible future development trends of MEMS

The future of MEMS technology in biomedical applications is poised for further innovation:

Integration with AI and Machine learning: AI and machine learning are expected to improve MEMS sensor data analysis and prediction, with the integration of MEMS sensors with the technologies. This integration has led to intelligent as well as adaptive health monitoring systems.

Advanced materials and manufacturing techniques: Current research on microsystems includes study on the technology of MEMS with enhanced parameters such as performance, biocompatibility, and durability of the devices. Development in such areas increases the variety of uses and improves the performance characteristics of the MEMS-based sensors.

Expansion into personalized medicine: MEMS technology is expected to be included in the field of personal medicine; thus, health-monitoring services could be provided according to the patients’ profile and requirements.

MEMS have ended the conventional approaches of biomedical sensors, which have enabled the development of compact, accurate, multifunctional modalities for monitoring health. MEMS technology is likely to evolve and progress in the near future, which open new options for biomedical sensors as instruments for patients’ treatment and helpers in medical experiment.
