Real-time data given by biomedical sensors is contributing to the concept of personalized medicine. These sensors enable healthcare practitioners to adapt treatment schedules to the need of every patient because personal data from patients is constantly collected and assessed. Thus customization reduces the odds of episodes and stays in the hospital because it enhances the efficacy of treatment and brings better ways to handle chronic diseases.

That biomedical sensors enable telemedicine and remotely monitored care makes healthcare more effective. Due to the home monitoring the patient can get better and easier treatment instead of having to visit hospital frequently. This improves the availability of healthcare and lessens the pressure from general illnesses on the health facilities for consumers from other regions.

Also biomedical sensors are critical for enhancing safety outcomes for patients and serving better clinical goals. The sensors that measure the vital signs like blood oxygen levels and pulse rate for instance, give a signal to the healthcare providers, of the changes in the health state of the patient and trigger an appropriate reaction and action. When the patient is actively managed, adverse consequences are averted, and necessary treatment can always be provided on time.

Further, the use of biomedical sensors with EHR systems helps optimal health informatics. The continuous flow of data from the sensors to EHR systems enables health trends to be monitored, better diagnostics to be made, and care givers to effectively and efficiently coordinate their efforts.

Real-time, constant, and noninvasive biomedical sensors are vital in today's healthcare environment because of their potential to contribute to individualization and distance treatment, patient protection, and data control. All of these are integrative to enhance the approach towards healthcare improvement as well as the realization of improved health status.
