An assessment of the quality of biomaterial datasets was necessary for establishing their quality, reliability, and suitability for use in ML. Quality evaluation can be performed in terms of reliability, relevance, bias, and inconsistency in the data. Completeness and, for example, error rate for a set of samples are the main measures that should be prioritized to evaluate dataset quality. The checking of the tested data against more conservative standards and the inclusion of published articles further improve the reliability of the dataset. Stringent quality control steps are required to be put in place to preserve data integrity so that the datasets upon which we train as well as test our ML models are credible.

# 2.5.2 The use of open-source datasets to advance biomaterials science

The development of open-source datasets was crucial in biomaterials research since it involves providing and sharing usable data with the community. One important characteristic of open-source data is the collaboration and rapid progress enabled by researchers' ability to use existing datasets and contribute new results. Some examples of open-source platforms containing biomaterial data include the Open Materials Database and the Data Commons project, where people can access and use the “stuff” data in a more comprehensive way. Such platforms usually feature tools for data visualization and analysis, making them even more suitable for ML endeavors. Encouraging a culture of open data for biomaterials can reduce data shortages, enhance the development of novel open-source datasets for use in ML, and improve the accuracy and flexibility of existing biomaterial-related ML models.

Exploiting Dataset Bias in Machine Learning Models’ explores the impact of dataset biases on the performance and generalizability of machine learning systems.

Bias in the datasets was a major problem with ML, especially when used in the biomaterials context. Bias evolved from sampling proportions, incomplete sample coverage, and the exclusion of certain material conditions. Biases of this nature can result in the development of a model that is inaccurate in its nature and exhibits a low level of ability for the devised ML algorithm to be applied in other areas. Dataset bias was addressed by adopting measures to achieve balance in biomaterial type and experimental condition representation. This was because methods such as data augmentation, oversampling, as well as the use of heterogeneously synthesized datasets reduce bias, thereby enhancing the general performance of a model. Making documentation on datasets more transparent, as well as scheduling audits, can help in the proper characterization of bias and thus aid in evolving fair and accurate new learning systems for biomaterials.
