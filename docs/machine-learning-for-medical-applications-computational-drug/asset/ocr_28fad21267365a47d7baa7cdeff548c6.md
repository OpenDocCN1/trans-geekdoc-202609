Informed Consent: The use of data for training machine learning models ethically requires obtaining the consent of the data subjects. This includes explaining how the data can be utilized, the dangers involved, and the advantages of the research. The consent process must be explained in detail so that individuals have an opportunity to refuse.

Compliance with Regulations: Like other drug discovery and development models, the machine learning models for drug toxicity have to meet specific regulatory requirements and guidelines from the FDA and EMA. These regulations govern the assessment and approval of prediction models for safety and other performance criteria before they are used in clinical practice.

Validation and Verification: To ensure that machines are learning correct and reliable information and that the models are valid, regulatory bodies require validation and verification. This includes presenting the outcomes of applying the model to diverse datasets, documenting the validation process, and providing evidence of how the model performs across different conditions.

Documentation and Reporting: Auditable evidence of the development process, model parameters, and validation must be available as part of applications to the regulatory authorities. This enhances the general understanding of the intended use of the model as well as its compliance with the set regulatory requirements.

Post-Market Surveillance: Another element that can be learned is that after the model has been put to use, AI must be constantly monitored for its performance, efficiency, and safety. Monitoring and updating occur post-market, where the user provides feedback, monitors specific model outcomes, and revises the model when problems arise.

Ethical Review Boards: Consulting committees for ethics ensure that all matters concerning ethics are considered and also provide advice when designing machine learning models. These boards are there to ensure that the use of the model and studies involving it conform to societally and ethically acceptable standards.
