# 3.4.1 Voting schemes and its uses

In ensemble learning, several kinds of voting methods are used right now, and each of them varies in some way depending on the type of prediction, such as material property, under consideration. The least complicated method is averaging or majority voting, where each model in the ensemble “vote” for a specific prediction, and whichever option is most preferred by the majority of the models is adopted as the final output. This method is particularly applied when the individual models yield comparable accuracy but have different sensitivities to the data set. There is also weighted voting, where each model casts a vote in proportion to its accuracy and confidence in the prediction it favors. This technique is useful when some models perform better in certain areas than others, allowing the inputs of a few models to be weighted more heavily in the final prediction.

Soft voting was another method in which the probabilities given by the models were collected and then averaged, with the prediction having the highest probability being chosen.
