Delayed release: The delivery of drugs at a set time, after a specific time, or in answer to certain biological stimuli. Developed for selecting the right time for drug delivery and enhancing the drug availability.

Targeted release: Epidermal targeting and thus nonsystemic, increased drug effectiveness, and less toxicity.

Factors influencing drug release: Several factors govern drug release kinetics in delivery systems, including:

Formulation design: The choice of material, its disposition, and architecture of the delivery systems: matrices, nanoparticles, and implants influence the rates of diffusion and release of the drugs.

Environmental conditions: Physicochemical properties such as pH, temperature, and enzymatic activity in biological compartments determine drug solubility, stability, and release kinetics in/from matrices.

Drug properties: Drug solubility, molecular weight, and chemical stability of drugs affect the diffusion of drugs through the carrier materials and their interaction with bio-system tissues.

Mechanisms of Release: An overview of the processes that control drugs release from controlled and delivery systems is presented in tabular form, namely diffusion, dissolution, by erosion and pressure of osmotic gradients with each type to be specific to the treatment plan required.

Clinical implications and applications: Optimizing drug release profiles is crucial for enhancing treatment efficacy, patient adherence, and therapeutic outcomes across diverse medical disciplines:

Chronic disease management: Chronic dosing forms are convenient for use and allow reduction of dosing frequency, thus increasing the rate of patient compliance and further disease management.

Precision medicine: When the preparation is targeted to deliver medication to the affected tissues or cells only, thereby minimizing exposure to healthy tissues, therapeutic ratios are optimized through targeting release.

Combination therapies: Multimodal release systems combine synergistic drugs or therapeutic agents for each particular disease pathology and individual dosing regimens.

Advancements: Advancements in drug delivery technology and biopharmaceutical sciences continue to drive innovation in optimizing drug release profiles:

Nanotechnology: Drug carriers help with drug stability, bioavailability, and target delivery systems of nanoparticle-based medicines, transforming precision medicine and personalized therapy.

Biocompatible polymers: New biomaterials allow establishing a controlled release profile of desired agents and permit safety, efficacy, and tailor-made therapeutic strategies.
