Also, one can no longer presume that the ML models are fixed parameters that work to a certain set of rules or parameters, and never adjust. Standardizing future practices on monitoring, verifying, and updating of the frequently used ML algorithms become imperative for practicality and safety.

Patients are involved in the research and development of the biomimicry-based innovation and personalized drug delivery systems, therefore, ethical scruples must embrace the use of patient data in those analyses. Such issues should very clearly state the aspects of ownership, sharing, and commercialization of data so as to be protective of the patients. Procedures should follow regulation for research using de-identified patient information so that the use of such information is ethical while at the same time making sure that patients are informed of how their data is used and, in the process, the society gains some benefit from the research results.

Personalized medicine, also in the form of drug delivery systems, needs multidisciplinary practice consideration among clinician-scientists, ethicists, and regulators. The idea of collaboration in these platforms guarantees that ethical and regulatory issues are part and parcel of the creation and application of these technologies. Public involvement with multiple stakeholders is a way forward in responding to the multiple concerns of society as it creates trust for the implementation of personalized medicine.

# 5.5 Future trends in ML applications for personalized drug delivery

One of the emerging fields in personalized drug delivery can be revolutionized by the ML technologies through propelling the inventive techniques in improving therapeutic effects in patients. Several major trends are predicted to shape the evolution of the ML applications in this field based on more advanced algorithms; data integration, and a new generation of delivery platforms.

The value of multi-omics for comprehensive patient characterization is considered one of the most significant trends. This stoichiometric model of omics data must be fed progressively into the ML models to offer an overview of what a person is made of on the genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic and metabolomic levels. The four-dimensional approach enables clearer identification of disease origins and development of medicine, specific to patients’ need. Such data types can be integrated to enable machine learning algorithms to find complex connections and folds of data that would not be discerned using individual data types. This helps to predict individual patient’s response to medicine and tailor further therapy accordingly.
