### 2.3.1 Multiplatform and personalized medicine

Advancements in the past few years have also made it possible to combine various data that are multidimensional including proteomics, metabolomics, and genomes in improving treatment methodologies. These broad databases are applied by precision medicine to build up intensely individualistic treatment therapies that involve the complexity of biological systems. Several omics layers can be integrated, and the healthcare practitioners are capable of enhancing the accuracy of the models involving illness development, as well as therapy response.

### 2.3.2 Evolution of possible ways of treatment

It was expected that with advances in ML and AI, future therapeutic methods would become more personalized and data-oriented. Because these technologies can analyze vast amounts of data and discover new patterns and correlations, they have the potential to revolutionize customized medicine. The dynamic adjustment of therapies based on patient data could be enhanced by integrating AI and ML with real-time wearable devices. This evolution marks a transformation of healthcare from a reactive system to one that continuously adapts to meet patient needs.

## 2.4 Key figures and contributions in the history of personalized medicine

Several key pioneers have made significant contributions to the advancement of personalized medicine through their innovative research and strategic approaches. As illustrated in Figure 2, these individuals laid the foundational groundwork for today's trends in individualized and personalized medicine, helping to transform the field into a practical and accessible clinical discipline.

### 2.4.1 Sir Francis Crick and James D. Watson

The Nobel Prize was awarded to Sir Francis Crick and James D. Watson for their discovery of the DNA double helix structure in 1953. This breakthrough provided critical insights into the molecular structure of genes and heredity, forming the foundation for an individualized approach to medical treatment. What they found helped explain how genetic information is stored and transmitted from one generation to the next, paving the way for the development of genomics and personalized medicine.

### 2.4.2 Linus Pauling

Molecular biology and genetics were enriched by two-time Nobel Prize winner Linus Pauling. Through his work about nature of chemical bonds and structure of proteins, particularly the investigations of sickle cell anemia, he explained how mutations caused diseases. Pauling’s work has given impetus to molecular medicine, which was at the basis of current therapies adapted to individual genomolecular characteristics.

### 2.4.3 Baruch S. Blumberg and Robert L. Wilner

The presented paper was devoted to the substantial contributions of Robert L. Wilner and Baruch S. Blumberg for the enhancement of knowledge regarding the variations at genetic level and their correlation with diseases. The efficacy of the discovery made by Blumberg concerning hepatitis B virus and liver cancer showed that genetic and viral risks have to be considered when conducting disease risk assessment. Their work emphasized that the basis for prevention and treatment of the disease can be identified precisely with the help of the concept of the personal genome and the environment.

### 2.4.4 Leroy Hood

Leroy Hood was a systems biology scholar whose achievements relate to the progress of high-throughput technologies as well as the adaptation of the technologies for individualized medicine. His contribution was in the Human Genome Project and development of new tools such as automatic DNA sequencers and microarrays. Due to Hood, large-scale genetic analysis became possible and such large-scale processes are required to study individual differences, which can then be used for personalizing medicine.

### 2.4.5 Eric Lander

Eric Lander is a well-known name in genomics and the Human Genome Project. The possibility of developing complex diseases and gene-based therapies that are unique to an individual are presented in Lander’s research as some of the new insights into the hereditary components of complex diseases. He has played an essential role in the process of translating genotype information into phenotype, relevant in personalized medicine.

### 2.4.6 Francis Collins

Among those calling for personalized medicine most loudly was Francis Collins, the director of the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and head of the Human Genome Project. Framing of genetic diseases and development of personal treatment modalities have come to be enhanced immensely due to leadership in sequencing human genome by Collins and his research in genetics. To enhance treatment delivery, his work has revealed how essential it is to integrate genetic data into the practice.

### 2.4.7 George Church

George Church was also involved in various projects, which involve genomics and synthetic biology; he was a professor in the department of genetics in Harvard Medical School. Church has done significant work on genome sequencing, gene editing technologies, and personal genomics has come in handy in shaping the future for personalized medicine. His work has been devoted to leveraging these technologies to build individualized steady-state therapies and enhance disease control methods.

### 2.4.8 James P. Allison and Tasuku Honjo

For the development of cancer therapy by means of an enhanced ability of the immune system to find cancer cells, James P. Allison and Tasuku Honjo received the Nobel Prize in Physiology. They identified immune checkpoint inhibitors that harness the body’s immune system to attack tumor cells, changing the treatment of a number of forms of cancer. This strategy was a good example of how personalized medicine concepts are applied in developing individualized therapies for cancer patients.

All prominent scientists engaged in the development of personalized medicine and all have provided original contributions and discoveries in the respective fields. Altogether, their contributions advanced PM from a conceptual idea towards a variety of actual practices that shape progress in healthcare and make the targeted, patient-specific approaches achievable.
