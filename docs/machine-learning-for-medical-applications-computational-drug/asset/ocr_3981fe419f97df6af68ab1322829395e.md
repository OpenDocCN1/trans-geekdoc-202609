# 3.3 Noninvasive sensors

As a subfield within the broader domain of biosensors, noninvasive sensors have gained prominence due to their ability to collect multiple physiological parameters without penetrating the human body. This approach enhances patient comfort while minimizing the risk of complications typically associated with invasive procedures. As shown in Figure 4, this section provides a concise overview of the main types of non-invasive sensors, their operational principles, and their applications in continuous health monitoring.

# 3.3.1 Optical sensors

Optical sensors employ principles of operation that are founded on the use of light for the measurement of physiological characteristics. One of the most widely known optical sensors is the pulse oximeter which measures blood oxygen saturation levels and pulse rates using light. This is done using the principle of a photoplethysmography, where the amount of light absorbed by the oxygenated and deoxygenated blood hemoglobin clearly differs. Other optical sensors include NIRS sensors, used in tissue oxygenation and blood flow measurements and reflectance, as well as transmittance sensors, utilized in glucose monitoring of diabetic patients. Optical sensors can be used in a quasi-continuous and real-time mode because they are noninvasive in most cases.

# 3.3.2 Electrochemical sensors

Electrochemical sensors monitor physical characteristics by ascertaining the electrical activity of a chemical process. An example that one uses often is the without-holes sweat sensing, which consists of determining the composition of sweat so as to check the position of electrolytes. These sensors routinely use microelectrode arrays that help to record and measure the electrochemical activity with metabolic and hydration indicators. New technologies in electrochemical sensor system enable wearable devices for the determinations of biomarkers in sweat instead of the regular invasive blood sampling.
