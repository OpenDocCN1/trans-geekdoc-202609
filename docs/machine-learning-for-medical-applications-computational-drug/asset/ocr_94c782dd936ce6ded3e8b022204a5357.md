Blending: Similar to stacking, but base models are used to integrate predictions via a weighted average for aggregation. Blending usually involves the use of a holdout data set in the training of the meta-model. Most of the time, blending can be faster compared to stacking, but it may result in slightly lower accuracy. During blending, the required models are successfully combined and are typically effective at various aspects of the prediction task.

Voting: Voting, in which the forecast outcomes of several models are integrated through a simple average for regression and by employing voting based on the majority rule for classifications, was used for voting. In voting ensembles, an equal weight was provided as all models were considered equal to the last output, and the total output was the consensus of the models. Voting can be implemented using simple models such as decision trees, support vector machines, and neural networks, among others.

Boosting and Bagging with Hybrid Elements: Boosting and bagging can also be combined with other categories of machine learning techniques to form hybrid models. For instance, integrating boosting algorithms, such as GBMs, with neural network selection methods can improve the performance of the models. Additionally, using multiple versions of a single model, which are combined through bagging techniques, provides high stability and helps minimize overfitting.

Multi-View Learning: It is called multi-view learning when integrating models trained with different views of data. Each view represents a unique aspect of the data and the feature sets present in the current situation. Multi-view learning can help improve predictions, as in addition to the usual model's predictions, additional models trained on these views are taken into consideration, providing a broader understanding of the data.

Hybrid models offer several advantages in the context of drug toxicity prediction:

Enhanced Predictive Accuracy: Ensemble learning, where many models are used, has the ability to detect the complex relationships and forces in toxicity data that other models cannot identify. Consequently, undesirable medication responses as well as potential side effects are determined much more accurately.

Improved Robustness: There are always advantages to using hybrid models since they neutralize the shortcomings inherent in a particular base model. For example, if one model is very sensitive to noise and another one overfits the data, then the integrated models result in a more stable and reliable predictive model.

Handling Diverse Data Types: As seen, drug toxicity prediction uses heterogeneous formats of data such as chemical characteristics, biological tests, and records. Combined models can incorporate different types of information through various base models, ensuring that the analysis of toxicity is comprehensive.
