In MC, autoencoders fill the gap in working with high-dimensional data including spectroscopy, field data, as well as other analytical measurements. Fundamentally, these properties are latent within the data; autoencoders reduce their dimensions, allowing for a better understanding of the properties themselves. The mapping can also capture details that were previously hard to grasp due to the size of the feature space. For instance, in the study of the properties of polymer composite materials and alloys, autoencoder types can deliver important features, valuable for the definition of the best material combinations in terms of performance.

# 2.3.1 Benefits and advantages

When using autoencoders for the purposes of dimensionality reduction, the most significant advantage is their ability to learn nonlinear transformations of data. That is, autoencoders do not rely on comparatively simple linear transformations to find nonlinear relationships in the data, as PCA does. This feature is highly important for MC scenarios where the relationships between numerous material parameters are significantly nonlinear. The autoencoder is also able to perform data cleaning by training the network to map noisy data points into noise-free representations, thereby improving the quality of the reduced-dimension features for further analysis.

# 2.3.2 Challenges and considerations

As useful as autoencoders are when used in MC, there are some issues to consider. Some of the most important aspects that should be adjusted in the architecture of an autoencoder include the number of layers and the size of the latent space. The choice of the number of layers and the size of the latent space can significantly affect the performance of the autoencoder. One notable drawback associated with using this model is overfitting, particularly if the size of the dataset is small compared to the size of the model. Additionally, the interpretation of the compressed representation remains problematic because the representation learned by autoencoders may not align with the physically relevant aspects of the material.

It was expected that future research on autoencoders for MC would explore ways of making those models more explainable and integrating them into other machine learning methods. These are some implications for future work: possibly, a better approach for building improved models could be to develop multilayer autoencoder models that include some type of filtering, which supports marked-up output labels to utilize the results of supervised learning methods, thereby adding more robust and interpretable results.
