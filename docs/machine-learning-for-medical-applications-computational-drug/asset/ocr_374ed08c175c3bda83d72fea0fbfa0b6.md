# 4.4.1 Wireless communication technologies

The lack of cables is another important characteristic of today's biomedical sensors since they all rely on wireless schemes. Several advancements have significantly improved the performance and reliability of wireless sensor systems.

Bluetooth low energy (BLE): Based on the current literature, BLE technology is accepted as a standard for low-energy and short-range communication in wearables sensors. This makes it suitable in devices with high frequency data update requirement, whilst supporting long battery life.

Near-field communication (NFC): NFC technology enables short-range high-frequency wireless communication between various sensors and other devices. It is used in such areas as body glucose monitoring without physical contacts and identification of patients, where data transfer has to be fast and secure.

Zigbee and LoRaWAN: These protocols are used in sensor networks that entail high-range transmission and minimal power usage. Zigbee is ideal for devices that need multiple connections with sensors within the same personal area network, while LoRaWAN is ideal for knowledge transfer across expansive areas, as required in remote health monitoring.

5 G connectivity: The emergence of 5 G technology improves the data channeling process by providing increased speed of transmitting data with low latency, making the wireless sensors more effective for conveying real-time health information. This advancement aids the growth of complex health monitoring systems that need processing and feedback with little delay.

Wireless power transfer (WPT): The advancement in WPT technology can allow the charging of sensors wirelessly such that they do not require battery replacement. This is especially helpful in implantable sensors and in long-term wearable devices.

# 4.4.2 Battery-less sensor technologies

Battery-less sensors rely on alternative energy sources to operate without the need for conventional batteries. These technologies overcome the limitations of battery life and significantly reduce the need for frequent maintenance. As illustrated in Figure 7, one key approach is energy harvesting, where ambient energy from the environment is captured and converted into electrical power for sensor operation. Emerging energy scavenging techniques include piezoelectric energy harvesting, which generates power from mechanical vibrations, and thermoelectric energy harvesting, which converts heat gradients into electrical energy.
