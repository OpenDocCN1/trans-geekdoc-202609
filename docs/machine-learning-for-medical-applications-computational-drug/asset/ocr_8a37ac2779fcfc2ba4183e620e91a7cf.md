cluding but not limited to cancer and cardiovascular disorders. Selecting strategies and implementing preventive and corrective action plans based on a risk/need assessment indicates a move up in managing chronic and multifaceted health conditions.

While personalized medicine has reached a high level of penetration there are some difficulties. Socioeconomic, geographic, and systemic differences that affect patients’ abilities to receive tailored patient care are also the major challenges to equal care. Eligibility for the cost of sophisticated diagnostic devices and genetic testing limit underserved population that in turn worsens health disparities. Reported matters also draw concern on the ethnic bias and demographics in conducting genomic studies and undermining the generalization of genomic treatments among ethnic and demographic populations.

That is where a wide-scope approach is necessary to solve these problems, alongside measures connected to reduction of prices, advancement of the healthcare system, and attempts to diversify the investigated topics. It is thus only important that the concept of the personalized medicine has to be guaranteed and is to be provided regardless of the patient’s financial status. Besides, combining the effective universal strategies studied previously with the targeted methods, the introduction of the concept of personalized medicine with traditional medical seems to bring a ray of hope for enhancing the effectiveness of medical practices as a whole.
