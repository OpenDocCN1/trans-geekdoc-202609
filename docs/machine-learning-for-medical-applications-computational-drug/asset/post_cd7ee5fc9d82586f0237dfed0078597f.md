Intracranial pressure (ICP) probes are implantable devices for monitoring the pressure of the skull in patients with diseases of intracranial dynamics, including traumatic brain injury. These sensors are most commonly located in the brain system and their role is to continuously record the ICP. Given the valuable information from the ICP monitors, the treating physicians and surgeons can go ahead with intervention measures such as changing medication dosages that are intended to alter brain pressure in cases affecting the patient.

Cochlear implants are advanced technological tools developed to help people diagnosed with severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss. These implants have an external auditory processor and an internal receiver-stimulating electrode that is fixed in the cochlea of the patient. The external processor gathers sound source and converts these into electrical signals that can be received by the internal electrodes. These electrodes thus interfere with the auditory nerve so as to enable the user to have an understanding of sound. Cochlear implants refer to a developmental assistive technology that enhances the process of hearing and communication for those with hearing loss.

Neurostimulators can be surgically inserted into the body to specifically adjust the activity of the neural tracts to serve a therapeutic role. Some of these devices are deep brain stimulators and spinal cord stimulators, which are employed to manage multiple neurology disorders. Pulse generators are placed directly in the brain through surgical procedures and used to provide electrical signal to control dysfunctional brain signals involved in ailments like Parkinson's disease and essential tremor. Spinal cord stimulators are surgical devices placed in the epidural space of spinal cord and deliver electrical impulses to the patient's body to control chronic pain by altering the pain's message en route to the brain. Both kinds of neurostimulators offer designed therapies that adapt, depending on the reaction of the individual.

## 2.3 Remote monitoring systems

Telemonitoring systems are one of the latest innovations in the field of medical technology since it allows the assessment of the conditions of the patient, together with the determination of what actions to take without having to physically check up on the patient. These systems use different types of sensors and communications technologies to gather and transmit health information to the healthcare givers to enhance effective and timely management of patients’ health.

Telemonitoring devices are developed to be positioned at the patient’s home environment to enable endless regular tracking of the patient’s health status without constant referrals to the healthcare facilities. These include weight measurement through a digital scale, home blood pressure monitoring, and oximeter for blood oxygen levels. Such devices, when combined with mobile application, help patients to monitor their health over a period and to share the acquired information with physicians for remote evaluation and treatment.

Telemedicine is an innovative approach that allows the patient and/or healthcare provider to consult almost everything through the Internet. Some of these platforms comprise common amenities like video calls and messaging, and access to records of the health institution, among others. Using such technologies, patients can get medical consultation, examination, and even diagnosis without having to visit a healthcare facility. This makes it possible to enhance the use of telemedicine by connecting the telemedicine work with remote monitoring devices in order to give a view of the patient, in addition to enabling better virtual care.

Telemonitoring of blood pressure is an advanced tool used by patients to record and convey their blood pressure levels to healthcare givers without direct contact.

These monitors include automatic cuff inflators and digital displays that measure and display systolic and diastolic blood pressure. They represent key components of remote monitoring systems, including sensor mechanisms, communication methods, and real-time data transmission and analysis for clinical oversight. Data is wirelessly transmitted using Bluetooth communication technologies, allowing healthcare providers to track blood pressure trends, adjust medications, and manage hypertension without requiring in-person visits. Such continuous monitoring enables early detection of potential health issues, leading to more effective and timely hypertension management.

Digital stethoscopes improve traditional auscultation by transforming sounds into signal data that can be documented and examined. These devices are sometimes incorporated with wireless systems in order to forward heart and lung sounds to other sites for analysis. Specifically, by using the technology of the digital stethoscopes, one can control a patient’s cardiovascular and respiratory systems from remote, which means that doctors can make more precise diagnosis and apply necessary measures, when needed. The recorded sounds are listened to by other practitioners, which enhances the assessment process as well as the multidisciplinary management of the patient’s healthcare.

Environmental/ambient sensors are responsible for measuring factors around a patient’s environment that impact health, such as temperature, humidity, and quality of air. These sensors are always employed, together with other types of remote monitoring devices, in order to offer a broad perspective of the patient’s home environment. For instance, sensors can be used to identify variations that exist within rooms that endanger the respiratory system of patients. The information that can be obtained can serve as a basis for identifying areas that need change in the patient’s environment and their general condition.

## 2.4 Diagnostic and imaging sensors

Sensors used in diagnostics and imaging are one of the precursors of modern medicine as they give the needed information on the internal functioning of the human body and its changes. The features of these sensors allow for the diagnosis of diseases, determination of the most appropriate strategies of treatment, and assessment of the outcome of interventions. The use of imaging devices has improved over time through technological improvement, making them precise and more versatile.

![](img/211c754cd1dd84920a171f497b33b689_58_0.png)

Figure 4: Diagnostic and imaging sensors.

MRI and CT are two modern radiation diagnosis facilities that help doctors to visualize deeply settled pathologies in detail. MRI involves the use of strong magnetic fields and radio frequency waves to produce a detailed picture of the soft tissue areas of the body, including the brain, muscles, and organs. Figure 4 shows some of the diagnostic and imaging sensors used in today’s advanced ICT medical technologies for the purpose of improving accuracy and early detection of diseases as an underlying goal. Computed tomography (CT scanners) delivers cross-sectional images by applying X-ray and provide close views of bones, organs, and blood vessels. MRI and CT imaging give crucial information while diagnosing ailments that include tumors, fractures, internal bleeding syndromes, and neurological problems.

Ultrasound uses sound waves of high pitch to present the internal structures of the body in real-time. This noninvasive imaging modality is commonly used for studying soft tissue areas, including the abdomen, pelvis, and fetus during pregnancy. As for ultrasound imaging, it is appreciated owing to its noninvasiveness and mobility and the possibility to deliver real-time information. The technology consists of different forms, B-mode for structural evaluation and Doppler ultrasound for evaluating vessels’ functionality. Ultrasonic imaging devices have improved over the years – the quality of images captured has improved and new features have been added to the devices to enable more specific uses such as echocardiography and ultrasound musculoskeletal system.
