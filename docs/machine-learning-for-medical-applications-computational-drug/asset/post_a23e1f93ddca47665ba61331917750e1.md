### 2.4 Evolution of blockchain-based EHR solutions

A decentralized safe replacement to standard EHR systems came with the rise of blockchain EHRs. Because sharing of healthcare data required more secure and semantic communication, the idea of using blockchain technology came up in the 2010s.

Blockchain technology in its early stages of evolution mainly provided a means for supporting “transaction ledgers” and for securely creating and confirming digital currency such as bitcoins. Others began to realize how blockchain can preserve and handle special health information in addition to monetary applications.

As per many specialists, at an early stage of enhancement of blockchain-based EHR systems, all the emphasis was given to demonstrating projects to explore how useful blockchain was in healthcare. Although these initial attempts suffered from concerns of scalability and compliance, they helped showcase basic use cases of blockchain technology in terms of data security and the ability to share data.

![](img/211c754cd1dd84920a171f497b33b689_19_0.png)
Figure 4: Evolution of blockchain-based EHR Solutions.

Solutions that integrated blockchain technology across a healthcare system came to the fore as the technology advanced and gained adoption. These solutions brought new technologies and functionalities in order to satisfy the demands of patients and healthcare organizations. All of these emerging trends address the issues of conventional EHR systems and open new possibilities for healthcare data handling using the advantages of the main characteristics of the blockchain: decentralization and cryptographic security.

### 2.5 Key milestones in the evolution of blockchain-based EHR solutions

- Development of healthcare-specific blockchain platforms: Started to develop blockchain solutions with an attempt to target the field of healthcare with different characteristics such as consensus algorithms and conditional contracts.
- Integration with existing healthcare infrastructure: Blockchain-based EHR solutions are designed to interface with existing healthcare information systems to enhance interoperability, allowing diverse systems to exchange and share data while preserving privacy and ensuring data integrity (see Figure 5).
- Standardization and regulatory compliance: To meet legal requirements related to regulations such as the GDPR and HIPAA, standardized guidelines have been developed for blockchain-enabled electronic health record systems.

### 2.6 Key components and architecture of blockchain-based EHR systems

The characteristics of blockchain systems and the materials required for their implementation also guarantee the secure and compatible management of patient health information. An illustration of the components and design of the health record systems based on the blockchain paradigm is presented in Figure 6 while arguing about the patient's privacy and protection of their data. Figure 6 shows the basic building blocks and architecture of blockchain-based EHR systems, as well as an explanation of how blockchain was incorporated in order to make EHRs more secure and sharable for patients and clinicians.

- Distributed ledger: Clinical data of the patients is safely stored in the blocks, which constitute a major component of the blockchain EHRS. The ledger provides data security in that it presents a clear and time-tagged view of transactions. EHR's state change happens with each transaction that creates a block that links to the previous block cryptographically.

![](img/211c754cd1dd84920a171f497b33b689_21_0.png)
Figure 6: Key components and architecture of blockchain-based EHR systems.

- Smart contracts: Smart contracts refer to terms that, when fulfilled, execute agreements, perform actions without any supervision, and are written into the blockchain. It revealed that smart contracts control data access rights as well as other aspects of EHR systems based on the blockchain. Smart contracts mention who has access to certain clinical information for purposes of data privacy and security.
- Consensus mechanism: Protocols with the designation consensus mechanisms maintain consensus in blockchain systems and make users aware of the transaction legitimacy. BFT, PoS, and PoW are known as consensus mechanisms. To ensure all the participants of the network are aware of the valid status of EHR transactions and security of the ledger in blockchain systems, suitable consensus methods are used.
- Cryptographic hashing: A data block in the blockchain process is the element from which we get a unique hash by applying cryptographic hashing techniques. These identities firmly link block elements safely and act as electronic labels of the contents of the data. The members can confirm the trustworthiness of the blockchain and identify illegal modifications to the data using hashed values.
- Decentralized network: Every node within a network established for the processing of blockchain records holds an updated version of the ledger. From the decentralized configuration of this system, there are lesser chances of a point being targeted when one weak point has been eliminated. In peer-to-peer networks, protocols allow nodes to link with each other and ensure data replication and synchronization within a network.
- User Interfaces and Applications: Healthcare consumers connect to EHR systems on blockchain networks through user interfaces and applications tailored to the stakeholders of a particular healthcare provider. These interfaces allow users to navigate EHRs, review medical documents, manage access permissions, and conduct transactions. In addition to integrating with traditional healthcare institutions, these systems offer capabilities via web interfaces and mobile tools, enabling broader engagement and interaction.

## 3 Security and privacy in blockchain-based EHR systems

### 3.1 Importance of security and privacy in healthcare data

Sensitivity, reliability of patient information, security, and privacy turn into critical factors when managing health information. In the healthcare data management system, the statement, “secure and private” is an important aspect to enhance patient trust while adhering to government requirements and protecting patient data from exploitative use.

The two practices to prevent cyber threats and unwanted access to healthcare data are defense measures. Patient information such as personal identification details and personalized/routine treatment plans is stored by healthcare firms and attracts high monetary value from cyber criminals. The consequences of violation of healthcare data are severe: financial loss, damage to people’s image, threats of identity theft, and possible harm to life of patients. To avoid situations where patient identity is disclosed, proper measures such as encryption and firewalls must be working as required.

From Figure 7 below, it emerges that emphasis on security and privacy is critical to healthcare data. Collection, storage, and transmission of data in this sub-sector deserve the same level of protection as the protection of information from unauthorized access. The ordinary citizens and consumers expect healthcare organizations and providers to protect their data correctly and honorably, so that they alone can decide who gains access to their personal health information. To ensure that patients continue to have confidence in them and work beneficially with doctors, it is important to protect patients’ information because it is legally and ethically right. The general public rarely presents itself to seek medical assistance from doctors after a breach of its privacy has happened.

![](img/211c754cd1dd84920a171f497b33b689_23_0.png)
Figure 7: Importance of security and privacy in healthcare data.

The privacy of healthcare data is important in terms of general data protection regulations, for example, GDPR and health information privacy and accountability act (HIPAA). These norms define specific expectations on how patient medical data safety and privacy can be maintained along with activities as simple as data anonymization and as severe as breach notification. Should these regulations be flouted, severe consequences could develop with a legal aspect as well as the deterioration of the image of the healthcare providers.

In any healthcare data management, to protect the privacy of the patients, in addition to the legal requirement, security and privacy are key to protect the patient details. The healthcare organizations have to implement both security and privacy measures. Including the security and privacy of data in the strategic perspectives of these organizations lowers the levels of risks associated with data leakage and enables a high level of ethical and lawful practices in healthcare settings.
