Methods Based on Models: In model-based techniques, data are fitted into a statistical model, and outliers are determined from the model-fitted graphs. BCM was used in methods like Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) for estimating the probability of the data. Low-probability points are called failures. Another method is One-Class SVM, in which outliers are identified with the help of a single judgment boundary drawn through most of the data samples.

Techniques Based on Reconstruction: Using models such as autoencoders, reconstruction-based methods apply a lower-dimensional representation to reconstruct the data. In other words, anomalies are detected based on reconstruction errors, which are the differences between the original data and the data that is recreated. A large value of reconstruction errors suggests that the data point is an outlier. This method works well for detecting unstructured patterns from large storage volumes.

Structural models in drug toxicity prediction can also use anomaly detection approaches, where an important method identifies effects that are not typical for drug toxicity models. For instance:

Detecting Rare Adverse Reactions: Anomaly detection can also be applied to clinical trial information to identify patterns of rare adverse reactions that have not been identified during the pre-clinical and clinical development phases.

Identifying Novel Toxicity Patterns: When anomaly detection was applied to molecular data, entirely different patterns of toxicity were discovered, which were not detected by other methods, and led to the identification of new toxicophore groups and toxic mechanisms.

Enhancing Safety Monitoring: Data analysis, in conjunction with anomaly detection, can assist in the early detection of unfavorable side effects, thereby providing better chances of treatment and increasing patient safety.
