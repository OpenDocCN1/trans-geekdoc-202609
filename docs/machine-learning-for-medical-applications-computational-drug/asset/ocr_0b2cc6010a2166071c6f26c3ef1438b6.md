# 4.1.5 Cochlear implants

Cochlear implants are electrical devices that help people who are profoundly deaf to appreciate sound in some ways. The preference for biocompatible materials for cochlear implants includes an outer casing covered with silicone material and electrodes made of platinum. These materials must be chosen carefully to avoid hazardous effects and harm to the delicate parts of the inner ear. It has also been observed that the design and materials used must be appropriate in order to avoid tissue reactions and achieve the optimal effect on the stimulation of the auditory nerve.

One of the major achievements in medical development is the use of biocompatible materials in both implants and prostheses, which restore patients’ functionality and improve their quality of life. Due to advances in patient needs, the market now offers new materials and technologies that enhance device functionality, durability, and safety for both clients and health professionals.

# 4.2 Drug delivery systems

To develop advanced drug delivery systems capable of delivering precisely controlled and highly localized therapeutic inputs, the use of biocompatible materials is fundamental. These materials enable systems that can release medication at optimal rates and target specific sites in the body to maximize therapeutic efficacy while minimizing side effects. # 4.2.1 Controlled-release formulations

Pharmaceuticals reach the target site in the body slowly through the administration of therapeutic substances with defined release rates using controlled-release drug delivery systems. By doing so, it reduces the administration intervals, enhances patient compliance, and contributes to the sustenance of therapeutic concentrations of such medications in the circulatory system. Due to their degradation in a controlled manner that releases the medicine in a steady and systematic way, biodegradable polymers such as PCL and PLGA are commonly used in the construction of such devices. Depending on the requirements of the therapy, these materials can be developed into hydrogels, nanoparticles, and microspheres, among others.
