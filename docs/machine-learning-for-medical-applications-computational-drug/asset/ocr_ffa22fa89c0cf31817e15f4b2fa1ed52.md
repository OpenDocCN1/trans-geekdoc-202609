# 1 Introduction

The environment in the healthcare sector is changing dramatically as a result of discoveries in biomedical sensor technologies [1]. These innovations are transforming continuous health monitoring by giving real-time, accurate information about the physiological signs [2–4]. Biomedical sensors are defined as a series of devices used to measure the human body's diverse parameters [5]. This chapter explores the options of designing and developing AI biomedical sensors for patient treatment improvement and as efficient instruments in health treatment [6, 7]. The emphasis is on the major developments of the project, the use of AI for optimizing the characteristics of the sensors, and their capabilities and the role of these technologies in long-term health monitoring [8].

Biomedical sensors fall under optical, chemical, and mechanical types, depending on the type of health parameter that is to be monitored [9]. For example, optical sensors use light to measure oxygen levels, glucose level, and concentrations of other biomarkers in the blood. Biochemical sensors quantitate specific chemicals present in a solution; for instance, glucose, using electrochemical reactions [10–12]. Mechanical sensors respond to the physical alterations in such values as strain; these can provide valuable information on joints’ movements, gait, and muscles activity [13]. These sensor types are also described in detail in this chapter, together with an overview of the manner in which they operate, the fields that they are aptly suited for, and the modern technological improvements that have been made with for their precision and performance [14].

Hybrid/multimodal sensors are seen as the next evolutionary advancement in the field of health monitoring [15, 16]. These systems integrate several sensing technologies to measure many parameters at once, on a single instrument [17]. A device comprising optical and mechanical sensors can track both cardiovascular state and physical activities, giving a better picture of the patient’s conditions. This chapter describes the different types of hybrid and multimodal systems that positively impact, such as increased data quality, higher patient compliance, and opportunities to create more individual health interventions.

Remote surveillance is most valuable in geriatric care, as keeping patients’ chronic diseases under control is always challenging [18]. Diabetes, hypertension, and heart diseases, for instance, call for relentless assessment of body’s various parameters in elderly people [19]. Responsive wearables, implanted sensors, and telemetric monitoring tools are intended to answer these needs, providing continuous support and monitoring [20]. The current chapter highlights the deployment of these long-term monitoring solutions with regard to patient care, early health disorder detection, and for the enhancement of the quality of the life of the elderly.
