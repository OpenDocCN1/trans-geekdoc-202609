# 6.5.3 Economies of scale

Thus, specialization has great importance, where costs per unit of biomedical sensors products also decline with increase in volumes, as indicated by economies of scale. Manufacturing is about economies of scale because fixed costs are apportioned over a larger number of products, thus enabling the lowering of costs per unit. To take advantage of scale economies, there is a need to focus on setting up efficient production facilities, high level of automation, and flow of supplies. Extended cooperation with other industries and making use of existing distribution platforms also help to attain cost efficiencies as well as general cost reduction in the sensor technologies.

# 6.5.4 Areas of integration and system-level cost aspects

Biomedical sensors are neither solely evaluated in terms of cost nor in terms of an individual sensor's lifespan, but rather on the sensors' efficiency and cost as part of a greater system within the healthcare industry. This means that the total cost related to a system involves issues to do with data handling, interfacing with other systems, and maintenance of the system. For instance, sensors that need further data processing tools to analyze the collected information cost extra amounts of money apart from the initial cost.
