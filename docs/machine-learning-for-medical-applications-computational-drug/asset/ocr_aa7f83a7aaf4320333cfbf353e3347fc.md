There are two more relevant categories of issues, which include ethical and regulatory factors. Biomedical materials utilizing ML have to be in line with strict governing regulatory protocols and ethical best practices to protect users, particularly their patients’ sensitive information. Anticipating and managing the regulatory factors concerning ML-driven innovation requires understanding some of the regulatory concerns such as data protection, validation of the models, and the question of demographic bias in the model prediction. It is important for these applications of ML to fit into this regulatory compliance while at the same time remaining ethical in their implementation.

Solving such issues entails the joint cooperation of researchers, practitioners, and regulatory authorities in finding ways of improving quality, model explainability, and integration of ML in work environments as well as interacting with the ethical and legal challenges. Overcoming these challenges is crucial to pushing progress in ML applied to biomedical materials creation and bringing this potential to life.

# 3 Machine learning algorithms for biomedical materials

# 3.1 Overview of supervised learning techniques in material science

Supervised learning is one of the principal branches of machine learning (ML) that has found significant application in materials science, particularly for the prediction and optimization of biomedical materials. In this approach, input features such as structural, chemical, or physical attributes are mapped to known output labels, enabling the model to learn specific patterns during training. This learning paradigm proves especially valuable for tasks like material classification, property prediction, and performance optimization, where labeled data is available. The strength of supervised learning lies in its ability to build predictive models (P-models) that can generalize effectively to new, unseen datasets, which is a key requirement in designing next-generation biomedical materials.
