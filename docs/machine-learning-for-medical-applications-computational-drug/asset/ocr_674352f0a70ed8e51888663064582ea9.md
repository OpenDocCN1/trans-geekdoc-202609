# 2.5 AI-based identification of novel drug targets from omics data

Omics technology has become an integrated strategy in analyzing molecular mechanisms underlying numerous diseases in biomedical research. An exciting opportunity in drug design was the discovery of novel targets based on omics information, and AI is a critical component of such efforts.

Another way AI can be used to identify pharmacological targets from the omics data is data integration from proteomics, metabolomics, transcriptomics, and genome data. These integrated datasets utilize machine learning procedures including ensemble in addition to deep learning concerning the mark, molecular markers linked to diverse disease conditions. It is possible to identify the genes and metabolic pathways that are under- or overexpressed in disease conditions, which can be considered potential future drug targets.

Analysis of omics data starting from complex networks offers a promising approach to identify new treatment plans within biological networks. Omics data integrated with functional annotations and pathways are used to construct gene co-regulatory networks, protein-protein interaction networks, and metabolic networks. Network propagation algorithms as well as community detection algorithms on these networks, can be used to prioritize nodes within these networks that are associated with functionally relevant disease processes. These algorithms can help identify potential drug targets relevant to disease-associated pathways and how simulated information flows through what are likely to be highly connected subnetworks.

With the help of AI, new cell type-specific targets involved in the development of illness can be identified on the basis of single-cell omics data. We have single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), which provides unprecedented details on the heterogeneity of cells within tissues and tumors. Single-cell omics data is also valuable for computational analysis of clustering algorithms and trajectory inference that can pinpoint cell type-specific markers and disease networks. These markers and networks hit cell type-specific vulnerabilities, potential therapeutic targets of precision medicine.
