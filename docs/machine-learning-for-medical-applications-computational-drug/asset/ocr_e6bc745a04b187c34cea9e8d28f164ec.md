# 5.1.3 The issue in applying the various concepts of new structural dynamics for predictive modeling of additive manufacturing

There are challenges associated with using predictive modeling in additively manufactured biomaterials. One of the significant difficulties is the description of factors influencing the final properties of the material of interest, as well as the dependencies between process characteristics and final results. The scattered nature of experimental data, due to variations in materials and conditions, also affects the precision of predictive models. Due to the presence of multiple process parameters and numerous material characteristics as input variables, the input space is quite high-dimensional and thus leads to issues such as overfitting of the model. While attempting to solve these problems, it is necessary to address tasks such as the application of high-level ML methodologies, the selection of sufficient approaches for data collection, and the constant verification of the model to determine whether the created predictive models can be useful in real life.

It was probable that future research about predictive modeling in AM of biomaterials would depend on improvements in specific ML algorithms, as well as computational methods. New approaches that are currently at the prestudy level and which include deep learning and hybrid models hold the key to enhancing both the accuracy and robustness of the predictions, since the models are able to capture additional data complexity beyond the linear model. The combination of data acquisition and the usage of prognosis models allows real-time alterations in the fabrication procedures, thereby improving the flexibility and productivity of AM. Fourth and finally, the richer sources of data that are available, utilizing a broader spectrum of materials as well as the conditions under which manufacturing processes are carried out contribute to building less-specialized, all-encompassing models. In the process of further development of additive manufacturing as a tool for creating high-performance structures, further research into predictive modeling of processes is essential for enhancing the potential of biomaterials in various sectors.
