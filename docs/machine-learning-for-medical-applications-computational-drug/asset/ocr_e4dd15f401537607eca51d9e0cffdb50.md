# 2.4 Key properties that determine biocompatibility

The capacity of a material to safely and efficiently interface with biological systems is fundamentally determined by its biocompatibility, which is evaluated using a range of physical, chemical, and biological parameters. These features are essential for the successful application of materials in the biomedical field, particularly in implants, prosthetics, and drug delivery systems. Figure 2 provides an overview of the key biocompatibility assessment parameters and their relevance across various biomedical applications.

Chemical composition: The composite nature of a material determines how it behaves with biological systems due to its chemical features. A material cannot be made of elements that cause toxicity when present in tissue. Most biomaterials have chemically stable compositions that are chemically inactive and do not degrade into toxic substances. It is also important for the material to contain no toxic elements, especially when the material is in contact with live tissues.

Surface properties: These parameters merge within a system where the interfacial aspects of the material, including roughness, porosity, and surface energy, govern the behavior of a material system with biological systems. These properties affect cell attachment, protein absorption, and tissue incorporation. For instance, the right type of roughness helps in the attachment of cells and encourages tissue formation but, in contrast, it could be disadvantageous if the surface was too rough. Surface chemical characteristics illustrate that using coatings to control these interactions at the surface level is also possible.
