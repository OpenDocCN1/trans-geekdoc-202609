## 5 AI-driven lead optimization strategies

### 5.1 Generative models for de novo drug design

As potentially capable of generating new chemical substances with required characteristics and activities, generative models seem to have become useful in the de novo drug designing process. There are numerous methods that generative models employ to navigate chemical space and develop new drugs with favorable pharmacological features: GANs and VAEs.

One of the key applications of generative models in de novo drug discovery is the generation of novel molecules with desired properties using probabilistic models such as variational autoencoders (VAEs). VAEs learn compact representations of chemical structure space from input molecular data and are capable of generating new chemical compounds by sampling from a learned latent space, effectively producing structurally novel molecules that still retain essential characteristics of the originals. In contrast, generative adversarial networks (GANs) employ a generator and a discriminator network, where the discriminator distinguishes between real and generated molecules, while the generator aims to produce molecules indistinguishable from real ones. As illustrated in Figure 11, the ability of GANs to learn from large molecular databases allows scientists to synthesize a wide array of new chemical entities with tailored pharmacological profiles.

The generative models allow for optimization of molecular properties and activities not only by adjusting the given parameters but also by redefining them. Although the LMMS is a generative model of molecules, the result generation is done in conjunction with a reinforcement learning function wherewith scientists can direct the outcome of molecular structure generation towards the achievement of some aims such as optimum potency and minimum toxicity pharmacokinetic characteristics. Akin to how a human learns through feedback on the preference of certain generated objects, reinforcement learning agents receive feedback regarding the likelihood of the generated molecules for reinforcement of better quality and diverse structures.

These generative models can be used to design structure from scratch. Molecular libraries can be designed to hit a certain biological target. This means that by pretraining generative models on target protein structures, the researchers can obtain libraries containing compounds with high affinity and selectivity to the target protein of interest. This focused strategy to de novo drug design can potentially speed up the process of the identification of lead compounds, and also reduce off target interactions, which result in an efficient drug discovery process.

In structure-based design, generative models can be employed to create molecules with defined three-dimensional conformation and binding modes. Biased generation by knowledge of protein-ligand interactions and pharmacophore features ensures that generative models generate the compounds likely to interact favorably with the target protein. The structure-guided approach of de novo design increases the probability of developing new leads with improved affinities and selectivity.

### 5.2 Multi-objective optimization in lead optimization

Multi-objective optimization (MOO) is also useful in lead optimization to achieve versatile goals for potency at the target, selectivity over related proteins and solubility, as a mixed mission in seeking the best drug candidates. As applied to AI-based lead optimization, MOO approaches provide a structured and time-effective paradigm for addressing the multifaceted relationships implied by the drug design process at hand and for prioritizing the compounds with the most favorable pharmacologic processes based on a set of external attributes.

One of the major advantages of multi-objective optimization (MOO) in lead optimization is its ability to provide a diverse set of Pareto-optimal solutions, collectively known as the Pareto front. The Pareto front offers a graphical representation of the trade-offs between competing objectives, enabling researchers to balance conflicting criteria such as potency, selectivity, and ADMET properties. By evaluating candidate solutions based on dominance relationships and Pareto optimality, traditional optimization techniques such as genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization, and evolutionary algorithms have been adapted to handle multiple objectives. As shown in Figure 12, these methods facilitate the selection of lead compounds that satisfy a project's specific goals and constraints, while also representing different compromise scenarios within the solution space.

It is also notable that MOO techniques include machine learning models for realizing organization of queries in the high-dimensional and nonlinear chemical space. Hence, some algorithms like Bayesian optimization use probabilistic models, including the Gaussian process, with acquisition functions to search for the areas of promising chemical space. As the probabilistic model used in Bayesian optimization is updated upon observations, the method can conclude the areas with promising leads in terms of further optimization within the Pareto frontier. Altogether the same, the reinforcement learning algorithms can bring the adaptive sampling techniques to devise the study of assorted and enlightening areas of the chemical space.

Also, depending on the type of current leads, MOO in lead optimization incorporates domain-specific data and specialists’ suggestions in a search for efficient drug prototypes. This enables MOO algorithms to guide the optimization process toward compounds that partially meet medicinal chemistry constraints, pharmacological guidelines, and safety concerns. With MOO frameworks, it is possible to utilize predictive models of molecular properties and activities for the generation and optimization of lead compounds; this allows the effective exploration of the Pareto front and improved speed of identifying promising candidates.

The analyses of SAR through artificial neural networks can help the prediction of activities of compounds for new chemical entities and assist in decisions regarding lead compounds essential for the experimental confirmation. Hypothesis-building approaches developed from SAR data ascertain the probability of compounds having a series of specified biological activities due to their structural resemblance to active compounds. It is important to stress that the described methodology of AI-based SAR analysis of compounds can offer important insights on the action of drugs that target specific receptors, as well as define the relationship between the chemical structure of compounds and their interactions. When used to dissect the contribution of each molecular feature of the compound substructures to compound activity, SAR models can reveal pharmacophore elements, binding motifs, and intervals within target proteins. This mechanistic model of SAR makes structure-based rational drug design methods such as structure-based optimization and ligand-protein interaction analysis manipulate compound activity and this augurs well for potency, selectivity, and the drug-like properties.

AI-driven SAR analysis can provide information for successive lead optimization cycles by designing specific compound collections and by providing recommendations for the chosen series of analogs to be synthesized and tested. Combined application of SAR models, medicinal chemistry, computational chemistry, and experimental activities make it possible for the researchers to optimize the lead compounds that elicit a desirable pharmacological effect without causing off-target and toxicity effects. Integral to this process is the use of iterative approaches to lead optimization, so as to efficiently mine chemical space and quickly surface promising leads with desirable drug-like profiling.
