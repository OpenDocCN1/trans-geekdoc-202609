Economic constraints also influence specific patient’s access to the new biomedical sensors and devices. Patients with few resources would be hard put to afford such gadgets and the cost of sustaining them. The use of devices during treatment means an additional cost, which is financed by the patient himself, as also the expense related to the care of devices, apart from the cost of repair, which is also additionally paid by the patient himself. Economic barriers can be addressed through case approaches between healthcare providers, policy makers, and device manufactures to ensure that the latest technologies are provided to all stakeholders, including the needy and the indigent.

# 5.5 Technical and operational issues

It can be illustrated that technology and operation factors are the major concerns in biomedical smart sensors and related devices manufacturing. Solving these issues act as a precondition for achieving satisfactory levels of device stability, efficiency, and consumer enjoyment. There are a number of technical and operation challenges within these technologies affecting the application and uptake of these solutions.

Accuracy and reliability of the sensors that are used in developing biomedical devices are always questionable. Biosensors should be accurate in order to detect physiologic parameters such as temperature, heart rate, and blood glucose. The variations of the sensors can be caused by aspects such as environmental, users, and degradation of the devices. These problems call for frequent calibration and maintenance for the system to offer a standard performance. Future developments include improved method of calibration and the invention of sensors with self-calibration in a bid to minimize the number of manual adjustments.

The data fusion derived from multiple sensors and devices present tremendous technical difficulties. Mobile biomedical devices, in particular, require integration with EHR systems as well as other healthcare applications and technologies. The interoperability of the systems is a significant challenge to ensure that the whole picture of patient health condition is represented. It is important to establish unified formats and communication protocols in order to make integrations and consequently data from various sources can be combined and analyzed. Solving the issues of interaction involves the action of manufacturers of devices, application developers, and healthcare institutions to set universal norms.

Battery management is always a major issue in the use of wearable and implantable biomedical systems because of their need for long-time use. Short battery life affects the device's functionality and, often, user adherence, especially for devices that need to be used for extended periods continuously. Battery technology, electronic parts efficiency, and efficient power use are central to prolong battery longevity and lessen the need to charge batteries. This has prompted researchers to look for other solutions of power supply, including using power from body movements, to improve device sustainability.
