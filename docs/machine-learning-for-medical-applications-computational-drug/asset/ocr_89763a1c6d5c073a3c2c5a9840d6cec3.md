# 2.3.2 Wearable and implantable device compatibility

Biomedical sensors are thus developed to be incorporated in wearable and implantable devices, which makes the choices beyond chronic disease monitoring flexible. Smartwatches and fitness trackers offer product-oriented methods for permanent body surveillance that are nonintrusive. While the wearable sensors provide external and physically tangible measurements of the body, implantable sensors provide slightly more direct measurement over the internal physiological conditions, like intracranial pressure and heart rate. They also make it possible to monitor virtually any condition on a continuum, from simple check-and-balance monitoring of healthy lifestyles for busy people to severe chronic disease management.

# 2.3.3 Transmission of data and material and person-to-person interaction

Success in the communication to the healthcare providers of the sensor data is crucial for detailing the health status. Biomedical sensors are enhanced with communication functionalities that depend on Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, etc. to send information to remote monitoring systems. This connectivity ensures that the data is transferred on time and securely, thus improving the on-time retrieval of information to make decisions regarding patient care.

# 2.3.4 Personalization and adaptation

Contemporary biomedical sensors are created in a way that they capture patient’s needs in a particular healthcare situation. These sensors can be adjusted based on the various features related to the physiologic attributes and pathophysiologic conditions of the patients. For instance, individual algorithms are used in adjusting the monitoring parameters to the acuteness and specificity of the patient’s conditions, increasing the applicability and efficiency of the collected information.
