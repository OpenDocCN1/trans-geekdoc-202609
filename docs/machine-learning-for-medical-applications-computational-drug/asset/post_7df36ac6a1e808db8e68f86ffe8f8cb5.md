Personalized medicine is not only an effort to bring improvements to treatment methods but also attempts to avoid diseases through early detection of at-risk individuals due to difference in genetic and molecular characteristics [5].

An important part of large-scale individualized medicine is the development of targeted drug delivery systems to deliver agents with therapeutic activity and minimize the side-effect profiles [6]. These technologies apply proteomics and genetics knowledge to control the method and form of medication administration, according to individual patient characteristics [7]. For enhancing treatment outcomes and reducing the chances of drug-induced side effects, novel carriers can be designed to deliver the drug selectively to the specific target, say tumor tissues [8]. It is easier to design such systems today since nanotechnology and biomaterials have improved to give exact control over the rate of medication release and the targeting strategies [9]. Personalized medicine distribution also improves the general patient experience and patient outcomes since they are less susceptible to negative effects, and receive better therapeutic end-products [10].

Also, it has to be mentioned that the combination of multi-omics data, including genomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, is a major leap forward in the progress of making personal medicine [11]. Here, the omics data are integrated in a way that takes into account the biological context to better understand the systems view of biology and disease [12]. By combining these three approaches, genomic data can be mapped and compared to proteomics and metabolomics, which helps researchers and clinicians define new biomarkers, learn more about the dynamics of the disease process, and create new and precise diagnostic and therapeutic protocols [13]. For example, the association of mutations with the expression levels of proteins and alterations in metabolic profile facilitates the understanding of the development and pathogenesis of diseases [14]. Such an approach allows the definition of the corresponding therapeutic regimens that are applicable singly for each client, depending on the molecular markers, thus providing more effective and individualized approaches to treatment [15].

In this context, it can be said that technology is contributing to the development of personal medicine due to the provision of the instruments necessary for the assessment of other complex bioinformatics and the subsequent implementation of these findings in practice [16]. The opportunity to ascertain whole genomes at a very high speed and a lower cost is now available, which make a significant shift in genomic analysis, enabling advances in sequencing technologies like NGS [17]. Additional comprehension on proteins and its activities are also facilitated by proteomics technologies such as mass spectrometry and protein microarrays in large-scale research [18]. By applying ML and AI algorithms, patterns and predictions to support differentiated treatment decisions are identified [19, 20]. Advances in these technologies are well used in the provision of customized medicine and in improving patients’ experiences.

It is expected that research and innovations go on unfolding and strengthening, expanding the application of personalized medicines more in the future [21]. There is a growing prospect for futuristic technologies such as gene editing with technologies like CRISPR-Cas9, use of multiple omics data, and integration of precision oncology [22]. These should enhance the precision of patient care, which includes treatment and diagnosis of illnesses, in the future [23]. It also has its drawbacks, for instance, some issues of the ethical character of data privacy in the process of machine learning; the equitable distribution of precision medicine; and most importantly, the need for proper setting of protocols in this field [24]. Meeting these challenges is important for maintaining the further evolution of personalized medicine as safe and accessible for everyone [25]. It is the accurate, efficient, and customer-oriented treatment that is said to be delivered by the march of what is known as personalized medicine.

## 2 Historical evolution of personalized medicine

### 2.1 Early developments in personalized medicine

The genesis of the idea of PM can be dated back to the systematic inclusion of patient characteristics into medical practice through rudimentary means. The notion of individualized medicine can be traced back to the ancient civilizations, where people's standards of management of diseases included initial adaptation of management according to patient’s distinguishing features and manifestations. The concept of developing dynamically unique medicine for every person has been in practice with the improvement of scientific and up-to-date technology.

The foundation of modern genetics and its relevance to health and disease was established in the early nineteenth century with Gregor Mendel’s discovery of inheritance patterns. Mendelian genetics introduced the fundamental principles of heredity, demonstrated through Mendel’s experiments on pea plants. These foundational concepts remain essential to the advancement of personalized medicine. ### 2.2 The role of genomics in shaping modern personalized medicine

Genomics have helped the development of the modern so-called individualized medicine, as it has transformed the conventional paradigms of disease management, diagnosis and treatment. The reflection on the novelty of genomics in the medical practice is evident, given that the availability of this sort of technology enables much better understanding of the relevant genetic roots of numerous diseases and the subsequent more refined technique of approaching their treatments.

### 2.2.1 Newer methods of genomic sequencing

The prospective and innovation of analyzing DNA sequences as well as their capacity has been enhanced by advancements in genomic sequencing, including first-generation Sanger technique to the second-generation NGS. The discipline is said to have experienced a revolution because of NGS technology, enabling the rapid and comprehensive sequencing of whole genomes, whole exomes, and targeted regions. This skill has facilitated the identification of research-based treatment strategies through establishing a variety of disease-associated genetic variants and thereby contributing to the easy development of alternative treatment profiles for each patient.

### 2.2.2 Discovery of gene signatures and diseases links

Genomic research has explained the complicated relationship between genes and susceptibility to illness by identifying these variations. By organizing variations in the form of insertions, deletions, and single nucleotide polymorphisms, several researchers have been able to associate some genetic anomalies with certain diseases. Based on this information, genetic markers that are important for risk assessment, diagnosis, and development of disease-specific patient treatments have been discovered.
