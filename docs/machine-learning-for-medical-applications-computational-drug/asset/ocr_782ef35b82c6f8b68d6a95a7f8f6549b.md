# 6.3 Cost and accessibility

The way therapies are going to be broader adopted and incorporated in the theory and practice of medicine is challenged by significant impediments associated with costs and availability of customs-designed medications. Owing to reliance on genetic, environmental, and lifestyle attributes to ascribe care profiles, personalized care medicine requires advanced technology and expertise often. Such aspects put costs of personalized healthcare solutions higher and limit the range of access to them for many clients and providers.

Among all the barriers experienced by advanced-heart-failure patients, the difficulty of kin + cost factors rose to the top, and one of the primary concerns is the high costs of genomic sequencing and genetic testing. Sequencing costs have declined over the last 10 years, but the price of sequencing a human genome is still out of reach for most individuals and healthcare organizations in LMICs. The understanding and analysis of genomic data necessitates integration of bioinformatics as well as personnel, which escalate the expenses for personalized medicine.

Also, the identification of the disease-specific and individualized therapies and treatment regimens require significant amount of investment and testing. Biomedical firms spend a great deal of money in finding and developing individual substances and creating targeted therapies; most of the time, the patented medicines are expensive. Compared to traditional economic model targeted diseases tend to be smaller patient groups with unique genetic makeup, meaning the economic potential of personalized medicine to a particular treatment is likely to be less compared to the cost per patient.

The third significant challenge lies in the issue of availability: where individuals are not offered anything resembling the so-described “personalized care,” disparities are likely to increase already existing disparities in health. Perhaps, patients who are from the underprivileged groups receive fewer treatment from different specialists and information pertinent for personalizing healthcare. Socio-economic factors affect a patient’s ability to afford the diagnostic tests and the targeted treatment plans leading to disparities in the benefits of personalized medicine.
