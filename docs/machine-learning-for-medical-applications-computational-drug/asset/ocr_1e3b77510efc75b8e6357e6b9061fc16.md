Titanium and its alloys: Titanium is one of the more frequently used metals in biomedical applications, especially in the fabrication of dental prostheses as well as orthopedic implants. It has been stated that titanium is highly biocompatible due to the formation of a persistent oxide layer on the surface of the material, which minimizes corrosion and enhances its ability to bond with bone tissue. Due to their superior mechanical characteristics, including high strength and low density, alloys such as Ti-6Al-4V are incorporated in bearing critical loads. Increased bone formation and integration make titanium implants stable enough to achieve a long-term service life and high survival rate.

Stainless steel: The third metal that was used in medical devices was stainless steel, especially the 316L grade steel. Through enhanced chemical properties, outstanding mechanical properties, and excellent workability, it finds practical application in a number of fields; these include surgical equipment, external fixators, and vascular stents. Nevertheless, stainless steel exhibits fairly better biocompatibility than other metals used in industrial applications, but its efficiency is slightly lower, particularly in highly corrosive environments due to repeated contact with body fluids. To address these challenges, specialized coatings and surface modifications were employed to improve its biocompatibility.
