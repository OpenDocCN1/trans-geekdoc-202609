# 3.2.4 Therapeutic methods and techniques

Medical treatments in traditional medicine are automatically developed in line with treatment plans that result from clinical trials with multiple patient group specimens. These strategies are intended to benefit most patients, but say nothing about the pharmacogenetic variations that define individual metabolism and drug effects. Personalized medicine is aimed at selecting the best possible treatment regimen, based on the patient’s individual traits, including genetic profile, biomarkers, and other aspects of lifestyle. It helps them make choices of what treatments to offer and go for the ones that work best for the patient, without many side effects.

# 3.2.5 Monitoring and adaptation

In traditional applications, change and follow up of management strategies are generally residual and are based on patient’s responses to the planned management at successive clinic visits. It is less flexible, but changes occur, as needed; it may be slower and less specific than the case above. Personalized medicine is more of a preventative strategy and involves the use of real-time data and analytics on patients’ responses to treatment, so adjustment can be made instantaneously. This means that there is real-time variation in the kind of treatment being applied depending, on the daily performance of the patients and their changing health conditions, thus achieving better patient care as well as health outcomes.
