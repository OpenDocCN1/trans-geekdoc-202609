Community governance: Community governance models select the participation of a larger and organized community of stakeholders in the governance of kinds of blockchain-based EHR systems, including the healthcare providers, patients, developer, and researcher. Governance communities include the open source, contributors for the development, the decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs), and community steering that directs the design, development, and the steering of the blockchain network that permits stakeholders to participate in. Community governance frameworks involve openness and the development of effective involvement of participants in the decision-making process about the management model of the EHR system.

Federated governance: Consortium and community types of governance are distinguished as well but with the focus on the idea, which lets several independent entities retain control over their nodes within the overall federated blockchain network. Federated governance allows organizations to remain independent and sovereign over their data and action while engaging in cooperation activities at the level of federation. Overlapping mechanisms include the creation of the governance structures, policies, procedures, and interfaces for management, communication, and consensus among the federated members.

Hybrid governance: There is a blend of consortium/community/federated/hierarchical governance models where different governance models are combined in line with the strengths required to suit the needs of blockchain-incorporated EHR systems – introducing hybrid governance models that provide for structuring the required tiers of governance at the consortium, community, organizational, levels of the blockchain network. This is because hybrid systems offer flexibility, customization, and opportunities for organizations of every structure and in different jurisdictions to adapt and meet the needs of all stakeholders and legislation.

# 6.1 Importance of data integrity in healthcare data management

In fact, the consistency and dependability of the patient information as it cycles through its life is the reason for data integrity, which is an important function in healthcare data management. At this juncture, it is equally important for the quality, reliability, and accessibility of healthcare data in view of EHR systems from which patient care, clinical decision-making, and compliance can be derived.
