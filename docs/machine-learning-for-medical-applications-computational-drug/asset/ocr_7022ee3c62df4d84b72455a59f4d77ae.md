Boosting is another method of ensemble learning, which requires using a number of weak learners several times, and putting their solutions together to form one powerful model. Each one effectively increases the overall performance of the ensemble by focusing on the cases where the earlier models misclassified each weak learner gradually. Since gradient boosting methods can support the high-order and nonlinear connections, with various data types, they are applied for hit recognition tasks. Some of these algorithms are XGBoost and LightGBM.

Stacking, often referred to as meta-learning, is a flexible method of ensemble learning that employs the outcomes of many base models integrated as input features of a meta-learner. The idea is in learning the meta-learner that combines the base models' predictions in a way that takes the advantage and mitigates the disadvantages of the base models. For further improvement of integrated hit detection performance, stacking can be used to combine results of multiple machine learning methods, models specific to the chemical domain, and other heuristics and rules based on expert knowledge.

Also, the identification of hit for the development of drugs incorporates random forest, which is an ensemble learning method generated by the decision tree. More decision trees are created from randomly selected characteristics and samples based on random forests that incorporate to come up with the final result. The use of random forests is suitable to solve detection of hits with high-dimensional data and complicated relations but combined prediction of many decision trees increases prediction accuracy and decreases the probability of overfitting.
