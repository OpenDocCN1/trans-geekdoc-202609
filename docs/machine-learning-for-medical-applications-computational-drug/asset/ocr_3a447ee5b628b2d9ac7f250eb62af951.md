Two important enabling technologies are miniaturization and microfabrication, which play an important role in the further development of biomedical sensors. MEMS and nanotechnology are in the forefront in these developments, thus making it possible to incorporate both the sensors and actuators within single highly effective devices. MEMS allow for manufacturing of miniaturized elements in the form of sensors and actuators with high sensitivity and accuracy, as used in self-monitoring and diagnosing devices such as blood glucose monitors and health monitoring wearable devices. Nanotechnology can help in the integration of nanoscale sensors as well as other devices that exhibit better performance features; higher sensitivity and selectivity being examples.

Among the advanced manufacturing technologies, the so-called additive manufacturing has recently gained a status of a revolutionary technological tool in biomedical devices fabrication. This technique makes it possible to build thin-walled structures with close contours and unimpeded through-holes with high accuracy, and detail additive technologies such as 3D printing are used to produce implants, prosthetics, and surgical instruments, which have the required dimensions identical to the individual patient's needs. Materials utilized in 3D printing for biomedical applications are biodegradable polymers, metals, and ceramics. The capacity to rapidly model and produce individualized gadgets promotes overall effectiveness in device development and the benefits experienced by the patient.

The advancement of stretchable and flexible electronics has led the way for the prolonged biomedical use of wearable and implantable applications. These technologies involve the use of new material, which is then processed in new methods to make devices that can bend, to fold around the body and also bend with the motions. Flexible electronics are generally based on materials such as organic semiconductors and conductive polymers because they are both flexible and offer conductivity. Examples of such metamaterials-based applications are wearable health monitoring systems, bendable sensors for physiological signal acquisition, and smart clothing with inbuilt electronics. The enhancement of flexible electronics increases comfort for the end-users and device performance in biomedical application.

Manufacturing quality and reliability are very critical in ensuring that biomedical devices meet certain safety and performance standards when used. Modern production operations minimize manufacturing variation by utilizing technology such as automated inspection system, statistical quality control, and failure analysis. This involves controlling how institutions and their accessories get contaminated in a very high standard, to minimize this aspect's effect on the devices. Compliance with the requirements, as prescribed by the FDA and ISO, is critical in achieving devises’ reliability and guaranteeing that it is suitable for use in clinical practice.
