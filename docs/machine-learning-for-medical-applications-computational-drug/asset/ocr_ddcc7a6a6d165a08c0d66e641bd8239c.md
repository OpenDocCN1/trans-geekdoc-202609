# 3.4.3 Voting mechanisms: opportunities and challenges with a focus on voting optimization

Despite the benefits voting mechanisms present, there were some issues that the authors came across. One major concern was the issue of model congeniality – that is, models with similar statistical appearances overwhelmed the bags, to the disadvantage of the ideal balance required to enhance the stability of the ensembles. In reference to this, appropriate selection and diversity in the models included in the ensemble are the correct measures. Another problem was the calculation of weights in the case of weighted voting, where weights must be tuned and then validated to ensure that the ensemble’s performance was indeed optimized.

The difficulties of functional form dependence on the scalability of voting mechanisms arise if the number of data points becomes large. To cope with the growing complexity of the movements involved in large ensembles, hardware computational and storage factors must be factored in. Ongoing research works proposed in adaptive voting methods, where weights and voting strategies are changed according to the data and prediction context, help in overcoming the above-discussed problems and provide better results for the ensemble models used in material science.

Regarding the voting mechanisms, it was mentioned that the future of voting mechanisms in material property predictions is expected to experience new developments due to the use of technology in voting mechanisms and material science. There is potential for the enhancement of these approaches, including the dynamic voting procedure, which is capable of adapting depending on the specifics of the data, the structure of the prediction task, and meta-learning, where the ensemble also identifies the voting method that is appropriate for the input.
