Classification based on the nature of interaction:

Invasive sensors: These kinds can be implanted into the body with the purpose of directly measuring physiologic variables, e.g., glucose monitors under the skin.

Noninvasive sensors: These sensors do not penetrate the skin’s surface and include wear and tear heart monitors, pulse oximeters, and thermometers.

Minimally invasive sensors: These sensors do not have to be embedded into the body; they only require contact with the body, like micro-needle glucose monitoring sensors within the body cavities.

Classification based on sensing mechanism:

Electrochemical sensors: These types of sensors use electrochemical process for the determination of determined analytes in body fluids, which are used in glucose and lactate determination.

Optical sensors: Sensors of this type rely on optical methods to measure changes of biological tissues, for example, pulse oximeter and optical coherence tomography.

Piezoelectric sensors: These sensors translate mechanical stress into electrical signals, and are widely applied in pressure sensing and motion detection.

Capacitive sensors: These sensors measure changes in capacitance due to movements of the body tissues; they are used in touch switches and respiratory rate measuring devices.

Magnetic sensors: These sensors detect magnetic fields resulting from physiological processes, and are used in magnetoencephalography (MEG) and such other equipment.

This extensive classification demonstrates the available variety of biomedical sensors for constant surveillance, which proves the specificity and specialization of the sensors, aimed at addressing multiple clinical demands. It is therefore important to identify and define the various types and classifications of such sensors in order to advance and improve the implementation of continuous health monitoring systems, thereby enhancing personalized healthcare solutions.
