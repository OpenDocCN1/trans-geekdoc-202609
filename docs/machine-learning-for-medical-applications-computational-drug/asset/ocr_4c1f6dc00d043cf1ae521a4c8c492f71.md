Among the most transformative advancements in recent biotechnology, CRISPR-Cas9 stands out as a revolutionary tool in the field of personalized medicine. As depicted in Figure 9, this gene-editing technology enables precise modifications to specific regions of the genome, making it possible to correct mutations responsible for various genetic disorders. For patients with inherited conditions, CRISPR allows scientists to edit or replace defective DNA sequences, paving the way for the development of individualized, genetically tailored therapies that directly address each patient’s unique genetic profile.

Personalized medicine is one of the main applications of CRISPR, and one of its main areas of use is in treating genetic diseases. The usefulness of such techniques includes being able to cure diseases such as sickle cell anemia and cystic fibrosis, among others that are as a result of single gene defects. CRISPR technology offers the opportunity to work on cure rather than symptoms – this is in contrast to treatments based on genetic prescription built to address the specific genetic abnormalities in every individual. Current scientific and clinical investigations are underway to determine the possibility and efficacy of these gene modification techniques in the management of different inherited diseases.

CRISPR and other gene-editing technologies are being used not only in individualized cancer therapy but also heredity issues. Cancer develops because of the specific genetic changes that are inherent in most cancerous tissues.
