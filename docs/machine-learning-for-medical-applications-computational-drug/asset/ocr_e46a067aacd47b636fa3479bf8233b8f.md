# 4 AI Applications in molecular docking and binding affinity prediction

# 4.1 Deep learning models for protein-ligand binding prediction

In molecular docking and binding affinity prediction, deep learning models have emerged as potent tools in enhancing the accuracy of prediction of interactions of ligands and proteins. Conventional methods of machine learning fail to accurately estimate binding affinities as more complex interactions in protein and ligand structures can be identified by deep learning using neural networks with multiple layers of abstraction.

CNN is one of the widely used deep learning structures in protein-ligand interaction prediction. CNNs are particularly effective in detecting regional features and peculiarities in protein-ligand complexes designed for analyzing spatial information, including molecular structures. CNNs can learn the hierarchical representation of the protein-ligand interactions and extract the proper features for the binding affinity prediction as a result of applying filters for convolution to the molecular input representations. While making an accurate prediction of binding affinities and identifying these interaction residues in the binding site of the protein, the CNN-based models have been shown to be superior.

GNN was another deep learning technique in the prediction of protein-ligand interaction. Since chemical structures are graphs and GNNs are designed to work with graph structured data, GNNs are ideal for this purpose. GNNs are able to predict the binding affinities and also simultaneously capture the chemical and spatial interactions of the protein-ligand interface interaction by compounding information about atoms and bonds in the proximity. The assessment of GNN-based models shows potential in shedding light on the structural aspects of protein-ligand interactions and achieving reliable binding conformations.

The RNNs and long short-term memory (LSTM) network have been used in procedures in which predictions of interaction between protein ligand are involved. Since RNNs and especially LSTMs allow modeling time dependencies and sequence properties it is reasonable to use it to model dynamic processes such as ligand binding and unbinding. Using sequential data representations of protein-ligand complexes, RNN-based models can forecast binding affinities and the poses of ligands with increased precision when targeting flexible complexes of ligands.

The information- and data-fusion approach using a combination of CNNs, GNNs, and RNNs was used for protein-ligand binding predictions. These hybrid models use the advantages of more than one network architecture to create rich representation of protein-ligand interactions and optimize the prediction accuracy. The current work demonstrates the effectiveness of the hybrid deep learning models in achieving better accuracy and reliable binding affinity and binding pose predictions for drug discovery.
