Secondly, machine learning algorithms are good at handling big data. Radiology work and dealing with diagnostic images lead to the production of large amounts of data, especially when high-resolution images are used. The availability of large data sets is well managed with machine learning techniques since they do not require tools to manipulate big data but rather computational resources. This scalability is important for instances where it is necessary to compile models on various data while imaging.

Another strength was that the different machine learning models could operate well across different data sets and imaging environments. Traditional methods are not efficient in comparing variability in imaging protocols, patient demographics, and equipment used. Over time, machine learning algorithms can learn from various datasets with a view to enhancing their resilience and versatility. Additional advancements in this capability are achieved through techniques such as transfer learning, where models sourced from one dataset can be reused with other imaging modalities.

Multimodality data fusion was also enabled by machine learning. It is possible in holistic diagnostics to obtain more objective data about a patient’s state in the field of medical imaging by combining one patient’s CT, PET, and MRI data. Multimodal learning helps to increase the accuracy of diagnosis and enables more adequate therapeutic choices by learning the complementary information obtained from different modalities.
