MEMS technology offers several advantages in biomedical applications.

## 4.4 Advances in wireless and battery-free sensor technologies

Modern developments in wireless and battery-less sensor technologies are expanding to new technological frontiers in biomedical monitoring by extending the potential for conveying a steady flow of information about people's health, while avoiding the problems of inconvenience and limitations associated with batteries. These innovations help to solve some issues that include power constraints, integration of small devices, and handling of real-time data.

### 4.4.1 Wireless communication technologies

The lack of cables is another important characteristic of today's biomedical sensors since they all rely on wireless schemes. Several advancements have significantly improved the performance and reliability of wireless sensor systems.

- Bluetooth low energy (BLE): Based on the current literature, BLE technology is accepted as a standard for low-energy and short-range communication in wearable sensors. This makes it suitable in devices with high frequency data update requirement, whilst supporting long battery life.
- Near-field communication (NFC): NFC technology enables short-range high-frequency wireless communication between various sensors and other devices. It is used in such areas as body glucose monitoring without physical contact and identification of patients, where data transfer has to be fast and secure.
- Zigbee and LoRaWAN: These protocols are used in sensor networks that entail high-range transmission and minimal power usage. Zigbee is ideal for devices that need multiple connections with sensors within the same personal area network, while LoRaWAN is ideal for data transfer across expansive areas, as required in remote health monitoring.
- 5G connectivity: The emergence of 5G technology improves the data channeling process by providing increased speed of transmitting data with low latency, making the wireless sensors more effective for conveying real-time health information. This advancement aids the growth of complex health monitoring systems that need processing and feedback with little delay.
- Wireless power transfer (WPT): The advancement in WPT technology can allow the charging of sensors wirelessly such that they do not require battery replacement. This is especially helpful in implantable sensors and in long-term wearable devices.

### 4.4.2 Battery-less sensor technologies

Battery-less sensors rely on alternative energy sources to operate without the need for conventional batteries. These technologies overcome the limitations of battery life and significantly reduce the need for frequent maintenance. - RFID technology: Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) sensors work when the devices receive energy from an external radio frequency field. These sensors are commonly integrated in low-power use applications like monitoring medical implants and intelligent IDs for patients.
- Solar-powered sensors: Wearable sensors with incorporated solar cells can use the available light to produce energy. This technique is particularly beneficial for sensors situated in environments of natural light, decreasing the usage of batteries and increasing the equipment's lifespan.
- Electromagnetic induction: In this technique, a current is created in the components of the sensor by applying electromagnetic fields, where the components do not have their own power source. It is useful in diverse sensor structures such as those that are applied in health-focused devices, including health monitors.
- Biofuel cells: Biofuel cells produce electricity through bio-redox reactions, for example, glucose, and can be employed to supply electrical energy to implantable biosensors. This technology is based on sustainable energy delivery system, completely mimicking body's own chemistry for charging batteries that are used for powering up the metabolic parameter sensors.

Advancements in miniaturization and integration technologies are enhancing the capabilities of wireless and battery-free sensors:

- MEMS: MEMS allows making small sensor/actuator elements with intrinsic wireless communication possibilities. The capacitive and resonant characteristics of MEMS-based sensors are utilized due to their efficient functioning and higher tolerance for miniaturization for wearable and implantable medical applications.
- Flexible electronics: The advancement of flexible and stretchable electroactive material enables the formation of skin-like sensors that can easily conform to different body surfaces of a patient. These allow designing of flexible sensors that people could wear for long-term use without discomfort and which can move with the body.
- System-on-chip (SoC) integration: The system-on-chip technology applies many functionalities and communications of the sensor and its components in a single chip, cutting the size of complex sensor systems. This integration optimizes the usage of such wireless as well as battery-less sensors and enhances their efficacy in real-life applications.

## Improvement of the product's durability and reliability

Technological advancements have also focused on improving the durability and reliability of wireless and battery-free sensors:

- Robust packaging materials: Innovations in the packaging of the sensors prevent them from the effects of moisture as well as mechanical stress, improving the durability of the sensors.
- Self-healing materials: Integrating concepts of self-healing materials in a sensor help to increase the durability of devices and make necessary repairs to minor damages to sensors.
- Temperature and humidity tolerance: The development of different material of sensors and their structure has enhanced the ability to withstand different climates and temperature levels, which widely enhance using the sensors in different types of environments.

These developments in wireless and battery-less sensor platforms are opening up new opportunities for better, longer lasting and more diverse biomedical monitoring applications. These solutions help address problems in terms of power infrastructure, scale, and data transfer, and thus improve the performance of current health monitoring systems.

## 4.5 Hybrid and multimodal sensor systems for comprehensive monitoring

Hybrid and multimodal sensors are one level up in biomedical monitoring, providing greater capabilities due to the ability to evaluate the patient's condition more thoroughly. These systems incorporate many sensing subsystems to monitor and/or analyze the parameters of human body to overcome the drawbacks of single-mode sensor.
