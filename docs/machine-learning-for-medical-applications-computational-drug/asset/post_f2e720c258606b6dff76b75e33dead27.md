## 6 Data integrity and auditability in blockchain-based EHR systems

### 6.1 Importance of data integrity in healthcare data management

In fact, the consistency and dependability of the patient information as it cycles through its life is the reason for data integrity, which is an important function in healthcare data management. At this juncture, it is equally important for the quality, reliability, and accessibility of healthcare data in view of EHR systems from which patient care, clinical decision-making, and compliance can be derived.

Single point of failure makes centralization a danger to data integrity. The key problems in using a central design are coordination, rerouting, and backup, which means that data could be compromised not only when the system was down, but also due to hardware failure.

Lack of transparency: Standard EHR systems are essentially opaque and do not readily provide audit trails of records where modifications have been made. Since there are no obvious audit trails it was very hard to determine the genuineness, origin, and sanctity of EHR data. Batching entry, feature variety, and various modes of documentation lead to lack of standardization, system error, and a lack of metadata about modifications, thus posing threats to the validity and reliability of data.

Limited interoperability: Problems of interfacing in traditional EHR systems restrict the communication and transfer of information concerning the patient between the healthcare organizations and systems. Most of the data is in incompatible formats and applications use proprietary standards and have data in different silos, which makes integration and correlation of data a nightmare. Inadequate data exchange specifications and interface connection limit interconnectivity of EHRs, thereby affecting the quality of information shared and coherence of patient care.

Data security risks: Legacy traditional EHR are vulnerable to many factors of the data insecurity such as hacking, exposure, and even cyberattacks. Lack of strong authentication, poor control of access, and limited encryption mean that EHR data can be compromised by being released or changed. Independent threats include other risk sources, which are internal adversaries, motivated actors, and personnel mistakes whereby insiders expose the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of patient data.

Limited data validation: Typical models of EHRs do not have sufficient features for data validation to maintain the correctness, comprehensiveness, and coherence of patients’ records. Traditional methods of data entry, pre-implementation data validation rules, and an overall limited data validation scale increase the chances of data entry error, omission, and inconsistency. Lacking automated validation components in data quality checks, such problems in EHR data remain unseen, thus impairing the complexity, quality, and general soundness of the data.

### 6.3 Ensuring blockchain data integrity and immutability

One of the promising strategies to ensure data consistency and verified inalterability of entries in EHR systems is applying the blockchain mechanism. The methods listed below show how blockchain accomplishes these goals:

Decentralized and Distributed Ledger: Blockchain stores data across multiple nodes in a network, forming a decentralized and distributed ledger. This structure is achieved through a chain of blocks, where each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous one, creating a tamper-evident link between blocks. This decentralized nature enhances data integrity and resistance to tampering, as no single entity has full control over the data, a key advantage of blockchain technology.

Cryptographic hashing: Blockchain employs cryptographic hash algorithms to generate unique digital fingerprints, known as hashes, for each block of data. These hashes are determined based on the block’s content, making it virtually impossible to alter the data without affecting the hash. If any modification occurs, the block’s hash changes, alerting the network to potential tampering thereby maintaining trust and immutability across the system.

Consensus procedures: The additional transactions flowing into the blockchain, their sequence, and legitimacy are confirmed by consensus procedures, which form the basis of block chains. There are agreement mechanisms such as proof of work (PoW), proof of stake (PoS), and Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) that ensures that node members are in agreement regarding the current state of the blockchain. Interested parties cannot manipulate the information that was stored in the systems since the blockchain negates such actions by allowing only the nodes in the system to approve such changes.

Immutable data records: The beauty of the blockchain technology is that once data is entered on the blockchain, it cannot be changed. New blocks are added to the blockchain at the core, in a sequential chain and are nearly impossible to manipulate without consensus of the entire network to the historical records. The article explains that these characteristics guarantee confidentiality of data and the EHR network’s reliability since any alteration of records is easily identified by other members of the network.

Transparent audit trails: Blockchain makes it possible to guard and tag all business transactions and even changes to the information in the EHR. It maintains a visible log of who accessed or modified the data provided by the timestamp and digital signature inherent in every block of a blockchain transaction. This allows stakeholders to verify the authenticity and origins of data, which enhance accountability, traceability, and credibility of the EHR data.

Data encryption and access controls: These EHR systems normally utilize encryption and access controls, which belong to reliable structures, to secure the patient’s data. Database information is in encrypted form with cryptographic keys to help maintain the privacy of the data. Smart contracts’ distributed ledger system-based access control limits the ability of users to access EHR data and thus increases security, data accuracy, and privacy.
