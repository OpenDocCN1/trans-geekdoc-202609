# 4.5 Key challenges in applying machine learning to medical images

When it comes to applying machine learning to medical imaging, one is bound to encounter a myriad of challenges that cause the novelty and effectiveness of these methods to stumble. To increase the efficiency and apply the characteristics of machine learning systems in medical imaging, we need to solve the following question.

Radiological diagnosis information was often criticized in terms of the quality and variability of data acquired from medical imaging. Indeed, some of the differences in picture features are due to differences in patient situations, equipment parameters, and techniques. This unpredictability makes it difficult for machine learning algorithms to work as they require reliable and quality data to learn from. A common problem of identified models that work well in isolation and patterns, yet fail to address a range of clinical experiences, could be due to inconsistent data.

This chapter provides an overview of data annotation and labeling that is important in the formulation of machine learning models. Annotation of medical images is also a time-consuming process that typically necessitates the services of expert radiologists. This suggests that four possible reasons slow down the availability of training data: annotated datasets are rare, and manually labeling them is expensive. Further, inconsistencies in labeling lower the model’s reliability and produce incorrect results.

Of all the AI technologies, especially machine learning models, deep learning algorithms, for instance, have been described as hard to explain. Due to the fact that these models are inherently “black-box,” it is hard to understand how and why decisions are being made – this makes it that much harder for clinical practitioners to accept. Machine learning must be easily integrated with note-keeping, with clinicians having the capability of comprehending and authenticating the results generated by the algorithms. Developing ways to increase the explainability of such models is still a significant challenge.
