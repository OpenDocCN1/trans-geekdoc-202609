
## 3 Core concepts and definitions

### 3.1 Defining personalized medicine

The modern medical practice that goes by the name of precision medicine means that therapy matches the individual patient. This idea refers to the decision-making process, treatment, and therapy, individualized to a patient’s genetic predisposition, living environment, and personal traits. Personalized medicine is not identical with the medical model that tends to provide the same treatment for millions of patients, while considering certain peculiarities in an individual in order to enhance the effectiveness of the treatments.

Personalized medicine is basically about integrating a multitude of data – genomic, proteomic, and metabolomic – to drive medical decisions. For instance, genetic data can reveal information about how likely the individual will respond to a specific treatment, and what is the probability of the individual showing adverse effects to medication. With this information, healthcare professionals enhance the protective strategies, organize more directed and effective therapeutic processes, and improve clients’ outcomes.

As part of delivering customized treatment, the integration of advanced diagnostic tools and high-throughput technologies is essential for obtaining accurate and comprehensive insights into a patient’s condition. As illustrated in Figure 3, this includes the use of high-throughput screening (HTS) to identify diverse biomarkers and next-generation sequencing (NGS) to perform in-depth genome analysis. These technological advancements enhance the understanding of each individual’s unique biological characteristics and support the development of personalized treatment strategies tailored to specific patient profiles.

Personalized medicine is larger than the genetic approach to the patient; the concept integrates the patient, the physician, and the healthcare system. It involves blending the objective client-centered health parameters, physician-registered health status, and the context in which the individual exists. To this end, medical therapies are aligned to treat comorbid medical conditions, lifestyles, environments, and genetic proclivity, among other relevant aspects.

Personalized medicine includes the factors of stratified medicine, which divides patient groups by some criterion, to be more effective. As such, this stratification promotes the effectiveness and safety of medical treatments by targeting patient groups that are likely to benefit from specific therapy techniques.

### 3.2 Differentiating personalized medicine from traditional medicine

Personalized treatment and conventional treatment are two systems that are different in terms of methods employed and the goals addressed. It is important to learn the differences between these approaches to realize the progressive character and prospects of the work in the field of personalized medicine.

#### 3.2.1 Treatment and treatment plan/philosophy

Conventional medicine relies on prescriptive principles and most treatment approaches are drawn from controlled scientific studies that involve a large pool of patients. It implements impersonal evidence and technique to solve acute conditions and diseases. Whereas, the latter concept of disease management is governed by the philosophy of ‘Personalized Medicine,’ where treatment regimens are developed according to the patient’s specific attributes and conditions. This approach is based on the knowledge that genetic as well as environmental factors and habits play a certain role in disease distribution and treatment outcomes. Personalized medicine seeks to achieve the best results to be obtained from therapeutic interventions after considering such aspects of an individual.

#### 3.2.2 Role of genetic information

In traditional care system, treatment choices are made in accordance with different signs and symptoms, disease diagnostic categories, approximate age, sex, and color of the patient. Whenever genetic information is to be taken into consideration, it is rarely at the core of a treatment plan. Personalized medicine is closely associated with genetic information. Personalized medicine tries to determine general biomarkers inherent in the human body due to genetic differences that affect diseases and medicines. This, then, is employed to come up with treatments and treatment plans for diseases that are more suitable according to the genetic makeup of the patient.

#### 3.2.3 Diagnosis and risk assessment

The diagnosing and risk evaluation in traditional medicine is not individualized, but generalized; diagnostic criteria and risk factors are the same for most patients. Though often useful for straightforward diseases and illnesses, this approach can easily exclude differences that are present in specific patients. At the human level, tailor-made therapy increases diagnostic precision and evaluation of the individual’s risk by using patients’ multiple genetic, genomic, and molecular profiles. It ensures that all deviations and inclinations that define disease development and progression are noted, resulting in accurate diagnoses and risk management.

#### 3.2.4 Therapeutic methods and techniques

Medical treatments in traditional medicine are automatically developed in line with treatment plans that result from clinical trials with multiple patient group specimens. These strategies are intended to benefit most patients, but say nothing about the pharmacogenetic variations that define individual metabolism and drug effects. Personalized medicine is aimed at selecting the best possible treatment regimen, based on the patient’s individual traits, including genetic profile, biomarkers, and other aspects of lifestyle. It helps them make choices of what treatments to offer and go for the ones that work best for the patient, without many side effects.

#### 3.2.5 Monitoring and adaptation

In traditional applications, change and follow-up of management strategies are generally reactive and are based on patients’ responses to the planned management at successive clinic visits. It is less flexible, but changes occur, as needed; it may be slower and less specific than the case above. Personalized medicine is more of a preventative strategy and involves the use of real-time data and analytics on patients’ responses to treatment, so adjustment can be made instantaneously. This means that there is real-time variation in the kind of treatment being applied depending on the daily performance of the patients and their changing health conditions, thus achieving better patient care as well as health outcomes.

### 3.3 Essential components of personalized treatment plans

Individualized treatment strategies aim to achieve the least harm and the most benefit from healthcare, dealing with the individual patients’ particular characteristics. Several crucial elements are involved in the formulation of these plans:

The optimal patient profiling mechanism is detailed patient profiling.

Many personalized treatment approaches rely on extensive patient profiling, which entails the collection and analysis of comprehensive data related to the patient’s condition. As illustrated in Figure 4, this includes demographic information, clinical symptoms, medical history, lifestyle habits, environmental exposures, as well as genetic, proteomic, and metabolomic data. Integrating these diverse data sources enables the creation of highly accurate and individualized treatment plans, as they provide a holistic view of the patient’s health profile.

#### 3.3.1 Molecular and genetic characterization

Molecular and genetic characterization is essential so as to identify certain molecular and genetic signatures that actually affect the disease process and the response to certain treatment regimens. As the name implies, various methodologies are used, including genomics, gene panels, and RNA profiling to help discover the mutated genes, gene activity, and molecular signaling routes. Such information assists in recommending an individual’s specific therapy and developing a treatment model that responds to the molecular characteristics.

#### 3.3.2 Individual therapy models

Integrated individualized therapies are components of the unique treatment. These cover choosing and fine-tuning the therapy according to the patient, his/her phenotype, tumor stage, and previous treatment outcomes. Therapies refer to targeted drugs that are used and individualized chemotherapy. The hypothetical aim of the personalized treatment is to design it in order to achieve the highest therapeutic effect and minimal toxicity based on the patient’s biology and clinical characteristics.

#### 3.3.3 Dynamic treatment adjustments

Flexible treatments are also necessary parts of individualized treatment plans since constant patient evaluation and response define the need for modifying the treatments’ approaches. This means that after clinical examination, imaging, and biomarkers, the treatment can be altered immediately, since it is a continuous process. This setup also guarantees that the therapeutic management continually adjusts for the patient’s changing status and response to treatment, thereby increasing the efficiency and safety of the total treatment process.

#### 3.3.4 Main provisions of the interdisciplinary approach

A prime necessity is enhancing the synthesis of multidisciplinary care structure for the formulation of care strategies. Multidisciplinary teamwork involves working with geneticists, oncologists, pharmacologists, and other specialists to fully address patients’ concerns. This structural approach promotes construction of integrated management plans due to the associated systemic relationships between genetic, environmental, and clinical factors.
