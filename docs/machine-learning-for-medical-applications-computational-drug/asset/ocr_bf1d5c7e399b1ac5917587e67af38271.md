CNNs have been increasingly studied for many different medical imaging tasks because of their efficiency in analyzing and representing a certain set of image data. Key applications include

Image classification: CNNs are applied to sort medical images into different groups and types, such as sorting between the various types of tumors or applying the model to a new image to determine whether it contains a disease. This application assists the diagnostic process by providing instant and accurate classification based on features that have been learned.

Image segmentation: CNNs are used in splitting medical images to outline structural areas of tissue. For example, there are methods like U-Net that target exact segmentation tasks to provide required boundary information for the organs, lesions, etc.

Disease detection and diagnosis: Help identify diseases through imaging data for abnormality patterns and anomalies. For instance, CNNs help in finding markers for cancers and diabetic retinopathy, making the diagnosis more accurate at the earliest stage.

Image enhancement: CNNs can improve the quality of medical images by filtering out noise and improving the resolution artifacts. Higher resolution can help improve the performance of diagnosis and intervention, enhancing analysis and interpretation.

Radiological image interpretation: CNNs assist radiologists in making conclusions from intricate imaging information by offering further information in a specific aspect. This collaboration of human and artificial intelligence coming together makes the overall diagnostic work function even better.

RNNs and LSTM networks are refined methods of machine learning, which are particularly valuable for the analysis of sequential data and temporal structures. Although these architectures are widely used in natural language and time-series data, they provide useful features for some processes in medical imagery as well.
