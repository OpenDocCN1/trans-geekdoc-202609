## 5 Smart contracts for access control and governance

### 5.1 Introduction to smart contracts in blockchain technology

Smart contracts contain the terms of the contract straight up in the code. This shall mean that in the implementation of the contractual provisions, enforcement shall be done via a blockchain network without the intermediaries. An important part of the blockchain infrastructure is smart contracts capable of performing agreements, business logic, and transactions programmatically and in a decentralized manner.

In its simplest form, smart contracts are self-executing programs that reside on blockchain, which execute automatically once predetermined conditions are met. These conditions are often called triggers and are coded into a smart contract and occur on their own when activated by outside events. Although smart contracts are initiated by the user, the set actions are executed without the user’s direct interjection and with no human interference.

One feature of smart contracts is its potential to facilitate customizable and credible cross-party transactions. Smart contract results of those executions are transparent and can be audited as well since they occur on a blockchain, which is inherently open and immutable. Therefore, today there is no need to have middlemen and other reliable parties for supervision and verification of the transactions, which reduces the costs, time, and risks associated with the traditional methods of contract execution.

The use of smart contracts can automate a large spectrum of agreements and transactions ranging from banking, real estate, supply chain, as well as healthcare. Since blockchain is an emerging technology to support health information sharing, smart contracts play a decisive role in the consent process, governance, and access rights in blockchain-based EHR systems. Smart contracts ensure that only authorized parties have access to patient health information and that data sharing happens in accordance with set rules and permissions as also described in data sharing agreements, automate consent management procedures, and enforce access control measures.

### 5.3 Role-based access control (RBAC) in EHR management

Another access control technique employed in utilizing access to delicate healthcare data is the widely used RBAC controlling access to EHRs based on roles of people in an organization. RBAC prevents unauthorized personnel from accessing certain data and, at the same time, provides complicated information to employees, since access rights and privileges are delegated in relation to users’ jobs. RBAC is one of the main foundations in healthcare data management and is critical for ensuring data availability, confidentiality, and data integrity of EHR data.

Using ACL control matrices (ACMs) to specify the permissions and access privileges of certain users inside the EHR system is one method for integrating fine-grained access control with smart contracts. These ACLs can be updated dynamically by smart contracts on the blockchain in response to modifications in user roles or permissions. The accompanying smart contract checks the user’s identification and credentials against the access control list when the user seeks access to a certain resource or a specific activity inside the EHR system. It then grants access in accordance with preset criteria.

Another way of achieving fine-grained access control with smart contract is to apply ABAC. In ABAC models access control decisions are made based on assignments of attributes, conditions related to the user, resource, other contextual factors, and its environment. Smart contracts can assess these attributes into the contract, with flexibility to change the permission profile depending on the situation. For example, a smart contract should only allow a specific user to gain access to a specified patient’s file if that user has the right credentials to access patient and client information if he/she is given the authority by the patient and only if the access is compliant with the requisite regulatory laws.
