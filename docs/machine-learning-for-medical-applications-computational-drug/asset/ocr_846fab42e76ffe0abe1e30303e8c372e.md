# 5.1.4 Interaction with biological fluids

Another important factor was the adverse effect of the materials on biological fluids like blood. Now we understand that interactions between the material and these fluids influence the stability and functionality of the material. For example, polymers that possess strength against hydrolysis are not very capable of degrading toxic substances when in contact with body fluids. The ability of the materials to interact chemically with blood components, such as clotting factors, affects their applicability in uses like blood-contact devices. Polymer types that are least reactive in biological solutions are typically more biocompatible.

# 5.1.5 Long-term stability and chemical durability

The material’s chemical stability over the long term was critical in ensuring biocompatibility over an extended period of time. The chosen materials for chronic implant devices should not undergo chemical alterations within the body. Instability factors, including the leaching of free and unbound monomer products, are usually injurious to biological processes. Hence, it is evident that materials with good chemical stability and resistance to environmental forces are essential for maintaining the stable biocompatibility of the materials, along with the reliability of their operation.
