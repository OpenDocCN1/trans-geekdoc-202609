
Semantic interoperability: Semantic interoperability that allows data to be shared as well as understood by receiving IT systems and applications is crucial in guaranteeing the meaningful and consistent sharing of EHRs. To get semantic interoperability, there is a need to use common coding schemas, vocabularies, ontologies, and data types to encode clinical terms and their associations. Semantic incompatibilities of terminologies, coding discrepancies, and semantic vagueness inhibit semantic integration, subsequently causing data variation, mistakes, and misunderstanding.

The main hurdles to achieving efficient interoperability in the exchange of data in the sphere of healthcare include the lack of unified data models, the absence of unity in the EHR systems being used, and data management problems that give rise to integration problems and semantic misinterpretations. These challenges call for increased working together by members in the healthcare system to establish and implement interoperability roadmaps, data management policies, technical integration solutions, and semantic convergence. These interoperability challenges require healthcare organizations to ensure that data exchange is done effectively, care coordination is enhanced, and patients’ outcomes are improved in blockchain-based EHR systems.

## 4.2 Role of Blockchain in Enabling Interoperability

Blockchain technology has the potential to help with healthcare data exchange interoperability issues and make data sharing across different EHR systems easier. Blockchain networks’ distributed and decentralized architecture, along with its cryptographic security characteristics, provides a number of benefits for improving interoperability in the transmission of medical data:

- **Decentralized data management:** Distributed ledgers holding patient health data are maintained in duplicate by every member of decentralized networks of nodes, which are the foundation of blockchain-based EHR systems. Because of its decentralized design, peer-to-peer communication and cooperation between healthcare professionals, patients, and other stakeholders was made possible without the requirement for a central authority to permit data transmission. Blockchain lessens dependency on centralized repositories and eases interoperability issues brought on by incompatible EHR systems by decentralizing data management.

- **Consensus methods:** In order to reach a consensus among network users on the legitimacy of transactions, blockchain depends on consensus methods like PoW. Ensuring that every node in the network has a shared knowledge of the blockchain’s current state, consensus methods help to build confidence and agreement on the accuracy of EHR data. Blockchain improves interoperability by encouraging confidence between many parties and enabling safe data sharing by offering a shared and unchangeable record of transactions.

- **Smart contracts:** Smart contracts built into blockchain hold the potential for enabling self-execution of exchange and interaction protocols of data contained in multiple EHRs. Specifically, smart contracts conceptualize policies governing the access, modification, and sharing of EHR information, while guaranteeing compliance with interoperability standards and data management policies in various systems. Smart contracts become effective means for increasing the speed and integrity of data exchange between participants in healthcare systems through the automation of exchange processes and compliance with standards for interoperability.

- **Data standardization and semantic interoperability:** Blockchain-based EHR systems can take advantage of standard structure and exchange formats, and standard terminology and knowledge representation systems for semantically seamless use and interchangeability of EHR data. The use of common data understandings and common languages enables EHR systems built on the application of blockchain technology to effectively share and exchange these patient details without a high probability of data incompatibilities, errors, and misunderstandings. Interoperability is further promoted in this case by standardization combined with blockchain technology, both on the technical and the semantic levels of sharing health information.

- **Patient-controlled data sharing:** EHR systems built on blockchain technology provide patients the ability to have control over their personal health data and grant access to just certain physicians and approved organizations. For privacy, security, and consent management, patients are allowed to provide access to specific EHR data to specific parties by signing them digitally with digital signatures and cryptographically with keys. Blockchain allows for fine-grained management of read/write access to specific data fields and places data ownership back in the hands of the patient, which emphasizes both patient sovereignty and patient centricity while simultaneously promoting high levels of system integration.

Blockchain has a central function in unlocking the interoperability feature of health information exchange by integrating decentralization, consensus, smart contract, and information convergence with patient-directed data accessibility. As a result, with the common key issues of interoperability addressed and the use of blockchain technology in facilitating a decentralized, secure, and transparent data exchange within blockchain-based EHR systems, it is about time that the healthcare system within this connected environment delivers value and improves outcomes for patients.

## 4.3 Standards and Protocols for Interoperable EHR Systems

Interoperability within EHR systems entails compliance with standard data and structural interoperability, communication, as well as the relationship between EHR systems. A number of standards and protocols have been developed, which offer standards and guidelines for representing, exchanging, and integrating EHR systems. Some of the key standards and protocols for interoperable EHR systems include:

- **HL7 (Health Level Seven):** Specifications that relate to the exchange, combination, sharing, and access to electronic health information are referred to as HL7 standards. IT infrastructure of healthcare operates on HL7 standards, which offer data formats, communication formats, and interaction standards for clinical care, public health, and administrative healthcare transactions, among others. HL7 standards are applicable to the management of EHR information as well as facilitate integration of information from one HIT system with another. Some of these standards are HL7 Version 2 (V2) and HL7 Version 3 (V3).

- **FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources):** FHIR is a relatively modern interoperability standard unveiled by HL7 that employs Web-based technologies to allow timely and seamless exchange of health information. Examples of these technologies include RESTful APIs (Application Programming Interfaces). EHR content is the defined structure of the data, namely patient identification data, observations derived from the patient, prescribed medications, etc., based on the FHIR resources using a concept of modularity and extension. FHIR's basic and fine-grained features make it ideal for interoperability of EHR systems to enable developers to construct lightweight as well as interoperable applications that are capable of acquiring EHR data.
