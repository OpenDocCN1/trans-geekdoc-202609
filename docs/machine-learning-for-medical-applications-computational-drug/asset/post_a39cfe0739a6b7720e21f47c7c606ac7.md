Assessment of disease progression: While chronic pulmonary diseases require constant observation of additional respiratory sensors to evaluate the disease progression and effectiveness of the treatment, such factors ensure the identification of the changes in the respiratory parameters over time, hence enabling the healthcare providers to come up with the correct decision when it comes to the management of treatment services and interventions.

Emergency response and alerts: Modern respiratory monitoring sensors are capable of being set to generate alarms in case certain abnormal respiratory rates are detected. This feature is especially useful in patients with critical diseases of the lungs and thus could prevent severe complications.

### 5.3.2 Challenges and considerations

Accuracy and reliability: The sensor technology used in respiratory monitoring has been considered vulnerable to factors such as position of sensors, movement of the patient, and environmental factors. There is a need to standardize the sensors to provide dependable results in disease control and security of the patients.

Data integration and interpretation: Real-time respiratory rates analysis and monitoring based on the data from different sensors is challenging. Ordering, integration, and analysis of patient's multimodal data are needed, especially for the purpose of decision support.

Patient compliance and comfort: In particular, compliance with respiratory monitoring is critical when managing diseases, as evidenced by patient's noncompliance with wearing a face mask emanating from virus outbreak. Comfort and easy to use of these respiratory sensors is another factor that determines its usage, where a poorly designed sensor remains a collector's item despite its high-tech functions.

Pulmonary disease management can significantly benefit from using respiratory monitoring sensors as diagnostic tools, especially in an outpatient setting, and as adjuncts to the treatment of chronic respiratory conditions. Future advancements in technology predict that advanced and accurate, easy-to-use and integrated respiratory sensors would further improve disease management, hence optimizing patient outcomes.

## 5.4 Neurological monitoring

Neural check is one of the most effective approaches to handling neurological conditions to assess brain and neuronal communications and total neurological well-being. Recent advancements in biomedical sensors have greatly improved the diagnosis and treatment of many neurological disorders, including epilepsy and neurodegenerative diseases, among others.

### 5.4.1 EEG sensors

EEG electrodes are essential components in neurological assessments, used to detect the brain's physiological activity by capturing electrical signals from neurons. EEG sensors are commonly applied in the diagnosis and monitoring of conditions such as epilepsy, sleep disorders, and brain tumors. As illustrated in Figure 9, recent advancements have led to the development of multifunctional EEG devices that are more affordable, compact, and suitable for use in natural environments eliminating the need for bulky, complex equipment. These innovations enhance mobility and allow for continuous monitoring, facilitating the analysis of seizure activity and the evaluation of cognitive functions in the management of neurological disorders.

### 5.4.2 ICP monitors

ICP sensors are essential for patients developing certain conditions, such as traumatic brain injury (TBI). These sensors keep a check on the pressure that is exerted inside the cranial cavity and contain helpful information about the condition of the brain. Today's ICP monitors are made with sophisticated materials and technologies to provide a high degree of precision and durability. Sometimes, they have wireless features and connectivity with other monitoring systems, which enable intracranial pressure to be monitored continually and from a distance.

### 5.4.3 Brain-computer interface (BCI)

BCIs are a new form of neurological monitoring that enable the brain to interface with other devices. BCIs rely on different modes of sensing, including electroencephalogram and implantable sensors, which help in identifying the brain signals. These systems are employed to embrace individuals with quadriplegic disorders and others who are not capable of voluntary movement; the technique allows manual control of assistive technology, solely through brain signals. Current innovations in BCI concern themselves with how the signals are made finer as well as how the hardware may be rendered more comfortable and how the new generation of prosthetic devices as well as communication appliances are incorporated into the system.

### 5.4.4 Portable electrical stimulation apparatus

Portable neurostimulation devices also provide a unique paradigm to neurological monitoring and treatment. Treatment tools employ electrical currents to adjust the putative neuronal activity to help patients with conditions such as chronic pain, depression, and Parkinson's disease. New trends in wearable neurostimulation are emerging; these include but are not limited to miniature, easily wearable devices, which can be used for continuous stimulation. Many of these devices also include some sort of apparatus for measuring physical responses to stimulation and adjust the therapy regimen according to the feedback received.

### 5.4.5 Intelligent prostheses and adaptive neuroprosthetics

Smart implantation and responsive neuro technology can therefore be viewed as sophisticated applications of constant neurological monitoring and treatment options. These implants, which usually come with sensors and stimulation systems, are intended to perform a dynamic interfacing with neural activity. Some examples include deep brain stimulators that can have programmable modes that change stimulation parameters according to real-time neural recordings. They are employed for diseases such as Parkinson's to deliver applicable clinical treatments as per the neurological state of the patient.

Neurological monitoring with biomedical sensors has improved the diagnosis, continual tracking of data in real-time, and patient-tailored therapy for neurological disorders. These technologies enhance the scope of an easily integrated approach in the neurological health of patients, while enhancing the quality of the years of research done in neuroengineering and neurology. Further developments are assumed to increase the accuracy, usability, and efficiency of neurology monitoring techniques in future years.

## 5.5 Long-term monitoring solutions for geriatric care

Intermittent monitoring systems are important in geriatric care because elderly patients often suffer from multiple chronic illnesses requiring comprehensive management. The use of long-term remote biomedical sensors offers significant advantages by improving the quality of care, enhancing health outcomes, and enabling timely interventions for older adults.

### 5.5.1 Wearable health monitoring devices

According to findings, wearable health monitoring devices are widely used in the management of chronic diseases in the geriatric population. These devices are easy to wear, avoiding any inconvenience to the patient, and are intended for constant monitoring of different vital parameters, including pulse, blood pressure, and blood sugar level. Some of the enhanced wearables also target aspects such as falls, physical activity, and sleep, which are essential for elderly users. For example, Smartwatches with cardiovascular features can help caregivers if a patient is experiencing cardiac arrhythmias or abnormal blood pressure.

### 5.5.2 Remote health monitoring systems

Tele-care technologies enable continuous acquisition and transmission of health information from the elderly to the healthcare givers without having to make frequent home visits. Such systems often include measurements by sensors of indices like glucose, pressure, and saturation of oxygen. Information is sent over secured networks to the healthcare givers, who in turn can work on the information to make informed decisions on the changes in the treatment plan.
