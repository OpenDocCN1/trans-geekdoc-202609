# 2.5.3 Challenges and considerations

Problems of transfer learning in material science: Although transfer learning has some benefits, it also has certain disadvantages. Selecting a suitable pretrained model, considering the specific requirements of the material design task is the first and most important decision to make. Thus, the suitability of the information transmitted has to be checked, and the relevance of the source domain to the target domain has to be evaluated. Delicate adjustments in parameters are often required, and during the feature extraction phase, overfitting is very possible. The model must be trained properly to capture intricate details in the new material data. Effective management to improve the utility of transfer learning strategies requires understanding both the source and target communities to overcome these challenges.

Potential developments in transfer learning in material science further strengthen the idea that the future is bright if improved models and techniques are employed. Additions to the transfer learning area could involve developing focused pretrained translation models for material science data, as well as utilizing domain adaptation methods to integrate data from different material datasets. There are predictions that further advancements in transfer learning methodologies will be stimulated by improvements in computational resources and the availability of large, high-quality data. Further improvements in these approaches mean achieving faster rates at which materials can be discovered and optimized for biomedical applications, which would be a huge boost to biomedical science.

# 3 Ensemble methods and hybrid approaches

# 3.1 Random forests for classification and regression in material science

Random forest is a type of ensemble learning technique that improves the results of classification and regression decision trees by using the predictions of several decision trees. This method is also efficient in situations where scientific data are complex and/or variable, particularly in the field of material sciences. As in many other branches, high-dimensional and variable data often require stable models. While decision trees provide a single output for any specific input, random forests enhance that output and provide a more reliable model for investigating material characteristics and behaviors by integrating the results of a large number of decision trees.
