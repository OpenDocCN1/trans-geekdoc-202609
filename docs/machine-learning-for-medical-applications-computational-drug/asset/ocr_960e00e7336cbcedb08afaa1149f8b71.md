# 6.2 Regulatory challenges in approving personalized therapies

Personalized medicine implementation is associated with a unique set of challenges in meeting regulatory requirements that define personalized therapy approval and commercialization. Unlike conventional medicines, these highly developed individual treatments often involve targeted standards where the information about genetic predispositions, surroundings, and habits of the patient are taken into account firstly. Due to this level of individualization, the existing legislation needs to evolve to ensure that the impacts of these drugs on their targets are positive, safe, and of good quality due to their special features.

In particular, one of the main regulatory concerns, when approving personalized therapies, is the requirement of developing new types of clinical trials. Conventional clinical trials are ISO QS 1901 conformant, meaning developed for assessing treatment patterns expected to be applied to large groups of patients, but the utilization of personalized medicine requires more flexible approaches to clinical trials. For example, basket-and-umbrella trials let check the efficacy of the targeted therapies through multiple biomarkers across different subgroups of patients. In particular, both general and specific trial designs, which include lesser patient numbers and additionally call for different statistical techniques to compare the effectiveness of treatments and safety profiles.

Also, different therapies thus require companion diagnostic tests that can determine which patients will benefit most from the treatment. The use of these diagnostics and therapies as co-developed and receiving simultaneous approval complicates the regulatory process. It means that regulators must ensure the validation of such tools and verify that they provide accurate information for patient treatment. In general, additional regulation combined with overlapping jurisdictions for therapeutics and diagnostics approval increases the level of fragmentation.

Another key challenge lies in defining appropriate regulatory strategies for emerging targeted therapies, including gene and cell-based treatments. As shown in Figure 14, these therapies involve technologies and manufacturing processes that differ significantly from traditional chemical synthesis used in conventional drug development. Consequently, they demand a more research-oriented regulatory approach. Regulatory agencies must establish specific criteria for production standards, quality control, and long-term safety monitoring. Mechanisms like the FDA’s Breakthrough Therapy Designation aim to accelerate the approval of such innovative, molecularly targeted therapies without compromising safety and efficacy.

Several ethical and data privacy issues stand as other immense regulatory barriers. Genetic and medical information can be often collected and analyzed for treatment purposes with some regard to the client’s individuality. Regulators have the important responsibility that the required data has to be available for the development of personalized medicines while patient’s identities have to be protected.
