## 2 Overview of biomedical sensors and devices

### 2.1 Types of biomedical sensors and devices

Wearable sensors represent a transformative advancement in personal health monitoring, offering continuous, noninvasive measurements of various physiological parameters. These devices have become integral to both personal fitness and clinical applications, providing real-time data that can significantly enhance health management and preventive care.

There are two most common forms of wearable sensors, which include smartwatches and fitness trackers and basic traditional watches with additional sophisticated health sensors. These gadgets usually entail including heart rate sensors, step counter, sleep pattern and physical activity sensors. Most of the models use GPS for the tracking of outdoor physical activities and then offer further statistics such as caloric consumption and stress. Different types of biomedical sensors and devices that are wearable, implantable, and diagnostic are depicted in Figure 1, where the various sensors and devices are designed to measure a diverse physiological parameter. These devices collect data and using some algorithms, guide the user on their general fitness and health; they are helpful to both the regular fitness fanatic as well as the chronically ill.

Skin-mounted patches and adhesive sensors are aimed for skin on-surface wear where it monitors different health parameters continually. These sensors are mainly employed for observing cardiac activity and blood glucose levels and dehydration. The commonly used adhesive patches are thin and flexible to prevent weighing the patient down and also to allow the patient to go about his/her normal business without much disturbance. For example, ECG adhesive monitors the heart rhythms for weeks, while glucose adhesive monitors the blood sugar levels in an unobtrusive manner. It is also convenient that the data from these sensors can be delivered to mobile devices for real-time analysis and display of results.

Already, we have smart clothes that have sensors placed to the cloth so that people do not need extra devices to monitor their health. These garments incorporate a range of underlying sensors to monitor factors such as heart rate, respiratory rate, body temperature, and muscle activity, among others. Examples of smart clothing are sportswear that help improve performance and recovery from diseases, and other apparels that help monitor long-term diseases. Technical innovations in textiles also in the sphere of sensors and the shrinking of the devices let smart clothing become much more comfortable and versatile, so they are worn not only during sports activities, but can also be used in healthcare.

Wearable ECG monitors are considered as unique gadgets primarily intended for constant registration of the activity of the heart. Wrist band, chest harness or other wearable forms, and wearable ECG monitors help to regulate heart problems as they involve monitoring arrhythmia, heart rate variability, and other aspects of cardiac structure and function. Consequently, the constant data gathered by those gadgets can be examined for flagging of possible cardiovascular disorders with a view to taking suitable remedies on time alongside handling heart-related complications.

CGM devices are designed to be worn by patients with diabetes for tracking blood glucose levels at a constant rate during everyday activities. These devices generally comprise a small sensor that is inserted under the skin, which checks the glucose level in the interstitial fluid. The data is then transmitted to a wearable receiver, thereby giving users an instant feedback on the glucose values and trends. The benefits of CGM are that it can improve the overall diabetic management since patients can adjust the doses of insulin more accurately, monitor patterns of glucose level, and use fewer fingerstick tests.

### 2.2 Implantable sensors

Implantable sensors are sophisticated biomedical devices intended to be implanted within the human body to deliver permanent constant surveillance and control of many physiological elements. The type of data that such sensors provide is highly accurate, stable over extended periods, and in real-time, which serves the patient with chronic diseases and the conditions that require comprehensive medical treatment.

Pacemakers and cardiac monitors are among the most widely used implantable sensors, designed to manage and monitor heart health. Pacemakers are small electronic devices implanted in the chest to regulate heart rate, especially in patients suffering from conditions like atrial fibrillation or heart block. Several implantable sensors, as illustrated in Figure 2, have been developed for continuous health monitoring and data recording.

These sensors can monitor the heart’s electrical activity and continuously analyze heart rate and rhythm. For instance, modern pacemakers can provide detailed, real-time feedback on cardiac activity, enabling healthcare providers to adjust treatment plans based on accurate, up-to-date information. Additionally, portable yet invasive devices like implantable loop recorders offer long-term ECG monitoring and can effectively detect arrhythmia-related abnormalities over extended periods, which is highly beneficial for preventive care and diagnosis.

Insulin pumps and glucose sensors are important diabetes technology tools that allow for constant check and accurate regulation of blood sugar levels.
