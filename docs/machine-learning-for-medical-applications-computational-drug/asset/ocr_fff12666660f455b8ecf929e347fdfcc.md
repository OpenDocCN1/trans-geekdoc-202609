# 2.5.3 Multimodal and hybrid sensors, and their development

The integration of different sensors is gaining consideration as solutions to achieve multiple and diverse modalities of health conditioning. These sensors use multiple modalities, including optical, electrical and chemical, for the measurement of a number of physiological parameters at one time. Using multiply sensing technologies allow for enhanced and accurate health monitoring systems. For instance, the hybrid sensors include capabilities for heart rate monitoring, blood oxygen level detection, and physical activity, which give a broader picture of the client's condition. This trend can be explained by the shift to more advanced and complex health monitoring platforms.

# 2.5.4 New techniques and technologies of noncontact and telemetric recording

Contactless transducers and home monitoring devices are steadily growing popular because they are more convenient to patients. New technologies in optical sensing and bioimpedance sensing are progressively allowing the monitoring of physiological signals without stern procedures. Tele-monitoring technologies that have resulted from developments in wireless communication and cloud computing make it possible to obtain health data from remote and relay updates to physicians and nurses. This trend is in line with the increasing embracement of telemedicine. Heinrichs and Hunt (2014) note that this trend supports the escalating use of telemedicine and remote patient management solutions, especially for chronic illnesses and to areas that lack adequate care resources.

These market trends and innovations demonstrate the constant growth of biomedical sensor technology, the constant attempt to refine health monitoring capacity, and the constant push to improve patient prognosis and meet new healthcare demands. It is believed that with such advancements, new materials, AI analytics, and multimodal sensing would continue to revolutionize biomedical sensors, and advance the future of the field-invasive sensing techniques, such as optical sensors and bioimpedance sensors, which are enabling the measurement of physiological parameters without the need for invasive procedures. Remote monitoring technologies, facilitated by advancements in wireless communication and cloud computing, allow for continuous health data collection and transmission to healthcare providers from remote. This trend supports the growing adoption of telemedicine and remote patient management solutions, particularly in managing chronic diseases and providing care in underserved areas.
