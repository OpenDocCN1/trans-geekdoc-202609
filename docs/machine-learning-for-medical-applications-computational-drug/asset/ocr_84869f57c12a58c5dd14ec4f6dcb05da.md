Environmental assessment and intelligent homes improve geriatric care by specifically reducing environmental hazards for the elderly. The home environment can be sensed through analyzers placed within a home to measure a variety of values such as room temperature, fresh air levels, and presence of hazardous items like gas moisture. Health monitoring devices can be connected to home systems for smart home systems to deliver comprehensive care by adjusting the environment and notifying the caregivers about the problems. For instance, automated systems can control heating and cooling, depending on the health input and notify caretakers if an elderly person is inactive from the usual rhythm.

# 5.5.6 Challenges and considerations

Several challenges have to be considered when aiming for adopting long-term monitoring solutions in geriatric care – these include the ability of patients, their geriatric age, their comfort to use the devices, proper data privacy and security issues, and correct incorporation into the existing geriatric care practice. Also important are aspects such as possible psychological effects of constant supervision on the elderly and the necessity of caregivers’ training for the proper interpretation and utilization of the data received from the sensors.

Telemonitoring technologies for the elderly care have significant roles in the long-term management of illnesses in the elderly population. Specifically, these solutions offer enhanced, real-time, thus more effective, ongoing information and intervention, hence a positive impact on the specific group’s health.
