Genomic profiling to identify specific genetic dysregulation and alterations within cancer cells is the primary approach of personalized oncology. As a result of such approaches as NGS, one can identify the set of mutations that drives tumor growth and hence cancer. Oncologists can elect to use a treatment type that would selectively attack the genetic profile present in each patient’s tumor through identification of these molecular signatures. New drugs that are more effective, with fewer side effects than ordinary chemotherapy, have been developed to counter some genetic flaws. For instance, tyrosine kinase inhibitors exist for EGFR gene mutation and PARP inhibitors for BRCA1/2 gene mutations.

Individualized oncology methods are immunotherapy and targeted agents, which employ the immune response to deal with malignancies. Knowledge of the types of environments in which tumors grow and how cancers evade immune recognition has given rise to protein regulators of immune response called immune checkpoint inhibitors, which include anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibodies. More examples of what constitutes a personalized treatment plan being used to enhance the immune system’s ability to detect and destroy cancer cells are adoptive cell therapies such as CAR-T cell therapy as well as personalized cancer vaccines.

A second key area of individualized oncology care is the use of tumor biomarker testing to inform therapy. For instance, tumor mutational burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), and selected protein expression are associated with tumor sensitivity to specific treatments. High TMB and MSI are known to correlate with higher rates of response to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Emphasizing these biomarker tests in the clinical arsenal, oncologists are able to identify which treating strategy works for particular patients and provide the best possible results.

The matters demonstrated by precision oncology indicate how important it is to monitor patients’ outcomes and adapt the therapy regimen, accordingly. Liquid biopsies in blood samples, which involve the examination of ctDNA, enable the evaluation of alteration in tumor mutation profiles. Regarding this, treatment management can be adjusted depending on the development of mutations and suitability of the existing regimen of treatment.

In addition, personalized medicine assists in the exploration of advanced technique of treatment where treatments are performed depending on the patient’s unique genetic makeup. Technological developments have greatly enhanced the capacity to find new biomarkers for cardiovascular diseases through genomics and proteomics. Individualization allows the development of treatments targeting these aims since non-targeted interventions would be more toxic and less effective than the current therapies. For instance, targeted drug delivery systems and gene therapies are becoming potential solutions to enhanced and sophisticated cardiovascular diseases, which include heart failure and atherosclerosis diseases.

Mobile health, digital health, and wearable technology also help support the concept of personalized medicine in cardiovascular care. Pulse oximeters, blood pressure, temperature, and other health indicators help to gain on-the-spot information about changes in giving targeted interventions and to assess results. Personalized medicine also adapts these data into the management, which means alteration in the routine management plan according the feedback and overall condition of the patient.

## 5.3 Customizing treatment strategies for neurological disorders

Personalized medicine is an advanced treatment approach that tailors therapy for neurological disorders based on the individual characteristics of each patient. As shown in Figure 11, this strategy leverages emerging technologies and discoveries in genomics, neuroimaging, and biomarker identification to enhance the precision and effectiveness of diagnosing and managing complex neurological conditions, including Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, epilepsy, and multiple sclerosis.

Lack of simplicity and homogeneity are the characteristic features of most neurological sickness where multiple genetic, biochemical, and environmental factors determine the natural history and response to treatment. It is therefore of highly heterogeneous structure, and traditional treatment methods often involve applying an approach that may not be useful to many people. It is important to understand that, for example, certain diseases in certain patients for which medicines have not been developed yet can be a problem in general for personalized medicine and work is needed to correct these things and offer better treatment.

Among the main developments that enabled enhancing the approaches to managing neurological disorders, the development of genetic and molecular biomarkers is noteworthy. In the genetics context, a lot of genetic variations have been discovered to be linked with neurological disorders mostly through the studies of their causes and possible treatments. For instance, mutations within genes encoding amyloid precursor protein (APP) and presenilins have been identified to cause familial Alzheimer’s disease resulting in therapies that seek to influence amyloid-beta synthesis and deposition.

Biomarkers are also useful in prognosis and response to treatment in neurological disorders as well. Neuroimaging biomarkers include MRI and PET that give details of the anatomical and functional abnormality of the brain thus helping in disease diagnosis at early stages and to triage treatment response. Genetic and proteomic researches in biomarkers from cerebrospinal fluid and blood altered biochemistry in brains are biopsy-free diagnostic and prognostic instruments.

Personalized medicine also makes use of the environmental factors that affect the patient especially on the care that is being given. Low-complexity interventions including diet and exercise can be used alongside drugs to enhance the treatment of neurological ailments. Such factors are incorporated in individualized treatment interventions, which help improve the general outcome of therapeutic approaches.

Several challenges have to be addressed in order to get to the potential of personalized medicine for neurology. Some of them are the need for vast research that enables identification of relationships between genetics and diseases, for the development of clinical decision support systems, and for integration of personalization techniques into clinical practice. Ethical concerns linked with genetics testing of patient and privacy also have to be managed adequately to retain the trust and engagement in the process of the patient.

## 5.4 Applications of personalized medicine in rare and genetic diseases

Molecular medicine has been defined as personalized medicine as it was accepted as a novel therapeutic modality in the management of rare and genetic disorders and carries remarkable promises to the patients who have had very few therapeutic choices in the previous treatment regimens. Personalized medicine in that respect entails matching care interventions with genetic characteristics of the individual client to enhance diagnosis and treatment plans.

In choosing comparison targets, it is therefore understood that rare and genetic diseases pose the most formidable challenge because of their nature and diversity. Conventional diagnostic techniques do not have the required efficiency, often causing the doctor to miss the correct diagnosis. These issues are solved by personalized medicine using genomics-sequencing technologies to identify such genes accountable for these diseases. Whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing are coordinated processes used in the sequencing of an individual's genomic DNA, and the former helps identify carriers of pathogenic variants that would be ignored in standard sequencing. It improves the outcomes of diagnosing because it is accurate and allows clinicians to offer early, appropriate measures.

While being used in the diagnosis of rare and hereditary disorders, the most significant application of personalized medicine is in the development of distinct drugs. Since what genetic basis such ailments have is obvious, scientists can develop drugs that would specifically address these genetic changes in diseases. This method has been particularly useful in diseases such as cystic fibrosis, for which therapies developed to address the CFTR gene mutation have made impressive performance. In the same way, developing more individualistic medication has come up with PARP inhibitors that selectively stick to cancer cells harbored by BRCA mutations raising the treatment positives in hereditary diseases like ovarian and breast cancer associated with BRCA mutations.

Another manifestation of the idea of personalized medicine that has application for rare and genetic diseases is gene therapy. Compared to symptom-focused treatments that have existing gene-based approaches, gene therapy intends to fix the source of a wide range of genetic diseases by inserting genes in a patient's cells. New developments in modern technologies like CRISPR CAS 9 have given relatively promising solutions to many genetic disorders including SMA (spinal muscular atrophy) and various forms of genetic blindness.

Personalized medicine also has significance in untangling complicated genetic disorder through early diagnosis and tailored medical approach. For example, in diseases like familial hypercholesterolemia, it is possible to test for genes; therefore once individuals are found to possess those genes, lifestyle changes and the use of drugs can be administered early enough to prevent the situation from escalating. Hugo-Kaplan, A, et al., report that for conditions such as phenylketonuria, patients can develop their meal plan in consideration with metabolic tests, thus avoiding severe structural backups.
