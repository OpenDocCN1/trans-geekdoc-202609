# 3.4.2 Customization and versatility

The first advantage of composite materials was their flexibility and the ability to modify their properties to suit particular cases in biomedical fields. It must be understood that composites are designed to possess certain characteristics such as flexibility, durability, and biodegradability by varying the nature and ratios of the component materials. Due to this tailoring, materials suitable for different applications, for instance, rigid orthopedic implants, are produced.

# 3.4.3 Biomedical engineering applications

As composite materials solve specific problems associated with single-material systems, they are applied in biomedical engineering. For example, composites can be utilized in the production of dental implants, where flexibility is obtained from polymers while strength is derived from ceramics. Scaffolds used in tissue engineering are made from biodegradable polymers and ceramics, which, on one hand, provide structure and, on the other, support tissue development and regeneration.
