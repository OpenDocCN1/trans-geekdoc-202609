# 2.3.1 Multiplatform and personalized medicine

Advancements in the past few years have also made it possible to combine various data that are multidimensional including proteomics, metabolomics, and genomes in improving treatment methodologies. These broad databases are applied by precision medicine to build up intensely individualistic treatment therapies that involve the complexity of biological systems. Several omics layers can be integrated, and the healthcare practitioners are capable of enhancing the accuracy of the models involving illness development, as well as therapy response.

# 2.3.2 Evolution of possible ways of treatment

It was expected that with advances in ML and AI, future therapeutic methods would become more personalized and data-oriented. Because these technologies can analyze vast amounts of data and discover new patterns and correlations, they have the potential to revolutionize customized medicine. The dynamic adjustment of therapies based on patient data could be enhanced by integrating AI and ML with real-time wearable devices. This evolution marks a transformation of healthcare from a reactive system to one that continuously adapts to meet patient needs.

# 2.4.1 Sir Francis Crick and James D. Watson

The Nobel Prize was awarded to Sir Francis Crick and James D. Watson for their discovery of the DNA double helix structure in 1953. This breakthrough provided critical insights into the molecular structure of genes and heredity, forming the foundation for an individualized approach to medical treatment. What they found helped explain how genetic information is stored and transmitted from one generation to the next, paving the way for the development of genomics and personalized medicine.
