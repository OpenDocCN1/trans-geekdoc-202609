# 5.2.4 Issues and innovations

Despite the advancements, several challenges remain in glucose monitoring.

Cost and accessibility: Unfortunately, some of the most modern glucometers and other CGM systems that provide significant benefits to diabetic patients are too costly to be afforded by many patients. Continual work is being done on this front to try and bring down the costs of these resources.

Sensor accuracy and calibration: Although innovation has enhanced the performance of the sensors, frequent calibration and maintenance are necessary to affirm the quality of the data received. Some of the research activities are centered on designing sensors that need less calibration but offer higher accuracy.

User comfort and compliance: Maintaining user comfort and promoting adherence to continuous glucose monitoring remain two key issues. Constant advancements made in relation to this area are directed toward increasing sensors' comfort for their users.

It is also important for glucose monitoring sensors because such sensors serve as an invaluable data source that would support improvements in the general management of diabetes, glucose control, and other related health outcomes. The optimization of technology and its connection with other diabetes care also aids to strengthen the glucose mapping systems that eventually result in good results for diabetes patients.

# 5.3 Respiratory monitoring sensors for pulmonary diseases

Respiratory monitoring sensors are of great importance when it comes to management of pulmonary diseases as they offer real-time monitoring of the respiratory function. These sensors are useful in monitoring the course of chronic illnesses like asthma, COPD, and ILD in order to make management of the condition better and result in better results for the patients.
