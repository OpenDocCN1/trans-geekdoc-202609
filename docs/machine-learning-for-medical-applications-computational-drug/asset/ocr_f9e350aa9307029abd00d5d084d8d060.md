# 6.4 Technical challenges

Applying MM entails the sheer number of technical complexities that have to be overcome to deliver on the promise of a new model of healthcare delivery. These issues stem from the case in which huge amount of genetic, environmental, and clinical information have to be put into reasonable and actionable treatment plans. Given the ongoing progress in the field of personalized medicine, it is critical to surmount these technical challenges in order to advance the application and use of PM in clinical settings.

The first major technical issue that has emerged as pivotal for developing personalized medicine is the issue of handling and integrating complex biological databases. The regulatory mechanisms involved in gene expression are complex; hence, the current high-throughput sequencing technologies produce massive amounts of genomic data and the handling, analysis and storage of such data requires advanced computational tools. Given the nature of these datasets, sophisticated tools in bioinformatics and strong computational support are required for analysis and identification of potential trends in this flow of data, which still remains highly challenging and computationally intensive.

Data integration is another great challenge that faces the process of implementing the Big Data strategy. Instead, personalized medicine is based on addressing all types of data related to a patient's genomes, which include proteomics, metabolomics, and clinical history data. The integration of ACM data from various sources presents problems falling under data format, quality, and standard issues. The issue of data convergence also holds some preconditions as it is necessary to establish the set of guidelines and clear formats for the piping of data from various origins to conduct the integration of such data.
