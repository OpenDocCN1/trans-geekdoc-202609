# 6 Integrating AI with experimental methods in preclinical testing

# 6.1 AI-driven analysis of high-throughput screening data

HTS assays furnish a massive volume of information on compound activities against many targets, for offering unique prospects into the relationships of chemical compounds to biological systems. HTS data are big and often complex and pose problems for analysis and interpretation. The availability of low-cost, high-throughput screening data is a significant advantage of AI-oriented approaches, which seize valuable information from HTS data, select potential lead compounds, and prioritize compounds for further in vitro studies.

The application of developing classification models to identify hit chemical structures with desired biological activity is one of the foremost uses of AI organized for HTS data analysis. For instance, some of the machine learning algorithm models that can be trained on the HTS datasets to classify substances as active, based on their structural and activity profile include but not limited to: support vector machines (SVMs), random forests, and deep neural networks. These algorithms can work faster than lead identification because while the activity of new compounds is unknown, algorithms can make this prediction and prioritize hits based on the further validation and the labeled data.

The identities of SARs and pharmacophore characteristics that initiate chemical actions are unveiled with the help of AI analysis of the outcomes of HTS operations. AI approaches can also define the molecular nature of a hit compound’s potency, selectivity, as well as efficacy based on chemical structures of hit compounds and the activity map corresponding to them. The “rational design and development of lead compounds with better pharmacological profile” and therapeutic utilization of compounds are based on understanding SARs at molecular levels.
