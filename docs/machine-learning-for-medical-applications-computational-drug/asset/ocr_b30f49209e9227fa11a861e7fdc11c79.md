# 6.4 Wearability and patient compliance

Comfort and user compliance are two of the biggest issues concerning the design of biomedical sensors, more so if these devices are to be used for long-time monitoring across various population groups. It is thus critical to overcome these challenges to enable biomedical sensors play the right role in health monitoring and management.

# 6.4.1 Environmental comfort and design implications

This is why biomedical sensors that have a great design ensure that they do not cause any discomfort to the patients who use them. Measuring devices that cause discomfort, also result in low compliance from the patient and thus poor measurements. The improved flexibility in the design of wearable sensors is attributable to emerging features in material science such as the possibility to incorporate flexible and lightweight wearable material with enhanced breathability. New breakthroughs like stretchable electronics and skin-like adhesives guarantee that the sensors that wrap around the body’s curves are nonirritant. Ergonomic designs that also do not exert much bulk and weight on the user equally enhance the comfort.

# 6.4.2 Durability and longevity

The above-discussed key performance indicators indicate that high measurements of durability and longevity are vital and more preferable in biomedical sensors, particularly in the long-term monitoring endurance of the patient. Unfortunately, wearables and sensors must be ready to face the day-to-day use and can be subject to moisture and sweat abrasion. These prognosticators become more durable as a result of the progress of material sciences and protective coats used in these devices, hence they do not need frequent replacements. For instance, coatings such as water protection and sweat proofing of sensors minimize the likelihood of their failure under some circumstances.

# 6.4.3 Ease of use and integration

The do-culture and the ability to wear biomedical sensors seamlessly in day-to-day life radically affect compliance. Sensors should be easy to use, with little training needed to work with them. Clear and uncluttered designs, as well as clear control elements, can make using the developed apps easy-to-use and thus enhance patient compliance with existing health management practices.
