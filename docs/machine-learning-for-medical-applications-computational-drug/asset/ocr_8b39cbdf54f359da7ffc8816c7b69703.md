# 4.1.3 Whole-exome sequencing (WES)

WES is centered on sequencing only those regions of the DNA that direct protein sequences. Although WES reaches only about 1–2% of genomic DNA, the method allows for extracting information on coding exons, in which most mutation-causing diseases are located. The development of WES technologies has also improved on its sensitivity and specificity making the methodology ideal for tagging genetic sequences that are associated with inherited diseases. WES is used to diagnose and identify disease-related genes when WGS is not practicable because of the cost of information content.

# 4.1.4 Advanced single-cell sequencing technologies

Single-cell sequencing technologies enable single-cell-level genomic, transcriptomic, and epigenomic data analysis. This method helps to discover gene expression profiles and cell subpopulations masked in a large tissue section, thanks to advances in single-cell sequencing, understanding of the detailed tissues, tumor microenvironments, cellular processes, and the path of the disease. In the field of cancer, single-cell sequencing is fairly useful in defining tumor subtypes and understanding their role in resistance to treatment and tumor heterogeneity.

# Next-generation and third-generation sequencing platforms

The limitations associated with the short-read sequencing modality can be surmounted with the assistance of long-read sequencing technologies provided by the Oxford Nanopore Technologies and Pacific Biosciences (PacBio). Previously, using short-read sequencing, only simple regions of variance could be studied, while now complex genomic areas, including repeated sequences and structural changes, can be restored. Long-read sequencing provides the higher-level genomic architecture necessary to understand structural variations and their health and illness implications. It also enhances the quality of genome assembly.

As the improved and broader genetic details became accessible, advancements in genomic sequence technologies provide a rather strong impact to individualize therapy. Using such technologies, people have effectively diagnosed the connection between certain genetic traits and specific illnesses, have enhanced the accuracy of diagnostics, and have been able to draw up personalized treatment. Thus, the concept of personalization of medicine for therapies can be combined with escalating effectiveness as sequencing technologies are implemented in the process of its functioning.
