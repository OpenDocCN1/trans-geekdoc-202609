In those cases where features are well-established and domain experience is critical, the manual technique could serve well. In cases where data sets are large and the characteristics to be extracted are complex and hard to define, some variant automated techniques are advantageous.

In fact, the strategy lying somewhere between purely automatic and purely manual is the most effective one. Automated feature selection can serve as an enlarged and optimized feature set, supplementary to the features first defined through manual methods for the design of new automated systems.

The main decision to be made when selecting a model for medical imaging was between classical machine learning models as well as understanding the advantages and disadvantages of each. The decision affects the effectiveness and relevance of machine learning applications in medical image analysis.

More specifically, some of the compared attributes that have been reported to have significant results are SVMs, k-NN, and the decision tree, to name but a few. These methods handcraft features from photographs and then use learning algorithms to classify with respect to these features.

SVMs: SVMs are good with data sets of high dimensions and can be used in classification exercises. Despite the fact that SVMs have been found to be effective in numerous medical image applications, it has been observed that the quality of feature extraction and the size of the dataset that can be managed often influence the function of an SVM.

k-NN: One of the simplest algorithms of instance-based learning is k-NN, which classifies new instances with the aid of the majority of its nearest neighbors. It is easy and fast; however, if no proper dimensionality reduction methods are applied, it becomes computationally intensive and not very accurate for high dimensions.

Decision trees: They are also good for decision-making and, more importantly, interpretable because they divide the data space into regions according to feature values. Nevertheless, they are prone to overfitting, especially if the dataset is complex and involves medical image data to an important extent, and often require the use of ensembles, for instance, random forest, in order to work efficiently.
