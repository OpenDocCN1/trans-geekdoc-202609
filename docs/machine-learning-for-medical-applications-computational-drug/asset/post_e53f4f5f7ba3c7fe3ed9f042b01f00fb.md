CNNs and RNNs in particular, have been found to yield the greatest results when it comes to analyzing biomedical sensor data. ## 5.3 Signal processing techniques

Signal processing is critical for the functionality and performance of AI-integrated biomedical sensors in their basic form. These techniques are indispensable to get useful information from raw data collected by the sensors as these are usually contaminated by noise, artifacts, and many other types of distortions. Improvement also occurs in accuracy in data due to efficient signal processing leading to increase in performance of AI and efficiency of health monitoring systems.

### 5.3.1 Noise reduction and filtering

Noise reduction is a significant component in signal processing that requires filtering out of interference and improving the quality of information produced by the sensors. Examples are digital filtering techniques like low-pass filter, high-pass filter, band-pass filter, and band-stop filter. They are used to reduce those frequencies that are related to noise while passing through frequencies of the signal of interest. Nonstationary noise is also addressed by means of adaptive filtering techniques that alter their parameters according to signal properties.

### 5.3.2 Signal denoising techniques

EMD and wavelet transform are two distinctive denoising techniques applied in signal processing used for identifying important signatures from noisy signals. From a signal, the wavelet transforms split up a signal spatially and temporally to different scales, thus enabling developers to remove noise from such a signal. Empirical mode decomposition, in a way, de-noises complex signals by unwrapping it to recover components dubbed intrinsic mode functions.

### 5.3.3 Feature extraction and selection

Feature extraction is the process of selection and filtering down of various characteristics, which defines the architecture of the signal relevant in health monitoring. Fourier and time-frequency analysis are employed to transform the signal to the frequency domain in which features like amplitude and frequency are measurable. Other techniques in machine learning also assist in feature selection because, through using certain algorithms, the importance of each feature to the results and, therefore, which ones are most appropriate to use in precision and classification are considered.

### 5.3.4 Data fusion and multisensory integration

Multifactor data fusion methods integrate information from various sensors in order to build a picture of a patient's condition. Techniques like Kalman filters and Bayesian fusion are used to amalgamate information acquired from different sources of data to get over the possible drawback of individual sensors and to enhance the accuracy of health appraisal. One gains the anomaly detection performance as high as the other by fusing information from multiple sensors for improving the reliability of the health monitoring systems.

### 5.3.5 Real-time signal processing

Only applications that require real-time response and input processing require real-time signal processing. Other techniques such as digital signal processing (DSP) algorithms and fast Fourier transform (FFT) enable fast analysis of the data gathered through the sensors pointing to real-time monitoring and alarm production. The application of real-time processing ensures the identification of changes in a patient's condition and timely intervention, thus improving the effectiveness of combating health tactics.

Multisensor fusion refers to the ability to merge data acquired from different sensors into a single and coherent database. This process improves the quality and reliability of health information because different sensor modalities are utilized to their full potential in the system.
