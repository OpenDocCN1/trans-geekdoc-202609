# 3.3.2 Environmental influences

Environmental factors can be almost anything from geographical location, exposure to pollutants, job dangers, and dietary habits that one can think of. Such outside variables can have the capacity to greatly influence a person's health experience, change the risk of disease, and interpersonal relationships with genes. For instance, the genetic susceptibility can become worse due to exposure to hazardous substances such as heavy metal and lead to disorders such as cancer. # 3.3.3 Lifestyle influences

Concepts and processes related to determinants on the health impact include social support, and regime changes, lifestyle, and diet, physical movement, and substance abuse including smoking and alcohol end use. All these aspects can determine the genesis and evolution of diseases, intervene in genetically determined processes, and modulate the outcome of therapies. Personalized medicine involves lifestyle profiling to obtain information about a person's health status. For instance, diet and exercise plans can be adjusted based on individual lifestyle factors to address metabolic disorders such as obesity, thereby improving overall wellness and enhancing therapeutic outcomes.

# 3.3.4 Integration of factors

Combining the genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors in the development of different aspects of an individual is fundamental for complete personalization of the treatment. It leads to development of a specific treatment plan for a patient that would be based on all the relevant aspects of that patient's life. For example, patients with the hereditary propensity for cardiovascular disease are recommended changes in food to eat and their physical activities, ways to minimize environmental risks, and drugs to take.
