These techniques amalgamate all data from various sensors, giving a clear and concise picture of the health state of the patient. Artificial intelligence and machine learning are used to process and analyze the large volume of data obtained from the multi-sensor modalities in order to make immediate and personal health decisions. For example, data from heart rate monitors, glucose sensors, as well as activity trackers could give comprehensive information about a patient's cardiovascular health, metabolism, and activity, in comparison with traditional checkups that are done once a week, and therefore the intervention could be more efficient.

# 4.5.4 Some applications in personalized and preventive medicine

Hybrid and multimodal sensor systems are of great importance in terms of personalized and preventive health interventions for individual health status. These systems allow for specific approaches to health monitoring, derived from a broad understanding of the patient's physical condition. For instance, multimodal sensors used in continuous glucose monitoring can give information on how diet, exercise, and stress influence the levels of glucose in the blood, to aid develop suitable strategies in controlling the diabetes. Further, HS can play an important role in support of the preventive measures by detecting early symptoms of potential health problems and thus calling for timely actions against diseases.

Hybrid and multimodal sensor systems are a significant innovation in biomedical monitoring, which extends all the crucial abilities required in the total health overview and management. Due to the drawbacks of single-mode sensors, these systems utilize a number of the abovementioned technologies to enhance the accuracy of data collected, patient compliance, and overall healthcare delivery results. Even so, devices with hybrid and multiple modal sensors are bound to become crucial as multifaceted technology expands in the future toward fulfilling personalized and proactive healthcare goals.
