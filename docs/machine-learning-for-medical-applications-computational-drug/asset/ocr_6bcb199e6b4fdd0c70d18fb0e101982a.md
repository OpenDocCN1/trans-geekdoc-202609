# 4.3.1 Scaffold design and function

Scaffolds are considered fundamental units in tissue engineering, providing physical support for cells to adhere, grow, and differentiate. Selection of proper biocompatible materials to possess the correct mechanical properties, porosity, and biodegradability was a major concern to be addressed during the scaffold designing phase. Due to their controlled degradation rates, structural flexibility, and selectivity for specific tissue types, polymers such as PLA, PGA, and their copolymers are commonly used. To enable native tissue formation and remodeling to progress uneventfully and for the scaffold to be effectively integrated into the host tissue, the scaffold must be ECM-mimetic.

# 4.3.2 Bioprinting and advanced fabrication techniques

Bioprinting and fabrication have advanced tissue engineering through innovation and enhanced approaches. Tissues with highly defined tissue organization can then be built by laying down compatible materials, cells, and bioprinting. Due to layer-on-layer deposition, researchers create three-dimensional structures of the scaffolds that closely resemble the natural tissue architecture. These technologies allow the biological properties of the scaffold to be altered effectively and enable the incorporation of various types of cells, which naturally promote improved tissue integration and functionality.

# 4.3.3 Cell-seeding and growth factors

The correct approach in this regard is the proper planting of cells on scaffolding and the application of growth factors that promote tissue growth, which are also requirements of tissue engineering. Biocompatible materials used in the construction of scaffolds must encourage cell adhesion and proliferation for the construct to be biologically functional. Antifibrotic molecules, such as ECM proteins, can be incorporated into the scaffold material to enhance the development of new tissue and levels of cell activity. This procedure allows for the generation of physiological tissue constructs that can be compared with real tissues.
