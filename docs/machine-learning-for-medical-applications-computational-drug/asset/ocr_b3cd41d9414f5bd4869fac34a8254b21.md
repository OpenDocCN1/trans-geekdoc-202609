# 2.3.6 Navigation and alert interfaces

Biomedical sensors also apply their functionality in user interfaces that are meant for improving patients’ interaction and adherence. Most of the sensors come with easy-to-read displays and notification systems that give real-time results of the state of health. The messages and notifications options can be tailored to send alerts whenever any out-of-the-ordinary number is received, in order to help the users manage their health better, and reminding them to follow the prescribed treatment plans.

These functionalities demonstrate the benefits of biomedical sensors in state-of-the-art health monitoring systems and show that such systems can not only deliver but indeed do deliver accurate, up-to-date, and individualized health information. Sensor technology is still in the process of expanding its capabilities, and the future development of this technology only seeks to amplify these traits and advantages toward serving patient needs with regard to their health.

# 2.4 Role of sensors in preventive healthcare

Biomedical sensors are very crucial in the area of preventive healthcare, where the detected health complications can be treated before they worsen. The major goal of preventive healthcare is to recognize the causes of sickness and prevent their development into chronic diseases, which has a positive impact on the general health and the overtaxing of facilities.

The constant tracking of physiological parameters is one of the basic responsibilities of biomedical sensors when it came to preventive healthcare. Health monitoring sensors intended for use in capturing data at regular intervals, including pulse rate, blood pressure, and glucose levels, are useful in identifying symptoms as well as potential signs of a certain health complication. Through the constant collection and study of such data, sensors make it possible to detect changes in physiological thresholds and, therefore, the existence of pathological states that require treatment.

Also, biomedical sensors help to identify the patients with certain risk factors and known diseases so as to develop individual approaches for their corresponding management. For instance, sensors used in diabetes glucose monitors present an opportunity for the constant monitoring of glucose levels in the body of diabetic patients to help adjust their diet, exercise regime, and the effect of diabetes on the body.
