# 5.4 Data fusion and multisensor integration

Data fusion and multisensor integration are two effective innovations that have added more accuracy and inclusiveness to the concepts of biomedical sensors in monitoring health systems. Figure 13 shows the use of the data fusion techniques into the multisensor systems to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of information gathered in systems environments. Such technologies interconnect several sensors and produce a less arbitrary picture of a patient's condition, making the identification of health status and subsequent intervention more accurate.

Figure 13: Data Fusion and Multisensor Integration.

Multisensor fusion refers to the ability to merge data acquired from different sensors into a single and coherent database. This process improves the quality and reliability of health information because different sensor modalities are utilized to their full potential in the system. For example, integrating data obtained from electrochemical sensors, which count glucose levels with optical sensors, which measure blood oxygen saturation offers a more holistic view of a patient's metabolism and respiration. It enables better diagnosis and treatment since the two features are integrated into one system.

Multisensor integration generalizes this idea as it compiles data from a wide range of different sensors, all of which measure different physiological values. It further allows for the detection of several condition markers, which makes for better evaluation of overall health status of the patient; for instance, a wearable device with temperature-sensing, acceleration, and heart rate to detect the thermos regulation mechanisms, physical activity, and cardiovascular state of the wearer simultaneously. This form of data collection is particularly useful where disease processes affect many physiological systems in serving the multifaceted health needs of clients.
