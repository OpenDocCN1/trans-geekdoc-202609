# 5.5.3 Wearable sensors for internal recording

As for implantable sensors, it is possible to monitor internal processes of chronic conditions, for which accurate and long-term surveillance is needed. These are implantable sensors that can monitor parameters such as intracranial pressure, glucose, and heart function. Some of the conditions that are best monitored through implantable sensors are diabetes and heart diseases because they give real-time information that is very vital in the management of the diseases. Recent developments in biocompatibility of materials as well as the above-mentioned aspects of miniaturization have enhanced the safety and comfort of these devices, making them potential candidates for use in elderly patients.

# 5.5.4 Integrated health monitoring systems

Central monitoring systems are developed from a single patient-health-monitoring unit and include more sensors and technologies to produce an overall and detailed information of the patients’ state. Such systems comprise wearable devices, home-based sensors, and other telemonitoring methods for multiple health parameters’ monitoring. For instance, an integrated system consolidates sensors for heart rate, blood glucose, and fall to ensure that it covers all aspects. Such systems facilitate team work, in that they make it possible for several caregivers to view several facets of a patient’s condition and make decisions based on the information presented.

# 5.5.5 Fall-risk identification and mitigation systems

Many fall-detection and prevention systems are essential components of monitoring in geriatric settings for long-term care. In elderly people, fall-risks are prevalent and often result in severe consequences. Present-day automated fall-prevention devices incorporate accelerometers, gyroscopes, and pressure sensors to detect a fall and notify the caregiver services instantaneously. Further, certain systems offer features for assessing potential falls by analyzing successfully whether changes in gait can be made before a physical fall takes place. Such systems improve the level of safety and the possible level of autonomy for elderly people by offering the ability to respond to falls as soon as they happen.
