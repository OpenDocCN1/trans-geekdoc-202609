# 6.1.3 Sources of accuracy issues

There are various factors that can attest to accuracy problems in biomedical sensors. These include:

Environmental factors: Fluctuations in temperature, humidity, and even electro-magnetic interference affect the sensor’s abilities. Biomedical sensors should be able to function properly, irrespective of the prevailing environmental characteristics, hence the need to design portable biomedical sensors.

Biological variability: The sensor is influenced by the difference in politics across individuals, be it the skin color of the tissues. There is biological variability of the human body that sensors should capture, and hence, sensors must be standardized.

Sensor degradation: Sensors, as any other equipment, can also become damaged over time from exposure to bodily fluids and physical abrasion. This is primarily due to problems associated with ageing of the sensors and the need to recalibrate the system to reflect the changes as they occur.

Interference from other substances: Chemical-type sensors respond not only to the quantity of interest but also to any chemical interferences in the sample. For instance, glucose sensors are affected by presence of other sugars in the blood stream. Such interference poses some challenges and it necessitates an effective calibration and correction algorithms.

# 6.1.4 The main challenges in calibration techniques

Calibration techniques for biomedical sensors can face several challenges, including:

Complexity of calibration processes: Of course, there are sensors that need multiple-point calibration with certain equipment. These processes often tend to become complex and making them simple without compromising on the accuracy is difficult.

Standardization issues: In order to manage heterogeneity, there is a need to agree on calibration procedures and the use of calibration metrics. A high level of variability means that sensors cannot be standardized, and this brings about differences in their operation and the interpretation of results supplied by these gadgets.

Real-time calibration: However, in dynamic situations where reconfiguration is necessary, as in the cases of wearable sensors, calibration in real-time is a problem. Work is still underway to come up with ways of making real-time calibration without affecting the sensor functionality.
