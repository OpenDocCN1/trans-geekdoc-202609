# 5.2.4 Wettability

Wettability, which is the ability of a material to interact with a liquid, determines the way biological fluids and their cells respond to the surface. In the present work, hydrophilic surfaces, which are surfaces that favor access to water, preferentially improve cell adhesion and proliferation because of their ability to allow protein deposition and cell spreading. Nonadherent hydrophobic surfaces, which do not adhere to water, significantly deter cell attachment and result in unfavorable bioresponses. Wettability can also be controlled through plasma treatment modification in order to better suit the material for biological applications.

# 5.2.5 Surface stability and degradation

The mobility of a material’s surface is important in the preservation of biocompatibility because of the resilience it provides to the surface over time. Alterations in the surface chemistry of the material due to changes in topographical features like roughness, hydrophobicity, and the chemical composition of the top layer, caused by exposure to body fluids, influence the functionality of the material as well as the body’s response to the material. For biodegradable materials, controlled degradation is critical to allow the release of degradation products and prevent the loss of mechanical integrity from being too abrupt. Controlling the stability of the outer surface is especially crucial, as surface stability helps predict overall performance in the long term, and its usage in medical applications requires safety and reliability.

Surface features remain core aspects due to their influence on the biomedical performance of medical materials and devices. Through this system, with improved surface chemistry, topography, roughness, wettability, and stability, the contact of materials with biological systems is improved, with reduced complications. New developments in surface science and technology of surface modification also help to enhance the design and functional applications of biocompatible materials.

# 5.3 Mechanical properties

In this context, the mechanical properties of biocompatible materials are critical for determining their suitability in biomedical applications. Key attributes such as strength, flexibility, hardness, and fatigue resistance play a major role in how a material performs within the biomechanical environment of the human body.
