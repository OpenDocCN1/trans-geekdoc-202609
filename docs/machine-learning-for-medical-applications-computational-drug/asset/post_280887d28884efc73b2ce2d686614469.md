### 5.2.2 Deep Q-networks: improving Q-learning with deep learning

A new approach was integrated with the Q-learning algorithm, namely involving DL methods in DQNs to help resolve the problems of large-sized state spaces. Therefore, while traditional Q-learning is not capable of dealing with highly complex data, DQNs are developed to solve more complicated material optimization problems because they use neural networks to approximately determine Q-values. DQNs can be employed in material science to decide on the refinement of methods that demand searches in incredibly large state as well as action spaces, such as chemical vapor deposition of composite materials. DQNs are used in the training process to allow for learning and to generate insights from approximately large data, enhancing the optimization formula by providing higher efficiency.

### 5.2.3 Challenges and considerations

There are several challenges when it comes to applying Q-learning and DQNs for material process optimization. Designing a well-defined reward function that aligns with the objectives of the material process presents a particularly significant challenge. This chapter discusses how the design of the reward function is crucial when conducting both learning and optimization. All methods based on DL, including DQN models, are computationally expensive, especially during the learning phase, particularly when working with large state and action spaces. From the points discussed above, one can also understand that managing convergence and stability during DQN training is not easy and can require extensive prior tuning of hyperparameters as well as careful design of the neural network's architectural structure. Solving these problems necessitates ongoing efforts to enhance algorithms and expand their applicability to a broader range of material science tasks.

Future developments in Q-learning and DQNs for enhancing material process optimization are likely to be stimulated by new technologies and methods. Combined with other machine learning approaches, including transfer learning and meta-learning, the overall performance and applicability of RL approaches improve for applications in material science. Newly developed hardware and computational facilities, capable of processing and adopting higher-dimensional and intricate problems, are expected to be achieved. Advances in reward functions and exploration functions enhance the performance of Q-learning and DQNs in optimizing and expanding material processes. This emerging prospect, along with these forthcoming advances, paves the way for extremely significant implementations of RL in material science and engineering.

## 5.3 Policy gradient methods for dynamic material property adjustments

Policy gradient methods refer to a set of RL algorithms that deal with the direct optimization of policy through estimation of the gradient of the expected reward with regard to policy parameters. These methods are especially useful for problems where the action space is continuous and the environment is either continuous and high-dimensional, dynamic and unknown, as in the case of adjusting the material properties in real applications. Using gradient ascent to learn and update the policy, policy gradient methods can therefore make timely corrections to the chosen material properties to realize optimal performance in accordance with specified objectives.

### 5.3.1 Using policy gradient methods in materials science

As applied to material science, policy gradient methods can be used in a way that allows material properties to change over time based on experiential feedback from the environment. For instance, in a dynamic environment of adaptive biomedical materials technology, policy gradient methods can be applied while designing materials to optimize material characteristics, including hardness, elasticity, and conductivity, at successively higher levels of the technology. These methods embrace real-time performance data and feedback, hence enabling the enhancement of material properties to match certain functional expectations and dynamic characteristics. This approach is especially beneficial in cases where material performance needs to be changed more regularly, for instance, in the case of responsive medical apparel or implants.

### 5.3.2 Strategies for the application of policy gradient techniques

Policy gradient techniques implementation presupposes several critical steps. First, a policy network was built to correlate a given set of actions to the current state of the material system. To train the policy network, the so-called policy gradient theorem was then used, which calculates the gradient of the anticipated reward with respect to the policy parameters. For better training stability and efficiency, a few techniques such as REINFORCE with Monte Carlo estimates of return, PPO, and TRPO can be used. As the policy parameters were updated toward the maximal expected reward, the material attributes were changed dynamically in response to the observed performance.

### 5.3.3 Challenges and considerations

Since policy gradient approaches have their advantages in the context of material property adjustment, they present several challenges. The main challenge is computational, particularly in the ability to train the policy network when the state and action spaces are high-dimensional. Besides, the quality of the reward function, which must appropriately represent the material property performance criteria, defines to what extent policy gradient approaches are effective. Additionally, placing an overemphasis on achieving convergence and stability in the training process highlights the importance of hyperparameter tuning and designing techniques to overcome problems such as overfitting and policy divergence. Solving these challenges requires the use of enhanced algorithmic methods coupled with high-power computational methodologies for reliable policy optimization.

It was expected that the policy gradient methods in the future for material science policies would experience both improvements in algorithms and use cases. Developments involve combining policy gradient methods with model-based RL to cyber one's framework in order to effectively tackle complex material systems across a wider variety of situations. Further, advances in computational trends, together with the enhancement of the reward function, enhance the efficiency of policy optimization. As these methods continue to advance, they have the potential to facilitate significant developments in the enhancement and realization of adaptive design and optimization of materials, with mechanisms and concepts aiming at more intelligent material systems across multiple applications.

## 5.4 Multiagent reinforcement learning for collaborative material discovery

The principles of Reinforcement Learning (RL) extend naturally to multi-agent environments, giving rise to Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), where multiple agents interact, coordinate, and optimize their actions collectively to achieve shared objectives. In the context of material discovery, MARL conceptualizes the process as a multi-agent system, where various agents collaboratively explore and optimize material properties. This framework enables researchers to model the complex interdependencies and interactions that often exist in material design and synthesis tasks. By leveraging MARL, researchers can address challenges related to flexibility, scalability, and cooperation, ultimately enhancing discovery outcomes and accelerating innovation in material science. Figure 10 illustrates the MARL-based approach to collaborative material discovery, showing how agent interactions drive efficient exploration of material design spaces.

### 5.4.1 Case studies of applying the MARL concept in material discovery

In material discovery, what can be applied in MARL is the ability to model and analyze a number of parameters of the research process. For example, one agent may be assigned the responsibility of data gathering, another for property prediction, and yet another for experimental assessment. These agents can cooperate to search material design spaces, share information, and adjust the strategies within those spaces as a result of their interactions. MARL makes it possible to achieve the required improvements in material discovery procedures by establishing connections between the agents, enabling them to draw conclusions from each other's actions, and providing methods to fine-tune the overall performance of the discovery system. In this regard, this collaboration can rapidly facilitate the search for potential materials and optimize the formulation of new material systems.

Looking at two main approaches, one strategy is decentralized learning, in which the decentralized agents take subproblems autonomously but keep communicating and sharing information with other decentralized agents in order to address given problems collectively.
