# 2 Introduction to machine learning in biomedical materials

# 2.1 Historical overview of biomedical materials development

The developmental process of biomedical materials can be divided into distinct milestones, which make a substantial impact on medical science and the treatment of patients. The start was in the early part of the twentieth century with the introduction of biocompatibility, the starting point toward designing implants that could be compatible with the tissues of the body. The first biomaterials had certain requirements involving mechanical properties and the biocompatibility of stainless steel and titanium, so they were chosen. These materials lacked design flexibility for integration with body tissue, which resulted in problems such as inflammation, infection, and implant rejection.

The evolution of polymer-based materials took a significant turn in the mid-twentieth century, driven by the demand for flexible, durable, and biocompatible solutions in medical applications. Polymers such as silicone and polyethylene emerged as ideal candidates due to their low cost, mechanical strength, and biocompatibility, making them suitable for a wide range of medical devices, including valves and joint replacements. This era also marked the beginning of tissue engineering, as researchers started exploring materials that could support and stimulate tissue regeneration. The introduction of biodegradable polymers, such as polylactic acid (PLA) and polyglycolic acid (PGA), revolutionized the field by enabling the development of scaffolding materials that naturally degrade in the body, reducing the need for follow-up surgeries. In the latter twentieth and early twenty-first century, the development of biomedical materials applications shifted from simply bio ceramics and biopolymers to bioactive and smart materials. Bioactive materials consist of bioactive glass and hydroxyapatite that are intended to be bioreactive with the surrounding biological systems on a molecular level, enhance bone formation, and improve the apposition of implants with the neighboring tissues. New types of materials, such as shape memory alloys and hydrogels, came into existence, offering functions that respond to stimuli, including temperature and pH impulses.
