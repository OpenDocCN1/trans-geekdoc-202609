# 2.2.3 New frontiers in the use of genomic profiling for targeted therapy

Currently, targeted therapy focuses on genomic testing, which analyzes the gene structure of a patient’s tumor in the body. It simplifies the selection of the appropriate medication that work, based on the specific genetic abnormalities and variations within cancer cells. Further, the approach of focusing on the specific molecules that cause the disease brings the efficiency of treatments to a higher level, not to mention of the reduction of the likelihood of side effects.

# 2.2.4 Application of genomics, in conjunction with other omics platforms

The distinction between disease processes and individualized treatments has been considered with the use of genomics, combined with other omics technologies like transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics. To get an improved insight into the molecular mechanisms of diseases, genetic data is integrated with data on protein concentrations, gene transcription rates, and metabolic networks. The holistic approach enables easy development of treatment programs for each patient that involves integration of many biological levels of information.

# 2.2.5 Ethical and practical implication of genomic medicine

As discussed in the paper, a number of practical and ethical concerns are associated with the extended use of genomics in personalized medicine. As a measure to ensure that free genomic information is protected and that it is treated in a fair way, concerns such as privacy, consent, as well as discrimination have to be met squarely. In addition, data by DNA requires high standards of data management and interpretation to feed into clinical practice for accurate decision-making.
