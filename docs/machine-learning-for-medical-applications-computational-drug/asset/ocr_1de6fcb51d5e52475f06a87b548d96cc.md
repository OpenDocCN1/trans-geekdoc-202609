Magnesium and its alloys: Owing to its potential use in degradable implants, magnesium, as well as its alloys, has attracted significant interest. This is because the gradual degradation of magnesium in the human body, which eliminates the need for surgical removal, enhances its biocompatibility compared to other materials. Some uses of magnesium-based alloys include temporary implants and bone-fixing elements. Through methods of both alloying and surface treatment, the rate of degradation of magnesium can be controlled to match the rate of tissue healing in the body so that while it acts as a temporary support structure, it is gradually absorbed by the body.

Noble metals: Due to their high corrosion resistance and biological compatibility, noble metals, including gold and platinum, are used in some biomedical applications. For example, platinum is used in medical implants and electrodes, while gold is used in dental materials and diagnostics. These metals are nonreactive, which mitigates potential chemical interactions and allows their application in conditions where biocompatibility and stability are essential.

# 3.2 Ceramics in biomedical applications

Ceramics possess an intimate combination of several properties: high strength, chemical stability, and biocompatibility. Thus, ceramics have found great demand in biomedical applications. Two of these materials are employed in a range of implants and medical devices, which enhance their mechanical properties and functionality.

Orthopedic implants are perhaps the most widely recognized application of ceramics in medicine. Due to their high corrosion stability and mechanical properties, ceramics containing alumina (AlO₄) and zirconia (ZrO₃) are usually used in hip and knee replacement operations. These materials are highly valued for their ability to withstand the mechanical loads related to joint arthroplasty and, at the same time, minimize the likelihood of wear debris causing inflammation of the implant.

This strategy has proven to be very effective when it comes to dealing with ceramic applications, coming out most exemplary. In regard to dental crowns, bridges, and veneers, it was the lithium disilicate and leucite-reinforced ceramics that were employed. Two of their esthetic properties, which are their transparency and color match ability, are also characteristic of natural teeth, providing a combination of cosmetic and functional benefits. Dental ceramics’ compatibility with the biologic system equally ensures the tissue compatibility around the ceramics, hence reducing the chances of adverse reactions.
