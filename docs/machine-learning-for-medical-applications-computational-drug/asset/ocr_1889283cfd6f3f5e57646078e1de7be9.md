# 3 Core concepts and definitions

# 3.1 Defining personalized medicine

The modern medical practice that goes by the names of precision medicine means that therapy matches the individual patient. This idea refers to the decision-making process, treatment, and therapy, individualized to a patient’s genetic predisposition, living environment, and personal traits. Personalized medicine is not identical with the medical model that tends to provide the same treatment for millions of patients, while considering certain peculiarities in an individual in order to enhance the effectiveness of the treatments.

Personalized medicine is basically about integrating a multitude of data – genomic, proteomic, and metabolomic – to drive medical decisions. For instance, genetic data can reveal information about how an individual is likely to develop a specific disease, how most likely the will individual respond to a specific treatment, and the what is the probability of the individual showing adverse effects to medication. In this information, healthcare professionals enhance the protective strategies, organize more directed and effective therapeutic processes, and improve clients’ outcomes.

As part of delivering customized treatment, the integration of advanced diagnostic tools and high-throughput technologies is essential for obtaining accurate and comprehensive insights into a patient’s condition. As illustrated in Figure 3, this includes the use of high-throughput screening (HTS) to identify diverse biomarkers and next-generation sequencing (NGS) to perform in-depth genome analysis. These technological advancements enhance the understanding of each individual’s unique biological characteristics and support the development of personalized treatment strategies tailored to specific patient profiles.

Personalized medicine is larger than the genetic approach to the patient; the concept integrates the patient, the physician, and the healthcare system. It involves blending the objective client-centered health parameters, physician-registered health status, and the context in which the individual exists. To this end, medical therapies are aligned to treat comorbid medical conditions, life styles, environs, and genetic proclivity, among other relevant aspects.

Personalized medicine includes the factors of stratified medicine, which divides patient’s group by some criterion, to be more effective. As such, this stratification promotes the effectiveness and safety of medical treatments by targeting patient groups that are likely to benefit from specific therapy techniques.
