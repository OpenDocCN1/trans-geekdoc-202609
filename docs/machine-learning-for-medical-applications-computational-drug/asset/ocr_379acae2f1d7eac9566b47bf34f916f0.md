Model Selection: L1 base models and G meta-learner were key to the success of stacking with L2 models. The efficiency of the ensemble highly depends on the choice and placement of these models.

Stacking continues to be an effective method of combining different models in machine learning to improve basic predictions. Its use in predicting drug toxicity makes it evident that it has the capacity to enhance safety evaluation and prevent adverse drug reactions.

# 4.4 Voting classifiers

Voting classifiers offer a simple form of ensemble learning to improve classification accuracy: Instead of relying on a single model’s prediction, voting classifiers use multiple models. In Figure 14, depicting the voting classifiers, a certain number of models contribute to the final prediction in an ensemble method, either by voting or majority rule. Voting classifiers take advantage of the variety among the different models by combining the outputs of many base classifiers to arrive at a final decision.

Majority Voting (Hard Voting): In majority voting, the class with the highest vote count is chosen as the final prediction. Every classifier in the ensemble provides its opinion on which class should be assigned. This method uses a simple majority rule and was earlier most efficient when the base classifiers are diverse and effective. Majority voting is particularly important when models have distinct advantages and disadvantages, as their combined results offer a reasonable outcome.

Averaging (Soft Voting): Finally, the last prediction was made by considering the class with the highest average probability value. Soft voting consists of averaging the predicted probabilities of all the classes for all base classifiers. This method can enhance the model's capability to handle uncertainty and was useful while working with probabilistic data. When confidence in the predictions of the base classifiers differs, “soft” voting turns out to be better than “hard” voting.
