# 6 Current trends and future directions

# 6.1 The rise of precision oncology and targeted therapies

The combination of genomic, proteomic, and clinical data has paved way to enhanced cancer therapy and even development of personalized and/or precision medicine. Due to the fact that they are specifically tailored to target aspects of tumor phenotype, genetic, epigenetic, and pharmacologic approaches to personalized therapy of cancer, they aim to optimize the beneficial effects of treatments and minimize side effects.

Some of the most significant technological developments in genomic profiling Genetic profiling technology is a key driver in the evolution of precision oncology, as it enables the demographic identification of patients’ genetic tendencies. Targeted Exome Capture (TEC), a high-throughput sequencing technique, has revolutionized the analysis of complex cancer genomes by facilitating the identification of specific genetic changes and variations associated with various cancer types. This wealth of genetic information supports the identification of molecular targets suitable for precise therapeutic intervention. For example, the discovery of actionable mutations such as EGFR in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has led to the development of targeted inhibitors, significantly improving patient outcomes. # 6.1.1 Clinical development of specific anticancer agents

Selective agents have now become the mainstay of personalized cancer care. These therapies are intended to target only molecular structures related to cancer, such as mutated proteins functioning in signaling pathways. The targeting used in the case of chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) and HER2 positive breast cancer are perfect examples of success in target therapies. These therapies have the added benefit over conventional chemotherapies in terms of targeting tissues but with a lesser degree of damage to neighboring tissues and thus higher therapeutic index.
