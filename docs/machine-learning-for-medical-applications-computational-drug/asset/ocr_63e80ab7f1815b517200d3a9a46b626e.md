Another approach to HTVS relies on molecular descriptors and fingerprints translating the chemical compounds into numerical values. Molecular descriptors reflect native properties of molecules, for example size, shape, and charge, while molecular fingerprints express structural details of chemical compounds. Similarity-based methods based on the present invention and kernel methods can then be used to determine the similarity of compounds between the original compounds and those of a chemical library. Compared to other potential style compounds, congeneric structure style compounds that have higher affinity to the receptor and lower binding energy are selected to continue experimental verification.

Ensemble models combine decisions of several base models that were trained in different techniques of data. Ensemble learning minimizes overfitting and collects data from several sources that are complements of the other to present more accurate and credible predictions that are made from different models.

Molecules and their hierarchical representations, atom interconnections, functional groups, and other complex features make HTVS a suitable area of application for deep learning methods, especially CNN and GNN. Some of these deep learning models can take molecular graphs representation of compounds as input and predict their bioactivity accurately.

# 3.2 QSAR modeling for predicting compound activity

The primary reason for using QSAR modeling in the discovery of hits in drug development is that their activities can be modeled based on the structures of the compounds. Thus, biological activity measures, for example binding affinities, are related quantitatively to molecular descriptors applying QSAR models, which are based on machine learning.

QSAR (Quantitative Structure–Activity Relationship) modeling begins with the identification of key molecular descriptors that characterize the chemical structure and properties of molecules. These descriptors can include molecular weight, lipophilicity, hydrogen bonding capacity and sites, as well as electronic properties. Furthermore, descriptors derived from molecular structures can be groundbreaking, particularly in understanding spatial configurations and binding patterns with biological macromolecules.
