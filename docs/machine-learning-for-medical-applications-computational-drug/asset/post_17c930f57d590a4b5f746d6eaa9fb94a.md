## 6.3 Remote patient monitoring systems

RPM devices involve integrated biomedical sensors and communication technology and are recognized as an indispensable aspect of modern medical practice to provide constant patient vigilance without direct clinical control. Through these technologies, remote data collection, transmission, and analysis are accomplished, and it has some benefits in patients as well as healthcare workers.

RPM systems monitor numerous health parameters and physiological signs employing different biomedical sensors. These sensors are often integrated into wearable technology devices, for example, smart wristbands and watches, fitness trackers, and into home healthcare devices, for example, glucose and blood pressure meters. RPM systems are excellent in presenting an all-round view of a patient’s health status by periodically measuring factors such as pulse rate, blood pressure, blood sugar level, and oxygen level, among others.

One of the advantages of RPM systems is its ability to offer proactive management and early treatment for chronic illness. Diabetic patients can, for instance, use glucometric devices that allow medical practitioners to get values from patients undergoing treatment. This helps to be able to change the further program of work and make adequate decisions within a short time if there are signs of deterioration. In a similar way, hypertension patients enjoy the convenience that comes with constant monitoring of blood pressure since it simplifies management of prescriptions and avoidance of complications among patients with cardiovascular diseases.

Also, RPM systems are required to enhance the patient’s engagement and compliance with various treatment regimens. Such solutions help people to become more engaged in their healthcare process through using their own health data shared through mHealth applications. In these applications, feedback involves patient-tailored advice, which makes patients change their habits and focus on following a treatment regimen, adopting better lifestyles, and assuming more responsibilities for their treatment.

These systems have a lot of advantages: the number of visits of doctors and other caregivers during direct patient consultations is reduced and this can potentially decrease the load on medical centers and patients. This is from the viewpoint of the healthcare professional. From the process of continuous monitoring, providers can therefore tweak care plans and come up with better judgments. This method leads to enhanced diagnostic and therapeutic processes as well as enhanced impact of care.

Several examples of RPM systems show that they are useful in many areas of medicine. For example, COPD patients managed through telemonitoring have had enhanced control of exacerbations and consequently enrollment reduction in hospitals. Likewise, RPM systems for individuals recovering from surgery have also indicated early signs of postsurgical complications and reduced rehabilitation duration.

Alongside the benefits, there are still challenges to RPM systems’ general adoption. Thus, implications of new technologies have to be identified and met, which would allow ensuring the efficient and moral use of technology; concerns such as data protection, safety, and integration into the existing framework of healthcare should be considered. In addition, ongoing investigations are necessary to enhance RPM systems’ precision, reliability, and relevance.

## 6.4 AI in telehealth and telemedicine

Telehealth and telemedicine are defined as the use of modern telecommunication technologies that allow the healthcare stakeholders to attend to the needs of the patients, no matter where they are located, as well as to provide medical professionals, physicians, patients, and other stakeholders with valuable information in a more efficient and effective manner – thanks to the integration of AI in telehealth and telemedicine. Telehealth platforms can harness these technologies to improve diagnostics and patient engagement and customize care for those who use telehealth services thereby revolutionizing the delivery of care.

Telemedicine and telehealth are enhanced by AI through the ability to enhance data analysis and decision support. To make better decisions, healthcare professionals rely on the results of the analysis of patient information, including EHRs, medical images, and PROMs that are carried out by ML algorithms and NLP. For instance, diagnostic instruments can “read” images of the patient’s scans to look for signs of disease like cancers and then report back to radiologists who attest to its accuracy of up to 95%. This capability helps maintain a fast and accurate diagnosis, which is of great help to clinicians in making the right decisions.

The other major area where AI has made a big impact in telehealth is in the creation of virtual health assistants and chatbots. Such devices can interact with patients in order to obtain symptoms data, propose a preliminary diagnosis, and reveal the actions to be taken. These virtual assistants filter patient complaints and provide the information on illnesses to avoid overload of healthcare officials. This is especially beneficial in the event that the user is in a remote area where access to medical practitioners can be very rare.

In telemedicine, AI is also used to identify patients’ needs and provide appropriate follow-up service. Analytical models for predictive assessment work through patterns in records to identify potential sickness risks and to formulate individual treatment plans. Using wearable sensors, AI, for instance, tracks patients with chronic diseases such as diabetes and alerts the patient in real time. Preventive measures in healthcare are promoted and the management of such diseases is enhanced by the targeted approach.

The impact of AI in telehealth is in data integration and data interoperability to improve the efficacy of the platform. AI systems can combine patient data not only from mHealth applications, wearable devices, and home monitoring applications, but also from the patient’s EHR. Such an approach enhances continuity of care since care providers are compelled to work as a team in delivering optimum care and the resultant care plans are based on a coordinated perception of the patient’s ailment.

AI also has an impact on the technical side when applied to telehealth with regard to access and equity questions. Since teleconsultations offer the possibility to overcome language barriers, telehealth platforms can reach populations and communities that hardly have access to relevant healthcare services. This helps spread the knowledge of medical professionals and also helps achieve fairness with medical services.

## 6.5 Success stories and clinical trials

Several success stories involving AI integration with biomedical sensors exist, and multiple significant clinical trials have been conducted proving the effectiveness of such technologies.

A new success story is the creation of AI-based wearable technology for constant blood glucose monitoring. Abbott Diabetes Care and Dexcom are leading manufacturers of glucose sensors that utilize AI algorithms to give out glucose level readings in real time. Some of these devices are being tested with a level of efficacy that has reduced incidences of hypoglycemia and enhanced glycemic control in diabetics. For instance, the Dexcom G6 continuous glucose monitoring system has been tested scientifically and clinically.
