# 4.2.4 Afflation of combining nanotechnology with microfluidics

A marriage between nanotechnology and micro fluidics has taken the sensor design to a new level. Microfluidics deals with the movement of small volumes of fluids through microchannels and that when integrated with nanotechnology, produces compact, sensitive, and fast sensors. There is the employment of nanomaterials inside microfluidic systems for biosensing and improvement of biomarker detection as well as for integrated sample preparation and for on-chip analysis. Combined, these result in higher sensitivity and shorter times for detection, which makes the process ideal for use in the diagnosis at the point of care.

# 4.2.5 Novelty in nanotechnology: nanostructured sensing platforms

The inclusion of nanomaterials by current advances in nanostructured sensing platforms results in improved accord sensitivity and multiscale function. These include optical sensors using nanoparticle with SERS and field-effect transistors using nanowire for the detection of biomolecules, without the need for label. These platforms take advantage of the unconventional characteristics of nanomaterials to provide very accurate measurements of low concentrations of analytes.

Nanotechnology has brought broader possibilities to biomedical sensing through refinement of technology to produce some of the most sensitive sensors for the identification of physiological and biochemical parameters. It is also anticipated that as research advances at the nanoscale levels, nanotechnology would open new horizons for improvement in sensor development and application in health assessment, prognosis, and disease prevention.

MEMS technology reflects a brand-new stage for sensor technologies, with their unique feature being that combines mechanical and electronic elements on a micro scale. MEMS technology, when applied to biomedical fields, has improved the accuracy, functionality, and flexibility of sensors used in chronic health monitoring systems.
