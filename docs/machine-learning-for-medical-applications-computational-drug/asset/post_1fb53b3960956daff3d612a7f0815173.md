
These augmentations make models invariant to changes in image acquisition settings and enhance generality from one situation to another.

Synthetic image generation: To improve the training dataset, compose more realistic synthetic pictures using techniques such as GANs. New variations and situations not included in the training data set are introduced through synthetic photos.

A crucial step in image processing, referred to as noise reduction, assists in enhancing the acquisition of images by eradicating aberrant distortions and impediments to assessment. A number of techniques are used to lower noise and improve the clarity of medical images:

- Filtering techniques: In order to blur the pictures and reduce noise while preserving characteristics, techniques such as bilateral, median, and Gaussian are used. While median filters are excellent for removing salt-and-pepper noise by replacing a pixel's value with the median of the pixels in its immediate vicinity, Gaussian filters use a weighted average of the nearby pixels.
- Wavelet transform: Specifically, it breaks the images into different frequency bands and then attenuates the noise in the WT domain individually. It provides a good tradeoff between noise removal and detail preservation, qualities that make it suitable for enhancing medical images.
- Denoising autoencoders: A class of NNs that learn a mapping from a noisy image to a clean image. They are trained to make clean images out of noisy inputs and, in so doing, learn to remove noise while preserving the relevant details.

## 5.3 Feature extraction methods: manual vs. automated

Prior to analysis and classification, extracting features from medical images was one of the most critical steps in image processing, called feature extraction, which involved segregating and identifying specific characteristics. There are two ways to approach this process: manually and automatically. As with virtually everything in life, there is a positive side and a negative side to each of them.

Historically, feature extraction has been a complex process that involved designing and implementing a number of algorithms and heuristics with the purpose of extracting relevant features from medical images according to the knowledge and experience of the human expert in the field. This approach includes techniques such as:

- Edge detection: Boundary detection in images is achieved by using methods such as Sobel, Canny, and Prewitt. These methods usually emphasize thin lines around different structural areas, for example, when outlining a tumor.
- Texture analysis: Measuring the textural features of areas in an image with statistical methods, including energy, contrast, and uniformity of textured areas, is needed to characterize an image. Texture analysis can provide information about the type of tissue and the structural arrangement of the tissues.

- Shape analysis: Identifying geometric features such as shape, size, and orientation of various structural components, figures, and forms. Conventional approaches such as contour detection and region growing are employed to detect and measure the attributes of the anatomy.

In the traditional manual feature extraction process, measurements depend on the expert knowledge of the specific area, and more effort is needed when designing and tuning the extraction pattern algorithms. Although this kind of approach provides certain specific functions with substantial efficiency, it is always characterized by prolonged and intensive work, including even feature-value calculations. Also, the features extracted manually do not include all the needed information suited for other types of data.

Automatic feature extraction allows one to extract features directly from image data by applying machine learning and deep learning methods. Key methods include:

- CNNs: CNNs employ numerous layers of convolution filters to obtain the hierarchical features from the raw picture data themselves. As you see, these networks can, with almost no help from the user, extract highly abstract and complex features such as texture, form, and pattern.
- Unsupervised learning algorithms called autoencoders map input pictures into another subspace and then map them back. From the other features, fundamental data properties are stored in the encoded form for utilization in secondary analysis.
- Feature acquisition: During the training stage itself, other kinds of network architectures, such as restricted Boltzmann machines and deep belief networks, extract features from the visual input. They capture relationships and correlations that can be seen in the pictures.

Some of the biggest advantages of performing automated feature extraction include the fact that the algorithm can sometimes identify features that are not easily discerned using traditional methods as well as the system’s ability to sift through thousands of records. This method proves to be quite flexible and can easily be applied at scale and also tends to fit well with various imaging techniques and environments too. Since the learned features do not always coincide with human perception, the automated methods are hard to explain and consume a lot of processing resources.

There was always a decision to be made whether to use techniques based on a human input system for feature extraction in a specific medical imaging application, depending on the requirements of any given job. In those cases where features are well-established and domain experience is critical, the manual technique could serve well. In cases where data sets are large and the characteristics to be extracted are complex and hard to define, various automated techniques are advantageous.

In fact, the strategy lying somewhere between purely automatic and purely manual is the most effective one. Automated feature selection can serve as an enlarged and optimized feature set, supplementary to the features first defined through manual methods for the design of new automated systems.

## 5.4 Model selection and training: classical algorithms vs. deep learning models

The main decision to be made when selecting a model for medical imaging was between classical machine learning models and deep learning models, as well as understanding the advantages and disadvantages of each. The decision affects the effectiveness and relevance of machine learning applications in medical image analysis.

More specifically, some of the classical algorithms that have been reported to have significant results are SVMs, k-NN, and the decision tree, to name but a few. These methods handcraft features from photographs and then use learning algorithms to classify with respect to these features.

- SVMs: SVMs are good with datasets of high dimensions and can be used in classification tasks. They construct a hyperplane in order that the optimal margin between the classes is achieved as much as possible. Despite the fact that SVMs have been found to be effective in numerous medical image applications, it has been observed that the quality of feature extraction and the size of the dataset that can be managed often influence the performance of an SVM.
- k-NN: One of the simplest algorithms of instance-based learning is k-NN, which classifies new instances with the aid of the majority of its nearest neighbors. It is easy and fast; however, if no proper dimensionality reduction methods are applied, it becomes computationally intensive and not very accurate for high dimensions.
- Decision trees: They are also good for decision-making and, more importantly, interpretable because they divide the data space into regions according to feature values. Nevertheless, they are prone to overfitting, especially if the dataset is complex and involves medical image data to a significant extent, and often require the use of ensembles, for instance, random forest, in order to work efficiently.
