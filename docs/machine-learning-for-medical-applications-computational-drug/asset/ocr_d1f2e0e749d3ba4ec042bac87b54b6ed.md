# 3.3.1 S10Molecular and genetic characterization

Molecular and genetic characterization is essential so as to identify certain molecular and genetic signatures that actually affect the disease process and the response to certain treatment regimes. As the name implies, various methodologies are used, including genomics, gene panels, and RNA profiling to help discover the mutated genes, gene activity, and molecular signaling routes. Such information assists in recommending an individual’s specific therapy and developing a treatment model that responds to the molecular characteristics.

# 3.3.2 Individual therapy models

Integrated individualized therapies are components of the unique treatment. These cover choosing and fine-tuning the therapy according to the patient, his/her phenotype, tumor stage, and previous treatments outcomes. Therapies refer to targeted drugs that are used and individualized chemotherapy. The hypothetical aim of the personalized treatment is to design it in order to achieve the highest therapeutic effect and minimal toxicity based on the patient’s biology and clinical characteristics.

# 3.3.3 Dynamic treatment adjustments

Flexible treatments are also necessary parts of individualized treatment plans since constant patient evaluation and response define the need for modifying the treatments’ approaches. This means that after clinical examination, imaging, and biomarkers, the treatment can be altered immediately, since it is a continuous process. This setup also guarantees that the therapeutic management continually adjusts for the patient’s changing status and response to treatment, thereby increasing the efficiency and safety of the total treatment process.

# 3.3.4 Main provisions of the interdisciplinary approach

A prime necessity is enhancing the synthesis of multidisciplinary care structure for the formulation of care strategies. Multidisciplinary teamwork involves working with geneticists, oncologists, pharmacologists, and other specialists to fully address patient’s concerns. This structural approach promotes construction of integrated management plans due to the associated systemic relationships between genetic, environmental, and clinical factors.
