Looking at the contribution of ML to biomedical material discovery, the following discoveries can be noted. In recent years, the employment of predictive modeling and techniques of high-throughput screening has led to a drastic reduction in the time necessary to accomplish the screening of potentially favorable materials and a minimum dependency on the conventional, time and money-consuming empirical screening methods. Specifically, the use of transfer learning has led to many new and specific biomedical applications, which have been adapted based on existing models to create new solutions for the delivery of drugs and tissue engineering as well as for personalized medicine. All the above demonstrations show the effectiveness of applying ML in fulfilling specific medical requirements to transform material, enhance patients’ quality of life, and improve health-care technologies.

There are a few issues that need consideration to unleash the full potential of ML in biomedical materials science. Data acquisition from different sources, model explain ability, and demand for computational power are considered the challenging aspects that need further development. The main focus must be on data quality as well as on creating the proper means to integrate the models into the respective systems. Improving the explanation of these models within both the academic and applied communities is necessary for the sciences as well as for the application of ML findings in the field.

At the same time, the application of ML in biological materials research looks rather promising. Further advancements in the identification of material properties and their optimization are possible due to contributions from new algorithms and technologies, including deep learning and online data analysis. The integration of material property monitoring with advanced technologies, including modern modeling techniques and detailed imaging, can enrich the characteristics of materials and their performance. Also, questions of an ethical character – such as algorithmic prejudice and data protection – are crucial to providing for the proper advancement and deployment of forward-thinking ML systems.
