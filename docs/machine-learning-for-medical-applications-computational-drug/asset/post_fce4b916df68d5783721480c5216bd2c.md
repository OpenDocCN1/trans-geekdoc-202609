## 2 Historical background and evolution of personalized medicine

### 2.1 Origins of personalized medicine

The concept of pharmacogenomics that brought some ideas of individualized medicine has early roots in the notion of splitting medicine usage according to an individual’s genetic data. The field of pharmacogenomics, which emerged in the middle of the twentieth century aimed to understand how genes explain how drugs and other agents operate in the body. This means that by addressing the observation in patients that pharmacological response both in terms of effectiveness and safety varies, this strategy intends to show the way for more precise and effective treatment.

The major scientific advancement that set the ball rolling on the concept of personalized medicine was the discovery of the double helix DNA by Watson and Crick in 1953. The advancement means the scientific world now has a clearer idea of the means by which the genetic code holds sway over health and illness. The FAIR act was a basis for the Human Genome Project, which began in 1990 and came to completion in 2003. As a project that strived to map the whole human genome the initiative sought to identify and sequence the 20,000 to 25,000 genes that define man’s biology. The extreme success of the Human Genome Project gave a new impetus to genetics and created many chances for individualized therapy.

Genomics initiated in the early 2000s led to identification of several genetic variations associated with different diseases and patient responses to treatment. The knowledge of specific indicators that determine the rate of a given person’s susceptibility to specific diseases and reaction to various medications also saw major developments during this period. P4 Medicine adopted a principle that was popularized by pharmacogenomics – right drug, right dose, right patient.

Over this period, pharmacogenomics evolved to embrace and accommodate other influences that have an impact on human health in addition to genetic influences such as lifestyle and environmental factors. Management and implementation of these variables to tactics in the strategy for individualized treatment marked a shift to better understanding of patient-centered care. It also revealed the fact that the dependence of various diseases on hereditary factors should not rule out the other necessary environmental factors.

In the perspective of biomarker validation, personalized medicine has progressed because of high-throughput technologies such as bioinformatics and next generation sequencing. These technologies therefore make it cheaper and faster to analyze large genomic data sets, and so open up the possibility of identifying new biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets. As a result, the scope of individualized medicine analysis is much broader than that of pharmacogenomics, expanding to how individualized medications are developed, how preventative techniques are fit to an individual, and checking for disease susceptibilities.

Indeed, cancer care has been the forerunner of thoughtful, intense intentions toward hopeful therapeutics since genetic discoveries were effectively applied into the practice of oncology. Molecular alterations that are directed toward the cancers being treated are also a good example of personalized medicine in cancer therapy, for instance, trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancer and imatinib in chronic myeloid leukemia.

### 2.2 Milestones in the development of personalized healthcare

Personalized medicine has progressed through several evolutionary milestones relevant to the given field, which has made great leaps. These achievements describe the development from fundamental conceptual models to advanced statistical models to considerate customized medical treatment management.

### Pharmacogenomics: historical perspective and development (1950–1960)

The idea of pharmacogenomics started to emerge as soon as scientists started to find the genetic differences affecting metabolite pathways of some drugs. Some of the earliest pieces of work clearly identified differences in the activity of enzymes, which can cause variations in drug metabolism among people. These years provided the basis for comprehending the issues connected with the genetic effects of drug effectiveness and side effects.

The Human Genome Project announced in February 1988 was formally launched in October 1990.

An initiative with a scientific approach from all over the world known as Human Genome Project (HGP) focused on mapping the whole human genome. The astonishing feat completed in 2003 furnished genomic details, which are part of personalized treatment, for instance, detailed genetic blueprint. It helped in increasing appreciation of inheritance and tailor-made solutions by offering an understanding of the varieties of genetic makeup the human body has concerning various diseases.

### Advancements of technology in genomic sequencing in the 2000s

Next-generation sequencing, the advancements of which formed the basis for the achievements of the HGP, began in the early 2000s. Technological advancements such as next-generation sequencing (NGS) enhanced genomic sequencing in terms of cost and time thus embracing its use in clinical diagnosis. This development led to a criterion that allows genetic data to be used in the customized medicine system.

The latter was inaugurated by the arrival of targeted therapies in the course of the decade 2000–2010.

One of the key shifts in customized medicine was the development of targeted medicines. Imatinib for treating CML and trastuzumab for treating HER2+ BC were among examples of genetic breakthroughs that were used in creating drugs, which acted specifically once certain biological markers were identified. These treatments demonstrated more about steps that are used to plan and execute interventions that improve patient outcomes more.

### Big Data integration and AI (2010–present)

There has been a shift in the customized medicine subsector through the integration of AI and big data analysis. Genetic, clinical, and environmental data are gathered for a large number of patients and then preprocessed to look for tendencies and predict the responses by means of large and complex formulas and machine learning. Therapeutic and diagnostic treatments get enhanced by the use of AI solutions and help in designing individual therapy plans.

### Personalized medicine initiative development and partnerships (2010s onwards)

Several efforts and partnerships have been created to advocate the adoption and innovation of personal medicine. The most recent example was the Precision Medicines Initiative in the United States and similar initiatives in other countries. These programs aim at facilitating collaboration between academics and/or physicians on the one hand and patients on the other, and fostering research and exchange of data in the domain of personalized medicine with the vision of enhancing the latter's implementation in clinical work.

### Individualized therapy in mainstream ambulatory care setting: current and future

The latest achievement is the further entrenchment of personalized medicine into a myriad of typical clinical operations. Genome sequencing and Big Data utilization in medicine and precision medicine are making their way into routine practice more and more often. These trends have shifted towards more personalized care designated as an effort to enhance patients' outcomes and interventions and prevention processes.

## 2.3 Evolution of treatment approaches

One of the major revolutions in the flow of treatment strategies in medicine is a shift from the generalized approach to treatment to the individualized approach to treatment: a shift that personifies future-focused and precision medicine. An analysis of the evolution of treatment approaches suggests that while, initially, strategies are very global, addressing the context generally, specialist approaches have been developed to consider different features of different patients.

It is important to appreciate the ability of the theory to impose generalized treatments in hierarchical structures.

At first, there were very few individual and varied medical cures with most methods being rather crude and pragmatic, not indicative of the actual condition of the patient. The usage of these treatments presumed that the patients of different categories.
