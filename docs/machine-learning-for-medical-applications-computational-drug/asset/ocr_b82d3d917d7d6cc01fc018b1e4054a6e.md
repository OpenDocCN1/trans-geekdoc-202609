One of its promises is saving the lives of patients, contributing to the efficiency of treatment methods and enhancing the diagnostic accuracy provided by combinations of disparate source of information concerning the patient [3]. Recent advances in high-throughput sequencing techniques have also advanced the scope of this strategy by permitting complex examination of the implications of genetic modifications for health and disease [4]. Personalized medicine is not only an effort to bring improvements to treatment methods but also attempts to avoid diseases through early detection of at-risk individuals due to difference in genetic and molecular characteristics [5].

An important part of large-scale individualized medicine is the development of targeted drug delivery systems to deliver agents with therapeutic activity and minimize the side-effect profiles [6]. These technologies apply proteomics and genetics knowledge to control the method and form of medication administration, according to individual patient characteristics [7]. For enhancing treatment outcomes and reducing the chances of drug-induced side effects, novel carriers can be designed to deliver the drug selectively to the specific target, say tumor tissues [8]. It is easier to design such systems today since nanotechnology and biomaterials have improved to give exact control over the rate of medication release and the targeting strategies [9]. Personalized medicine distribution also improves the general patient experience and patient outcomes since they are less susceptible to negative effects, and receive better therapeutic end-products [10].

Also, it has to be mentioned that the combination of multi-omics data, including genomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, is a major leap forward in the progress of making personal medicine [11]. Here, the omics data are integrated in a way that takes into account the biological context to better understand the systems view of biology and disease [12]. By combining these three approaches, genomic data can be mapped and compared to proteomics and metabolomics, which helps researchers and clinicians define new biomarkers, learn more about the dynamics of the disease process, and create new and precise diagnostic and therapeutic protocols [13]. For example, the association of mutations with the expression levels of proteins and alterations in metabolic profile facilitates the understanding of the development and pathogenesis of diseases [14]. Such an approach allows the definition of the corresponding therapeutic regimens that are applicable singly for each client, depending on the molecular markers, thus providing more effective and individualized approaches to treatment [15].

In this context, it can be said that technology is contributing to the development of personal medicine due to the provision of the instruments necessary for the assessment of other complex bioinformatics and the subsequent implementation of these findings in practice [16].
