The two future paths that are considered important include: The coupling of ML algorithms with real-time monitoring solutions – Daily health data such as vital signs, medication compliance, and even physical activity, for example, the kind of real-time health data that wearables and biosensors currently monitor. Machine learning algorithms can analyze different data flows in real-time, which allows offering individuals dynamic and adaptive choices of medications. For instance, continuous monitoring of patients with chronic diseases can be done through ML algorithms, where information from glucose monitoring devices is processed and changes in insulin dosages are made in real-time to keep the patient’s blood sugar levels optimal. Full accuracy and responsiveness with such high measures are expected to significantly improve the management of some chronic diseases.

More significant opportunities emerge if machine learning is applied directly to the drug development pipeline. Using trial and error or actual data on biological systems takes nearly substantial time but by using machine learning algorithms to mimic and forecast their behavior, one can fasten the process in arriving at the design and testing of new drug delivery systems. The time and cost within the laboratory experiment can also be saved when using virtual screenings on medication candidates and their delivery systems. Also, better medicinal drug formulations and delivery systems can also be obtained by using optimization of formulations with the help of ML, hence leading to increased stability, bioavailability, and efficacy. This issue increases the tempo of new drug delivery solutions development with regard to specific therapeutic needs.

## 6.5 Recommendations for overcoming barriers and advancing ML in biomedical research

In the field of ML, there is a huge prospect to improving medicine administration and in research in the biomedical field. There are several barriers that must be addressed to harness the mentioned potential. Some of the tactical recommendations are as follows, in an effort to go past these barriers and enhance the use of machine learning in biomedical research.

First of all, it becomes crucial to bring together representatives of different faculties. Multidisciplinary teams such as computer science, biomedical engineering, pharmacological, and clinical medicine need to be included in order to advance the field with respect to the use of ML. Effort should be made to bring in experts in the biomedical domain as well as in the application of ML in solving such challenges. The lessons learned from the creation of interdisciplinary research centers and funding initiatives aiming at use of ML in healthcare can be used to ensure similar approaches in the future.

Secondly, the methods of improving the data availability, coupled with interoperability, dominate the use of applying ML in biomedical research. The healthcare industry is a large data producer with EHRs, genomics, imaging, and patient-reported outcomes. Standardization of data forms as well as setting up of data exchange mechanisms and regulation of data security issues are critical. There is a requirement for developing databases and cloud-based systems that are in line with the regulations needed for patient identification, yet allowing researchers to gain access to the data needed.

Thirdly, significant importance must be given to data quality and its management. Deep-learning techniques rely heavily on training data, which, as stated, means that an ML model is only as good as the data it was fed. Recommendations should be to concentrate on data cleansing and normalization and effective validation. Examples of such techniques include natural language processing, image recognition, and other contemporary data preprocessing methods that can help improve dataset completeness by automating data preprocessing jobs. Crowdsourcing and large-scale annotation can increase the capacity of curating data and diversify the datasets we can put together.

Fourth, endorsing progress in the methodological advances in developing and validating ML algorithms is crucial to the biomedical field. Scholars should pay more attention to the enhancement of the XAI models that can help improve the interpretability of the models. The last stage involves comparing the constraints of the learned functions with the gold standard benchmarks, and real-world clinical data is important in establishing the reliability as well as the generalizability of the developed ML models. Adopting more openness in science, including reporting of code, algorithms, and prescriptive benchmark datasets can enhance parity and improve the pace of algorithm optimization/scrutiny and maturity.

Fifth, transitioning toward obtaining regulatory permission and public acceptability in the execution of biomedical research using the ML approach demand answering ethical and regulatory questions. It means that there is a necessity of collaboration between regulatory bodies and other parties in order to determine rules regarding creation, verification, and utilization of machine learning models in the context of the healthcare industry. Privacy and confidentiality of the patient data means that one has to follow the data protection laws in the country of operation, which include the GDPR and HIPAA. It, therefore, should be noted that ML should be properly implemented with ethical models of algorithmic fairness, patient's self-governance, and eradicating conducive bias.

## 7 Conclusion

The application of ML improves drug delivery systems; in fact, it seems to have revolutionized biomedical science as it dramatically improves efficiency and accuracy of the drug development processes. It has looked at how ML approaches estimate properties of necessary materials like biocompatibility, degradation rates, and mechanical properties that are relevant for drug delivery. Exclusive formulas or models in machine learning have shown advancements in works such as the adjustment of the release rate or speed of the drug, with a view to enhancing effectivity in patient-specific treatment procedures. The chapter also emphasizes how ML applies adjustments in nanoparticle construction parameters and refreshes the combination of experimental and computational methods in the design of targeted delivery systems. Nevertheless, there are some issues that have not been solved yet – the problem of compliance with the requirements of relevant legislation, integration of the data into the program, as well as the question of the stability of algorithms. The lack of ethical considerations in developing ML technologies is prohibitive in the use of the technology in healthcare. In future, coordinated work of researchers, clinicians, and policymakers should be considered as key to the utilization of ML in drug delivery systems in order to facilitate the development of personalized medicine and for better quality of patient's treatment.
