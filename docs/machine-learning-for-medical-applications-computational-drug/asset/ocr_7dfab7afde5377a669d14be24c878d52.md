Wearable ECG monitors are considered as unique gadgets primarily intended for constant registration of the activity of the heart. Wrist band, chest harness of other wearable forms, and wearable ECG monitors help to regulate heart problems as they involve monitoring arrhythmia, heart rate variability, and other aspects of cardiac structure and function. Consequently, the constant data gathered by those gadgets can be examined for flagging of possible cardiovascular disorders with a view to taking suitable remedies on time alongside handling heart-related complications.

CGM devices are designed to be worn by patient with diabetes for tracking blood glucose levels at a constant rate during everyday activities. These devices generally comprise a small sensor that is inserted under the skin, which checks the glucose level in the interstitial fluid. The data is then transmitted to a wearable receiver, thereby giving users an instant feedback on the glucose values and trends. The benefits of CGM are that it can improve the overall diabetic management since patients can adjust the doses of insulin more accurately, monitor patterns of glucose level, and use fewer fingerstick tests.

# 2.2 Implantable sensors

Implantable sensors are sophisticated biomedical devices intended to be implanted within the human body to deliver permanent constant surveillance and control of many physiological elements. The type of data that such sensors provide is highly accurate, stable over extended periods, and in real-time, which serves the patient with chronic diseases and the conditions that require comprehensive medical treatment.

Pacemakers and cardiac monitors are among the most widely used implantable sensors, designed to manage and monitor heart health. Pacemakers are small electronic devices implanted in the chest to regulate heart rate, especially in patients suffering from conditions like atrial fibrillation or heart block. These sensors can monitor the heart’s electrical activity and continuously analyze heart rate and rhythm. For instance, modern pacemakers can provide detailed, real-time feedback on cardiac activity, enabling healthcare providers to adjust treatment plans based on accurate, up-to-date information. Additionally, portable yet invasive devices like implantable loop recorders offer long-term ECG monitoring and can effectively detect arrhythmia-related abnormalities over extended periods, which is highly beneficial for preventive care and diagnosis.

Insulin pumps and glucose sensors are important diabetes technology tools that allow for constant check and accurate regulation of blood sugar levels.
