When there are large numbers of chemical descriptors and absorption outcomes for which the latter is known, it is possible to use commonly used ML algorithms such as, SVM, random forest, and GBM and learn complex relationship. Through the use of these predictive models, the researchers are able to select for further development, lead compounds with the best absorption profiles.

ML can estimate compound distribution characteristics: BBB penetration and tissue distribution. Employing lipophilicity, molecular size, and hydrogen bond donor and acceptor counts as predictor variables, ML algorithms can estimate the propensity with which the compounds are able to cross the biological barriers to reach their sites of action in vivo. These predictions allow for the determination of how lead compounds distribute in the body and allow chemical modifications to be made in order to improve target interaction and overall activity.

Substances can be predicted based on features like CYP450 interactions and metabolic stability using machine learning models. Through training with compound sets possessing characterized metabolic outcomes, the ML algorithms unveil structural markers related to metabolic activation and metabolic deactivation and estimate the probability of metabolic clearance in vivo. Currently, ML models have become an integral part of lead optimization approaches to predict the properties of the lead compounds, specifically ADMET characteristics. By analyzing the lead candidate’s safety and pharmacokinetic characteristics, ADMET prediction helps discover and optimize active compounds suitable for further development and enhancement.

Machine learning models assist in lead optimization by predicting some characteristics of the absorption of chemicals such as oral bioavailability and intestinal permeability. Other approaches, for instance, SVMs, random forests, and GBMs, can be learnt from a data set of some compounds for which the absorption characteristics are known and look for more intricate associations between the causal chemical descriptors and the absorption results.

The applicability of the framework and the ML models are demonstrated in the ability of the models to predict toxicophore properties like mutagenicity, hepatotoxicity, and cardiotoxicity. Because of this, the training of an ML algorithm on datasets of compounds, which have a known toxicological profile allows for the determination of structural alerts and pharmacophore patterns, which correlate with the compound toxicity and odds of reaching toxicity. These predictions allow researchers to categorize lead compounds as to which of them are safe to continue development before clinical trials in order to avoid potential dangerous reactions and safety issues.
