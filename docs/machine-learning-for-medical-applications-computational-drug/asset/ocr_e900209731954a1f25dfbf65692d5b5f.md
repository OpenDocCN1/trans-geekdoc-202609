# 2.2 Big data analytics for predictive modeling

Decision science and biomaterials have advanced significantly in incorporating big-data analytics into biomaterials design, offering the potential to greatly enhance biomaterials manufacturing. The use of biomaterials and applications in biomaterials science and engineering is growing in terms of complexity and the number of applied fields. Thus, the amount of experimentally, simulated, and clinically based data is increasing rapidly. Such a wealth of information demands powerful methods of analysis to reveal significant insights, describe material behavior, and facilitate the discovery of new bio materials. These large, complex data sets can be effectively handled, analyzed, and interpreted by big-data analytics, offering investigators a window into the seemingly chaotic data.

Every big data analysis is built on the foundation of ML algorithms to compute scalable datasets and provide accurate estimations of biomaterial characteristics. These include simple regression models as well as complex deep learning networks that are able to handle multidimensional data sets of biomaterials. From the previous datasets, the results of ML include usage life, cytocompatibility, and mechanical properties of the materials. Besides greatly decreasing the time needed for experimental verification, this predictive ability also minimizes the costs of material characterization, which is crucial in the initial stages of biomaterials design.

This was even beyond the range of the quite conventional process of property prediction for expected properties of new material types, encompassing the discovery of completely new biomaterials with specific desired characteristics. Using modern tools of data analysis, including clustering and pattern recognition, it was possible to categorize materials and forecast their properties including their behavior under certain conditions. By leveraging previous results from experiments and simulations, researchers can make assumptions about how newer materials will perform in tests and determine which strategy to incorporate next during the design process. This rational approach increases the chances of developing biomaterials that fit the purpose of their use in areas like tissue engineering, drug delivery, and regenerative medicine.

Big data analytics has many potential applications in biomaterials design, but before those applications can be fully realized, a number of issues need to be resolved. Data heterogeneity is one of the main issues, as biomaterial data are frequently gathered from a variety of sources such as computer models, clinical trials, and experimental results. Sophisticated data preparation techniques are needed to integrate these heterogeneous datasets into a coherent analytical framework.
