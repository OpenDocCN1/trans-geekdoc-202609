As ML technologies continue to evolve and integrate into biomaterials research, addressing ethical considerations and ensuring responsible use become increasingly important. The deployment of ML models must consider issues such as data privacy, model transparency, and the potential for bias in predictions. Setting proper ethical standards and regulatory mechanisms becomes crucial for guiding the usage of ML solutions in the biomaterials industry and ensuring that the solutions meet ultimate goals and align with societal preferences. These include the nondisclosure of data, ensuring that models contain no bias against individuals, and maintaining transparency in the process through which predictions of individual behavior are generated and used. Appropriate use of ML technologies not only contributes to the scientific development of the biomaterials field but also helps overcome skepticism and nonacceptance factors among stakeholders.

# 2 Data-driven approaches in biomaterials design

# 2.1 High-throughput data collection for biomaterials

Large-scale data acquisition has therefore been established as a critical factor in the design and synthesis of biomaterials, especially when involving ML techniques. This highlights the importance of broad-ranging datasets because the range of biophysical conditions and the interaction of material properties are diverse. These databases form the basis of a number of ML algorithms used to forecast material performance, determine optimal characteristics, and discover new biomaterials with improved functions. The problem raises the question of how to collect high-throughput data accurately and comprehensively over time, which, in turn, necessitates a cross-disciplinary approach, as well as technologies and methods. Specific issues that need to be resolved to achieve maximum advantage from high-throughput data include data quality, handling of information, and dealing with variability in testing conditions during experimentation in biomaterials design.

Current advancements in technology make flow cytometry a comparatively fast and accurate tool for accomplishing high-throughput data collection in biomaterial sciences. Instrumental approaches, including robotic systems and microfluidics, allow for conducting rapid and parallel experiments and examining multiple properties of the material at once under different conditions. Such technologies can help in the creation of large data sets, which are generated with minimal human input, low probabilities of error, and high-test reproducibility. Furthermore, the synergistic use of enhanced imaging systems and sensing tools enables the observation of material property shifts simultaneously, across a range from the molecular to the macroscopic level. These innovations not only improve the quality of the collected data but also extend the range of analyzed parameters, providing more information on biomaterial behavior.
