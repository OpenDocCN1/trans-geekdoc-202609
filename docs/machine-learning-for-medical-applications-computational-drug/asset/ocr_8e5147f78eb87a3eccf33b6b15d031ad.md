This chapter discusses several machine learning technologies employed in medical image processing, highlights the milestones that have been achieved, and addresses the challenges that have been encountered [6, 7].

An important development in the field of medical image analysis has been the CNN [8]. Recognition and identification of images have been transformed concerning activities associated with object identification and image classification because of their capacity to recognize hierarchical structures in pictures automatically [9]. It has been stated that CNNs can also acquire high rates of success in patterns and anomaly detection of medical images, which improve diagnosis [10, 11]. The components of CNNs, including their multilayer structure, activation functions, and training methods, are discussed in this segment of the chapter [12]. It also depicts the versatility and usefulness of CNNs by elaborating on their use in different medical imaging environments, radiology and pathology among them [13].

SVMs present a further technique for using anomaly detection, in addition to picture categorization [14]. SVMs are very good at handling high-dimensional data and in segregating data that contain complicated patterns [15]. This part of the chapter was devoted to the analysis of the theoretical foundations of SVMs, with emphasis on the concepts of hyperplane and kernel function [16]. It also examines how SVMs are used for medical imaging activities such as tissue segmentation and tumor detection [17]. The general advantages and limitations of SVMs in comparison with other machine learning algorithms employed in medical image analysis are provided and stated to present an objective view of the applicability of the mentioned techniques [18].

The use of medical image analysis has progressed even further with the use of deep learning architectures since it consists of several neural network models [19]. These models are particularly useful in detecting details and relating closely interwoven sociohistorical layers within picture data due to their numerous layers and huge parameters [20]. Several advanced techniques in the deep learning field, like recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs) used for enhancing picture quality and diagnostic proficiency, are also discussed in this section [21]. Case examples are given to demonstrate that deep learning methods have enhanced early illness diagnoses, treatment planning, and other areas of medical imaging [22].

There are a number of challenges that need to be addressed to apply machine learning in medical images efficiently: challenges related to the large sizes of data, challenges needed to achieve stability in the algorithms, and also challenges in terms of the ethics of machine learning usage [23]. Realizing that the challenges caused by processing constraints can contribute to the lack of algorithm transparency and pooled data, the last part of the introduction describes ongoing research [24].
