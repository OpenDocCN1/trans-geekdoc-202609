ML has started picking up as an important tool in material science because of the opportunity it offers in speeding up the search for, as well as the fine-tuning of, materials. In biomedical applications, it means the identification of materials that are safe for the human body, the modeling of those materials and their behavior in biological milieu, and the fine-tuning of materials to perform for specific medical therapies. Implicit in material design and discovery, ML has decreased the dependence on the conventional costly and time-consuming experimental processes in anticipation of the outcomes of material reactions with biological systems.

This has boosted the growth of ML in material science due to the availability of large-scale, high-dimensional data sets involving data from proteomics, genomics, and other biological fields. These data sets provide a vast collection of data for the training of ML models, enabling accurate prediction-making, and nurturing creativity in the creation of these materials. Advancements in the capability of using ML to analyze and assess large data sets have opened up new ways through which novel materials with tailored properties are synthesized, enhancing precision and efficiency in the biomedical field.

# 2.3 Key differences between traditional and ML-driven approaches

The more conventional strategies for designing biomedical materials rely on the use of experience and experiments. These conventional methods depend on testing and adjusting various properties of the material through successive trials and errors. It was quite tiresome, and many resources were consumed as well as time taken in trying to establish appropriate materials. The process generally follows a linear progression: although it was possible to make predictions in design, innovation occurs in an iterative process of conceptualization, synthesis, testing, and evaluation, with comparatively low levels of foresight.

As opposed to this, approaches under ML-IfA utilize superior computational procedures to optimize and expedite material creation. In contrast to conventional techniques, the ML-aided approaches employ mathematical models to forecast characteristics of the fabric and its response without actual experimentation. There is a wide range of data used to train these models, which encompasses data regarding molecular structure, biological activities, and noted experimental results. As data and correlations between these patterns are understood by the ML algorithms used, it is possible to accurately and efficiently estimate the performance of the new material.

Handling and analysis of data are one of the key differentiators between ML-driven techniques and traditional techniques. While traditional approaches are likely to be severely limited by small datasets and manual analysis, the ability to recognize refined interconnections and enhance material properties also suffers.
