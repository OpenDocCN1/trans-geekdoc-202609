## 5.4 Ethical considerations and regulatory implications in personalized drug delivery

There are important ethical and regulatory concerns when customized medicine is introduced in the distribution of medicine. To prevent possible unethical and bias use of the customized medication delivery systems, these issues should be tackled as modern technologies like ML advance individual treatment methods.

Pritchard, Hanlon, and Karles reside with the fact that data security and patient's privacy are the leading ethical concerns when it comes to individualized medicine delivery. Database management remains a critical requirement of using ML algorithms, as it requires accumulation of the genomic, proteomic, and clinical data of patients who are to be treated. Advanced data protection has to be well implemented because misuse and unwanted access has to be prevented. It is important to maintain patient privacy and cover all the necessary aspects before using patient's data. Thus, there is a task to form ethical rules and detailed information protection protocols so as not to violate patient rights and lose people's trust.

Equality and access to individualized treatment raise critical ethical concerns in the era of AI-driven healthcare. While smart technologies offer the potential for highly targeted and effective treatments, they may also unintentionally exacerbate disparities in medication access due to differences in socioeconomic status, healthcare infrastructure, and geographic location. Advanced therapies must be made equitably available, regardless of a patient's background. Achieving this goal requires the implementation of policies and practices that promote fair access to personalized care, ensuring that no individual is excluded. This involves addressing biases in AI algorithms, recognizing how underrepresentation in datasets affects predictive accuracy, and adopting strategies to increase minority inclusion in clinical trials and research efforts. Figure 10 highlights these disparities and outlines strategies for ethical and inclusive implementation of AI-based treatment systems.

In ethical practice, individualized medicine delivery systems based on machine learning need to be easily explained. Patients and clinicians require knowledge of how machine learning algorithms come up with some of the recommendations and the decisions that are made. This requires design of understandable algorithms as well as communication of the basic principles and limitations of these systems. This is important so that healthcare professionals who are using the tools developed by ML can understand how to use them and have been appropriately trained. This enables them to engage the patients effectively and develop sound clinical decisions.

This has a regulatory implication, which means that delivery of personal medication has to meet a validation and approval procedure that is very rigorous. Defining authorities have to adapt to it because the environment of customized medicine and ever progressing machine learning configuration is already rapidly evolving. Among them is the development of standards concerning verification and standardization of machine learning algorithms that are used in medication administration systems. From the standpoint of purity of results, of patient safety, and of efficacy, it is crucial that these models are precise, consistent, and reproducible. Also, one can no longer presume that the ML models are fixed parameters that work to a certain set of rules or parameters, and never adjust. Standardizing future practices on monitoring, verifying, and updating of the frequently used ML algorithms become imperative for practicality and safety.
