# Genomics: Exploring the concept of gene mapping

Some of the basic understanding of the variation and patterning of genes can be acquired from genomes, which is the means of studying the whole DNA of an organism and all of the genes it contains. Earlier, these differences could not be analyzed comprehensively, but due to advanced high-throughput sequencing technologies, SNP, CNVs, and structural variations can now be studied. This genomic data is helpful in the determination of disease susceptibility, launching of treatment plans, and tailored treatment for individuals. Consequently, decisions on therapy targeting the cancer can be made, once the mutations responsible for tumor development in cancer patients are evaluated through genomic profiling.

# Proteomics: Protein function and expression

Proteomics refers to the comprehensive study of proteins, including their structures, interactions, functions, and conformational dynamics. Unlike genomics, which reveals genetic potential, proteomics provides direct insight into cellular processes by analyzing actual protein activity within biological systems. As shown in Figure 5, key areas of focus in proteomics include post-translational modifications, protein–protein interactions, and expression levels, often studied using advanced techniques such as mass spectrometry and two-dimensional gel electrophoresis. By investigating how protein regulation changes during normal and pathological states, researchers can identify early disease markers, discover novel therapeutic targets, and develop clinically relevant biomarkers particularly for diseases such as cancer and cardiovascular conditions.
