# 2.1 Origins of personalized medicine

The concept of pharmacogenomics that brought some ideas of individualized medicine has early roots in the notion of splitting medicine usage according to an individual’s genetic data. The field of pharmacogenomics, which emerged in the middle of the twentieth century aimed to understand how genes explain how drugs and other agents operate in the body. This means that by addressing the observation in patients that pharmacological response both in terms of effectiveness and safety varies, this strategy intends to show the way for more precise and effective treatment.

The major scientific advancement that set the ball rolling on the concept of personalized medicine was the discovery of the double helix DNA by Watson and Crick in 1953. The advancement means the scientific world now has a clearer idea of the means by which the genetic code holds sway over health and illness. The FAIR act was a basis for the Human Genome Project, which began in 1990 and came to completion in 2003. As a project that strived to map the whole human genome the initiative sought to identify and sequence the 20,000 to 25,000 genes that define man’s biology. The extreme success of the Human Genome Project gave a new plot to genetics and created many chances for individualized therapy.

Genomics initiated in the early 2000s lead to identification of several genetic variations associated with different diseases and patient responses to treatment. The knowledge of specific indicators that determine the rate of a given person’s susceptibility to specific diseases and reaction to various medications also saw major developments during this period. P4 Medicine adopted a principle that was popularized by pharmacogenomics – right drug, right dose, right patient.

Over this period, pharmacogenomics evolved to embrace and accommodate other influences that have an impact on human health in addition to genetic influences such as lifestyle and environmental factors. Management and implementation of these variables to tactics in the strategy for individualized treatment marked a shift to better understanding of patient-centered care. It also revealed the fact that the dependence of various diseases on hereditary factors should not rule out the other necessary environmental factors.
