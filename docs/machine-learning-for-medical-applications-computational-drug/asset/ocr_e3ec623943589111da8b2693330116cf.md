# 4.5.4 Therapeutic wearables

The devices are designed to have therapeutic value in that they can be used to track signals. Some examples of biocompatible materials include the following: transdermal drug delivery systems and neurostimulation units. For instance, transdermal systems in the management of chronic conditions involve containing the medicine in patches that integrate biocompatible polymers and hydrogels for the predictable, controlled release of the drug. In the same manner, wearable neurostimulation devices use biosensors to supply electrical current to the body for relief from chronic pain, enhancing the patient’s experience through nonsurgical interventions.

# 4.5.5 Wearable sensors for chronic disease management

Wearable devices are important in maintaining supervision and providing constant readouts for the management of chronic ailments. Smart biocompatible materials are applied in monitoring diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and asthma, among other conditions. For instance, biosensors used in continuous glucose monitoring are safe and implanted to measure glucose in interstitial fluid, helping those with these conditions manage them better. Through the information collected by these devices, treatment regimens are modified, and potential adverse effects are avoided, making life for patients suffering from chronic ailments far more comfortable.

The research has demonstrated the use of advanced biocompatible materials for wearables, which have transformed personalized health care through continuous monitoring, comfortable fit, and innovative treatments. As technology improves, the use of these materials continues to expand opportunities to advance wearable health devices, enhancing preventive patient care.

# 5 Factors influencing biocompatibility

# 5.1 Chemical composition and its impact on biocompatibility

The biological performance of biocompatible materials and their degree of biocompatibility depend mainly on their chemical nature. Three major aspects focus on the ability to achieve optimal biological performance of a material designed for medical use: formulation chemistry, the minimization of side effects through design aimed at optimizing therapeutic results of the intended material, and the design of a material that would not adversely affect existing treatments.
