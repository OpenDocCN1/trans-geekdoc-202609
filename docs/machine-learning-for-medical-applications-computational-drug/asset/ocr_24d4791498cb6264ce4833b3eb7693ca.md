# 6.4.4 Beauty and customization

On patients’ acceptance and compliance, the looks of products matter. Sensors that patients find unattractive are not likely to be worn regularly. Acceptance of sensors can be improved by the advancement of sensors that are more complementary to the clothing. Key elements for self-customization, including designs and colors, enables patients to choose devices that suit them best in relation their preferences, thus enhancing wearability.

# 6.4.5 Effects of psychological and patient involvement

The discussed psychological effect of biomedical sensor affects the compliance of the patient. This is mainly because there are devices that are designed to measure health indicators over some period continually. Constant evaluation can cause anxiety in the patient, especially if the patient feels he/she is at risk of developing some health condition. To this end, the promotion of the virtues of constant surveillance and the assistive nature of sensors in healthcare is likely to increase patient activation. Information concerning how the data from the sensors is handled, and the extent to which patients feel in control of their health would remove these concerns and improve compliance.

# 6.4.6 Barriers to wearability

Possible factors that limit the wearability of biomedical sensors include social economic status, cultural endowment, and health literacy. Some types of sensors are costly, making them difficult to acquire, and may, therefore, hamper proper usage and usability. The use of cost-efficient solutions that are also easily paid for through insurance is also core to the affordability objectives to make their use wide spread. Also, culturally appropriate designs of products and services as well as educational programmers can help patients overcome health literacy learnt barriers to use.
