# 4.5.1 Multiplexed multiple sensing

Hybrid sensor systems integrate two sensing modalities, which include optical, chemical, or mechanical sensors, in a single system. It also means that there can be measurement of more than one parameter at a given time, which makes for a proper assessment of a patient. For instance, a single sensor combines electrochemical and optical principles and can measure glucose and blood oxygen saturation, which can be of definite use for diabetics.

# Increased efficiency, data accuracy, and reliability

Hybrid systems are developed with the use of multiple sensing technologies and helps deliver more accurate and reliable results in terms of health. One modality offsets the weaknesses of the other to eliminate areas of vulnerability and improve the capabilities of each sensor. For example, integrating optical and mechanical sensors would ensure that wherever there are problems like motion artifacts where wearable devices are used, there be better approaches to data acquisition.

# Pages 49: The emergence of instruments for multimodal health monitoring

Wireless vital signs are novel health monitoring gadgets that are created to have multiple parameters that are sensed by several sensors. These devices can track the number of beats per minute, the number of breaths per minute, activity, and even temperature, all at once. Illustrations include state-of-the-art wearable devices that incorporate accelerometers, gyroscopes, and optoelectronic sensors to obtain detailed outlines of the physical activity as well as physiological indices. Such multimodal systems can allow for constant and highly granular tracking of health and are especially useful when it comes to controlling various chronic diseases and providing treatment plans.

# 4.5.2 Patient compliance and comfort can be easily enhanced

Combining several sensors into one monitoring device also improves patient compliance and comfort. Multimodal sensors, with integrated multiple sensors, are typically developed to be small in size, lighter in weight, and less invasive to patients, compared with the use of multiple devices. For example, a single wearable device that has both the heart rate monitor and activity tracker replaces another device and thus enhances the overall compliance of patients to adhere to monitoring, improving user experience.
