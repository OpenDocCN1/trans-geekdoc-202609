# 6.2.3 Integration with biological tissues

The successful coupling of a biocompatible material and a biological tissue requires the ability of the identifiable material to not only accomplish a particular function but also elicit appropriate responses from the surrounding biological environment. This integration is especially difficult in functional applications such as tissue engineering of materials that need to support cell proliferation and tissue regeneration as well as serve as the scaffold system. Any materials that are used must support cell attachment as well as cell growth and division, in a way that minimizes inflammation and rejection.

# 6.2.4 Durability vs. biodegradability

The biodegradable materials by design had one key problem regarding the rate of degradation and the functional performance, which could not always be attained. Whereas biodegradation was desirable for short-term implants and carrier systems, a precise rate of degradation was required for the carrier material to reflect the intended durability and performance of the implant. If degradation occurred at high rates, the intended application would fail prematurely and be unable to function properly. On the other hand, if the degradation rate was very low, the long-term effects would be detrimental, requiring the component's removal. Ensuring that it degraded at an optimum rate to meet its intended function was the key challenge.

# 6.2.5 Regulatory and safety considerations

In addition to the functional requirements a biocompatible material must meet, there are compliance issues that call for the prevalent two-dimensionality, whereby an applicant has to prove both biocompatibility and functionality, particularly as the broader society demands that material testing be safe and practical. The regulatory bodies set certain rules to ensure that the material used possesses the required safety features while simultaneously demonstrating efficacy. Meeting these regulations as well as striving for better material functionality is not always easy and involves planning throughout the entire product development cycle.
