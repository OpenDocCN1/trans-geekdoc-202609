Provision of insurance cover and reimbursement policies are key determinants of the extent to which biomedical devices are available to the public. Some of the popular and the most recent sensors and devices, which are still regarded as hi-tech, are not included in insurance. Another factor is reimbursement rate; this is a factor that distinguishes one region provider from another and which impacts the cost of these technologies to the patients negatively. Restriction of insurance frequently leads to the situation when the patient has to pay for the services personally; this, in turn, plays a role in discouraging the patient from using advanced devices and, therefore, not reap the related advantages.

Extensive variations in the geographical distribution of healthcare facilities influence the availability of biomedical sensors and devices. While in some areas, factors such as institutional, human, and technical resources remain a significant constraint in the coverage and implementation of advancement technologies, especially to populations in developing nations. A small percentage of individuals in those regions do not get the same benefit of advanced diagnostic and therapeutic technology due to the lack of access to current sophisticated devices. These geographic disparities mean that specific efforts must be made to begin building better healthcare systems and to extend the technological options available.

In the deployment of advanced biomedical sensors and devices, healthcare givers benefit from competency in their profession. Due to the developmental nature of these devices, together with the appropriate interpretation and handling of data, clinicians must continue learning and receive backup. Education and information services are critical to making sure that practitioners can utilize the implemented technologies successfully. In return, due to the costs and difficulties involved in arriving at a level of complete training and support, these devices are not yet popular.
