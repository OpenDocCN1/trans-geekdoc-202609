## 6 Integrating AI with experimental methods in preclinical testing

### 6.1 AI-driven analysis of high-throughput screening data

HTS assays furnish a massive volume of information on compound activities against many targets, for offering unique prospects into the relationships of chemical compounds to biological systems. HTS data are big and often complex and pose problems for analysis and interpretation. The availability of low-cost, high-throughput screening data is a significant advantage of AI-oriented approaches, which seize valuable information from HTS data, select potential lead compounds, and prioritize compounds for further in vitro studies.

The application of developing classification models to identify hit chemical structures with desired biological activity is one of the foremost uses of AI organized for HTS data analysis. For instance, some of the machine learning algorithm models that can be trained on the HTS datasets to classify substances as active, based on their structural and activity profile, include but are not limited to: support vector machines (SVMs), random forests, and deep neural networks. These algorithms can work faster than lead identification because while the activity of new compounds is unknown, algorithms can make this prediction and prioritize hits based on the further validation and the labeled data.

The identities of SARs and pharmacophore characteristics that initiate chemical actions are unveiled with the help of AI analysis of the outcomes of HTS operations. AI approaches can also define the molecular nature of a hit compound’s potency, selectivity, as well as efficacy based on chemical structures of hit compounds and the activity map corresponding to them. The “rational design and development of lead compounds with better pharmacological profile” and therapeutic utilization of compounds are based on understanding SARs at molecular levels.

These two complementary analyses of HTS data increase the understanding of the compounds’ activities and biological effects to make better selections of lead compounds with favorable pharmacological profiles.

### 6.2 Predictive modeling for toxicity assessment

Toxicogenomics is a new and valuable tool in preclinical testing; predictive modeling of toxicity assessment helps to determine possible adverse effects of drug candidates at the initial stages of the drug development process. These models employ AI methods to parse structural arrangements, biological information, and chemical characteristics to predict and rank potential compounds to possess benign toxicology to facilitate their subsequent examination.

Chemical structure- and physicochemical property-based toxicity prediction represents a vital application of predictive modeling in modern toxicity assessment. To decode the complex relationships between chemical descriptors and toxicity endpoints, various machine learning algorithms including Random Forests, Support Vector Machines (SVMs), and Neural Networks are applied to datasets comprising compounds with known toxicological profiles. These models enable the prediction of toxic effects, such as hepatotoxicity and cardiotoxicity, based on features like molecular structure, key molecular fingerprints, and other physicochemical properties. As illustrated in Figure 14, such predictive models are instrumental in identifying potentially hazardous compounds early in the drug development pipeline.

The accuracy and robustness of toxicity predictions and the toxicity assessment by predictive modeling enable the incorporation of many types of data such as animal, clinical, and in vitro toxicological data. The algorithms can accumulate information from different sources and, depending on them, reveal certain risks that can be left unnoticed if an individual dataset was analyzed. This integrative approach to toxicity assessment improves the assurance of the compound safety profile; it advances the decision-making process for drug development.
