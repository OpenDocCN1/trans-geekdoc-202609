# 3.2 Security challenges in traditional EHR systems

To address requirements including the GDPR and HIPAA, of safeguarding healthcare-related information is required. Essential measures required for preserving patient health information are covered by regulations and include data encryption and limitation of access to these data. Failure to meet these legal obligations can attract heavy penalties, legal problems, and loss of reputation to the healthcare organizations.

In healthcare data management, to protect patient's identity and build and sustain patient confidence, and to meet regulatory requirements, both security and privacy are crucial. If the patient data were to be adequately protected, healthcare entities need to enforce security and privacy mechanisms. By ensuring protection of privacy during data handling processes, firms can reduce the threat of data violations concerning the patients’ confidentiality.

Centralization: The conventional EHR systems rely on centralized databases resident on separate servers of the third-party vendors of the implementing healthcare facilities. Since most of the patient's information was stored in one point where it was easy to share and retrieve the information, the system was extremely prone to hacking, unlawful access, and data corruption. Many patients’ electronic health records are at risk; just one successful attack on a central EHR system can enable fraudulent activity, identity thefts, and other vices.

Insider threats: This threat arises from the fact that when workers, vendors, and contractors have access to personal patient information, they are a risk to healthcare businesses. There are inherent dangers in the different types of insider threats, both accidental and of the palindromic kind, in which the internal attacker aims to simply steal and corrupt patient data for personal reasons. Standard EHR structures are often deficient in strong auditing, and inside threats are often hard to detect and prevent.
