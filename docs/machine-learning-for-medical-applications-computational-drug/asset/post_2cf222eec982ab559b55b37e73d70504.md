## 6.2 Transfer learning and domain adaptation

Transfer learning also addresses problems related to low sample sizes and other related issues that stem from the irregular distribution of data. Thanks to these methods, models can transfer information from one task to another and therefore facilitate performance on similar but different tasks. Figure 22 shows the process of transfer learning and the adaptation of the gained knowledge to another but related domain. Crucially, this feature is most valuable in areas such as predicting medication toxicity, where large, labeled datasets are scarce, and models need to be robust across domains.

Transfer learning is the act of enhancing a model that works on a new but related task by using a model that was developed on a large and, in many cases, different data set. This method uses features or similarities between the source task and the new task that one wants to perform. In practice, transfer learning often refers to changes in a model's architecture for a specific assigned task. Using prior knowledge reduces the need for extensive training on new data each time, which is achieved in this way.

### Feature Extraction
The method identified in this paper uses a pre-trained model for feature extraction. As for the new job, the higher levels of the model are changed, whereas the lower ones, more basic in their functionality, are retained. Some of the characteristics were then extracted from the pre-trained model and input into a new model developed to achieve the intended job.

### Fine-tuning
It involves fine-tuning a model with certain weights adjusted to serve another purpose apart from the one it was trained for. Typically, the last layers of the model are trained on the target data, while the earlier layers of the model are locked to reutilize the domain invariances learned from the source domain data. Likewise referred to as fine-tuning, it allows the model to adapt the learned representations to the specific details of the new job.

It used to be called “domain-specific pre-training” when a model was pre-trained on a domain similar to the target one. A model learned on chemical compounds, for instance, can be further trained using data on the toxicity of drugs. This intermediate pre-training was important because it introduces features that, depending on the target dataset, need to be fine-tuned.

Domain adaptation is concerned with the process of transferring learned information from one source domain to another related but distinct domain. To address the shift between the source and target domains, it involves modifying either the model or its parameters. Thus, the objectives in the present work are focused on enhancing the model's ability in the target domain and reducing the domain shift.

### Domain-Invariant Feature Learning
This approach was employed with the view of learning features that are invariant to the given source and target domains. The so-called domain adversarial training promotes the model to seek representations that reduce the differences between domains to make the learned features more transferable.

### Instance Weighting
Example-based schema adaptation includes the modification of weights of the instances from the source domain during the training process according to their similarity with the target domain. This technique is beneficial in terms of correcting the deceptive focus of the main model toward the parameters of the target domain.

### Data Augmentation
To address the domain shift of the target domain, splintered data augmentation procedures produce additional samples. Due to the ability to change the values with completely synthetic data, these methods can fill the gap between the source and the target domain and lead to performance enhancement.

### Model Regularization
Auxiliary functions applied to strengthen the recovery regularizations are utilized to impose constraints that ensure the model performs well across domains. It is easy to lose performance when transitioning between domains, but methods like domain-specific regularization assist with this.

Transfer learning and domain adaptation offer significant advantages in drug toxicity prediction:

### Leveraging Pre-Trained Models
As we explained before, transfer learning can help utilize models trained on chemical datasets and then fine-tune them to perform specific toxicity prediction tasks with limited data. This approach shortens the time required to develop models and increases the accuracy of predicting outcomes.

### Handling Data Scarcity
A similar approach, with models trained on related but more abundant data sources, can be utilized in situations where annotated toxicity data is scarce by means of domain adaptation. This method enriches the generalization potential and the ability to provide highly accurate predictions regarding new therapeutic opportunities for the model.

### Cross-Population and Cross-Study Adaptation
Domain adaptation and transfer learning allow the use of models in different samples and under different study conditions. This is important to ensure that toxicity forecasts are accurate and generalizable to various patients and experiments.

### Domain Discrepancies
A major concern remains how to handle the wide disparities that exist across the domain differences between the source and target. Promising approaches for mitigating domain shift and improving model transferability are still evolving.

### Model Interpretability
It is important to ensure that when using transfer learning and/or domain adaptation, the interpretability of the models does not suffer but is even enhanced, as is desirable in practical scenarios such as drug safety.

### Scalability and Efficiency
The key was to build frameworks for transfer learning that were effective and adaptable for mass use in learning from large amounts of data.

Transfer learning and domain adaptation are really useful and important in attempting to improve machine learning models for the prediction of drug toxicity, as well as in other areas of study. These methods are, therefore, useful tools for handling the limitations of data by transferring knowledge and prior insights from related contexts and tasks.

## 6.3 Explainable AI and model interpretability

When developing and utilizing machine learning models, XAI and model interpretability were vital, especially in critical domains such as medication toxicity prediction. Given both the practical complexities of machine learning models and the ethical concerns, it was increasingly important to understand how the models make decisions and to ensure the forecasts are not only accurate but also understandable.

Despite the higher generalization ability obtained through deep neural networks and other complex models, the relationships the models learn are complex themselves and operationalize the machine learning model into a “black box” that contains very intricate internal mechanisms. It is crucial to build trust in these models by providing the reasons behind certain decisions made by the model, especially in areas such as adverse drug response prediction, where decisions have a significant impact.
