# 2.1 Linear models

Linear models represent a fundamental concept used in supervised learning, often applied to the determination of risks and side effects of drugs. These models are developed based on the notion of achieving a direct relationship between the predictor variables and the target outcome. Linear models are relatively easy to interpret in the interest of drug safety and are thus well suited for the described setting.

Linear regression strives to find the relationship between a continuous dependent variable and one independent variable by estimating through the use of a fitted line formula derived from the observed data. In the case of quantifying the toxicity levels of drugs based on their chemical and biological characteristics, linear regression can be utilized.
