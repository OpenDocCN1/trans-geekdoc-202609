# 4.5.3 Advantages and disadvantages in applying Bayesian method

Together with offering robust tools for quantifying uncertainty and decision-making, Bayesian techniques present some specific challenges as well. There are two drawbacks here: one is the implementation complexity of Bayesian techniques, which remains a major headache in the current early stages of development when computation time is impacted by complicated models. Techniques such as the MCMC can be both resource-intensive and time-consuming when carrying out sampling from the posterior. Additionally, if we recall that it has a significant impact on the results, the selection of correct prior distributions is critical. Specifying priors requires careful reflection to ensure that the selected priors properly characterize the current knowledge, which forms the basis of learning, yet allows for the incorporation of new data. Nevertheless, Bayesian approaches are being applied with increasing ease and relevance to material science problems due to the development of processing power and improved algorithms.

The future publication of Bayesian methods for biomedical-related materials would possibly entail the application of other algorithms within the bandwidth of machine learning techniques and the advancement of new computational approaches for the determination of uncertainties. For instance, integrating Bayesian methods with DL approaches could help develop models for material property prediction under risks and uncertainties. In this respect, recent developments in approximate Bayesian computation (ABC) and variational inference provide promising new avenues for addressing the computational issues tied to the use of Bayesian techniques when working with large-scale material datasets. Enhancements in these techniques mean that uncertainty can be well measured, and decisions can be made effectively in material science, thus opening up new possibilities in biomedical material design and application.
