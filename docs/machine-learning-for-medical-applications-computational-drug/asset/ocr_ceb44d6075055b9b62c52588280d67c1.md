# 3.3 Applications of deep learning in biomedical materials

In the field of biomedical materials, deep learning, a branch of ML characterized by the use of multilayered neural networks, has shown revolutionary potential. In particular, it is perfectly suitable for a range of tasks within this domain because it allows for the fine representation of relationships and identification of nonlinear dependencies based on large datasets.

Therefore, using predictive modeling of material properties means that the information provided was extracted from the data accumulated in the course of global communication.

CNN-based deep learning algorithms have been successfully applied to predict the properties of biomedical materials. By training on large datasets that encompass a wide range of material characteristics and performance outcomes, these models are capable of accurately forecasting mechanical strength, thermal conductivity, biocompatibility, and other critical attributes. This predictive power significantly reduces experimental costs and time, allowing researchers to focus their efforts and resources on the most promising candidates. Moreover, it supports more informed decision-making early in the materials development pipeline, improving the efficiency of R&D processes. # 3.3.1 Design of drug delivery systems

Drug delivery systems have evolved and improved using deep learning. Specific product systems and different time sequences are processed using RNNs and transformers so that materials can be developed that are capable of releasing therapeutic agents in a highly targeted manner. They can predict the kinetics of drug liberation through different materials to enhance formulations for particular therapeutic objectives as well as enhance the quality of patient care.
