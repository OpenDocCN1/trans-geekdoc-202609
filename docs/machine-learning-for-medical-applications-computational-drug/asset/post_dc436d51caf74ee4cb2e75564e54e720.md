Temperature measurement is one of the first features of the HM process to assess the ability of the body to regulate temperature and to detect fever. Current temperature sensors are incorporated into wearable, smartwatch bands and devices, and connected to remote controllers and thermometers. This type of sensor provides continual temperature readings, which enable users to identify febrile diseases and all other diseases that relate to temperature. New technologies in temperature measurement also help combine the temperature readings with other parameters of a patient state, which is valuable.

Multiparameter monitoring systems utilize information from various invasive/non-invasive vital signs sensors to give the overall picture of a patient. These systems integrate the readings of pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and temperature into one platform for easy health evaluation and monitoring of trends. Multiple parameter devices are frequently employed in patient care and in telemonitoring applications for those who have a chronic illness. These systems improve the accuracy of the assessments of the health status further by integrating data from the different sensors.

## 3.2 Chronic disease management

COPD stands for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, which is one of the chronic diseases that requires the active monitoring of a patient and in offering regular treatment. On analyzing the role of management of chronic diseases, the researcher noted that it impacts patient's QOL, risk of complications, and healthcare spend. Biomedical sensing and, particularly, the technological developments of the related portable devices have greatly improved the diagnosis, follow up, and management of chronic diseases with more precision, and in real-time.

Another area that has seen continuous monitoring technologies being important is in the management of diabetes. CGM systems provide constant monitoring and tracking of blood glucose levels and provide the patient's real-time information of their glucose levels. These systems involve a tiny strip of glucose sensor that is placed under the patient's skin to record the glucose level in the interstitial fluid; the information is relayed to an electronic monitoring mobile application. The World Health Organization’s comprehensive framework for chronic disease management, which involves: a) Patient data, b) Treatment plans, and c) intimately connected real-time monitoring to deliver the best results is presented in Figure 7. The combination of CGM with insulin pumps has allowed for closed-looped insulin delivery systems, which adjust the dose of the insulin intake, depending on the level of glucose, hence improving glycemic control and minimizing the use of fingerstick tests. In glucose control, these technologies are useful due to the adjustment that allows for timely appropriate changes and minimization of both hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia incidences.

Cardiac care management is of high importance to maintain control over health conditions like hypertension, heart failure, and arrhythmias. Wearable ECG and pocket blood pressure measurement equipment give continuous details on the performance of the heart and blood pressure to recognize early anomalies. Trans-telemer IT and implantable cardiac monitors are used to monitor abnormal rhythms and different cardiac problems, including arrhythmia, which need intervention. These devices help to control cardiovascular illnesses by offering necessary information needed in the alteration of medications and changes in habits to prevent worsening of situations and adverse effects on patients.

Neurological disorders like epilepsy, Parkinson, and multiple sclerosis are handled with improvements to the monitoring technologies. Smart clothes and integrated circuitry deal with motor coordination, seizures, and other signs occurring in neurological disorders. For instance, wrist-worn accelerometers can get data on tremors in Parkinson’s disease patients, which is beneficial to update the therapy. In the same way, seizure detection gadgets can provide caregiving informants and other medical decision makers with an insight into the deterioration, resulting in the need for a change of medication dosage. These technologies improve the care of patients with neurological disorders by offering real-time data on the management of the diseases for better quality patient outcomes.

Respiratory healthcare management entails managing diseases that include asthma, COPD, and sleep apnea. Monitoring devices are portable spirometers and peak flow meters that enable patients to monitor their lung function and symptoms, simultaneously. In the case of sleep apnea, CPAP devices with tracking capabilities offer information of the apnea episodes and the effects of therapy. Further, wearable sensors are primarily designed to continuously assess respiratory rate and oxygen saturation levels to help treat, assess the efficiency of the interventions, and inform patients and therapists about emergent complications. These monitoring tools enhance the effective management of respiratory ailments through providing nurses with tools to intervene appropriately and fashion out individualized patient care procedures.

Serum creatinine and electrolyte levels are paramount in CKD patients with dialysis. Smart and bio-accepted devices like gems and chips can monitor some aspects such as blood pressure, fluid, and electrolyte ratios which are significantly important to renal health. Dialysis machines that support intricate monitoring systems guarantee appropriate treatment by constantly analyzing and drawing parameters from real time. These technologies are useful in taking care of chronic kidney disorders by facilitating adjustments of the patients’ treatment regimes, assessment of complications, and general patient care.

## 3.3 Preventive and predictive health

Preventive and predictive health categories of technology minimize the chances of somebody coming down with a particular health issue before the situation becomes hopeless. These technologies, which involve using imageries and data acquisition devices, sensors, data collection, and analytics, and backed up by AI computing power, provide solutions for effective health management, early disease identification, and proactive health management. The incorporation of these tools into healthcare can in fact be observed as having the ability to alter the course of ailments and diseases that would otherwise constantly plague humanity.

Diseases diagnosis has a massive impact on successful outcomes of a treatment and on the overall percentage of people’s life, immediately after being affected by various health conditions. One of the main functions of the diagnostic sensors and imaging equipment is to detect diseases in their infancy. For instance, wearable electronic devices that record physical signs include heart rates blood pressure discrepancies that indicate additional problems. Likewise, screening devices that involve biomarkers and genetic information can always point out people with high risks of acquiring diseases like cancer and cardiovascular conditions. Early detection helps our bodies, lives, and overall health situation to improve through early intervention, and hence better prognosis.

The data collected from wearable sensors, EHR, and genomics give an understanding of how susceptible an individual is to develop particular diseases known as risk assessment and prediction models. Statistical analysis and prognosis tools are used to identify patterns and connection between data to give a customized risk profile. In Figure 8, there is a depiction of how preventive and predictive health strategies operate together, with the aim of improving the probabilities of early diagnosis- and patient-orientated intercession for superior patient outcomes. For example, algorithms can estimate one’s probability of becoming a diabetic, depending on the height, exercise regimen, and genes. These models can help choose the methods for prevention, according to the risks, and can include everything from changing a patient’s habits to prescribing certain medications.

Smart gear gadgets are those that are developed to track the number of steps taken, the amount of sleep obtained, and other factors that define health fitness. Information like how many steps a person has taken, his/her heart rate, and the quality of the sleep each day give a lot of information about a person’s health. The information is frequently used to deduce specific recommendations for the subject regarding physical exercise, sleep quality, and overall health. The everyday application of wrist-worn fitness trackers encourages healthy behavior, help in the achievement of individual training objectives, and advance a chronic disease-prevention framework.

Environmental exposures, in this case, entails keeping track of things like quality of air, temperature, and the humidity level, which affect the health of an individual. CO sensors, SO₂ sensors, NO – NO₂ – NOx sensors, air quality monitors, AQI sensors, PM2.5 Matter 2.5-micron sensors, Ozone sensors, VOC sensors, and Radon sensors can determine the concentration inside environments and can track exposure risks in respiratory diseases and cardiovascular diseases. It is equally possible to determine the relationships between environmental conditions and the status of people’s health, which allow individuals and healthcare professionals to avoid frequent exposure to various hostile environment conditions and diseases.

Comprehensive health and indicative data are generated from the data harvested from all health monitoring gadgets and probabilities’ model. As analytic interfaces, personalized health platforms offer guidance based on specific measures of health, genetic data, and behavior. These include information on nutrition, physical activity, use of medications, and how to be more active. This offers the possibility of achieving tailored health intelligence to reduce health risks and improve preventive health planning, according to people’s needs, choice, and susceptibility.

## 3.4 Data acquisition and analysis

Data collection and interpretation, therefore, form the cardinal steps in enhancing the use of biomedical sensors and devices. HISs are the processes whereby information regarding a patient’s health is gathered, analyzed, and used for the purposes of diagnosis and treatment planning as well as further management of the person’s health. Over the years, new technologies of data acquisition and analysis have boosted the potential and utility of biomedical devices.
