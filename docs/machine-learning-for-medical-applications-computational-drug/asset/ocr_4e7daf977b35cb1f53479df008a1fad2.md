# 2.4.1 Technologies in genomic sequencing

The advance globalization of individualized medicine owes it to the high-throughput genomic sequencing technology. Technique such as NGS drastically changed the analysis of large-scale genomic data by providing new knowledge about genetic variants and how they influence treatment responses and disease probabilities. Due to a decrease in sequencing costs, and an increase in speed of data retrieval, genomic profiling is now a regular feature of personalized treatment, providing more precise determination of genes that are linked to particular diseases.

# 2.4.2 Bioinformatics about computational biology

Computational biology and bioinformatics have come to the aid in explaining such complex biological data. Application of genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic databases and evaluation of biomarkers are significantly aided by the availability of software tools and algorithm for these purposes. These computational methods enable the integration of multi-omics data, providing a system-level view of disease biology, specific to each patient, and refining the subsequent treatment.

# 2.4.3 Diagnostic innovations

Novelties in diagnostic tools have also extended the number of opportunities for individualized approach to healthcare. The combination of molecular imaging and high-resolution imaging enables labelling and imaging of disease status and therapeutic outcomes at the molecular level. Advancements in point-of-care testing and wearable health monitors have made it possible to monitor patients’ health at close quarters, and quickly alter the management plans, depending on the patient’s overall condition.

# 2.4.4 Artificial intelligent and advanced computing

Personalized medicine now employs ML and AI technologies to fine-tune its analysis of data and work more accurately. Using machine learning, big data is explored, patterns are identified, and the outcomes of various treatment approaches are estimated.
