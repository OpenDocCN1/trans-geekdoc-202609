Biomedical sensing, which involves the use of sensors in the diagnosis of diseases, is made possible by machine learning algorithms, which can perform complex computations, identify patterns, as well as make future predictions. With the help of these algorithms, biomedical sensors give better, reliable, and personalized health assessment and help promote better results of patients and the effectiveness of healthcare delivery.

# 5.2 Deep learning and neural networks

Neural networks and deep learning form core aspects of AI that have hugely improved the efficiency of biomedical sensors. These technologies play a role in analyzing stringent health data and finding patterns in sensor data to support continuous health monitoring research.

Deep learning is a category of machine learning that utilizes neural networks, which consists of many layers, therefore called “deep.” As stated earlier, deep learning algorithms are well-suited for interpreting high dimensional signals from biomedical sensors, wearable bioelectrical sensors, and image sensors. These algorithms are particularly helpful in the large and complex datasets of health monitoring because they can learn characteristics from raw data and do not need feature engineering.

CNNs and RNNs in particular, have been found to yield the greatest results when it comes to analyzing biomedical sensor data. Figure 12 specifically presents deep learning and neural networks in terms of their hierarchical design and connection, which work on improving data analytics. CNNs excel at detecting spatial relationships in data and can used, for example, in image analysis of regions of the human body and spatially localized particularities in data originating from sensors. RNNs, including LSTM networks, perform well in handling sequence data, which is crucial in tracking time-series data with sensors such as electrocardiogram (ECG).
