
## 3.2 Security challenges in traditional EHR systems

To address requirements including the GDPR and HIPAA, safeguarding healthcare-related information is required. Essential measures required for preserving patient health information are covered by regulations and include data encryption and limitation of access to these data. Failure to meet these legal obligations can attract heavy penalties, legal problems, and loss of reputation to the healthcare organizations.

In healthcare data management, to protect patient's identity and build and sustain patient confidence, and to meet regulatory requirements, both security and privacy are crucial. If the patient data were to be adequately protected, healthcare entities need to enforce security and privacy mechanisms. By ensuring protection of privacy during data handling processes, firms can reduce the threat of data violations concerning the patients’ confidentiality.

### Centralization
The conventional EHR systems rely on centralized databases resident on separate servers of the third-party vendors of the implementing healthcare facilities. Since most of the patient's information was stored in one point where it was easy to share and retrieve the information, the system was extremely prone to hacking, unlawful access, and data corruption. Many patients’ electronic health records are at risk; just one successful attack on a central EHR system can enable fraudulent activity, identity thefts, and other vices.

### Insider threats
This threat arises from the fact that when workers, vendors, and contractors have access to personal patient information, they are a risk to healthcare businesses. There are inherent dangers in the different types of insider threats, both accidental and of the malicious kind, in which the internal attacker aims to simply steal and corrupt patient data for personal reasons. Standard EHR structures are often deficient in strong auditing, and insider threats are often hard to detect and prevent.

### Inadequate authentication and access controls
Lack of strong techniques in authentication and poor controls to access in the conventional EHR systems are serious issues. Some of the vulnerabilities include: password-based authentication was vulnerable to brute force attack, phishing, and password guessing and this would compromise user accounts and make it easy for unauthorized person to get access to the patient's record. Lack of proper access control measures exposed patient records to information invasions, modifications, and destruction by end-users who are not privileged to do so.

### Vulnerabilities in legacy systems
These prove that most of the conventional EHR systems in use are based on older technologies with inherent software defects, and hackers have laid their eyes on them.

These vices were exploited by cybercriminals in order to compromise data and infuse damaging malignant software, by gaining unlawful access into EHR systems and confidential patient information. Correcting and upgrading the existing systems was complex, besides which they increased the security hazards.

### Absence of data encryption
Some of the encryption methods supporting the protection of patient information in transit and at the time of storage are missing in the preexisting EHR systems. In the context of databases over the networks, the data remains unencrypted and is easily vulnerable to getting intercepted, methodologies allowed unauthorized access and access by the adversaries. In brief, without encryption, the privacy and confidentiality of the patient information are compromised since medical records are vulnerable to being lost, modified, and disclosed.

Numerous security flaws in traditional EHR systems jeopardize patient data's availability, confidentiality, and integrity. Healthcare companies must lay down strong security measures to address these issues, including encryption, access limits, auditing procedures, and recurring security assessments. Switching to blockchain-based, decentralized EHR systems presents viable ways to reduce security threats and improve data security, privacy, and integrity in the administration of medical records.

## 3.3 Enhancement of blockchain security in EHR management

Blockchain technology comprehensively eliminates the major shortcomings of typical EHR systems through several ways that enhance security in EHR management. The adoption of blockchain technology eliminates the central administrator in managing EHRs, cryptographic methods, and consensus procedures enhance security. These benefits include:

### Decentralization
A copy of the distributed ledger containing EHRs is maintained on the file by each node in the chain of decentralized nodes of a blockchain. As a result of this decentralization there is no single point of failure, thereby reducing the risks of unauthorized access to EHR data. In blockchain, all nodes in the network contain a copy of the ledger and therefore even if the ledger of a particular node is compromised the blockchain remains secure.

In blockchain, there is a record of transactions that cannot be altered in any form, and for each block of data a hash link was formed with the previous block of data. Once a transaction was written to the blockchain, it cannot be altered without the cooperation of the majority of users in the network it serves. EHR data is guaranteed to be entirely traceable as well as unaltered due to its inherent feature of being unalterable by other parties.

### Cryptographic security
To protect EHR data during its lifetime possession, blockchain adopts features of cryptology such as encryption, digital signatures, and hashing. Since every transaction performed on the blockchain is hashed, a unique ID can be obtained from it, which links it to its previous block. It should be noted that only the specified users can send transactions to a blockchain using the digital signatures that check the validity of the transactions. Secure EHR data are used by applying encryptions to the data that are stored in the blockchain to increase security and privacy.

### Consensus processes
It is on the basis of consensus processes that the users of a network can be effectively convinced to accept the legitimacy of transactions on blockchain. Security prevents immoral individuals from making any alterations to the chain by making sure that every node in the distributed system reaches a consensus of the status of the chain in question. Consensus procedures enhance the data stored in the blockchain EHR by strengthening participants’ trust as mentioned below.

### Access control and permission
Access control and permission include EHR data access control and permission rules that were developed using smart contracts which are self-executing contracts implemented on blockchain. To ensure that only the right people for the specific patient interact with information, smart contracts include rules and permission settings for EHR record use or modification. Smart contracts decrease the probability of unauthorized access leakage in blockchain-based EHR systems because of a robust access control system involved.

EHRs benefit from several features and approaches offered by the blockchain such as decentralization, immutable ledger, cryptographic security, consensus mechanism, and access control in enhancing the security of managing EHRs. Blockchain-based EHR solutions are secure due to the features that support patient confidentiality, integrity, and privacy, while providing a clear and secure way to store, maintain, and exchange patient health information.
