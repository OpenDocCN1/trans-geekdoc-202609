# 2.5 Benchmark datasets for machine learning in biomaterials

Benchmark datasets play a foundational role in the development and evaluation of machine learning (ML) models within the field of biomaterials. These datasets typically compile comprehensive information on material properties, fabrication techniques, and performance metrics, derived from both experimental studies and computational simulations. Prominent examples include the Materials Project and the Cambridge Structural Database, which offer vital data for training and validating predictive models focused on biomaterials. These repositories provide insights into characteristics such as mechanical strength, thermal conductivity, and biological compatibility. Their importance, such datasets often face challenges in terms of completeness, sample diversity, and representativeness across different material types and use cases. # 2.5.1 Standardization of benchmark data sets of biomaterials

The development of standard benchmark datasets was, therefore, fundamental to the progress of ML research in biomaterials. Gathering relates to setting general structural components, values of measurements, and attributes for metadata. It was such standardization that enabled comparison throughout and built strong ML models when there was a standardized set through which to train and test. In the planning of sharing and standardizing initiatives, extensive groups of biomaterials should be involved, including common classes of biomaterials such as polymers, metals, and ceramics. The datasets, which should be extended to include conditions that pose realistic repair demands, accustom AI to a broader spectrum of materials, improving the predictability of repair tasks.
