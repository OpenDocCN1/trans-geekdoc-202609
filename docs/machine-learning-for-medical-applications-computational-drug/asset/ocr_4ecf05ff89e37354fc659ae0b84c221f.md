# 3.3.4 Bioimpedance sensors

Bioimpedance sensors identify how much tissue resist an electrical current by using of an electric current that passes through the body. These sensors can measure changes in impedance and the data that is fed back can reflect tissue composition, fluid status, and the general health state. In body composition analysis, bioimpedance techniques are applied in applications such as body composition determination, cardiac recovery, and fluid status determination. Optical and electrical bioimpedance measurements can be noninvasive and are ideal for constant supervision of changes in the body's physiological conditions, and can be incorporated into wearables health monitors.

# 3.3.5 Thermal sensors

Thermal sensors record changes in skin temperature of the body. Examples of thermal sensors include infrared thermometers and temperature-sensitive patches, by which temperatures are measured from the body without touching the patient. These sensors are suitable for use in detecting fever, observing the body temperature while people are physically active, and for evaluating thermal balance. Recent advancements in thermal sensors have made it possible to produce wearable devices that track temperatures consistently, and provide useful information for diseases like fever and hypothermia.
