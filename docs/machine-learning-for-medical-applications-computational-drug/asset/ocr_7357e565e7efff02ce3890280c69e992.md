# 3.4 Wireless and wearable sensor technologies

The advancements in the wireless and wearable sensors systems are a step up evolutionarily in the biomedical sensors and have revolutionized how health data is collected. Such capabilities have effectively solved many weaknesses associated with conventional wired and static sensors, expanding new prospects for constant and concealed health surveillance.

Wired sensors have posed insecurities as patients can only undergo collection of their health data if connected through some wires. The data collected by these sensors is wirelessly communicated to external instruments using radiofrequency, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and other wireless technology interfaces. It also improves the feasibility measures of collecting data, real-time observation, and prompt execution of health information. The use of wireless sensors is especially seen in cases where subject mobility was an issue – in this case, during physical activity they leave their beds better monitored than when stationary, therefore giving a better picture of the overall patient health.

Wearable sensors such as smartwatches and fitness trackers are some of the essential everyday technologies normally used to manage our health. These sensors are always evaluating a profile of numerous physiological parameters and personal adornments are expected to be worn, often in clothes. Examples of biological parameters include; body temperature, respiration rate, heart rate, and physical activity. Due to enhanced surface area-to-volume ratio and higher reactivity, nanoscale materials provide an opportunity to develop highly sensitive and selective sensors, including nanowires, nanotubes, and nanoparticles; it is believed that the sensor used in detection of different physiological indicators and biomarkers can benefit from these nanomaterials.
