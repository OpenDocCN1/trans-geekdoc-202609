Technological developments in power management have played an important role, especially in the miniaturization of biomedical devices. New solutions in power supply, micro batteries, energy harvesting technologies, and low-power electronics have provided ways to build devices to have longer power operation times and small sizes. Power supply methods like kinetic energy, thermal energy, photo voltaic, and radio frequency that are inherent in the human body can be used to supply power for wearable and implantable devices, thus easing the frequent battery replacement of the devices used in continuous monitoring systems.

The development in miniaturization and wearability has made drastic changes in terms of comfort for the users as well as general appearance. Contemporary wearables are portable, inconspicuous, and ornamental, and therefore acceptable to be worn at workplaces and other social settings. This involves the creation of slim and sectional form and the application of Asiatic materials that do not affect the skin, such as wrist bands that people wear as a fashion accessory, and eye ware and clothing that users have adopted; user comfort is maintained since health monitoring is done without causing any inconvenience.

# 4.2 Connectivity and communication

Communication, networking and connectivity play central roles in the development of biomedical sensors that facilitate flow of information, real-time coordinated operations, and in exchange of information between machines, humans, and physicians. These technologies have made a broad range of enhancements to biomedical devices to enhance patient health management and results.

Accessibility and compatibility are interrelated to wireless networking technologies for biometrics sensors and devices, and a number of mobile applications. Some of the most familiar technologies are Bluetooth, wireless fidelity, and cellular technology, each with its characteristics suited to specific application. Bluetooth is commonly employed in transmitting data and short distance communication between the wearable devices and the smart phones. Wi-Fi gives larger coverage and higher data transfer rate, and is used in cases where devices need perpetual data streaming to home-based healthcare systems. Internet networks such as 4G and 5G support remote monitoring by enabling the devices to broadcast data to distant locations, hence enabling tracking of health information, regardless of the place.
