Cryptographic hashing: Blockchain employs cryptographic hash algorithms to generate unique digital fingerprints, known as hashes, for each block of data. These hashes are determined based on the block’s content, making it virtually impossible to alter the data without affecting the hash. If any modification occurs, the block’s hash changes, alerting the network to potential tampering thereby maintaining trust and immutability across the system.

Consensus procedures: The additional transactions flowing into the blockchain, their sequence, and legitimacy are confirmed by consensus procedures, which form the basis of block chains. There is agreement mechanism such as proof of work (PoW), proof of stake (PoS), and practical byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT) that ensures that node members are in agreement regarding the current state of the blockchain. Interested parties cannot manipulate the information that was stored in the systems since the blockchain negates such actions by allowing only the nodes in the system to approve such changes.

Immutable data records: The beauty of the blockchain technology is that once data is entered on the blockchain, it cannot be changed. New blocks are added to the blockchain at the core, in a sequential chain and are nearly impossible to manipulate without consensus of the entire network to the historical records. The article explains that these characteristics guarantee confidentiality of data and the EHR network’s reliability since any alteration of records is easily identified by other members of the network.

Transparent audit trails: Blockchain makes it possible to guard and tag all business transactions and even changes to the information in the EHR. It maintains a visible log of who accessed or modified the data provided by the timestamp and digital signature inherent in every block of a blockchain transaction. This allows stakeholders to verify the authenticity and origins of data, which enhance accountability, traceability, and credibility of the EHR data.

Data encryption and access controls: These EHR systems normally utilize encryption and access controls, which belong to reliable structures, to secure the patient’s data. Database information was in encrypted form with cryptographic keys to help maintain the privacy of the data. Smart contracts’ distributed ledger system-based access control limits the ability of users to access EHR data and thus increases security, data accuracy, and privacy.
