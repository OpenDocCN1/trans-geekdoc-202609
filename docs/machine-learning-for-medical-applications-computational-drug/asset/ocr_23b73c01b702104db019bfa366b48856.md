# 2.3.1 Techniques for feature selection in biomaterials

In the design of biomaterials, a variety of feature selection strategies are used, each having specific benefits and uses. Based on statistical measurements, filter techniques like mutual information and correlation coefficients assess each feature’s importance. Recursive feature elimination is one of the wrapper approaches that evaluates feature relevance by repeatedly training models and eliminating the least important features. Embedded techniques that penalize less significant characteristics during the model training process include LASSO (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator). When applied correctly, these approaches facilitate the understanding of which characteristics are most important, hence yielding models in biomaterials research that are more accurate and easier to understand.

# 2.3.2 Some of the difficulties in feature selection and dimensionality reduction

In biomaterials research, feature selection and dimensionality reduction present many problems, although they are very important. Of these, the major challenge is that reducing the dimensionality of the dataset subjects the model to the risk of losing significant information, which could affect its predictability. Additionally, it is difficult to apply standard definitions of biomaterial feature selection and dimensionalal reduction techniques because of their high nonlinearity and the tight coupling of variables. Instrument selection must be scientifically analyzed because, depending on the exact goals of the study and the data type, certain procedures yield different results.
