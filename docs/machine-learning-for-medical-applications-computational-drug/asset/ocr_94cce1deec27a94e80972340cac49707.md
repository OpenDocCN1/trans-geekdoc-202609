# 4.2 Role of blockchain in enabling interoperability

Blockchain technology has potential to help with healthcare data exchange interoperability issues and make data sharing across different EHR systems easier. Blockchain networks’ distributed and decentralized architecture, along with its cryptographic security characteristics, provide a number of benefits for improving interoperability in the transmission of medical data:

Decentralized data management: Distributed ledgers holding patient health data are maintained in duplicate by every member of decentralized networks of nodes, which are the foundation of blockchain-based EHR systems. Because of its decentralized design, peer-to-peer communication and cooperation between healthcare professionals, patients, and other stakeholders was made possible with the requirement for a central authority to permit data transmission. Blockchain lessens dependency on centralized repositories and eases interoperability issues brought on by incompatible EHR systems by decentralizing data management.

Consensus methods: In order to reach a consensus among network users on the legitimacy of transactions, blockchain depends on consensus methods like PoW. Ensuring that every node in the network has a shared knowledge of the blockchain’s current state, consensus methods help to build confidence and agreement on the accuracy of EHR data. Blockchain improves interoperability by encouraging confidence between many parties and enabling safe data sharing by offering a shared and unchangeable record of transactions.

Smart contracts built into blockchain hold the potential for enabling self-execution of exchange and interaction protocols of data contained in multiple EHRs. Specifically, smart contracts conceptualize policies governing the access, modification, and sharing of EHR information, while guaranteeing compliance with interoperability standards and data management policies in various systems. Smart contracts become effective means for increasing the speed and integrity of data exchange between participants in healthcare systems through the automation of exchange processes and compliance with standards for interoperability.

Data standardization and semantic interoperability: Blockchain-based EHR systems can take advantage of standard structure and exchange formats, and standard terminology and knowledge representation systems for semantically seamless use and interchangeability of EHR data. The use of common data understandings and common languages enables EHR systems built on the application of blockchain technology to effectively share and exchange these patient details without a high probability of data incompatibilities, errors, and misunderstandings. Interoperability is further promoted in this case by standardization combined with blockchain technology, both on the technical and the semantic levels of sharing health information.
