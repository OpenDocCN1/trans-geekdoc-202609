# 4.4.3 Surface modification coatings

Coatings applying surface modification are employed to alter the surface properties of materials in order to enhance biocompatibility. The basic properties of the surface, such as roughness, wettability, and chemical nature, can be altered by techniques such as chemical vapor deposition, plasma treatment, and self-assembled monolayers. Such changes provide the possibility to improve cell adhesion, proliferation, and integration with the surrounding tissues. A number of areas are possible for the use of surface modification coatings; these include tissue engineering scaffolds, dental prostheses, and orthopedic implants.

# 4.4.4 Hydrophobic and hydrophilic coatings

Hydrophobic and hydrophilic coatings are intended to regulate the relationship between medical devices and body fluids. Hydrophobic coatings have no affinity for water and are deployed to minimize biofilm formation and tissue attachment, whereas hydrophilic coatings have an affinity for water that favors the integration of the device and tissue. Special applications include the ability to control the level of lubrication of joint implants and the use of hydrophilic surfaces for drug delivery.

# 4.4.5 Bioactive coatings

Biologically active coatings are designed to exhibit suitable reactions with biological tissues, thereby eliciting certain responses such as tissue bonding. Essential stimulatory substances, such as growth factors, which stimulate cell activity and accelerate the healing process, are often incorporated into these coatings. One example of bioactive glasses is their use in the coatings of implant components; such coatings improve implant connection while promoting the development of bone at the site of the implant. Bioactive coatings enhance the field of regenerative medicine through tissue healing and the more efficient functioning of medical tools and devices.
