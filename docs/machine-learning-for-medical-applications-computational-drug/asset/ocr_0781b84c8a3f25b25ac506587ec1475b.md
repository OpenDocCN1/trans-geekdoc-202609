# 2.3.3 Temperature sensors

Temperature sensors are meant to detect changes in physical temperature that are an early sign of many diseases. These sensors are associated with wearable gadgets such as smart and wearable devices to monitor changes in body temperature. For instance, in fever monitoring, the thermal sensors give real-time data to produce indicators of febrile conditions thus enabling early medical intervention. These sensors have to be accurate and respond fast to ensure proper health monitoring is achieved.

# 2.3.4 Pressure sensors

Physiological pressure measurement devices measure different forms of pressure including blood pressure. For estimating blood pressure, which as a variable fluctuates over time, pressure sensors are applied in devices used for continuous health monitoring, to track trends in cardiovascular health. These sensors operate on the change in pressure and turn that change into an electrical signal to compare pressure and look for signs of health problems.

# 2.3.5 Bioelectrical sensors

Bioelectrical sensors in turn record electrical signals produced by endogenous physical functions. ECG and EEG are prominent examples, which are used to monitor the activity of the heart and the head, respectively. ECG sensors are attached to the body in an attempt to pick up electrical product of the cardiac contractions and assists in the diagnosis of arrhythmias and other cardiac abnormalities. EEG involves acquiring of brain wave patterns by use of sensors to diagnose conditions such as epilepsy and sleep disorders. These sensors are basic in the determining and treating of various cardiac and neurological conditions.

As such, all kinds of biomedical sensors have been developed to address certain concerns using diverse technologies in order to yield relevant health information. These sensors become smarter when connected to AI technologies, meaning that the data received is real-time, precise, and broader health data.
