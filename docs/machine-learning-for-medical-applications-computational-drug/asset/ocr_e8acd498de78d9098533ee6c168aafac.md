Using ML algorithms, it was possible for researchers to reduce the time taken in searching for new materials and enhance the ones available to meet medical needs [4]. This chapter identifies that ML was changing the course in the biomaterials field and analyzes its contributions to the state of the art and its possibilities to advance the subject in the future [5].

Something like big data and the previously unimaginable speed with which it was capable of analyzing information was one of the perks of using ML in the field of biological materials research [6]. Biomedical research generates vast amounts of data from multiple sources, including but not limited to Omics technologies, clinical trial data, and high-throughput experiments [7]. These datasets can be provided to ML algorithms for analysis to determine correlations that inform the creation of medicinal materials [8]. There are strategies, like predictive modeling, which can predict future material performance while avoiding time-consuming and costly empirical tests [9, 10]. This capacity results in enhanced medical solutions by shortening the development process and enhancing accuracy in material expectations [11, 12].

One major advancement in the employment of ML to biological materials was transfer learning. Called transfer of concept, this method assisted in mapping current models to new problems, where the model was derived from one job but applicable to another, although different [13, 14]. The technique of transfer learning in biomedical materials research used models with superior datasets from general material science and applied them to biomedical material applications [15]. To determine the behavior of the biomedical materials, including drug delivery systems and tissue scaffolds, the model intended to predict the chemical aspects can be adjusted [16]. In this strategy, new knowledge was derived from previous knowledge and experience so that new models do not have to be designed as much, and time and effort are saved [17].

To fully achieve these objectives, the integration of a machine learning approach into biological materials research has some issues that must be addressed [18]. This is especially a problem for data, as biomedical research often employs data in multiple formats and of varying quality from various sources [19]. It is obvious that, in order to construct stable ML models capable of providing exact and valuable information, various datasets must be combined properly [20]. In addition, the validation and acceptance of results by the scientific community require, in turn, that ML models are understandable [21]. To tackle this problem, researchers have to find ways to increase the interpretability of ML models to enable proper validation of the outcomes and to incorporate the findings into practice effectively [22].

Possible future trends of applied ML for biomedical materials science point to discovering new algorithms, enhancing data integration strategies, and addressing the problem of ethics [23]. Research into and application of ML technology can only progress; thus, there is more potential for refining the models that can be constructed to explore increasingly complex data sources and gain a deeper understanding of how various materials behave [25].
