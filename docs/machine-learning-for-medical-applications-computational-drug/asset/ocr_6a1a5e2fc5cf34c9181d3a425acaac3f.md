A number of issues still remain in the use of AI-driven biomedical sensors, as mentioned in the above discussion, Data verification, calibration problems, and continuous real-time processing are three major elements requiring further research and technological advancements. To address these issues, it is critical to unlock the full benefits of AI sensors and achieve high levels of system dependability in a clinical environment. More commitment to technological development and extensive procedures of testing should be central to these concerns and the progress of the field.

As the future unfolds, there exist ample possibilities in having biomedical sensors to transform the aspect of personalized health and human care. As technologies that support more personalized, predictive, and preventive medicine, these devices are well positioned to revolutionize how health status is detected and addressed around the world. That is why it appears that these advancements in AI as well as the development of sensors have further possibilities of development, creating an even more effective health monitoring. Alvino et al. earlier pointed out that as the field continues to advance, it is expected that biomedical sensors driven by artificial intelligence play a more central role in defining the future of healthcare, thus enhancing the quality of the systems and consequently the health of patients internationally.
