Assessment of disease progression: While chronic pulmonary diseases require constant observation of additional respiratory sensors to evaluate the disease progression and effectiveness of the treatment, such factors ensure the identification of the changes in the respiratory parameters over time, hence enabling the healthcare providers to come up with the correct decision when it comes to the management of treatment services and interventions.

Emergency response and alerts: Modern respiratory monitoring sensors are capable of being set to generate alarms in case certain abnormal respiratory rates are detected. This feature is especially useful in patients with critical diseases of the lungs and thus could prevent severe complications.

# 5.3.2 Challenges and considerations

Accuracy and reliability: The sensor technology used in respiratory monitoring has been considered vulnerable to factors such as position of sensors, movement of the patient, and environmental factors. There is a need to standardize the sensors to provide dependable results in disease control and security of the patients.

Data integration and interpretation: Real-time respiratory rates analysis and monitoring based on the data from different sensors is challenging. Ordering, integration, and analysis of patient's multimodal data are needed, especially for the purpose of decision support.

Patient compliance and comfort: In particular, compliance with respiratory monitoring is critical when managing diseases, as evidenced by patient's noncompliance with wearing a face mask emanating from virus breakage. Comfort and easy to use of these respiratory sensors is another factor that determines its usage, where a poorly designed sensor remains a collector's item despite its high-tech functions.

Pulmonary disease management can significantly benefit from using respiratory monitoring sensors as diagnostic tools, especially in an outpatient setting, and as adjuncts to the treatment of chronic respiratory conditions. Future advancements in technology predict that advanced and accurate, easy-to-use and integrated respiratory sensors would further improve disease management, hence optimizing patient outcomes.
