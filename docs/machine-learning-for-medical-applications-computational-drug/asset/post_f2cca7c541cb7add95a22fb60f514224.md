## 5.5 Performance evaluation metrics: accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, ROC-AUC

There was a range of valuable quantitative measures used to estimate the performance of machine learning models in medical imaging, and all these measures together provide a comprehensive view of the models’ work. The four primary metrics that are utilized consist of ROC-AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy.

Accuracy is the percentage of images classified correctly out of all the images that had been sorted. It shows how well the model predicts the class of unknown samples. It should not be expected to obtain equal accuracy for the performance of the model in the context of unbalanced data sets where certain conditions are rare because the model is inclined toward the most frequent class.

Recall, another name for sensitivity, defines how well a model is capable of correctly identifying positive conditions, such as determining an illness where it exists. Indeed, in medical imaging, for instance, where missing a positive case has major implications, this statistic is very important. High sensitivity implies that the model can single out subjects with this condition, and this is a significant factor in identifying the disease early enough after diagnosis.

The accuracy of negative observations, i.e., condition absence, is what specificity measures in the model. This measure helps estimate the performance of the model in rejecting false positives, which is necessary to avoid unnecessary further tests. High specificity ensures that the model can determine with a lot of certainty whether a patient is not affected by the illness.

Every point on the curve represents sensitivity together with (1 - specificity) over a range of cutoffs of the diagnostic test in question. The discrimination between the positive and negative situations in the total sample was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). A higher value of AUC indicates that, over a range of threshold levels, the model performed better in distinguishing between the presence and absence of the condition.

The output layer contains the outcomes offered by the CNN model, which include probability estimates in classification tasks and regression operations. CNNs have been increasingly studied for many different medical imaging tasks because of their efficiency in analyzing and representing a certain set of image data. Key applications include:

- **Image classification:** CNNs are applied to sort medical images into different groups and types, such as sorting between the various types of tumors or applying the model to a new image to determine whether it contains a disease. This application assists the diagnostic process by providing instant and accurate classification based on features that have been learned.
- **Image segmentation:** CNNs are used in splitting medical images to outline structural areas of tissue. For example, there are methods like U-Net that target exact segmentation tasks to provide required boundary information for the organs, lesions, etc.
- **Disease detection and diagnosis:** CNNs help identify diseases through imaging data by detecting abnormality patterns and anomalies. For instance, CNNs help in finding markers for cancers and diabetic retinopathy, making the diagnosis more accurate at the earliest stage.
- **Image enhancement:** CNNs can improve the quality of medical images by filtering out noise and correcting resolution artifacts. Higher resolution can help improve the performance of diagnosis and intervention, enhancing analysis and interpretation.
- **Radiological image interpretation:** CNNs assist radiologists in drawing conclusions from intricate imaging information by offering further information in a specific aspect. This collaboration of human and artificial intelligence coming together makes the overall diagnostic work function even better.
