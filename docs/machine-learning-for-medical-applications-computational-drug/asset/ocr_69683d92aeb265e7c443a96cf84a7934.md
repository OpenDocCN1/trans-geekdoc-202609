# 2.5 Challenges in adopting machine learning for material development

Before the true potential of ML as a foundational tool for the creation of biomedical materials can be achieved, several more challenges must be faced within the present field. Suddenly, the volume and variety of data required to achieve efficient ML applications became a problem. To create powerful ML techniques, it's necessary to use datasets that are appropriate, diverse, and of high quality. Nevertheless, owing to considerations related to the nature of biological systems, sources of variability in the methodology of data collection, as well as the general limitations on research materials in the course of experiments, attaining such data can be problematic in the sphere of biomedical materials. This means that unreliable model predictions are brought about by these limitations, and this is something that impacts the results of material development.

Another significant challenge was the traceability of the models to interpretation. Higher levels of ML algorithms, like deep learning models, work in the “black box” format, where predictions are made without necessarily providing an explanation of the system’s workings. It becomes even harder to assert and believe in the outcomes in cases of opacity regarding the mechanism of choosing the ML models. The mathematical model had to be accurate to ensure that the predictions made by the model could be used for the development of biomedical materials. In the Holobloc process, model interpretability was important as the final results of the model must reliably match the experimental outcomes and the norms and standards set for the approval of different biomedical materials.

As expected, one of the challenges of using ML was how to incorporate it into the current operations effectively. The conventional material development process often relies on registered methods and processes, which limit the application of utilizing ML radically. To include ML in these procedures, this part implies key changes in workflows such as the implementation of data management systems, changes to experimental approaches, and personnel training. These changes can be costly and may meet a lot of resistance from stakeholders who favor the traditional mode of planning.
