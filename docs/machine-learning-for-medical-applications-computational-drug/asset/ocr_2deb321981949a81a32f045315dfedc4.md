Further advancement was brought by nanotechnology, implying the ability to design and build nanomaterials with better properties and features. The application of numerous nanostructured materials, like carbon nanotubes and nanoparticles, has been researched mainly for biological purposes and includes tissue engineering, medication delivery, and imaging techniques. Therapies can therefore be made more accurate and efficient in a clinical approach since cells can be altered by tackling materials at the nano level.

As the complexity of biological issues grew, the limitations of traditional material development approaches became clearer. The needs of present-day medicine, which imply the synthesis of different branches and the analysis of large amounts of information, are too complex for trial-and-error approaches that were the driving force behind most of the progress. This realization led to the incorporation of computational and ML approaches into the modern biomedical materials development age. This development can help researchers predict and enhance the properties of materials and accelerate the discovery of new ones; in addition, it is helpful for providing patients with targeted and faster care.

The aforementioned historical development shows that biomedical material science is a fast-growing field that has required frequent innovation to meet changing healthcare demands. As the field unfolds, the application of ML is the luminous future that presents itself as the most promising area to dramatically advance new biomedical materials in medicine.

# 2.2 The emergence of machine learning in material science

The incorporation of ML into material science signifies a shift in the long-standing approach to materials and science, especially in biomedical material science. In the past, the discovery of materials used in biomedical applications was based on the accumulation of knowledge derived from experience and from identifying materials which, upon testing, meet the desired performance criteria in the development process. Although these are the traditional approaches to new product development, they have been known to bring about long development cycles and high costs; success has depended on mere luck rather than organized ingenuity.

ML has become an exciting new tool in the world of material science, allowing the large-scale analysis of data and more accurate predictions of materials' characteristics and properties. Algorithms of the supervised as well as unsupervised learning type have been useful when it comes to the task of finding a pattern within large data that would go unnoticed. This capability is significantly valuable in biomedical materials, as the relationships involving materials and other living structures are always rich in diversity and quite complex.
