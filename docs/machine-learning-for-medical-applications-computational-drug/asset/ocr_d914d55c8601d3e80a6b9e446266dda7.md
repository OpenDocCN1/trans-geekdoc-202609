# 4.1.2 Dental implants

As for replacement teeth, dental implants provide support. Titanium is popular due to its great strength, its ability to assimilate into the bone, and its generally good compatibility. The functional and esthetic rehabilitation that mimics the lost tooth structure is achieved by the stable anchorage of titanium implants to the bone tissue. There have been advancements in technology for the surface treatment of implants through etching techniques, such as the use of acid and plasma spraying, which have enhanced the bond and efficiency of dental implants.

# 4.1.3 Cardiovascular implants

Pacemakers, valves, and stents are other cardiovascular implant devices required to address diseases and illnesses of the cardiovascular system. Some of the naturally compatible materials used include silicone and polyurethane; the devices designed are highly compatible with the circulatory system. The newest advance in cardiovascular implants is the production of drug-eluting stents, which are coated with biocompatible polymers that release medicine to combat restenosis. Thus, the materials must exhibit minimal propensity to cause inflammation and thrombosis and be capable of withstanding the harsh and active conditions of the cardiovascular system.

# 4.1.4 Prosthetic limbs

These artificial limbs are designed to mimic lost limbs, which allow patients to experience improved mobility as well as functionality. The biocompatibility of the materials utilized in prosthetics includes lightweight polymers, carbon fiber-reinforced plastics, silicone rubber, and other flexible materials. Carbon fiber composites offer a high strength-to-weight ratio, making prosthetic limbs durable yet comfortable. Used in liners and sockets, silicone elastomers provide cushioning characteristics and a skin-simulating texture to enhance fit and mitigate skin chafing. New prosthetic developments, such as the addition of sensors and the incorporation of motorized attachments, depend on these materials to provide better performance and user experience.
