Lack of transparency: Standard EHR systems are essentially opaque and do not readily provide audit trails of records where modifications have been made. Since there are no obvious audit trails it was very hard to determine the genuineness, origin, and sanctity of EHR data. Batching entry, feature variety, and various modes of documentation lead to lack of standardization, system error, and a lack of metadata about modifications, thus posing threats to the validity and reliability of data.

Limited interoperability: Problems of interfacing in traditional EHR systems restrict the communication and transfer of information concerning the patient between the healthcare organizations and systems. Most of the data is in incompatible formats and applications use proprietary standards and have data in different silos, which makes integration and correlation of data a nightmare. Inadequate data exchange specifications and interface connection limit interconnectivity of EHRs, thereby affecting the quality of information shared and coherence of patient care.

Data security risks: Legacy traditional EHR are vulnerable to many factors of the data insecurity such as hacking, exposure, and even cyberattacks. Lack of strong authentication, poor control of access, and limited encryption mean that EHR data can be compromised by being released or changed. Independent threats include other risk sources, which are internal adversaries, motivated actors, and personnel mistakes whereby insiders expose the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of patient data.

Limited data validation: Typical models of EHRs do not have sufficient features for data validation to maintain the correctness, comprehensiveness, and coherence of patients’ records. Traditional methods of data entry, pre-implementation data validation rules, and an overall limited data validation scale increase the chances of data entry error, omission, and inconsistency. Lacking automated validation components in data quality checks, such problems in EHR data remain unseen, thus impairing the complexity, quality, and general soundness of the data.
