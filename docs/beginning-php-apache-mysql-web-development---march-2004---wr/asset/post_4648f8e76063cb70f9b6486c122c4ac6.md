
## Choosing a Third-Party Host

Look into the type of access your host provides. Obviously, your host will give you FTP access so you can upload your files to the Web server. Some, however, will give you FTP access but nothing else. The problem is that you are likely to run into a situation in which you want to configure your server. For this you will need either OR or SSH access to the command line.

In fact, the ability to configure is often necessary when performing tasks that usually aren't allowed by hosts by default. For example, consider htpasswd. With htpasswd, you can deny and allow access to certain files and directories by the users you allow in the htpasswd file. (Check Chapter 11 for more information on htpasswd)

Along with that, most hosts allow you to use cron jobs, but are not likely to set them up for you. Therefore, you need to tell the server and edit the crontab file to allow you the ability to run scheduled tasks. There are countless configuration settings that you might want to change if your host allows you to configure them. Keep that in mind when choosing your hosting solution.

## Administration GUIs

Certain hosts offer Administration GUIs (Graphical User Interfaces) or User Control Panels as a feature of their packages. A lot of people don't really care for GUIs, but when you don't have a choice, either you don't have sufficient access to the server or you don't fully understand how to get things through tunnel, a point-and-click solution is a wonderful tool.

The interface can be as simple as one allows you to view information about your server, or it can be complex as one allows you to install applications and programming languages with the click of a button. Also, keep in mind that if you have a client that wants to be able to administer its e-mail users, many of the control panels have utilities that allow the client to do themselves. With such a feature, rather than having to call you or the hosting company to set up an e-mail account, the client can simply log on to the control panel and set up and delete users as the needs arises.

## Bandwidth and Site Usage

Bandwidth and site usage factor into the overall price of hosting. Hosting companies usually give out only so much bandwidth usage per month. Should you go over that amount, there is usually a hefty cost.

Consider the following issues when looking into bandwidth:

- Web site traffic
- E-mail usage
- Database connections
- Streaming media

If you have heavy activity in any or all of these areas, before you know it, you will get billed for bandwidth usage. You need to consider how many people will visit your site on average. In addition, hosts count e-mail usage in the end-of-the-month calculation used to bill you. Some even go so far as to monitor your FTP access and count toward the total bandwidth used.

## Appendix G

Database connections don't really relate to bandwidth usage, hosts often limit the amount of database connections you can make another way to control the number of people allowed to visit the site at one time.

Finally, streaming media is very heavy on bandwidth; should you plan to use it as a form of conveying information to the end users of your site, your hosting could rise dramatically.

## Pricing

You need to consider all the preceding areas when figuring out how much your host is worth to you. Rather than total price, look at the price feature. You won't often get all the features you want for your site, as long as you get most of them and you choose the host that has the lowest price per feature, you will probably make a wise hosting choice.

When using price to make your choice, ask yourself how much a particular feature is worth to you. Remember that some hosting companies require that you sign up for a full year and they won't offer a refund of any sort should you receive poor service and/or decide the service isn't worth the money you are paying. It's best to find a host that will allow you to choose either monthly, quarterly, yearly hosting options. That way you don't have to wait a full year to leave if you're dissatisfied. Keep in mind that when you choose a shorter option, such as monthly or quarterly, the host will often charge a little more than you pay up front, or they may charge setup fees that might be waived if you pay up front.
