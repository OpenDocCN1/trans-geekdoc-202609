# Adding E-Commerce to the Comic Book Fan Site

It's time to show how you can easily incorporate an e-commerce section on our Comic Book Appreciation site. You will need a few things to get started:

- Something to sell
- Some mechanism for your customers to pick what they want to buy
- Some way for your customers to pay for what they want to buy
- Some process to get the merchandise to your customers

Let's break it down (break dancing gear optional) and talk about each of these things individually.
The first two can help you with; the second one are really outside the scope of this book, beyond a good discussion.

# Something to Sell

Before you can sell something, you have to have something to sell. Duh. Next topic. No, seriously, retailers spend money researching what will sell and what not, what the hottest trends are, and what the latest technology has to offer. All that being said, your ideas for products will likely come from one or more of the following categories:

- Your ownership: You will most likely know what your customers want based on inherent knowledge of the focus of your Web site. For example, if you have a site for collectors of old tractors, you probably know what products would appeal to your typical customer because you are the typical customer.
- Something you couldn't easily find: You may also have been looking for a specific product or group of products, only to find that they do not exist on any particular site until you create and pull them all together. (For example, www.giftsforeigne.com was created to be a compilation of products that appeal to engineers).
- Your inventions: Another item you might sell from your site is a new invention or design you have created from your little old brain. Many building artists and inventors sell their stuff on the Web, where they can reach a multitude of potential buyers.
- Promotion of your site: Many other sites offer promotional items for sale to tout the URL of site. This acts as a "win-win" for both parties; the customers can proclaim their support for a cool site and the site makes a few bucks and gets its name out for all to see.

So whether you're reinventing the wheel, selling specific types of wheels, taking a bunch of wheels and putting them together, selling wheels with your name on, you must create a product base, and it may not be as easy as it looks.

# Shopping Cart

Now that we know what we are going to sell, we have to have some way for our customers to choose the specific products they want to buy; this involves a shopping cart. You can hook yourself up with ready-made shopping cart software, or can use a cart straight from a programming script, such as PHP (or CGI). Because we're on this topic, we may well get a little crazy and talk a bit about the pros and cons of each.

# Shopping Cart Software

There are numerous shopping cart programs that can easily help your customers and make it easy for them to pick what they want. While these programs can be expensive, you can also take care of things such as security, complex product option selection, maintaining customer information, and keeping track of previously placed orders.

An example of shopping cart software is Cart32. Available at www.cart32.com, this is a widely used shopping cart program that provides numerous configuration options for a Webmaster. Features include Web-based administration of your cart and pending/sent orders, the ability to use your own database or to store product and customer information, automatic credit processing and e-confirmation, complex product options and discount structures, online tracking through the major carriers for your customers to track their orders, inventory management, and customization of the look of your cart. The costs are $29.95 to set up and $29.95 per month if you have Cart32 host the cart portion of your site for you. Many hosting companies have Cart32 for the cart they offer to their customers.

These types of shopping cart programs are popular because they enable you to set up your store and running with relative ease and they take care of the security issues for you.

# Your Own Cart Software Code

Remember when you depend on someone else to supply your shopping cart for you, you are at the mercy of their servers and their software. If they are down, so is yours. If their servers catch a virus, it affects you and your customers, too. Plus, there may be a function you need that they don't offer, or the cost may be prohibitive for your newly created site. Whatever reason, some you may want to code your own script, and for those of brave enough to tread these waters, let's go!

We'll start with a very simple shopping cart system that will consist of several files:

- create.php: Creates the main database and the necessary tables.
- createmp.php: Creates a temporary table to store shopping cart information before the customer actually checks out.
- products.php: Populates the database with product information.
- cbashop.php: Acts as the home page for our little store and lists our available products.
- getprod.php: Retrieves detailed information about a single product.
- add.php: Adds a product to our shopping cart.
- cart.php: Displays the contents of the quantity of any item in our shopping cart.
- change.php: Allows us to change the quantity of any item in our shopping shopping cart.
- delete.php: Deletes an item from the shopping cart.
- checkout.php: The first step in the checkout process; this is where the customer enters billing and shipping information.
- checkout.php: The second step in the checkout process; this is where customers verify the accuracy of their orders and make last-minute changes.
- checkout.php: The final step of the checkout process, where the customer actually sends the order to, and receives an order number and confirmation. The information is put into the database and deleted from the table, a customer number is assigned (if it's a new customer), and an order number is assigned, as well. E-mail confirmations are sent to the customer and us.
