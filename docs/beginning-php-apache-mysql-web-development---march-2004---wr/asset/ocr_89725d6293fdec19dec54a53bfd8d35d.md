# Using Log Files to Improve Your Site

Earlier in the chapter, we touched on how using the stats can improve your site. Your logs, in many cases, your only source of feedback from your visitors. You can't know what you're right or wrong without any feedback, so your only tangible evidence, these stats are quite valuable. There are several different areas you probably want to pay attention to, depending on the specific needs of your site.

# User Preferences and Information

You can't please all the people all of the time, but you can certainly try. You care about your users like you obviously want to tailor your site to the most common visitor, and try to minimize the number of visitors who won't have the optimal viewing experience. You want to know what percentage of visitors are using which browsers so and test your site against the most popular browsers of your audience. You also care how many unique and not-so-unique visitors are coming to your site so you can tell if your site is gaining a new following, maintaining its current one. You also want to know what screen size they are using, so you can tailor the look of your site to be the best it can be for the most visitors.

# Number of Hits and Page Views

Remember, a “hit” is any request made to the server, whereas a “page view” is a request for a page (as an HTML page). Hits can consist of images, files, or anything that requires activity from the server. This doesn't really give you an accurate count of how many people are viewing a page, so you typically go by page views.

You want to see which pages get the most page, and are the most popular so if you need to make something about your site, you can make sure it appears on those pages. For example, if you have a new product to promote—if no one visits the “new products” page, it won't do much good to only post it there. If the homepage of your site is the most popular, you want to post after information on that page, so you make sure that everybody knows your sites about your new product.

You also want to be able to look at the pages that are doing well and compare them with the pages that aren't doing so. The content of both pages is clear and concise. What is it about the popular pages that make them so great? Can you make your losers closer to the winners in page design, content?

# Trends over Time

It's rewarding to see your site become more popular as time goes on, but it creates a big dip in your stuff if things are going downhill. Tracking popularity over time can help you discern if your site is waning, or if it's more popular around certain seasons of the year. If your site golf equipment and you notice a dip in page views during the winter months, obviously you don't have to worry about it, as your business is a seasonal business and this dip is understandable. You notice that during the winter months your average visitor is coming from Florida (makes sense, eh?). Perhaps you can work with marketing to develop an advertising strategy tailored to the loyal Floridians during those months. (Yes, we said “Work with marketing.” It happens, get over it!)
