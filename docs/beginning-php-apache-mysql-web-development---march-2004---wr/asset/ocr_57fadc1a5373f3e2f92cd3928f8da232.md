# Chapter 15

So, if users are not logged in, we give them links to log in or register as a new user. If they are logged in, they can log out or view their profile. And, if they are administrators, they will have access to the admin functions.

That brings us to the admin.php page. We were able to get here by clicking the Admin link, which is available only if you are logged in as the administrator. So, far, good. What if the user attempts to access the admin.php page directly?

Try it yourself. Load index.php in your browser, and make sure you are logged out. Once you are logged out, load admin.php by typing it directly in the address bar of your browser. It should load with no problem. Now, edit one of the files on the main admin page. Again, nothing is stopping. Indeed, when you click the Update button, the data will be saved.

But... you are not logged in! Is this possible? You have not checked the user's credentials once or he got into the page.

Just as you are responsible for checking IDs in your bar case when waiters slip in, you are responsible for the users' access to your site. If you don't want people to access a page, you not only have to bar access any link loading the page, but kick them off the page if they are successful in loading it.

Fortunately, it is easy to do. At the top of your page, simply check their credentials (Those are up to you). They need a access level! Do they need to be logged in?, and then redirect them to another page if they dont (shameyou.php, or simply back to index.php).

You can do things to make your site more secure. Most are way beyond the scope of the book. A look at the link we gave earlier could help you if you are interested in learning more about security. Just don't ever think you "secure enough" if you haven't considered the risk of unauthorized access.
