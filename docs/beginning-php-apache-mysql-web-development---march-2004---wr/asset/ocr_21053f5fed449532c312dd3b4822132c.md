You may notice that we don't worry about the situation in which an article is not passed. As it stands, you viewarticle.php without the "article" parameter in the URL, you will simply get a page that consists of site title, search, and a menu (all included in header.php). The rest will be blank. If that's the desired result, then it's fine. You may decide to redirect the user back to the home page if $_GET['article'] is empty. If so, don't forget to include http.php and use redirect() before header.php.

You may have noticed that we left out pages, login.php and forgotpass.php. They are important pages, of course, but are mostly simple HTML. The includes at the top and bottom for header.php and footer.php are the only PHP. We're sure you can figure out how those two pages work.

# Summary

Well, if you didn't writer's cramp before, you probably do now. We hope this application has given you some insight into the separation of content and design. Because of the application we designed, you can easily modify the look and feel of the application by either altering your header and footer files, or using a CSS file to set different styles. This won't matter to your users, who will still be able to enter articles without ever having to worry about what the article looks like on the Web when it's published.

We also hope that you understand the importance of updating your site enough to draw users again and again. By adding an application like this to your site, and allowing users to add content for you, you create a dynamically changing site with fresh information. Just think about all the ways you could implement such a design:

+   - Creating a message board. (We examine this in more detail in Chapter 15.)

+   - Adding a daily comic. Perhaps you have an artist who can draw a comic every day. You could create an application that allows him or her to upload strips, and allows users to comment on them. You can see an example of this at www.userfriendly.org.

+   - Compiling photo journals. While back, there was a project in which photographers went over the world, and in a 24-hour period, they took their pictures and uploaded the digital images, and in the central office typed out descriptions and allowed people to view them online. It was a very ambitious project and a perfect example of a CMS application.

The bottom line is that if you have content you want to be able to update on a regular basis, you definitely to implement a CMS application. And now, you have the basic tools to build one on your own!
