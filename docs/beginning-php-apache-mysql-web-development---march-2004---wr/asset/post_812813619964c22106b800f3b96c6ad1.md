## E-Commerce Any Risks

As we briefly mentioned before, you can integrate e-commerce into your site the right way or you can do it the wrong way. Prevent yourself from looking like an idiot and virtually ensuring e-commerce failure, we recommend doing things the right way: Good-of-mouth travels slowly, but we know bad-of-mouth spreads quickly. Also, with so many sites out there competing for attention, want to elevate yours above the rest.

This may sound harsh, but there are some things to remember about the most challenging characteristics of your potential customers:

- Your customers are impatient. They don't want to wait for your pages to load or for answers to questions. They are busy people just like you, and if they don't find what they need right away they are out on something else.
- Your customers are distrustful. Who wants their personal information all over the Web? They certainly don't. They don't want credit card number to be used by every geek online. They don't want to give tons of money and never see product they purchased. They don't want to order from you one week and have you go bankrupt.
- Your customers want lot for little. In this age of website competition, where people can compare prices with a few clicks, they are striving to get the best deal they can. They want to make sure they are getting the best deal, but they also appreciate the value-added services of a high-quality website.
- Your customers are generally lazy. They don't have to put any effort into finding the right product on your site or figuring out what you're trying to say or what your policies are. They don't want to work at trying to get the checkout process to work, and they don't want to have to filter pages and pages of text information.
- Your customers aren't forgiving. You basically have one chance to make a good impression on customers. Nothing can eliminate a sale (and future sales for matter) faster than bad experience. Whether it is something minor such as spelling mistakes and broken images on your site or major such as faulty merchandise, customers are likely to remember bad longer than something good. They will also be more likely to share a bad experience than good one.
- Your customers may not be as technically savvy as you are. Yes, there are actually people who still use dial-up with 56K. There are people who still use 14" monitors and there are people who have never made an online purchase in their lives. Remember these people and don't leave them behind when designing your site. If you do, you are alienating a huge percentage of the population.

Don't worry: satisfying customers is not hard, but a little effort can go a long way. We've included some general guidelines to follow. After reading this, you may think "Well, duh, kidding" but you'd be surprised by how many big well-known companies don't follow.

## Information Is Everything

Your customers have to get as much information as possible about your product because they can't actually see, feel, touch, and smell what you have to offer. Your site is window to your customers; whatever blanks you leave in your product description, policies, company history, or checkout process will have to be filled in customer's imagination. That may in certain circumstances you not want your customers to make incorrect assumptions that leave them dissatisfied after the fact, or their uncertainty prevent the sale altogether.

Besides textual information, graphics are a very important part of the sale. There is a fine balance between adding too many graphics to your site, which could cause patients to wait longer than they need to, and providing high-quality pictures so they can actually see what they're getting.
