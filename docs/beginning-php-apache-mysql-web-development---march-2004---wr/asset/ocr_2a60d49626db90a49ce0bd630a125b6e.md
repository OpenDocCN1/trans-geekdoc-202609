# Chapter 14

Your customers want a lot for a little. In this age of Web site competition, where people can compare prices with a few mouse clicks, they are striving to get the best deal they can. They want to make sure they are getting the best deal, but they also appreciate the value-added services of a high-quality Web site.

Your customers are generally lazy. They don't have to put any effort into finding the right product on your site or figuring out what you're trying to say or what your policies are. They don't want to work at trying to get the checkout process to work, and they don't want to have to filter pages and pages of text information.

Your customers aren't forgiving. You basically have one chance to make a good first impression on your customers. Nothing can eliminate a sale (and future sales for that matter) faster than a bad experience. Whether it is something minor such spelling mistakes and broken images on your site or major such as faulty merchandise, your customers are likely to remember bad lot longer than something good. They will also be more likely to share a bad experience than a good one.

Your customers may not be as technically savvy as you are. Yes, there are actually people who still use dial-up with 56K. There are people out who still use 14" monitors and there are people out who have never made an online purchase in their lives. Remember these people and don't leave them behind when designing your site. If you do, you are alienating a huge percentage of the population.

Don't worry: Satisfying customers is not hard, but a little effort can go a long way. We've included some general guidelines to follow. After reading this, you may think, "Well, duh, kidding" but you'd be surprised by how many big, well-known companies don't follow.

# Information Is Everything

Your customers have to get as much information as possible about your product because they can't actually see, feel, touch, and smell what you have to offer. Your site is window to your customers, whatever blanks you leave in your product description, policies, company history, or checkout process will have to be filled in the customer's imagination. That may be in certain circumstances, you do not want your customers to make incorrect assumptions that leave them dissatisfied after the fact, or their uncertainty prevent the sale altogether.

Besides textual information, graphics are a very important part of the sale. There is a fine balance between adding too many graphics to your site, which could cause your patients to wait longer than they need to, and providing high-quality pictures so they can actually see what they're getting.
