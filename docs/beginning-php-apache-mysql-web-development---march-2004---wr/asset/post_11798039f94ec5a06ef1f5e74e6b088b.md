As long as you have keywords, you do a search. In the following SQL statement, you'll notice the MATCH and AGAINST keywords. This is the syntax used to do a text search in those fields. They must be full-text indexed fields in order to perform this search. Visit www.mysql.com/doc/en/fulltext_html for more information on full-text indexed.

```php
$result = NULL;
if (isset($_GET['keywords'])) {
  $sql = "SELECT article_id FROM cms_articles *
  WHERE MATCH (title,body) \
  AGAINST ('*" . $_GET['keywords'] . '\*\') \
  ORDER BY MATCH (title,body) \
  AGAINST ('*" . $_GET['keywords'] . '\*\') DESC";
```

```php
$result = mysql_query($sql, $conn)
or die("Could not perform search: " . $mysql_error());
```

If you didn't find a match, so:

```php
echo "<h1>Search Results</h1>";

if (!$result || mysql_num_rows($result)) {
  echo "<p>No articles found that match the search terms.</p>";
}
```

Otherwise, loop the results and display them as snippets on the screen:

```php
} else {
```

# Mailing Lists

Ah, yes. The mailing list. Two simple words that never meant harm. That is, until a few years, when someone decided to put the two together, and junk mail was born. Or sure, mailing lists are used for more than junk mail. After all, how are you going to receive your magazine unless your name and address are on a list? What does this have to do with PHP? In the world of e-mail and your site, it is a perfect way for you to communicate with all of your users!

Specifically, we are going to discuss the following:

- Administering a mailing list
- Creating a mailing list
- Spam
- Opt-in and opt-out

So, are you ready to learn how to spam your Web site users? We hope not, because that is not what we are going to do. Yes, we are going to use mailing lists. And, we are going to send out “mass” e-mails. However, we are going to be responsible, and send e-mails only to those who have agreed to be recipients. Right? Let's get started.

# First Things First

Before you create the mailing list, you must have something that you intend to send to your recipients. The train of thought usually goes like this: You decide that you have some information you want to share with your users. This is a newsletter, information about the next comic convention, or Web site updates they may want to know. You format the information you wish to share, using plain text or HTML. You try to figure out how you are going to e-mail this information to member of your Web site. If you can figure out how to send it, you hope that your users will be able to read and appreciate what you sent with their many types of e-mail.
