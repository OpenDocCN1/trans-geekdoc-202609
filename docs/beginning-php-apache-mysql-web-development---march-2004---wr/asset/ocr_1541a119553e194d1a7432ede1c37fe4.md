# An Introduction to PEAR

PHP is a terrific scripting language. It is relatively easy to learn, especially if you already know other programming languages such as JavaScript, C++, or Perl. In no time at all, you can get some excellent pages to walks your boss, on your server, accessing databases, authenticating users, and providing dynamic, up-to-date content for your visitors.

So, you just spent six months creating your company's Web site. It's nearly perfect—users are being served up-to-the-minute content, and you have set up a complex Content Management System that enables almost anyone in the company to create new content. It's pretty, and you feel pretty damned good about it.

As you sit here through the appendices, wondering if there are any more useful nuggets of information, in walks your boss, the IT Manager. She tells you once again what a fine job you did, congrats on the promotion—you know the usual. As she gets to leave, she stops in the doorway and casually mentions something that is going to completely overload your work schedule:

> Oh, by the way, HR is switching to an Oracle-based database accounting package. It's pretty slick. And we decided that we'll be using Oracle anyway, all of our databases will be switching to Oracle. That won't be a problem, will it?

Of course, the problem here comes from the way PHP accesses databases. The wonderful thing about PHP is that it supports a wide variety of databases. The bad thing is that it database-specific commands for each one.

Every developer has had this thing, so you are not alone. It might not be too difficult to change your code, but it will probably be time consuming, especially if you have hundreds of pages to update.

+   - MySQL: mysql_query("SELECT * FROM table")
- Microsoft SQL: mssql_query("SELECT * FROM table")
- FrontBase: fbsql_query("SELECT * FROM table")
