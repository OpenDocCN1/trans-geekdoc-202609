# 11.1.2 Transformer-based models

Transformer models are initially framed to focus on the natural language processing. The drastic advancements in the transformer led to the deployment of transformer models in the field of computer vision [5] for detection of cancer diseases in different organs of humans. Transformer-based models are capable of performing distinct tasks including object classification, image enhancement, segmentation, localization, integration of images, and caption assignment. The distinct transformer models performs well in comparison with the existing state-of-the-art models for image classification.

Vision transformers provide higher accuracy in classification, but are in need larger datasets for attaining the desired accuracy and struggle to detect the lung cancer in the case of small datasets. Combination of convolutional layers with vision transformers reduces the parameters and time required for training and detection [6]. Simultaneous processing of convolutional neural networks and vision transformer models offers expected performance and reduces the computational complexity while dealing with vision transformers [6].

Pre-trained vision transformers classify lung diseases with immense accuracy in comparison with hybrid models combining vision transformers and the prevailing VGG16 convolutional neural networks [7]. Even though the deep convolutional neural networks provide accurate results in combination with vision transformers, the pre-trained vision transformer model outperforms the hybrid CNN models.

Optimal vision transformer models use image preprocessing techniques including the Gaussian filtering and mean filtering to extract attributes by eliminating noise and artifacts in the input image [8]. Hybrid Walsh-Hadamard for drawing the attributes, seat algorithm for segmenting the preprocessed image, and classification through optimal vision-based transformer models offer 99% accuracy in the classification of skin cancers [8].
