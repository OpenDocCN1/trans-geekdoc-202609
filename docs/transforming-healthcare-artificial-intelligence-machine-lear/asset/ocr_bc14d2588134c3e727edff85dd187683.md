# 4.5.2 Algorithms

ML models have shown significant promise in predicting malnutrition and related health conditions. These models are capable of processing massive volumes of data and spotting intricate patterns that conventional statistical techniques might miss. Examples of ML algorithms that are frequently used in this discipline are NN, decision trees, and random forests. These algorithms predict a child's nutritional status based on multiple variables, such as weight, height, age, and socioeconomic factors. Furthermore, at-risk individuals who may not exhibit obvious symptoms of malnutrition are headed for bad health outcomes that can be found using ML algorithms. Early identification allows for timely interventions, such as providing nutritional supplements or implementing public health campaigns to promote healthy eating habits. However, the success of these models largely depends on the quality and diversity of the datasets used. As previously mentioned, models trained on region-specific datasets may not generalize to other populations. To guarantee that ML models may be successfully deployed in a variety of scenarios, researchers must place high priority on the acquisition and utilization of high-quality, diverse datasets.

The surveyed use a range of ML techniques, with the commonly ones being Random Forest, Logistic Regression, and Decision Trees. In particular, Random Forest appears as a powerful tool across several studies for predicting childhood malnutrition, stunting, and obesity, primarily due to its ability to handle large datasets with complex variables. XGBoost, another commonly used algorithm, demonstrates high accuracy and efficiency in handling both small and large datasets, making it a popular choice for studies focused on health outcome predictions.
