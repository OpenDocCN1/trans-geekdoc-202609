# 7.1 INTRODUCTION

An increase in blood glucose levels is the cause of diabetes. Long-term persistence of this anomaly will lead to irreparable damage to the blood vessels. Diabetes also increases the risk of renal failure, eyesight loss, bleeding gums, lower limb amputation, foot sores, and nerve damage. Patients with diabetes have an increased risk of heart attack and stroke. Diabetic Nephropathy, which affects the kidneys, Diabetic Neuropathy, and Diabetic Retinopathy (DR), which affects the retina, are the names given to these disorders on the areas of the body that are impacted by an increase in blood glucose levels [1,2].

Treating a disease under early predictions is most efficacious in healthcare. Among different health problems, eye ailments are expanding nowadays, and numerous individuals are affected by visual deficiency. So, it is more advisable for the diabetic patients to make periodic eye checks, monitoring the functions of retina regularly. optical coherence tomography (OCT), fundus fluorescence angiography (FFA), and fundus photography are few testing techniques. DR is caused by a lesion that causes blood leakage in retinal vessels, which occurs mainly in diabetic patients due to elevation of blood sugar levels. Further visualization can also detect stroke, which happens in the part of the brain due to sudden stop of blood supply. Inspecting the eye retina helps to diagnose stroke. Also, glaucoma is an example of the Fundus disease, which is caused by high pressure of fluids in eye interior resulting in the absence of the peripheral leading to no vision in patients.
