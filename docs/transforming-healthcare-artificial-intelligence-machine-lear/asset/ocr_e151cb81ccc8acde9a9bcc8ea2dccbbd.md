Access to healthcare was another major challenge. Patients in rural and remote areas often had to travel long distances to receive care, while the shortage of healthcare workers in many regions further exacerbated the problem. Furthermore, the traditional system struggled to cope with chronic disease management, which requires continuous monitoring and early interventions—something that was difficult to achieve with limited data and manual processes.

The system required an overhaul, that would integrate technology to enhance efficiency, accessibility, and the quality of care.

# 17.1.3 The need for technological transformation

The advent of digital technology promised to address these longstanding challenges by revolutionizing healthcare at its core. Artificial intelligence (AI), 5G networks, cloud computing, and other enabling technologies offered new possibilities for transforming the healthcare landscape. These innovations allowed for a shift from reactive to proactive care, improved the accuracy and speed of diagnostics, and enhanced patient engagement through personalized treatment plans.

Digital technologies, such as AI, provide the ability to process large volumes of patient data to uncover insights that are beyond human capacity, while 5G networks enable seamless real-time communication, making remote care, telemedicine, and wearable health monitoring deis reality. Cloud computing, on the other hand, offers scalable solutions for data storage, management, and collaboration, ensuring that healthcare providers can access and share critical patient information across different locations.
