External or natural sources, including hair, distracting items, lighting,
shadows, and different image capture techniques, are frequently sources
of noise and artifacts. The Gaussian filter is used in images to eliminate
Gaussian-weighted average values of nearby or surrounding pixels. One
linear filter implemented to minimise noise and enhance image quality is
the Gaussian filter. A softened image is produced by the Gaussian smoot-
ing, and the Gaussian distribution's standard deviation dictates the
deree of smoothing [28].

Due to the huge size and excellent quality of the images (768×560 pixels
with the RGB colour scheme), processing can be computationally intensive.
The pixels are shrunk to a resolution of 52×52 pixels while maintaining
all of the important details to speed up calculation. The bigger image size is
ubjected to the Gaussian filter. Unwanted components like hair and stray
spots can be eliminated using the Gaussian filter by substituting incorrect
pixels with the average of nearby pixels. The selection procedure uses three
images—three malignant and three benign. Equation (18.1) illustrates how
the Gaussian equation enhances photos by removing noise and unwanted
artifacts.

```
$G(p,q) = \frac{1}{2\pi\omega^2} e^{\frac{p^2-q^2}{2\omega^2}} (18.1)$
```

where $p$ represents the distance from the 0, $q$ is the distance along the $x
axis, and $\omega$ is the standard deviation of the Gaussian distribution.
