# 16.3.3 Introduction to 5G applications in healthcare

With the emergence of 5G technology, healthcare providers have new chances for impacting patient care and service delivery. In contrast to the earlier mobile generations, 5G has ultra-low latency plus higher bandwidth, faster speed in data transfer, and can allow connectivity of many devices in a go. Such technical process enhances a variety of applications in healthcare that were previously limited by network constraints [13].

Telemedicine has received a big impetus with 5G, supporting the consultations between the patient and the doctor over a real-time, high-definition video. The improved connection via 5G would allow the performance of remote surgical operations by machinery with precision, based on the control of robots by experts from several miles away. This would be a great relief to patients living in rural or underserved areas without having to seek special care [14].

Apart from telemedicine, remote patient monitoring (RPM) is another area likely to be revolutionized by 5G. Wearable devices and IoMT sensors will be able to automatically monitor vitals of a patient at one time, transmitting huge bundles of data in real time to healthcare workers. The data is transacted in real time, making it possible to detect signs of trouble early enough for appropriate healthcare actions to be taken, hence improving patient outcomes [15].

# 16.4 TELEMEDINE AND REMOTE CONSULTATIONS

# 16.4.1 Growth of telemedicine during COVID-19

The COVID-19 outbreak has played the role of turning point for telemedicine, gradually steadily accelerating its momentum of adoptions across the globe. With lockdowns and social distancing in force, and healthcare facilities jammed to capacity, telemedicine is evidently the only way to deliver healthcare without an accompanying risk of added viral transmission [1]. With the outbreak of the pandemic, telradiology has emerged as an alternative process in rendering a safe form of consultation in person between patients and health providers. Real-time video consultations, remote diagnostics, and medication management can help continue health systems to function under increased resource strain by the platform [4]. It helped to shift tides of care absorption from exposure centres back to non-COVID-19-related chronic care, mental health, and non-emergent medical needs.
