## 15.1 INTRODUCTION

Over the past few decades, the telecommunications industry has experienced a profound transformation, propelled by the swift advancement of mobile network systems. From the early days of 1G analog networks to the advanced 6G networks of today, each generation has introduced new capabilities and enhanced the way we communicate. The introduction of 5G technology represents unparalleled speed, minimal latency, and the ability to connect a vast number of devices simultaneously. However, these advancements bring with them a set of new challenges, including increased network complexity, higher operational costs, and the need for real-time responsiveness. To address these challenges, the incorporation of machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) into mobile network systems has emerged as a transformative approach [1] (Table 15.1).

The evolution from 1G to 6G showcases the continuous advancements in mobile network technology, with each generation building on the previous to enhance communication capabilities. From basic voice calls in 1G to the high-speed, low-latency, and highly connected world of 5G, and the anticipated ultra-high-speed and AI-driven applications of 6G, these advancements have profoundly impacted how we communicate, work, and live.

ML and AI offer powerful tools for analyzing vast amounts of data, predicting network behavior, and making intelligent decisions. These enable mobile networks to operate more efficiently, reliably, and adaptively. One of the most significant advancements is the development of self-organizing networks (SON), which leverage AI to automatically manage and optimize network configurations without human intervention. This not only reduces operational costs but also enhances network reliability and performance.
