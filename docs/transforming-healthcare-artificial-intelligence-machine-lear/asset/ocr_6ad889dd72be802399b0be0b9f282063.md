Chiao-Yao Wang et al. in proposed paper introduce Programmable Gradient Information (PGI) to address data loss in deep learning models by ensuring complete input information for accurate gradient updates. It presents the Generalized Efficient Layer Aggregation Network (GELAN), which leverages PGI to enhance lightweight models' performance on object detection tasks. The results on the MS COCO dataset demonstrate GELAN's superior parameter utilization compared to state-of-the-art methods and the potential for PGI to improve both lightweight and large models [7].

It proposes an innovative method for real-time PPE identification in industrial and construction environments using YOLOv8, which replaces conventional sensor-based systems with an affordable CV solution. In this technique, Barbabyev uses the Safety HELmet (HEL5K) and Color Helmet and Vest (CHV) datasets, which contain 5,000 pictures and eight object types. Strong performance was shown by the YOLOv8x YOLOv8 models, specifically in identifying helmets and vests, improving worker safety on construction sites [8].

In order to address ongoing difficulties despite current rules, Zhao and associates introduce an intelligent detection approach that uses a few-shot graph neural network (GNN) to monitor PPE use for electrical workers. The model incorporates privacy and label preservation, and it obtains a similarity probability of 79%–100% when only 50 types per PPE were used for training. By setting off alarms for noncompliance with PPE rules, it seeks to improve safety [9].

Sasha Bash et al. proposed YOLOv5-M, a modified version of YOLOv5, for detecting PPE and face masks, demonstrating superior performance over state-of-the-art detectors like Faster-RCNN, SSD, YOLOv3, and YOLOv5 on the CPE-5 dataset. It also performs effectively on face mask detection tasks and includes features for calculating distance among healthcare workers. This system has the potential for real-time implementation in healthcare settings to ensure COVID-19 safety measures [10].

Arfan utilized a dataset from 132 construction sites to examine a PPE detection system using the SSD algorithm, achievingAP50(B) of 0.768, mAP95(B) of 0.831, and recall(B) of 0.693. The system performs satisfactorily in identifying PPE, but it needs to be improved with scale, complicated surroundings, and fluctuations in picture capture. The research advances PPE detection systems, improving worker safety and health across all of industries [11].

With an average precision of 91.7% on the CHV datasets, Tasfia Mohona proposed the YOLO algorithm—specifically YOLO8—which is used in this paper to present a vision-based PPE detection system. The technology overcomes the drawbacks of human inspection and conventional vision-based techniques in difficult situations such as dim or remote locations. The YOLOv8 models performed better in term of speed, efficacy, and accuracy were tested on PPE detection average precision, recall, F1-score, and training speed [12].
