# 16.5.3 Continuous monitoring and early detection

Continuous monitoring and early detection take the lead in the 5G-enabled future of healthcare [3], 5G enabling seamless real-transmission of data from connected medical devices at all times, significantly improves the monitor-ing by healthcare providers to identify health-related issues at their onset. Seamless data transmission for wearables and sensors is facilitated by devices smart, glucose monitors, ECG sensors, and blood pressure cuffs, which vital health data continuously. With 5G technology’s ultra-low latency and high bandwidth, data is transmitted to healthcare providers without interruption. This allows for a comprehensive overview of various aspects, which can be utilized for timely interventions.

Thanks to uninterrupted monitoring empowered by 5G, healthcare providers will be able to identify non-standard patterns or fluctuations in health metrics at a very early stage. For instance, wearable technology can detect irregular heart rhythms, high blood pressure, or atypical levels of glucose, automatically alerting medical staff. Such premature identification allows to provide medical care that may prevent complications and even hospitalizations.

For patients with chronic illnesses like diabetes, myocardial infarction, or respiratory disorders, continuous surveillance is of paramount importance. The province of 24/7 health metric monitoring by 5G-powered IoT devices enables physicians to update treatment plans at any time based on current data [1,3,4]. This proactive approach in managing chronic diseases helps to avoid disease progression and enhances patient outcomes. RPM is made achievable from distant locations by healthcare providers, enabling them to keep tabs on patients’ conditions, particularly in rural areas or underserved regions. Real-time data transmission allows doctors to assess patients’ conditions without in-person visits, ensuring continuity of care despite geographical barriers. An ideal example would be post-surgery monitoring or for patients with limited mobility.
