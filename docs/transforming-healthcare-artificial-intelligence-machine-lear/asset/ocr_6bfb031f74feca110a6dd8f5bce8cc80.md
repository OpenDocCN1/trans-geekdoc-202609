# 18.5 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE

As a result, this study shows the accuracy with which the proposed method—combines sophisticated preprocessing, segmentation, and classification techniques—can identify skin cancer. The Cross Attention-U-Net method effectively identifies non-target regions, while the Gaussian filter improves raw images by eliminating spots and hairs. The GLCM technique improves classification accuracy by extracting the most discriminatory characteristics. The AWSO solves the feature dimensionality problem. Based on the ISIC dataset, the Hybrid Attention DCNN-BiLSTM model performs exceptionally well classifying skin cancer from input samples. The model achieves 95%, 93%, 92%, and 94% in F1-score, precision, accuracy, and recall, outperforming previous methods in these demands. These demonstrate the exceptional capacity to compromise recall and precision, which presents a significant opportunity for clinical melanoma detection applications. To improve the model's performance, future research will concentrate on fine-tuning its parameters and investigating fresh datasets for broader applicability. This work offers a good starting point for creating more precise and effective skin cancer detection techniques.
