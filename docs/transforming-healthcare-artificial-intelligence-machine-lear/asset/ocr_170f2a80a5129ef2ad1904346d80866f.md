# 12.1 INTRODUCTION

Computer vision has influenced the global shift towards information societies since the late 20th century. Computer vision is always evolving due to the introduction of newer, enhanced, targeted, and sophisticated technologies. Computer vision tools are starting to become essential for everyday life and all human undertakings. Most sectors, including the socioeconomic, political, educational, medical, and healthcare sectors, are integrating state-of-the-art computer vision tools developed by the industries into their systems and procedures get solutions tailored to their needs. When wireless and sensor technologies are combined, a patient with a disability can now perform at a higher level or maintain or improve upon it. One of assistive technology that enables people with motor disabilities to operate with an operator is an autonomous robotic hand. In 1961, Unimate unveiled the first industrial robotic arm, which later developed into the PUMA arm. arms find application in many different industries, including the transportation of items and the movement, and assembly of parts in factories. Applications for robotic hands in healthcare include endoscopic treatments, surgery, and physical rehabilitation. Many studies have been carried out in an effort to create a robotic hand with intelligence for various uses.

A ten-degree-of-freedom anthropomorphic robotic hand with positive-negative actuation and a hybrid soft-rigid structure was proposed by researchers [1]. One studied the design methodology for a unique robotic hand and provided experimental data to support the hand's overall functionality [2]. A novel method combining robotics with the Internet of Things was demonstrated by researchers. In order to operate a Six-DOF arm, this device places four sensors uniquely upon the fingers with the greatest flexibility on both hands.
