# 5.1 INTRODUCTION

The knee is an intricate joint that functions similarly to a door hinge, enabling flexion and extension of the leg for sitting, squatting jumping, and running. The four parts of the knee are cartilage, ligaments, tendons, and bones. At the apex of the knee joint is the femur, also referred to as the thighbone [1]. The bottom of the knee joint is the tibia or shinbone. The place where the femur and tibia be met by the patella and kneecap. The substance that protects the bones in the knee and helps ligaments glide over while shielding the bones from shock is called cartilage. The knee is made up of four ligaments that stabilize and keep the bones together like ropes. The tibia (shinbone), patella (kneecap), and femur (thighbone) combine to form the knee joint. Tendons attach the bones in the upper and lower legs to the muscles knee joint. In order to offer some cushioning, the kneecap rests in front of the knee. Ligaments bind adjacent bones. To keep the bones connected and maintain the stability of our knee, they function as sturdy ropes.

A frequent issue that impacts any of generation is knee discomfort. It could be the outcome of an injury like a torn cartilage or a damaged ligament. Infectious diseases, gout, and arthritis are the medical disorders that matter due to its complexity and multitude of parts, the knee is susceptible to a wide range of ailments. Trauma to one or more of the tissues that comprise the knee joint is referred to as a knee injury. These kinds of ailments result from falls, violent knee twists, high impacts from auto accidents, or other forces [2]. Bruising and pain are the most typical indications of a knee injury. Furthermore, the knee could buckle or freeze. An unstable knee can give us the impression that our knee is popping out.
