i. **Input Layer:** Here, the input images could be varied in size, yet, it is standardized to fixed measurements like 257×257 or 353×353 pixels to fill up the network structure. The input is shown in RGB with three color channels. This resizes the input reliably in all samples without deteriorating spatial resolution requirements for pose estimation.

ii. **Backbone Network:** The backbone of PoseNet is based on MobileNet. MobileNet is a highly efficient and lightweight CNN for low-resource environments, such as mobile and edge devices. The encoder of PoseNet is made up of several layers of depthwise convolutions, followed by batch normalization and ReLU activations. Depthwise separable convolutions dramatically reduced the complexity of the model while preserving critical spatial features, leading to high-level feature maps from the input images.

iii. **Head Keypoints:** This is an output layer that predicts the locations of keypoints in an image. The output is represented as heatmaps, which are essentially probability distributions over the possible locations of keypoint. These heatmaps are directly derived from high-level feature maps produced by the backbone network. This is called heatmap regression, where the network connects offsets for every keypoint to refine their location. By regressing these offsets, the model's accuracy is well established at the pixel level, hence improving the precision of keypoints by a notable margin.

iv. **Output Layer:** The final output layer renders the predicted coordinates for each keypoint with an associated confidence score. The coordinates are the x and y locations for the keypoint in the image, while the confidence score is the keypoint likelihood of being found in that location.

v. **Post-Processing:** Applications of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) are made to eliminate the redundant keypoints and overlapping detections. NMS suppresses the lower-confidence detections and retains only the highest-probability set of keypoints related to each person detected in the image. This filter will ensure that the final keypoint predictions are not only non-redundant as well.

# 13.3 DATASET DESCRIPTION

The dataset is obtained by using a live camera and consists of various yoga asanas by human actors at different places and times. Such a dataset is labeled meticulously to ensure better classification and correction by the model. The provided datasets serve as the foundation for training the model in real-time pose estimation, classification, and feedback mechanisms. They encompass six yoga postures: Mountain Pose (Tadasana), Tree Pose (Vrikshasana), Downward Dog (Adho Mukha Savasana), Warrior I (Virabhadrasana I), Warrior II (Virabhadrasana II), and Chair Pose (Utkatasana).
