## 3.7.3 Data augmentation

When obtaining high-quality data is difficult in any machine learning application, data augmentation extends its reach. It can also aid in enhancing the performance and resilience of models in all academic domains. Model reproducibility can be increased because the model can perform better on bright images rather than dark images, so we can collect more low-light training data.

Since augmentations are CPU-constrained processes, training time can be reduced. Since augmentations are CPU-limited processes, less is spent on training. To deal with an imbalanced dataset, several methods have been put forth to address the unbalanced dataset issue, which may be divided into two groups: developing resampling algorithms and preprocessing data to provide new samples [22]. One technique for balancing a dataset's distribution is to resample. To do this, samples are either oversampled in the minority class, which has a smaller number of data, or undersampled into the majority class, where the number of data is more in order to balance the data. Nonetheless, a popular method for creating free data samples to supply the image in a different format is called data augmentation.

The process of applying augmentations to already-existing photos in our dataset is called image augmentation. By going through this procedure, the model will be able to generalize and perform better on unknown pictures.

- 1. Geometric Transformations: These randomly flip, chop, rotate, stretch, and zoom photos using geometric transformations. We need to apply many changes carefully to the same picture because it may cause the model to perform less well.
- 2. Color Space Transformations: These randomly alter the brightness, contrast, and RGB color channels
- 3. Kernel Filters: These help to arbitrarily alter the image's sharpness or blurriness
- 4. Random Erasing: This removes a fragment of the original picture.
- 5. Mixing Images: This combines and integrates separate photos.

## 7.4 PROPOSED COMBINED CAE AND CNN FRAMEWORK

In our proposed framework, we have combined CAE and CNN models. In our framework, the CAEs are used for:

- Image Denoising: By learning to rebuild the original picture from its noisy counterpart, CAEs are used to remove noise from images. In order to train the network to rebuild the clean picture from its noisy counterpart, pairs of clean and noisy images are used. The model may be used to denoise any fresh image after it has been trained.
- Dimensionality Reduction: With CAEs, an image's dimensionality may be decreased without sacrificing any of its key characteristics. This may help lower the amount of storage and processing power needed to process photos. A sizable picture dataset may be utilized for training the model, which can be used further to extract characteristics from fresh photos.
- Reconstruction: Images that are missing, blurry, partially or completely corrupted can be restored using CAEs. This is due to the model's ability to extract characteristics that are resistant to these kinds of distortions and yet allow for the reconstruction of a recognizable image.

The classification procedure is carried out by CNN using the features that were extracted from the CAE. Figure 7.4 shows the structure of our proposed CAE and CNN model. The CAE extracts features and reduces data. It reduces the amount of data while keeping all the important elements. An image dataset is compressed using consecutive convolutions. The lowest feasible dataset representation, known as the Bottleneck layer, receives the output next.

## 7.4.1 Dataset description

In our research, we have used the APTOS 2019 dataset for DR detection and prediction. The 3,662 samples in the Asia Pacific Tele-Ophthalmology Society 2019 Blindness Detection (APTOS 2019 BD) collection were arranged by India's Aravind Eye Hospital. The fundus images were taken in various settings and from several rural India participants. Later, a trained medical professionals examined them. They identified the samples using the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy Severity Scale (ICDRSS). The APTOS 2019 BD [23] samples have been categorized into five groups according to the system: no DR, mild DR, moderate DR, severe DR, and proliferative DR, which are shown in Table 7.2, and its visual representation is shown in Figure 7.5.
