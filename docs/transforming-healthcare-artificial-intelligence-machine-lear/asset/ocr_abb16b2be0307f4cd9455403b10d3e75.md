# 4.5.3.1 The importance of early detection of malnutrition, stunting, and obesity

Obesity, stunting, and malnutrition are major public health concerns, particularly in low- and middle-income nations, even if they impact wealthier countries. Approximately 45% of all fatalities in children under five are attributed to malnutrition, making it one of the world's top causes of child mortality. Chronic malnutrition stunting impairs both physical and cognitive growth. Conversely, obesity raises the risk of chronic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disorders.

Identifying children who are at risk in their development is one of the main objectives of predictive modeling in the field of child nutrition research. Early detection is crucial since interventions carried out within the first 1000 days of life (from conception to the child's second birthday) can significantly affect growth and cognitive development. The impacts of stunting or starvation are even harder to recover after this critical window.

The primary objective of many studies that employ predictive models to identify children who are at risk of malnutrition or stunting based on critical characteristics such as weight, height, BMI, and age. These markers offer crucial information about the general health and nutritional state of a child. Researchers can develop models that predict a child's risk of malnutrition, stunting, or obesity by integrating these physical parameters with demographic data, including socioeconomic status, geographic location, and access to healthcare.

# 4.5.3.2 Predicting child mortality and developmental outcomes

Numerous studies go beyond simply identifying children who are malnourished to include the prediction of future developmental outcomes and child death. Child mortality is strongly correlated with malnutrition, especially in its acute and severe forms. Due to their compromised immune systems' inability to successfully fight infections, children who are wasted—that suffering from malnutrition—have a significantly increased risk of dying from illnesses including pneumonia, diarrhea, and malaria.

Child mortality can be estimated via predictive models that incorporate rate to malnutrition. Healthcare professionals can therefore give high-risk situations priority and make sure that children in critical condition get life-saving interventions as soon as possible. Furthermore, children's long-term developmental outcomes can be estimated using predictive models. For example, poor cognitive development is frequently associated with chronic malnutrition, as evidenced by stunting, and this can have an impact on a child's school attainment and future output. Early prediction of these outcomes allows treatments to be designed to enhance a child's cognitive and educational development in addition to improving their nutritional health.
