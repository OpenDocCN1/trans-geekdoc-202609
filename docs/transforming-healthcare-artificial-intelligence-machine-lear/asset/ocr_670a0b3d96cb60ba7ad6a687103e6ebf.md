# 48 Transforming Healthcare

Step-1: Prepare the patient physically and psychologically
Step-2: Prepare skin appropriately for electrode placement
Step-3: Place the ECG electrodes at appropriate places
Step-4: Connect the lead wires to the ECG connector and check whether the waveform is visible on the monitor
Step-5: Ensure all monitor alarms are on and audible
Step-6: Do frequent checks on the ECG electrodes and monitoring setup
Step-7: Replace the electrodes at least daily, or more frequently as required.

A typical ECG waveform consists of five types of waves such as:

+   1. P-wave
2. T-wave
3. Q-wave
4. R-wave
5. S-wave.

Figure 2.8 shows the illustration of a typical ECG waveform along with its components.
Various intervals from the ECG waveform are used for diagnosis of various diseases:

+   1. RR-Interval: It is used to identify the period of an ECG signal and it denotes the time interval between two adjacent R-waves. If it is identified as irregular, it is an indication of heart disease as arrhythmia.
2. PR-Interval: It measures the time interval between the starting of P-wave and that of the QRS-complex. It is an indication of the time for the impulse to reach the ventricles from the sinus node.
3. QT-Interval: It represents the time interval between the beginning of a Q-wave and the end of a T-wave. If the interval gets exceeded from the normal value, there is an increased possibility of cardiac death.
