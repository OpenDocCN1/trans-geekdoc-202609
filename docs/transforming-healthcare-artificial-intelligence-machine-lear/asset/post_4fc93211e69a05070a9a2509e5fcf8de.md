## 2.4.1.2 Body blood pressure (BP)

Blood pressure (BP) is the force of blood against the body's blood vessel walls as blood flows through the circulatory system of the human body. BP rises or lowers throughout the day, and this variation is based on our activities. But we need to know the reason for any fluctuations or abnormalities (High BP/Low BP) that tell us the health conditions. Generally, BP varies based on eating habits, stress levels, or physical tasks. To maintain normal BP levels, it is advised to go for a disciplined lifestyle and proper medication by physicians.

Measurement of blood pressure is represented by two numbers such as systolic pressure (top number) and diastolic pressure (bottom number). Systolic pressure is the pressure in the arteries when the heart beats, whereas the diastolic pressure is the pressure when the heart rests between beats. Table 2.2 shows different values of systolic and diastolic pressure for different BP levels.

As the physician's advice, normal BP readings are below 120/80 mmHg (Systolic) and above 90/60 mmHg (Diastolic). If the BP readings are outside the mentioned range of values, it indicates high BP (also called hypertension) or low BP (also called hypotension). Even though both values are important, systolic pressure is more important since it causes the heart to function harder than normal functioning. Arteries extend the left ventricle of the heart, and lead to stroke and cardiac arrest. When it further elevated, it comes into contact with blood vessels and disturbs oxygen flow. It will further lead to eye blindness and kidney failure.

Table 2.3 shows the categories of high blood pressure along with their corresponding systolic and diastolic values.

| Blood pressure category | Systolic value (top number), mmHg | Diastolic value (bottom number), mmHg |
|---|---|---|
| Low | <90 | <60 |
| Normal | <120 | <80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | <80 |
| High | 130–139 | 80–89 |
| High | >140 | >90 |
| Hypertensive crisis | >180 | >120 |

From the above table, as per the physician's advice, it is observed that the blood pressure is 132/60, it is considered as Hypertension (Stage 1) since the top number lies in the range (130–139) mmHg.

High blood pressure cannot be identified by the symptoms, hence it is said to be asymptomatic one. Since it does not cause symptoms or indications, it will further lead to serious health issues as stroke or cardiac arrest. Due to this reason, high blood pressure is also mentioned as a silent killer. There will be some side effects pointed by the physicians, which are given below:

- 1. Severe headache
- 2. Dizziness or giddiness
- 3. Paleness in face
- 4. Chest pain
- 5. Nose bleeding
- 6. Breathing trouble
- 7. Irregular heartbeat
- 8. Blood in urine.

If any of the above symptoms are observed by the patient at their home, they are further advised to have interaction or meeting with a physician for further testing or treatment. They provided risk factors and common causes of high blood pressure for prevention. They are:

- 1. Obesity
- 2. Diabetic history
- 3. Sedentary lifestyle
- 4. Poor dieting
- 5. Smoking
- 6. Alcohol drinking
- 7. Old ageing
- 8. Family history of high BP.

Similar to high blood pressure, it also points out the causes, symptoms, and advice for low blood pressure. When the blood pressure is 90/60, it is considered as low blood pressure. It can also affect the body. Some of the common symptoms observed from the patients are:

- 1. Body weakness
- 2. Dizziness/giddiness
- 3. Blurred vision
- 4. Nausea
- 5. Tiredness
- 6. Pale and sweating in skin
- 7. Fast and deep breathing
- 8. Stress.

Some of the common risk factors for getting low BP or hypertension are given below:

- 1. Diabetic
- 2. Older ageing
- 3. Having heart problems
- 4. Having liver oriented diseases
- 5. During pregnancy
- 6. Having hormonal imbalances
- 7. Having nutritional deficiencies
- 8. Alcohol consumption.

Over the effects of having hypotension, it is recommended to follow the tips given by the physicians. Some of them are:

- 1. Drink plenty of water
- 2. Add more salt intake, if necessary
- 3. Avoid alcohol
- 4. Avoid standing for a longer time
- 5. Have frequent intake of minimum quantity of meals.
