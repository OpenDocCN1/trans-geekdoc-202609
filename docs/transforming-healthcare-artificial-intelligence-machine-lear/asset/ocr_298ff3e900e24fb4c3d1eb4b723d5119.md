# 16.5.2 Real-time data transmission with 5G

The real significance of integrating 5G technology with healthcare is that there is real-time transmission of data, which is a vital component of the IoMT. Unlike its predecessors, 5G as the IMT2020 standard includes ultra-low latency, often less than one millisecond and high bandwidth, to guarantee instantaneous and reliable transmission of enormous volumes of data [1]. The potential of providing fast information opens new applications for patient care and remote monitoring. Figure 16.4 shows key aspects of real-time data transmission with 5G in healthcare.

+   1. Continuous Patient Monitoring: Wearable devices and smart sensors produce enormous data on the patient, such as vital signs, levels of activity, and other metrics of health. 5G facilitates the real-time transmission of data to healthcare providers, ensuring continuous monitoring of the health status of the patients—particularly those with chronic conditions, in real time, such as heart disease, diabetes, or respiratory insufficiency [3].

+   2. Immediate Medical Interventions: Now, with data being transmitted almost in real time, health professionals will quickly identify abnormal readings or the early signs of active or impeding medical emergencies. For example, if the heart rate or oxygen level of a patient drops suddenly, an alert could be sent to medical staff and immediate intervention initiatedpotentially saving lives [4].
