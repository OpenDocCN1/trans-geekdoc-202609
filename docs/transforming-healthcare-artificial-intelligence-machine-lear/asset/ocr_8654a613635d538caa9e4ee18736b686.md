In similar skin cancer model where autoencoder was used, there was a dramatic improvement in training accuracy scores to over 99%, but test performance plateaued around 91%. It indicates that the model performs well in the derived data but cannot venture out and begin using all the sparse data. This could be attributed to the high degree of intra-heterogeneity of skin lesion images or the level of the automatically annotated relevant features elsewhere. Similar to the eye disease model using LSTM, the model also achieves a training accuracy of 99% and moderate performance on test data that stabilises around 89%. This lesser test performance may be due to the sequential data and difficulties in capturing temporal relationships between different features of eye diseases.

The osteoarthritis detection model obtained effective performance based on ResNet-50, where the proportion of correctly identified cases during training almost reached 100%. During the test, it stabilised at approximately 93%. Transfer learning in this model enables reasonably good generalised in various models. However, differences in training and testing performance still exist.
