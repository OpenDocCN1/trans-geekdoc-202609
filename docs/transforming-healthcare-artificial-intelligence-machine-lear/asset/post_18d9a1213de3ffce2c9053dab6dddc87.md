## Hybrid dense block convolutional transformers for cancer detection and severity analysis

Sharmila Arumugam, Tamilselvan Kumar Subramaniam, and Dhivya Priya Loganathan

### INTRODUCTION

Cancer is one of the most deadly diseases of the century. It occurs in almost all parts of the human body including brain, blood, skin, and stomach and affects humans irrespective of their ages. Detection of cancer in the initial stages may have probability of survival. Brain tumor is an imperative type of cancer that has both initial and cancerous stages and is difficult to detect in the initial stages. MRI, Computer-aided tomography, several machine learning (ML), transformer-based models and deep learning models, and combination of artificial intelligence (AI) techniques were employed in the detection of tumors in benign stages as it has higher probability of survival.

### Convolutional neural networks

Comparison between distinct convolutional neural networks is used to classify the breast cancer. The combination of attention mechanism with the DenseNet architecture surpasses other models of convolutional neural networks with the attention mechanism. The attention mechanism focuses mainly on the desired features and skips the attributes, which take less part in the classification, thereby attaining the desired performance.

### DenseNet

Gastrointestinal disease classification is performed by incorporating a lightweight transformer model consisting of residual convolutional blocks to extract the desired features, the transformer models to select the attributes required for classification, and attention models to induce channels and classification, which outperform the vision transformer models and deep convolutional network (CNN) models including efficient.

### DBCT for lung cancer detection & severity analysis

DenseNet, MobileNet, reset models, VGG models, and inception and GoogLeNet models. The lightweight transformer model consumes very less time and offers the higher accuracy than other image classification techniques.

### ResNet

Comparative study was conducted between the pre-trained ResNet-18 model of convolutional neural networks and the vision transformer model VIT-16 in predicting breast cancer. ResNet-16 outperforms the vision transformer. The vision transformer can be able to achieve the highest accuracy then ResNet-16 trained on large number of datasets.

### Transformer-based models

Transformer models are initially framed to focus on the natural language processing. The drastic advancements in the transformer led to the deployment of transformer models in the field of computer vision for detection of cancer diseases in different organs of humans. Transformer-based models are capable of performing distinct tasks including object classification, image enhancement, segmentation, localization, integration of images, and caption assignment. The distinct transformer models performs well in comparison with the existing state-of-the-art models for image classification.

Vision transformers provide higher accuracy in classification, but are in need larger datasets for attaining the desired accuracy and struggle to detect the lung cancer in the case of small datasets. Combination of convolutional layers with vision transformers reduces the parameters and time required for training and detection. Simultaneous processing of convolutional neural networks and vision transformer models offers expected performance and reduces the computational complexity while dealing with vision transformers.

Pre-trained vision transformers classify lung diseases with immense accuracy in comparison with hybrid models combining vision transformers and the prevailing VGG16 convolutional neural networks. Even though the deep convolutional neural networks provide accurate results in combination with vision transformers, the pre-trained vision transformer model outperforms the hybrid CNN models.

Optimal vision transformer models use image preprocessing techniques including the Gaussian filtering and mean filtering to extract attributes by eliminating noise and artifacts in the input image. Hybrid Walsh-Hadamard for drawing the attributes, seat algorithm for segmenting the preprocessed image, and classification through optimal vision-based transformer models offer 99% accuracy in the classification of skin cancers. Skin cancer detection using vision transformers model offers better accuracy with optimal parameters surpassing the existing models.
