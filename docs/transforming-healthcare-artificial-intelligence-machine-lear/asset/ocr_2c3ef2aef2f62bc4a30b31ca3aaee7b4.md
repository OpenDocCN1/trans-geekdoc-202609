With the XGBoost model, it is observed that the enhancement in training accuracy is lucid, and within a relatively short time, it is nearly 100% by the last epoch. However, the test accuracy remains stagnant to about 87%. This pattern indicates that the gradient boosting mechanism of XGBoost is very competent for pattern recognition. However, there are high variations in model training and test accuracies, meaning further improvements may be needed. In particular, stroke prediction is quite tricky because of the nature of risk factors, and this may require incorporating other, complex ensemble methods to improve the results.

The prediction model for eye disease using ML also demonstrated a similar pattern, with training accuracies almost reaching 100% and test accuracies at about 84%. The significant percentage difference in training and testing accuracies suggests that there is a possibility of overfitting, which may be attributed to the sequential or time structure of eye disease data. Measures such as temporal cross-validation, data augmentation, or recurrent models may improve generalisation to avoid such problems in later versions.
