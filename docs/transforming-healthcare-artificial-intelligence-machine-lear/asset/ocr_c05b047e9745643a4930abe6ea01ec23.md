# 4.5.3.3 Machine learning in predictive modeling

The application of ML for health-related predictions has grown significantly in the last few years. ML models are excellent at digesting massive datasets, finding patterns, and producing precise predictions. ML algorithms are trained on datasets containing a range of indicators related to malnutrition, stunting, and obesity, including anthropometric measurements (e.g., height, weight, and BMI), clinical information, socioeconomic factors, and even environmental influences (e.g., access to clean water and sanitation). Based on the patterns they find in the data, these algorithms then learn to categorize kids into various groups, such as low risk for stunting or high risk for malnutrition.

Several popular ML models are employed in studies on prediction health, such as:

+   **Decision Trees:** Because decision trees handle both numerical and categorical data and are simple to read, they are a common tool for predicting malnutrition. In order for these models to function, choices (like whether a child is malnourished) are divided into a set of inquiries depending on several factors (age, weight, height, and socioeconomic status).

+   **Random Forests:** Random forests, often utilized in health prediction models, are an extension of decision trees. To increase accuracy, they function by merging the predictions made by several decision trees. When working with huge datasets containing numerous variables, as is frequently the case in research predicting malnutrition, random forests are quite helpful.

Logistic regression is a statistical method commonly used to determine the probability of an event, such as whether or not a child would be underweight. When the result is binary (e.g., malnourished vs. not malnourished), it works very well. Another approach that works for classification is support vector machines (SVM). One example of a task that SVM is useful for determining if a child is in danger of stunting or not. The way it operates is by determining which hyperplane best divides data points from various classes. Traditional methods are not the only way that ML may be applied to predict malnutrition.
