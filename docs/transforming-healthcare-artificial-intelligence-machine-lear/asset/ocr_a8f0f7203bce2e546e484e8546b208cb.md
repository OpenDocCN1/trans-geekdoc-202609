# 3.6 CONCLUSION

AI and ML have profoundly changed the diagnostics and disease treatment world. The evolution of medicine towards precision, dynamism, and personal engagement has begun. As exhibited in the previous sections of this chapter, deep learning-based methods among others, these models have been effective in detecting multiple diseases, from autism to skin cancer and even cardiovascular and ocular diseases. They have led to increased diagnostic accuracy and improved timing and preparation of treatment, thus improving patients' lives. The benefit recognised as the most incredible about AI and ML in healthcare is that the amount of data available does not limit them; they collate and learn the regularities on their own and identify aspects which may have eluded the sight of the clinicians. This ability is helpful in a situation where a timely and correct diagnosis can mean success or failure in treating complicated and ambiguous illnesses. AI models are also capable of assimilating new information, and as a result, they make adjustments to evolutions in practice and breakthroughs in medical research. Nevertheless, even if progress is evident, there are still problems. Areas such as privacy, how the model is understood, or the fact that we need more diverse data for AI to be more effective are critical issues that require more work to improve their state.
