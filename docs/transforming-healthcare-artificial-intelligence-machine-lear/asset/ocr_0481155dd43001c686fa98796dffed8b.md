Advancements in technology have improved the precision, speed, and availability of diagnosis. Some specific modifications in the diagnostic instrument include:

+   - Radiology: Imaging techniques, such as ultrasound, MRI, CT, or positron emission tomography (PET) scans, are non-invasive techniques for visualising internal structures and diagnosing diseases. These techniques are vital in diagnosing cancer, heart disease, and neurological diseases.
- Molecular Diagnostics: Laboratory techniques for molecular disease diagnosis include PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction), next-generation sequencing (NGS), and microarrays for “fingerprint” diagnosis of diseases by specific genes, proteins, and other types of molecules. Molecular diagnostics is popularly used in personalised medicine, as it recognises oncogenes with DNA mutations in the case of cancer.
- Point-of-Care Testing (POCT): Portable and fast-diagnosing devices have equipped healthcare professionals to diagnose conditions at the patient’s bedside. POCT has revolutionised efficiency medicine, infection disease management, and health services in remote places.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML): The inclusion of AI’s symmetry in diagnostics is the same as that of the ability to do predictive analysis followed by detection of disease in its inception stages. AI can use a large pool of medical data, such as radiological images or patients’ histories, and has proven its worth in diagnostics, especially in radiology, dermatology, and pathology.

# 3.2.2 Key approaches to disease treatment

Pharmacological Treatments: Medications are one of the most utilised and efficient measures in managing various ailments. Modern drug inventions have created medicines that can intervene in specific pathways within certain diseases. For example, antibiotics might be possible to battle infectious bacterial pollution in the human body successfully. In contrast, antiviral pharmaceuticals improved the chances of patients suffering from illnesses such as HIV and hepatitis.
Surgical Interventions: Surgery involves the management of diseases other than physical disabilities; these diseases may include tumours, organ injuries, or other rebuffing problems. Developments in non-invasive procedures, like laparoscopic techniques and robotic-assisted operations, have decreased recovery periods and enhanced accuracy, thus obtaining great surgical results.
