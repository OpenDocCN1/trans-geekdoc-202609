# 2.4.1.2 Body blood pressure (BP)

Blood pressure (BP) is the force of blood against the body's blood vessel walls as blood flows through the circulatory system of the human body. BP rises or lowers throughout the day, and this variation is based on our activities. But we need to know the reason for any fluctuations or abnormalities (High BP/Low BP) that tell us the health conditions. Generally, BP varies based on eating habits, stress levels, or physical tasks. To maintain normal BP levels, it is advised to go for a disciplined lifestyle and proper medication by physicians.

Measurement of blood pressure is represented by two numbers such as systolic pressure (top number) and diastolic pressure (bottom number). Systolic pressure is the pressure in the arteries when the heart beats, whereas the diastolic pressure is the pressure when the heart rests between beats. Table 2.2 shows different values of systolic and diastolic pressure for different BP levels.

As the physician's advice, normal BP readings are below 120/80 mmHg (Systolic) and above 90/60mmHg (Diastolic). If the BP readings are outside the mentioned range of values, it indicates high BP (also called hypertension) or low BP (also called hypotension). Even though both values are important, systolic pressure is more important since it causes the heart to function harder than normal functioning. Arteries extend the left ventricle of the heart, and lead to stroke and cardiac arrest. When it further elevated, it comes into contact with blood vessels and disturbs oxygen flow. It will further lead to eye blindness and kidney failure
