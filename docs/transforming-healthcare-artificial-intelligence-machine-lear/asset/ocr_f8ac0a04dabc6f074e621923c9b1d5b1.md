# 16.5.4 Transmission of high-resolution medical data

It is regarding the ultra-connections and low delays of 5G, which enable the fast and efficient transmission of huge volumes of high-resolution medical data [4]. This kind of development in data transmission is redefining healthcare with numerous benefits in diagnostic and treatment planning that can be extended to e-consultation. Medical imaging modalities such as MRI, CT scans, X-rays, and ultrasounds large files critical in accurate disease diagnosis. With 5G tech, transmission of such high-res images to any consulting specialist is almost instant from wherever is located. The faster access to detailed images helps healthcare workers make more accurate and timely decisions in responding to medical cases.

Since 5G allows sharing high-resolution images and videos in real time, remote diagnostic support becomes significantly easier [12]. For instance, a rural health unit can upload detailed imaging to the cloud, from which specialists at urban centres can conduct a real-time view of the information. This is particularly beneficial for patients in remote areas, allowing for expert consultations without the need to travel. Telemedicine is also enhanced by 5G, facilitating the transfer of high-resolution videos during virtual consultations. Clinicians can share diagnostic visuals with high clarity, such as images of internal organs or lesions, leading to more accurate assessments and fostering patient-doctor interactions that closely resemble in-person consultations [7]. The delivery of 3D imaging and augmented reality in surgical planning involves complex high-resolution datasets. 5G networks can support this steering at the edge without any delays, allowing surgeons to review and interact with detailed anatomical models [21].

# 16.5.5 Integration with AI and machine learning

From a mere analysis of the presented data, 5G-AI and ML integration has paved the way for hi-fi diagnostic techniques and intrusive personalization plans. The ultra-reliable, low latency, and increased 5G network capacities create what infrastructure would support huge amounts of real-time data generated by a wide variety of healthcare devices and wearables in monitoring systems, thereby facilitating application of AI and ML [21].

Provided that 5G technology can support real-time data transmission by IoMT devices—including wearable health monitors, medical imaging equipment, and electronic health records—algorithms implemented in AI can also process output data in real time to identify patterns, anomalies, and precursors of diseases. For example, wearable devices for 5G can continuously monitor vital health parameters of a patient, transmitting this data over the 5G network to AI systems for analysis regarding indications of issues such as arrhythmia or hypertension.
