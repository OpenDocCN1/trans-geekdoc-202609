For obtaining an ECG waveform, the ECG electrodes should be accurately placed, which needs to follow some standard protocols or procedures. The most common locations in the human body for ECG electrodes are:

+   - Limbs: ECG electrodes are placed on the arms and legs, typically on the wrists and ankles. They can be represented as I, II, III, AVR, AVL, and AVF, and they can record electrical activity between the parts of limbs.
- Chest: Electrodes are placed on specific locations across the chest wall. They can capture electrical impulses travelling through different regions of heart and provide a comprehensive view of its activity.

ECG electrodes are of different colours, and each of them is identified by a specific code based on its placement. Table 2.5 shows the placement and colour of electrodes.

+   RA Placement: A white-coloured electrode is placed directly below the clavicle and near the right shoulder.
LA: A black-coloured electrode is placed directly below the clavicle and near the left shoulder.
RL Placement: A green-coloured electrode is placed on the right lower abdomen.
LL Placement: A red-coloured electrode is placed on the left lower abdomen.
V Placement: A brown-coloured electrode is placed on the chest.
