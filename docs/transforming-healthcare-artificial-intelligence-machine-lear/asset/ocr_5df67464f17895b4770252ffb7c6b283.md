# 4.1 INTRODUCTION

Childhood malnutrition is a major global health concern that has catastrophic effects on children's physical and mental development. The World Health Organization (WHO) states that malnutrition accounts for nearly 45% of all pediatric fatalities globally, making it a serious public health concern (Mahmood et al., 2022). Malnutrition is a worldwide problem, but it is more severe in low- and middle-income countries due to a number of social, cultural, environmental, and economic factors that contribute to its increased occurrence. The rate of malnutrition in India is startlingly high. Despite notable advancements in healthcare and economic prosperity, childhood malnutrition is still a problem. According to the Global Hunger Index (GHI) 2020, India is 94th out of 107 countries, indicating that the country still has major issues with malnutrition. The National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) shows that 35.5% of children under five (too short for their age) are wasted (too thin for their height), and 32.1% are underweight (too thin for their age) (Argwaw et al., 2023). These numbers highlight how malnutrition is complex and pervasive in India. The high rate of malnutrition in India is caused by several factors, such as low income, poor sanitation, little knowledge of appropriate child feeding practices, and restricted access to healthcare and nutrient-dense food. The COVID-19 epidemic has made matters worse by upsetting food supply networks, raising unemployment, and driving more families into poverty, all of which harm children's nutritional outcomes.

Children's malnutrition is a complex issue that is fueled by a wide range of variables, including cultural and environmental influences, poverty, poor access to nutrient-dense food, and inadequate healthcare. The interaction of these raises the danger of malnutrition and makes treatment more challenging. Due to restricted access to nourishing food and healthcare facilities, children from underprivileged populations in India—especially those living in rural areas—are disproportionately affected. Economic issues are a major contributor to the persistence of malnutrition.
