# 5.2 RELATED WORKS

Xue et al. developed a fully automated approach featuring a DL model for ACL tissue segmentation (ACL-DNet) and a classifier for ligament status sensitivity (ACL-SNet) [11]. The efficacy of the method was calculated by metrics like sensitivity, specificity, and area under the curve (AUC), comparing it to a group of three orthopedists on a holdout test set. The ACL-DNet achieved a Dice coefficient of 98%±6% on MRI datasets, while the classification demonstrated 97% sensitivity and 97% specificity. In contrast, alternative models had sensitivity ranging from 84% to 90% and specificity between 86% and 92%.

Liu and Wu developed an advanced DL model that integrated T-distribution-based slice attention mechanism with a penalty weight loss function to enhance ACL detection [12]. The T-slice attention module created a robust slice filtering system, while incorporating class relationships and replacing traditional cross-entropy loss with a penalty loss function significantly boosted classification accuracy. This approach improved diagnostic performance across six different backbone networks, with the VGG-Slice-Wheat model achieving an AUC of 0.9590.

Souras et al. introduced a highly discriminative novelty score that utilizes aleatory semantic uncertainty from the classes of the YOLOv5-nano object detection model [13]. For tissue density, they continue using global scores (probability vectors) when applying to the entire sagittal segment. The second part of their pipeline involves the MINIRECT series classification model to assess ACL tears in knees. To evaluate generalization, they conducted cross-database testing with two public databases (KneeMRI and MRNet) and a validation-only database from the University General Hospital of Larissa, Greece. Their method consistently outperformed out-of-the-art approaches on the KneeMRI dataset (p-value<0.05) and achieved higher accuracy and sensitivity over the MRNet dataset.

Zhang et al. presented a new approach for diagnosing ACL tears using a CNN optimized by Modified Golden Search Algorithm (MGSA) [14]. This method effectively extracts key features from MRI images of the knee with an optimized CNN architecture, achieving an impressive accuracy of 99.6%, surpassing other methods.

Wang et al. proposed a lightweight attention graph neural network (GNN) with a conditional random field (CRF), called ACGN, for classifying ACL ruptures in knee MR images [15]. They utilized a metric-based meta-learning strategy for independent testing through multiple node classification tasks and designed a lightweight feature embedding network using knowledge distillation to extract image features. GNN layers were employed to identify sample dependencies and complete the classification, with refining attentions in each GNN layer. To address overshooting and overfitting, they applied self-boosting attention, node attention, and memory attention for graph initialization, node updating, and layer correlation.
