+   Socioeconomic Factors: Income levels, education, and access to healthcare can significantly influence nutrition and health outcomes. Including socioeconomic data allows ML models to consider the broader social determinants of health.

+   Geographical Location: Datasets often include location information, such as country or region, which is crucial for studying how environmental, cultural, and economic factors impact nutritional health.

+   Lifestyle Factors: Some datasets include information about lifestyle behaviors like physical activity, smoking, and diet, which are key factors influencing BMI and overall health. These variables add context to the biometric data and help improve model predictions.

# ### 4.5.1.3 Challenges with dataset generalizability

Large-scale datasets offer a plethora of information, but one of the biggest obstacles is their lack of generalizability. A large number of the datasets utilized in nutrition are region-specific, which means they were gathered from certain nations or geographical regions with distinctive economic, cultural, or nutritional circumstances. For instance, a number of research on malnutrition has concentrated on India and Sub-Saharan Africa, two areas with high rates of undernutrition but different causes and manifestations of the condition.

These region-specific datasets might not yield models that can be applied to other areas with distinct nutritional landscapes, even though they are useful for comprehending the local environment. For example, a ML model based on data from Southeast Asia or Latin America may not work effectively when applied to populations where the dietary, cultural, and socioeconomic aspects are different from those in Sub-Saharan Africa. Additionally, data from low-income regions, where malnutrition is commonly connected to undernutrition rather than overnutrition, may not be appropriate for predicting health disorders like obesity, which are more common in high-income countries.

# ### 4.5.1.4 Improving dataset diversity

The use of various datasets that cover a wide range of populations has been stressed by academics as a means of addressing the generalizability issue. For the models to be more robust, these datasets should contain information from different nations, socioeconomic classes, and cultural backgrounds. Researchers can make sure that ML models can apply to larger populations instead of just geographic areas or demographic groups by training the models on a wider variety of datasets.
