# 4.4 SYMPOMS OF MALNUTRITION

Child malnutrition presents a range of symptoms, depending on the type and severity. In acute malnutrition, or wasting, children may appear extremely thin with visible bones and muscle. Chronic malnutrition, also known as stunting, manifests in poor growth, resulting in children being significantly shorter for their age (Figure 4.2).

Common signs of malnutrition include fatigue, irritability, and lack of energy. Affected children may have dry skin, brittle hair, or hair loss. In severe cases, swelling of the feet or face (edema) can occur, in cases of kwashiorkor, a severe form of protein deficiency. Other symptoms include frequent infections, as malnutrition weakens the immune system, making children more susceptible to illnesses.
