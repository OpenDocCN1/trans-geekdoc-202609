# 7.3.1 Convolutional autoencoder (CAE)

One kind of neural network that may minimize noise in data is the CAE, which works by understanding the underlying structure of the input. The typical architecture of CAE [19] is shown in Figure 7.2. It consists of three components: a bottleneck that picks key characteristics, an encoder that compresses input while maintaining valuable features, and a decoder that reconstructs the data. Using a dataset, the CAE model may be used to generate new pictures and produce an unsupervised classifier. In order to reconstruct the pictures and comprehend the latent space of the data, this model employs an encoder-bottleneck-decoder architecture.

# 7.3.1.1 Encoder

The input data is compressed by the network's encoder before being sent to the bottleneck layer. Despite being substantially smaller than original input, the compressed knowledge representation retains most of its properties. This portion of the network is made up of convolutional blocks that are followed by pooling layers, which further aid in the creation of a compressed representation of the data. An ideal encoder should provide a smaller but identical output to the input. To replicate it, the encoder must be sensitive to the input but not excessively sensitive. Oversensitivity would cause the model to overfit the data by remembering the inputs.

# 7.3.1.2 Bottleneck layer

The most crucial layer of an autoencoder is the bottleneck. The compressed knowledge that is sent to the decoder is stored in this module. By letting only the most crucial portions of the compressed representation get through to the decoder, the bottleneck limits the flow of information.
By this, the input data is guaranteed to have the most amount of information retrieved from it and the most beneficial connections discovered. This architectural feature also serves as a safeguard against overfitting by preventing the network from directly remembering the input data. A network's compressed representation of an input dataset is known as its latent space.
This latent space is difficult to immediately see and typically holds hundreds of dimensions. More intricate neural networks are often termed as "black boxes" since their hidden regions are so difficult to see. Latent space in a CAE corresponds to better data representation. The weights of the network layers are encoded in this enormous matrix of tensors that make up the space structure.

# 7.3.1.3 Decoder

It is the Decomponent that attends to recreate an image based on its latent features. From the Bott layer, the decoder will get the compressed information, and then it uses upsampling and convolutions to reconstruct original image. To measure the network's performance, the Decoder's output is compared to the ground truth.
