## 16.6.2 Bandwidth and stability requirements

The efficient running of 5G-enabled health applications, as remote surgery, is very much dependent on bandwidth as well as stability. Unlike earlier generations of mobile networks, 5G ensures high throughput of data with low delays, ideally tailored for critical healthcare services where quick and steady connections are required. Bandwidth is essential; if remote surgeries and advanced telemedicine applications are to become common, huge volumes of data will need to travel large distances. For example, in a remote surgery, one may be required to stream 4K or even 8K video feeds in real time, which may call for bandwidths of several gigabytes per second. Fifth-generation mobile networks have peak rates of 10 Gbps, ensuring such data-intensive streams are transmitted at maximum speed and high quality. This kind of bandwidth is necessary for accurate visual details in performing intricate surgical tasks [11].

Ultra-low latency is critical; in the case of real-time healthcare applications such as remote surgery and robotic-assisted interventions, any delay caused by data transmission cannot be afforded. The ultra-low latencies in 5G, which can reach 1 millisecond, ensure near-instant data transfer.

## 16.6.3 AI and ML in diagnostics and personalized medicine

Indeed, the connection of AI and ML with 5G technology in the health sector has completely revolutionized diagnostics and personalized medicine. The high speed of connectivity and ultra-low latency facilitate the quick collection and processing of enormous patient-related data, allowing AI and ML algorithms to offer precise, individualized healthcare solutions. Enhanced diagnostics benefit significantly; both AI and ML models utilize large datasets of medical imaging, patient history, and genetic information for pattern recognition and anomaly detection. With 5G, high-resolution images—MRI, CT scans, X-rays—can be transmitted in real time to remote diagnostic AI systems, which can quickly scan the images to help experts identify conditions like tumors, broken bones, or infections with greater precision. The low latency specifications of 5G ensure that results from diagnostics are nearly instantaneous, speeding up decision-making in critical environments [4].

Personalized treatment plans are made possible through AI and ML by leveraging genomic data, lifestyle factors, and the medical history of the patient. Healthcare providers can continuously monitor patient data reactions in real time on 5G-enabled networks and adjust treatment protocols accordingly. In oncology, for instance, AI can evaluate feedback, recommend dosage adjustments or alternative treatments to the physician [2]. Real-time predictive analytics are how a patient's tumor is responding to therapy, and based on real-time feedback also enhanced with 5G and the IoMT. Wearable and implantable devices can transmit patient data to AI without disrupting its bliss, enabling ML algorithms to recognize early signs of ill health, such as irregular heartbeats or changes in blood sugar levels. This allows for predictions of potential health crises before they occur, leading to proactive healthcare interventions that mitigate associated risks.

## 16.6.4 Management of chronic diseases

Management of chronic diseases can be a significant challenge in the health sector, as it often requires continuity in observations and adjustments in medications and lifestyle. The confluence of 5G, AI, and the IoMT opens new avenues for the effective and efficient management of chronic conditions. Continuous monitoring and data reporting are essential; for patients with chronic conditions such as diabetes or heart disease, 5G networks will support real-time continuous data reporting from IoMT devices like glucose monitors, heart rate sensors, or blood pressure cuffs to healthcare providers. This includes the constant reporting of vital measurements, allowing any health professional to track them easily [11]. Continuous monitoring guarantees that potential issues are detected at the very first stage, helping to avert acute complications.

Personalized medicine management is another key benefit. AI and ML algorithms analyze patient data to derive optimal medication regimes. For instance, in diabetes management, AI could use 5G-transmitted blood sugar levels to recommend specific insulin doses for a patient. This approach reduces the burden of treatment responses while minimizing side effects [10].

## 16.7 CHALLENGES AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS

On the other hand, many are the challenges that face the implementation of 5G technology in healthcare, and these issues need to be addressed. The sooner we take them into consideration, the worthier we will be prepared to embrace the future of 5G for the healthcare.

## 16.7.1 High costs of 5G infrastructure deployment

Indeed, the nature of 5G infrastructure installation is entirely founded on conglomeration with the new technology, iteration of current network equipment, supplementary antenna, and extending fibre optic networks. Implementing 5G networks, especially at its inception in a remote area or underserved area, may turn out to be costly for health institutions. Thus, financing becomes one of the key challenges limiting large implementation of 5G-enabled healthcare services, especially in developing regions. Addition to the aforementioned costs on the devices, health institutions would also need to invest in the infrastructure further for adoption; this is an additional cost towards compatibility. Strategic investments are going to be important in removing these cost barriers between public-private partnerships and governmental stimuli for infrastructure development.
