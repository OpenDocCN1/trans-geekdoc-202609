# 16.2.3 Potential of 5G in various industries

5G technology is capable of revolutionizing a string of industries with better connectivity, higher data speeds, and low latency [12]. In healthcare, 5G will revolutionize telemedicine by enabling high-definition video consultations and remote surgeries. It supports real-time monitoring of patients through wearables and IoT for chronic disease management, which in turn facilitates predictive-controlled analytics with AI. The ultra-low latency of 5G is critical for remote-controlled medical procedures and improving the quality of remote diagnostics. In smart manufacturing, IoT devices and machines communicate in real time, with robotics and automation to bring efficiency by lowering downturns. This is achieved through enhanced data speeds, which facilitate advanced predictive maintenance and process optimization.

For the transportation sector, 5G fosters V2X communication to make vehicles autonomous and enhance traffic management. It ensures that all information is in real time and guarantees safety while enhancing the efficiency of transportation networks. In the entertainment industry, 5G benefits streaming quality by enabling high-definition and immersive experiences like virtual reality and augmented reality, as it supports seamless transfer of large media files. It is also designed for enhanced gaming interaction [5]. Lastly, the development of smart cities is supported by 5G as it enables all infrastructure components to be connected, allowing traffic lights, surveillance cameras, and environmental sensors to communicate with each other.

# 16.3 IMPACT OF 5G ON HEALTHCARE

# 16.3.1 Current state of healthcare technology

Healthcare technology is now at a point where rapid advancement is taking place, driven by innovations in digital health tools, EHRs, wearable devices, telemedicine, and the IoMT [13]. Communication capabilities enable patient care along with diagnostics, treatment planning, and health management provided by these technologies [14]. Telemedicine and remote care have recently gained more popularity, especially in the context of COVID-19, providing remote consultations and treatment to patients. However, with existing communication technologies (mostly 4G), real-time interaction is challenging, and seamless care remains an illusion. Issues such as latency, reduced video quality, and intermittent connectivity may impede high-quality remote care. Wearable devices and IoMT, such as heart rate monitors, glucose sensors, and fitness bands, produce enormous volumes of data that contribute to monitoring patients and providing preventive healthcare.
