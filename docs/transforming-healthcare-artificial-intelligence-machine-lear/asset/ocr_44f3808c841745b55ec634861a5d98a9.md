Kumar and Sinha [1] proposed a comprehensive framework for Yoga Pose Detection and Classification using deep learning. Their method involves the collection and preprocessing of a diverse dataset of yoga poses, incorporating image normalization and augmentation techniques. The framework leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and the PitPaf method for pose estimation, achieving high accuracy and precision. This approach demonstrates strong potential for applications in fitness apps and virtual yoga poses.

Borthakur et al. [2] introduced Yoga Pose Estimation Using Angle-Based Feature Extraction, a method optimized for low-power devices. By employing the Extremely Randomized Trees (ERT) algorithm, they achieved 91% accuracy through manual feature extraction from human pose estimation. Their approach enhances pose detection and user safety, with discussions on integration of motion capture, wearable sensors, and advanced 3D techniques.

Kulkarni et al. [3] explored Yoga Pose Recognition Using Deep Learning, focusing on CNNs and short-term memory (LSTM) networks. Their system aims to improve pose recognition accuracy and efficiency, providing real-time feedback for self-correction. This research emphasizes the role of technology in making yoga accessible and effective, contributing to advancements in automated yoga analysis and instruction. Thoutam et al. [4] developed an Automated Yoga Pose Estimation System targeting six poses: Cobra, Lotus, Corpse, Mountain, Triangle, and Tree. Their system integrates CNNs and LSTMs, achieving up to 99.33% accuracy by extracting 12 joint angles.
