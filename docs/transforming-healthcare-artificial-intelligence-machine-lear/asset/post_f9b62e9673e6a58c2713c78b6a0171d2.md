## 17.3.7.2 Cons of AI in healthcare

The use of AI in healthcare presents challenges such as data privacy concerns, potential biases in algorithms, and the need for significant infrastructure and training for proper implementation [5].

- **Data Privacy and Security Concerns:** The use of AI in healthcare involves processing large amounts of sensitive patient data, raising concerns about data privacy and security. For example, a data breach in an AI-driven healthcare system could expose personal health information, leading to potential misuse and loss of patient trust.

- **High Implementation Costs:** Integrating AI technologies into healthcare systems requires significant upfront investment in software, hardware, and training. For example, smaller healthcare facilities may struggle to afford the initial costs of AI technologies, leading to disparities in access to advanced care.

- **Potential for Bias and Inequality:** Algorithmic Bias: AI systems can inherit biases from the data they are trained on, potentially leading to unequal treatment of patients from different demographic groups. For example, if an AI model is trained on data from one ethnic group, it may not perform as well for patients from other groups, leading to disparities in care.

- **Lack of Human Oversight:** There is a risk that healthcare professionals may over-rely on AI systems, potentially overlooking their clinical judgment or missing nuanced aspects of a patient's condition. For example, a clinician might rely too heavily on an AI diagnosis, even when subtle signs contradict the AI's findings, potentially leading to incorrect treatment.

## 17.4 THE IMPACT OF 5G IN HEALTHCARE

### 17.4.1 Enhancing telemedicine remote monitoring

- **High-Speed Connectivity:** One of the key benefits of 5G is its ability to provide high-speed, low-latency connections, which is crucial for telemedicine services [6]. Unlike 4G, where lags in communication can affect the quality of remote consultations, 5G offers real-time audio and video interactions. This ensures seamless virtual consultations, in rural or remote areas with traditionally poor internet infrastructure.

- **Continuous Monitoring:** 5G enables remote monitoring of patients with chronic conditions such as diabetes or cardiovascular diseases through wearable devices. The real-time transmission of data to healthcare providers allows for prompt interventions when abnormalities are detected, reducing the need for frequent hospital visits.

- **Scalability:** With the massive bandwidth offered by 5G, healthcare providers can serve more patients simultaneously without compromising the quality of service. This scalability is critical in managing public health crises, where telemedicine platforms may experience surges in demand.

### 17.4.2 Real-time remote surgeries and robotics

- **Ultra-Low Latency:** 5G's ultra-low latency is a game-changer for real-time remote surgeries. Surgeons can operate on patients in different locations using robotic systems with minimal delay in transmitting video and control signals. This precision is vital in procedures where millisecond-level accuracy is required, such as neurosurgery or cardiovascular interventions.

- **Global Access to Expertise:** 5G allows specialists to perform surgeries remotely, making high-quality care accessible to underserved areas. For example, a leading surgeon in a major city could remotely guide or even perform surgery on a patient located in a rural hospital, dramatically improving patient outcomes.

- **Robotics Integration:** The use of robotics in surgery is enhanced with 5G connectivity, as it allows for more precise and responsive control of robotic instruments. Surgeons can manipulate robots with pinpoint accuracy from miles, reducing complications and enhancing the success rate of complex procedures.

### 17.4.3 The role of IoT in connected health via 5G networks

- **Internet of Medical Things (IoMT):** 5G plays a crucial role in the development and deployment of the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT). Connected health devices, including wearable sensors, implantable devices, and smart home medical equipment, all benefit from 5G's capacity to handle large volumes of data while maintaining low latency and high reliability [6,7].

- **Interoperability:** 5G networks facilitate better integration and interoperability among various healthcare devices. Seamless connectivity enables healthcare systems to aggregate data from multiple sources—such as heart monitors, glucose sensors, and fitness trackers—providing a comprehensive view of a patient's health.

- **Predictive Health Monitoring:** IoT devices, powered by 5G, can collect and analyze patient data continuously. When combined with AI and big data analytics, this ecosystem can predict potential health issues, providing early warnings to patients and healthcare providers for timely interventions.

### 17.4.4 5G-Enabled healthcare devices

- **Wearables and Mobile Health Devices:** 5G enables a new generation of smart healthcare devices that offer real-time health tracking. Wearables, such as smartwatches or fitness trackers, can monitor vital signs like heart rate, oxygen levels, and blood pressure, sending this data to healthcare providers in real time. This connectivity allows for more proactive care and real-time adjustments to treatment plans [8].

- **Smart Implants:** 5G can power smart implants, such as pacemakers and insulin pumps, that can communicate with healthcare systems and respond automatically to changes in a patient's condition. The devices can receive updates remotely and adjust treatment without the need for invasive procedures.

- **Mobile Diagnostic Tools:** Portable diagnostic tools like ultrasound, ECG monitors, and blood analyzers are also evolving with 5G technology. These tools can be used in remote areas to perform critical diagnostics, the data instantly transmitted to specialists for interpretation, making healthcare more accessible and timely.

In summary, 5G technology has the potential to reshape healthcare by enabling real-time communication, enhanced device connectivity, and more accurate, timely patient care. From remote surgeries to personalized monitoring, 5G provides a foundational infrastructure for the future of connected health.

## 17.5 CLOUD COMPUTING AND BIGDATA IN HEALTHCARE

### 17.5.1 Cloud-based electronic health records (EHRs)

- **Centralized Data Storage:** Cloud-based EHRs allow healthcare organizations to store, access, and manage patient data from a centralized location. This contrasts with traditional on-premises systems, where data is siloed, limiting access and collaboration.

- **Accessibility and Efficiency:** Cloud-based EHRs offer healthcare providers real-time access to patient data from any location, enabling more efficient patient care, especially in multi-site hospitals or during remote consultations. Physicians can retrieve patient histories, results, or imaging reports instantly, which speeds up diagnosis and treatment.

- **Cost-Effectiveness:** Cloud-based systems eliminate the need for costly IT infrastructure and maintenance. Instead, healthcare providers can scale services on demand and pay only for what they use, making it a more cost-effective solution for small-to-sized healthcare facilities.

### 17.5.2 Data interoperability and collaboration among healthcare providers

- **Seamless Data Exchange:** One of the most significant advantages of cloud computing in healthcare is the ability to improve data interoperability. Cloud platforms facilitate the exchange of health information between different healthcare providers, such as hospitals, pharmacies, and specialists, ensuring patients receive consistent care across multiple settings.
