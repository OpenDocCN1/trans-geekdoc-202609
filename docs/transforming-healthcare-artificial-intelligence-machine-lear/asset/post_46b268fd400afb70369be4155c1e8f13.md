### 1.3.1. Normal lung sound

Most lung sounds are tracheal, bronchial, vesicular, and bronchovesicular [76]. Auscultation location, manufacture, appearance, and auditory attributes differ. The trachea creates noise due to turbulent airflow [77]. This sound is detected above the sternum during inspiratory and expiratory auscultation.

Both tracheal sounds end early. Tracheal noises are higher-pitched, hollow, non-musical, rougher, and louder than other lung sounds because they travel a straighter, bigger trachea [78,79]. Tracheal sound intensity diminishes at 800Hz and disperses between 100 and 5,000 Hz [78,79,80].

Airflow from the trachea airways creates bronchial sound around the second and third intercostals. It is stronger and lasts twice as long in the expiratory phase as the tracheal sound [81]. Blunt, non-musical, harsh, high-pitched, tubular bronchial and tracheal sounds have similar frequency energy distribution [82].
