Figure 5.1 illustrates the anatomy of ACL. Over 2 million ACL injuries occur annually worldwide, and the number is increasing at a pace of 4–6 every annually. Compared to men, women are 2–8 times inclined to suffer ACL damage. In activities like soccer, rugby league, gymnastics, volleyball, basketball, hockey, and snowboarding, over 70% of ACL damages are sustained. ACL damages are normal in New Zealand and Australia than in other nations, with 58.2 and 77.4 per 100,000 individuals, respectively. The incidence of ACL injuries among individuals between the ages of 6 and 18 has risen through the previous 20 years by at least 2.3% year, with greater rates in females 16 years and boys 17 years old [51]. Greater involvement in exercise and sports may contribute to the rise in ACL tears amongst kids and teenagers by raising the likelihood of rupture. Knee injury registers are kept up to date by numerous nations, athletics teams, and organizations worldwide. ACL tears constitute 86.5% of knee harm, according to observational research conducted in northern India. According to the research, 44.3% of the instances of ACL tear occurred in individuals between the ages of 18 and 25.

The knee's ACL serves a number of purposes, including:

+   - Knee Stability: The ACL maintaining knee stability, particularly in restraining the shin bone from moving out front of the thigh bone.
- Deterring Twisting: The ACL assists in keeping the knee from twisting or disintegrating.
