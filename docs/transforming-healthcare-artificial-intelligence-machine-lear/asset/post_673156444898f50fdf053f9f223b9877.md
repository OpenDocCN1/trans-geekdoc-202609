## 9 Design and implementation of PPE detection using YOLO9

## 9.1 INTRODUCTION

The use of personal protective equipment (PPE) has become increasingly important in recent years health management agencies in many countries require PPE equipment, as safety helmets, vests, to safeguard the personal safety of workers. As a result, monitoring employees who are wearing PPE has become crucial for safety. Conventional techniques are unable to identify infractions in real time, which results in mishaps, injuries, and monetary losses. The YOLO (You Only Once) model has become well-known among them due to its rapid and accurate performance. YOLO9, the most recent version, includes notable enhancements, and it a,,,, to of

## 9.2 LITERATURE SURVEY

Gallo et al. [3] proposed system for real-time PPE detection that uses edge computing to analyze video streams locally on an embedded Raspberry Pi with an Intel Compute Stick 2, reducing reliance on cloud-based solutions. It evaluates pre-trained convolutional neural networks for performance and latency. The system ensures efficiency, reliability, and privacy by avoiding continuous cloud transmission.

The MAR-YOLO model enhances PPE detection by improving on YOLO8 with a re-parameterizable backbone, R-C2F and RASFF for better speed and accuracy. Using a custom dataset of 2,750 images, Bao et al. found that MAR-YOLO achieved a 6.7% improvement in AP50 and a 10.2% improvement in AP75, with a mean average precision (mAP) of 74.7%, outperforming other lightweight models by 4.95% [4].

Liu Yepeng et al.'s improves PPE identification by developing a unique dataset for luminous helmets and hard hats and refining the YOLO5 model. The enhanced AL-YOLO5 model achieves a 0.4 mAP implementation and a 0.9 AP increase in data-limited categories by adding an attention mechanism and an advanced loss function. This greatly lower false detections and improves the handling of overlapping frames [5].
