A typical ECG waveform consists of five types of waves such as:

- 1. P-wave
- 2. T-wave
- 3. Q-wave
- 4. R-wave
- 5. S-wave.

Figure 2.8 shows the illustration of a typical ECG waveform along with its components.
Various intervals from the ECG waveform are used for diagnosis of various diseases:

- 1. RR-Interval: It is used to identify the period of an ECG signal and it denotes the time interval between two adjacent R-waves. If it is identified as irregular, it is an indication of heart disease as arrhythmia.
- 2. PR-Interval: It measures the time interval between the starting of P-wave and that of the QRS-complex. It is an indication of the time for the impulse to reach the ventricles from the sinus node.
- 3. QT-Interval: It represents the time interval between the beginning of a Q-wave and the end of a T-wave. If the interval gets exceeded from the normal value, there is an increased possibility of cardiac death.

## 2.4.2 Non-clinical services

Suggestions from expert health professionals regarding mental health disorders as stress, anxiety, insomnia, dementia, and schizophrenia from people from remote places are categorized as telehealth non-clinical services.

## Transforming Healthcare

As per survey made by the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), 1 among 7 people suffers from a mental health disorder. During 2017, there are about more than 197 million people who have experienced mental health problems. Most of the people faced depression as a mental health issue. As per World Health Organization (WHO) report, there were many men committing suicide than due to sadness.

Mental health problems can have more significance in one's own personal life, which further affects their personal relationship, employment, status, etc.

Some of the common mental health problems experienced by many people are as follows:

- 1. **Depression**: People feel stressed or frequently, do not show interest in their activities, feel lonely or helpless, become irritated by their actions, and get angry and frustrated.
- 2. **Anxiety**: People feel nervous and tension, are scared about their actions, feel that they are in dangerous situation, and are not feeling relaxed.
- 3. **Insomnia**: Symptoms of insomnia include difficulty in getting enough quality sleep and not feeling well rested.
- 4. **Dementia**: Symptoms of dementia include forgetfulness and limited thinking skills due to which person feels impaired.
- 5. **Schizophrenia**: It is a type of mental disorder that affects a person's ability to feel, think, and act clearly. People often experience false belief, as well as difficulties with concentration and memory.

After frequent counseling by telephone communication or video conferencing, the people will feel free of mental health problems.

## 2.43 Implementation of tele-healthcare system

Figure 2.11 shows the overall system implementation for tele-healthcare services for clinical factors. It can also be extended for non-clinical services such as mental health disorders.

As per mentioned procedural steps, various parameters like body temperature, blood pressure readings, SpO₂, respiration rate, and ECG waveform readings are measured, and the corresponding data (in DICOM format) is stored in local database for storage and retrieval. At the server side, with the implementation of AI algorithms, the data can be processed like converting DICOM files into pdf files and analyzing their nature and severity. Further, a report of results is generated, and the same is to be forwarded to the hospital for physician's text-based messages, with audio and video signals provided from the patients side and server side. Data information can also be further stored on cloud platforms for easy access and retrieval whenever needed [19–20].
