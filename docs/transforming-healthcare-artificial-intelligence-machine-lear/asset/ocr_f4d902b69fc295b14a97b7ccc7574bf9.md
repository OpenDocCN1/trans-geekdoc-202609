The UNICEF database, which aggregates information from numerous national and international surveys, is one of the most used datasets in nutritional health prediction. This database is especially useful for studying child nutrition since it includes information on feeding habits, anthropometric measures, and health outcomes for children. This information enables researchers to look into both overnutrition and undernut (Varthi Mahendra et al., 2019).

The Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS), which gather standardized data on population, health, and nutrition from emerging nations, are frequently utilized source. DHS records researchers to analyze the nutritional status of children and adults across various locations and demographics since they contain a wide range of factors, including age, sex, weight, height, and socioeconomic status. These databases are especially useful in regard because low- and middle-income nations usually lack data collection on malnutrition (Amir-Din et al., 2022).

Platforms like Kaggle offer open-source data in addition to institutional datasets academics can utilize to train their ML models. One dataset set to be predict health outcomes connected to nutrition is the “BMI-prediction of health status” dataset from Kaggle, which contains records of people's weight, height, BMI, age, gender, and lifestyle characteristics. Due to the dataset's open access, several researchers can apply and build upon different ML methods to increase the accuracy of their predictions.

# 4.5.1.2 Key fields used in nutritional health datasets

Several key variables are frequently used in ML models for predicting malnutrition or related conditions like obesity:

+   - **Age:** Age is one of the most fundamental variables in predicting health outcomes. In nutrition-related studies, it is essentially important as growth standards differ widely across age groups, particularly for children.
- **Height and Weight:** Body Mass Index (BMI), Height-for-Age Z-score (HAZ), Weight-for-Age Z-score (WAZ), and Weight-for-Height Z-score (WHZ) are few examples of the indicators that are calculated using these biometric measurements. These metrics are widely used to assess malnutrition (undernutrition or overnutrition) in children and adults.
- **Gender:** Gender is another critical variable, as nutritional needs and growth patterns differ between males and females. Gender differences are often considered in predictive models to improve their accuracy.
