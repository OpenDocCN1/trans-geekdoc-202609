# 7.3.2 Convolutional neural network

Due to the widespread nature of processing and interpreting visual input, CNNs find extensive practical applications in a variety of fields, including social media, healthcare, and automotive. CNNs are utilized to help in imaging and diagnosis in medicine. To help with diagnosis and treatment, a CNN [20,21], for instance, may examine medical pictures like X-rays or pathology slides to find abnormalities of illness. When processing images, CNN is more effective like the human brain's connection patterns. The visual cortex is important for perception and processing. In CNN, artificial neurons are designed to process visual input efficiently, and these models can understand whole images.

The typical structure of CNN is shown in Figure 7.3. CNNs are normally employing several layers to pick up unique features from an input picture. Depending on the complication of the task for which it is designed, CNN model may have hundreds, thousands, or even more layers. The outputs of one layer are given to the next layer for identifying intricate patterns. The number of layers that build up a CNN is often divided into three kinds: convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers.

When data are moving via these layers, the difficulty of CNN is increased and also able to detect abstract features. In convolutional layer, a tiny matrix of weights called filter or kernel traverses the receptive field of an input picture to look for the presence of characteristics.
