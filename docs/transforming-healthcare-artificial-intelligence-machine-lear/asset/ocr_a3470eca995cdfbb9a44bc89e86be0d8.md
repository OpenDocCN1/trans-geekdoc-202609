Experimental Results: Performance evaluations reveal significantly reduced response times compared to traditional services and measurable improvements in patient survival rates when AEDs arrive within the first few minutes (Rivera & Gomez, 2022; Lopez & Garcia, 2022).

# 6.4.3 Health monitoring in remote areas

Description: AIoT drones can be deployed to rural and inaccessible locations to collect vital health data—including heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation—from patients, transmitting this information in real time to healthcare providers for analysis (Chen & Liu, 2022; Martinez & Lopez, 2022).

Components: The drones carry medical diagnostic tools integrated with vital sign sensors, GPS modules for navigation, and communication systems for secure data transmission (Rivera & Gomez, 2022; Hall & King, 2022), as depicted in Figure 6.5.

Experimental Results: Comparative studies show a high correlation between drone-collected health data and measurements obtained through conventional medical equipment, indicating accuracy and reliability (Bennett & Turner, 2022; Lopez & Garcia, 2022). Timely health monitoring in such areas has led to improved patient outcomes and early intervention (Foster & Gray, 2023; Chen & Liu, 2022).
