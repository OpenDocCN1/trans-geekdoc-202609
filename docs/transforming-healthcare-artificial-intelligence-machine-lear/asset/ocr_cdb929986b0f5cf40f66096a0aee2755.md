**Random Signal:** Signals belonging to other categories, such as periodic signals, which have one or more characteristics with suitable random behaviour, are also referred to as random signals.

**Modulated signal:** During modulation, a signal and a sinusoid are combined to produce a new signal. A modulated signal is the result of combining the carrier and modulating signals. The practice of altering one or more properties of a periodic waveform, referred to as the carrier signal, with a separate signal that often carries the data that will be known as modulation in electronics and telecommunications. The phrases “analogue” and “digital” modulation are used when modulating the signal is analogue or digital, respectively.

**Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR or S/N):** In science and engineering, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is a crucial measurement used to assess the strength of a desired signal relative to background noise levels. Typically expressed in decibels (dB), SNR compares the intensity of the signal to the level of noise present. A ratio greater than 1:1 (greater than 0 dB) indicates that the signal is stronger than the noise (Umar et al., 2012) (Figure 10.4).
