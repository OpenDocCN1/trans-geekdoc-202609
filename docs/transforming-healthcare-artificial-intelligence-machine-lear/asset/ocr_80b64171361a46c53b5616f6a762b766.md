# 17.3.7.2 Cons of AI in healthcare

The use of AI in healthcare presents challenges such as data privacy concerns, potential biases in algorithms, and the need for significant infrastructure and training for proper implementation [5].

+   **Data Privacy and Security Concerns:** The use of AI in healthcare involves processing large amounts of sensitive patient data, raising concerns about data privacy and security. For example, a data breach in an AI-driven healthcare system could expose personal health information, leading to potential misuse and loss of patient trust.

+   **High Implementation Costs:** Integrating AI technologies into healthcare systems requires significant upfront investment in software, hardware, and training. For example, smaller healthcare facilities may struggle to afford the initial costs of AI technologies, leading to disparities in access to advanced care.

+   **Potential for Bias and Inequality:** Algorithmic Bias: AI systems can inherit biases from the data they are trained on, potentially leading to unequal treatment of patients from different demographic groups. For example, if an AI model is trained on data from one ethnic group, it may not perform as well for patients from other groups, leading to disparities in care.

+   **Lack of Human Oversight:** There is a risk that healthcare professionals may over-rely on AI systems, potentially overlooking their clinical judgment or missing nuanced aspects of a patient's condition. For example, a clinician might rely too heavily on an AI diagnosis, even when subtle signs contradict the AI's findings, potentially leading to incorrect treatment.
