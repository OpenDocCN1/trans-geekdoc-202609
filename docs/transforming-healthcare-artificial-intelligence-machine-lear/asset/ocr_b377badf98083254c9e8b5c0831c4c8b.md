#  14.4.3 Arduino

Figure 14.5 shows the Arduino Uno, an open-source microcontroller based on the Microchip ATmega328P. Known for its simplicity and versatility, it has 28 pins, including six analog I/O pins, 12 digital I/O pins, and dedicated serial communication pins.

#  14.4.3.1 Key features

The Arduino Uno uses the Microchip ATmega328P microcontroller, known for its reliability and efficiency in interfacing with sensors and controlling hardware. It operates at 5 V, the standard voltage for powering and communicating with components like sensors, displays, and actuators.

#  14.4.3.2 Input voltage range

The Arduino Uno accepts an input voltage between 7 V and 20 V, allowing flexible power options through a 5 V supply or external sources like batteries or adapters. These features make it ideal for projects involving sensing, data acquisition, and control, offering a balance of functionality, ease of use, and adaptability.

#  14.4.4 Pulse oymeter sensor

Figure 14.6 illustrates the pulse oymeter sensor, specifically the MAX30100 module, used to measure an individual's oxygen saturation level (SpO2) and heart rate. The sensor has red and infrared (IR) LEDs, essential for measuring these health parameters.
