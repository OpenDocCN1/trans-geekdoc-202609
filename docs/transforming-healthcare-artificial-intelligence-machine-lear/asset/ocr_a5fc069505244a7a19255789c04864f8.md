+   **Latency**: 5G can reduce latency significantly to 1 ms, compared to 4G's average of 50 ms or 3G's range of 100–500 ms. This kind of delay is very important for real-time responding applications like remote-controlled robotics and autonomous driving.
**Network Capacity and Efficiency**: 3G introduces advanced technologies such as MIMO and beamforming, which enhance network capacity and efficiency, compared to 4G and 3G, which use less advanced antenna technologies, leading to higher interference and congestion levels. The massive MIMO of 5G allows more simultaneous connections and better signal quality, and the beamforming ensures better signal direction to user equipment [7].
**Connection Density**: 5G can support up to 1 million devices within one kilometer, thereby major surpassing 4G's capacity of 100,000 devices and that of 3G, which had much lower support. This will be the very key feature for IoT devices and smart city infrastructure to proliferate [11].
**Network Slicing**: With 5G comes network slicing, a capability to create multiple virtual networks within one single physical 5G infrastructure. Each slice can be set up to meet given specifications for bandwidth, speed, latency, or reliability. Such flexibility was not available on 4G or 3G networks, which tend to offer a more uniform service across all users and applications.
