# 4.5.4.3 Algorithm selection

Another crucial factor to take into account is the selection of ML algorithms. The merits and demerits of various algorithms differ based on how difficult the task at hand is. For example, because Random Forest and XGBoost can handle enormous datasets and intricate variable interactions, they have proven to be very accurate in a variety of health-related predicting tasks. These models, however, can be computationally demanding and sophisticated, which makes them less appropriate for some applications where computational simplicity and interpretability are important considerations. Simpler models, like logistic regression, on the other hand, have the benefit of being more clear and straightforward to use, which can be helpful in certain situations, as binary classification jobs where interpretability is crucial, even though they may be less accurate for complex tasks. Moreover, because they decrease overfitting and increase accuracy, ensemble models—which incorporate several algorithms—are frequently used for more complex forecasts. But simplicity is important, especially when it comes to simple categories or environments with limited processing power or resources.

In the final analysis, the primary goal of any future ML research in the healthcare sector is building models based on a range of representative datasets. It should also address ethical issues pertaining to transparency and fairness, and carefully choose algorithms that are appropriate for the particular predictive task at hand. Through concentration on these demands, scholars can augment the efficacy, dependability, and moral rectification of ML frameworks in the medical field.

# 4.5.4.3.1 Conclusion

Predicting childhood malnutrition is an essential step in solving one of the world's most urgent public health problems. We may learn a great deal about the major factors that contribute to child malnutrition, including socioeconomic status, access to healthcare, food habits, environment and sanitation, by using data-driven models. Health systems may detect high-risk children and communities using predictive analytics. This enables prompt interventions that can stop the progression of malnutrition-related problems. By guiding policymakers and healthcare professionals in allocating resources, predictive models not only improve the capacity to predict malnutrition but also present viable remedies.
