# 16.4.3 Benefits of rural and underserved areas

The potential effect of 5G is to bring healthcare access to underprivileged rural areas and bridge it to extend high-quality medical services in regions where there is typically insufficient medical infrastructure [17]. One of the prime benefits that 5G can bring to such areas is access to specialist care. A common issue in rural areas is the lack of healthcare specialists, which forces patients to travel long distances for consultations or treatment. With 5G-enabled telemedicine, travel will not be necessary for consultations to begin, and the associated costs of physical travel can be lowered. Additionally, real-time monitoring in many rural and underserved areas has been challenging due to a lack of resources. However, ultra-low 5G latency makes it possible to transmit real-time data from wearable devices and IOMT sensors [18].

This kind of monitoring provides healthcare providers with up-to-date information about patients, ensuring timely detection of health issues and intervention. In terms of emergency response, the ability to transmit large volumes of data in the shortest possible time is essential. 5G enables the instant sharing of high-resolution medical images, visual signs, and other necessary data from specialists to local healthcare workers [17]. This capability can significantly impact patient outcomes in rural areas, where there are very few emergency care facilities. While 5G may not entirely eliminate the need for on-site surgeries, it enables the feasibility of having surgeons in city centers remotely guide local surgical teams at nearby hospitals in real time.
