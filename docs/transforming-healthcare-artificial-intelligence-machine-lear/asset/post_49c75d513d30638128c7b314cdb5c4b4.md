
## 16.5.2 Real-time data transmission with 5G

The real significance of integrating 5G technology with healthcare is that there is real-time transmission of data, which is a vital component of the IoMT. Unlike its predecessors, 5G as the IMT2020 standard includes ultra-low latency, often less than one millisecond and high bandwidth, to guarantee instantaneous and reliable transmission of enormous volumes of data [1]. The potential of providing fast information opens new applications for patient care and remote monitoring. Figure 16.4 shows key aspects of real-time data transmission with 5G in healthcare.

- 1. Continuous Patient Monitoring: Wearable devices and smart sensors produce enormous data on the patient, such as vital signs, levels of activity, and other metrics of health. 5G facilitates the real-time transmission of data to healthcare providers, ensuring continuous monitoring of the health status of the patients—particularly those with chronic conditions, in real time, such as heart disease, diabetes, or respiratory insufficiency [3].

- 2. Immediate Medical Interventions: Now, with data being transmitted almost in real time, health professionals will quickly identify abnormal readings or the early signs of active or impeding medical emergencies. For example, if the heart rate or oxygen level of a patient drops suddenly, an alert could be sent to medical staff and immediate intervention initiated potentially saving lives [4].

- 3. High-Resolution Medical Imaging: CT scanners, MRI machines, and portable ultrasound devices produce big high-resolution images vital for proper diagnostics.

## 16.5.3 Continuous monitoring and early detection

Continuous monitoring and early detection take the lead in the 5G-enabled future of healthcare [3], 5G enabling seamless real-transmission of data from connected medical devices at all times, significantly improves the monitoring by healthcare providers to identify health-related issues at their onset. Seamless data transmission for wearables and sensors is facilitated by smart devices, glucose monitors, ECG sensors, and blood pressure cuffs, which vital health data continuously. With 5G technology’s ultra-low latency and high bandwidth, data is transmitted to healthcare providers without interruption. This allows for a comprehensive overview of various aspects, which can be utilized for timely interventions.

Thanks to uninterrupted monitoring empowered by 5G, healthcare providers will be able to identify non-standard patterns or fluctuations in health metrics at a very early stage. For instance, wearable technology can detect irregular heart rhythms, high blood pressure, or atypical levels of glucose, automatically alerting medical staff. Such premature identification allows to provide medical care that may prevent complications and even hospitalizations.

For patients with chronic illnesses like diabetes, myocardial infarction, or respiratory disorders, continuous surveillance is of paramount importance. The province of 24/7 health metric monitoring by 5G-powered IoT devices enables physicians to update treatment plans at any time based on current data [1,3,4]. This proactive approach in managing chronic diseases helps to avoid disease progression and enhances patient outcomes. RPM is made achievable from distant locations by healthcare providers, enabling them to keep tabs on patients’ conditions, particularly in rural areas or underserved regions. Real-time data transmission allows doctors to assess patients’ conditions without in-person visits, ensuring continuity of care despite geographical barriers. An ideal example would be post-surgery monitoring or for patients with limited mobility.

## 16.5.4 Transmission of high-resolution medical data

It is regarding the ultra-connections and low delays of 5G, which enable the fast and efficient transmission of huge volumes of high-resolution medical data [4]. This kind of development in data transmission is redefining healthcare with numerous benefits in diagnostic and treatment planning that can be extended to e-consultation. Medical imaging modalities such as MRI, CT scans, X-rays, and ultrasounds produce large files critical in accurate disease diagnosis. With 5G tech, transmission of such high-res images to any consulting specialist is almost instant from wherever is located. The faster access to detailed images helps healthcare workers make more accurate and timely decisions in responding to medical cases.

Since 5G allows sharing high-resolution images and videos in real time, remote diagnostic support becomes significantly easier [12]. For instance, a rural health unit can upload detailed imaging to the cloud, from which specialists at urban centres can conduct a real-time view of the information. This is particularly beneficial for patients in remote areas, allowing for expert consultations without the need to travel. Telemedicine is also enhanced by 5G, facilitating the transfer of high-resolution videos during virtual consultations. Clinicians can share diagnostic visuals with high clarity, such as images of internal organs or lesions, leading to more accurate assessments and fostering patient-doctor interactions that closely resemble in-person consultations [7]. The delivery of 3D imaging and augmented reality in surgical planning involves complex high-resolution datasets. 5G networks can support this steering at the edge without any delays, allowing surgeons to review and interact with detailed anatomical models [21].

## 16.5.5 Integration with AI and machine learning

From a mere analysis of the presented data, 5G-AI and ML integration has paved the way for hi-fi diagnostic techniques and intrusive personalization plans. The ultra-reliable, low latency, and increased 5G network capacities create what infrastructure would support huge amounts of real-time data generated by a wide variety of healthcare devices and wearables in monitoring systems, thereby facilitating application of AI and ML [21].

Provided that 5G technology can support real-time data transmission by IoMT devices—including wearable health monitors, medical imaging equipment, and electronic health records—algorithms implemented in AI can also process output data in real time to identify patterns, anomalies, and precursors of diseases. For example, wearable devices for 5G can continuously monitor vital health parameters of a patient, transmitting this data over the 5G network to AI systems for analysis regarding indications of issues such as arrhythmia or hypertension.

Real-time predictive analytics for patient care would be within the capacity of healthcare providers once 5G fosters the AI functionality of processing enormous data. For instance, AI may analyze historical health data from a patient to predict the short-term likelihood of events leading to cardiac arrest, enabling precautionary actions that lower the risk for emergencies. This type of predictive approach is particularly useful in managing chronic conditions, as it aims to customize patient surveillance and intervention plans. AI and ML depend on large and diversified datasets to develop personalized treatment plans. With 5G-enabled ultra-fast data transmission, comprehensive data for each patient—including genetic information, lifestyle habits, and medical history—can be made available for access by AI systems for analysis [22].

## 16.5.4 Scalability and ubiquitous connectivity

The high scalability and ubiquitous connectivity that 5G technology delivers are essential for transforming healthcare systems—particularly in growing a network of IoMT devices that will soon reach billions. The unmatched speed, ultra-low latency, and high capacity of 5G networks enable healthcare providers to connect a vast number of devices, sensors, and applications, situated in environments ranging from urban hospitals to rural clinics [14,21,22].

One of the most critical aspects supported by 5G is the massive simultaneous connectivity of devices, which is vital in healthcare, where the use of IoMT devices is expected to be urged [23]. Hospitals can have thousands of sensors, monitors, and medical devices installed on their premises, communicating over the same network. This high density allows for effective surveillance of patients, establishing a comprehensive health status baseline. With 5G, healthcare providers will also be able to scale their monitoring services. Patients in remote or underserved areas can utilize various IoMT devices for real-time monitoring of health conditions. The transmission of data from these devices to healthcare professionals will be seamless and continuous, reducing the need for in-person visits and creating an effective and scalable model of remote monitoring. This not only improves access to healthcare but also alleviates the infrastructural burden on healthcare systems [21].
