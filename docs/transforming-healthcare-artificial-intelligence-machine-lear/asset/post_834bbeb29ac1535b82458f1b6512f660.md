## 16.4.3 Benefits of rural and underserved areas

The potential effect of 5G is to bring healthcare access to underprivileged rural areas and bridge it to extend high-quality medical services in regions where there is typically insufficient medical infrastructure [17]. One of the prime benefits that 5G can bring to such areas is access to specialist care. A common issue in rural areas is the lack of healthcare specialists, which forces patients to travel long distances for consultations or treatment. With 5G-enabled telemedicine, travel will not be necessary for consultations to begin, and the associated costs of physical travel can be lowered. Additionally, real-time monitoring in many rural and underserved areas has been challenging due to a lack of resources. However, ultra-low 5G latency makes it possible to transmit real-time data from wearable devices and IoOMT sensors [18].

This kind of monitoring provides healthcare providers with up-to-date information about patients, ensuring timely detection of health issues and intervention. In terms of emergency response, the ability to transmit large volumes of data in the shortest possible time is essential. 5G enables the instant sharing of high-resolution medical images, visual signs, and other necessary data from specialists to local healthcare workers [17]. This capability can significantly impact patient outcomes in rural areas, where there are very few emergency care facilities. While 5G may not entirely eliminate the need for on-site surgeries, it enables the feasibility of having surgeons in city centers remotely guide local surgical teams at nearby hospitals in real time.

## 16.4.5 Remote-Controlled Robotic Surgery

Remote robotic surgery in the 5G era, with its near-instantaneous data transmission and ultra-precise control over surgical robots, was identified as one of the utmost latency and high-reliability applications that could enable surgeons to carry out operations in time and from any corner of the globe. This capability allows operations to be performed virtually, ensuring very low latency and high reliability, thus making it market-ready [18,19]. The following benefits succinctly elaborate on how 5G enhances remote robotic surgery: ultra-low latency for real-time control is crucial, as delays of even milliseconds are unacceptable in a telesurgery setting.

The ultra-low latency of 5G technology, enables surgical robotic instrument control in real time. This also provides zero-time gap feedback, ensuring that robotic arms move in absolute coordination with the surgeon's instructions, thereby reducing the chances of errors while performing delicate procedures. High-definition video streaming is another requirement for remote robotic surgeries, as it allows the operating surgeon to have a clear view of the surgical site [13]. Due to 5G's bandwidth capabilities, it supports high-resolution transmission that is free from buffering or lagging, ensuring that surgeons have a real-time view with precise details of the operational area. Additionally, with 5G, an expert surgeon can conduct operations on patients in rural or underserved areas where specialized medical treatment may not be readily accessible. By operating robotic surgical systems from remote locations, skilled surgeons can bring their expertise to patients without the need for travel, cutting both the cost and time of transferring the patient to an advanced healthcare facility [21].

## 16.5 REMOTE PATIENT MONITORING AND IoMT

### 16.5.1 Overview of Internet of Medical Things

Such is the case with the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT), directly drawing on the integration of medical devices and software applications with healthcare systems through connected sensor networks and wearables, with additional support from smart devices [18]. This technology ecosystem enables real-time data collection from the human body and the transmission of the gathered data for further analysis, which greatly contributes to improved patient care and clinical circumstances. It involves various devices, such as wearable health monitors, including smartwatches, fitness bands, and biosensors, which keep continuous on vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, glucose, and saturation, providing continuous health data. Additionally, smart medical devices like insulin pumps, pacemakers, can automatically adjust treatment based on the real-time data of the patient, offering personalized and responsive healthcare management [21].

RPM systems help in observing a patient's health by healthcare practitioners outside clinical settings and are particularly beneficial in cases of diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease. Data is collected through connected devices and forwarded to healthcare providers for evaluation and action. Telemedicine platforms enable virtual consultations, allowing for remote monitoring and interaction of the patient by a healthcare professional through video calls, chat services, and secure data exchange. Furthermore, connected diagnostic tools, including portable ECG monitors, smart thermometers, and digital stethoscopes, transmit measurements to the attending healthcare provider, enabling them to diagnose the patient remotely and provide timely intervention [2].
