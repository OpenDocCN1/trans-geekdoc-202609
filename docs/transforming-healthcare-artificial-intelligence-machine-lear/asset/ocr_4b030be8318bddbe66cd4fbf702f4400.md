# 17.1 INTRODUCTION

# 17.1.1 Overview of traditional healthcare practices

For decades, the healthcare industry was predominantly driven by man-
ual processes and human expertise. Medical records were kept on paper,
diagn tools were limited in their ability to deliver timely and accu-
rate, and patient data management systems lacked interoperability.
Physicians relied heavily on physical consultations and their personal exper-
ience to diagnose and treat conditions. While this system provided the
foundations for modern medicine, it was often slow, inefficient, and unable
to fully address the growing complexities of patient care.

The delivery of healthcare was reactive in nature, often addressing condi-
s after they had developed into more serious stages. This led to extended
hospital stays, higher healthcare costs, and varying levels of access to qual-
ity. Geographic location played a significant role in healthcare delivery,
as access to specialists and advanced treatments were concentrated in urban
centers, leaving rural and underserved areas with limited care options.
The overall healthcare ecosystem was fragmented, with little ability to harness
data for proactive or preventive care.
