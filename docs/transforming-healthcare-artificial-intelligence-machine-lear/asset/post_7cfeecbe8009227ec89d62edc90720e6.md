## Role of AI and ML in lung disease detection based on lung sound analysis using deep learning algorithms

Nivaashini Mathappan and Priya Lakshmipathy

### I.1 INTRODUCTION

World Health Statistics [1] estimated 6.86 million deaths in 2023, making it the third highest cause. Acute lower respiratory tract infections, COPD, asthma, occupational lung illness, and others affect [2]. The lack of healthcare workers is increasing COVID-19-related pneumonia. Obstructive pulmonary disease, which is easy to diagnose, may worsen it. COPD patients with COVID-19 perform poorly. COPD was common in ICUs and killed 65% of critically ill patients [3]. Major causes of death following COVID-19 are pneumonia and asthma. Thus, early pulmonary identification is attracting focus [4,5]. Preventing epidemics and lung diseases requires early diagnosis and treatment of respiratory complications [6].

Experts identify lung disorders by X-rays, stethoscopes, and CT. Computer-based lung sound analysis uses graphical representations, data, noise suppression, and machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) to evaluate lung sound behavior thoroughly [15]. The results are accurate, unbiased, and quantitative [16,17,18]. Automatic research findings are kept for later study [20]. Characterizing lung sounds is challenging due to overlapping feature distributions [21]. DL identifies illnesses using electronic stethoscope or mobile device biometrics. DL and AI are used in COVID-19 [25,26,27], lung auscultation, chest radiology, and cough sound analysis. Computerized techniques and lung recording are enhancing diagnostic accuracy. DL architectures include CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs, HMMs, and SVMs. A revolutionary breathing sound approach will enhance AI analysis and application. DL may account for classification mistakes to expedite lung diagnosis and therapy [28].

Medical experts utilize obsolete methods [29]:

1. Lung tests measure oxygen production and function. Tests usually include spirometry. Inhaling into spirometer measures exhaled air.
2. Chest X-ray: Detects emphysema and lung issues. COPD is primarily emphysema.
3. CT scan: Rarely used when other methods fail. CT scans may suggest COPD.
4. Analyzing arterial blood gas measures breathing-induced oxygen.

Limitations of current methods:

1. Surgery and cardiac patients cannot use spirometry.
2. Testing might cause dizziness, nausea, and dyspea.
3. Radiation from CT and X-rays endangers patients.

Under constraints and with the rise of artificial intelligence, ML and DL algorithms were investigated, created, and used to help doctors identify respiratory diseases quickly. With the correct methodology, respiratory organ images or sounds may identify respiratory diseases.

## Role of AI & ML in lung disease detection

A hybrid CNN-GRU-based DL algorithm analyzes lung sound anomalies to determine lung diseases. This project will provide an automated method to classify lung sounds as normal, bronchitis, asthma, heart failure, lung fibrosis, pleural effusion, pneumonia, and COPD.

Contributions of the paper:

- A hybrid DL lung sound analysis model uses GRU and CNN. The model increases respiratory sound temporal pattern recognition and feature extraction to classify pneumonia, bronchiectasis, and COPD.
- Mel-frequency cepstral coefficient (MFCC) is a popular sound processing method that extracts lung sound characteristics. The model may identify illness-related auditory patterns.
- Digital and AI for real-time, remote diagnostics are recommended over traditional stethoscopes. Sharing and investigating respiratory sounds may help doctors overcome subjectivity and inter-listener variability.
- The hybrid CNN-GRU model outperforms DL and ML with 93% accuracy on test data. This illustrates how successfully the recommended model diagnoses lung diseases from respiratory sound recordings.

## 1.2 RELATED WORKS

### 1.2.1 Using ML and DL algorithms

ML and DL lung sound analysis may detect and track lung problems [6]. The ML and DL algorithms can extract useful information from lung sound recordings. Following feature retrieval, ML and DL algorithms may train classification models [30]. DL comprises CNNs, RNNs, decision trees, k-NN, SVM, and random forests [31].

Data augmentation may improve ML/DL models. Data augmentation adds noise, temporal stretching, pitch shifting, and other alterations to sound recordings to produce synthetic data. Trained ML and DL models may assist doctors in detecting lung diseases after verification. Lung disease classification methods are compared based on interpretability, processing resources, and dataset features. These models may benefit from ensemble, hyperparameter, and transfer learning [32]. These are used for automatic teleconsulting smart equipment classification [33].
