# 12.28 The Metaverse

12.28 To the authors it seems clear that in many jurisdictions this notion of joint authorship will apply to avatars where the avatar user applies more than a minimal level of creativity and is consequently able to demonstrate an original expression of authorship that has become fixed in a tangible form of expression, namely the avatar.

12.29 In order to try and tackle these problems before they arise it will be necessary to first appreciate where there is a contract such as an end-user license that deals with the question of the avatar's copyright. It is submitted that a derivative authorship is subordinate to joint authorship with the result that a derivative work author only owns the copyright in the original material that had been added to the derivative work and does not have rights in the underlying work. If the derivative work creator should lose the right to use the underlying work or that right is revoked, then they cannot exploit the derivative work because that would then infringe the copyright in the underlying work. If an avatar were to be regarded as a derivative work, then the author of that derivative work would have rights in the avatar which might prevent others from copying it, but those rights would not allow them to use the avatar without permission from the owner of the copyright in the underlying work namely the Metaverse platform owner.

12.30 The derivative ownership/authorship situation is most likely to arrive where an avatar is based upon an existing copyrighted character or where the avatar is based upon the template provided by the platform owner and the personalisation or adaptation is not such that the degree of originality to attain the form of joint ownership has been attained. Much will then depend on the degree of adaptation that an avatar user is allowed to make and actually does make. To make matters even more complicated avatars could be considered a collective work in the same way as an anthology encyclopaedia in which various contributions which are separate and independent are collected to form a whole. On such a view an avatar might be considered to be no more than an individual contribution to the assembled collective work. In such scenario, because each contribution to the collective work is viewed as distinct from copyright in a collective work as a whole, each user would likely be the creator or author of their avatar, they would own their avatar's copyright whilst the platform owner and Metaverse provider would be considered the creator of the computer programme and any other displays and/or visuals that the programme was able to generate without significant input from that avatar user.

12.31 In terms of tips for both Metaverse platform owners and users of copyrighted works in the Metaverse, the authors suggest that all contractual agreements and licences that relate to copyrighted works should be reviewed and checked in detail so that their scope and effect is understood. Wherever possible there should be registration of copyrights in assets and software to be used in the Metaverse. In addition, technological measures to prevent unauthorised distribution of the copyrighted works and to prevent security circumvention should be implemented wherever possible.
