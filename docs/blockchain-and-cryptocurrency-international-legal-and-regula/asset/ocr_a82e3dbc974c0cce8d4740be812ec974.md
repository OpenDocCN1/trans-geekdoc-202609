Indeed, in relation to the UK, the authors wrote Cybersecurity Law and Practice,¹ which sets out the framework of civil and criminal laws governing cyberspace. Cyberspace is essentially the space of the Metaverse, and it is submitted that the same legal framework, the law of computers and the online, digital environment, will apply to the Metaverse. That legal framework includes cybercrime and offences under the Computer Misuse Act 1990 and liability under the Data Protection Act 2018 and the General Data Protection Regulations 2018.

12.36 The Computer Misuse Act 1990 will remain useful for prosecuting cyber criminality in the Metaverse. A computer is not defined in the Act but the interpretation adopted by the courts is very wide and, as well as the contemporary meaning of computer, includes any device capable of storing, processing or retrieving information. Section 1 creates a hacking offence where a person interferes with data or someone else’s computer without permission for the specific data or computer. This is the core unauthorised access or what is colloquially termed the hacking offence and an additional facilitation offence is created by section 2 where the unauthorised access, or hacking, is with intent to commit another offence. Section 3 criminalises acts to impair the working of the computer, such as a ransomware, Trojan horse or denial of service attack and is so widely interpreted as to cover the situation where information that told a lie about itself was inserted into a computer; for example information that purported to derive from an authorised source when, in fact, it did not, would constitute the offence. One can imagine countless situations where such a misrepresentation and offence might occur in a Metaverse context.

12.37 In addition to these offences the Act further criminalises the secondary parties who prop up cyber criminals by making, supplying or obtaining the electronic or other materials for use in these attacks. They then will be caught by the legislation when we move in to the Metaverse and Web 3. Significantly these offences can be prosecuted extraterritorially, so beyond the borders of the UK and across the globe where a significant link to the UK can be demonstrated. This link will exist not only where the accused was a British National but also where any computer in the cyber attack chain was located in the UK. This could be the device used to launch the attach, the target or victim computer or any computer in between. In the context of the Metaverse this will enable a prosecution and UK jurisdiction where cyber crime touches a UK based computer.

12.38 The Online Safety Bill is, at the time of writing, still passing through the House of Commons and not yet law. If one assumes it is enacted in its current or very similar form, then the question is whether the regime it creates for a regulated online service will apply to the Metaverse.
