Blockchain technology, whilst it can allow for the secure distribution of content to authorised users, can also be an agent for distribution of material that infringes copyright. **3.53**  There is also the issue of how liability is attributed. **3.54**  These are questions that do not have answers as yet and will, of necessity, have to be resolved by the courts at some point in the future.

**3.55**  The blockchain, however, can also provide significant benefits to intellectual property owners.

**3.56**  There are advantages to using blockchain in this field as well as the difficulties outlined above. Due to the immutability of blockchain, registration of work creates a full proof record of ownership. It also aids any subsequent licensing or assignment of the work, creating a permanent record in a similar way.

**3.57**  Using the Proof of Existence platform is a good example of the use of blockchain technology in order to evidence ownership. The process is achieved by the owner anonymously uploading a document, and a hash being generated providing proof of ownership, thus achieving an audit trail in the event of subsequent dispute.

**3.58**  Blockchain confirms who the creators are, when things were created, and puts it all in an immutable form so nothing can be edited or questioned.

# What is the legal status of decentralised autonomous organisations?

**3.59**  Decentralised autonomous organisations (‘DAOs’) are used to execute smart contracts and record activity on the blockchain. For more information on DAOs, see **Chapter 11**.

**3.60**  They are therefore a quasi-tribunal in the event of a dispute. Whether they welcome that responsibility or seek the obligations it may impose is an unanswered question. What is their legal status? It is unlikely that they have any since they, in the absence of agreement, do not derive any from statute or case law.
