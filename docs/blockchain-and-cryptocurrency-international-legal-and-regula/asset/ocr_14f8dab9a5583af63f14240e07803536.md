While the proposal does not mention crypto, its new rules would let regulators probe into crypto platforms and even decentralised finance (DeFi) protocols. Theoretically, the SEC is proposing to ‘expand Regulation ATS for alternative trading systems (ATS) that trade government securities, NMS stock, and other securities’, ‘extend Regulation SCI to ATSs that trade government securities’ and ‘amend the SEC rule regarding the definition of an “exchange”’.

The aforementioned SEC Chair Gary Gensler said that the amendments aim to close a ‘regulatory gap’ stemming from trading platforms that are not registered as exchanges or brokerages with the SEC. The proposed rules would extend existing regulations concerning platforms that trade treasuries and other government securities. This proposal would bind those platforms to register and be subject to regulation, which would ‘promote resiliency and greater access in the Treasury market’. This expansion of the exchange definition could also serve as a backdoor way to rein in crypto. ‘The expansive definition that’s being proposed for exchanges will cover a lot of potential platforms that haven’t thought necessarily that they would be covered including those in the traditional security space, as well as in the crypto space.’ That aligns with the SEC chair’s position for tighter DeFi oversight. ‘Even though they are decentralized, with no central entity in charge, DeFi projects that reward participants with incentives or digital tokens could enter territory that is subject to SEC regulation.’

+   3. There will be a 30-day window counting from 26 January 2022 for blockchain industry insiders, trading platforms, and other stakeholders to comment on the new SEC proposed plan – an unusually short comment period for such a significant regulatory proposal. Coupled with President Biden’s planned executive order as may reflect regulators’ wish to tighten the grip on the exploding decentralised finance market.

+   4. For those interested in non-fungible tokens (NFTs), the US Treasury has indicated that NFTs will remain outside of the scope of financial regulation on the basis that the sale of NFTs typically depends on the utility or unique value the NFT gives the holder and is more akin to a digital collector item than a financial services product. Although the Treasury acknowledged that the NFT market has grown considerably in recent months and new types of NFTs have emerged which blur the boundary, the Treasury stated that it is not the government’s intention to apply financial promotions regulation to non-financial products. In addition, as the NFT market is evolving rapidly and remains at an early stage of development, the Treasury said that the government does not yet have sufficient information on risks and use-cases.
