# 9.3 Right to be forgotten and right to erasure

9.3 Since that landmark decision, the law has moved on and the right to rectification and, more significantly, the right to erase are now a central plank of the General Data Protection Regulation (‘GDPR’). This came into force in all European Union jurisdictions in May 2018.

9.4 The importance of the principles which are enshrined in the GDPR cannot be overstated. It places personal data at the front and centre of data regulation going forward, and it imposes severe maximum fines for breach. The decision by the UK to leave the EU ushered in, effective from 1 January 2021, the UK GDPR.⁴ There are some changes from the EU GDPR but many of those are beyond the scope of this work. Significantly the UK Parliament passed the Data Protection Act 2018 which adopted the principles of GDPR and the principles referred to above, ie those affecting the rights of erasure, rectification or inaccuracy, remain relevant for the purposes of UK data protection legislation.

9.5 It would also be a mistake for those living outside Europe to dismiss the importance of the GDPR. Due to strict rules on the transfer of data to countries and parties outside of the European Union, and in an effort to ensure high standards are met and kept when using personal data across the world, those in the United States, South America and Asia are realising the importance of compliance and the consequences of non-compliance.

9.6 Whilst a detailed analysis of the provisions of UK GDPR is outside the scope of this work, it is of assistance to examine a few provisions in order to properly assess the significance of the Regulation on blockchain in particular, and to examine how other decentralised ledger technologies (‘DLTs’) are seeking to deal with the challenges of compliance.

# Right to erasure

9.7 This is an obligations-based provision codified in Article 17(1)(a)–(f) of the UK GDPR.⁵ It provides for personal data to be erased immediately where the data is no longer needed for the original purpose for which it was collected or where the consent has been withdrawn and there are no overriding legitimate grounds for processing that data.
