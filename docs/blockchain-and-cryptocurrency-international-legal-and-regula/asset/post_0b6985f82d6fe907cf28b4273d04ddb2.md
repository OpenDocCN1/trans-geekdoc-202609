## Decentralised autonomous organisations: regulation and liability

### UNDERSTANDING DECENTRALISED AUTONOMOUS ORGANISATIONS

11.1 It is essential to first understand what a decentralised autonomous organisation (‘DAO’) is. To do that, one also needs to understand what a decentralised organisation is. These are not organisations in a conventional sense in that, whilst they do have some hallmarks of a company, they do not have people that are organised into a form of hierarchy and led, in their entirety, by the directing human mind. A decentralised organisation is a computer program that has no manager or leader. It involves users and runs on a peer-to-peer network. A peer-to-peer network means that each computer gets information from every other machine on the network rather than from one big central server. The collective contents of the network are at the command of each connected machine and enable the direct exchange of services or data between computers. In such an environment, service desktops and personal computers that make up a network become equal peers that contribute some or all of their resources to the overall computing effort. In a decentralised organisation, a set of users connect and interact with one another pursuant to a protocol which is programmed through code and is enforced on a blockchain.

11.2 Taking a decentralised organisation first, it operates under the usual concepts of a company but it has a management structure that is decentralised so that there is no board of directors or other central brain that is managing it. In the case of a decentralised organisation, the users who are interacting with each other are the ones making decisions according to the protocol that is imprinted in the code and enforced on the blockchain. The contract is imprinted in the blockchain and it maintains a record of important transactions.

11.3 An example would be that, in a conventional company, there would need to be a record of a shareholder’s holdings and the ability for shareholders to cast votes on various issues. In a decentralised organisation, this would be effected via the contract which was encoded in the blockchain through the interaction of the decentralised organisation’s users.

11.4 At today’s evolutionary stage, a DAO materialises as a smart contract – a piece of code – executed on top of an increasingly opaque stack of distributed networking and consensus technology, such as the Ethereum blockchain or similar blockchains.

11.6 The important distinction between a decentralised organisation and a DAO is the addition of the word ‘autonomous’. That means what it says. DAOs are autonomous via a combination of contracts that encode the laws and behaviours of the organisation. These are not traditional paper contracts but coded contracts (smart contracts) that have been encoded into the blockchain, thus creating one all-encompassing, comprehensive decentralised smart contract.

11.7 The effect and impact of having a comprehensive single decentralised smart contract is very important. This means that, unlike any traditional company or indeed a decentralised organisation, the DAO has no requirement for human involvement or interaction. Because everything has been pre-programmed and encoded, the decentralised autonomous organisation can automatically execute contractual clauses in the blockchain when it regards conditions as satisfied and direct itself without the need for human intervention.

11.8 Decentralised organisations and DAOs do have some aspects in common, but the important distinction is that DAOs remove the human mind from the equation as human intervention is not required in any way. In contrast, a decentralised organisation will have smart contracts but operation will be reliant on users and human intervention. When one appreciates that a DAO runs autonomously on the blockchain and is controlled entirely by its code, without direction from the human mind, one begins to appreciate the difficulties that may arise both in practical terms and also in terms of regulation. To be clear, a DAO is capable of interacting with humans in relation to its functions, but it does not need humans to function and the decisions behind the services that it provides will exclude human direction. A DAO might be thought of as a decentralised organisation that has its own brain in the form of autonomous intelligence. It will become possible, in our view, to regard a DAO as close to artificial intelligence.

11.9 DAOs then might be viewed as an emerging type of organisation. They are particularly prevalent in the Ethereum blockchain ecosystem and are useful where a cohort looks to coordinate around a shared objective. That coordination can be facilitated through smart contracts and the advantages they bring by being implemented autonomously. This autonomous and immutable implementation is what gives a DAO an important advantage over traditional, hierarchically structured organisations. DAOs consequently bring new possibilities; they bring the power to solve old problems in new ways through their automated decision making and freedom from interference by managers' boards or other human participants that influence a traditional corporate entity.

11.10 DAOs are akin to a cooperative but without the human influence that often acts as a bar to cooperation and aided by the transparency that blockchain brings. A DAO is also decentralised and autonomously powered by smart code logic. In the context of a DAO, decentralised translates into a number of teams and individuals being responsible for and driving a set of goals so that job roles are not fixed and members leave and arrive in a fast-moving, fluid manner. It follows that a DAO ought to structure its smart contracts and coordinate in a way that provides benefits and incentives to its members. These incentives are not only in the positive feeling members get for participating and furthering the goals of an organisation they participate and believe in, but there can also be direct financial incentives through tokens, or non-fungible tokens (NFTs). The remuneration is not always financial though, as the NFTs a DAO generates for its activities or to reward members are often a key piece in establishing a communal identity within the overarching DAO itself. The transparency of DAOs, achieved through blockchain, is exemplified by each step or transaction being visible to anyone at any time. In addition, it cryptographically protects the anonymity of its members yet, by virtue of its nature, is truly transparent and open as to its actions and purpose.

### 11.11 To be fully functional a DAO would require:

- a set of rules through which it can operate;
- tokens or funding that it can use to reward their members for certain behaviours;
- tokens conferring voting rights for establishing the operation rules;
- a structure that enables each investor to configure the organisation.

### 11.12 DAOs can be an efficient and effective vehicle where speed of purpose is critical.

An example of this in recent action was ConstitutionDAO which managed to raise $47 million in Ether in November 2021 with a view to purchasing an original copy of the US Constitution. It failed in its quest but raised this huge sum in just five days in order to bid at the Sotheby's auction. Even though it was outbid at the auction it was a demonstration of the use case of DAOs and their capability to recruit large numbers of individuals to crowdfund a single, joint purpose. It also exposed one weakness of the DAO in the context of an auction bid, namely that its inbuilt transparency meant that competing bidders could see how much ConstitutionDAO had raised and, knowing the limit of its funds, could plan to pitch a winning bid just above that level. That strategy was likely used by the CEO of Citadel Securities when securing the winning bid.

### 11.13 The market for NFTs and a digital art market that is now, in global value terms the match of the traditional art market, has focussed attention on DAOs and their ability to quickly and efficiently raise significant funds for art purchases.

The PleasrDAO is a DAO that was established to purchase the Plplpleasr artwork and has gone on to identify and purchase more art works since. Its declared philosophy is that it

> 'distributes the ownership of the DAO in the form of tokens. That gives every member a fraction of its assets—the two NFTs and any funds in the treasury. Each member participates in the DAO's governance through a group chat. And if someone were to exit the DAO, their token would be up for grabs internally.'
