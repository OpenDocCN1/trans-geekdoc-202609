12.51 The preceding paragraphs should serve to demonstrate that the question as to which jurisdiction and whose laws will be a valid and searching question for the Metaverse. Jurisdiction is complex, it is made more complex by an increasing willingness of countries to enact law that are expressed as being extra-territorial by design; that is to say, that nation state is unilaterally declaring its laws as being applicable in the event of breach and/or a prescribed connection to that country. The UK, US, China and Russia have all passed laws with extra-territorial design and these laws include cyber and / or data protection legislation. That is particularly relevant in the context of the Metaverse given it is essentially cyberspace. The advent of these extra-territorial cyber laws and regulations will increasingly lead to a conflict of jurisdiction and geo-politically sparring. The internet has already stress tested our global laws and exposed not only the lack of harmony but the tensions that a borderless cyberspace can bring to laws conceived in an analogue, bordered world. Geo-politics and divergent political and cultural philosophies mean that there is no easy or likely solution in sight.

12.52 The hope must be that universal jurisdiction or something akin to that doctrine can be developed so that there can be certainty of outcome and the avoidance of conflict. Certainly, Metaverse owners can assist by creating user agreements and licences that incorporate standard terms governing conduct, but that approach also requires cooperation and external policing. Sadly, history tells us that international cooperation in this area is unlikely. The GDPR was expected to herald a brave new age in data protection with Europe leading the way in defending an individual’s personal information rights. And yet this laudable aim did not come to pass; Russia and China passed laws which ultimately gave the state sovereignty over data and the world has a fractured, dysfunctional set of cyber security laws. The authors nonetheless posit the possibility, however novel, and remote, that the Metaverse might evolve to become truly interoperable in every sense and enable a harmonisation of regulation and jurisdiction as never witnessed before.
