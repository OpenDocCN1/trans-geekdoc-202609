# CRYPTOCURRENCY

**1.14** ‘A cryptoasset is a cryptographically secured digital representation of value or contractual rights that uses some type of DLT and can be transferred, stored or traded electronically.’³ This rather wide definition that was adopted by the Cryptoassets Taskforce Report gives an idea of the many types of cryptoasset and why, for some, the term cryptoasset is preferred to cryptocurrency; cryptocurrency appearing narrower and more likely to risk an interpretation that its use depends on making payment or equating to a currency. There are of course, many different types of cryptoasset to include various cryptocurrencies which range from the best known Bitcoin to another 15,000 varieties according to Coinmarketcap.com (as at 14 December 2021). To this can be added the dozens of types of stablecoins which derive their value from an external asset be that fiat currency such as the US dollar (USD), or a commodity such as gold or by being backed by another cryptoasset or an algorithm. Whilst they may be called stablecoins and tethered to reduce their volatility, their stability or predictability is not perfect and they have been in the sights of regulators due to the true backing or detail being misleading.
