Any material interim changes to a DLT Provider's business model will need prior approval from the GFSC at which point consideration will be given to whether the complexity categorisation of the firm needs to be amended.

# 6.37 The application process at this stage, will largely mirror the application process applied to all other activities authorised and supervised by the GFSC. One exception is that once the firm has submitted a complete application and paid the balance of the assessed application fee, applicants will be invited to deliver a presentation to the GFSC. Any specific requirements based on the nature and complexity of the proposed business will be communicated at the time of the initial application assessment. Generally, the presentation is expected to cover the following areas:

+   - background on the key individuals driving the business including their relevant skills and experience;
+   - business plan, including structure of the company, products and services, the target market, and strategy;
+   - financial projections; and
+   - evidence how the firm will meet the nine regulatory outcomes/principles.

# 6.38 It is expected that GFSC staff present will include members from the Risk and Innovation team, any individuals required from the Panel of Experts, and any key GFSC decision makers. The presentation will be an integral part of the authorisations process and will give the applicant an opportunity to demonstrate how they will meet the GFSC's regulatory outcomes/principles.

# 6.39 Once a licence has been granted, an onsite visit will be completed. This will give the firm the opportunity to evidence to the GFSC that the processes and controls implemented and communicated during the presentation are effective and actually work in practice. That will complete the licensing process.

# Hong Kong

6.40 The blanket ban on cryptocurrency trading and ICOs bypassed Hong Kong, due to their separate political system from China, therefore resulting in many crypto-related businesses initially moving to Hong Kong. However, like South Korea, Hong Kong is very aware of the risks that come with developing technology and plans to ‘intervene when appropriate’.
