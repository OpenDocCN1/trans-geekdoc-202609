# Voluntary guidance in the US

8.83 The US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) publishes a set of guidelines, the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, which aims to mitigate organisational cybersecurity risks. The framework is divided into three parts, ‘Core’, ‘Profile’ and ‘Tiers’:

+   • The ‘Framework Core’ contains an array of activities, outcomes and references about aspects and approaches to cybersecurity.
+   • The ‘Framework Implementation Tiers’ are used by an organisation to clarify for itself and its partners how it views cybersecurity risk and the degree of sophistication of its management approach.
+   • A ‘Framework Profile’ is a list of outcomes that an organisation has chosen from the categories and subcategories, based on its needs and risk assessments.

8.84 An organisation using the Framework will develop a ‘Current Profile’ which describes its cybersecurity activities and outcomes to be achieved. The Framework then encourages the development of a ‘Target Profile’, which is a baseline profile tailored to the sector or type of organisation. Defined steps are then used to move the organisation from the current profile to its target profile.

8.85 The NIST Cybersecurity Framework identifies five ‘functions’ which are subdivided into a total of 23 ‘categories’. For each category, it defines a number of subcategories of cybersecurity outcomes and security controls, with 108 subcategories in all. The five functions are:

+   1. Identify.
+   2. Protect.
+   3. Detect.
+   4. Respond.
+   5. Recover.
