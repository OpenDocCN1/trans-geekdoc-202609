# GENERAL DATA PROTECTION REGULATION

**1.47** Finally, no book dealing with regulation after 25 May 2018 could be considered complete without mention of the General Data Protection Regulation (‘GDPR’ – see **Appendix 1**). On that date the GDPR came into force across Europe (including the UK) but impacted on every business worldwide that handles the personal information of European citizens. It mattered not where the business (or the personal data) was situated, only that it was the personal data of Europeans. In the scramble to comply, many reorganised their storage systems and set up new organisational policies to ensure this data was collected, processed, retained and erased in accordance with the principles of the GDPR; this meant there had to be a legitimate basis for dealing with such data and that it was not kept for longer than necessary, and it was erased when required, to include at the request of a data subject.

**1.48** What few failed to appreciate was how blockchain could comply with the GDPR and whether rights given to individuals could be consistent in the context of blockchain. **Chapter 9** looks at the tension between the rights to deletion and erasure, as enshrined in the GDPR, and the practical difficulties that might need to be addressed to comply. That tension arises from the right to be forgotten and the right to erasure or amendment as enshrined in the GDPR when set against the immutability of DLT and the blockchain whose composition and action run counter to the exercise of those rights.

**1.49** Chapter 9 considers when the GDPR’s provisions will bite and on what types of data and whether the conflict between these rights and blockchain’s immutability can be circumvented, cured or mitigated. Any organisation’s system that collects, stores or processes personal data and incorporates blockchain or other DLT should be scrutinised carefully to ensure that it will be capable of GDPR compliance.

# THE METAVERSE

**1.50** This Second Edition includes the Metaverse at **Chapter 12**. This was included because blockchain and cryptoassets are fundamental and intrinsic to its operation and the authors felt it was a development that could not be ignored. Non-fungible tokens (‘NFTs’) will play a particularly crucially role in the Metaverse. They will be the means by which ‘sales’ and transactions of property are effected and recorded on the blockchain. We explain the risks and legal challenges when using NFTs in the context of the Metaverse and emphasise the legal maxim, buyer beware; in short, virtual property should not be confused with real property and the legal scaffolding that surrounds virtual property in the Metaverse, such as user agreements or end user licences, may render ‘ownership’ different to the legal, conventional sense and can create a perilous scenario for the ‘buyer’.
