**10.3** This is now presented by the multitude of ESG metrics (*Environmental, Social, and Governance*) that many organisations have to comply with and show on their annual reports. ESG metrics are non-financial instruments that measure risks in organisations and are used in investment analysis as another indicator of exposure. These metrics can now be compiled, verified and stored immutably on blockchains via smart contracts and data oracles. They consist of:

+   - **Environmental metrics** which measure environmental (eg emissions, efficiency, waste and recycling).
+   - **Social metrics** which measure social and human capital risks (eg work force, health and safety, logistics and data security).
+   - **Governance metrics** which measure governance risks (eg opacity, ethics, and corporate structure including governance compliance and reporting).

**10.4** Due to the need for any organisations to reduce costs to increase profit margins many companies are tempted to pay mere lip service to their ESG responsibilities.[^9] Often obtaining cheaper raw materials from ethically questionable sources, not adhering to good human resources or corporate governance practices. Several important businesses have even undertaken ‘green washing’ exercises[^10] to hide or disguise their misdemeanours with a thin patina of credibility.
