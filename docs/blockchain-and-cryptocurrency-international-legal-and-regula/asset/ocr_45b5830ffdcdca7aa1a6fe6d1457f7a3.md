**2.76** The stated objective of the initiative was to launch a new global currency which would ultimately be used, via a standalone App, to purchase goods and services via Facebook, Facebook Messenger, and WhatsApp. It was said to have the backing of the Visa and MasterCard payment networks as well as PayPal, Uber and eBay. Proponents of the initiative stated that the new ‘coin’ will not create any new money, but the backing fiat currency would act as a reserve, allowing the digital partner to be used on the internet with the resulting fall in transaction services.

**2.77** Corporates have issued forms of coin before (JP Morgan or JPM Coin being one example), but Libra Coin appeared to be significantly different. The stated aim of Libra Coin was to produce a global currency – in other words, to be used as a medium of exchange rather than a commodity upon which to speculate. Because it was being backed by Facebook, with the apparent support of Visa, MasterCard and eBay, this cryptocurrency would have had the power to impact on traditional markets, without huge volatility, and to effectively be used on a day-to-day basis.

**2.78** Critics raised some disquiet that data may be shared across services. Facebook will be a member of the trusted companies via Calibra, a newly created subsidiary that would have offered a digital wallet for Libra. Individuals and merchants would have been able use Calibra to store, send and receive Libras, with Facebook able to potentially use this data created within Calibra for their own purposes. Facebook then pledged not to use Calibra data to improve ad targeting.

**2.79** The supporters of Libra Coin had pointed to the advantages of the new coin in terms of opening more opportunities for online commerce, the scope for using for micropayments by those without bank accounts, and the attendant benefits in paying for digital content.

**2.80** Understanding the regulatory environment, though, was crucial to correctly predicting the failure of Libra Coin. It was anticipated that Libra Coin will be prohibited in India and China, excluding a third of the world’s population from using the currency.
