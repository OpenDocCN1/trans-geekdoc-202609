7.88 The scope of the legislation is sufficiently broad to capture those issuing the gamut of tokens under an ICO. Further, the territorial affect is to cover every EU state.

7.89 It is highly unlikely that MiCA will come into force before 2024/2025; however, in summary, the scope of the regulation will extend to trading platform operators, custodial services, and exchange service providers, and will cover the placement of crypto assets, executing payment transactions in asset-referenced tokens and providing advice on crypto assets.

7.90 The proposed regime would only allow crypto asset services to be provided by a legal person with a registered office in an EU Member State, and that legal person would have to be authorised and licensed in an EU/ EEA Member State as a crypto asset service provider. ‘Passporting’ will be permissible throughout the EU once authorised in a Member State.

7.91 In order to obtain a crypto asset service provider licence, an entity will need to meet requirements similar to those applicable to financial service providers including capital requirements, organisational and conduct requirements.

7.92 E-money and stablecoin (‘asset referenced tokens’) issuers will be subject to more stringent regulation. These issuers will have to be established within the EU (excluding UK companies), and have higher capital reserves. These requirements will not apply to issuers of general crypto assets.

# CURRENT EU PROVISIONS

7.93 Until MiCA comes into effect, those trading in cryptoassets must abide by the guidance of the European Securities and Markets Authority (‘ESMA’), and the existing EU Directives.

The ESMA has issued two statements which constitute the extent of specific regulatory oversight, from the EU, on a supranational level.

7.94 Both statements were issued on 13 November 2017: the first alerted investors to the risks involved in investing in ICOs;47 and the second alerted firms, involved in ICOs, of the need to meet regulatory requirements.48
