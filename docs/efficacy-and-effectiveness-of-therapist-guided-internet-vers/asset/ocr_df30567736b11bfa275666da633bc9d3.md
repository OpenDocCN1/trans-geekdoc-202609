Some methods need accuracy for both A and B dependencies, whereas doubly robust methods can achieve consistent estimates with only As or B and Az correctly modelled. Even methods not doubly robust might be preferred over naive regression analysis approaches, which are biased whenever the non-flexible parametric model is mis-specified relative to reality. Even application of mis-specified models that miss some covariates tends to provide more accurate estimates than naive approaches that estimate ATE with simple regression or mean difference.

A targeted maximum likelihood estimator (TMLE) based on super learners has the asymptotic double-robustness property, but it is additionally efficient, well defined, and well behaved in finite samples. A TMLE integrates features from modern statistics, machine learning, and causal inference. A TMLE can optimise the bias-variance trade-off for a target parameter (eg, ATE) instead of the treatment-prediction and outcome-prediction models. The primary motivation to use a TMLE is its ability to combine multiple desirable properties, perhaps representing the state of the art in covariate-based counterfactual causal inference. The availability of such procedures enhances the value of administrative, population-based registries, which often contain abundant aetiological information.

Developments in Nordic countries enable the linking of psychotherapy data with information in national registries. In this study, we used TMLE on these rich information sources to estimate the efficacy difference between therapist-guided iCBT and fCBT in a causally informative design that is not an RCT, thus circumventing typical challenges of RCTs while providing evidence on the same causal question. Our naturalistic data inform about effectiveness, and the counterfactual analysis informs about both effectiveness and efficacy if all real patients would receive fCBT versus guided iCBT. The new gold standard for evidence will then consist of combined inferences from RCT meta-analyses and TMLE applied to naturalistic datasets. Our investigation serves as a pragmatic proof of concept for the use of TMLE and related methods to address the question of the ATE between fCBT and therapist-guided iCBT.
