
# Constructing, Configuring, and Managing a Firewall

Remember, though, you need to balance the needs of the organization with the security requirements. While it is true that a DMZ provides more protection than a single firewall, it also costs more.

Many firewall products are available for small businesses and those working from home. **SOHO** (small office/home office) **VPN** hardware firewalls are often built on a secure virtual private network (**VPN**) connection to the company network to transfer email and sensitive files. Consider a **SOHO** network one in which there are typically 25 or fewer computers and devices on the network.

In many cases, the **SOHO** firewalls have additional features, such as an antivirus tool, **IP** filtering, web content filtering, router options (router/firewall combinations), intrusion detection, and denial of service (**DoS**)/distributed denial of service (**DDoS**) attack detection. Some of the firewalls come with a **Linux** or **Linux**-like **OS** and use `ipchains` to manage their filter rules.

A **SOHO VPN** hardware firewall is the best solution when you already have a working network and want to provide remote access. For example, you may already connect your personal computers within your office. If you want to open a new office in another location, you could connect both offices with a **SOHO VPN** firewall at each office. It will create a secure connection to transfer sensitive data (bank information, customer information, or company-related information) from one office to the other. Another example is a mobile worker, such as a salesperson working in the field. The salesperson could use the **VPN**’s secure connection to access company files and presentations or customer-related information directly from the company office, even when working in the field.

Recall that a firewall allows you to restrict unauthorized access between the Internet and an internal network. It exists to block unauthorized connections and keep outside attackers from penetrating the internal network. It also prevents inside connections from reaching out to the Internet without authorization. By monitoring inside users, firewalls can prevent the sending of sensitive information, such as personally identifiable information or sensitive corporate data to the Internet.

## pfSense Requirements

As mentioned previously, one of the main advantages of **pfSense** is that it will run on a variety of hardware. In any case, you will need at least two network interface controllers (**NICs**) in your **pfSense** machine, because firewalls traverse two or more network connections. You may need a crossover cable, as well. Many switches today perform auto-sensing; in that case, there is no need to use a crossover cable.

There are many possible configurations for a **pfSense** installation, but the minimum requirements for **pfSense** are:

*   64-bit **Intel** or **AMD** processor
*   Must be able to boot from **USB**, optical drive, or **Memstick**
*   Two **NICs**

Installation of **pfSense** is simple. Visit the **Netgate** website at <https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/install/installing-pfsense.html> and follow the instructions. Before attempting any download, be sure that the system where the software will be installed meets or exceeds the minimum specifications. You also need a consistent online connection so that the installation process is not interrupted. If something does go wrong with the download, you can delete the application and start again.

## Planning a Firewall Implementation with pfSense

When planning a firewall, you should use standard hardware, typically from major manufacturers, which will provide you with considerable benefits, including:

*   **Flexibility**—You can easily add a redundant array of independent disks (**RAID**), redundant power supplies, additional random access memory (**RAM**), and a range of network interface cards.
*   **Performance and value for the money**—The average desktop **PC** will have many times the processing power and memory complement of most mid-range hardware firewall appliances. Office **PCs** have become a commodity, offering exceptional value for the money.
*   **Support**—The major **PC** manufacturers can support their products virtually anywhere in the world, including rapid, on-site repair or replacement if the customer requires. Alternatively, in most major cities, several third-party computer companies also support such hardware. In contrast, the support available for smaller manufacturers’ equipment is often limited to repairs done after the machine was shipped back to the factory.

What kind of firewall is right for an organization? Truthfully, no single correct and definite answer exists. A security policy developed by a **Fortune 500** company certainly will not be suitable for a small business owner (and vice versa).

The following are brief scenarios to illustrate different firewall designs to meet different needs.

### Firewalling a Small Organization: Packet Filtering or Application-Level Firewall, a Proxy Implementation

Imagine a smaller organization that requires Internet access by employees, but does not host a web server or a **VPN** server. All users in the organization require access to the Internet, and they need a layer of protection against Internet-based attacks.

A proxy server or a firewall such as **pfSense** would be sufficient. All users could access the Internet through the **pfSense** firewall, and it would provide protection against external attacks. Although **pfSense** is the example examined in depth in this chapter, many other products can provide the same level of service for a small organization.

### Firewalling Medium and Large Organizations: Application-Level Firewall and Packet Filtering, a Hybrid System

Employees at medium and large organizations typically access the Internet daily for a wide variety of different business purposes. Additionally, organizations of this size host a web server that has a high volume of access from customers. Mobile workers use a **VPN** server to access internal resources.

These organizations might decide that a **DMZ** with two application-level firewalls and packet filtering is adequate for their needs. The web server and **VPN** server belong in the **DMZ** to be accessible from the Internet, while the internal network requires additional layers of protection.
