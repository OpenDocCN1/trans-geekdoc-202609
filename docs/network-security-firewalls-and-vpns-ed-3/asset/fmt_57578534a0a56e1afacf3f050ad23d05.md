
The N-tier deployment creates a series of subnets separated by firewalls. The DMZ or extranet subnet serves as a buffer network between the Internet and the private LAN. The “N” in N-tier is a variable to be replaced with whatever number of tiers you actually build in your network.

### CHAPTER SUMMARY

There are several common network topologies, all with distinct benefits and negative considerations. Knowing the organization’s goals, the ability to employ each physical design, and how the network should be configured logically can aid in making the right topology decision. Although some small organizations could be served by the bus topology, it is usually reserved for very small installations. A mesh topology offers the most redundancy, but it is also the most expensive.

Although most topologies can be implemented to follow specific logical topographical frameworks, it can be less secure to implement some logical designs on physical networks that require all devices to know and recognize all other devices. It can also be less secure where flow from one system to another is precluded by physical connectivity to the next device, as in bus and ring implementations. Even if those devices should not communicate with the next device in the network, the device is still known to the computer; therefore, an attempt may be made to access it. This is why security is a necessary consideration prior to constructing a network, and it continues to be an important element as that network is managed and maintained.

This chapter also took a detailed look at IPv4 and IPv6, as well as various network infrastructures.

## CHAPTER 4

NETWORK SECURITY IS ESSENTIAL to every organization, from an individual computer at home to a multinational corporation. The foundations of security apply universally, no matter the size, purpose, or function of the computers and network.

Implementing network security requires you to work through the installation, deployment, and configuration of all components, but it must begin with thorough planning and design. That means considering layered defenses, proper use of protocols, communication management, intrusion detection and prevention systems, system hardening, and more. You can significantly improve the security of your organization’s computer systems and network based on some common, often simple, principles.

Following the suggestions in this chapter will reduce the risk of system compromise from accident, oversight, Mother Nature, or malicious intent.
