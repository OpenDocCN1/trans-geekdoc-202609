# Common Network Topologies and Infrastructures

# CHAPTER 3

COMPUTER NETWORKS allow organizations to share information and resources in order to allow for and enhance employee productivity. Instead of physically going to the location of a resource, if the desired item (such as a file, folder, software program, image, or video) is loaded on the network, and the user requesting the item has authority and is granted access to that item by the administrator, then the item can be accessed more quickly and efficiently from the user's desktop. This streamlines inventory management of data files and resources, and it can enhance communication and collaboration. Networks also provide centralized storage for important information and enables authorized users to access that information quickly and efficiently.

Networks can range in size from a few devices to thousands of interconnected computers, devices, and peripherals. Some networks are located in the same room or the same building, while other networks span the globe. Nontraditional devices, including gaming systems, smartphones, and Internet of Things (IoT) devices, are connectable and may constitute a network. How those networks are constructed, and the physical and logical ways in which each is laid out and communicates, is the network's topology.

Networks are composed of different devices connected together using a common addressing scheme; in this way, each device can be easily accessed, and messages to it can be quickly delivered. The primary methods of addressing are the use of Internet Protocol version 4 (IPv4) or Internet Protocol version 6 (IPv6) addressing. Addressing is how communication packets on a network can be sent and received by devices on the network.
