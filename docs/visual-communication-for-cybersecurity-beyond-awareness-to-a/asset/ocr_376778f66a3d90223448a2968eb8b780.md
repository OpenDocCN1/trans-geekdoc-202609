| Principle | Description | Illustration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Proximity | Objects that are closer together are perceived as more related than objects that are further apart. | [Visual: Two groups of 3x3 dots separated by space] |
| Figure/ground | Elements are perceived as either figure (the element in focus) or ground (the background on which the figure rests). | [Visual: Rubin's vase style graphic / red box] |
| Parallelism | Elements that are parallel to each other are seen as more related than elements not parallel to each other. | [Visual: Set of parallel diagonal lines] |
| Symmetry and order | People tend to perceive objects as symmetrical shapes that form around their centre. | [Visual: Brackets <> < > < >] |
| Continuity | Elements arranged on a line or curve are perceived as more related than elements not on the line or curve. | [Visual: Dots arranged on a curve] |
| Common region/element connectedness | Elements are perceived as part of a group if they are located within the same closed region. | [Visual: Dots inside a rectangular frame] |
| Good figure/Pragnanz/simplicity | People will perceive and interpret complex images as the simplest form(s) possible. The picture on the right is easier to read as three shapes than as one complex shape. | [Visual: Abstract black, red, and grey shapes] |
| Synchrony/common fate | Elements that move in the same direction are perceived as more related than elements that are stationary or that move in different directions. | [Visual: Red dots with directional arrows] |
| Uniform connectedness | Elements that are visually connected are perceived as more related than elements with no connection. | [Visual: Shapes connected by dotted lines] |
| Focal points | Elements with a point of interest, emphasis, or difference will capture and hold the viewer's attention. | [Visual: Grid of black dots with one red square] |
