# 1.1 INTRODUCTION

Steel bridge health monitoring deals with damage location identification and the extent determination inside the steel members utilizing collected vibration responses containing dynamic modal information of the steel bridge under analysis. The sensors used to collect the data present the challenge of data contamination due to unwanted noise from operational conditions and can be effectively eradicated by determining optimum sensor placing positions. The multi-objective optimization techniques utilize N Pareto optimum solutions to investigate and achieve more realistic results for real-world problems of damage determination in steel bridges. Steel-bridges-related problems such as damage identification, operational research, retrofitting decisions, service life forecasting etc., are solved using multi-objective optimization techniques available since the 1990s. The accurate and robust outcomes of optimization techniques enabled researchers to apply these techniques in various domains [1, 2].
