Where E is a \( n \times 1 \) eigenvector that meets all of the following conditions in Eq. (3.8). To construct a vector, each eigenvalue can be combined as \(\psi\) :

\(\psi = \left[ \psi_1, \psi_2, \psi_3, ..... , \psi_n \right]^t\)  
(3.9)

A fully connected state can only be achieved by having an \(\psi_2\), that is positive, as shown by the notation \(\psi_1 < \psi_2, < \psi_3 < ... < \psi_n\). It is also called “algebraic connectivity of \(\Gamma\) ” and can be determined by looking at its second-smallest Laplacian eigenvalue \(\psi_2\) and by checking to see whether there is at least one connection between nodes. There must be sufficient transmission power for a fully connected network under these conditions.

The foraging and mating behavior of the butterfly is modeled in a meta-heuristic algorithm called BOA. When compared to other meta-heuristics, BOA is distinguished by the fact that each butterfly has its distinct scent. The following is a possible formulation for the fragrance:

\(f = cI^a\)  
(3.10)

According to this formula, \(c\) represents the sensory modality, and \(I\) is the stimulus intensity; \(a\) denotes the power exponent depending on the extent of fragrance absorption \(f\) .

\(c\left(t+1\right)=c(t)+\left[\frac{0.25}{c(t).\text{Maxit}}\right]\)  
(3.11)
