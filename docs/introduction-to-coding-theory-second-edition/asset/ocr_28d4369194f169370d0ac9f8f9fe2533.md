We choose C2 to be the ternary sum-0 code [18,17,2]3. For C1 we use a linear [18,9,8]4 code (see also MacWilliams et al. [143]). Consider the cyclic quaternary codes of length 17 (see our theory in Chapter 13). The cyclotomic cosets are {0}, {1,4,13,16}, {2,8,9,15}, {3,5,12,14}, {6,7,10,11}.

The intervals {6,7,8,9,10,11} and {12,14,16,1,3,5} show that the cyclic codes C(C(2) ∪ C(6)) and C(C(1) ∪ C(3)) are [17,9,7]4 codes. The Roos bound (see Exercise 13.2.10), can be used to show that C({0} ∪ C(1) ∪ C(3)) has d = 8. In fact, I = {13,14} can be an interval, J = {0,4,8,16,3,7} is an interval minus a point (12) and I + J = {0} ∪ C(1) ∪ C(3). We have a chain [17,8,8]4 ⊂ [17,9,7]4. Application of construction X produces an [18,9,8]4 code.

As A2 has volume 6√3 and the Kschischang-Pasupathy lattice Γ is the union of M1 M2 = 4^9 3^17 cosets of A2^18, it has volume vol(Γ) = 6^18 3^9 / (4^9 3^17) = 3^10.

How about the packing radius ρ? Let us go over the argument from the preceding subsection again. Let x, y ∈ Γ, x ≠ y.

If x, y are in the same coset of A2^18, their distance is at least the minimum distance of A2, which is 2√3.

Let x ∈ N(v1, v2), y ∈ N(v1', v2'). If v1 ≠ v1', it follows that x − y has at least d1 = 8 coordinates with nonzero entries from A0. The distance between x and y is at least √8 × 1 = 2√2.

If, finally, v1 = v1', but v2 ≠ v2', then x − y has at least d2 = 2 coordinates with nonzero entries from A1. In this case, x, y are at distance ≥ √2 × 4 = 2√2.

We conclude that Γ has minimum distance 2√2 and packing radius √2. It follows from Equation 23.1 that the center density is δ(Γ) = ρ^36 / vol(Γ) = 2^18 / 3^10.

The original paper is Kschischang and Pasupathy [129].

# The Leech lattice

The 24-dimensional Leech lattice Λ is an extraordinary object. It is closely related to the extended binary Golay code [24,12,8]2. We will construct the Leech lattice by a variant of the coset code construction. Start from the one-dimensional chain Z ⊃ 2Z ⊃ 4Z ⊃ 8Z where we use {0,1} as representatives for 2Z in Z, {0,−2} as representatives for 4Z in 2Z and {0,4} as representatives of 8Z in 4Z.
