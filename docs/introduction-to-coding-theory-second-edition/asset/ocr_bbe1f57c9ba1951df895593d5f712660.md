**16.9 Lemma.** *Let f(X) be an irreducible polynomial. Then the period of a nonzero LSRS with characteristic polynomial f(X) is the order of X in the multiplicative group of the field F = 𝔽_q^r = 𝔽_q[X]/(f(X)). In particular, the period divides q^r − 1.*

# The trace representation

An LSRS can be described equivalently using the trace. As we made heavy use of field extensions and the trace in the theory of cyclic codes, this description probably is the most natural for us.

Consider an LSRS 𝒮 = (a_n) with entries in 𝔽_q whose characteristic polynomial f(X) is irreducible. We use the corresponding extension field F = 𝔽_q^r and the trace tr : F ⟶ 𝔽_q. Let ε ∈ F be the element corresponding to X, hence a root of f(X). The powers 1, ε, …, ε^{r−1} form a basis of F over 𝔽_q. There is therefore a uniquely determined element u ∈ F such that tr(ε^i u) = a_i for i = 0, …, r − 1 (see Exercise 16.2.2). In fact, this formula describes not just the initial values of the LSRS but the LSRS as a whole:

**16.10 Theorem.** *Let 𝒮 = (a_n) be an LSRS over 𝔽_q and f(X) its characteristic polynomial, irreducible of degree r. Let F = 𝔽_q^r be the extension field described by f(X), tr : F ⟶ 𝔽_q the trace and ε ∈ F a root of f(X). There is an element u ∈ F such that*

$$a_n = tr(ε^n u) \text{ for all } n.$$

**PROOF** The initial segment a_0, …, a_{r−1} is right. It suffices to show that the sequence (tr(ε^n u)) satisfies the same recurrence relation as (a_n). Let n ≥ r. Then

$$tr(ε^n u) - \sum_{i=1}^{r} c_i tr(ε^{n-i} u) = tr\big((ε^r - \sum_i c_i ε^{r-i}) ε^{n-r} u\big) = 0$$

as ε^r - \sum_i c_i ε^{r-i} = f(ε) = 0.

Theorem 16.10 is Theorem 6.24 of Lidl and Niederreiter's *Introduction to Finite Fields and Their Applications* [134], a standard textbook in this area.
