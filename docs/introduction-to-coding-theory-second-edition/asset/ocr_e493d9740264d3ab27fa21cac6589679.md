# FIGURE 1.4: Pascal's triangle

The triangle is known as the **Pascal triangle**. It can be used to compute the binomials recursively. In fact, each label not on the border is the sum of the labels directly to its northeast and to its northwest, in formulas
$$inom{n}{m} = inom{n-1}{m-1} + inom{n-1}{m} \text{for } 0 < m < n.$$

A direct formula to compute the binomials is
$$inom{n}{m} = \frac{n(n-1)\dots(n-m+1)}{m(m-1)\dots2\cdot1}.$$

As examples, $\binom{4}{2} = \frac{4\cdot3}{2}=6$, $\binom{5}{2}= \frac{5\cdot4}{2}=10$, $\binom{6}{3}= \frac{6\cdot5\cdot4}{3\cdot2}=20$ and in general $\binom{n}{2}= \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
