The error probability is $P \le 165p^3$.

Let $p = 10^{-4}$. Without coding, the block error probability is $P \approx 4 \cdot 10^{-4}$. With an information rate of $R = 4/7$, we reach $P \le 2.1 \cdot 10^{-7}$, and, with an information rate of $R = 4/11$, this can be pressed down to $P \le 1.65 \cdot 10^{-10}$. The repetition code is suddenly looking rather stupid in comparison.

# 2.4 Duality

**Basic concepts:** Dot product on $\mathbb{F}_2^n$, orthogonality, dual space, binary repetition code, sum zero code, check matrix, P-transform.

As is often the case in mathematics, duality is of great benefit also for coding theory. Everybody knows the dot product from physics. We can use it in our binary context as well.

**2.8 Definition.** The dot product is defined on $\mathbb{F}_2^n$ by

$(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)\cdot(y_1,y_2,\dots,y_n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i y_i.$
