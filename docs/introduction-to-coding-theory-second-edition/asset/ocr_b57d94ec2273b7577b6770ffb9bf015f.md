# Exercises 4

4.1. Prove the following slight generalization of Lagrange interpolation:
Let $a \ge 0$ be a natural number, $x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n$ different nonzero $\in F$ and $y_1,\dots,y_n$ arbitrary in $F$. Then there is exactly one polynomial $p(X)$ of degree $< n$ with coefficients in $F$ which satisfies

$$x_i^a p(x_i)=y_i,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n.$$

4.2. Let $a \ge 0$ be a natural number and $q$ a prime-power. Define a $q$-ary code of length $q-1$ with coordinates indexed by $0 \ne u \in \mathbb{F}_q$ and with codewords indexed by the polynomials $p(X)$ of degree $< k$, where $k < q$. The entry of the codeword indexed by $p(X)$ in coordinate $u$ is $u^a p(u)$.
Determine the dimension, minimum distance and strength of this code.

4.3. Prove: The dual of a linear MDS code is an MDS code.

4.4. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $[n,k,d]_q$-code. Determine the parameters of $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ as an orthogonal array. Derive the Singleton bound (in the case of linear codes).

4.5. Give another simple proof of the Singleton bound for linear $[n,k,d]_q$ codes based on Gauß elimination (no calculations needed).

4.6. In order to understand why covering arrays are also known as qualitatively independent partitions, consider the $CA(10;3,5,2)$ which we gave as an example. Interpret the rows as the ground set $X$ (of 10 elements). Then every column describes a partition of $X$ into two parts.
Express the defining property of a strength 2 covering array in terms of this family of partitions.

4.7. Prove the following basic fact: if $f(X)$ is a polynomial with coefficients in the field $F$, and $u \in F$ such that $f(u)=0$, then $f(X)$ can be written in the form $f(X)=(X-u)g(X)$, where $g(X)$ is a polynomial.

4.8. The finite geometric series is the identity
$$(X-1)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} X^i = X^n -1.$$ Prove this identity.

4.9. Let $q$ be a complex number of absolute value $|q|<1$. Prove
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} q^n = 1+q+q^2+\cdots = 1/(1-q).$$ This is the infinite geometric series.

4.10. Show the identity $(X-u)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} X^i u^{n-1-i}= X^n - u^n.$

4.11. Solve Exercise 4.7 using the geometric series.
