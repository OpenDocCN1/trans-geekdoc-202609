V with Gram matrix B = (b_{ij}). Let w_i = ∑_{l=1}^k t_{il} v_l and T = (t_{ij}) be the matrix describing this change of basis. Then B = T A T^t.

**PROOF** Simply compute

b_{ij} = (w_i, w_j) = (∑_l t_{il} v_l, ∑_r t_{jr} v_r) = ∑_{l,r} t_{il} a_{lr} t_{jr}.

This is the (i, j) entry of T A T^t.

The most important point about the base change is what remains invariant. Consider the determinants: det(B) = det(A) det(T)^2. It changes but squares are mapped to squares and nonsquares are mapped to nonsquares.

# 17.36 Definition. Let A be a (k, k) matrix with entries from 𝔽_q, where q is odd. The discriminant of A is

disc(A) = { +1 if det(A) is a square; −1 if det(A) is nonsquare; 0 if det(A) = 0. }

*The discriminant of a bilinear form is the discriminant of its Gram matrix.*

Theorem 17.35 shows that the discriminant of the Gram matrix is invariant under change of basis. It is therefore an invariant of the bilinear form. This shows how to distinguish hyperbolic planes from anisotropic two-dimensional spaces. As the determinants of the Gram matrices in standard form are −1 and −ν, respectively, they have different discriminants.
