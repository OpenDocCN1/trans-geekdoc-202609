# Prioritizing Consumer Education and Awareness

It is essential to educate consumers on how AI will be used in financial services and their rights. Banks and similar financial operators should provide information to their customers on AI use in decision-making, where they can file a complaint if they feel unfairly treated. In the age of AI-driven finance, as [41] also underlines in her work on algorithmic accountability, consumer education is necessary to give individuals control.

Doing so will require studying, monitoring, and updating strategies continuously to ensure AI in finance equitably acts for all shareholders’ interests.

# The importance of model interpretability in finance

Increasingly, however, explainable AI (XAI) is being recognized as a crucial antidote to many key ethical dilemmas raised by financial AIs, particularly their ability to impart partial explainability to AI models. If anything, the importance of model interpretability in finance cannot be overstated, as it intrinsically relates to compliance, trust, and transparency in financial decision-making.

The significance of XAI for finance is discussed in this section by referring to a case study highlighting the role of explainable and interpretable AI models. This is one area among others where the spotlight on XAI has increased dramatically over recent years, following a broader increase in interest across communities focused on AI, as shown in Figure 1. The percentage of accepted papers addressing interpretability, explainability, causation, fairness (e. g. , to bias), and/or privacy at major AI conferences like NeurIPS has been increasing over time since 2014, according to the most recent version on AP CIOX from the authors of The State of AI Ethics Report. Industry involvement, likewise, is on the rise, as evidenced by another example: an increase in the number of AI ethics papers submitted by companies—plotting the affiliation-wise total of accepted FAccT conference submissions from 2018 until 2021 [59].
