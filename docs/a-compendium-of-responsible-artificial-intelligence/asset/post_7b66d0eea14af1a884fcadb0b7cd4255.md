## Discriminatory Algorithms in Credit Scoring and Lending

Credit scoring algorithms, essential in decision-making processes, have undergone various changes in using artificial intelligence and other machine learning algorithms. Despite these models striving for enhanced outcomes in effectiveness and efficiency, there are higher chances of reinforcing or worsening existing bias. A study focusing on Indonesia's financial sector highlights the challenges of innovative credit scoring (ICS) systems, which attempt to be more inclusive but deal with issues such as adequate data privacy, data inaccuracy and potential discrimination. The Indonesian experience uncovers concerns in many parts of the world regarding AI-enhanced credit scoring fairness and transparency [15].

There has been rapid integration of algorithmic credit scoring (ACS) technology in Vietnam, which has raised general concerns about injustice and threats towards personal privacy. This is further worsened by the absence of any governing bodies, indicating a call for legal systems that will allow for the use of ACS in a way that is not counterproductive to social justice [16].

## Standardising Fairness in AI Systems

Standardizing evaluation methods is necessary for tackling the ethical problems in AI-based finance. Studies emphasizing debiting capability assessment for small consumer loans find that minimum ethical standards in artificial intelligence, mainly financial services, must be in place. These factors include internal audits of algorithmic results, reducing biased factors and other associated practices, and providing sufficiently understandable and logical justifications for applied algorithms. Such practiced standardization helps alleviate the unfair impacts and abusive practices in credit scoring [17].

## Mitigating Racial Bias in Credit Scoring Models

The issue of racial bias in credit scoring models is another cause for concern. The potential benefits of integrating fairness algorithms with decision-making trees are illustrated in the study aimed at addressing the racial bias of the credit scoring models based on P2P lending data. This approach reduces unfairness and average cost in terms of credit scoring models and shows the way towards fairer applications of AI in finance [18].

## Case Studies Showcasing Biased Outcomes

The financial sector has suffered significant setbacks when bringing AI technologies to the market and offering backing services primarily due to biased deployment. This subsection looks into the case studies and shows several of these biases from recent studies in the field.

### i. Case Study 1: Gender Bias in Pretrained Vision-and-Language Models

By following the previous work on the scope of gender biases present in vision-and-language pre-training models [18], the study focused on bias transfer, particularly the enhancement or reduction of bias before and after fine-tuning three distinct sets of models. Inequity amplification before and after fine-tuning was found to be independent, and long-term gender-neutral pretraining diminished group differences. This case study is significant since it explains how biases can be constructively embedded in such systems and what measures are provided to contain them.

### ii. Case Study 2: Perceptions of Algorithmic Fairness in Hiring

Undertook an empirical review of crowd employee perception focusing on whether democracy is applied in the hiring process by algorithms, particularly algorithms for recruitment [19]. It was evident from the study that the level of fairness perceived by the users was higher when they were given information about the procedure of making the judgment, even when the decision was based on sensitive characteristics such as sex. This case study highlights the role of awareness in AI systems in engendering the fairness perception of the populace.

### iii. Case Study 3: Addressing Bias in Machine Learning Systems

This case study offered an elaborate approach toward bias and fairness in machine learning systems [20]. This lesson highlighted the importance of equipping data scientists and other practitioners with skills to address and prevent discrimination from occurring in practice and the specific requirements for biases while making decisions throughout the project lifecycle.

### iv. Case Study 4: Human Preferences and Fair AI

This study explained why it might be an issue whenever users of fair AI algorithms are also humans, explicitly using the college-recommendations as a case study. From the survey, respondents were more inclined to use the initially biased system than the unbiased system, hence tensions between human preferences and justice. This case analysis explains the challenges of correcting bias in AI recommendations and the importance of accounting for user bias.

## Transparency and Explainability

### The “Black Box” Problem and its Implications for Financial Decisions

The increasing use of Artificial Intelligence in the banking industry has created the “black box” problem. This concern refers to the lack and demand for adequate transparency and rationale in AI systems, especially in decision-making of high precision and worth, such as that in the financial sector. This section looks into the consequences of this problem aided by current evidence from particular studies.

There is often a “black box” in many of the AI models used in banking, which becomes a barrier to how the users and even the authorities understand how decisions are made within the models; hence, it becomes problematic. Such opacity may pose problems of trust, especially when it comes to critical areas within finance that are highly sensitive. This was underlined in the study conducted by Thanos Konstantinidis [21], who noted that this is why most finance professionals prefer traditional rather than deep learning models since they are less prone to overconfidence bias. The rapid advancement of explainable AI (XAI) in finance shows a new perspective on addressing the transparency challenges of automated systems [22]. It illustrates why the interpretability of black box AI in mission-critical processes such as finance is central to the adaptation of explanatory interfaces provided by XAI. Their work also aims to clarify these models and is directed towards developing XAI for image segmentation and diagnosis of XAI integration into the model.
