However, as machine learning, and particularly deep learning, became the dominant approach in AI, the “black box” problem emerged. The lack of understanding about how AI systems arrive at decisions led to growing concerns from both the public and regulators. The development of transparency and explainability frameworks has been driven by the need to demystify AI and ensure that stakeholders can understand how these systems operate.

# Key Milestones and Advances

Several key milestones mark the evolution of transparency and explainability in AI:

+   • **Early AI (1950s-1980s):** The focus during this era was on symbolic AI and expert systems, which were transparent by design. Rule-based systems like MYCIN (1970s) in healthcare were easy to interpret, as they made decisions based on predefined rules.
+   
+   • **Machine Learning Boom (1990s-2000s):** As AI shifted toward machine learning and statistical methods, transparency started to diminish. Methods such as neural networks and support vector machines became popular due to their high performance, but their interpretability came at a cost.
+   
+   • **Deep Learning Era (2010s-Present):** The advancement of deep learning has resulted in remarkable achievements in fields such as image recognition and natural language processing, specifically with the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Yet, due to their intricate nature, these models are often labeled as “black boxes” which has triggered a need for explainable AI.
+   
+   • **Modern Explainability Tools (2016-Present):** With the rise of regulatory pressures and societal demands for accountability, tools like LIME and SHAP were developed to make AI systems more interpretable. These tools represent significant advances in explaining complex models without sacrificing their predictive power.
+   
+   • **Regulatory Influence (2020s):** Transparency and the right to explanation have been highlighted in recent regulations, including the EU’s AI Act and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This regulatory environment has accelerated the need for both industry and academia to focus on developing more transparent and explainable AI systems.

# Core Concepts of Transparency

Transparency in AI refers to the capacity of an AI system to offer transparent information about its inner mechanisms, allowing users to understand its decision-making. This involves four core concepts: model interpretability, algorithmic transparency, data transparency, and explainability.
