Utilitarianism is a belief system that places importance on actions that lead to the highest level of happiness or welfare. AI’s goal is to make systems that help the greatest number of people. It aims to achieve the best outcomes while minimizing negative impacts. Yet, there are those who contend that utilitarianism may rationalize actions that harm minority groups to benefit the majority.

Deontological ethics, also known as duty-based ethics, prioritizes following rules or duties regardless of the outcomes. This ideology promotes upholding basic rights, impartiality, and equity. In AI, deontological ethics informs the creation of systems that uphold privacy, consent, and non-discrimination, prioritizing ethical norms even at the expense of efficiency.

Virtue Ethics shifts focus to the character and virtues of individuals, such as honesty and compassion. This idea supports AI systems that act right and help society. It also emphasizes the importance of ethical guidelines for people who develop AI.

The concept of Rights-Based Ethics highlights the importance of safeguarding individual rights, such as privacy and autonomy. This principle is significant in stopping AI systems from breaking users’ rights. It also helps to create designs that protect personal information and avoid intrusive surveillance.

Ethics of Care prioritizes relationships, empathy, and well-being, particularly for vulnerable populations. The text suggests that AI systems should incorporate empathy and support, while considering the needs of those affected by their implementation.

Social Contract Theory posits that moral obligations arise from societal agreements. In AI, this theory emphasizes aligning systems with societal norms and values, ensuring that AI serves the common good and upholds public trust.

# Utilitarianism

Utilitarianism is a moral theory that focuses on the consequences of actions and aims to maximize overall happiness or well-being. It follows a simple principle: actions are considered morally right if they result in maximum benefit for the largest number of individuals. When applied to the field of artificial intelligence (AI), utilitarianism provides a structure for evaluating the effectiveness and ethical significance of AI systems based on their impact on society.

When applied to AI, utilitarianism emphasizes designing systems that optimize benefits across diverse populations. For instance, AI applications in healthcare could be developed to enhance diagnostic accuracy and treatment efficiency, thus improving health outcomes for many patients. Similarly, AI systems used in public services, like traffic management or disaster response, are evaluated on how effectively they reduce risks and enhance overall societal welfare.
