If anything, the importance of model interpretability in finance cannot be overstated, as it intrinsically relates to compliance, trust, and transparency in financial decision-making.

The significance of XAI for finance is discussed in this section by referring to a case study highlighting the role of explainable and interpretable AI models. ### Case Study: The European Union's GDPR and the Right to Explanation

A pertinent example illustrating the significance of XAI in financial services is a version of GDPR implemented by the European Union, which further provides for what has come to be known as a ‘right to explanation.’ Enacted in May 2018, this regulation has far-reaching consequences for AI-driven financial services. One of these is the potential to require explanations for automated decisions that impact them, including those from AI financial systems (i.e., credit scoring or loan approvals) under GDPR.

### Enhancing Trust and Transparency

GDPR has made a fine point about why financial institutions must leverage XAI techniques for the right to explain. Financial institutions can better foster customer trust if explainable reasons for AI-driven decisions are plain today. Finance hinges on trust, and one of the most significant concerns around AI models is how opaque they are. XAI addresses this issue by explaining AI's decisions, making them more transparent and understandable for users. An example of XAI in use comes from a study conducted in [60] that tested model-agnostic interpretability methods and found insights into how advanced AI algorithms reason to make decisions.

### Compliance with Regulatory Standards

The case idea of GDPR also reminds us of the importance of XAI in ensuring regulatory standards are fulfilled. Financial institutions must comply with GDPR if they operate in or serve customers from the EU, including explaining automated decisions. In finance, for example, this has led to a change in the level of complexity that they are willing to accept from AI. Compliance—a legal AND ethical obligation—compels our financial institutions to be clear and responsible in their operations[61]).

### Mitigating Risks of Bias and Discrimination

XAI helps identify and eliminate biases present in AI models. We cannot remove potential biases in AI systems without seeing and understanding the decision-making. When it comes to finance, decisions can radically change someone’s life, so fairness and non-discrimination are king. Field [62] states that in AI fairness toolkits, XAI can help audit bias in the model.

### Facilitating Better Decision-Making

XAI also provides insights into the pros and cons of automated models to the financial community, helping to enhance decision-making in finance. On top of that, financial professionals can use this information to inform their decisions better—blending AI recommendations with human judgment and expertise. The combination of AI and human decision-making can strengthen financial services further.

### Techniques and Tools for Enhancing AI explainability

Autonomous financial decision-making is a new area that has emerged as an application of advanced machine learning methods nowadays. The greater transparency in how and why decisions are made, the more intricate AI models become—a hidden layering of choices that becomes almost impossible to explain or trust for stakeholders. This is where explainable AI (XAI) comes in—offering a collection of techniques and tools that help make the decisions made by an artificial intelligence system more transparent and interpretable.

### Shapley Values and Linear Regression Comparison

One of the significant breakthroughs in XAI has been with Shapley values. Unlike traditional linear regression models that can be simpler, the explanations seem exaggerated when taken to a complex financial scenario – but Shapley values conserve this complexity by giving detailed reasoning behind an AI decision. This technique breaks down a prediction into the contribution of all features, allowing for an impartial and complete picture of how each input impacts the output. For example, in a credit scoring AI model, Shapley values tell us how much every borrower’s attribute (income, credit history ...) contributes to her/his own score, simplifying consumer and expert decision-making.

### User-Centric XAI Approaches

Another critical element of Explainable AI in finance is that the target audience is business and non-technical employees using model outputs to monitor/audit their predictions. Research has indicated that the specific needs of various user groups are often overlooked in the development of XAI techniques. This oversight can lead to AI systems that are suboptimal or even harmful, especially in critical areas like finance. To address this issue, several studies, including the EUCA user study, have been conducted to identify end-user requirements. By employing a human-in-the-loop approach, we aim to create user-centric feature/exemplar/rule-based explanations that are more accessible and aligned with users’ explanatory needs.
