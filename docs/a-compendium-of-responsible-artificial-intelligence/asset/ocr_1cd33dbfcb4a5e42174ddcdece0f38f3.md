# Objective Metrics for Explainability

The pursuit of developing objective metrics to measure the level of explainability in AI systems is a new frontier for XAI. These metrics enable us to quantify human comprehension of an AI system by comparing it to theoretical models like Achinstein's Theory of Explanations. In the financial sector, where decisions carry significant financial weight these metrics are crucial for assessing and enhancing AI explainability, making them indispensable.

# Knowledge Graphs for Decision Support

Knowledge graphs (KG) are a powerful technique to improve the explainability of human-based decision-making. They model a domain's financial entities and events in an ordered manner, allowing users to navigate this information interactively. Integrating KGs with AI models would enable financial institutions to provide decision support systems that are significantly more transparent and interpretable. In a different scenario, the work can be like the VTA of KGs for econometrics so that analysts and investors can easily understand how various economic factors are related and correlated with stock market price trends.

XAI evolution in finance is an important puzzle that facilitates trust and transparency around AI applications. Shapley values, user-centric approaches (i.e., counterfactual explanations targeting specific end-users), objective explainability metrics, and knowledge graphs are some techniques at the forefront to provide more explicable AI systems. While this technology is continually evolving, we need to continue refining these techniques and tooling for all stakeholders to have access and visibility into the financial benefits of AI.
