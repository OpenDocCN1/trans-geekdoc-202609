# # Inclusive AI Governance

*Accessible and Helpful AI for a Varied Crowd of People:* A System Recognizing the Different Backgrounds and Capabilities of People or Socioeconomic Conditions. Inclusive AI governance means making these systems accessible and helpful to a wide section of people without biases towards any section of society based on their background, abilities, or socioeconomic status. Whosoever issues that are related to algorithmic bias, access, and that of the divide between digital and non-digital are to be considered. The developers of AI should make attempts to be inclusive of people with disabilities, marginalized communities, and underprivileged families with less access to such technologies. They could include AI-friendly system designing, training, and support for underprivileged communities and ensure equitable sharing of AI benefits.

Fairness and inclusivity are related in many respects. An unfair AI system is likely to be exclusionary, and vice versa. To ensure that AI governance happens both fairly and inclusively, it has to address the problems of bias and discrimination in society. These could include policies that enhance diversity and equity in the development teams of AI and bring about social impact assessment of AI systems, engagement with diverse stakeholders to ensure that all parties’ needs are taken into consideration.

By advocating for equity and inclusion in AI governance, we will be in a better position to use AI technologies to amplify the benefits of society in an equitable rather than unjust manner. Equity and inclusion are both ethical imperatives but are also quite critical to the long-term sustainability and acceptability of AI systems. Once these principles are placed as givens, public trust in AI will grow, and full potential will be realized in terms of this transformative technology.

# # Governance Frameworks and Types

# ## Centralized vs. Decentralized Governance

Centralized or decentralized governance structures have to be considered as a first choice in the creation and deployment of accountable AI systems. Centralized governance forms are characterized as having a control or governance can prove to make effective decisions, consistently impose policies, and maintain a real focus on the superordinate goals at the top of the body. The problem is that such governance might prove too rigid to be adaptable to circumstances. Overreach and abuse of power may also become a problem because a centralization of authority may leave too much control in the hands of a single authority on the development and deployment of AI.
