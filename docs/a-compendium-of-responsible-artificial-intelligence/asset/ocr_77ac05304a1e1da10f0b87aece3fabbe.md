# Human-Centric Approaches

# Bias Training and Awareness for Developers

Bias training and awareness for developers are key components of human-centric approaches to reducing bias in AI systems. Since developers play a critical role in designing, training, and deploying AI models, their understanding of potential biases in data, algorithms, and outcomes is essential to creating fair AI systems.

**Bias training** educates developers on the various forms of bias—such as historical, selection, and algorithmic bias—that can infiltrate AI systems. Through training programs, developers learn how biases arise during the data collection, model training, and decision-making processes, as well as the ethical implications of biased AI. These programs often include guidelines for recognizing biases in datasets, ensuring diverse data representation, and applying fairness constraints during model development.

Awareness initiatives emphasize the importance of regularly assessing AI models for fairness, using tools like bias detection frameworks and fairness metrics. Additionally, fostering a culture of **diversity and inclusion** within AI development teams ensures that a variety of perspectives are considered, helping to detect and prevent potential biases that might otherwise go unnoticed.

By equipping developers with the knowledge and tools to identify and address bias, these programs help promote the creation of more fair and equitable AI systems.

# Education on Implicit Bias and Ethical AI

Educating developers on implicit bias and ethical AI is a crucial human-centric approach to mitigating bias in AI systems. Implicit biases are unconscious attitudes or stereotypes that can influence decision-making, including how AI models are designed, trained, and deployed. Developers must be aware of these biases to create fairer AI systems.

**Implicit bias training** helps developers recognize their own subconscious biases, which can affect choices such as data selection, feature engineering, and model tuning. By understanding how personal and societal biases can shape AI outcomes, developers are better equipped to make informed, ethical decisions in model development.
