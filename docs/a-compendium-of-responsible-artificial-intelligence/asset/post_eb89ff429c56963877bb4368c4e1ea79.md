## Market Volatility and the Role of Algorithmic Trading

The conjunction of AI-based finance and the volatility of the market and its components, such as algorithm trading, is complicated and can cause substantial economic and societal issues. The New York Stock Exchange has also witnessed the growth of fully automated algorithmic trading: automated execution of specific trading strategies and management of positions: over time, in short time frames, and generally high trading activity utilizing software and computer technology that, to a degree, substitutes traditional human action. Although it has resulted in enhanced efficiency and liquidity in the markets, it poses challenges to the volatility of the market and the health of the economic systems.

On the other hand, algorithmic trading has penetrated the world of finance exceptionally rapidly and is responsible for a considerable proportion of the trades performed on leading stock markets. Machines are increasingly capable of processing market information faster than human traders. They analyze data, identify trading opportunities, and execute trades. The speed and logic of algorithmic trading can boost market liquidity and lower transaction costs, thereby making the financial market more efficient and competitive.

Nevertheless, using algorithms in trading also comes with challenges, especially regarding market volatility. Algorithms are one of the worst in terms of volatility-inciting factors. There are also risks associated with algorithmic trading, which stem from the fact that some models tend to have herding effects. This phenomenon was most noticeable during the "Flash Crash" Event of 2010 when it was primarily advanced trading software that was blamed for the massive sell-off in US stocks. A study by [38] examines in detail the events surrounding the flash crash of 2010, noting the contribution of high-frequency trading strategies to the volatility of the markets. The concern that the practice of algorithmic trading might pose a danger in creating problems on a systemic level is quite valid, as it is an elastic twist and paradox. The markets are increasingly interlinked, which implies that one market can insulate the other and vice versa. High frequency and high turnover of trades through the algorithmic system provide the means and power to carry such shocks across markets faster, which can result in more severe instability in the markets. [39] raises concerns that algorithmic trading could pose risks potentially creating systemic problems. This perspective is valid, highlighting the paradoxical and elastic nature of such issues. The markets are increasingly interlinked, which implies that one market can insulate the other and vice versa. Another significant issue when it comes to algorithmic trading is the presence and usefulness of human oversight. As much as algorithms can perform trades and analyze large data sets in split seconds, the absence of contextual and situational reasoning becomes a limitation. This was evident during the Flash Crash, where algorithmic trading continued even in volatile market conditions. A report by [27] emphasizes the need for human intervention in algorithmic trading and advocates for additional measures to prevent it from becoming a significant risk.

In addition, there are moral issues related to fairness and equality in algorithmic trading. Also, there is a worry that such technologies may disadvantage some traders who are not part of large institutions because larger institutions controlling significant funds are more capable of incorporating advanced large investment trading algorithms. This angle has been addressed by [40] when studying the effects of HFT on market integrity and fairness.

## The Bigger Picture: Broader Ethical Implications

### The Effects of AI-driven Financial Choices on Society

The impact of AI on financial decision-making also raises important social issues like justice and economic inequality. AI's pervasive influence on markets, institutions, and our daily lives have already been prominent in the financial industry. This is a significant consideration, as AI-directed financial decisions, such as in credit scoring or investment strategies, can advantage or disadvantage different social and economic groups. Moreover, AI's ability to process vast amounts of data allows it to identify valuable insights, whether it's promising mortgage payers or profitable investment opportunities. Some authorities worry that AI-driven financial decisions could be based on biased data or favor specific demographic groups, which may widen socio-economic disparities. The opposite of Augur is featured in [41], discussing how AI applications in finance could make inequality worse. AI-driven financial decisions, including lending-related ones, significantly impact individuals and communities, making social justice an important issue. When considering the power and alignment of AI-driven financial decisions (especially those regarding lending) to people in need, social justice cannot be ignored. As [42] wrote in “Automating Inequality,” AI can perpetuate systemic inequality when it is biased or unjust, especially for marginalized populations which may be put at greater risk by this technology. The social implications of financial AI raise profound questions and challenge our conception of the broader tech-societal relationship. AI is expected to transform the role of financial systems when it comes to consumer accessibility and interaction with institutions while also adapting newer AI-driven economic policies. This advancement blurs the boundaries between technology and society, challenging our ethical frameworks and all the regulatory measures we have established. It discusses the wider social implications for society of integrating AI, urging us to reflect more rigorously on this ethical evolution [43]. However, the broader societal impacts of AI-driven finance go beyond social equity and economic welfare. It should also ensure that issues like data security, privacy, and the reduction of human agency are addressed. There are concerns that the human element of financial services, including personal touch and discretion, might be lost if AI becomes prevalent in decision-making at mainstream banks. Striking the right balance between efficiency, personalization, algorithmic accuracy, and human judgment is crucial. A study by [44] warns that a human rights-centric approach to AI in finance will be critical to alleviate the potential threats of an insolvent world. However, the consequences of AI-supported financial decisions on society are infinitely more intricate and sophisticated.
