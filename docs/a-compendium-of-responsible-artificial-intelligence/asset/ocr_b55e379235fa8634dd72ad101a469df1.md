# Future Directions for Research and Practice

AI in the finance domain, more specifically from the ethical point of view, will provide a plethora of future research and practice. As a developing industry, it offers challenges that need to be solved and opportunities that can be seized upon; it also requires an outlook-focused approach rather than just solving problems in real-time. Future work should focus on refining novel ethical underpinnings specific to AI in finance. The current ethical principles are a good starting point, but they must be updated as AI evolves and becomes more intimately entwined with the financial system. Leisure and hospitality will likely always have a different reputation than a noble trade like medicine. However, it is time to transition away from static (and, therefore, ultimately meaningless) ethical guidelines in tech. It means refining current principles and identifying and integrating novel financial-specific ethical considerations. This enterprise can find a point of departure in the work done by researchers like Anna Jobin [73] analyzing AI ethics globally. This is an essential line for future research work in more sophisticated XAI methods. As AI finance models get more compiled, the requirement for neutral interpretability is even more urgent. In a new voice, future research could yield novel approaches and tools to enhance explainability of AI decision-making processes more human-in-the-loop to (often lay) stakeholders. Future research could also investigate how these explanations are best communicated to ensure everyone can access treatments and the rationale (and evidence). The work IDC is referencing, such as the research by Marco Tulio Ribeiro [60] on model interpretability, forms a basis for this study. Drawing on this research, we will explore some ways AI can be rolled out across different functions within finance and examine the critical data privacy and security issues that arise when doing so (a topic to pursue more fully at a later date). As more financial institutions use AI models to manage and analyze massive volumes of sensitive data, it is critically important for these organizations to maintain the privacy and security of that data. Looking ahead, research should investigate emerging technologies and methods for protecting financial data in AI systems and practices of overseeing data around AI. The independently conducted cyber security and data protection research, like some mentioned in the Journal of Cybersecurity, can feed into these efforts.
