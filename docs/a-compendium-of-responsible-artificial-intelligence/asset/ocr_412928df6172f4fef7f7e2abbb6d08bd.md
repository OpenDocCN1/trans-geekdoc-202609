Although it has resulted in enhanced efficiency and liquidity in the markets, it poses challenges to the volatility of the market and the health of the economic systems.

On the other hand, algorithmic trading has penetrated the world of finance exceptionally rapidly and is responsible for a considerable proportion of the trades performed on leading stock markets. Machines are increasingly capable of processing market information faster than human traders. They analyze data, identify trading opportunities, and execute trades. The speed and logic of algorithmic trading can boost market liquidity and lower transaction costs, thereby making the financial market more efficient and competitive.

Nevertheless, using algorithms in trading also comes with challenges, especially regarding market volatility. Algorithms are one of the worst in terms of volatility-inciting factors. There are also risks associated with algorithmic trading, which stem from the fact that some models tend to have herding effects. This phenomenon was most noticeable during the ‘Flash Crash’ Event of 2010 when it was primarily advanced trading software that was blamed for the massive sell-off in US stocks. A study by [38] examines in detail the events surrounding the flash crash of 2010, noting the contribution of high-frequency trading strategies to the volatility of the markets. The concern that the practice of algorithmic trading might pose a danger in creating problems on a systemic level is quite valid, as it is an elastic twist and paradox. The markets are increasingly interlinked, which implies that one market can insulate the other and vice versa. High frequency and high turnover of trades through the algorithmic system provide the means and power to carry such shocks across markets faster, which can result in more severe instability in the markets. Another significant issue when it comes to algorithmic trading is the presence and usefulness of human oversight. As much as algorithms can perform trades and analyze large data sets in split seconds, the absence of contextual and situational reasoning becomes a limitation. This was evident during the Flash Crash, where algorithmic trading continued even in volatile market conditions. A report by [27] emphasizes the need for human intervention in algorithmic trading and advocates for additional measures to prevent it from becoming a significant risk.
