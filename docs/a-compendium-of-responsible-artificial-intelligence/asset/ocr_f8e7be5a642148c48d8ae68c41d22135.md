The most immediate impact is the replacement of boring and monotonous tasks, such as operational processes, with automated ones. As the need for processing and analyzing vast volumes of information grows and becomes more complex, the need for actual human labour is continuously dwindling as more tasks such as data-entry, customer assistance, financial research, and report generation are now being achieved by AI Systems. This shift towards automation has raised concerns about whether these changes would lead to job losses, especially for forms of employment that mainly require these low-level reproduced functions. According to [35], particular attention is given to a study of occupations that suggests possibilities of being automated and shows unhealthy tendencies in the finance sector. This includes roles like a loan officer, line of credit cashier and bank teller, where tasks are monotonous and, therefore, easily automated. The study recommends that as time goes by, technology will be incorporated into finance and other sectors even more than banking because of the current advancement of AI and machine learning.

Though there are costs of job losses resulting from the introduction of AI, not everything is negative. On the one hand, such types of employment may decrease in demand, but new businesses and branches will appear. The increasing implementation of AI technologies in financial services has caused rising demand for business professionals such as data scientists, machine learning and AI solutions, and AI ethics specialists. These involve designing and managing AI-based systems, evaluating AI results and AI usage in strategic planning decisions. Transforming a topology of finance that capitalizes on the very technologies of AI requires a change in the approach to skills and training development. As these routine tasks are habitually performed by technologies, which are uncommon to the most common occupations, AI can hardly imitate skills such as critical thinking, complex problem-solving tactics, and communication skills. According to [36], an increasing risk is that respondents could lose their jobs due to automation and the advancement of artificial intelligence, highlighting the necessity of upskilling. Additionally, the deployment of AI in the financial sector and its associated job losses paint a broader picture for society. Social tension and inequalities in employment opportunities may rise, as some groups may be more significantly impacted than others by job losses than others. [37] underscores the potential bias suggesting that automation-driven job displacement might disproportionately affect individuals based on their education and income levels. The potential for unemployment due to automation demands significant attention from businesses and governments as they navigate the transition to an AI driven economy.
