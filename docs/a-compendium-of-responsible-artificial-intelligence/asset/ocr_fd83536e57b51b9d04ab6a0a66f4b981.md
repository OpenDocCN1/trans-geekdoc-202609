The High-Level Expert Group on AI published the ethical guidelines of the European Commission that emphasize fairness in AI systems, including the avoidance of discrimination or exclusion.

Privacy: AI systems should respect the privacy of individuals and protect personal data. Providing appropriate data protection, getting informed consent, and limiting the collection and use of personal information are all part of it. Compliance to data protection laws and regulations and the adoption of privacy-enhancing technologies help safeguard privacy. Besides other global firsts, a US law like the California Consumer Privacy Act gives individuals the right to know what personal information is collected about them.

An individual has specific rights concerning their personal data including the right to access collected information, the right to delete personal data, and the right to opt out of the sale of their personal information [10].

Safety and Robustness: AI systems should be developed and designed to be safe and robust, thereby minimizing the risk of unintended or harmful consequences. This would encompass systems to ensure the system is reliable and accurate, identify and mitigate risks. Safety and robustness would be achieved through extensive testing, validation, and designing of failsafe mechanisms. The US Department of Defense established a Joint Artificial Intelligence Center, JAIC, responsible for the development and application of AI capabilities for the US military. The JAIC establishes guidelines governing the development and deployment of AI systems to ensure safety and robustness.

Human Oversight: The development and deployment of AI systems are overseen by humans to ensure that these systems can be applied responsibly and ethically. This would involve maintaining control over the AI system, monitoring its performances, and intervening appropriately to rectify or adapt to emerging problems. Thus, human oversight may be achieved through committees, the development of ethical guidelines, and the training of AI developers and users. The OECD has developed principles for AI governance that emphasize not just what AI “ought” to do in itself but also the necessity of ensuring that AI systems are used in accordance with human values and rights.

These core governance principles form a basis for the development of responsible AI. By adhering to these principles, organizations can more effectively ensure that AI systems are used beneficially and do not cause harm.
