# Regulatory Challenges: The Evolving Landscape of AI in Finance

Certainly, the nascent regulatory backdrop for banking-as-an-artificial-intelligence will present distinct and daunting challenges. Indeed, in a world where financial institutions are increasingly using AI to manage risk and engage with customers, the traditional idea of regulation is beginning to seem outdated and overwhelmed. It takes a nuanced understanding of the specific advantages and risks AI presents in finance and an agile regulatory approach primed for transformation with emerging technologies. Fairness and transparency are key concerns in regulatory frameworks for AI systems. AI machine learning-based algorithms may sometimes serve as “black boxes” since it can be hard to understand how they arrive at their results. Thus, this will open room for errors in finance, where every decision is disclosed and accountable. The complexity of these systems makes it even harder to harness them ethically, and any bias that perpetuates would result in regulation against similar AI-based decision-making.

The European Union’s GDPR, which includes a right-to-explanation provision, offers the first real-generation rule regarding this challenge. The GDPR mandates a right to explanation be provided for people over automated judgements, setting a new precedent for transparency in AI [45]. Yet another regulatory matter that needs addressing is the systemic risks of AI in finance. AI can create new systemic risks when used for trading, risk management, and other financial operations. Today’s prime example is AI-driven high-frequency trading (HFT), which has perhaps exacerbated market volatility for instance, the 2010 Flash Crash. The regulators need to know these risks and build strategies accordingly. This requires a deep understanding of AI technology and reinvention of current financial regulations. An explanatory note here would also guide the reader by clarifying how this Financial Stability Board report highlights these systemic risks and provides possible recommendations [27]. Data privacy and security are among AI-driven finance’s most critical regulatory considerations. AI is a technology that relies on vast amounts of data to learn and function, and financial institutions gather this data in large volumes. This raises questions in terms of data security and data privacy, specifically with the rise of cyberattacks and ransomware. Regulators must do more than just ensuring that financial institutions protect customer information—they need to guarantee it is handled responsibly, on par with with upholding privacy laws. For AI and big data, regulations on the horizon include the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) or even GDPR, to name a few, dealing with the privacy of information in EU member states drafting, for example—the Data Protection Directive that is soon going to be supplemented by new regulation [46].
