Regarding certain ethical controversies, financial institutions will have to take active steps in monitoring and managing bias that may be part of AI systems. This includes, among other actions, regularly auditing AI models to identify discriminatory impacts, incorporating measures to prevent biases during AI model creation, and disclosing the decision-making processes of AI systems. Additionally, it is necessary to establish legal frameworks that ensure actuaries and legal professionals adhere to standards of fairness and non-discrimination when making credit decisions with AI systems and when providing loans.

# Case Studies Showcasing Biased Outcomes

The financial sector has suffered significant setbacks when bringing AI technologies to the market and offering backing services primarily due to biased deployment. This subsection looks into the case studies and shows several of these biases from recent studies in the field.

# i. Case Study 1: Gender Bias in Pretrained Vision-and-Language Models

By following the previous work on the scope of gender biases present in vision-and-language pre-training models[18], the study focused on bias transfer, particularly the enhancement or reduction of bias before and after fine-tuning three distinct sets of models. Inequity amplification before and after fine-tuning was found to be independent, and long-term gender-neutral pretraining diminished group differences. This case study is significant since it explains how biases can be constructively embedded in such systems and what measures are provided to contain them.
