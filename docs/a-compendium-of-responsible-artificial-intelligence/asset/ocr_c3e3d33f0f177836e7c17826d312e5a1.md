# # Core Concepts of Explainability

AI transparency hinges on explainability, enabling individuals to understand the logic behind AI-driven decisions. This involves providing clear and concise explanations for AI outcomes, helping to identify biases, errors, or weaknesses in the decision-making process. By illuminating AI systems’ inner workings, techniques like feature attribution, model interpretability, and model-agnostic explanations build trust and accountability. This results in more reliable AI applications, as users can understand the reasons behind AI-driven decisions, promoting a more informed and responsible use of AI technology.

# # What Constitutes Explainability?

# ## Definitions and Theoretical Foundations

The concept of explainability in AI pertains to a system’s capability to make its decision-making methods, results, and forecasts understandable to users. This concept is rooted in cognitive science, where effective communication and understanding are paramount. By providing clear explanations, AI systems can enhance user trust, promote transparency, and enable more informed decisions. Theoretical foundations of explainability involve understanding how humans process and interpret information, as well as how AI models can be designed to align with human cognitive abilities [7].

# ## Explainable AI vs. Interpretable AI

Explainable AI (XAI) and Interpretable AI (IAI) are often used interchangeably, but they have distinct differences. Explainable AI provides post-hoc explanations for its decisions, offering insights into the reasoning behind its outputs. In contrast, Interpretable AI is designed to be transparent and understandable in real-time, without requiring additional tools or explanations. IAI’s inherent transparency enables users to comprehend the decision-making process as it occurs, fostering trust and facilitating immediate feedback. While XAI provides retrospective clarity, IAI offers proactive understanding, making it an essential aspect of building trustworthy AI systems.
