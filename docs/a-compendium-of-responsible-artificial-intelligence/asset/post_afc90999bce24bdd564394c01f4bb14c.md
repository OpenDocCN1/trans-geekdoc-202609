### Introduction

AI's coming has brought about a revolutionary epoch in different fields, with finance being one of the areas most affected. Traditionally known for its reliance on quantitative analysis and decision-making, finance found AI to be its trusted partner, prompting numerous innovations and improvements [1]. However, incorporating AI into this industry is not straightforward. The application of AI in finance goes beyond technological progress; it converges with ethical, social, and legal aspects, which call for a detailed understanding of what it implies [2].

AI occupies multiple positions in finance, including boosting customer service via chatbots and employing complicated algorithms for high-frequency trading (HFT). Such developments have made operations more efficient while at the same time opening new doors in terms of financial analysis and forecasting [3]. However, as more critical tasks like credit scoring and fraud detection are automated by AI systems, there is an outcry over issues like bias, transparency and accountability [4]. Ethical problems posed by the use of AI in finance are not just theoretical but have real impacts on individuals and companies. Therefore, solving these ethical matters assumes the utmost gravity as global financial institutions increasingly depend on AI.

AI technology in healthcare cuts back on many administrative and business processes and enables better clinical decisions, as illustrated during the COVID-19 pandemic [7]. Also, the education sector has dramatically improved with the rise of AI inventions. Management education has mainly seen a spur of adoption of AI and virtual reality technologies in educational institutions post-pandemic. This integration has taught children the information and skills they need to thrive in a technologically driven world [8].
