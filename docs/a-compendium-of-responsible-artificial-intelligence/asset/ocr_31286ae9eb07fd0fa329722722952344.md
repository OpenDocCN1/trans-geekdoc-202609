# Table 6 Best Practices in Implementing Responsible AI

| Key Implications | Detailed Analysis |
| :--- | :--- |
| Stakeholder Engagement | • Engaging many stakeholders, such as customers, philosophers from ethics, attorneys, etcetera, is also challenging in AI development.<br>• By doing this, these ethical issues can be detected early, ensuring they are proactive and not only reactive. It also guarantees that the AI systems being built reflect the values of the larger society. |
| Ethical AI Frameworks and Guidelines | • Following well-defined frameworks and guidelines for the same (like the EU’s Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI or IEEE Ethically Aligned Design) will ensure a structured approach to the responsible development of AI.<br>• All of these frameworks provide detailed instructions on how to navigate ethical dilemmas in AI. |
| Continuous Monitoring and Evaluation | • AI systems operating in the financial industry should be continuously monitored. This is necessary as they should perform well and not get biased with time.<br>• This practice requires regular audits, performance reviews, and updating. |
| Education and Training | • Providing complete training and education to AI developers, users, among others. That education should encompass both the technical and ethical sides of AI.<br>• Ethical AI fosters individuals to build and use systems responsibly and ethically. |
| Collaboration with Regulatory Bodies | • Engaging regulatory bodies and politicians improves the ability of regulations to support responsible AI while encouraging innovation.<br>• This partnership will also help set common ethical AI manufacturing standards. |
