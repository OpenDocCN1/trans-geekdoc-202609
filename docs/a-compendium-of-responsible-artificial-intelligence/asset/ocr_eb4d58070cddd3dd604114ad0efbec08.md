This chapter delves into the pervasive issue of bias in artificial intelligence, examining its origins, manifestations, and societal implications. It categorizes bias into three main types : data bias, algorithmic bias, and human bias highlighting how each can lead to unfair and discriminatory outcomes. The chapter details various bias factors: such as underrepresentation of data, poor modeling techniques and human judgment influence during design. It also offers strategies for mitigating biases in AI, including diversifying data, implementing FAIR algorithms, and establishing a robust evaluation framework. The chapter aims to underscore the critical importance of addressing bias in AI, providing stakeholders with the necessary tools to develop more equitable and just technological solutions.

# ## Introduction

Bias in AI refers to the systematic and unfair discrimination that can emerge from artificial intelligence systems, influencing their outputs and decisions in ways that perpetuate inequality and injustice [1]. This phenomenon arises when AI algorithms, learning from historical data, inadvertently incorporate and amplify pre-existing biases present found in the data or system design. Bias in AI can manifest in various forms, such as skewed hiring practices, discriminatory lending decisions, or unequal access to services. The roots of AI bias are multifaceted, often stemming from biased training data, flawed algorithmic design, or a lack of diverse perspectives during the development process. Addressing bias in AI is crucial for ensuring that these technologies operate fairly and equitably across different demographics and use cases. This chapter explores the underlying causes of AI bias, including data representation issues and algorithmic limitations, and discusses strategies for mitigating its impact.
