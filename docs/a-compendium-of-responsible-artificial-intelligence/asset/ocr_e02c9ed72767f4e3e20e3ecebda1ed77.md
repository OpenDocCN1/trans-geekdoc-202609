# Ethical Aspects: Fairness and Transparency

The ethical issues presented by the cases connected to Apple Card are about fairness, implying transparency. AI with biased outcomes leads to fairness problems. While the algorithm may not have considered gender, there was little to no means of determining whether or how other correlated variables influenced decisions, leading to bias over years or even generations. These standards are associated with ethical and other concerns as they influence how AI's decision generation mechanisms treat different individuals, ensuring fairness across different groups.

A lack of transparency was also a significant problem. Understanding why AI makes specific calls can be challenging, as many of the tools underpinning it have a 'black box' nature. Sometimes, the reasons behind AI's decisions to grant different credit limits to customers remain opaque even to the customers and observers themselves as seen in the Apple Card case. As an ethical issue, transparency is about trust and accountability in AI systems. One paper in [54] argues the importance of explainability in AI, particularly in high-risk domains such as banking.

# Regulatory Aspects: Oversight and Compliance

The Apple Card episode helped expose where the regulatory cracks are in a nascent financial AI landscape. The current legislation may need to be revised to cope with the novel challenges posed by AI, like explainability and algorithmic bias. The resulting chaos compelled regulatory bodies to take notice, reflecting a growing awareness that AI in finance lacks sufficient oversight, whether it involves enforcing existing laws or amending them to address new AI-related risks and ethical dilemmas. A report in [55] asserts specific requirements such as responsibility and fairness or transparency that are prerequisites for AI to be seen as trustworthy. However, there are also the technological and ethical (not to mention legal) sides of an Apple Card dispute with which these pieces can comfortably fit together. It highlights the difficulty in constructing AI models that are technically and ethically sound, as well as compliant with regulation—particularly within a sector like banking. Similarly, this will also have to be the landscape on which AI applications and their impacts should be deployed in the forthcoming finance horizon based on regulatory compliance, fairness, and transparency.
