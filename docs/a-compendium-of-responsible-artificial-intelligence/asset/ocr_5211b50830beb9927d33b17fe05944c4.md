# The Bigger Picture: Broader Ethical Implications

# The Effects of AI-driven Financial Choices on Society

The impact of AI on financial decision-making also raises important social issues like justice and economic inequality. AI's pervasive influence on markets, institutions, and our daily live have already been prominent in the financial industry. This is a significant consideration, as AI-directed financial decisions, such as in credit scoring or investment strategies, can advantage or disadvantage different social and economic groups. Moreover, AI's ability to process vast amounts of data allows it to identify valuable insights, whether its promising mortgage payers or profitable investment opportunities. Some authorities worry that AI-driven financial decisions could be based on biased data or favor specific demographic groups, which may widen socio-economic disparities. The opposite of Augur is featured in [41], discussing how AI applications in finance could make inequality worse. AI-driven financial decisions, including lending-related ones, significantly impact individuals and communities, making social justice an important issue. When considering the power and alignment of AI-driven financial decisions (especially those regarding lending) to people in need, social justice cannot be ignored. As [42] wrote in “Automating Inequality,” AI can perpetuate systemic inequality when it is biased or unjust, especially for marginalized populations which may be put at greater risk by this technology. The social implications of financial AI raise profound questions and challenge our conception of the broader tech-societal relationship. AI is expected to transform the role of financial systems when it comes to consumer accessibility and interaction with institutions while also adapting newer AI-driven economic policies. This advancement blurs the boundaries between technology and society, challenging our ethical frameworks and all the regulatory measures we have established. It discusses the wider social implications for society of integrating AI, urging us to reflect more rigorously on this ethical evolution [43]. However, the broader societal impacts of AI-driven finance go beyond social equity and economic welfare. It should also ensure that issues like data security, privacy, and the reduction of human agency are addressed. There are concerns that the human element of financial services, including personal touch and discretion, might be lost if AI becomes prevalent in decision-making at mainstream banks. Striking the right balance between efficiency, personalization, algorithmic accuracy, and human judgment is crucial. A study by [44], warns that a human rights-centric approach to AI in finance will be critical to alleviate the potential threats of an insolvent world. However, the consequences of AI-supported financial decisions on society are infinitely more intricate and sophisticated.
