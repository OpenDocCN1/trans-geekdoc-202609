# Potential Pitfalls of AI in Finance

However advantageous it may seem, implementing AI in banking is not an easy task. Perhaps one of the key challenges is the ethical side of AI deployment, such as the privacy issues and the biases associated with algorithms. Deploying AI technology when constructively making financial decisions reigns on substantial questions regarding such structures being presented with justice and equity. Furthermore, there is the pressure that these artificial intelligence systems will consolidate existing mindsets or create new discrimination models, more so in credit scores and fraudulent activity detection [12].

An additional risk that must be fully addressed is providing AI systems in an objective and non-liable manner for the users. If financial institutions exploit AI for their core processes, the probability of cyber-attacks and data theft would also increase. The confidence and assurance of the customers whose funds are being managed by the financial institution rests on how secure the AI-enabled systems are [14].

# Balancing Promise and Pitfalls

To fully realize the potential of AI in banking, a clear-headed approach is necessary to embrace the opportunities offered by AI in banking and, at the same time, curtail its risks. AI helps improve productivity and spur development but also inevitably brings in ethical, regulatory and safety concerns that must be handled [12]. That is why rules and principles for the ethical and responsible use of Artificial Intelligence in finance need to be worked out to ensure that AI’s advantages in banking will be realized while maintaining respect for moral principles and the trust of clients.

It is common knowledge that AI has much to offer in transforming the finance sector but concerns about what can go wrong must be placed on the table. Hence, a more cautious and more balanced approach is necessary, taking into account risks such as ethical concerns to ensure that the benefits of AI are fully realized.
