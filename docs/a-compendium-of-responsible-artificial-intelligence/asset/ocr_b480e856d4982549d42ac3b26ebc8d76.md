# Comparative Analysis of Governance Models

Developing responsible AI necessitates the establishment of robust governance frameworks and standards. These frameworks provide a guiding structure for the development, deployment, and usage of AI systems so that they are aligned with ethical principles as well as societal values. The specific approaches may vary, but there are some core governance principles that are considered important across the models.

Accountability: AI systems should be designed and deployed to ensure accountability for their actions. This would include who might be accountable for decisions made by the AI system, and policies on how any adverse consequences or harms may be redressed. Accountability mechanisms can be adopted in forms such as transparency, traceability, clear roles, and defined responsibilities. For example, under the General Data Protection Regulation of the European Union, organizations are mandatorily required to appoint a Data Protection Officer who is responsible for ensuring compliance of their organization with the data protection laws.

Transparency: AI systems must be designed and operated in such a way that their decisions make sense. It clearly explains algorithms, data, and processes used within the system. Techniques include explainable AI, data provenance, and public disclosure of AI system capabilities and limitations to improve transparency. The framework for AI risk management as established by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology is of primary relevance since it focuses on the aspect of transparency. This requirement stands at a high level in terms of compliance, ensuring that stakeholders are informed regarding the purpose, design, and limitations of the AI system.
