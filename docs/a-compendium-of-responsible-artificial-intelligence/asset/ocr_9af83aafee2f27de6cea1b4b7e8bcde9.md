# Key Provisions

+   1. Risk-Based Classification: AI systems are classified into four categories based on their risk to rights and safety: minimal, limited, high, and unacceptable risk. High-risk AI systems, such as those used in employment or law enforcement, are subject to stricter regulations.

+   2. Transparency Requirements: High-risk AI systems must provide clear information about their purpose, functioning, and potential biases. This includes documentation on how bias has been assessed and mitigated.

+   3. Bias Mitigation: Developers of high-risk AI systems are required to implement measures to detect, prevent, and mitigate bias. This includes conducting impact assessments and ensuring fairness across different demographic groups.

+   4. Human Oversight: High-risk AI systems must have mechanisms for human oversight to ensure that automated decisions do not lead to unfair or discriminatory outcomes.

# U.S. Civil Rights Act (Title VI) and Fair Housing Act

# Overview

In the U.S., existing civil rights legislation, such as the Civil Rights Act (Title VI) and the Fair Housing Act, provides a foundation for addressing bias in AI, particularly in areas such as employment and housing.

# Key Provisions

+   1. Discrimination Prohibition: These laws prohibit discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin in various domains. They apply to AI systems used in these areas, requiring that they do not produce discriminatory outcomes.

+   2. Enforcement: Regulatory agencies, such as the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), are tasked with enforcing these provisions and addressing complaints related to biased AI systems.

# California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)

# Overview

The CCPA, enacted in California, focuses on data privacy but has implications for bias mitigation by enhancing transparency and user control over personal data.

# Key Provisions

+   1. Data Access and Control: Consumers have the right to access, delete, and opt-out of the sale of their personal data. This transparency helps ensure that individuals can understand and control how their data is used by AI systems.
