This chapter delves into transparency and explainability in the field of artificial intelligence, exploring they contribute to accountability and trust in AI systems. It begins by defining both concepts, distinguishing them, and examining their interrelation. The chapter discusses the implications of these principles in applications, with a particular focus on high-stakes domains, such as healthcare, finance, and criminal justice, where decisions directly or indirectly impact individuals and society. Additional challenges addressed include model complexity versus interpretability, and potential strategies for enhancing explanatory responses, such as using interpretable models and post-hoc explanation techniques. The chapter also reviews regulatory frameworks and ethical guidelines aimed at introducing greater transparency into AI. This encompasses best practices and research that enable both practitioners and policymakers to create AI systems that are effective and intelligible and just. The ultimate goal is to foster a better-informed public discourse about the deployment of AI technologies.

# ## Introduction

Transparency and explainability have become crucial principles in the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence (AI) to ensure the responsible and efficient use of these technologies for societal benefit [1]. Transparency refers to the level of openness and accessibility of an AI system’s inner workings, allowing stakeholders to understand the decision-making process. In contrast, explainability refers to the ability of an AI system to offer logical and easily comprehensible explanations for its results. As AI systems become more intricate, their choices may appear unclear, creating difficulties in establishing trust, responsibility, and ethical application. This chapter examines the fundamental principles of transparency and explainability, emphasizing their significance in reducing risks, building trust, and promoting ethical utilization of AI advancements.
