This legislation demands strict standards in terms of the collection, use, and storage of personal data: for example, the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the United States. The personal data that AI systems deal with on behalf of other people should be aligned with such regulations and only ensure that data is processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner.

In addition to data protection laws, AI developers and organizations may be subject to other appropriate regulations based on the application or industry. For example, if it is used in the medical field, an AI system has to abide by the regulation on a medical device or patient-privacy regulation. Similarly, if an AI system is used in the finance industry, it must adhere to the rules on financial services or securities trading.

Adhering to these regulatory demands substantial oversight of data in AI development, requiring organizations to consider the following:

+   • Data minimization: Collecting only data that is focused on a specific outcome.
+   • Data retention: That enables holding it for no more than a reasonable period.
+   • Data security: Ensuring that proper technical and organizational measures are in place to protect against unauthorized or unsolicited access to such data.
+   • Data subject deletions: It should grant individuals the right to access, correct, or request the deletion of their personal data.
+   • Accountability: Demonstrating that the organization has implemented appropriate safeguards to ensure compliance with data protection laws.

Additionally, AI developers and organizations are likely to follow ethical codes or rules of conduct, that outline how to develop and use the AI responsibly. These guidelines typically cover aspects such as fairness, transparency, accountability, and human oversight.

Building trust in AI is essential, and this can only be achieved by respecting legal and ethical obligations, thereby avoiding legal and reputational risks. Failure to comply may attract heavy fines, penalties, and harm to an organization's reputation. It is by understanding and addressing the legal and ethical implications of AI that developers and organizations can develop responsible and beneficial AI systems.
