# Table 5 Core principles of responsible AI

| Principles | Detailed Analysis | References |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Transparency and Explainability | • As a practical matter, interpretability is the ability to explain what your machine learning model does so human beings can understand it. As such, the functioning of financial AIs must be optimized for full transparency. This is not just discussed in technical terms, but it happens with the clear communication of all stakeholders.<br>• So, the AI systems that approve mortgages: If you want an AI system to be permitted to make mortgage approvals, it externally has to look fair—as described above.<br>• But from a technical perspective, this fairness also needs enabling so that the machine’s decisions can be communicated in human language and thus understood by regulators or loan seekers. Research emphasizes the importance of XAI in critical contexts. | [62] |
| Fairness and Non-Discrimination | • AI in finance should be developed to deliver fairness and non-discrimination, meaning it does not reinforce bias or prejudice.<br>• This involves looking at the trained AI data to make sure there are no historical biases and stereotypes included in it.<br>• This means AI models for pricing insurance cannot have gender or race as part of the model. Fairness in AI has been well-studied from the bias detection and mitigation perspective. | [63] |
| Privacy and Data Security | • Since the AI systems for banking touch such sensitive personal information, it needs to be processed using the most substantial privacy principles with specifically rigid security necessities.<br>• That means actions to achieve compliance with Data Privacy Legislation, such as GDPR and advanced defences, are correlated with minimizing possible damage.<br>• Works have reinforced that data privacy in AI should be given top priority with secure AI frameworks. | [64] |
| Accountability and Oversight | • Financial AI decisions need to be transparent, and there must be independent oversight of how these systems operate. These are the nuts and bolts of AI—its governance mechanisms.<br>• However, someone must oversee AI’s decisions to ensure they are acceptable upon closer examination or when there are warning signs impacting its valuable career potential.<br>• A wide range of law and ethics studies surrounds accountability in AI. | [30] |
