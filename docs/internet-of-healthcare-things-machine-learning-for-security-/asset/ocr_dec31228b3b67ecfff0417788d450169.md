VALIDATION MECHANISMS FOR IoHT 29

It is not uncommon to notice that the general requirements of security for any IT based system are equally applicable to the IoT environment; however, the scale and scope of the requirements stand enhanced due to the likely damage that could result in case of any compromise. Preserving confidentiality, including denial of data, by making them inaccessible, whether it is sensitive medical data, or the details of the patients, doctors, providers and supporting institutions to unauthorized users improves the level of security. Integrity and encryption ensure that data, whether it is patient-related or other sensitive data, is not changed or modified while in transit by any adversary action. More importantly, the integrity of stored data and content should not be compromised, claiming extra vigil and protection. Here, although the emphasis is on medical data, protection is equally applicable for other forms of data as well as its metadata.

The weakest node or link represents the most vulnerable point prone to attract the attention of attackers, and such weak nodes either in isolation or on a cumulative basis influence the overall security of the system. It is observed that the most vulnerable, and resistance-offering category of nodes, are devices that have IoT capability—yet nevertheless part of the end user device family. The vulnerability arises both from the hardware features of the devices with embedded IoHT capabilities, as well as user behavior, determining the willingness to follow and implement recommended protocols.

Protection in the form of authentication and authorization should be implemented at all devices, interfaces and links to ensure protection against attacks. Both of the protection mechanisms are needed when the device contacts the data transmitter, and when the latter interacts with the server designated to collect and analyze all the data (data aggregator). Entire infrastructure must be equipped with resilience in the form of walls or denial of service mechanisms at the data transfer level, to prevent any access to seekers/hackers other than the domain of authorized users.
