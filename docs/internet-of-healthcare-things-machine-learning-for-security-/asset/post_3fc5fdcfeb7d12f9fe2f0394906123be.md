## 7.2 Overview of Blockchain

A blockchain is an encrypted block sequence that is validated by the network nodes and records a collection of transactions. In the block, every transactions are checked by their timestamp (a time marker that corresponds to the exact time and dates the block was made), ensuring its validity. A central aspect of blockchain technology is the hashing algorithm, which is a unidirectional function, both in terms of its encryption and its irreversibility. In a blockchain, completed transactions are represented chronologically in a block that is related to the previous block by its hash. Therefore, after the block is finished, it is a particle in an immutable chain of blocks referred as the ledger, so it can be safely stored. Each user has a copy of the blockchain and can verify it quickly to ensure that it has not been compromised. Through this encrypted, timestamped, irreversible, immutable blockchain, it is possible to keep information anonymity regardless of how the technology is used [2].
