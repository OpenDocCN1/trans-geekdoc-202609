Vendors’ group that develops applications to provide a range of medical services is the next level of stakeholder. This group should follow standards and protocols for communication, aligned with best industry practices, enabling secure functioning of the individual components as well as the sub-systems. Tendencies and practices that resort to adopting unlicensed and non-standard platforms, and overriding controls, should be avoided. It is the function and responsibility of the system integrator to establish qualification criteria for the selection of applications and devices that conform to, and comply with, secure list of standards and protocols. The often-observed painful practice among the developers is the claim that implementation and incorporation of security features within the applications would not be cost-effective, and the resources required to keep track of the updates and version changes is intense. The cost-effectiveness can be justified by the damage that could be done to the system; and ease updates, artificial intelligence may be deployed for scanning and making required changes.

Doctors and other medical professionals form the second important category of stakeholders in IoHT. Doctors are both users as well as providers, who help craft the services’ list based on which applications are developed. Doctors and their associated medical colleagues are the most critical resources in the healthcare value chain, yet present themselves as vulnerabilities from a user’s perspective. Their behavior and knowledge of operating the IoHT devices is key to the successful protection of data and its security thereof. Importantly, their input and feed are back of paramount importance to segregate data into three categories: sensitive data, personal data and non-personal data. Their judgement of response times and sense of assigning criticality can help design the appropriate security features of the IoHT.

Medical administrators usually make the call to authorize or restrain the budget for a system’s architecture, configuration, and security features. The tendency observed among such stakeholders is that cost saving is the driving force behind their measures: their consideration and decisions for system scoping and expenditure is guided by financial considerations. It is crucial for this group of stakeholders to be aware of the significance of incorporating security mechanisms right from design implementation, to ensure the long-term sustainability of it. 

Policy makers, including governments, function as stakeholders who prime the IoHT landscape with appropriate rules and regulations.
