Features of isolating parts of the network and always protecting the core (central server and storage) is a key feature of any security mechanism.

Classification of data into categories like sensitive, personal and non-personal would greatly help in designating access to various categories of users. Other than medical professionals, it suffices other users to see and transact with the kernel without having an insight into the core. An insurance company would be happy to deal with a patient identification number without seeing the personal details while processing a claim. At the time of admitting a claim, details of the patient may be verified once, rather than every time the insurance company deals with the stakeholders on the claim.

Defining a hierarchy of users, and designating the scope of access to data for each group, would help securing the privacy of the data.

# 2.6 Challenges to the Security Fabric

Any common user having one device (smartphone for example) engages with the device for conducting her normal communication needs either aware or not aware of the potential security risks associated with injurious injuries. The person uses the same device for IoHT, thus exposing the network/system of IoHT to the same risk as before, though the type of use and impact are varied. It is similar to a situation of a part of the body getting infected and the effect of infection growing and spreading to the rest of the system or interconnected network.

Providing secure access based on the principle of end-to-end authentication in terms of its status and health (infected or not) and such a screening methodology poses a challenge when multi-modal operations of common devices are prevalent as seen in the IoHT scenario. Protocols to scan and screen users and devices every time access is sought is both times consuming and expensive. Deployment of code of behavior and restraints of non-exposure to suspect form part of security mechanisms to protect against compromises. Attackers are trained to locate and identify the weakest point in the IoHT system to breach the entire network.
