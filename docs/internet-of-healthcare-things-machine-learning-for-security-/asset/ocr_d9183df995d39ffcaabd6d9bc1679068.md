# 4.2 Overview of Internet of Things

From the technical point of view, the Internet of Things (IoT) is simply termed as the conversion of physical devices, objects or things into a digital product. This brings a new vision or meaning to real-world things by making them more intelligent. This means adding computing capabilities to all things or devices ever connected to cloud end servers or not well as integrating backend support for utilizing and analyzing and controlling those devices. IoT is a term that anticipates the communication between objects in everyday life by utilizing various kinds of sensors as Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID), actuators that function to detect, capture and transfer essential data to the Internet from their setting. IoT is a concept that, through using high technology, environs communication between the physical and digital worlds. Owinging to the wide size of the digital context, the number of users and the number of smartphones, energy consumption rates have reached distressing levels over the past decade. The growth of linked devices in 2030 would be up to 100 billion [7]. Scientists who expect a massive information rate and an immense information size at the expense of the extraordinary release of carbon into the atmosphere. In later years, it is anticipated to grow. Clean or green energy is becoming an attractive study field in the advancement of technology because of these immense (CO₂) pollution, environmental and health issues. One of the fascinating innovations is the Internet of Things (IoT) [8]. It makes it possible to connect people and staff everywhere, with anything and everything, utilizing any connection and any service. It provides a forum for seamlessly connecting devices and sensors inside a smart world to people with highly sophisticated services. All types of data about the target are sensed and collected by sensors and then the data could be further processed and analysed to retrieve relevant detail to make smart utilities [9].
