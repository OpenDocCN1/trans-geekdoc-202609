# 3.3.3 Current Technologies in Use

Numerous technologies are in use to make sure the security & integrity of big data health. Commonly used techniques are:

+   1) **Certification:** Certification is the setting or confirmation that a statement about a company is valid and truthful and offers a fundamental opportunity in all societies. It provides access to your business, protects your customers' identities, and sees who your customers are.
Data collection can lead to rare problems, especially Man in the Middle Attacks (MITMs). Most cryptographic methods comprise point authentication specifically to prevent MITM attacks. Transport Layer Security (TLS) [8] & Secure Socket Layer (SSL) are cryptographic methods that ensure communication over networks such as the Internet. SSL & TLS encrypt network connection segments at the end of the transport layer. Various versions of the protocol are widely used in applications such as Web Browsing, Voice over IP (VoIP), Internet Fax & Messaging. Additional updates to hash methods such as SHA-256 [9] & Kerberos methods are available. In addition, sensitive 360° data can be verified using the Bull algorithm. This calculation was used to ensure data security and to track the relationship between the original and the replicated data. Anyone with permission can also read or write basic information. The researchers also proposed a new and simple validator model that uses a temporary pad algorithm. It eliminates the transmission of passwords to servers. As part of the healthcare system, health information provided by your service provider and user identity must be verified in each exam [10].

+   2) **Encryption:** Data encryption is an effective way to prevent unauthorized access to confidential information. This solution enables and maintains data logging throughout its lifecycle, from data centers to endpoints (including phones used by doctors, physicians and management). Encryption helps prevent prevent packet sniffing and storage device theft.

Medical institutions or providers must ensure that cryptographic systems are appropriate and convenient for patients and health workers. The number of keys stored for each collection should also be limited.

Although many cryptographic algorithms have been commonly created and very well used (Rijndael, RSA, AES, RC4 [9, 11, 12], DES, RC6, 3DES [13], IDEA and so on), they are still a challenge. It determines the appropriate encryption algorithm as the best way to keep your storage secure.
