## 3.2 Big Data Privacy & Security Issues
The Security & Privacy of Big Data is the main issue. Privacy is the ability to protect sensitive information from truly identifiable medical information. The focus is on the utilization and management of their information, such as developing policies to collect, share, and use patient’s data, and creating approval conditions. Security is generally defined as the protection against unauthorized access, but some include certain privacy and availability notifications. It focuses on obtaining information and protecting it from malicious attacks. Security is necessary to ensure information, and providing security alone is not enough. Table 3.1 emphasizes the additional contrast between security & privacy.

Table 3.1 Difference between security & privacy.

| Security | Privacy |
| :--- | :--- |
| Security is the "confidentiality, integrity & availability" of data. | Privacy is the appropriate use of user’s information. |
| Various techniques like Encryption, Firewall, etc. are used in order to prevent data compromise from technology or vulnerabilities in the network of an organization. | The organization can't sell its patient/user’s information to a third party without prior consent of the user. |
| It may provide for confidentiality or protect an enterprise or agency. | It concerns with patient’s right to safeguard their information from any other parties. |
| Security offers the ability to be confident that decisions are respected. | Privacy is the ability to decide what information an individual goes to and where to. |

## 3.3 Big Data Security Problem
Medical facilities store, maintain, and share a wealth of information to represent productive and legitimate issues, but have the disadvantage of lack of technical support & limited security. The situation is greater and more complicated in the medical field. Malicious hackers can use these data mining strategies and techniques to detect and discover sensitive information that could lead to data breaches. While security measures remain the avenue of communication in question, attackers are still the most vulnerable. Prices will steadily rise as methods of overcoming security measures are developed.

In terms of security and accuracy, researchers [4] argue that a high level of data security includes three aspects: information security, access control and data security. As a result, healthcare organizations must implement security controls and methods to manage appropriate security information, equipment and programming, and reliable clinical data on internal and external risks. When starting a business, you need to create a data lifecycle to make appropriate decisions about the maintenance, profitability, reuse, and analysis of registered or new data.
