The hypervisor must obey preferential instructions which can lead to performance penalties since the time taken for emulating trap mechanism instructions is thousands of times longer than executing the intrinsic instructions.

Different methods that do not include processor architectures in hypervisors should not match the specified ISA. A workaround is to change and execute the guest OS, resulting in para-virtualization, before its launch in a VM. These modifications replace hypercalls for critical instructions (instructions that alter the actions of the processor) or other OS modules, such as the I/O subsystem. Hypercalls are utilities that the hypervisor provides invoked by the guest OS. They are similar in OS to machine calls (syscalls). In addition, hypervisor functionality can be expanded by using PV, for maximizing the inter-VM communication implementation. Additionally, PV is also used to boost performance in environments where performance problems are faced by completely virtualized hypervisors.

In history, the ARM architecture is not virtualizable, because when executed in privileged mode, some sensitive instructions do not trap. However, as ARMv7 architecture, the current 32-bit ARM processors, including the Cortex-A15, have virtualization hardware support. There have been various degrees of research ventures aiming to what they need de-virtualization and have not been stabilized, they allow virtualization on ARM processors without hardware virtualization support. KVM/ARM is primarily designed to work with the virtualization extensions allowed to run the unmodified guest OS on ARM processors. The need for stronger and easier hypervisors led to hardware support for processor vendors, which culminated in hardware-assisted virtualization. A variety of methods may be used to support hardware. Various rights permitting hypervisor, application, and operating system kernel with multiple authorizations; and intrude virtualization for enhanced device hyper power. Therefore, the vendor of the processor decides the hardware-assisted support standard, the functionality required and the costs depending on the market niche.
