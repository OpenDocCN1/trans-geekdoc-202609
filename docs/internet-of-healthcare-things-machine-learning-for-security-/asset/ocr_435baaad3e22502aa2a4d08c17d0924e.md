# 2.4 Sources of Vulnerability

The primary source of vulnerability, whom via the entire system, or parts of it (if careful isolation methodologies are designed and implemented), is the beneficiary (patient). The plethora of devices found in the market, with associated applications for measurement of physical and biological features of the functioning of a human body, poses the real challenge to administer security while transferring data from devices to the next layer.

Patients tend to share their medical data with friends and third-party medical professionals to seek their opinion (to cross-check on their primary advice provided by the physician). Such actions expand the scope of vulnerability when login details are shared or access is made possible by proxy methods to medical records.

The most common source of vulnerability is the internet itself, being a ubiquitous platform. Hackers and attackers thrive on

+   1. the open access policy of the internet,
2. on the unbridled aspirations of the development community,

whom try and experiment with new products, algorithms, and protocols. All connectors and gateways in the network are potential sources of vulnerability, and system integrations, done piecemeal, are a sure recipe to invite cyber-attacks.

Allowing access to remote users is another source of vulnerability. It is observed when the operations and maintenance of IoHT as a service is outsourced, the vendor would have remote access to the system, to diagnose and rectify any alarms or malfunctioning. Granting remote access (usually through the internet) increases the risk of compromise unless the devices are encrypted and dedicated to IoHT servicing.

Another method to assess and locate vulnerabilities is to deploy specialized techniques like forensic investigations and risk management in wire-communications [4]. Such tools help the system integrator, responsible for network security, to identify the potential points of vulnerabilities in terms of points of intrusions, which would enable the designer to activate denial of service upon recognition of attempted intrusions.
