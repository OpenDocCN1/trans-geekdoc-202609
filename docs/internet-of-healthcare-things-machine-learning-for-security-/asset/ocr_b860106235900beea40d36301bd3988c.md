Amy et al. have presented a scoping review for health research data using the Twitter platform as a source of information. The review articles include electronic databases OvidEidPub, Ovid MEDLINE (R) Daily and Ovid MEDLINE(R), Ovid Embase, Ovid PsycINFO, Ovid EBM, CINAHL, Elsevier Scopus, and Thomas Reuters of Science Core Collection for developing a search strategy [11]. Bian et al. have presented an overview and evolution of the Internet of Things (IoT) with Twitter data trends. Sentiment analysis is carried out on public opinion on IoT and the result shows that an overall perception is positive over Twitter users. Authors have performed topic modeling to find topics on Twitter related to IoT search. It searches a predominant discussion over business, technologies, privacy and security issues in IoT [12]. Social media offer opportunity to research to get a better understanding of numerous domains. Health related issues are among the important issues where social media enhance availability for various health problems. Authors have carried out statistical, open vocabulary and Linguistic Inquiry Count (LIWC) analysis for evaluation of health disorders mental illness post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, bipolar disorder, and seasonal affective disorder (SAD) from data collected from user's opinion on Twitter social media. The correlations metrics are applied to analytics used in the research work and the results show quantifiable signals for numerous mental disorders in the Twitter data [13].

Lee, Agrawal and Chowdhury have proposed a flu surveillance system and used Twitter health data to monitor related symptoms in real time in the U.S. Twitter APIs are used to collect data related to flu symptoms followed through data pre-processing to clean the tweet data by removing unnecessary texts. The tweet is processed to extract text, time and user locations. Flu similarity is detected across the U.S. with geographical modeling. Text modeling is performed for flu-related comparison and interactive data visualization is done in support of the proposed system [14]. Paul Dredze have introduced the extended Ailment Topic Aspect Model (ATAM) for discovering a range of diseases with their symptoms. ATAM is used to analyze mentions of health-related tweets to monitor synonym surveillance over time for geographic regions. The ATAM model outcome shows correlations between model output and available public health data [15].
