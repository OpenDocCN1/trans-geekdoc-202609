## 8.2 Healthcare Industry

The Healthcare Industry is vast and has various sectors for providing healthcare facilities and products for patients. The interaction between different Healthcare stakeholders involve parties like the patient, the Doctor, the Hospital staff and Management, the Pharmaceutical company, the Insurance company, or the Government. Such parties and all intermediates need to have effective and error-free data conveyance for reliability and to avoiding fraudulent.

Healthcare mainly concentrates on maintenance and health improvement by prevention, management, diagnosis, treating diseases, illness, injuries, and other physical and mental ailments. Healthcare availability to the public varies across different countries and communities and is largely driven by the social and economic environment and government policies. However, healthcare organizations are established to meet the needs of the general public [1].

- **1) Primary Care:** This is the first healthcare-related service received by a patient. These services usually consist of a general practitioner/family physician who acts as the first contact for the patient in the treatment process. The Primary Care doctor should be able to accurately diagnose the patient based on his broad sense of knowledge.
- **2) Secondary Care:** This primarily involves acute care, usually given in hospital emergency departments. Here only essential treatment is given and if necessary, the patient is referred to a tertiary health department. Therefore, the care given by a secondary physician is usually for a brief window of time.
- **3) Tertiary Care:** This is a specialized healthcare-related service given to patients on referral from primary or secondary health departments. This sector requires more care and time to heal the illness, usually for complex medical and surgical processes.

### 8.2.2 Health Information Technology (HIT)

Before studying the Healthcare sector and its stakeholders, it is essential to know about HIT or Health IT and its components related to this chapter for better understanding later on. Health Information Technology (HIT) involves the processing and management of healthcare data that leads to efficient and effective communication and decision making [2].
Various components of HIT are:

- Electronic Health Record (EHR)—EHR stores extensive medical history of patients and organizes records from multiple providers together in one place.
- Electronic Medical Record (EMR)—EMR stores standard clinical and medical information of a patient, all gathered in the healthcare provider's office.
- Personal Health Record (PHR)—PHR is a privately maintained medical history of patients for personal use only.
- Medical Practice Management software (MPM)—It is a management software that is designed to facilitate everyday operations in a medical facility efficiently.
- Health Information Exchange (HIE)—HIE allows patients to securely share their confidential medical information electronically with healthcare providers without compromising their privacy.

### 8.2.3.1 The Patient

The Patient is the end receiver of services provided in the healthcare industry and becomes a significant stakeholder. The only way of assessing if the care and facility provided were up to the mark depends upon the patient's satisfaction and the recovery made in treatment. Patient satisfaction depends mainly on the quality of service and expenses incurred during the process.

This all should be measured while the information remains private or is shared only to the authorized parties, and there is transparency in the transactions being made. The presence of centralized management of healthcare databases makes it vulnerable to cybercrimes like data theft, manipulation, and misuse. This calls for a secure data flow and permission granting to authorize parties in the process, ensuring privacy and correct use of patient information.

### 8.2.3.2 The Pharmaceutical Industry

The role of pharmaceutical companies is to manufacture medicines or drugs which are required by a patient for his/her treatment, playing an important role in the healthcare sector. Before manufacturing medical drugs, the company has to invest in research and development before the mass-scale production stage. Due to all these intermediary steps, the cost of a medical drug increases.

A patient's health is greatly dependent on the medicines or drugs taken by him/her for treatment. Such high promotes counterfeiting of the medical drugs or meds taken by the patient. This can lead to serious health consequences and not just fraudulence. The framework of the pharmaceutical industry needs reformation and a powerful fraud detection system, ensuring no compromises to patients' health.

### 8.2.3.3 The Healthcare Service Providers

These include the doctors, nurses, or any other practitioner, and play a vital role in patient care. They determine the seriousness of the patient's condition and take appropriate measures for the welfare of the patient. The healthcare worker does this by examining the patient's current condition, measuring all the symptoms shown as well as considering the patient's medical history to determine the next appropriate step for treatment.
