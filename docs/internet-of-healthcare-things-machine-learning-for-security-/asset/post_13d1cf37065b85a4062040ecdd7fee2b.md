In a blockchain system, each user owns a pair of public key and a private key. The private key of the user is kept confidential and is used to perform digital signatures. Blockchain's work can be easily understood with help of Figure 8.5. First, a user digitally signs a transaction that he participated in and then makes a transaction request which is broadcasted to the P2P network.

The digital signature consists of two important phases: the signing phase and the verification phase. Suppose the sender wants to send a message to a receiver. While signing, the sender will encrypt data using their private key and then sends the receiver the message. While verifying, the receiver validates the data using the public key of the sender. This protocol allows the receiver to easily check if data is tampered with or not. One of the most widely used algorithms to perform digital signatures is the Elliptic curve digital signature algorithm [8].

After validation and verification of the transaction, a new data block is created. This block is then mined and added to the chain. The Blockchain is in its most trivial is a linked list that contains time-stamped series of immutable data connected by a hash of the previous block. ## 8.3.5 Challenges and Drawbacks of Blockchain

Although the technology seems very promising yet it faces some challenges. These challenges may be resolved in the future with innovative solutions and emerging technologies. Now we discuss some of the challenges based on our current technological infrastructure (as shown in Figure 8.6).

## Scalability:

Scalability is a major drawback of Blockchain Technology. When one uses the Bitcoin Network, the transactions completed depend on the network congestion i.e. the more the number of individuals or nodes the more is the possibility of the system slowing down. For instance, Visa and Bitcoin can process 65K and seven transactions per second respectively.

## Data Immutability:

The data once written in the nodes cannot be altered or removed. Immutability should only be ensured if the network nodes are distributed uniformly among different organizations. A blockchain network (peer-to-peer) can be manipulated by a person/organization or possesses 50% or more nodes participating in the network, making the chain vulnerable.

## Not Completely Secure:

Although Blockchain technology is safer than alternative platforms yet it is not 100% poolproof. There are ways in which Blockchain security could be compromised. Some of them are:

- 1. **51% attack**: In a 51% attack, if an organization can manage 51% or more of the network nodes, then it will take the management of the network. By doing so, they can alter the information within the transactions and additionally do double-spending.
- 2. **Double-spending**: Double spending can only be possible on the networks with an exposure to the 51% attack. Different consensus algorithms like Proof of Work (POW), Proof of Stake (POS), etc. can be used to prevent it.
- 3. **DDoSs attack**: During a DDoS attack, nodes of Blockchain are bombarded with similar kinds of requests which lead to congestion of the network, thereby crashing the entire network.

## Cost and Implementation Struggle:

The cost for implementation, execution and maintenance of blockchain technology is quite high. Some prices include the hiring of developers, licensing cost if one opts for a paid blockchain solution, expenses of the management of a team that is an expert in various aspects of blockchain technology, and so. The cost of the maintenance associated with the solution also needs to be taken care of.

Lastly, it can be concluded that blockchain is not quite ready for practical applications and there is the scope of improvement to make it more reliable for daily needs.

### 8.4.1 Electronic Medical Records (EMR) and Electronic Health Records (EHR)

As discussed earlier EHR contains comprehensive information of patients going beyond standard clinical data while EMR keeps track of patient’s medical and treatment history. Earlier, the patient’s data was was stored in printed documents. People were responsible for keeping track of their medical history by storing their prescriptions. The usage of digital records made it easier for patients and doctors to access medical history. Healthcare professionals generally have to work with limited data. This hampers subsequent diagnosis and appropriate treatment steps. Researchers also struggle to find desired data for undertaking various studies [15].
