## 5.2 Literature Review

IoHTs systems are designed to support existing healthcare infrastructure. User's opinion has significantly mattered for the growth of innovative technology and solutions and plays an important role in technology future. It should be measured how a user's opinion is going to affect the evolution of upcoming technological trends. Healthcare services have always required the incorporation of innovation and technology. Authorities can take the help of these sentiment dynamics for better decision making and prioritize resources.

Kumar et al. have presented an framework of public opinion of IoT on social media. A comparative study is carried out to find the relevance between research on IoT and social media. A social media analytical framework is proposed to analyze data from the microblogging platform Twitter. Social media and IoT data are analyzed using bibliometric analysis and social media analytics framework. The research article finds that the public's interest is about the application of IoT while researchers are interested in the IoT technical aspect [1]. Zhou et al. have performed sentiment analysis of text at local government areas (LGAs) data fetched from Twitter, a social microblogging platform. The study has been carried out in a pandemic period to evaluate the effect of COVID-19 on LGAs. The analysis shows that there is general sentiment positive polarity shifting to negative polarity with increased cases of COVID-19, political decision and social events [2].

Verma and Sapra have presented sentiment analysis for cardiovascular disease from text data collected from Twitter [3]. Medford et al. have presented an analysis on Twitter contents for the COVID-19 pandemic. The early stage of pandemic analysis is carried out to understand public beliefs and sentiments [4]. Sharif *et al.* have collected information from Twitter for creating a mental illness corpus. A rule based system is presented to analyze the level of depression as high, moderate and low information fetched from social media. Negative lexicon corpus from publicly available data is created for analyzing depression statement and a decision tree-based system is developed with linguistic style and expression sentiment features [5].

Kamharia et al. have presented a study on skin cancer growth from the last decades. Skin detection in an early stage benefits users in diagnosis and treatments. Carleen Haven has presented an overview of the relationship between healthcare system and social media. Social media impacted the healthcare system by providing communication for patients and medical practitioner. "Hello Health" is a popular application for support hospitals [8].

Lee et al. have presented ontology for health technology. The further subdivided into wearable technology, telemedicine and mobile health. The presented ontology is. The keywords are extracted from Google Trends, Web of Science and Hashtagify [9].

Grover et al. have presented a study of health technology from Twitter. The Twitter data is collected from 15,587 users and evaluated using social media analytics framework. The study suggests that users are using the Twitter platform for getting information for example acute, chronic and communicable diseases [10].

Amy et al. have presented a scoping review for health research using the Twitter platform as a source of information. The review articles include electronic databases OvidEidPub, Ovid MEDLINE (R), Ovid MEDLINE(R), Ovid Embase, Ovid PsycINFO, CINAHL, Elsevier Scopus, and Thomas Reuters of Science Collection [11].

Bian et al. have presented an evolution of the Internet of Things (IoT) with Twitter data. Sentiment analysis is carried out on public opinion on IoT and the result shows that overall perception is positive over Twitter users. Authors have performed topic modeling to find topics on Twitter related to IoT. It searches a predominant discussion over business, technologies, privacy and security issues [12].

Social media offer opportunity to research to get a better understanding of numerous domains. Health related issues are among the important issues where social media enhance availability for various health problems. Authors have carried out statistical, open vocabulary and Linguistic Inquiry Count (LIWC) analysis for evaluation of health disorders mental illness post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, bipolar disorder, and seasonal affective disorder (SAD) from data collected from user's opinion on Twitter social media. The correlations metrics are applied to analytics used in the research work and the results show quantifiable signals for numerous mental disorders in the Twitter data [13].

Lee, Agrawal and Chowdhury have proposed a flu surveillance system and used Twitter health data to monitor related symptoms in real time in the U.S. Twitter APIs are used to collect data related to flu symptoms followed through data pre-processing to clean the tweet data. The tweet is processed to extract text, time and user locations. Flu similarity is detected across the with geographical modeling. Text modeling is performed for flu-related comparison and interactive data visualization is done in support of the proposed system [14].

Paul Dredze introduced the Ailment Topic Aspect Model (ATAM) for discovering a range of diseases with their symptoms. ATAM is used to analyze mentions of health-related tweets to monitor synonym surveillance over time for geographic regions. The ATAM model outcome shows correlations between model output and available public health data [15].
