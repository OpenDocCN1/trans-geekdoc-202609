# 2.1 Introduction

The main priority for healthcare organizations providing automated services beginning at registration, up to treatment and follow up, is to protect service beneficiaries and their data. This entails focused contribution from a range of development community supplying devices, applications and system integrators factoring issues security and privacy inbuilt into their design specially for the domain of IoT in healthcare. Intending to enable and provide optimal protection, one needs to understand the types of threats to which the network and its subsystems and individual components (devices) could be exposed.

Data from the Zion Market Research indicates that the value of global IoT healthcare market is anticipated to reach $14 billion by 2024 with a CAGR of 12.2% between 2017 and 2024. The increase is attributed to the increasing use of cloud computing in healthcare facilities. One of the principal drivers for the exponential growth envisaged in IoHT is the fast adoption of cloud computing and the accepted norm of storing data and hosting services through the cloud rather than the traditional practice of premises bound.

A survey conducted by McKinsey in 2015 [1], was found that breach of security scored extremely high on the list of concerns that could potentially impede the growth of IoT with its lack of confidence in the infrastructure and services. In the case of IoT, exposure to vulnerabilities arising out of low security may be the deterrent—a point underscored by the survey conducted in collaboration with the Global Semiconductor Alliance GSA. Any discussion on the potential growth opportunities in IoT is weighed when dealing with the issue of provisioning adequate security and the costs thereof.

Healthcare devices and applications collect, store and pass on personal healthcare data to provide the designed services. In case of internet, these devices, gadgets and wearables form the nodes of the network and are connected in such a way that they are both accessible and accessed anytime and anywhere as per connectivity requirements. The internet, such networks which have their terminals or origins on the internet are prone to hacking and attacks. It is necessary and important that the system designers and developers consider factor in protection elements and considerations while crafting the infrastructure and services to render IoT in the healthcare sector [2].
