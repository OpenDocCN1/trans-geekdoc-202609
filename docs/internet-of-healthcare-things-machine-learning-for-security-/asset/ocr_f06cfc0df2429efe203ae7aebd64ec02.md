Sharif *et al.* have collected information from Twitter for creating a mental illness corpus. A rule based system is presented to analyze the level of depression as high, moderate and low information fetched from social media. Negative lexicon corpus from publicly available data is created for analyzing depression statement and a decision tree-based system is developed with linguistic style and expression sentiment features [5].

Kamharia al have presented a study on skin cancer growth from the last decades. Skin detection in an early stage benefits users in diagnosis and treatments. A deep learning framework is proposed for the classification of cancer detection and classification with an integrated capability to the Internet of Health and Things (IoHT). The proposed work used the concept of transfer learning and VGG19, Inception V3, ResNet50, and SqueezeNet are selected as pre-trained architectures for feature extraction from images. Transfer learning is applied to IoHT framework and performance evaluation shows computational complexity and higher accuracy [6]. Talpada *et al.* have presented the study to analyze the correlation between numerous sentiment analysis methods: Valence Awareness Dictionary and Sentiment Reasoner (VADER), TextBlob, Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) and Stanford CoreNLP. Domain-specific keywords affect sentiment orientation of the targeted text. A Twitter-based sentiment model is proposed to evaluate the influence of telemedicine for heart attacks and epilepsy. A manually annotated dataset of health related tweets is built. LSTM deep learning model provides better accuracy for large and evenly distributed dataset than VADER and TextBlob[7].

Carleen Haven has presented an overview of the relationship between the healthcare system and social media. Social media impacted the healthcare system by providing a paperless communication medium for patients and medical practitioner. "Hello Health" is a popular web-based application for providing medicine assistance to patients to support groups as well large hospitals [8]. Lee *et al.* have presented ontology for health technology categorized into health technology and health information. The presented ontology is further subdivided into wearable technology, biotechnology, telemedicine, medical technology and mobile health. Ontology keywords are extracted from Google Trends, Web of Science and Hashtagify [9].
