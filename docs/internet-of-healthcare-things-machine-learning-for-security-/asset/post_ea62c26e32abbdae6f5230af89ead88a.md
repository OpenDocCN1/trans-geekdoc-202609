## 12.3 Illustration of Brain Cancer

This section provides an illustration of brain cancer along with its types as well as grades of tumor. It also exhibits some of the symptoms that may help in the diagnosis of the tumor.

## 12.3.1 Brain Tumor

When the genetic DNA of the damaged cells gathers at a place then it is shaped into a brain tumor. An estimated eighty-seven thousand individuals in the US with non-cancerous tumors was diagnosed in 2020 [1]. It can be assumed that the ratio can be 60:20 for non-cancerous and cancerous tumors. For both male and females, the 10th most lethal reason for death is cancer associated with brain and another nervous system [2]. The estimation for death from a primary cancerous brain tumor and CNS tumor in 2020 consists of 10,190 men and 7,830 women (18,020 adults) [2]. A vigorous brain comprised of ordinary brain matters but when this brain tissue shows some anomalous tissues with the ordinary tissues then these anomalous tissues are baptized as a brain tumor and tumor's name is given on the tumor it originates from or on the origin of their location in the brain. A brain tumor can be homogeneous or heterogeneous depending on the kinds of tumor. The visual of tumor varies depends on its intensity level. The intensity level varies depending on the type of tumor. If a patient has a benign tumor with the high-intensity level means tumor's section seems brighter than the immediate area while a patient with a low-level intensity tumor means the tumor's section seems darker than the immediate area, but the borderline seems to be brighter than the tumor's section.

## 12.3.2 Types of Brain Tumors

Diverse kinds of brain cancer are available which can be approximately over 120 tumors, but brain tumor can be mainly to be of two types such as primary brain tumor (non-cancerous or benign), and secondary brain tumor (cancerous or malignant).

- **Primary Brain Tumor:** Primary brain tumor have their own borderline to grow gradually, and these restrictions cover only brain parts but not the additional portions of the body. Generally, for a primary tumor the signal intensity level remains unwavering. In 2020, the estimation of total patients to be diagnosed from primary cancerous tumors of the brain and spinal cord in the United States are 13,590 men and 10,300 women (23,890 adults) [2]. Some of the benign tumors are Meningiomas, Schwannoma, and Gliomas with its types such as astrocytoma (Grades I to IV), and Oligodendroglioma, etc.
- **Malignant Brain Tumor:** Secondary brain tumor (Malignant) does not follow any borderline to obey which makes these types of tumor more complex as well as deadly. This type of tumor grows faster than non-cancerous tumors. In the case of this type of tumor, the signal intensity level continues deviating as it might start from diverse locations and reach to brain. In 2020, the estimation of 3,700 cases in adolescents and young adults were expected to be diagnosed for malignant brain tumors [3]. Some of the malignant tumors that invade parts of the brain are lung cancer (metastatic lung cancer), breast cancer, kidney cancer, etc.

## 12.3.3 Grades of Brain Tumors

Brain tumor is classified into two types i.e., primary brain tumor which can also be known as low-grade tumor (Benign) and secondary brain tumor (Malignant) known as a high-grade tumor. The low-grade tumor is known to be less lethality in contrast to a high grade tumor. These types of brain tumor are further alienated into grades of tumor which ranges from grade I to grade IV. This grading of tumor was done by World Health Organization to further comprehend the risk level and provide appropriate type of treatment to the patients. These different grades of tumor are characterized according to the structure of tumor perceptible through the microscope. Grades I and II are considered primary brain tumor while grades III and IV are considered secondary brain tumor. As previously it was distinguished that the benign tumor was less deadly compared to the Malignant tumor accordingly grades I and II become less deadly compared to grades III and IV. Discussion of each grade of tumor along with some names of the tumor are stated below:

- Grade I tumor: A grade I tumor can be effortlessly removed as it seldom invades the brain to other parts of the human body and it has an advantage over other types of tumor that it grows at a slow rate. Gangliocytoma, Pilytic astrocytoma, Ganglioglioma, and Craniopharyngioma are some of the tumors that fall under the category of this grade tumor.
