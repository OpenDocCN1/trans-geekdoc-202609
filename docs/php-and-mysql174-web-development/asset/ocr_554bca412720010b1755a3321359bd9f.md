Even the exposure of a table full of names and addresses is a serious violation of security. Customers value their privacy very highly, and a large list of names and addresses, plus some inferred information about them (such as “all thousand of these people like to shop at online tobacco stores”) creates a potential sell item to marketing firms that do not play by the rules, spammers, and so on.

All of these are terrible scenarios, but the most commonly seen that cause huge problems are leakage of credit card numbers, and leakage of passwords.

The value of credit card numbers is obvious—anyone obtaining a list of valid numbers along expiration dates, cardholder names, and so on, can either use the themselves, or more commonly, sell a list of card numbers to the highest bidder.

Passwords may be less interesting. Once an attacker has access to sensitive data inside your application, you may be asking passwords who would have any future. The username and password John Smith to sign up for your photo sharing app stands a good chance of being the same password and what he uses for his online banking.

In some cases engineers put a lot of attention to protecting personally information, but miss more leakage of data. One good example of this which has been seen a few times is companies sharing logs, typically for one person to do research or market research.

Usage data this can be mined for kinds of interesting facts. If IPs are associated with the logs, you can uniquely identify patterns of a particular user and a good guess as to their location. If web server contains URLs, as they generally do, these URLs contain usernames, passwords, or information about what (potentially private) endpoints are available on the website.

It goes without saying that any of the data leaks will damage your reputation: you lose customers who are unwilling to trust you after a security incident.

# Reducing the Risk

To reduce the risk of exposure, you need to limit the methods by which information can be accessed and limited. This process involves designing in mind, configuring your server and software properly, programming carefully, testing thoroughly, removing unnecessary services from the web server, and requiring authentication.

You need to design, configure, and test carefully to reduce the risk of a successful attack and, equally important, to reduce the chance that an error will leave your information open to accidental exposure.

You also need to remove unnecessary services from your web server to decrease the number of potential weak points. Each service you are running might have vulnerabilities. Each one needs to be kept up to date to ensure that known vulnerabilities are not present. The services that you do not use be more dangerous.
