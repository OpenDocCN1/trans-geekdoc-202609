Some sites have specific times when they expect to do most of their business. Online
bookmaking sites experience high demand just before major sporting events. One way that
crackers exploited to profit from DDoS attacks in 2004 was by extorting money from online
bookmakers with the threat of attacking during these peak times.

One of the reasons that DDoS attacks are so difficult to guard against is that they can be carried
out in a huge number of ways. Methods could include installing a program on a target machine
 uses most of the system's processor time, reverse spamming, or using one of many
automated tools. A reverse spam involves sending spam out the target listed as
the sender. This way, the target will have thousands of angry replies to deal with, as well as
her real emails classified as spam

Automated tools to launch distributed DDoS attacks on a target. Without needing
knowledge, somebody can scan a large number of machines for known vulnerabilities,
compromise a machine, and install the tool. Because the process is automated, an attacker
can install the tool on a single host in less than five seconds. When enough machines have
been coopted, all are instructed to flood the target with network traffic. Machines that have
been compromised in such a way are sometimes called zombies or bots, and a collection of
compromised machines used to launch an attack is called a botnet.

# Reducing the Risk

Guarding against DDoS attacks is difficult in general. With a little research, you can find
t default ports used by some common DDoS tools and close them. Your router might provide
mechanisms to limit the percentage of traffic that uses particular protocols such as ICMP.
If every network administrator could be relied on to vigilantly monitor
his own network, DDoS would not be a problem.

It's generally a good idea to have a plan for what happens when you receive a massive influx
t traffic, for whatever reason.

One option is to block problematic traffic at your load balancer. Of course, this only
works as long as your load balancer services, and as long as the traffic is coming from a
recognizable set of IPs (some botnets).

Another option is to develop a mechanism to make some parts or all of the static site,
even temporarily, and push it to a content distribution network. This works for managing
the friendly kind of traffic.

Another is to implement some feature flagging in your application. This allows you to turn
features on and off. In times of high load, you might switch less-critical or more expensive
features to cope with the extra traffic.
