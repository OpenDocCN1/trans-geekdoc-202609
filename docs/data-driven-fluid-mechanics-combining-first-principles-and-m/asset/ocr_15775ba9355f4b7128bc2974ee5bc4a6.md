Observe that \widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\mathcal{H}}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}} is the inverse Fourier transform of \widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\mathcal{H}} (i.e., the frequency spectra of the filtered data) while \widehat{\boldsymbol{D}} = \boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}} is the Fourier transform of \boldsymbol{D} along its rows (i.e., in the time domain).

The representation on the left is a direct consequence of the link between convolution theorem and eigenvalue decomposition of circulant matrices introduced in (8.25). The representation on the right opens to an intuitive and graphical representation of the filtering process that is worth discussing briefly.

First, we introduce the cross-spectral density matrix \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}}. This collects the inner product between the frequency spectra of the data evolution; it is the frequency domain analogous of the temporal correlation matrix \boldsymbol{K}. These matrices are linked:

\boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}^{\dagger}\widehat{\boldsymbol{D}} = \boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}}[\boldsymbol{D}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{D}]\overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{F}} = \boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}}\boldsymbol{K}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{F}} \Longleftrightarrow \boldsymbol{K} = \overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{F}}\boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}}. \tag{8.38}

Since \boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{F}} = \mathbf{I}, the equation on the right is a similarity transform, hence \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}} and \boldsymbol{K} share the same eigenvalues. Introduce the eigenvalue decomposition of \boldsymbol{K} in (8.26):

\boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}} = \boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}^{T})\overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{F}} = \overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{P}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{P}}^{\dagger}, \quad \text{that is,}\quad \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}}[i,j] = \sum_{r=0}^{n_{t}-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{\mathcal{P}}[i]\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{\mathcal{P}}[j]. \tag{8.39}

We thus see that the eigenvectors of \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}} are the conjugate of the Fourier transform of the eigenvectors of \boldsymbol{K}. The outer product notation on the right shows that the diagonal of this matrix contains the sum of the power spectra of the temporal structures of all the modes. This is the sum of positive real quantities.

Consider now the cross-spectral density matrix of the filtered data in (8.36) and use the distributive property of the Hadamard product to get

\boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}\mathcal{H}} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}\widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\mathcal{H}} = (\widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}\odot\mathcal{H})^{\dagger}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}\odot\mathcal{H}) = (\widehat{\boldsymbol{D}}^{\dagger}\widehat{\boldsymbol{D}})\odot(\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}\odot\mathcal{H}) = \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}}\odot\underline{\mathcal{H}}. \tag{8.40}

having introduced the 2D symmetric frequency transfer function \underline{\mathcal{H}} = \mathcal{H}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}. The figure in Exercise 2 gives a pictorial representation of the magnitude of such 2D frequency transfer function for ideal low-pass, band-pass, and high-pass filters. In a low-pass filter, for example, the 2D frequency transfer function is unitary within a square \mathcal{H}[i,j] \neq 0, for all i,j \in [-i_{c},i_{c}]\times[-i_{c},i_{c}] and approximately zero everywhere else.

We thus see that the filtering of the data constraints the spectral content of the POD modes: writing the cross-spectral density of the filtered data in terms of its eigenvectors as in (8.38), we find

\boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}\mathcal{H}} = \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}}\odot\underline{\mathcal{H}} \Longleftrightarrow \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}\mathcal{H}}[i,j] = \sum_{r=0}^{n_{t}-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}^{2}\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}[i]\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}[j]. \tag{8.41}

Since \boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}\mathcal{H}} \approx 0 outside the band-pass range of the 2D filter, and since the entries along its diagonals are a summation of positive quantities, we conclude that frequencies removed from the data cannot be present in any of the POD modes.
