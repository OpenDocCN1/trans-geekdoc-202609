# 12.1.2 Change of Variables in Control Systems

The balanced model reduction problem may be formulated in terms of first finding a coordinate transformation

$$\boldsymbol{x} = \mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z}\tag{12.5}$$

that hierarchically orders the states in $z$ in terms of their ability to capture the input-output characteristics of the system. We will begin by considering an invertible transformation $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$, and then provide a method to compute just the first $r$ columns, which will comprise the transformation $\mathbf{\Psi}$ in (12.2). Thus, it will be possible to retain only the first $r$ most controllable/observable states, while truncating the rest. This is similar to the change of variables into eigenvector coordinates, except that we emphasize controllability and observability rather than characteristics of the dynamics.

Substituting $\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z}$ into (12.1) gives

$$\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.6a}$$ $$\boldsymbol{y} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.6b}$$

Finally, multiplying (12.6a) by $\mathbf{T}^{-1}$ yields

$$\frac{d}{dt}\boldsymbol{z} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.7a}$$ $$\boldsymbol{y} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.7b}$$

This results in the transformed equations

$$\frac{d}{dt}\boldsymbol{z} = \hat{\mathbf{A}}\boldsymbol{z} + \hat{\mathbf{B}}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.8a}$$ $$\boldsymbol{y} = \hat{\mathbf{C}}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.8b}$$

where $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$, and $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}$. Note that when the columns of $\mathbf{T}$ are orthonormal, the change of coordinates becomes

$$\frac{d}{dt}\boldsymbol{z} = \mathbf{T}^*\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{T}^*\mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.9a}$$ $$\boldsymbol{y} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z} + \mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{u}\tag{12.9b}$$
