consider three scales; these are identified by three filters with band-pass bandwidths Δf1 = [0, f1], Δf2 = [f1, f2] and Δf3 = [f2, fs/2]. We lump this information in a frequency splitting vector FV = [f1, f2, f3]. With three scales, the temporal correlation matrix has nine partitions:

$$\boldsymbol{K} = \boldsymbol{D}^{\dagger} \boldsymbol{D} = (\boldsymbol{D}_1 + \boldsymbol{D}_2 + \boldsymbol{D}_3)^{\dagger} (\boldsymbol{D}_1 + \boldsymbol{D}_2 + \boldsymbol{D}_3) = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{3} \boldsymbol{D}_i^{\dagger} \boldsymbol{D}_j \tag{8.45}$$

Following an MRA formulation, we assume that these scales are isolated by filters with complementary frequency response, that is,

$$\boldsymbol{D}_i = [(\boldsymbol{D} \overline{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathcal{F}}) \odot \mathcal{H}_i] \boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\mathcal{F}} \quad \text{with} \quad \sum \mathcal{H}_i \approx \mathbf{1} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{H}_i \odot \mathcal{H}_j \approx \mathbf{0}. \tag{8.46}$$

This ensures a lossless decomposition of the data. We now use (8.39) and (8.45) in (8.44) to analyze which portion of the cross-spectral density $\boldsymbol{K}_{\mathcal{F}}$ is taken by each of the contributions in (8.44). Figure 8.3 gives a pictorial view of the partitioning, which can be constructed following the graphical representation in Exercise 2. In wavelet terminology, the term $\mathcal{H}_1^{\dagger} \mathcal{H}_1$ is the approximation term at the largest scale. The other “pure” terms $\mathcal{H}_2^{\dagger} \mathcal{H}_2$ and $\mathcal{H}_3^{\dagger} \mathcal{H}_3$ are diagonal details of the scales 2 and 3. The terms $\mathcal{H}_i^{\dagger} \mathcal{H}_j$ with $i > j$ are horizontal details while those with $j < i$ are vertical details.
