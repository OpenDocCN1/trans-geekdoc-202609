As for the previous tutorial, a function is used to extract all the information about the grid (in this case stored on a different file) and the velocity field.

This test case is characterized by a large-scale variation of the free-stream velocity. A plot of the velocity magnitude in the main stream is shown in Figure 8.5. In the first 1.5 s, the free-stream velocity is at approximately U∞ ≈ 12 m/s. Between t = 1.5 s and t = 2.5 s, this drops down to U∞ ≈ 8 m/s. The variation of the flow velocity is sufficiently low to let the vortex shedding adapt, and hence preserve an approximately constant Strouhal number of St = f d U∞ ≈ 0.19, with d = 5 mm the diameter of the cylinder. Consequently, the vortex shedding varies from f ≈ 459 Hz to f ≈ 303 Hz. Interestingly, the POD assigns the entire evolution to a single pair of modes, and it is hence not possible to analyze the vortex structures in the shedding for the two phases at approximately constant velocity, nor to distinguish them from the flow organization during the transitory phase.
