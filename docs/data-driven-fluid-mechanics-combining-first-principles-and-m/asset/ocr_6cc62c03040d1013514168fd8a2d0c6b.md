The direct problem (13.11a) reduces to

$$\mathbf{q}(t) = \int_{0}^{t} \exp(\mathrm{L}(t-t')) \mathbf{f} \exp(i\omega t') dt', \qquad (13.23a)$$ $$= (i\omega - \mathrm{L})^{-1} [\exp(i\omega t) - \exp(\mathrm{L}t)] \mathbf{f}. \qquad (13.23b)$$

We see that equation (13.23a) represents a convolution of the harmonic forcing with the input response of the linear system (see also Chapter 5 for additional material). Before moving forward, we stress that the above solution assumes an asymptotically stable system, with all eigenvalues of L contained in the stable half-plane. In essence, we only consider the long-term response of the system and ignore transient processes of establishing this long-term response. As a consequence, the term in (13.23b) containing the matrix exponential can be neglected. We have

$$\mathbf{q} = (i\omega - \mathrm{L})^{-1} \mathbf{f} \exp(i\omega t) = \mathbf{q}_{w} \exp(i\omega t). \qquad (13.24)$$

Reformulating the adjoint equation in (13.11b) for our special case, we obtain

$$- \frac{d}{dt} \mathbf{q}^{\dagger} = \mathrm{L}^{H} \mathbf{q}^{\dagger} + \frac{2\mathrm{Q}}{\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}} \mathbf{q}_{w} \exp(i\omega t) w, \qquad (13.25)$$

which has the formal solution

$$\mathbf{q}^{\dagger} = (-i\omega - \mathrm{L}^{H})^{-1} \frac{2\mathrm{Q}}{\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}} \mathbf{q}_{w} \exp(i\omega t) w. \qquad (13.26)$$

From the optimality condition (13.19) and the expression for $\mathbf{q}^{\dagger}$ we get

$$- \frac{\|\mathbf{q}_{w}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{R}}^{4}} 2\mathrm{R} \mathbf{f} w = \left(\frac{\partial \mathrm{N}}{\partial \mathbf{f}}\right)^{H} \mathbf{q}^{\dagger} = \exp(-i\omega t) \mathbf{q}^{\dagger}. \qquad (13.27)$$

Combining with (13.24) and (13.26) yields

$$\mathrm{R}^{-1} (-i\omega - \mathrm{L}^{H})^{-1} \mathrm{Q} (i\omega - \mathrm{L})^{-1} \mathbf{f} = \sigma^{2} \mathbf{f}, \qquad (13.28)$$

with $\sigma^{2}$ as the sought-after response gain, defined as $\|\mathbf{q}_{w}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}/\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}$. Using the Cholesky factorization of the weights Q and R (see above), we can write the above expression more compactly as $$\big[(i\omega - \bar{\mathrm{L}})^{-1}\big]^{H} (i\omega - \bar{\mathrm{L}})^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{f}} = \sigma^{2} \bar{\mathbf{f}}, \qquad (13.29)$$ with $\bar{\mathrm{L}} = \mathrm{F}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{G}^{-1}$ and $\bar{\mathbf{f}} = \mathrm{G}\mathbf{f}$. As before, we obtain an eigenvalue problem for the gain $\sigma^{2}$: the optimal frequency response gain for a given frequency $\omega$ is the largest eigenvalue of $\big[(i\omega - \bar{\mathrm{L}})^{-1}\big]^{H} (i\omega - \bar{\mathrm{L}})^{-1}$, or, equivalently, the first singular value of $(i\omega - \bar{\mathrm{L}})^{-1}$. This latter operator is referred to as the resolvent. The optimal forcing and output are given, respectively, by the principal right and left singular vectors (Schmid & Henningson 2001, Schmid 2007). The resolvent has found widespread application in the investigation of fluid behavior (see, e.g., McKeon & Sharma 2010, Moarref et al. 2014, Yeh & Taira 2019).
