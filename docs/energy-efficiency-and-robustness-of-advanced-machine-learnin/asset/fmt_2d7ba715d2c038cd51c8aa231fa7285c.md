
### 7.3.4 Robustness Exploration Methodology

Our study explores the SNNs’ robustness under different adversarial noise budgets, and this, for different `V[t]` and `T` parameters combinations. [Figure 7.18](#chapter7.xhtml#fig7_18) gives an overview of the different components of our methodology. It is composed of the following two main steps. **(1)** The first step of the exploration is meant to exclude combinations of structural parameters that are not propitious for efficient learning in SNNs. Indeed, there is no interest in studying the robustness of SNNs with low baseline performance. **(2)** In the second step, for all `V[t]` and `T` settings that enable the SNN training to converge efficiently, we proceed with a robustness exploration.

![Figure 7.18: Methodology steps for exploring the SNN robustness, varying the threshold voltage V_{th}, the time window T, and the adversarial perturbation ε.](images/fig7_18.jpg)

[Figure 7.18:](#chapter7.xhtml#b7fig7_18) Methodology steps for exploring the SNN robustness, varying the threshold voltage `V[t]`, the time window `T`, and the adversarial perturbation `ε`.

[Algorithm 25](#chapter7.xhtml#comp07_3) details our robustness exploration methodology. Lines 1 and 2 browse the `n` threshold voltages and `m` time windows to explore. Once the training is launched (line 3), we proceed to the SNN learnability study for the given combination `V[t]` and `T`. As shown in line 4, the learnability is quantitatively verified by setting a minimum baseline accuracy level below which we consider the SNN learning inefficient. This value depends on the SNN architecture, learning method, dataset, and application. In our case study, we use this accuracy threshold equal to 70% as it is typically achieved by state-of-the-art SNNs \[[209](#refbib.xhtml#refbib209)\].

The security analysis starts from line 5. It generates adversarial examples with different noise budgets to fool the SNN. The noise budget models the aggressiveness allowed within the attack generation; the higher the noise budget, the more aggressive the attack is. First, the counter of successful attack generation cases is initialized (line 6). Then, we browse the dataset 𝒟 (line 7) to generate the adversarial attacks using PGD, as shown in line 8. Afterward, the algorithm verifies if the generated example can fool the SNN (lines 9-10), i.e., if the attack forced the output to a wrong label, and accordingly increment the adversarial success counter. Then the robustness is evaluated for every `ε` value as the rate of attacks for which the adversary failed to generate an adversarial example that fools the victim SNN (line 11). Hence, by tracking the accuracy slope w.r.t. `ε`, we can compare the robustness of each model to adversarial attacks.

### 7.3.5 Evaluation of the SNNs' Robustness

#### 7.3.5.1 Experimental Setup

Our experiments are performed using Norse \[[209](#refbib.xhtml#refbib209)\], a library that expands PyTorch \[[204](#refbib.xhtml#refbib204)\] with primitives for bio-inspired neural components, thereby allowing to train and run SNNs in the spiking domain. The adversarial attacks are implemented using Foolbox v3.1.1 \[[224](#refbib.xhtml#refbib224)\]. The SNN architecture is a Lenet-5 adapted to the spiking domain using the LIF neuron model and trained on the MNIST database \[[118](#refbib.xhtml#refbib118)\]. The experiments are run on an Nvidia GeForce RTX 2080 Ti GPU.

#### 7.3.5.2 Learnability Study

Before studying the robustness when varying the structural parameters, we need to define our exploration space. The default values of the threshold voltage and time window parameters are `V[t]` and `T` = (1, 64). Therefore, we focus on having an overview of the learnability of SNNs in the neighborhood of these settings. [Figure 7.19](#chapter7.xhtml#fig7_19) shows the accuracy heat map for different `V[t]` and `T` combinations. The horizontal and vertical axes denote `V[t]` and `T`, respectively. Different colors denote the accuracy of the SNN. Note that the highest-accuracy combination tends to be toward the top-left corner, i.e., low `V[t]` and high `T`. However, the heat map is not monotonic. For example, there are combinations with an accuracy lower than 16%, surrounded by combinations with an accuracy higher than 89%.

#### 7.3.5.3 Security Study

In this section, we investigate the robustness of SNNs while increasing the attacks’ adversarial noise magnitude in a white-box scenario. We first proceed to a holistic exploration under all previous combinations of `V[t]` and `T`. [Figure 7.20](#chapter7.xhtml#fig7_20) shows the accuracy degradation of SNNs under the PGD attack with noise magnitudes of 1 and 1.5.

![Figure 7.20: Heat maps showing the SNN accuracy for the MNIST dataset using different combinations of (V_{i},T_{j}). (a) Security analysis for ε = 1. (b) Security analysis for ε = 1.5.](images/fig7_20.jpg)

[Figure 7.20:](#chapter7.xhtml#b7fig7_20) Heat maps showing the SNN accuracy for the MNIST dataset using different combinations of `V[i]` and `T[j]`. **(a)** Security analysis for `ε` = 1. **(b)** Security analysis for `ε` = 1.5.

The first fascinating insight we extract from [Figure 7.20](#chapter7.xhtml#fig7_20) is that high baseline learnability (without adversarial attacks) is not a guarantee of robustness. Moreover, we notice a different evolution of the SNNs w.r.t. adversarial attacks based on their respective structural parameters. More specifically, two SNNs with a starting comparable accuracy may have different behaviors under attack. For example, both combinations `V[t]` and `T` = (0.5, 80) and `V[t]` and `T` = (0.75, 72) start with 97% accuracy. However, while the accuracy of the first combination (as highlighted in [Figure 7.20a](#chapter7.xhtml#fig7_20)) drops to 27% under `ε` = 1 attack budget, the second loses only 6% of its initial accuracy under the same attack noise magnitude.

[Figure 7.20b](#chapter7.xhtml#fig7_20) shows a more accentuated behavior for large adversarial perturbations (`ε` = 1.5), where more than half of the heat map has SNN accuracies lower than 20%. It is interesting to note that the accuracy of the SNN with `V[t]` and `T` = (0.75, 48) has dropped to 2% when `ε` = 1.5, while it was relatively higher (86%) when `ε` = 1. On the other hand, the SNN with `V[t]` and `T` = (1, 80) shows relatively high robustness against the PGD attack since the SNN accuracy has not dropped much (from 72% when `ε` = 0 and `ε` = 1 to 66% when `ε` = 1.5).
