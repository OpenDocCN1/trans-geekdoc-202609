# 2.4.2 The properties of the transposon statistics

In total, 38 of 1,556 genes in the first replicate of the Francisella Tularensis species had no transposon insertions. These genes were definitely essential. The rest 1,518 genes had varying insertion distributions in their gene bodies. The essentiality analysis of these 1,518 genes was thus the main objective of essential gene discovery.

The ultimate goal of the gene essentiality pattern discovery is to examine whether a transposon statistic (either the insertion statistic or the site statistic) of a transposon sequencing followed a bimodal density. Only the density of a transposon statistic can be bimodal, it is thus possible to determine a proper threshold to separate essential genes from non-essential genes.

Figure 2.40(a) shows the density estimated based on the logarithm-transformed insertion statistic and Figure 2.40(b) shows the density estimated based on the logarithm-transformed site statistic for the first replicate of the Francisella Tularensis species. However, neither had a perfect bimodality. Therefore, it was not easy to determine an optimal threshold to partition these genes into two clusters, to discover essential genes based on this type of densities. This means that some advanced statistic must be considered for a better gene essentiality analysis.
