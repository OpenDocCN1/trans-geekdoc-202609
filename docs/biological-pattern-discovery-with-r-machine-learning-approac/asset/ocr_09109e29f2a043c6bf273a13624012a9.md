# 7.2 Sequence alignment

Either DNA or RNA protein sequence analysis is one important area in modern biology. There are several separate subjects in the area of sequence analysis. Two subjects are the fundamental stones for the modern biology. They are the sequence homology alignment problem [Lipman, et al., 1989; Thompson Plewniak, 1999; Nguyen, et al., 216] and the sequence assembly problem [Mysers, et al., 2000]. Two or more than two sequences to discover how sequences are similar to each other so that the annotation of a novel sequence can be made. The latter results from the modern sequencing technology, where a sequencing equipment will generate short sequencing segments which are called sequencing reads for a genome. It is required to assemble these sequencing reads to generate longer sequencing segments before being aligned or mapped to a reference genome for further study.

The sequence homology alignment is used to determine how similar two sequences are. This approach has at least three benefits. First, species evolutionary history can be revealed through an alignment of the collected sequences for a species or an organism [Voronova and Chelomina, 2020]. This can also be extended to cross-species and cross-organisms studies [Burow, et al, 2020]. Second, the sequence homology alignment approach can help reveal some conserved segments which correspond to some important genes in relation with biological properties of a species or an organism, or in relation with some diseases [Caldovic, et al., 2014]. Third, the sequence homology alignment approach can be used to reveal genetic traits [Yu, et al., 2019].
