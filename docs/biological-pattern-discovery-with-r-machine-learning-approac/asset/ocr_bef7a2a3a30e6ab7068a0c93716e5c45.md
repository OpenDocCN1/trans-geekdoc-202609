ARHGAO28 shows a slow decaying Gamma density. The gene ATP10B shows a median decaying Gamma density. Therefore, their bimodality pattern may be different.

The delta expression of a gene was generated using the following approach. Suppose a vector of $K$ ($58$ for the letrozole drug data) case expressions were expressed as below, where $n$ was the $n^{th}$ gene in data,

```
$$x_n = (x_{n,1}, x_{n,2}, \dots, x_{n,K})$$ (6.38)
```

The expressions in this vector were sorted in an ascending order and expressed as below,

```
$$x'_n = (x_{n,1}, x_{n,2}, \dots, x'_{n,K})$$ (6.39)
```

A delta expression was calculated by the difference between all consecutive pairs of sorted expressions,

```
$$\delta_n = (x_{n,2} - x_{n,1}, x_{n,3} - x_{n,2}, \dots, x_{n,K} - x_{n,(K-1)})$$ (6.40)
```

Figure 6.32 shows that the Gamma density modelling worked very well for fitting the Gamma density to the delta expressions of three genes selected from the letrozole drug data. Based on an estimated Gamma density function, the significance of each delta expression can be evaluated.

Figure 6.33 shows the relationship between the Gamma $p$ values and the delta expressions for three genes from the letrozole drug data shown in Figure 6.32. In these plots, it can be seen that some genes contained significantly great delta expressions, as the genes ZMIZ2 in Figure 6.33(a) and ATP10B in Figure 6.33(b). However, some genes did not contain a significantly great delta expression, such as the gene ARHGAP28 in Figure 6.33(c). A gene containing no such great delta expression implies that the expressions of such a gene likely form a unimodal distribution. Otherwise, the expressions of such a gene may form a multi-modal distribution including a bimodal distribution or such as a gene with outliers.
