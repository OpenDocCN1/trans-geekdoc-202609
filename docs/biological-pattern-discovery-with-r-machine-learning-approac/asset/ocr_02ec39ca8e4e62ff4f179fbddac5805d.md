The other examines the nucleic acid composition pattern or the amino acid pattern on sub-sequences. Discovering the protein functional sites based on the whole sequence pattern is mainly based on the sequence homology alignment approaches. Through an alignment, it is possible to annotate and discover the functions of a novel protein as well as the functional sites. This process is called the protein function annotation [Lowenstein, et al., 2007; Huerta-Cepas, et al, 2017; Mahlich, et al., 2018]. This method has been widely used; for instance, the primary sequence comparison can enhance the protein function annotation performance in addition to the use of the 3D protein structuring technology [Whisstock and Lesk, 2003; Shatsky, et al., 2008].

Predicting and discovering protein functional sites based on sequences or peptides is an exercise of the local protein function annotation. This study includes the discovery of the protein cleavage sites [Thomson, et al., 2003; Yang and Berry, 2004; Berry, et al., 2004; Senawongse, et al., 2005; Yang, et al., 2005; Yang and Thomson, 2005; Sidhu and Yang, 2006; Yang and Hammer, 2007; Maji and Das, 2010; Maji and Das, 2010b; Cabuk, et al., 2018; Betancur-Anconca, et al., 2018] and the discovery of the post-translational modification sites [Lu, et al., 2011; Trost, et al., 2016; Ivanisenko, et al., 2019]. This kind of research targets local protein structures, such as the amino acid composition in the posttranslational cleaved peptides. This set collects two sets of peptides. One is composed of the experimentally verified peptides and the other is composed of the null peptides. A null peptide is a peptide in which there is no experimentally verified protein functional site.
