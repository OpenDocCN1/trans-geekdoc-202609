It tests the bimodality for a population of data through maximising the difference between two clusters of an empirical distribution [Hartigan, 1985; Hartigan and Hartigan, 1985]. Its null hypothesis is the unimodality.

To study the subprocess problem in biomedical practices, a gene expression bimodality pattern discovery project can be based on a single-object approach or a dual-object approach. The dual-object approaches assume the cooperation between two genes such as an oncogene and a suppression gene relationship to a disease development [Wappert et al., 2016]. The single-object approach takes one gene for the study. This chapter mainly focuses on the single-object approaches for gene expression bimodality pattern discovery. Most single-object approaches assume that the expressions of a gene follow a mixture of two distributions [Hellwig, et al., 2010].

The measure used by the PACK algorithm is similar to the Kurtosis test [Teschendroff, et al., 2006], where the bimodality of a gene is tested by a kurtosis profile, which is either negative or positive. A positive measure stands for a super-Gaussian and a negative measure represents a sub-Gaussian. The bimodality index approach, on the other hand, assumes that the expressions of a bimodal gene follow a mixture of two Gaussians [Wang, et al., 2009]. The relative discrepancy measure is also based on the assumption of a Gaussian mixture [Besserabova, et al., 2010]. By formulating a ratio between a bimodal distribution and a unimodal distribution, the likelihood ratio between them is used for the bimodality pattern discovery [Hu, 2008]. In addition to the use of a measure, cluster analysis has also been used to discover a bimodal distribution [Gormley and Tozeren, 2008].
