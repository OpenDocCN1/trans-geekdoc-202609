[Nussinov, 2015; Cortesi, et al., 2020; Gillen, et al., 2020; Tang, et al., 2020].

## 1.1 The responsive gene discovery problem

One major objective of a biological investigation through an experiment is to identify genes with some desired functions. When a species or an organism has experienced a stress and some phenotypic phenomenon has been observed, it is desirable to study which genes have responded to the stress. A gene which shows a significant response to a stress in an association with a phenotypical phenomenon or phenomenon change is called a responsive gene [Alves, et al., 2016]. Responsive gene discovery in a stress-related experiment is called a genotype-phenotype mapping process [Bassel, 2019; Bolker, 2019; Koch, 2019; Lian, et al., 2019; Kawasaki, et al., 2020; Kowalko, 2020; Morris, et al., 2020].

The main objective of a genotype-phenotype mapping process is to establish either a qualitative or quantitative associative model. Based on such a model, it is aimed to discover and describe the dependency relationship between the genotype data and the phenotype data in an experiment. Afterwards, either the interpretation of the existing phenomena or the prediction of future events is followed.

A responsive gene is also called an essential gene or a differentially expressed gene depending on an experiment. Normally, the proportion of responsive genes is small in most biological experiments. This provides a traditional basis for simplifying, but not completely ignoring the complexity of a biological system to focus focal points of interest [Lai, et al., 2008; Chen, et al., 2011; Zeng, et al., 2013; Alves, et al., 2016; Liu, et al., 2016; Antolovic, et al., 2019; Sidorenko, et al., 2019; Trolet, et al., 2019].

There are mainly two types of approaches for the genotype-phenotype mapping study. The two types depend on how an experiment has been carried out. Suppose a phenotypical phenomenon (change) within a biological system has been observed and the corresponding activity changes of all genes of the species have been recorded. When a researcher wants to do next is to find which genes are the responsive genes. For instance, suppose the bacterial growth has been observed in a transposon experiment and the transposon distribution across a genome has been estimated. The analysis of the distribution can identify genes which are essential to the bacterial growth [Langridge, et al., 2009; Zomer, et al., 2012; Yang, et al., 2017]. This kind of responsive gene discovery study is normally based on a single-condition experiment. If a biology experiment is composed of two conditions, a condition-wise comparison process can identify genes with different activities across two conditions. In this situation, the other approach is employed to discover the responsive genes, such as the gene expression pattern discovery [Tusher, et al., 2001; Smyth, 2004; Chagoyen and Pazos, 2010; Mingue and Dopazo, 2011; Chagoyen, et al., 2017; Miller, et al., 2019; Tsuda, et al., 2019].

## 1.2 The peptide function discovery problem

Predicting or discovering the protein function within a protein sequence has been one of the most researched subjects in biology. Proteins as macromolecules have a variety of roles in cells. Metabolism relies on the functional proteins for enzymatic function, working as the energy carriers as well as the hormones for chemical reactions in cells. The protein functions are researched mainly in two ways, i.e., the crystal structural analysis and the primary structure analysis. The crystallising experiment is a 3D technology to investigate the protein structures. For instance, the disordered segments of a protein can be discovered using the 3D crystallising experiment [Yang, et al., 2005]. The molecular structures of S1 and S2 subunits of SRAS were examined using the crystallising technique [Spiga, et al., 2003]. The study examined the shape and surface of the hydrophobicity in association with the known functional proteins so the cleavage pattern between the S1 and S2 subunits can be thoroughly examined. Another recent crystal structure study has examined the structure of non-structural protein nine (NSP9) of SARS-CoV-2, which can mediate viral replication, the role of promoting virulence as well as the role of replicating genomic RNAs. Through the use of the crystallising technology, it has been found that NSP9 of SARS-CoV-2 is highly structurally conserved [Little, et al., 2020].

## 1.2 The peptide function discovery problem

Predicting or discovering the protein function within a protein sequence has been one of the most researched subjects in biology. Proteins as macromolecules have a variety of roles in cells.
