In the initial stage shown in Table 7.2, the alignment cost (distance) was calculated when aligning residues of two sequences with gaps. The third row of the table, which was below sequence $y$, was for inserting gaps into sequence $x$. When aligning the first residue of sequence $x$ (T) with a gap, one gap was inserted into sequence $x$. Because of this, one unit of penalty was applied. This column of cells were filled with integers according to how many gaps were inserted. For instance, the cell (1, 0) was filled by a one and the cell (2, 0) was filled by a two.

The next stage was to consider how to fill a value for the cell (1, 1) by optimising three incremental alignments. The move was either a diagonal move, or a horizontal move or a vertical move. The horizontal move for this cell was the move from the left cell, the cell (1, 0) was an alignment between the first residue (T) of sequence $x$ and a gap. It had already a unit penalty due to the gap inserted into sequence $y$. The alignment of this horizontal move is shown below, where a bar was used to separate between aligned residues and the current alignment and the # key was followed by all remaining residues of two sequences were not used in current.

```

y: - | T #GGCT ⇒ cost = 1 + 1 = 2
```

In this horizontal move, the first residue (T) of sequence $y$ was picked up while no residue was picked up from sequence $x$ meaning a gap was inserted into sequence $x$. In the above equation, the first stands for the penalty when align the residue T of sequence $x$ with a gap and the second stands for the penalty when align the residue T of sequence $y$ with a gap. Therefore this horizontal move had a cost two. After this alignment, the remaining residues of two sequences were GGCT and GCT.

A vertical move was to move from the cell above the current cell, i.e., moving from the cell (0, 1) to the cell (1, 1). The cell (0, 1) was an alignment between a gap, which was inserted into sequence $x$ and the first residue (T) of sequence $y$. This alignment is shown below, where the first residue (T) of sequence $x$ was picked up for the alignment but a gap was inserted into sequence $y$. Remaining residues of two sequences were the same as above.

```
x: - T #GGCT
y: T - #GGCT ⇒ cost = 1 + 1 = 2
```

It was implemented by an alignment shown below, where both sequences contributed their first residues to the alignment. This alignment was moved from the previous gap to gap alignment, i.e., a NULL alignment to the current alignment between the residue $T$ of sequence $x$ and the residue $T$ of sequence $y$. Therefore this move was selected as the optimal alignment for this cell and a zero was filled into the cell. ## 7.2.2.2 The backward propagation stage

The search in the backward propagation stage starts from the most-right corner cell with the minimum alignment distance. If the alignment distance of the most-bottom-right cell is not the least, a search of the least distance is backwards to the left cells or upwards to the upper cells. In this case, gaps are inserted at the end of one sequence.

Based on Table 7.5, the best route for the cell (5, 4) was the diagonal move from the cell (4, 3). The alignment distance of the cell (5, 4) was one. Because the move for the cell (5, 4) was diagonal, the distance decomposition is shown below, where the distance in the cell (4, 3) was one, i.e., d(GCCT, GCT)=1 and the current alignment in the cell (5, 4) was 0, i.e., $\delta(T, T)=0.

$d(TGGCT, TGCT) = d(TGGC, TGC) + \delta(T, T) = 1 + 0$

The cell (4, 3) was moved diagonally from the cell (3, 2). Therefore the distance decomposition is shown below, where the residues on the right side of a bar was for the residues which were passed in the previous propagation steps

$d(TGGC, TGC) = d(TGG, TG) + \delta(C, T) = 1 + 0$

The cell (3, 2) was moved diagonally from the cell (2, 1). The distance decomposition is shown below,

$d(TGG, TG) = d(TG, T) + \delta(G, G) = 1 + 0$

The cell (2, 1) was moved vertically from the cell (1, 1). The distance decomposition is shown below,

$d(TG, T) = d(T, T) + \delta(G, -) = 0 + 1$

The cell (1, 1) was moved diagonally from the cell (0, 0). The final decomposition is shown below,

$d(T, T) = d(-,-) + \delta(T, T) = 0 + 0$
