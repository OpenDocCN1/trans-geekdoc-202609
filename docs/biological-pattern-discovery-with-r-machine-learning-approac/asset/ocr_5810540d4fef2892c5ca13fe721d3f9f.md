# 1.2 The peptide function discovery problem

Predicting or discovering the protein function within a protein sequence has been one of the most researched subjects in biology. Proteins as macromolecules have a variety of roles in cells. Metabolism relies on the functional proteins for enzymatic function, working as the energy carriers as well as the hormones for chemical reactions in cells. The protein functions are researched mainly in two ways, i.e., the crystal structural analysis and the primary structure analysis. The crystallising experiment is a 3D technology to investigate the protein structures. For instance, the disordered segments of a protein can be discovered using the 3D crystallising experiment [Yang, et al., 2005]. The molecular structures of S1 and S2 subunits of SRAS were examined using the crystallising technique [Spiga, et al., 2003]. The study examined the shape and surface of the hydrophobicity in association with the known functional proteins so the cleavage pattern between the S1 and S2 subunits can be thoroughly examined. Another recent crystal structure study has examined the structure of non-structural protein nine (NSP9) of SARS-CoV-2, which can mediate viral replication, the role of promoting virulence as well as the role of replicating genomic RNAs. Through the use of the crystallising technology, it has been found that NSP9 of SARS-CoV-2 is highly structurally conserved [Little, et al., 2020].
