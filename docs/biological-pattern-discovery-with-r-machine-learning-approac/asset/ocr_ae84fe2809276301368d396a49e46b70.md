There are currently two types of integrative studies for the interplay between the epigenetic signatures and the genetic signatures. The first type analyzes the epigenetic signatures and the genetic signatures separately followed by a consistency validation about how the discovered differentially expressed genes and the discovered differentially methylated sites are correlated in the relationship with a disease. For instance, this approach was used to discover aberrant genes in association with breast cancer [Chari, et al., 2010]. The cluster analysis algorithms have also been used to examine whether the epigenetic signatures and the genetic signatures show similar abundance pattern in relation with diseases [Kresse, et al., 2012].

The second type of approach models the epigenetic-genetic interplay directly. For instance, the correlation analysis was used to examine how the DNA copy number and the methylation ratio interact with the gene expression based on the deep sequencing transcript expression for a breast cancer [Sun, et al., 2011]. When studying the sperm motility, a mixture model has been constructed based on the pooled methylation data and the gene expression data [Pacheco, et al., 2011]. The correlation between the methylation and sperm motility was examined using regression models, where the positive regression coefficient was mapped to a hyper-methylated site and the negative one hyper-methylated [Sms, 2004]. In one study, 62 primary fibroblast samples were used to analyse how the epigenetic signatures influence the genetic signatures for the species, where the gene expressions were regressed on the neighbouring methylation sites [Wagner, et al., 2014]. Another study also employed only local and few epigenetic signatures to examine their contributions to the regulation of genes [Ping, et al., 2015].
