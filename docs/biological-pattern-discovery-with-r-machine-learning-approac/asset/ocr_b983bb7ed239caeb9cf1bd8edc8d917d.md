# Preface

Over time, the majority of biological data pattern discovery tasks have shifted from traditional univariate approaches, by which the activity of a single molecule is analysed using a statistical test, to more sophisticated approaches. Along with the fast development and modernisation of biotechnology such as the microarray technology, the next-generation sequencing and mass-spectroscopy technology, biological pattern discovery has gradually become multivariate, multi-species and multi-omics as well as network-based. It is the so-called big-data era, which not only has the promising prospect but also brings with it challenges such as the computing facility problem, the model complexity problem, the generalisation capability problem, and importantly the interpretation and extrapolation capability problem.

Researchers and scientists have therefore made huge efforts to overcome such challenges. Among many successful developments, machine learning has always played and still plays a very important as well as unique role in biological pattern discovery. Almost all machine learning areas have greatly engaged in biological pattern discovery from unsupervised to supervised machine learning, from deterministic modelling to stochastic modelling, from classical algorithms to more and more cutting-edge algorithms. The variety of biological data, such as from the microarray gene expression data to the next-generation sequencing data, from image data to spectroscopy data, have all seen the involvement of various advanced machine learning algorithms and approaches. The huge success of biological pattern discovery could not have been realised without the support of machine learning.
