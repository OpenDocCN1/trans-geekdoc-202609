# 6.4.2 COPA

The cancer profile outlier analysis algorithm (COPA) was perhaps the earliest for discovering heterogeneous DEGs [Tomlins, et al., 2005]. COPA has modified the $t$ statistic as a ratio of the distance between the $r^{th}$ (default value was the $9^{th}$) percentile of the case expressions and the median of all expressions over the median absolute distance (deviated from the whole population median of the gene). The COPA $t$ statistic is defined as below,

> $t^{COPA} = \frac{q_r(\mathbf{y}) - \lambda}{\sigma}

where $\sigma = 1.4826 = 	ext{median}\{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} - \lambda\}$, $\mathbf{x}$ stands for a vector of the control expressions and $\mathbf{y}$ stands for a vector of case expressions, $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ stands for the $r^{th}$ percentile of $\mathbf{y}$ and $\lambda$ is the median of both $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$. The calculation of the $r^{th}$ percentile is irrelevant to both of outliers. When the outlier number is small, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is small. However, when the outlier number is large, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is large. The $r^{th}$ percentile also depends on the distribution of a real data set. The COPA $p$ values are calculated by the permutation approach.
