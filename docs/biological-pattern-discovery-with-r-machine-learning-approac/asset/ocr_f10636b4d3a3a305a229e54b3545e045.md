# 3.1 A biology question — protease cleavage

A protease is an enzyme that interacts with polyproteins for several biological processes in cells [Antalis, et al., 2007]. For instance, a hepatitis C viral protein can cleave a polyprotein within an affected cell to release viral proteins. These released viral proteins will escape from an affected cell to invade and affect healthy cells to produce more viral proteins [Gottwein, et al., 2007; Scheel, et al., 2008]. An inhibitor is an effective clinical approach to stop or slow down the viral affinity [Lawitz, et al., 2019; Ong, et al., 2019]. Designing an effective drug against a viral protein requires the study of the amino acid composition trend in primary structure (peptides) around the cleavage sites of a specific protease [Cabuk, et al., 2018; Bancur-Ancona, et al., 2018].

A peptide is a sub-sequence with a few or a dozen of the amino acids. The function of a protease is to cleave a polyprotein at the targeted residues to release functional proteins. The interaction between a protease and a polyprotein highly depends on the specificity of the amino acids in the nearby areas of the interaction sites. Such an interaction site between a polyprotein and a protease in a polyprotein is called a protease cleavage site. The consequence of such an interaction or cleavage is the split of a polyprotein leading to the release of functional proteins.
