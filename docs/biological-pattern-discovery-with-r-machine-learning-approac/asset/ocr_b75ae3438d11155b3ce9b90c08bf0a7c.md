# 6.5 Gene expression bimodality pattern discovery

In addition to the heterogeneous gene expression pattern discovery problem, the gene expression bimodality pattern discovery problem is another important subject in gene expression pattern discovery. This is due to the fact that many human-related diseases as well as treatments such as cancers can hardly demonstrate genetic responses to a stress, especially in some therapeutic exercises [Farahani, et al., 2007]; Ertel, et al., 2010; Randade, et al., 2010; Sun, et al., 2010]. The main player for this phenomenon to occur is the bio-medical diversity across populations as well as across people. The most observable phenomenon is the subpopulation formation problem due to the intrinsic diverse biological nature on human [Basu, et al., 2011; Krejci and Grim, 2010; Emmink, et al., 2011; He, et al., 2011; Trujillo, et al., 2011].

The problem of the inconsistent responses across replicates or the subpopulation formation has been well studied in the biomedicine area along with the fast development of the high-throughput gene expression technologies. To well understand and interpret the issue of the genetic response diversity within a population of patients, the subpopulation formation is this critical subject to study [Wappett, et al., 2016]. The tool for studying such type of problems is called the gene expression bimodality pattern discovery problem.
