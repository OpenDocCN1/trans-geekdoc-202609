The breast cancer data set with 569 samples [Wolberg, et al., 1995] Wolberg, et al., also used to demonstrate how the random sampling approach can help reach the real data proportion. Among these 569 samples, 212 were malignant tumours. The malignancy ratio was therefore 0.373, i.e., 37.3% were malignant tumours in this data set. Many sets of half samples were drawn from this data set randomly. The malignancy ratio was calculated within the drawn sample for varying sampling times from ten to 1,000. For instance, K sampling times, K samples were drawn. K malignancy ratios were calculated for K samples. Afterwards, the mean values of K malignancy ratios within the drawn sample were recorded. It was expected when the times of random sampling was increasing, the estimated malignancy ratio within the drawn sample should approach the true ratio, i.e., 0.373. Figure 3.14 shows this simulation. From this plot, it can be seen that when the sampling times increased, the estimated malignancy ratio among randomly drawn samples was indeed approaching 0.373.
