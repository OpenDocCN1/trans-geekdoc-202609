Although an essential gene should not attract any transpos insertion theoretically, a transpos may be found in an essential gene because of several reasons [Lamichhane, et al., 2003; Deng, et al., 2013; Fels, et al., 2013]. For instance, the essentially of a gene may not be disrupted if a transpos is inserted within the distal regions of a gene because the truncated gene product may still retain functionality. Often, the essentiality of a gene may still maintain if a transpos is inserted in only a few sites of a gene. In addition to these biological reasons, there are also two technical reasons by which it is required to examine the transpos insertion pattern genome-wise to avoid missed discovery of essential genes. First, the transpos sequencing technology is not yet 100% accurate to distribute a transpos to the locations where it should go without any bias. Second, aligning short transpos sequencing reads to a reference genome will not be free of error. The shorter the sequencing reads for an alignment, the greater the error will be. Therefore, a few insertions occurring in a gene may be caused by either error or errors aforementioned.

Due to the aforementioned reasons, there have been three updated machine learning approaches for a robust gene essential pattern analysis based on the high-throughput transpos sequencing technology. First, a gene with a few insertion sites may be classified as an essential gene [Langridge, et al., 2009]. Second, a gene with a few insertions may also be classified as an essential gene [Zomer, et al., 2012]. Third, a gene with the transpos insertions only in its distal regions may also be classified as an essential gene [Yang, et al., 2017].

The density estimation approaches have been exercised for discovering essential genes for a long time. In the context of gene essentiality analysis, the basic principle of density estimation is to re-construct a density function, which is unknown in advance, for a transpos statistic such as the transpos insertions per gene statistic or the transpos insertion sites per gene statistic. A constructed density model is thus used for the interpretation and prediction.
