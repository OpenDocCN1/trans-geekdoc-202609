### 6.4.2 COPA

The cancer profile outlier analysis algorithm (COPA) was perhaps the earliest for discovering heterogeneous DEGs [Tomlins, et al., 2005]. COPA has modified the $t$ statistic as a ratio of the distance between the $r^{th}$ (default value was the $9^{th}$) percentile of the case expressions and the median of all expressions over the median absolute distance (deviated from the whole population median of the gene). The COPA $t$ statistic is defined as below,

$$t^{COPA} = \frac{q_r(\mathbf{y}) - \lambda}{\sigma}$$

where $\sigma = 1.4826 = \text{median}\{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} - \lambda\}$, $\mathbf{x}$ stands for a vector of the control expressions and $\mathbf{y}$ stands for a vector of case expressions, $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ stands for the $r^{th}$ percentile of $\mathbf{y}$ and $\lambda$ is the median of both $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$. The calculation of the $r^{th}$ percentile is irrelevant to both of outliers. When the outlier number is small, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is small. However, when the outlier number is large, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is large. The $r^{th}$ percentile also depends on the distribution of a real data set. The COPA $p$ values are calculated by the permutation approach.

### 6.4.4 ORT

### 6.4.2 COPA

The cancer profile outlier analysis algorithm (COPA) was perhaps the earliest for discovering heterogeneous DEGs [Tomlins, et al., 2005]. COPA has modified the $t$ statistic as a ratio of the distance between the $r^{th}$ (default value was the $9^{th}$) percentile of the case expressions and the median of all expressions over the median absolute distance (deviated from the whole population median of the gene). The COPA $t$ statistic is defined as below,

$$t^{COPA} = \frac{q_r(\mathbf{y}) - \lambda}{\sigma}$$

where $\sigma = 1.4826 = \text{median}\{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} - \lambda\}$, $\mathbf{x}$ stands for a vector of the control expressions and $\mathbf{y}$ stands for a vector of case expressions, $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ stands for the $r^{th}$ percentile of $\mathbf{y}$ and $\lambda$ is the median of both $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$. The calculation of the $r^{th}$ percentile is irrelevant to both of outliers. When the outlier number is small, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is small. However, when the outlier number is large, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is large. The $r^{th}$ percentile also depends on the distribution of a real data set. The COPA $p$ values are calculated by the permutation approach.

### 6.4.3 OS

Following COPA, the outlier-sum algorithm (OS) was proposed [Tibshirani and Hastie, 2007]. The outliers are defined by $\{y \in y_i; y > q_{75}(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i) + \text{IQR}(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}$. The OS $t$ statistics is defined as below, where $m$ is the number of the case expressions. The OS $p$ value is also calculated using the permutation approach.

$$t^{OS} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(y_i - \lambda)}{\sigma} \quad (6.11)$$

### 6.4.4 ORT

The numerator was further revised in the outlier robust $t$ statistic algorithm (ORT). In ORT, the range of outlier discovery was enlarged using the inter-quarter percentile [Wu, 2007]. OS and ORT employ a similar division strategy. OS makes a division between the outlier and non-outlier expressions while ORT divides the control expressions using the pooled expressions. ORT uses the same statistic but defines the outliers in relation to the case expressions only:

$${y \in y_i; y > q_{75}(x_i) + \text{IQR}(x_i)\} \quad (6.12)$$

### 6.4.5 MOST

The maximum ordered subset $t$ statistic algorithm (MOST) employs a ranking method [Lian, 2008]. For both the control expressions and the case expressions, two median values are calculated. They are $\mu_x = \text{median}(\mathbf{x})$ and $\mu_y = \text{median}(\mathbf{y})$. $$\omega = 1.4826 \times \text{median}(|x - \mu_x|, |y - \mu_y|) \quad (6.13)$$

### 6.4.2 COPA

The cancer profile outlier analysis algorithm (COPA) was perhaps the earliest for discovering heterogeneous DEGs [Tomlins, et al., 2005]. COPA has modified the $t$ statistic as a ratio of the distance between the $r^{th}$ (default value was the $9^{th}$) percentile of the case expressions and the median of all expressions over the median absolute distance (deviated from the whole population median of the gene). The COPA $t$ statistic is defined as below,

$$t^{COPA} = \frac{q_r(\mathbf{y}) - \lambda}{\sigma}$$

where $\sigma = 1.4826 = \text{median}\{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} - \lambda\}$, $\mathbf{x}$ stands for a vector of the control expressions and $\mathbf{y}$ stands for a vector of case expressions, $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ stands for the $r^{th}$ percentile of $\mathbf{y}$ and $\lambda$ is the median of both $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$. The calculation of the $r^{th}$ percentile is irrelevant to both of outliers. When the outlier number is small, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is small. However, when the outlier number is large, the difference between $q_r(\mathbf{y})$ and $\lambda$ is large. The $r^{th}$ percentile also depends on the distribution of a real data set. The COPA $p$ values are calculated by the permutation approach.

### 6.4.3 OS

Following COPA, the outlier-sum algorithm (OS) was proposed [Tibshirani and Hastie, 2007]. The outliers are defined by $\{y \in y_i; y > q_{75}(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i) + \text{IQR}(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}$. The OS $t$ statistics is defined as below, where $m$ is the number of the case expressions. The OS $p$ value is also calculated using the permutation approach.

$$t^{OS} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(y_i - \lambda)}{\sigma} \quad (6.11)$$

### 6.4.4 ORT

The numerator was further revised in the outlier robust $t$ statistic algorithm (ORT). In ORT, the range of outlier discovery was enlarged using the inter-quarter percentile [Wu, 2007]. OS and ORT employ a similar division strategy. OS makes a division between the outlier and non-outlier expressions while ORT divides the control expressions using the pooled expressions. ORT uses the same statistic but defines the outliers in relation to the case expressions only:

$${y \in y_i; y > q_{75}(x_i) + \text{IQR}(x_i)\} \quad (6.12)$$

### 6.4.5 MOST

The maximum ordered subset $t$ statistic algorithm (MOST) employs a ranking method [Lian, 2008]. For both the control expressions and the case expressions, two median values are calculated. They are $\mu_x = \text{median}(\mathbf{x})$ and $\mu_y = \text{median}(\mathbf{y})$. $$\omega = 1.4826 \times \text{median}(|x - \mu_x|, |y - \mu_y|) \quad (6.13)$$
