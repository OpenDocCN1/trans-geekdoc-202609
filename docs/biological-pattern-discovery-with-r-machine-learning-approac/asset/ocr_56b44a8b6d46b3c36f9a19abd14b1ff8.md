Before discussing the uncertainty of a cluster model structure can be well-handled using the K-means clustering algorithm, two measurements can be used for the discussion. Figure 2.28 shows one scenario where a data set is composed of two clusters. Two clusters have the same centre-to-centre (or between cluster) distance in three scenarios (panels). But, two clusters have different within-cluster variances in three panels. Here a term is defined as the sum of within-cluster variances, which is called the within-cluster sum of squares. It was 24,132, 15,831 and 4,155 in three panels of Figure 2.28, i.e., panel (a), panel (b) and panel (c), respectively. The sum of within-cluster variance is also called the total within-cluster variance. Based on the comparison of these three panels, it can be seen that the discrimination power between two clusters depends on the total within-cluster variance. A greater total-cluster variance may lead to a poorer discrimination power between clusters. However, a smaller total within-cluster variance may result in a better discrimination power between clusters. Therefore, the data presented in Figure 2.28(a) may have the poorest discrimination power or the worst clustering performance while the data presented in Figure 2.28(b) may have the best clustering performance.
