Another typical issue is the interaction between the genetic signatures and the epigenetic signatures such as the methylation sites and the DNA copy numbers [Comai, 2000; Chang, et al., 2003; Chari, et al., 2010; Baylin and Jones, 2011; Sandoval and Estelle, 2012; 2012]. In this type of research, it has been found that the epigenetic signatures closely interact with the genetic signatures for certain disease development. Therefore combined or integrated studies have been carried out for researching and discovering of the genetic-epigenetic interplay in association with cancers as well as cellular functions. The studies have analysed these two types of signatures either separately followed by a consistency analysis or a joint cluster analysis [Chari, et al., 2010; Pacheco, et al., 2011; Sun, et al., 2011; Kresse, et al., 2012; Ping, et al., 2015]. Recent studies of the genetic-epigenetic interplay have covered the heart disease development [Akerberg and Pu, 2019], the colorectal cancer function [Yeh, et al., 2020] and the gestational diabetes mellitus [Rosik, et al., 2020]. The research of the genetic-epigenetic interplay is still challenging when more and more complicated experiments have been conducted.

# 1.4 The spectral molecular discovery process

The advanced scientific research of the light and spectroscopy analysis have been used in many areas including biology. A spectrum is composed of the continuous light waves. Many biology researches employ the spectral technology for the discovery of novel chemicals and drugs [Petucci, et al., 2007; Cho, et al., 2008; Kim, et al., 2008; de Sanctis, et al., 2011; Pitteri, et al., 2011; Baygi, et al., 2016; Kumamoto, et al., 2019]. For instance, the singular spectrometry and automated baseline correction have been used to discover novel chemicals based on the NMR spectroscopy [de Sanctis, et al., 2011]. To analyse breast cancer is developed, the mass spectroscopy has been used to discover and verify proteomic signatures of breast cancer [Pitteri, et al., 2011].
