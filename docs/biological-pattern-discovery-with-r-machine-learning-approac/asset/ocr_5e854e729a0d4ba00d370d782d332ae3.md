Suppose the $k^{th}$ gene was the host gene of the $m^{th}$ DMS and the $m^{th}$ DMS was ranked at the top in the $g^{th}$ regression model constructed for the $g^{th}$ DEG. The gene order distance between the $g^{th}$ DEG and the $k^{th}$ gene was defined as below,

```
$|g - k|$
```

The second distance was called the base pair distance. The mean basepair of the $g^{th}$ DEG was denoted by $\omega_g$ and the methylation site of the $m^{th}$ DMS (the top-ranked DMS for the regression model of the $g^{th}$ DEG) was denoted by $\omega_m$, the base pair distance between them was defined as below,

```
$|\omega_g - \omega_m|$
```

All the gene order distances and base distances were binned into six intervals to study the trend of the distribution pattern. Figure 4.30 shows the gene order distance distributions of four models. It can be seen that all the distributions had the same pattern or trend, i.e., the distributions were severely skewed towards the right side favouring the great gene distances. This shows that the host genes of top-ranked DMS were far away from the target DEGs. Note that a target DEG was one of 1,250 DEGs.

The chi-square test validated the severeness or the significance of the skewness in four models. All $p$ values were extremely small. Therefore, there was no doubt that the most important contributors to the differential expression profile in most DEGs were the remote methylation sites than local ones. This proved the complexity of the genetic-epigenetic patterns in living cells. For instance, in the Lasso model, 77% (970 of 1,250 M2E models) of the target DEGs and the methylation sites of the top-ranked DMSs of these 970 DEGs were separated by more than 1,000 genes. About 34% (425 of 1,250 M2E models) of the target DEGs and the methylation sites of the top-ranked DMSs of these 425 DEGs were separated by more than 10,000 genes.
