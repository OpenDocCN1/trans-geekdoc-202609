# 7.2.1.1 The three evolution events

The first issue of sequence homology alignment is that a species or an organism can evolve, making its primary sequence structure slightly changed. There are three evolution events which may happen through an evolution process. They are the residue substitution, which is also called the mutation, the insertion and the deletion of a residue or a segment within a sequence [Tseng, et al., 2006; Rivas, et al., 2008]. The latter two are also called the indel process. A residue substitution means the replacement of a residue, i.e., an existing nucleic acid or an amino acid has been replaced by a new nucleic acid or a new amino acid in evolution. For instance, the second residue of LGPSSQT has been mutated from G to S in an evolution leading to a new sequence LGPSSQT.

The indel stands for two scenarios. First, residue(s) is introduced into a sequence through an evolution process. This is called the insertion. Second, residue(s) is missing through an evolution process. This is called the deletion. Suppose an original sequence is LGPSSQT.
