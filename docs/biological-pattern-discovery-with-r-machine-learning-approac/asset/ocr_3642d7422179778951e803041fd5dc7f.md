In a biological/medical research, the genetic signature space is composed of thousands of genes, which are examined simultaneously using either the microarray technology or the sequencing technology nowadays. The output of the former technology is called the microarray gene expression data, and the latter is called the sequencing count data. The phenomenon change can be observed in the output data of either technology by comparing two conditions. The main objective of gene expression pattern discovery is thus to search in the genetic space to discover a small subset of genes which have significantly different responses to a stress across experimental conditions.

An important understanding of a living system is that such a system will not use all its genes to respond to a stress [Liu, et al., 2016; Antolovic, et al., 2019; Siderenko, et al., 2019]. This is why the subset of genes, which have significantly different responses to a stress across two experimental conditions, is normally of a small size. To discover differentially expressed genes (DEGs) given two experimental conditions, it is required to compare genes one by one using a significance measurement. A measurement should be unified across different experiments or experimental data sets. Two commonly used significance measurements are the biological significance and the statistical significance [Chagoyen and Pazos, 2010; Mingue and Dopazo, 2011; Chagoyen, et al., 2017; Miller, et al., 2019].

# 6.1.1 The biological significance

The biological significance used to discover DEGs is the so-called fold change or the base two logarithm fold change. It has been exercised in biology since 1960s [Pauly, 1960; Hill and Sussman, 1964; Gilbert and Ehrenstein, 1969]. Suppose a case is denoted by $A$ and a control condition is denoted by $B$. The case normally represents the experimental group in which a stress is applied or a phenomenon change has been observed. The control condition normally represents the group in which no stress is applied or no phenomenon change has been observed. The fold change below is defined as the ratio between two mean expressions ($\mu_{g,A}$ and $\mu_{g,B}$). Note that $\mu_{g,A}$ and $\mu_{g,B}$ stand for the mean expressions of the $g^{th}$ gene.
