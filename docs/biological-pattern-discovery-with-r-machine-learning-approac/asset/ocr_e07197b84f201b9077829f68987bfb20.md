#  3.4.2 Receiver operating characteristic analysis

A confusion matrix is a fix-point evaluation approach. It does not measure how robust a classifier is. In medical applications, it is often required to shift a threshold so as to minimise the cost involved with the life loss. It is for sure missing a cancer patient diagnosis has a much greater cost than a falsely diagnosed cancer patient.

Figure 3.10 shows such an example of the impact of a threshold on the classification performance. There are densities for two classes from a constructed classifier in this figure. The left density curve is for one class and the right density curve is for the other class. Two densities have been overlapped meaning the misclassification will or less happen no matter what a threshold is used to separate two classes. For two thresholds shown on the horizontal axes, the misclassification rates shown as the shaded in two panels are different. In Figure 3.10(a), the left class has a greater misclassification rate than the right class. The grey shaded area (for the left class) is much larger than the dark shaded area (for the right class). But in Figure 3.10(b), the right class has a greater misclassification rate than the left class. The dark shaded area is much larger than the grey shaded area.
