The algorithm is composed of two stages, i.e., the forward propagation and the backward propagation. In the forward propagation stage, the cells in a dynamic programming table are filled by a value to minimise distances. These are the main components of the Sellers algorithm and are detailed later in this section. In the backward propagation stage, the best alignment with the minimum edit distance is discovered. The alignment statistic used in the Sellers algorithm is shown in Table 7.1.

Table 7.1. The alignment statistic of the Sellers algorithm. $u$ and $v$ are two amino acids.

| | |
|---|---|
| $\delta(u, v) = 0$ | $u = v$, no distance |
| $\delta(u, v) = 1$ | $u \neq v$, mismatch |
| $\delta(u, -) = 1$ | Gap inserted into the 2nd sequence |
| $\delta(-, v) = 1$ | Gap inserted into the 1st sequence |

## 7.2.2.1 The forward propagation stage

The edit distance between two sequences $x$ and $y$ is a linear sum of the pair-wise edit distances, each of which is the distance between a pair of aligned residues from two sequences or a gap. The definition is shown below,

> $$\delta(x,y) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \delta(\tilde{x}_i, \tilde{y}_i) = \delta(\tilde{x}_1, \tilde{y}_1) + \delta(\tilde{x}_2, \tilde{y}_2) + \cdots \delta(\tilde{x}_N, \tilde{y}_N) \quad (7.3)$$

where $N$ stands for the length of aligned sequences, $\tilde{x}_i$ and $\tilde{y}_i$ are the $i^{th}$ aligned pair of residues, $\delta(\tilde{x}_i, \tilde{y}_i)$ is the edit distance between $\tilde{x}_i$ and $\tilde{y}_i$. Note that $\tilde{x}_i \in \{x, -\}$ means that $\tilde{x}_i$ can be a residue from sequence $x$ or a gap, which is denoted by the hyphen key. Similarly, $\tilde{y}_i \in \{y, -\}$ means that $\tilde{y}_i$ can be a residue from sequence $y$ or a gap. In the latter discussion, $\tilde{x}_i$ is simplified by $x_i$ and $\tilde{y}_i$ is simplified by $y_i$ for the simplicity. The above equation clearly shows one property of sequence homology alignment, i.e., the whole alignment can be linearly decomposed to piece-wise sub-optimisation problems as aforementioned. Because of this linearity, the above equation can be re-written as below,

Such a sequence can become evolutionary sequence with the mutations, the deletions or the insertions. Suppose there are two sequences for an alignment. They are $x_1x_2 \cdots x_M$ and $y_1y_2 \cdots y_N$. There are three incremental alignments in the forward propagation stage. Suppose a local alignment between two sequences $x$ and $y$ has reached a stage that $x_1x_2 \cdots x_m (m < M)$ and $y_1y_2 \cdots y_n (n < N)$. There are therefore remaining residues from two sequences $x$ and $y$ as the alignment candidates. Two sequences with the remaining residues are denoted by $x_{m+1}x_{m+2} \cdots x_M$ and $y_{n+1}y_{n+2} \cdots y_N$. The first residue of sequence $x$ is $x_{m+1}$ and the first remaining residue of sequence $y$ is $y_{n+1}$. They are the likely candidates to be picked up for the next alignment. However, a deletion or an insertion can happen.

The three incremental alignments are:
- Diagonal move: align $x_{m+1}$ with $y_{n+1}$.
- Horizontal move: align $x_{m+1}$ with a gap.
- Vertical move: align a gap with $y_{n+1}$.

Suppose two sequences were prepared for homology alignment. They were x=CTAG and y=ACTA. Figure 7.1 shows a dynamic programming table for this alignment task. Sequence x was placed on the left side of this table vertically. Suppose sequence y was placed on the top of this table. Suppose previous alignments had done for three cells (C, A), (C, C), and (T, A). For the alignment of the cell (T, C), which was for the second residue T of sequence x and the second residue C of sequence y, there were three alignment moves. They were the moves from each cell, i.e., the cells (C, A), (C, C) and (T, A). The diagonal move was A from the cell (C, A) to the current cell (T, C). In this situation, it was assumed that the first residue C of sequence x and the first residue A of sequence y had been already aligned. The current alignment led to the alignment of CT versus AC. The horizontal move was from the left cell (T, A) to the current cell (T, C). In this situation, it was assumed that the previous alignment was the alignment of CT versus A. Because the residue T of sequence x was used in the previous alignment, the current residue C of sequence y had to be aligned with a gap. The current alignment led to the alignment of CT– versus AC. The vertical move was from the cell (C, C) to the current cell (T, C). In this, it was assumed that the previous alignment was the alignment of C– versus AC and the current alignment resulted in C–T versus AC. This is because the residue C of sequence y was used in the previous alignment and a gap had to be inserted in sequence y to align the residue T of sequence x.
