Based on the discussion of the example in Figure 3.10, a question arises, i.e., the robustness of a classifier. The question is how to determine whether a classifier is robust against the threshold variation for the discrimination between two classes of data points. In other words, the misclassification rate variation of a classifier can be less dependent on the threshold variation. There are two classifiers shown in Figure 3.11, where one classifier is placed in the upper panels and the other classifier is placed in the lower panels. The densities of these two classes are highly overlapped shown in the upper panels. Therefore, when a threshold is changed, a dramatic misclassification rate change will happen. For instance, the top-left panel shows a much great misclassification rate for the class on the left side. The top-right panel shows a great misclassification rate for the class on the right side. However two densities of classes have a light degree of overlap shown in the lower panels. When a threshold is changed, little misclassification rate variation may happen. Therefore the classifier shown in the lower panels is more robust than that shown in the upper panels against the threshold variation. Through this analysis, it can be seen that if a classifier is robust against the threshold variation, the overlap between the densities of two classes should be as small as possible.
