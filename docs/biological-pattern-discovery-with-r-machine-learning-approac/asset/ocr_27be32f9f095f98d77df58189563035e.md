Fig. 5.3. The impact of $\lambda$, which is a smoothing parameter, on the baseline discovery and the signal discovery performance. (a) $\lambda = 0.6$ (b) $\lambda = 1.1$ (c) $\lambda = 2$.

For instance, Figure 5.3 shows three models using the spine approach to estimate a baseline for the same spectrum using the waveslim package, in which a smoothing parameter was tuned for the discovery of the best baseline. Three smoothing parameter values were used for the demonstration of the impact of a smoothing parameter on the smoothing performance were different. Because of the use of different smoothing parameters, the outcomes were different. Figure 5.3(a) shows an over-estimated baseline because the regularisation constant was too small. In this situation, the error minimisation was strengthened while the degree of the smoothness was weakened. The consequence was that the peaks or signals were all included into the estimated baseline, hence the signals were hardly detected. Figure 5.3(c) shows an under-estimated baseline. When the regularisation constant was too large, the smoothing was strengthened while the error minimisation was weakened. The consequence was that the baseline was a straight line and some true peaks were missed and some false peaks were present. Figure 5.3(b) shows a well-estimated baseline, where the regularisation constant was more properly selected. In and only in this situation, the majority of the true signals were discovered and the chance of the presence of the false signals was minimised.
