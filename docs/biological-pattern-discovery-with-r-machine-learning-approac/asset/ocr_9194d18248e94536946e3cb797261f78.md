A baseline is commonly contaminated by noise of multiple reasons, such as equipment error, sample preparation error and sample variance. Noise certainly adds diff to the accurate and correct estimation of a baseline. Because the baseline of a spectrum is unknown and not in an analytic format and importantly contaminated by noise, estimating the baseline for a spectrum needs special algorithms. Most baseline estimation algorithms work on two parts, i.e., the error be minimised and the smoothness of a baseline curve must be maximised. Therefore almost every baseline estimation algorithm has to trade off between the fidelity (error) and the smoothness. A model that strengthens too much on the fidelity will end up with a peak spectrum, in which almost no signal can be detected. However, a model that strengthens too much on a peak with too many artifacts leading to many false signals and missed signals. The success of accurate signal discovery is thus highly related with the selection of a proper baseline estimation algorithm. This chapter has introduced several baseline estimation algorithms including the most classical ones, such as the Whittaker-Henderson algorithm, the spline algorithm and the wavelet approach and the advanced one, such as the Bayesian Whittaker-Henderson algorithm. One important issue of discovering signals from a spectra data set with multiple replicates is the alignment of the discovered peaks across replicates. This is because the signals in a spectrum will have two uncertainties. First, a signal may occupy an interval on the horizontal axis of a spectrum (the spectra analyser). Therefore identifying a unique spectra analyser for a signal needs a smoothing process. Second, a signal may have some variation across replicates regarding the spectra analyser values. An appropriate alignment of the discovered signals across replicates must be considered. This chapter has therefore introduced a novel approach to align discovered peaks across replicates within the Bayesian Whittaker-Henderson algorithm.
